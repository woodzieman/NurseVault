=== Pseudohypoaldosteronism Type I (Merck Manual Professional) ===
Section: Nephrology > Renal Transport Abnormalities
Source: https://www.merckmanuals.com/professional/nephrology/renal-transport-abnormalities/pseudohypoaldosteronism-type-i
Retrieved: 2026-09-25

There are 3 types of pseudohypoaldosteronism:

Autosomal recessive pseudohypoaldosteronism type I

Autosomal dominant pseudohypoaldosteronism type I

Pseudohypoaldosteronism type II

Inheritance is autosomal recessive or autosomal dominant.

Pseudohypoaldosteronism type I resembles other forms of hypoaldosteronism except that aldosterone levels are high.

Autosomal recessive pseudohypoaldosteronism type I

The autosomal recessive form tends to be severe and permanent. Infants are resistant to the effects of aldosterone due to mutations causing decreased activity of the epithelial sodium channels (ENaC) located on the luminal membrane of the collecting tubule (overactivity of ENaC causes potassium excretion and sodium retention—see Liddle Syndrome). The sodium channel in tissues other than the kidneys may be affected, leading to a miliary rash and/or complications similar to those of cystic fibrosis.

Autosomal dominant pseudohypoaldosteronism type 1

Children are resistant to mineralocorticoids due to mutations of the mineralocorticoid receptor. The autosomal dominant form is usually less severe than the autosomal recessive form, affecting mainly the mineralocorticoid receptor in the kidney, and may resolve somewhat as children age.

Pseudohypoaldosteronism type II

The very rare pseudohypoaldosteronism type II (also known as Gordon syndrome) is caused by mutations in WNK1, WNK4, KLHL3 or CUL3 and results in hypertension, hyperkalemia, and hyperchloremic metabolic acidosis in the context of normal renal function. Renin levels are generally low; aldosterone levels can be either normal or low. Hypercalciuria can be seen. The diagnosis can be confirmed by genetic testing. It can be treated very effectively with thiazide diuretics.

Diagnosis of Pseudohypoaldosteronism Type I

Plasma renin and aldosterone levels

The diagnosis is suspected based on clinical findings of hypovolemia, high serum potassium, low serum sodium, high renin and aldosterone levels, particularly in infants with a positive family history. The diagnosis is confirmed by genetic testing.

Treatment of Pseudohypoaldosteronism Type I

High-sodium diet and sometimes fludrocortisone

A high-sodium diet helps maintain volume and BP and increases excretion of potassium. If diet is ineffective, fludrocortisone 0.5 to 1.0 mg orally twice/day or 1 to 2 mg orally once/day can be given.
