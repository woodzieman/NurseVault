Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  General Information
  Monitor patients for new or worsening morphologic abnormalities or cytopenias (e.g., bone marrow suppression) while on romiplostim. Patients who develop cytopenias while receiving romiplostim should discontinue treatment; consider performing a bone marrow biopsy, including staining for fibrosis. Development or progression of reticulin fiber deposition in the bone marrow has occurred while on romiplostim treatment (see Mechanism of Action). Reticulin fiber deposition in the bone marrow may lead to bone marrow fibrosis. Complete blood counts and peripheral smear should be monitored at baseline, weekly during dose titration, and monthly once a stable dose is achieved. Worsening morphological abnormalities should result in discontinuation of romiplostim and consideration for bone marrow biopsy. No patients in the controlled clinical studies developed bone marrow fibrosis, while 10 out of 271 developed evidence of reticulin deposition in the bone marrow. One patient with idiopathic thrombocytopenic purpura (ITP) and hemolytic anemia developed bone marrow fibrosis in the extension study.
 
Discontinuation of romiplostim can cause thrombocytopenia and increase the risk of bleeding. In some cases, platelet counts may drop to concentrations that are lower than before drug initiation. During clinical trials, the thrombocytopenia usually resolved within 14 days after drug discontinuation. Complete blood counts, including platelets, should be monitored for at least 2 weeks after stopping romiplostim. Additionally, the risk of bleeding is especially increased in patients that are on concurrent anticoagulant therapy or antiplatelet therapy. If worsening thrombocytopenia occurs, consider alternative treatments for the platelets that are in accordance with current practice guidelines.
 
Use romiplostim with caution in patients with a neoplastic disease of the blood. Romiplostim stimulation of the TPO receptor on the surface of hematopoietic cells may increase the risk of hematologic malignancies. In a single-arm clinical study of 44 patients with myelodysplastic syndrome, 11 patients developed possible disease progression, 4 of which had confirmation of conversion to acute myelogenous leukemia. Romiplostim should not be used for the treatment of thrombocytopenia secondary to any cause other than chronic ITP.
 
Romiplostim is classified as FDA pregnancy risk category C and should be used during pregnancy only when the benefits of treatment outweigh the risks. No adequate and well-controlled studies in pregnant women exist. The possibility of pregnancy and contraceptive considerations should be discussed with the patient. A pregnancy registry has been established to collect information about the effects of romiplostim use during pregnancy. Prescribers or patients are encouraged to enroll in the registry by calling 1—877—675—2831.
 
It is not known if romiplostim is excreted into breast milk, however human IgG is excreted into human milk. Many drugs are excreted in human milk and because of the potential for serious adverse reactions in nursing infants from romiplostim, a decision should be made whether to discontinue nursing or to discontinue romiplostim, taking into account the importance of romiplostim to the mother and the known benefits of breast-feeding.
 
The safety and efficacy of romiplostim have not been established in children.
 
Patients with a known hypersensitivity to romiplostim should not receive this medication. In addition, injectable solutions of romiplostim in vials contain mannitol. Patients with mannitol hypersensitivity should not use romiplostim.

Adverse Reactions
  Headline
  Adverse Reactions
  General Information
  Discontinuation of romiplostim may result in thrombocytopenia of greater severity than was present before initiation of therapy. Patients may be at an increased risk of bleeding if this occurs. Concomitant use of antiplatelet agents or anticoagulants may increase the risk of bleeding further. In clinical studies, 4 out of 57 patients on romiplostim developed a more severe thrombocytopenia than was present prior to therapy after drug discontinuation. Thrombocytopenia typically resolved within 14 days of onset, however alternative treatments for worsening thrombocytopenia may need to be considered. Complete blood counts, including platelets, should be monitored for 2 weeks after discontinuation of romiplostim.
 
Romiplostim increases the risk for reticulin fiber deposition in the bone marrow (see Mechanism of Action); whether reticulin fiber deposition leads to bone marrow fibrosis has not been established. In clinical studies, 10 out of 271 patients exhibited bone marrow reticulin deposition. Each patient received romiplostim doses >= 5 mcg/kg weekly and 6 patients received romiplostim doses >= 10 mcg/kg weekly. Progression to bone marrow fibrosis with cytopenias was not observed in the clinical studies. In the extension study, 1 patient with ITP and hemolytic anemia developed bone marrow fibrosis. Monitor CBC and the peripheral blood smear at baseline, weekly during dose titration, and monthly after achievement of a stable dose. If a patient develops a new or worsening morphological abnormality or cytopenia, discontinue romiplostim and consider bone marrow biopsy including staining for fibrosis.
 
Follow romiplostim dose guidelines to achieve and maintain platelet counts >=50,000/mm3; romiplostim should not be used in an attempt to normalize platelet counts. Excessive doses of romiplostim (e.g., to increase platelet counts to > 50,000/mm3, to normalize platelet counts, because of medication errors) may result in thrombosis or thromboembolism. In clinical studies, the incidence of thrombosis and thromboembolic complications was similar between patients given romiplostim and patients given placebo.
 
Romiplostim may increase the risk of hematologic malignancy. In a clinical study of 44 patients with myelodysplastic syndrome (MDS), 11 patients had possible disease progression, 4 of which developed confirmed acute myelogenous leukemia (AML). In controlled studies of patients with chronic idiopathic thrombocytopenic purpura (ITP), incidence of malignancy was similar between romiplostim and placebo. Romiplostim should not be used for the treatment of thrombocytopenia secondary to MDS or any reason other than chronic ITP.
 
Antibody formation may occur as a result of romiplostim treatment. Lack of response or failure to maintain a response to romiplostim should prompt a search for causative factors. Neutralizing antibodies to romiplostim and/or thrombopoietin (TPO) may develop during treatment with romiplostim. If this is suspected, submit blood samples to Amgen (1—800—772—6436) for assay. In clinical studies, 1 patient out of 225 developed antibodies to romiplostim with neutralizing activity. If platelet counts do not rise to a level sufficient to avoid clinically important bleeding after 4 weeks of therapy at the highest weekly dose of 10 mcg/kg, discontinue romiplostim.
 
Adverse effects that occurred commonly (>= 5%) in two placebo-controlled studies of romiplostim include headache (35%), arthralgia (26%), dizziness (17%), insomnia (16%), myalgia (14%), pain in extremity (13%), abdominal pain (11%), shoulder pain (8%), dyspepsia (7%), and paresthesias (6%).

    new primary malignancy
    General Information
    Progression from myelodysplastic syndrome (MDS) to acute myelogenous leukemia (AML) has been observed in adult clinical trials with romiplostim. Romiplostim may increase the risk of new primary malignancy as romiplostim stimulates the TPO receptor on the surface of hematopoietic cells. A randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial (n = 250) of patients with severe thrombocytopenia and International Prognostic Scoring System (IPSS) low or intermediate-1 risk MDS was terminated early because of more cases of AML seen in the romiplostim treatment arm. During the 58-week study period, 14 patients (10 in the romiplostim group; 4 in placebo) progressed to AML (HR 1.2, 95% CI 0.38 to 3.84). Of the 250 patients, 210 entered a long-term follow-up study. With 5 years of follow-up, 29 patients (20 in the romiplostim group; 9 in placebo) progressed to AML (HR 1.06, 95% CI 0.48 to 2.33). The incidence of death (overall survival) was 55.7% in the romiplostim arm compared to 54.2% in the placebo arm (HR 1.03, 95% CI 0.72 to 1.47). There was a higher incidence of death in the romiplostim arm in the baseline low IPSS group (41.3% vs. 30.4%; HR 1.59, 95% CI 0.67 to 3.8). In a single-arm study of 72 patients with MDS, 8 patients developed possible disease progression, 3 of which had confirmation of AML. In addition, 3 patients experienced a decrease to baseline in peripheral blood blast cell counts after discontinuation of therapy.

Description
  Text
  Romiplostim is a parenteral thrombopoietin receptor agonist indicated for the treatment of immune thrombocytopenia (ITP) in patients who have had insufficient response to corticosteroids, immunoglobulins, or splenectomy and to increase survival in patients with hematopoietic syndrome of acute radiation syndrome (ARS). Romiplostim is produced by recombinant DNA technology in E. coli and increases platelet production by binding and activating the thrombopoietin (TPO) receptor, similar to endogenous TPO. As compared to recombinant human TPO (rhTPO), romiplostim shares no sequence homology to endogenous TPO. Patients receiving rhTPO have developed antibodies causing severe thrombocytopenia. The unique peptide sequence of romiplostim significantly limits, but does not completely remove, the risk of antibody formation. In clinical studies of non-splenectomized and splenectomized patients with chronic ITP, romiplostim produced response rates of 79% to 88% and increased platelet counts in a dose-dependent fashion. Romiplostim should only be used in patients with ITP whose degree of thrombocytopenia and clinical condition increase the risk of bleeding. It should not be used in an attempt to normalize platelet counts. Additionally, romiplostim is not indicated for the treatment of thrombocytopenia due to myelodysplastic syndrome (MDS), due to the potential for MDS progression during treatment. Efficacy studies could not be conducted in humans with ARS for ethical and feasibility reasons; hence, approval for this indication was based on efficacy studies in animals, romiplostim's effect on platelet count in healthy human volunteers, and on efficacy data supporting its effect on thrombocytopenia in patients with ITP. In patients with ITP, monitor CBC weekly during the dose adjustment phase, and then monthly once a stable dose has been achieved. Also, because platelets can drop and increase the risk of bleeding, monitor CBC weekly for at least 2 weeks after treatment discontinuation.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Romiplostim is a thrombopoietin-mimetic Fc-peptide fusion protein (peptibody) produced by recombinant DNA technology in Escherichia coli. Romiplostim shares no amino acid sequence homology with endogenous thrombopoietin (TPO) and therefore is not expected to elicit cross-reacting antibodies that cause thrombocytopenia, a problem that has occurred with previously studied recombinant TPO agents. The peptibody molecule contains 2 identical single-chain subunits, each consisting of human immunoglobulin IgG1 Fc domain (constant region), covalently linked at the C-terminus to 2 identical peptide sequences that each bind and activate the TPO receptor. The Fc component of the romiplostim peptibody extends the half-life, allowing it to remain active in the circulation much longer than endogenous TPO; it is eventually removed by the reticuloendothelial system.
 
In vitro, romiplostim binds to the TPO receptor (also known as cMpl), which consists of 2 cytokine receptor modules (CRMs). Romiplostim binds to the distal CRM, allowing the cMpl receptor to become active. As a result of this, romiplostim induces phosphorylation of JAK2 and STAT5 in TPO-dependent cell lines, promotes growth of TPO-dependent cell lines and megakaryocyte-colony forming cells, and increases megakaryocyte ploidy and maturation. Increased megakaryocyte production may lead to bone marrow reticulin deposition as transforming growth-factor beta (TGF-beta) is released from megakaryocytes. TGF-beta has previously been shown to increase reticulin fiber formation. Elimination of romiplostim is platelet-dependent; increased platelet counts will cause decreased serum concentrations of romiplostim.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Subcutaneous Route
    Description
    Peak serum concentrations are observed approximately 7 to 50 hours (median: 14 hours) after administration. Romiplostim displays dose-dependent increases in platelets. After a single dose of 1 to 10 mcg/kg in patients with chronic immune thrombocytopenic purpura (ITP), platelet counts were 1.3 to 14.9 times higher than baseline over a 2 to 3 week period. Patients with higher platelet counts exhibit lower serum concentrations of romiplostim, and patients with lower platelet counts exhibit higher serum concentrations of romiplostim. Serum concentrations of romiplostim do not correlate with doses administered.

Reconstitute the 250 mcg vial with 0.72 mL of Sterile Water for Injection. Dilute with 2.25 mL of 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection.
Reconstitute the 500 mcg vial with 1.2 mL of Sterile Water for Injection. Dilute with 3.75 mL of 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection.
Gently swirl and invert the vial. Do not shake. Dissolution should occur within 2 minutes.
Storage: Protect from light. Product reconstituted with Sterile Water for Injection without further dilution can remain in the original vial at room temperature or refrigerated at 2 to 8 degrees C (36 to 46 degrees F) for up to 24 hours, or held in a syringe at room temperature for up to 4 hours. Reconstituted product further diluted with Sodium Chloride Injection can be held in a syringe at room temperature or in the original vial refrigerated for no longer than 4 hours prior to administration.
 
Subcutaneous Injection
The vial contains overfill which should not be included as part of the injection volume.
Administer using a syringe with 0.01 mL graduations. Overdoses have been reported due to medication errors; verify the syringe volume reflects the correct dosage.
Do not pool unused portions from vials or administer more than 1 dose from the vial.
Inject romiplostim subcutaneously.
