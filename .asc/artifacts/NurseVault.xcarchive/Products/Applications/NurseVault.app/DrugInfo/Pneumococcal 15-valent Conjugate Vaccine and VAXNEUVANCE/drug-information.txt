Auto-generated on 2026-09-25 from the prescribing information in this file; full details follow below.

Drug Class: Pneumococcal 15-valent conjugate vaccine, PVC15 is a pneumococcal vaccine indicated for active immunization for the prevention of invasive disease caused by Streptococcus pneumoniae serotypes 1, 3, 4, 5, 6A, 6B, 7F, 9V, 14, 18C, 19A, 19F, 22F, 23F, and 33F in individuals 6 weeks and older.

Routes: intramuscular; Common adult doses: 0.5 mL/dose IM.

Common Side Effects (excluding routine GI upset): drowsiness, fatigue, fever, headache, irritability, urticaria

In an Overdose: No overdose/antidote information is listed in this file.

Key Nursing Considerations: Contraindications/precautions: This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components. PCV15 is contraindicated in individuals who have had a hypersensitivity reaction to any diphtheria toxoid-containing vaccine.

  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  Contraindications And Precautions
    Headline
    Drug Interactions
    General Information
    The coadministration of certain medications may lead to harm and require avoidance or therapy modification; review all drug interactions prior to concomitant use of other medications.
    Headline
    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components. PCV15 is contraindicated in individuals who have had a hypersensitivity reaction to any diphtheria toxoid-containing vaccine. As with any biologic product, the care team should have procedures in place to manage allergic reactions. The care team should have immediate availability of epinephrine (1 mg/mL) injection and other agents used in the treatment of severe anaphylaxis in the event of a serious allergic reaction to the pneumococcal vaccines.
    Headline
    Treatment-Related Restrictions of Use
    General Information
    Deferral of the pneumococcal 15-valent conjugate vaccine, PCV15 administration may be appropriate for a patient with a moderate or severe acute illness with or without fever. The Advisory Committee on Immunization Practices recommends that vaccinations be delayed during the course of a moderate or severe acute febrile illness and administered after the acute phase of illness has resolved. All vaccines can be administered to persons with minor illnesses such as diarrhea, mild upper-respiratory infection with or without low-grade fever, or other low-grade febrile illness. Vaccinate persons with moderate or severe febrile illness as soon as they have recovered from the acute phase of the illness.
    Headline
    General Information
    Headline
    immunosuppression
    General Information
    Individuals with significant immunosuppression may not have an adequate antibody response to pneumococcal vaccines. Immunosuppressed persons may include patients with human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) infection; severe combined immunodeficiency (SCID); hypogammaglobulinemia; agammaglobulinemia; altered immune states due to generalized neoplastic disease; or an immune system compromised by radiation therapy or drug therapy (e.g., chemotherapy or corticosteroid therapy with greater than physiologic doses). Short-term (less than 2 weeks) corticosteroid therapy or intra-articular, bursal, or tendon injections with corticosteroids should not be immunosuppressive. Additional doses of pneumococcal vaccines, outside the recommended childhood and adult vaccine, may be needed to provide sufficient coverage against pneumococcal disease. If elective splenectomy or immunosuppressive therapy is planned, complete necessary pneumococcal immunization at least 2 weeks before surgery or therapy initiation.
    Headline
    premature neonates
    General Information
    Apnea following intramuscular vaccination has been reported in some premature neonates. A decision about when to administer pneumococcal 15-valent conjugate vaccine, PCV15 should be based on consideration of the infant's medical status and the potential risks and benefits of vaccination.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    There are no adequate and well-controlled studies of pneumococcal 15-valent conjugate vaccine, PCV15 in pregnant individuals. Available data on the administration of pneumococcal 15-valent conjugate vaccine, PCV15 to pregnant individuals is insufficient to inform vaccine-associated risks in pregnancy. According to the Advisory Committee on Immunization Practices (ACIP), administration of inactivated vaccines during pregnancy has not resulted in adverse effects in the fetus. The ACIP recommends vaccination during pregnancy when the likelihood of disease exposure is high, potential infection would cause harm to the pregnant individual or fetus, and when the vaccine is unlikely to cause harm. Pneumococcal 15-valent conjugate vaccine, PCV15 given to female rats (2 times prior to mating, once during gestation, and once during lactation) showed no evidence of harm to the fetus. No adverse effect on pup weight up to post-natal day 21 was noted.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Pneumococcal vaccine is compatible with breast-feeding. In general, inactivated vaccines are not expected to pose a risk to breast-feeding individuals or their child. Breast-feeding does not appear to adversely affect immunization, and limited data suggest it may enhance the immune response to certain vaccine antigens.

Acetaminophen: (Moderate) Concomitant administration of antipyretics, such as acetaminophen, may decrease an individual's immunological response to the pneumococcal vaccine. A post-marketing study conducted in Poland using a non-US vaccination schedule (2, 3, 4, and 12 months of age) evaluated the impact of prophylactic oral acetaminophen on antibody responses to Prevnar 13. Data show that acetaminophen, given at the time of vaccination and then dosed at 6 to 8 hour intervals for 3 doses on a scheduled basis, reduced the antibody response to some serotypes after the third dose of Prevnar 13 when compared to the antibody responses of infants who only received antipyretics 'as needed' for treatment. However, reduced antibody responses were not observed after the fourth dose of Prevnar 13 with prophylactic acetaminophen.

Data show that acetaminophen, given at the time of vaccination and then dosed at 6 to 8 hour intervals for 3 doses on a scheduled basis, reduced the antibody response to some serotypes after the third dose of Prevnar 13 when compared to the antibody responses of infants who only received antipyretics 'as needed' for treatment. However, reduced antibody responses were not observed after the fourth dose of Prevnar 13 with prophylactic acetaminophen.

Description
  Text
  Pneumococcal 15-valent conjugate vaccine, PVC15 is a pneumococcal vaccine indicated for active immunization for the prevention of invasive disease caused by Streptococcus pneumoniae serotypes 1, 3, 4, 5, 6A, 6B, 7F, 9V, 14, 18C, 19A, 19F, 22F, 23F, and 33F in individuals 6 weeks and older. Pneumococcal vaccines provide immunity against pneumococcal disease, which may manifest as, but is not limited to, otitis media, pneumonia, bacteremia, or meningitis. The vaccines are designed to protect against the most frequent bacterial serotypes associated with pneumococcal infection. Pneumococcal 15-valent conjugate vaccine PCV15 is coupled to another antigen (diphtheria CRM197) to induce a more effective T-cell response. For guidance on determining which pneumococcal vaccine an individual needs and when, please refer to the PneumoRecs VaxAdvisor app or downloaded at https://www.cdc.gov/pneumococcal/hcp/vaccine-recommendations/app.html. PCV15 was FDA-approved in 2021.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Pneumococcal 15-valent conjugate vaccine, PCV15 contains 15 pneumococcal serotypes (1, 3, 4, 5, 6A, 6B, 7F, 9V, 14, 18C, 19A, 19F, 22F, 23F, and 33F). The polysaccharides of the pneumococcal serotypes in the vaccine are bound to a non-toxic diphtheria protein known as CRM197. By coupling the polysaccharides to this carrier protein, a T-cell dependent immune response is generated. The conjugated polysaccharide (antigen) can then be presented by major histocompatibility complex molecules, which signals the activation of T-helper cells. The T-helper cells are then able to stimulate B-cells to mature into antibody-secreting plasma or memory cells. The exposure to the vaccine produces pneumococcal capsule-specific antibodies that make the organism more vulnerable to phagocytosis and other host-defenses. Protection against invasive disease is conferred mainly by antibodies (Immunoglobulin G [IgG] directed against capsular polysaccharides) and opsonophagocytic activity (OPA) against S. pneumoniae. PCV15 induces IgG antibodies and OPA against the serotypes contained in the vaccine.

Premature Neonates younger than 37 weeks
In studies of 142 premature neonates (younger than 37 weeks gestation) who received a 4-dose series, serotype-specific IgG and opsonophagocytic activity (OPA) responses at 30 days postdose 3, predose 4, and at 30 days postdose 4 were similar to those observed in term infants.

    Adults with HIV Infection
In a double-blind study in adults with HIV infection and CD4 counts 50 cells/mm3 or higher and serum HIV RNA titer less than 50,000 copies/mL, patients received a single dose of pneumococcal 15-valent conjugate vaccine, PCV15 (n = 152) or Prevnar 13 (n = 150), followed by administration of Pneumovax 23 two months later. Serotype-specific OPA GMTs were higher after vaccination compared to pre-vaccination for all vaccine serotypes included in the pneumococcal 15-valent conjugate vaccine, PCV15. After sequential administration with Pneumovax 23, OPA GMTs observed at 30 days after Pneumovax 23 vaccination were numerically similar between the 2 vaccination groups for all 15 serotypes contained in the pneumococcal 15-valent conjugate vaccine, PCV15. The safety profile of the pneumococcal 15-valent conjugate vaccine, PCV15 was similar to the safety profile of Prevnar 13.
 
Pediatric patients with HIV Infection
In a double-blind study in patients 6 to 17 years with HIV infection and CD4 counts 200 cells/mm3 or higher and serum HIV RNA titer less than 50,000 copies/mL, patients received a single dose of pneumococcal 15-valent conjugate vaccine, PCV15 (n = 203) or Prevnar 13 (n = 204), followed by administration of Pneumovax 23 two months later. Serotype-specific IgG geometric mean concentrations (GMCs) and OPA GMTs were higher after vaccination compared to pre-vaccination for all vaccine serotypes included in the pneumococcal 15-valent conjugate vaccine, PCV15. Serotype-specific IgG GMCs and OPA GMTs were numerically similar for the 13 shared serotypes and higher for the 2 unique serotypes (22F and 33F) at 30 days after vaccination with pneumococcal 15-valent conjugate vaccine, PCV15 or Prevnar 13 and were numerically similar for all 15 serotypes contained in the pneumococcal 15-valent conjugate vaccine, PCV15 at 30 days after subsequent vaccination with Pneumovax 23. The safety profile of the pneumococcal 15-valent conjugate vaccine, PCV15 was similar to the safety profile of Prevnar 13.
 
Pediatric patients with Sickle Cell Disease
In a double-blind study in patients 5 to 17 years with sickle cell disease comparing a single dose of pneumococcal 15-valent conjugate vaccine, PCV15 (n = 70) to Prevnar 13 (n = 34), serotype-specific IgG GMCs and OPA GMTs were higher after vaccination compared to pre-vaccination for all vaccine serotypes included in the pneumococcal 15-valent conjugate vaccine, PCV15. IgG GMCs and OPA GMTs were numerically similar between the 2 vaccination groups for the 13 shared serotypes and higher in the pneumococcal 15-valent conjugate vaccine, PCV15 for serotypes 22F and 33F. The safety profile of the pneumococcal 15-valent conjugate vaccine, PCV15 was similar to the safety profile of Prevnar 13.
  Text
  Pneumococcal 15-valent conjugate vaccine, PCV15 is administered intramuscularly. The distribution, metabolism, and excretion of the vaccines have not been well-defined. The immunologic response and duration of immunity conferred by a pneumococcal vaccine is dependent upon the serotypes present in the vaccine and whether the polysaccharides are coupled/conjugated to various carrier proteins. In some individuals, immune response to the vaccine may not be sufficient to prevent pneumococcal infection.

      Preparation
Use vaccine as supplied; reconstitution is not necessary.
Before use and prior to attaching the needle and expelling air from the syringe, hold the prefilled syringe horizontally and shake vigorously to obtain an opalescent suspension. Multiple attempts may be required to achieve complete resuspension. Do not use the vaccine if it cannot be resuspended. Do not use if particulate matter is present after vigorous shaking or discoloration is observed.
Intramuscular Injection
Attach a sterile needle to the prefilled syringe.
Adults: Inject IM into the deltoid muscle or into the anterolateral thigh muscle.
Pediatric patients: Inject IM into the anterolateral aspect of the mid-thigh (for infants younger than 1 year) or the deltoid muscle of the upper arm (usually suitable for older children).
