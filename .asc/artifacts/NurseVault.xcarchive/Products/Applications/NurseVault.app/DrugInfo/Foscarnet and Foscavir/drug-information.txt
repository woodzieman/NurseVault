Drug Class: Foscarnet is an intravenous antiviral agent.

Routes: injection (IV/IM/SC); Common adult doses: 180 mg/kg/day IV.

Common Side Effects (excluding routine GI upset): fanconi syndrome, glomerulonephritis, hematuria, nephrogenic diabetes insipidus, nephrotic syndrome, nephrotoxicity, nocturia, proteinuria, renal failure (unspecified), renal tubular acidosis (rta), renal tubular necrosis, agitation, amnesia, anxiety, aphasia, ataxia, confusion, depression, dizziness, drowsiness, hallucinations, headache, hypoesthesia, insomnia, meningitis, muscle cramps, paresthesias, peripheral neuropathy, seizures, tremor

In an Overdose: Renal function impairment has been reported following overdosage with foscarnet. Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  General Information
  Foscarnet is classified as pregnancy category C. Studies in humans have not been done; however, animal studies have shown a slight increase in skeletal anomalies after administration of foscarnet. The safety of foscarnet in pregnancy has not been established, so it should be used only when clearly needed.
 
The manufacturer states that it is unknown if foscarnet is distributed into breast milk. Animal studies have shown that foscarnet distributes into breast milk at concentrations that are three times greater than maternal plasma concentrations. Breast-feeding should be avoided during foscarnet therapy until further clarification of the risk has been made. Consider the benefits of breast-feeding, the risk of potential infant drug exposure, and the risk of an untreated or inadequately treated condition. If a breast-feeding infant experiences an adverse effect related to a drug administered to the mother, healthcare providers are encouraged to report the adverse effect to the FDA.
 
Foscarnet is only indicated for use in immunocompromised patients with CMV retinitis and mucocutaneous acyclovir-resistant HSV infections.
 
Anemia has been observed in 33% of patients receiving foscarnet. This adverse reaction has required discontinuation of treatment in < 1% of patients. Anemic patients receiving foscarnet should be monitored for decreases in hemoglobin, which could exacerbate the symptoms of anemia. Foscarnet is only indicated for use in immunocompromised patients with CMV retinitis and mucocutaneous acyclovir-resistant HSV infections.
 
The major toxicity associated with foscarnet therapy is renal impairment. Patients should be monitored frequently for renal toxicity, with dose adjustment for changes in renal function. Dehydration increases the risk for renal toxicity; maintaining adequate hydration is very important during foscarnet therapy. Approximately 33% of patients developed significant renal impairment during clinical trials with foscarnet. Patients differ in sensitivity to foscarnet-induced renal dysfunction. Patients with renal disease should be closely monitored for renal toxicity; however, there appears to be no association between initial creatinine clearance or drug clearance and renal impairment with foscarnet. Nevertheless, following specific dosage guidelines for renal impairment is imperative (see Dosage). Baseline renal impairment has been associated with seizures occurring during treatment with foscarnet. Foscarnet is only indicated for use in immunocompromised patients with CMV retinitis and mucocutaneous acyclovir-resistant HSV infections.
 
Electrolyte imbalances have occurred during therapy with foscarnet. The effect on calcium may be related to the ability of foscarnet to chelate divalent metal ions. Hypocalcemia can be minimized by a slow rate of infusion. Changes in electrolyte balance increase the risk of developing seizures and cardiac dysfunction. Patients who have cardiac disease, who are receiving other drugs that influence serum electrolytes, or who have a preexisting seizure disorder or other neurological disease require close monitoring during treatment. Factors other than electrolyte imbalance that have precipitated seizures in patients receiving foscarnet include a low baseline absolute neutrophil count and baseline renal impairment. Foscarnet is only indicated for use in immunocompromised patients with CMV retinitis and mucocutaneous acyclovir-resistant HSV infections.

    Drug Interactions
    General Information
    The coadministration of certain medications may lead to harm and require avoidance or therapy modification; review all drug interactions prior to concomitant use of other medications.
    Headline
    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components.
    Headline
    dehydration, renal failure, renal impairment
    General Information
    Use foscarnet with caution in patients with preexisting renal impairment or renal failure (SCr greater than 2.8 mg/dL or measured 24-hour CrCl less than 50 mL/minute), as reduced drug clearance in these patients will result in elevated foscarnet plasma concentrations. In addition, nephrotoxicity is the major toxicity associated with foscarnet therapy, with a majority of drug recipients experiencing a decline in renal function during therapy. In most cases, foscarnet-induced nephrotoxicity is identified during the second week of induction therapy, but may occur at anytime during treatment. Ensure the patient receives adequate hydration prior to and during treatment, as dehydration may increase the risk for nephrotoxicity. Monitor renal function at baseline, 2- to 3-times per week during induction therapy, and once weekly during maintenance therapy. Some patients may require more frequent monitoring. Adjust the foscarnet dose based on the patient's renal function, and discontinue therapy if the CrCl falls below the limits of the dosing nomograms (0.4 mL/minute/kg).
    Headline
    bradycardia, cardiomyopathy, congenital long QT syndrome, coronary artery disease, females, geriatric, hypocalcemia, hypokalemia, hypomagnesemia, QT prolongation
    General Information
    Avoid use of foscarnet in people with baseline QT prolongation or who have conditions that may increase the risk of QT prolongation or torsade de pointes, including bradycardia, congenital long QT syndrome, hypocalcemia, hypokalemia, hypomagnesemia, geriatric adults, females, structural abnormalities that interfere with electrical conduction (e.g., cardiomyopathy, coronary artery disease, ischemic heart disease), or in those who have other additional risk factors for QT prolongation or torsade de pointes. The use of other medications that have been associated with QT prolongation or torsade de pointes may further increase risk. Obtain electrocardiograms (ECGs) and electrolyte concentrations before and periodically during treatment.
    Headline
    cardiac disease, electrolyte imbalance, neurological disease, serious adverse effects
    General Information
    Foscarnet has been associated with serious adverse effects including nephrotoxicity and seizures related to drug-induced changes in plasma minerals and electrolytes (hypocalcemia, hypophosphatemia, hyperphosphatemia, hypomagnesemia, hypokalemia). Additionally, foscarnet may cause dose- and rate-related decreases in ionized serum calcium without altering total serum calcium concentrations. Instruct patients to immediately report any symptoms associated with low ionized calcium (perioral tingling, extremity numbness, paresthesias), as slowing the infusion rate may decrease or prevent these symptoms. Particular caution and careful management of serum electrolytes is advised in patients with preexisting calcium or other electrolyte imbalance, especially in those with neurological disease, cardiac disease, and those receiving concurrent medications known to alter electrolytes or minerals. Frequently monitor drug recipients for changes in serum electrolytes and renal function. Ensure the foscarnet dose is adjusted for changes in renal function, the patient is adequately hydrated, and consider the use of mineral and electrolyte supplementation if needed. Ensure policies and procedures for managing abnormalities such as tetany, seizures, and cardiac disturbances are in place prior to administering foscarnet.
    Headline
    activities requiring coordination and concentration, driving or operating machinery
    General Information
    Instruct patients to avoid activities requiring coordination and concentration, including driving or operating machinery, until the effects of foscarnet on the individual patient are known. In some patients, foscarnet has been associated with the development of seizures, dizziness, and somnolence.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    There are no adequate and well-controlled studies of foscarnet use during human pregnancy. In animal studies, exposure to foscarnet was associated with a slight increase in skeletal anomalies in rats and rabbits given doses approximately one-eighth and one-third, respectively, the estimated maximum daily human exposure, based on AUC. Hence, these studies may not be adequate to define potential human teratogenicity. Use foscarnet with caution in pregnant individuals.
    Headline
    sodium restriction
    General Information
    Avoid the use of foscarnet in patients on sodium restriction using a controlled sodium diet. Additionally, avoid use when intravenous infusions of large amounts of sodium or water may not be tolerated (e.g., patients with cardiomyopathy). The sodium content of foscarnet is 240 micromole/mL or 5.5 mg/mL.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Advise patients to avoid breast-feeding while taking foscarnet. There are no data on the presence of foscarnet in human milk, its effects on the breastfed child, or its effects on milk production. Animal studies have shown that foscarnet distributes into the milk at concentrations that are 3 times higher than maternal plasma concentrations, suggesting a milk to plasma ratio of 3. Foscarnet is a potent and potentially dangerous drug that could cause renal toxicity, seizures, and deposition in bone and teeth in the exposed child.

Pregnancy And Lactation
  Pregnancy
    General Information
    There are no adequate and well-controlled studies of foscarnet use during human pregnancy. In animal studies, exposure to foscarnet was associated with a slight increase in skeletal anomalies in rats and rabbits given doses approximately one-eighth and one-third, respectively, the estimated maximum daily human exposure, based on AUC. Hence, these studies may not be adequate to define potential human teratogenicity. Use foscarnet with caution in pregnant individuals.

Adverse Reactions
  Headline
  Adverse Reactions
  General Information
  NOTE: Due to the complex nature of the disease for which foscarnet is intended, it is difficult to differentiate drug-induced adverse reactions from manifestations of the underlying disease process.Approximately 33% of patients developed significant renal impairment during clinical trials with foscarnet. Impaired renal status was characterized by an increase in serum creatinine >= 2.0 mg/dl. Both renal tubular necrosis and azotemia have been reported. Patients differ in degree of foscarnet-induced renal dysfunction (i.e., change from baseline creatinine clearance). Patients with pre-existing renal disease should be closely monitored for renal toxicity; however, there appears to be no association between initial creatinine clearance or drug clearance and renal impairment for foscarnet. Nevertheless, following specific dosage guidelines for renal impairment is imperative (see Dosage). Baseline renal impairment has been associated with seizures occurring during treatment with foscarnet. Toxic nephropathy and uremia are rare (<1%). Description
  Text
  Foscarnet is an intravenous antiviral agent. It is an organic analog of inorganic pyrophosphate and is structurally unrelated to other antiviral agents. Foscarnet is indicated for the treatment of cytomegalovirus (CMV) retinitis in persons living with HIV and acyclovir-resistant mucocutaneous herpes simplex virus (HSV) infections in immunocompromised patients. It is also used off-label for other types of CMV infection (gastrointestinal disease, CMV pneumonitis, neurological disease), herpes zoster infections, human herpesvirus 6 (HHV-6) infections, and progressive outer retinal necrosis (PORN) due to varicella-zoster virus in persons living with HIV. Unlike ganciclovir, foscarnet is not associated with major myelosuppressive toxicity and does not require phosphorylation before it becomes activated. Renal impairment is the major toxicity of foscarnet. Frequent monitoring of serum creatinine, with dose adjustment for changes in renal function, and adequate hydration with administration of foscarnet is necessary.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Foscarnet selectively inhibits the viral-specific DNA polymerases and reverse transcriptases at the pyrophosphate-binding site. This occurs through prevention of pyrophosphate cleavage from deoxynucleoside triphosphate and elongation of the viral DNA chain. Cellular DNA polymerases are not affected. Unlike acyclovir or ganciclovir, intracellular phosphorylation by thymidine kinase or other kinases is not a prerequisite for foscarnet activity, which makes it effective in vitro against herpes simplex strains that are deficient in thymidine kinase.
 
In vitro, foscarnet inhibits replication of the herpes simplex virus (HSV), varicella-zoster, Epstein-Barr virus, human herpesvirus 6, and cytomegalovirus. Foscarnet also inhibits HIV reverse transcriptase and hepatitis B DNA polymerase. Cytomegalovirus (CMV) isolates show variable sensitivity to foscarnet. Clinically, although statistically significant decreases in blood and urine CMV titers have occurred after treatment with foscarnet, a correlation has not been found between a reduction in positive blood cultures and clinical efficacy of the drug in patients with CMV infection. The virustatic actions of foscarnet are reversible following discontinuation of treatment.
 
Consider the possibility of viral resistance in those patients with poor clinical response or persistent viral excretion during therapy. Although in vivo data on the development of clinical resistance to foscarnet are limited and likely result from multiple resistant pathways, CMV and HSV isolated observed in cell cultures generated resistance through amino acid substitutions in the viral DNA polymerase. Some of these amino acid substitutions may confer cross-resistance with ganciclovir, acyclovir, and/or cidofovir.

    Description
    The plasma half-life of foscarnet was increased to 13 and 25.3 hours in patients with a creatinine clearance of 25 to 49 mL/minute and 10 to 24 mL/minute, respectively. Total foscarnet systemic clearance decreased to 0.46 and 0.43 mL/minute/kg, respectively.
  Text
  Foscarnet is administered by intravenous infusion. The mean apparent volume of distribution of foscarnet at steady-state was estimated to be between 0.41 and 0.74 L/kg. In vitro studies have shown that 14% to 17% of foscarnet is protein bound at plasma drug concentrations of 1 to 1,000 micromolar. Foscarnet accumulates in bone and is distributed into cartilage. Uptake of foscarnet into the bone matrix is likely due to its structural similarity to phosphate. Penetration into the cerebrospinal fluid also has been observed with concentrations that are 30% to 50% of plasma levels (mean CSF: plasma ratio of 0.66 to 0.69). CSF penetration is increased with meningeal inflammation. Foscarnet is not metabolized in the liver. Foscarnet exhibits triphasic kinetics. In patients with normal renal function, the distribution and elimination half-lives are 0.4 to 1.4 and 3.3 to 6.8 hours, respectively. The terminal half-life is approximately 88 hours. Approximately 90% of a dose is eliminated unchanged in the urine through glomerular filtration and tubular secretion. Tubular reabsorption also can occur.
 
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes and drug transporters: none

      Dilution
No dilution is necessary if administered via a central vein.
For peripheral vein infusion, dilute the commercially available solution with 5% Dextrose Injection or 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection to a concentration of 12 mg/mL or less prior to administration to minimize the risk of local irritation. Dilute under aseptic conditions.
Administer within 24 hours of first entry into a sealed bottle.
 
Intravenous Infusion
Administer by slow IV infusion using a controlled infusion device. Infuse all doses over at least 1 hour at a rate not exceeding 1 mg/kg/minute (i.e., 60 mg/kg/dose over 1 hour or 120 mg/kg/dose over 2 hours).
To avoid infusion-related reactions, ensure administration into veins with adequate blood flow to permit rapid dilution and distribution. Failure to do so may result in local irritation. Male and female drug recipients have experienced genital irritation/ulceration (including ulcerations of the penile epithelium) possibly related to the presence of the drug in the urine. Ensure adequate hydration and closely monitor personal hygiene.

      Intravitreal injection†
NOTE: Foscarnet is not approved by the FDA for administration via intravitreal injection.
In clinical studies, intravitreal injection was prepared and administered as follows:
Remove required dose from the commercially available vial (i.e., 24 mg/mL solution).
Pass the solution through a 0.22 micron filter prior to injection.
Clean conjunctiva with topical 5% povidone iodine solution.
Apply topical 4% cocaine HCl as an anesthetic.
Slowly injection into the lower temporal quadrant using a tuberculin syringe and 30 gauge needle.
Remove needle and apply a cotton-tippled applicator to the injection point to avoid reflux.

    Injectable Administration
    Description
    Foscarnet is administered by slow intravenous infusion. Do not administer by rapid IV infusion or by direct IV injection.
Monitor renal function and adjust doses accordingly.
Adequate hydration is recommended both prior to and during treatment to minimize renal toxicity.
It is recommended that 750 to 1,000 mL of 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection or 5% Dextrose Injection be given prior to the first infusion to establish diuresis.
With subsequent infusions, 750 to 1,000 mL of hydration should be given with 90 to 120 mg/kg/dose of foscarnet and 500 mL with 40 to 60 mg/kg/dose of foscarnet. Equivalent oral rehydration may be considered for certain patients.
After the first dose, administer the hydration fluid concurrently with each drug infusion.
When selecting hydration fluids, consider the sodium content of foscarnet (240 micromole/mL; 5.5 mg/mL), as administration of large quantities of sodium may not be tolerated by certain patients (e.g., those on sodium restriction using a controlled sodium diet).
Visually inspect parenteral products for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration whenever solution and container permit.

Dosage Adjustment Guidelines
  Hepatic Impairment
  Specific guidelines for dosage adjustments in hepatic impairment are not available; it appears that no dosage adjustments are needed.
  Renal Impairment
  Dosage for induction and maintenance therapy should be modified based on creatinine clearance (mL/minute/kg).
 
For HSV induction dosage of 40 mg/kg/dose IV every 12 hours
