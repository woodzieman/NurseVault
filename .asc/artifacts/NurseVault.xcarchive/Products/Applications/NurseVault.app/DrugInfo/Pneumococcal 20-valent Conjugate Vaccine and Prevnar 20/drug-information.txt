Drug Class: Pneumococcal 20-valent conjugate vaccine, PCV20 is a pneumococcal vaccine that provides active immunization for the prevention of invasive disease caused by Streptococcus pneumoniae serotypes 1, 3, 4, 5, 6A, 6B, 7F, 8, 9V, 10A, 11A, 12F, 14, 15B, 18C, 19A, 19F, 22F, 23F, and 33F in individuals 6...

Routes: intramuscular; Common adult doses: 0.5 mL/dose IM.

Common Side Effects (excluding routine GI upset): anaphylactic shock, anaphylactoid reactions, bronchospasm, dyspnea, edema, erythema multiforme, lymphadenopathy, rash, urticaria, drowsiness, fatigue, fever, headache, irritability, apnea, cyanosis, hypotonia, pallor

In an Overdose: No overdose/antidote information is listed in this file.

Key Nursing Considerations: Contraindications/precautions: This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components. The pneumococcal 20-valent conjugate vaccine, PCV20 is contraindicated in patients who have had a hypersensitivity reaction to any diphtheria toxoid-containing vaccine.

Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  Contraindications And Precautions
    Headline
    Drug Interactions
    General Information
    The coadministration of certain medications may lead to harm and require avoidance or therapy modification; review all drug interactions prior to concomitant use of other medications.
    Headline
    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components. The pneumococcal 20-valent conjugate vaccine, PCV20 is contraindicated in patients who have had a hypersensitivity reaction to any diphtheria toxoid-containing vaccine. As with any biologic product, the care team should have procedures in place to manage allergic reactions. The care team should have immediate availability of epinephrine (1 mg/mL) injection and other agents used in the treatment of severe anaphylaxis in the event of a serious allergic reaction to the pneumococcal vaccines.
    Headline
    Treatment-Related Restrictions of Use
    General Information
    Deferral of the pneumococcal 20-valent conjugate vaccine, PCV20 administration may be appropriate for a patient with a moderate or severe acute illness with or without fever. The Advisory Committee on Immunization Practices recommends that vaccinations be delayed during the course of a moderate or severe acute febrile illness and administered after the acute phase of illness has resolved. All vaccines can be administered to persons with minor illnesses such as diarrhea, mild upper-respiratory infection with or without low-grade fever, or other low-grade febrile illness. Vaccinate persons with moderate or severe febrile illness as soon as they have recovered from the acute phase of the illness.
    Headline
    immunosuppression
    General Information
    Individuals with significant immunosuppression may not have an adequate antibody response to pneumococcal vaccines. Immunosuppressed persons may include patients with human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) infection; severe combined immunodeficiency (SCID); hypogammaglobulinemia; agammaglobulinemia; altered immune states due to generalized neoplastic disease; or an immune system compromised by radiation therapy or drug therapy (e.g., chemotherapy or corticosteroid therapy with greater than physiologic doses). Short-term (less than 2 weeks) corticosteroid therapy or intra-articular, bursal, or tendon injections with corticosteroids should not be immunosuppressive. Additional doses of pneumococcal vaccines, outside the recommended childhood and adult vaccine, may be needed to provide sufficient coverage against pneumococcal disease. If elective splenectomy or immunosuppressive therapy is planned, complete necessary pneumococcal immunization at least 2 weeks before surgery or therapy initiation.
    Headline
    premature neonates
    General Information
    Apnea after intramuscular vaccination has been reported in some premature neonates. A decision about when to administer pneumococcal 20-valent conjugate vaccine, PCV20 should be based on consideration of the infant's medical status and the potential risks and benefits of vaccination.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    There are no adequate and well-controlled studies of pneumococcal 20-valent conjugate vaccine, PCV20 in pregnant individuals. Available data on the administration of pneumococcal 20-valent conjugate vaccine, PCV20 to pregnant individuals is insufficient to inform vaccine-associated risks in pregnancy. According to the Advisory Committee on Immunization Practices (ACIP), administration of inactivated vaccines during pregnancy has not resulted in adverse effects in the fetus. The ACIP recommends vaccination during pregnancy when the likelihood of disease exposure is high, potential infection would cause harm to the pregnant individual or fetus, and when the vaccine is unlikely to cause harm. In studies where PCV20 was administered to female rabbits (twice prior to mating and twice during gestation), no adverse effects on pre-weaning development were observed. There were no vaccine-related fetal malformations or variations.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Pneumococcal vaccine is compatible with breast-feeding. In general, inactivated vaccines are not expected to pose a risk to breast-feeding individuals or their child. Breast-feeding does not appear to adversely affect immunization, and limited data suggest it may enhance the immune response to certain vaccine antigens.

Acetaminophen: (Moderate) Concomitant administration of antipyretics, such as acetaminophen, may decrease an individual's immunological response to the pneumococcal vaccine. A post-marketing study conducted in Poland using a non-US vaccination schedule (2, 3, 4, and 12 months of age) evaluated the impact of prophylactic oral acetaminophen on antibody responses to Prevnar 13. Data show that acetaminophen, given at the time of vaccination and then dosed at 6 to 8 hour intervals for 3 doses on a scheduled basis, reduced the antibody response to some serotypes after the third dose of Prevnar 13 when compared to the antibody responses of infants who only received antipyretics 'as needed' for treatment. However, reduced antibody responses were not observed after the fourth dose of Prevnar 13 with prophylactic acetaminophen.

Data show that acetaminophen, given at the time of vaccination and then dosed at 6 to 8 hour intervals for 3 doses on a scheduled basis, reduced the antibody response to some serotypes after the third dose of Prevnar 13 when compared to the antibody responses of infants who only received antipyretics 'as needed' for treatment. However, reduced antibody responses were not observed after the fourth dose of Prevnar 13 with prophylactic acetaminophen.

    Headline
    General Information
    General Information
    Adverse drug reactions from pneumococcal 7-valent conjugate vaccine, PCV7 and pneumococcal 13-valent conjugate vaccine, PCV13 clinical trials are reported, as pneumococcal 20-valent conjugate vaccine, PCV20 is manufactured and formulated similarly to PCV7 and PCV13 and contains the corresponding polysaccharide conjugates included in those vaccines.
    Headline
    injection site reaction
    General Information
    Injection site reactions, such as injection site pain (50% to 81%), injection site swelling (4% to 12%), and injection site redness (5% to 9%) were reported in adult patients receiving pneumococcal 20-valent conjugate vaccine, PCV20 during clinical trials. Injection site reactions, such as injection site pain (39% to 83%), injection site redness (23% to 26%), and injection site swelling (15% to 17%) were also reported in pediatric patients (age: 2 months to 17 years) receiving PCV20.

    Headline
    anaphylactic shock, anaphylactoid reactions, bronchospasm, dyspnea, edema, erythema multiforme, lymphadenopathy, rash, urticaria
    General Information
    Rash (more than 1%), hypersensitivity reactions [face edema, dyspnea, and bronchospasm (less than 1%)], and urticaria (less than 1%) were reported in pediatric patients (age: 6 weeks through 15 months) who received pneumococcal 7-valent conjugate vaccine, PCV7 or pneumoccal 13-valent conjugate vaccine, PCV13 during clinical trials. Lymphadenopathy localized to the region of the injection site, vaccination-site dermatitis, vaccination-site pruritus, vaccination-site urticaria, anaphylactoid reactions (including anaphylactic shock), angioneurotic edema, and erythema multiforme have been reported during postmarketing experience with PCV13. Pneumococcal 20-valent conjugate vaccine contains 7 and 13 of the same polysaccharide conjugates as PCV7 and PCV13.

Description
  Text
  Pneumococcal 20-valent conjugate vaccine, PCV20 is a pneumococcal vaccine that provides active immunization for the prevention of invasive disease caused by Streptococcus pneumoniae serotypes 1, 3, 4, 5, 6A, 6B, 7F, 8, 9V, 10A, 11A, 12F, 14, 15B, 18C, 19A, 19F, 22F, 23F, and 33F in individuals 6 weeks of age or older. It is also indicated for active immunization for prevention of otitis media caused by S. pneumoniae serotypes 4, 6B, 9V, 14, 18C, 19F, and 23F in individuals 6 weeks through 5 years of age. It provides active immunization for the prevention of pneumonia caused by S. pneumoniae serotype 1, 3, 4, 5, 6A, 6B, 7F, 8, 9V, 10A, 11A, 12F, 14, 15B, 18C, 19A, 19F, 22F, 23F, and 33F in individuals 18 years of age and older. Pneumococcal vaccines provide immunity against pneumococcal disease, which may manifest as, but is not limited to, otitis media, pneumonia, bacteremia, or meningitis. PCV20 is coupled to another antigen (diphtheria CRM197) to induce a more effective T-cell response. For guidance on determining which pneumococcal vaccine an individual needs and when, please refer to the PneumoRecs VaxAdvisor app or downloaded at https://www.cdc.gov/pneumococcal/hcp/vaccine-recommendations/app.html. PCV20 was FDA-approved in 2021.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Pneumococcal 20-valent conjugate vaccine, PCV20 contains 20 polysaccharides from S. pneumoniae serotypes (1, 3, 4, 5, 6A, 6B, 7F, 8, 9V, 10A, 11A, 12F, 14, 15B, 18C, 19A, 19F, 22F, 23F, and 33F). The polysaccharides of the pneumococcal serotypes in the vaccine are bound to a non-toxic diphtheria protein known as CRM197. By coupling the polysaccharides to this carrier protein, a T-cell dependent immune response is generated. The conjugated polysaccharide (antigen) can then be presented by major histocompatibility complex molecules, which signals the activation of T-helper cells. The T-helper cells are then able to stimulate B-cells to mature into antibody-secreting plasma or memory cells. The exposure to the vaccine produces pneumococcal capsule-specific antibodies that make the organism more vulnerable to phagocytosis and other host-defenses. Protection against invasive disease is conferred mainly by antibodies (Immunoglobulin G [IgG] directed against capsular polysaccharides) and opsonophagocytic activity (OPA) against S. pneumoniae. Pneumococcal 20-valent conjugate vaccine, PCV20 induces IgG antibodies and OPA against the serotypes contained in the vaccine.

    Description

  Text
  Pneumococcal 20-valent conjugate vaccine, PCV20 is administered intramuscularly. The distribution, metabolism, and excretion of the vaccine have not been well-defined. The immunologic response and duration of immunity conferred by a pneumococcal vaccine is dependent upon the serotypes present in the vaccine and whether the polysaccharides are coupled/conjugated to various carrier proteins. Patients who received Pneumovax 23 within 1 to 5 years prior to PCV20 receipt experienced diminished immune responses to PCV20 compared to patients who received PCV13 at least 6 months prior and compared to patients who received PCV13 followed by PPSV23, with the last dose of PPSV23 given at least 1 year prior to PCV20. In some individuals, immune response to the vaccine may not be sufficient to prevent pneumococcal infection.
