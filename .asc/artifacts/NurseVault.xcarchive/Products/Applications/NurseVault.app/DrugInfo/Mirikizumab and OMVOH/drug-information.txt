Drug Class: Mirikizumab is a humanized monoclonal antibody that selectively binds the p19 subunit of interleukin-23 and inhibits IL-23-mediated inflammatory signaling.

Routes: injection (IV/IM/SC); Common adult doses: Ulcerative colitis: 300 mg/dose IV infusion for induction; 200 mg/dose subcutaneously every 4 weeks for maintenance.

In an Overdose: No overdose/antidote information is listed in this file.

Use mirikizumab with caution in people with hamster protein hypersensitivity as the medication is produced in Chinese Hamster Ovary (CHO) cells using recombinant DNA technology.

    General Information

    Headline
    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components. Use mirikizumab with caution in people with hamster protein hypersensitivity as the medication is produced in Chinese Hamster Ovary (CHO) cells using recombinant DNA technology.
    Headline
    General Information
    General Information
    Avoid vaccination of mirikizumab recipients with live virus vaccines because of the possibility of secondary transmission of infection. No data are available on the ability of live or inactive vaccines to elicit an immune response in individuals being treated with mirikizumab. Complete all age appropriate immunizations as recommended by current immunization guidelines before initiating mirikizumab therapy.
    Headline
    infection
    General Information
    Mirikizumab may increase the risk of developing an infection or reactivating a latent infection. Do not start mirikizumab in an individual who has any clinically important active infection until the infection resolves or is adequately treated. Consider the risks and benefits before prescribing the drug to individuals with chronic infections, a history of recurrent infections, underlying conditions that may predispose them to infection, or who live in certain infection endemic areas; these individuals may not be appropriate candidates for mirikizumab therapy. Instruct individuals to seek medical attention if signs or symptoms of an infection occur. If an individual develops a serious infection or is not responding to standard therapy for an infection, discontinue mirikizumab until the infection resolves and monitor closely. Evaluate all individuals for tuberculosis (TB) infection before initiating mirikizumab therapy; the drug must not be administered to people with active TB infection. For individuals with a history of latent or active TB in whom an adequate course of treatment cannot be confirmed, consider initiating treatment against the TB infection before administering mirikizumab. Carefully consider the benefits and risks of mirikizumab before drug initiation. Monitor mirikizumab recipients closely for signs and symptoms of active TB infection during and after treatment.
    Headline
    hepatic cirrhosis
    General Information
    In people with evidence of hepatic cirrhosis, consider treatment options other than mirikizumab. Evaluate liver enzymes and bilirubin at baseline for mirikizumab recipients before initiation and for at least 24 weeks of treatment. Monitor thereafter according to routine individual management. A case of drug-induced liver injury has been reported. If liver enzyme elevations occur, a prompt investigation of the cause is recommended to identify potential cases of drug-induced liver injury. Interrupt treatment if drug-induced liver injury is suspected, until this diagnosis is excluded. Instruct mirikizumab recipients to seek immediate medical attention if they experience symptoms suggestive of hepatic dysfunction.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Available data from mirikizumab use in pregnancy are insufficient to evaluate a drug associated risk of major birth defects, miscarriage, or other adverse maternal or fetal outcomes. Although there are no data on mirikizumab, transport of endogenous IgG antibodies across the placenta increases as pregnancy progresses, and peaks during the third trimester. An enhanced pre- and post-natal development study conducted in pregnant monkeys at a dose 20-times the maximum recommended human dose (MRHD) revealed no adverse developmental effects to the developing fetus, or harm to infant monkeys from birth through 6 months of age. The clinical significance of these findings for humans is unknown. There is a pregnancy exposure registry that monitors outcomes in people who become pregnant while receiving mirikizumab; patients are encouraged to enroll by calling 1-800-545-5979.
    Headline
    neonates and infants exposed to this medication in utero
    General Information
    Mirikizumab may cause immunosuppression in neonates and infants exposed to this medication in utero. Monitoring for the development of serious infection is recommended during the first 2 months after birth for infants exposed to mirikizumab in utero. There are no data regarding infant serum levels of mirikizumab at birth and the duration of persistence of mirikizumab in infant serum after birth. Although a specific time-frame to delay live virus immunizations in infants exposed in utero is unknown, a minimum of 2 months after birth should be considered because of the half-life of-mirikizumab.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Use mirikizumab with caution during breast-feeding. There are no data on the presence of mirikizumab in human milk, the effects on the breastfed infant, or the effects on milk production. However, maternal immunoglobulin G (IgG) is known to be present in human milk. The effects of local gastrointestinal (GI) exposure and limited systemic exposure in the breastfed infant to mirikizumab are unknown. However, because mirikizumab is a large protein molecule, the amount in milk is likely to be very low. It is also likely to be partially destroyed in the infant's GI tract and absorption by the infant is probably minimal. Waiting for at least 2 weeks postpartum to resume mirikizumab therapy may minimize transfer to the infant. The Global Consensus Group for Pregnancy and Inflammatory Bowel Disease considers mirikizumab use to be acceptable during breast-feeding. Consider the benefits of breast-feeding along with the maternal clinical need for mirikizumab.

Interactions
Cholera Vaccine: (Moderate) Patients receiving immunosuppressant medications may have a diminished response to the live cholera vaccine. When feasible, administer indicated vaccines prior to initiating immunosuppressant medications. Counsel patients receiving immunosuppressant medications about the possibility of a diminished vaccine response and to continue to follow precautions to avoid exposure to cholera bacteria after receiving the vaccine.
Dengue Tetravalent Vaccine, Live: (Contraindicated) Avoid administration of the live dengue virus vaccine with immunosuppressive drug therapy and prior to immune recovery following treatment with immunosuppressive drug therapy. When feasible, administer indicated live virus vaccines at least 4 weeks before planned immunosuppression or wait until at least 3 months after discontinuation. The time to restoration of immune competence may be longer in some patients. Patients with altered immunocompetence may be at increased risk for severe adverse reactions due to uninhibited growth of the attenuated live virus. Additionally, vaccines may be less effective if administered during a period of altered immunocompetence.
SARS-CoV-2 (COVID-19) vaccines: (Moderate) Patients receiving immunosuppressant medications may have a diminished response to the SARS-CoV-2 virus vaccine. When feasible, administer indicated vaccines prior to initiating immunosuppressant medications. Counsel patients receiving immunosuppressant medications about the possibility of a diminished vaccine response and to continue to follow precautions to avoid exposure to SARS-CoV-2 virus after receiving the vaccine.

    anaphylactoid reactions, erythema, infusion-related reactions, pruritus, rash
    General Information
    Serious hypersensitivity reactions and infusion-related reactions, including anaphylaxis (anaphylactoid reactions) have been reported during intravenous infusion of mirikizumab. Infusion-related hypersensitivity reactions, including mucocutaneous erythema and pruritus, were reported during IV infusion induction therapy for ulcerative colitis. Infusion-related hypersensitivity reactions (0.4%) and rash (4%) were reported by individuals treated with mirikizumab during clinical trials. If a severe hypersensitivity reaction occurs, discontinue mirikizumab immediately and initiate appropriate treatment.

    infection, pharyngitis, rhinitis, sepsis, sinusitis
    General Information
    Mirikizumab may increase the risk of infection. Upper respiratory tract infections (e.g., COVID-19, nasopharyngitis, pharyngitis, rhinitis, sinusitis, and upper respiratory tract infection) were reported in 8% to 14% of individuals with ulcerative colitis and 28% of individuals with Crohn's disease receiving mirikizumab. Herpes viral infection (e.g., herpes zoster, herpes simplex, and oral herpes) was reported in 2% of individuals with ulcerative colitis receiving mirikizumab compared to 1% of individuals receiving placebo. In the induction study for individuals with ulcerative colitis, infections were reported by 15% of individuals treated with mirikizumab and 14% of individuals treated with placebo. Serious infections were reported by less than 1% in both groups. Serious infections in the mirikizumab group included intestinal sepsis, listeria sepsis, and pneumonia. In the maintenance study for individuals with ulcerative colitis, infections were reported by 24% of individuals treated with mirikizumab and 23% of individuals treated with placebo. A case of COVID-19 pneumonia was reported as a serious infection in the mirikizumab group. In the Crohn's disease study, 39% of individuals reported an infection through week 52 compared to 35% of individuals receiving placebo. Serious infections in the mirikizumab group included abscess, cellulitis, pneumonia, and sepsis. Instruct individuals to seek medical advice if signs or symptoms of clinically important acute or chronic infection occur. If a serious infection develops or an infection is not responding to standard therapy, monitor the patient closely and do not administer mirikizumab until the infection resolves.

    The development of immunogenicity (anti-drug antibody formation) may occur with the use of mirikizumab. Description
  Text
  Mirikizumab is a humanized monoclonal antibody that selectively binds the p19 subunit of interleukin-23 and inhibits IL-23-mediated inflammatory signaling. Unlike ustekinumab, it does not inhibit IL-12. Mirikizumab is indicated for moderately to severely active ulcerative colitis (UC) and Crohn disease (CD) in adults, for induction of remission and, in individuals who respond, maintenance of remission. In the phase 3 CD trial, 45.4% of mirikizumab-treated adults achieved clinical remission at week 52 compared with 19.6% receiving placebo; endoscopic response and corticosteroid-free remission also favored mirikizumab. In CD studies, benefit was demonstrated in patients with and without prior biologic-treatment failure. Adult CD guidelines recommend mirikizumab for induction and maintenance of remission in moderately to severely active CD. The American Gastroenterological Association (AGA) categorizes it as a higher-efficacy option in adults who have not previously received advanced therapy and as an intermediate-efficacy option after previous advanced-therapy exposure. In adult UC trials, approximately 65% of mirikizumab recipients achieved clinical response and 24.2% achieved clinical UC remission at week 12. Among induction responders who continued into maintenance, 49.9% achieved clinical remission at week 52, compared with 25.1% receiving placebo. Adult UC guidelines recognize mirikizumab as an effective advanced therapy for moderately to severely active disease. The American College of Gastroenterology recommends mirikizumab for induction and, in responders, maintenance of UC remission, while the AGA categorizes it as an intermediate-efficacy option in both advanced-therapy-naive and advanced-therapy-experienced adults. Mirikizumab may increase susceptibility to infection or worsen an existing infection. Individuals should be evaluated for tuberculosis (TB) infection before initiating treatment. Mirikizumab was initially FDA-approved in 2023.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Mirikizumab-mrkz is a humanized IgG4 monoclonal antibody that selectively binds to the p19 subunit of human IL-23 cytokine and inhibits its interaction with the IL-23 receptor. IL-23 is involved in mucosal inflammation and affects the differentiation, expansion, and survival of T cell subsets, and innate immune cell subsets, which represent sources of pro-inflammatory cytokines. Research in animal models has shown that pharmacologic inhibition of IL-23p19 can ameliorate intestinal inflammation. Mirikizumab-mrkz inhibits the release of pro-inflammatory cytokines and chemokines.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Intravenous Route
    Description
    Mirikizumab exhibited linear pharmacokinetics with a dose-proportional increase in exposure over a dose range of 60 mg to 2,400 mg given as an intravenous injection in healthy volunteers. The geometric mean (CV%) Cmax, AUCtau, and Ctrough at steady state are 99.7 mcg/mL (22.7%), 538 mcg x day/mL (34.4%), and 2.75 mcg/mL (101%), respectively after a 300 mg induction infusion of mirikizumab in individuals with ulcerative colitis. The geometric mean (CV%) Cmax, AUCtau, and Ctrough at steady state are 332 mcg/mL (21%), 1820 mcg x day/mL (38%), and 10.4 mcg/mL (108%), respectively after a 900 mg induction infusion of mirikizumab in individuals with Crohn's disease.
    Name
    Subcutaneous Route
    Description
    Mirikizumab exhibited linear pharmacokinetics with a dose-proportional increase in exposure over a dose range of 200 mg to 400 mg given as a subcutaneous injection in healthy volunteers. There was no apparent accumulation of mirikizumab concentrations in serum over time when administered as a subcutaneous injection every 4 weeks to subjects with ulcerative colitis or Crohn's disease. Following administration to individuals with ulcerative colitis, median (range) Tmax was 5 (3.08 to 6.75) days post-dose and the geometric mean (CV%) absolute bioavailability was 44% (34%). Following administration to individuals with Crohn's disease, median (range) Tmax was 5 (3 to 6.83) days post-dose and the geometric mean (CV%) absolute bioavailability was 36.3% (31%). The geometric mean (CV%) Cmax, AUCtau, and Ctrough at steady state in individuals with ulcerative colitis were 10.1 mcg/mL (52.1%), 160 mcg x day/mL (57.6%), and 1.70 mcg/mL (83.3%), respectively after a 200 mg subcutaneous injection of mirikizumab at week 12 and every 4 weeks thereafter for up to an additional 40 weeks. The geometric mean (CV%) Cmax, AUCtau, and Ctrough at steady state in individuals with Crohn's disease were 13.6 mcg/mL (48%), 220 mcg x day/mL (56%), and 2.52 mcg/mL (88%), respectively after a 300 mg subcutaneous injection of mirikizumab at week 12 and every 4 weeks thereafter for up to week 52. Injection site location (abdomen, upper arm, or thigh) did not significantly influence bioavailability of mirikizumab following subcutaneous injection.

  Text
  Mirikizumab is administered subcutaneously and intravenously. A positive relationship was observed between mirikizumab average concentration and rates of clinical remission and clinical response in individuals with ulcerative colitis in clinical studies. Mirikizumab exhibited linear pharmacokinetics with dose-proportional increase in exposure. The geometric mean (CV%) total volume of distribution in subjects with ulcerative colitis was 4.83 L (21%) and 4.4 L (14%) in individuals with Crohn's disease. Mirikizumab is expected to be degraded into small peptides and amino acids via catabolic pathways in the same manner as endogenous IgG. Geometric mean (CV%) clearance in individuals with ulcerative colitis was 0.0229 L/hours (34%) and the geometric mean (CV%) elimination half-life was 9.3 days (40%). Geometric mean (CV%) clearance in individuals with Crohn's disease was 0.0202 L/hours (38%) and the geometric mean (CV%) elimination half-life was 9.3 days (26%). Clearance is independent of dose.

  Route Specific Administrations
    Administration Details
      Name
      Intravenous Administration
      Description
      For Ulcerative colitis (Intravenous induction regimen):
Preparation:
Only administer intravenously by infusion; the IV infusion must be administered by a qualified health care professional using aseptic technique.
Each vial contains mirikizumab 300 mg/15 mL and is for single use only.
Dilute prior to IV infusion administration. Using an 18 to 21 gauge needle, withdraw 15 mL of mirikizumab solution from the vial and inject the medication into an intravenous infusion bag containing 50 mL, 100 mL, or 250 mL of 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection or 5% Dextrose Injection. Do not mix with other medications. Do not dilute with solutions other than 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection or 5% Dextrose Injection.
Gently invert the infusion bag to mix the contents. Do not shake the prepared infusion bag.
Storage of Prepared Infusion: Start the infusion immediately after preparation. If not used immediately, store the diluted infusion solution in the refrigerator between 2 and 8 degrees C (36 and 46 degrees F). Use the diluted infusion solution within 48 total hours of vial puncture, of which not more than 5 hours are permitted at non-refrigerated temperatures not to exceed 25 degrees C (77 degrees F). Protect from heat and light. Do not freeze.
 
IV infusion:
If stored refrigerated, allow the diluted solution to warm to room temperature prior to beginning the infusion.
Connect the intravenous administration set (infusion line) to the prepared infusion bag and prime the line.
Administer the prepared IV infusion over at least 30 minutes.
Do not infuse through the same intravenous line with other drugs or solutions other than 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection or 5% Dextrose Injection.
At the end of the infusion, flush the line with 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection or 5% Dextrose Injection.
Administer the flush at the same infusion rate as used for mirikizumab administration.
The time required to flush mirikizumab solution from the infusion line is in addition to the minimum 30-minute infusion time.
 
For Crohn's disease (Intravenous induction regimen):

Only administer intravenously by infusion; the IV infusion must be administered by a qualified health care professional using aseptic technique.
Each vial contains mirikizumab 300 mg/15 mL and is for single use only.

Dilute prior to IV infusion administration. Using an 18 to 21 gauge needle, withdraw 45 mL of mirikizumab solution from a total of 3 vials and inject the medication into an intravenous infusion bag containing 100 mL or 250 mL of 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection or 5% Dextrose Injection. Do not mix with other medications. Do not dilute with solutions other than 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection or 5% Dextrose Injection.
Gently invert the infusion bag to mix the contents. Do not shake the prepared infusion bag.
Storage of Prepared Infusion: Start the infusion immediately after preparation. If not used immediately, store the diluted infusion solution in the refrigerator between 2 and 8 degrees C (36 and 46 degrees F). Use the diluted infusion solution within 48 total hours of vial puncture, of which not more than 5 hours are permitted at non-refrigerated temperatures not to exceed 25 degrees C (77 degrees F). Protect from heat and light. Do not freeze.

If stored refrigerated, allow the diluted solution to warm to room temperature prior to beginning the infusion.
Connect the intravenous administration set (infusion line) to the prepared infusion bag and prime the line.

Do not infuse through the same intravenous line with other drugs or solutions other than 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection or 5% Dextrose Injection.
At the end of the infusion, flush the line with 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection or 5% Dextrose Injection.
Administer the flush at the same infusion rate as used for mirikizumab administration.
The time required to flush mirikizumab solution from the infusion line is in addition to the minimum 90-minute infusion time.
      Name
      Subcutaneous Administration
      Description
      There are 100 mg/mL and 200 mg/2 mL products. Each full maintenance dose will require 2 prefilled pens or 2 prefilled syringes given as 2 consecutive injections in any order.
Subcutaneous administration is intended for use under the guidance and supervision of a health care professional.
Patients may self-inject subcutaneous mirikizumab after training in subcutaneous injection technique. Provide proper training for patients and/or caregivers on the subcutaneous injection technique.
Refer to the manufacturer provided "Instructions for Use" for detailed instructions on preparation and administration.
The vial, prefilled pen, and prefilled syringe are not made with dry natural rubber latex.
 
Subcutaneous injection:
Before injection, remove the prefilled pen or prefilled syringe from the refrigerator and leave at room temperature for 45 minutes.
Sites for subcutaneous injection include the abdomen, thigh, and back of the upper arm. Instruct patients to inject in a different location (such as upper arms, thighs or any quadrant of abdomen) than previous injection. If using the back of upper arm as an injection site, the injection must be performed by another person.
Do not inject into areas where the skin is tender, bruised, erythematous, indurated or affected by any lesions.
The prefilled syringe and prefilled pen do not contain preservatives; therefore, discard any unused product. Do not reuse.
Missed dose: If a dose is missed, administer the dose as soon as possible and resume dosing every 4 weeks.
Storage: If needed, the prefilled pen and prefilled syringe may be stored at room temperature up to 30 degrees C (86 degrees F) for up to 2 weeks in the original carton to protect from light. Once a prefilled syringe or prefilled pen has been stored at room temperature, do not return to the refrigerator. If these conditions are exceeded, the prefilled pen(s) or prefilled syringe (s) must be discarded.

Visually inspect parenteral products for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration whenever solution and container permit. The solution should be clear to opalescent, colorless to slightly yellow to slightly brown, and free of visible particles.
Do not use if solution is cloudy or contains visible particles or if solution has been frozen.
