  General Information
  All infants should receive protection against RSV-associated lower respiratory tract infection (LRTI) with either maternal RSV vaccination or administration of nirsevimab or clesrovimab to the infant. No product is preferred; base selection on parental preference, product availability, and timing of birth relative to the RSV season.
When administered concomitantly, clesrovimab is not expected to interfere with immune response to other vaccines.

  Adverse Reactions
    Headline
    laboratory test interference
    General Information
    Clesrovimab may cause laboratory test interference with some immunologically-based RSV diagnostic assays (rapid antigen tests). Confirmation using a reverse transcriptase polymerase chain reaction (RT-PCR) assay is recommended when rapid antigen assay results are negative and clinical observations are consistent with RSV infection.
    Headline
    erythema, injection site reaction, rash
    General Information
    Injection site reactions have been reported with clesrovimab use. Injection site erythema (3.8%), injection site swelling (2.7%), and rash (2.3%) were reported in early and moderate preterm infants, late preterm infants, and full-term infants (n = 2,412) during clinical trials. Comparable findings were observed in early studies of clesrovimab 100 mg administered to preterm and full-term infants (n = 64) with injection site erythema and pain each reported in 3.1% of infants, and injection site swelling in 1.6%.

Description
  Text
  Clesrovimab is a respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) F protein-directed fusion inhibitor indicated for the prevention of RSV lower respiratory tract disease. RSV is a leading cause of lower respiratory tract infections (LRTIs), hospitalizations, and deaths in infants and young children. Hospitalization rates are highest in infants, especially those born prematurely. Children with chronic conditions like congenital heart disease, chronic lung disease of prematurity, or immunodeficiencies are also at higher risk. The extended half-life of clesrovimab allows a single dose to provide protection against RSV for the entire season. In clinical trials, clesrovimab significantly reduced the incidence of RSV-associated medically attended lower respiratory infections and hospitalization in infants born at 29 weeks or older with efficacy rates of approximately 60% and 84%, respectively, compared to placebo. The study showed clesrovimab effectively reduced RSV-related disease through 150 days after administration. Another clinical trial demonstrated that clesrovimab provides comparable protection to palivizumab in preventing RSV-associated severe disease and hospitalization in high-risk infants, including preterm infants and those with chronic lung disease or congenital heart disease, over 150 days post-dose. The most common adverse reactions were injection-site erythema, injection-site swelling, and rash. Clesrovimab was FDA-approved in June 2025.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Clesrovimab is a respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) F protein-directed fusion inhibitor. The RSV fusion (F) protein is essential for viral entry and transmission and a primary target for protective immunity. Clesrovimab is a recombinant immunoglobulin G1 kappa (IgG1k) monoclonal antibody that provides passive immunity against RSV. It contains 3 animo acid substitutions (YTE) in its Fc region that enhances binding to the neonatal Fc receptor, reducing lysosomal degradation and extending serum half-life. Clesrovimab specifically targets site IV on the RSV F protein to prevent fusion of the viral and cellular membranes and viral entry into host cells.
 
Pharmacodynamics
RSV serum neutralizing antibody (SNA) titer correlates with clesrovimab serum concentrations and increased in a dose-dependent manner by day 150. After IM administration, the RSV neutralizing antibody titers in the serum were estimated to be approximately 7 times higher than baseline at 4 hours and maximum titers were reached by day 7, for a typical infant weighing 5 kg. The duration of protection extends through 5 months.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Intramuscular Route
    Description
    The pharmacokinetics of clesrovimab are approximately dose-proportional after a single IM administration of 20 to 210 mg in infants. The AUC from day 1 to day 150 and Cmax after IM administration of 105 mg was 6,470 mcg x d/mL and 120 mcg/mL, respectively. The concentration at day 150 was 10.3 mcg/mL. The median Tmax occurred at 6.5 days. When given in the first RSV season, clesrovimab serum exposure was similar in neonates, infants, preterm neonates, infants born at 35 weeks or less, and in neonates and infants with chronic lung disease or chronic heart disease.

  Clesrovimab is administered via intramuscular injection. The Vd is 830 mL for a typical infant weighing 5 kg. Clesrovimab is metabolized into small peptides by catabolic pathways. In a typical infant weighing 5 kg, the estimated apparent clearance is 19.7 mL/day and the terminal half-life is approximately 44 days. Among infants who received clesrovimab, development of anti-drug antibodies (ADA) had no noticeable effect on pharmacokinetics.

Administration
  General Information

  Route Specific Administrations
    Administration Details
      Name
      Intramuscular Administration
      Description
      For IM administration.
Clesrovimab must be administered by a health care provider.
Remove clesrovimab prefilled syringe from the refrigerator and allow it to come to room temperature (approximately 15 minutes).
Do not shake the prefilled syringe.
Unscrew the tip cap by twisting it counter-clockwise. Do not remove the Luer Lock adapter and the finger flange extender.
Attach a sterile Luer Lock needle by twisting in a clockwise direction until the needle fits securely on the syringe. Due to the viscosity of the product, use a 22- to 25-gauge needle.
Inject the entire contents of the prefilled syringe intramuscularly into the anterolateral aspect of the thigh. Do not inject into the gluteal area or areas where there may be a major nerve trunk and/or blood vessel.
Storage: Clesrovimab may be kept at room temperature between 20 and 25 degrees C (68 and 77 degrees F) for a maximum of 48 hours. After being removed from the refrigerator, clesrovimab must be used within 48 hours or discarded. Do not refreeze.
    Name
    Injectable Administration
    Description
    Visually inspect parenteral products for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration whenever solution and container permit. Clesrovimab is a clear to slightly opalescent, colorless to slightly yellow solution. This product should not be used if particulate matter or discoloration is found.
Do not use if the pre-filled syringe has been dropped or damaged, the security seal on the carton has been broken, or the expiration date has passed.
Clesrovimab can be given on the same day as other childhood vaccines. When given with other vaccines, administer in separate syringes and at different injection sites.
Do not mix clesrovimab with any other vaccines or medications in the same syringe or vial.
There is no information available regarding the use of clesrovimab with immunoglobulin products.
