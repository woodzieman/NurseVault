    Headline
    helminth infection
    General Information
    Treat patients with a preexisting helminth infection with anti-helminth therapy before starting lebrikizumab. If a patient becomes infected while receiving lebrikizumab and fails to respond to anti-helminth treatment, discontinue lebrikizumab until the infection resolves.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Use lebrikizumab with caution during breast-feeding. There are no data on the presence of lebrikizumab in human milk, its effects on the breastfed child, or its effects on milk production. Endogenous IgG and monoclonal antibodies are transferred to human milk. The effects of local gastrointestinal exposure and limited systemic exposure in the breastfed infant to lebrikizumab are unknown. Consider the developmental and health benefits of breast-feeding along with the clinical need for lebrikizumab and any potential adverse effects on the breastfed child from the medication or the underlying maternal condition.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Available data on lebrikizumab use during pregnancy are insufficient to evaluate a drug-associated risk of major birth defects, miscarriage, or other adverse maternal or fetal outcomes. Monoclonal antibodies are actively transported across the placenta. Transport of human IgG antibodies across the placenta increases as pregnancy progresses and peaks during the third trimester; therefore, lebrikizumab may be present in infants exposed in utero. The potential clinical impact of lebrikizumab exposure in infants exposed in utero should be considered. In animal reproduction studies, no effects on embryo-fetal development were observed after subcutaneous administration of lebrikizumab to cynomolgus monkeys during organogenesis at doses up to 18 times the human exposure at the maximum recommended human dose (MRHD). Providers are encouraged to report all pregnancies to the manufacturer at 1-800-LillyRx (1-800-545-5979).

    conjunctivitis, keratitis, keratoconjunctivitis
    General Information
    Conjunctivitis was the most frequently reported eye disorder. Most cases of conjunctivitis and keratitis were mild or moderate in severity and recovered or resolved without treatment interruption or discontinuation. During the initial 16-week treatment period of the monotherapy trials, conjunctivitis, including allergic conjunctivitis, was reported by 61 subjects (10%) in the lebrikizumab 250 mg every 2 weeks group and 10 subjects (3%) in the placebo group. The safety of lebrikizumab was also evaluated across 4 randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, multicenter trials in 891 patients with moderate to severe atopic dermatitis. Co-morbid conditions included asthma, allergic rhinitis, food allergy, and allergic conjunctivitis. Atopic and vernal keratoconjunctivitis (less than 1%) were also reported during clinical trials.

Description
  Text
  Lebrikizumab is a human IgG4 monoclonal antibody that is an interleukin-13 antagonist. It is indicated for the treatment of moderate-to-severe atopic dermatitis (eczema) in adults and pediatric patients 12 years and older weighing at least 40 kg whose disease is not adequately controlled with other topical prescription therapies or when use of those therapies is not advised. Lebrikizumab is administered subcutaneously and may be used with or without topical corticosteroid therapy. It may also be used with topical calcineurin inhibitors; however, use of these topical agents should be reserved for problem areas only, such as face, neck, intertriginous and genital areas. Lebrikizumab may alter immunity and increase the risk of infection following administration of live vaccines; complete all age-appropriate vaccinations prior to initiating therapy. Lebrikizumab was FDA-approved in September 2024.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Lebrikizumab is an IgG4 human monoclonal antibody that binds with high affinity and slow off-rate to interleukin (IL)-13 and allows IL-13 to bind to IL-13 receptor alpha 1 but inhibits human IL-13 signaling through the IL-4 receptor alpha/IL-13 receptor alpha 1 receptor complex. IL-13 is a naturally occurring cytokine that is involved in Type 2 inflammation, which is an important component in the pathogenesis of atopic dermatitis. Lebrikizumab inhibits IL-13-induced responses including the release of proinflammatory cytokines, chemokines and IgE. Lebrikizumab-bound IL-13 can still bind IL-13 receptor alpha 2 allowing subsequent internalization and natural clearance of IL-13.
 
Lebrikizumab was associated with reduced the levels of serum periostin, total immunoglobulin E (IgE), thymus and activation-regulated chemokine (TARC/CCL17), pulmonary and activation-regulated chemokine (PARC/CCL18), and monocyte chemotactic protein-4 (MCP-4/CCL13). The clinical relevance of these biomarkers is not completely understood.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Subcutaneous Route
    Description
    Following a single subcutaneous 250 mg dose of lebrikizumab, peak serum concentrations were achieved approximately 7 to 8 days post dose. The absolute bioavailability for a subcutaneous dose was approximately 86%. Following a subcutaneous dose of 250 mg every 2 weeks, the Cmax of lebrikizumab was 108 mcg/mL, the average concentration was 100 mcg/mL, and the trough concentration was 87 mcg/mL. Following a subcutaneous dose of 250 mg every 4 weeks, the Cmax of lebrikizumab was 63 mcg/mL, the average concentration was 51 mcg/mL, and the trough concentration was 36 mcg/mL. Following a subcutaneous dose of 250 mg every 8 weeks, the Cmax of lebrikizumab was 43 mcg/mL, the average concentration was 26 mcg/mL, and the trough concentration was 11 mcg/mL. Lebrikizumab exposure increases dose-proportionally over a subcutaneous dose range of 37.5 to 500 mg. Steady-state concentrations were achieved at week 4 following the approved recommended loading doses. Injection site locations did not influence the absorption of lebrikizumab.

      Preparation
Remove lebrikizumab from the refrigerator.
It is not necessary to allow the prefilled pen or prefilled syringe to warm to room temperature before use.
Do not microwave the pens or syringes, run hot water over them, or leave them in direct sunlight.
Do not shake the pens or syringes.
Do not remove the needle cover until right before lebrikizumab is administered.
After removal from the refrigerator, prefilled pens or syringes may be kept at room temperature up to 30 degrees C (86 degrees F) and must be used within 7 days or discarded. Do not put pens or syringes that have reached room temperature back into the refrigerator.
The drug solution does not contain preservatives and each prefilled pen or syringe is for single-use only; therefore, discard any unused product.
 
Subcutaneous injection
Administer subcutaneously into the thigh or abdomen, except for the 2 inches (5 cm) around the navel. The back of the upper arm can also be used if a caregiver administers the injection.
Rotate the body area with each subsequent set of injections; do not use the same body area 2 times in a row. Do not inject lebrikizumab into skin that is tender, damaged, bruised, or scarred.
 
Instructions for use
Prefilled Pens
Wash your hands and clean the injection site(s) with an alcohol wipe. Allow the alcohol to dry before injecting. Do not touch or blow on the injection site after it has been cleansed.
When ready to inject, make sure the pen is locked, twist off the gray base cap and discard it. Do not put the needle cap back on after it has been removed. Do not hold the plunger rod or plunger head while removing the needle cover. Do not touch the needle or allow it to touch any surface; throw it away if this does occur.
Place and hold the clear base flat and firmly against the skin. Keep the clear base on the skin, then turn the lock ring to the unlock position.
Press and hold the purple injection button and listen for 2 loud clicks. You will hear a click to let you know that the injection has started, and the gray plunger will start to move. Hold until the gray plunger is at the bottom of the viewing window. Keep pressing until you hear the second click. The injection may take up to 15 seconds.
Lightly press a cotton ball or gauze onto the injection site if you see any blood. Do not rub the injection site. If needed, cover the injection site with a small bandage.
Prefilled pens are single-use only; the drug solution does not contain preservatives. Immediately discard used pen and any unused product in a proper sharps disposal container.
Prefilled syringe
Wash your hands and clean the injection site(s) with an alcohol wipe. Allow the alcohol to dry before injecting. Do not touch or blow on the injection site after it has been cleansed.
When ready to inject, remove the needle cap by pulling it straight off. Do not put the needle cap back on after it has been removed. Do not hold the plunger rod or plunger head while removing the needle cover. Do not touch the needle or allow it to touch any surface; throw it away if this does occur.
Gently pinch and hold a fold of cleansed skin.
Insert the needle completely into the fold of the skin at a 45-degree angle.
Push the plunger rod down slowly and steadily as far as it will go until the syringe is empty.
Lift thumb off of the plunger and the needle will automatically retract, move back into the syringe, and lock into place.
Lightly press a cotton ball or gauze onto the injection site if you see any blood. Do not rub the injection site. If needed, cover the injection site with a small bandage.
Prefilled syringes are single-use only; the drug solution does not contain preservatives. Immediately discard the syringe and any unused product in a proper sharps disposal container.
