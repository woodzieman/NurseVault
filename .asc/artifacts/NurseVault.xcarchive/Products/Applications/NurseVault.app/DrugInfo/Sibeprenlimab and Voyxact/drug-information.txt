    Headline
    infection
    General Information
    Sibeprenlimab suppresses the immune system by decreasing antibody production and may increase the risk of infection. Individuals with chronic or recurring infections may have an increased risk of developing a serious infection. Prior to initiating sibeprenlimab, assess individuals for active infections. Monitor individuals for signs and symptoms of infection during treatment. Consider interrupting sibeprenlimab treatment until the infection is controlled if a serious infection develops. There are limited clinical trial data with concomitant use of sibeprenlimab and systemic immunosuppressants. Consider the potential for increased immunosuppression when coadministering sibeprenlimab and immunosuppressants or when initiating sibeprenlimab before or after immunosuppressive therapy.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    There are no available data on the use of sibeprenlimab in pregnancy to evaluate for a drug-associated risk of major birth defects, miscarriage, or other adverse maternal or fetal outcomes. Administration of sibeprenlimab subcutaneously to pregnant monkeys did not result in any adverse effects on embryo-fetal or postnatal development at exposures approximately 10-times the clinical exposure at the maximum recommended human dose (MRHD) based on area under the curve (AUC). Immunoglobulin A (IgA) nephropathy is associated with adverse maternal outcomes (e.g., increased rates of cesarean section, pregnancy-induced hypertension, pre-eclampsia and pre-term delivery), and adverse fetal/neonatal outcomes (e.g., stillbirth and low birth weight). Monoclonal antibodies like sibeprenlimab can be actively transported across the placenta as pregnancy progresses. The transfer of endogenous immunoglobulin G (IgG) antibodies across the placenta increases as pregnancy progresses, and peaks during the third trimester. Therefore, sibeprenlimab may be present in infants exposed in utero. Consider the potential clinical impact of sibeprenlimab exposure in infants who were exposed to sibeprenlimab in utero. Report exposure of sibeprenlimab during pregnancy by contacting 1-833-869-9228 or visiting www.VOYXACT.com.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Use sibeprenlimab with caution during breast-feeding. There are no data on the presence of sibeprenlimab in human milk, the effects on the breastfed infant, or the effects on milk production. Consider the developmental and health benefits of breast-feeding along with the mother's clinical need for sibeprenlimab and any potential adverse effects on the breastfed child from sibeprenlimab or from the underlying maternal condition. Endogenous maternal immunoglobulin G (IgG) and monoclonal antibodies are transferred into human milk. The effects of local gastrointestinal exposure on sibeprenlimab in the breastfed infant are unknown.

  Adverse Reactions

    General Information
    Sibeprenlimab suppresses the immune system by reducing antibody production and may increase the risk of infection. Infection was reported in 49% of sibeprenlimab-treated subjects compared with 45% of subjects receiving placebo in clinical trials. Upper respiratory tract infection was the most common infection reported and occurred in 15% of sibeprenlimab-treated subjects compared to 14% of subjects receiving placebo in clinical trials. Most adverse reactions were reported as mild to moderate in severity and resolved without treatment interruption or discontinuation.

    antibody formation
    General Information
    In a sibeprenlimab clinical trial, 88 of 256 (34%) evaluable subjects treated with sibeprenlimab developed anti-drug antibody formation. Of the 88 subjects who tested positive for anti-drug antibodies (ADA), 21 subjects (23.9%) developed neutralizing ADA. Sibeprenlimab exposure was approximately 40% lower in subjects with immunoglobulin A nephropathy (IgAN) who developed ADA compared to subjects with undetectable ADA over the treatment period. The reduction in urine protein/creatinine ratio based on 24-hour urine collections (uPCR-24h) from baseline to Month 9 was numerically lower in subjects who developed ADA (41.6%) compared to those without ADA (52.7%). The clinical significance of uPCR reduction according to the presence or absence of ADA is not clear. The presence of ADA did not have a clinically significant effect on the incidence or severity of adverse reactions. The observed incidence of ADA is highly dependent on the sensitivity and specificity of the assay. Differences in assay methods preclude meaningful comparisons of the incidence of ADA in this study compared with the incidence of ADA in other studies.

Description
  Text
  Sibeprenlimab is an A Proliferation Inducing Ligand (APRIL) blocker, indicated to reduce proteinuria in adults with primary immunoglobulin A nephropathy (IgAN) at risk for disease progression. Sibeprenlimab is approved for this indication under accelerated approval based on reduction of proteinuria. It has not been established whether sibeprenlimab slows kidney function decline over the long-term in individuals with IgAN. Continued approval for this indication may be contingent upon verification and description of clinical benefit in a confirmatory clinical trial. Sibeprenlimab suppresses the immune system and may increase the risk of infections. Additionally, sibeprenlimab may also interfere with immune responses to vaccines and increase the risk of infection from vaccines. Live vaccines are not recommended within 30 days prior to initiation of sibeprenlimab or during treatment as safety has not been established. Sibeprenlimab was FDA-approved in November 2025.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Sibeprenlimab binds to A Proliferation Inducing Ligand (APRIL) with a dissociation constant (KD) of 0.95 pM. This binding blocks signaling at the B cell maturation antigen (BCMA), transmembrane activator calcium modulator and cyclophilin ligand interactor (TACI) receptors. Inhibition of APRIL leads to a reduction in serum concentrations of galactose-deficient immunoglobulin A1 (Gd-IgA1), a molecule that is involved in the development of immunoglobulin A nephropathy (IgAN).
 
Pharmacodynamics
After administration of a single 400 mg subcutaneous dose of sibeprenlimab to healthy subjects, serum concentrations of APRIL were decreased by more than 90% by Day 3 and the reduction was maintained until Day 42. In subjects with IgAN treated with sibeprenlimab every 4 weeks, APRIL suppression of more than 90% was observed by Week 4 and was sustained throughout treatment. Serum Gd-IgA1, immunoglobulin A (IgA), immunoglobulin G (IgG), and immunoglobulin M (IgM) concentrations decreased within 4 weeks, reached a plateau by Week 24, and by Week 48 mean serum concentrations were reduced from baseline by 67% for Gd-IgA1, 69% for IgA, 35% for IgG, and 75% for IgM.

  Text
  Sibeprenlimab is administered subcutaneously. The volume of distribution of sibeprenlimab is 4 L. As an immunoglobulin 2 (IgG2) monoclonal antibody, sibeprenlimab is expected to be degraded by proteolytic enzymes via catabolic pathways in the same manner as endogenous immunoglobulin G (IgG). The mean apparent clearance of sibeprenlimab after 400 mg doses every 4 weeks is 206 mL/day. The mean terminal half-life is 9.3 days after a single 400 mg sibeprenlimab dose; steady-state is reached after 20 weeks of dosing.
 
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes and drug transporters: none

  Route Specific Administrations
    Administration Details
      Name
      Subcutaneous Administration
      Description
      Remove the sibeprenlimab prefilled syringe box from the refrigerator.
Allow the prefilled syringe to reach room temperature naturally 15 to 30 minutes prior to administration.
Do not warm the prefilled syringe or let the prefilled syringe sit in direct sunlight.
Hold the middle of the prefilled syringe near the viewing window to carefully lift the prefilled syringe out of the box.
Do not lift the prefilled syringe by the plunger or shake or roll the prefilled syringe.
Do not remove the gray needle cap from the prefilled syringe until it is ready to be injected.
Choose an injection site on the front of the upper thigh or stomach. Caregiver or health care provider may choose the back of the upper arm as the injection site.
Do not inject within 2 inches of the navel, into moles, scars, bruises, or skin that is tender, damaged, hard, or red, or into the same spot used for the last injection.
Clean the injection site with an alcohol swab and let the skin air dry. Do not touch, fan, or blow on the injection site after it has been cleaned.
Hold the prefilled syringe with 1 hand and pull the needle cap straight off with the other hand. It is normal to see a drop of liquid at the end of the needle.
Do not touch or recap the needle or allow it to touch any surface.
Do not touch or pull back the plunger.
Do not use a prefilled syringe with a bent or broken needle.
With the needle cap off, hold the prefilled syringe in 1 hand. With the other hand, gently pinch the skin at the injection site. It is important to pinch enough skin to inject under the skin and not into the muscle.
Insert the entire needle at a 45-degree angle into the pinched skin using a dart-like motion. Avoid touching the plunger until the needle is inserted.
Push the plunger as far as it will go until all the medication is injected. Remove the thumb off the plunger to allow the needle guard to automatically cover the exposed needle.
Gently lift the syringe away from the injection site. Do not rub or massage the injection site.
Dispose of the prefilled syringe in a sharps container. Do not throw away the prefilled syringe in the trash.

Missed dose: If a scheduled dose of sibeprenlimab is missed, administer the missed dose as soon as possible and then resume dosing every 4 weeks thereafter.
Storage: Store sibeprenlimab in the refrigerator at 36 to 46 degrees F (2 to 8 degrees C). Do not freeze. Keep the prefilled syringe in its original container to protect from light. Do not expose to heat or direct sunlight. Once the sibeprenlimab syringe has reached room temperature, do not return it to the refrigerator. Do not use sibeprenlimab if it has been at room temperature for 7 days or longer.
