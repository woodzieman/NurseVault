Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  General Information
  Botulism immune globulin, BIG-IV should not be used in patients with a prior history of severe reaction to other human immunoglobulin preparations. Similarly, use caution in patients with sucrose hypersensitivity because Botulism immune globulin, BIG-IV (BabyBIG) contains sucrose as a stabilizer. Botulism immune globulin, BIG-IV contains trace amounts of immunoglobulin A (IgA). Persons with selective IgA deficiency have the potential for developing antibodies to immunoglobulin A and could have anaphylactic reactions to the subsequent administration of blood products that contain immunoglobulin A. During administration of BIG-IV, the patient's vital signs should be monitored continuously and the patient should also be carefully observed for any associated symptoms. Epinephrine should be available for the treatment of an acute anaphylactic reaction. If hypotension or anaphylaxis occurs, the administration of BIG-IV should be immediately discontinued and supportive care given as needed.
 
Botulism immune globulin, BIG-IV should only be administered as an intravenous infusion, since other routes of administration (e.g., intramuscular administration) have not been evaluated.
 
Botulism immune globulin, BIG-IV is a derivative of human blood. As with other products derived from or purified with human blood components, the remote possibility of contamination or infection with hepatitis, Creutzfeldt-Jakob disease (CJD), and other bacterial or viral infections exists in patients receiving the BIG-IV. Screening plasma donors for prior exposure to certain viruses, testing for the presence of viruses, and inactivating and/or reducing viruses has reduced the risk of transmission of infectious agents. The manufacturing processes are designed to reduce the risk of transmitting viral infection; however, none of the processes are completely effective. There is also the possibility that unknown infectious agents may be present in this product. Botulism immune globulin should be given only if a benefit is expected. All infections thought by a physician to have been possibly transmitted by BIG-IV should be reported to the manufacturer. The health care provider should discuss the risks and benefits of this product with the legal guardian of the patient.
 
Botulism immune globulin, BIG-IV should be used cautiously in patients predisposed to acute renal failure which includes patients with any degree of pre-existing renal impairment or renal disease, diabetes mellitus, dehydration (volume depletion or hypovolemia), sepsis, paraproteinemia, or who are receiving known nephrotoxic drugs. Many licensed immune globulin intravenous (human IGIV) products have been reported to be associated with renal dysfunction, acute renal failure, osmotic nephrosis, and death. Those IGIV products that contained sucrose as a stabilizer and were administered at daily doses of 400 mg/kg or greater accounted for a disproportionate share of the total number of cases reported. Botulism immune globulin, BIG-IV (BabyBIG) contains sucrose as a stabilizer and should be administered at the minimum concentration available and at the minimum rate of infusion practicable.
 
No FDA pregnancy category rating has been officially assigned to Botulism immune globulin, BIG-IV. Animal reproduction studies have not been conducted with botulism immune globulin. Therefore, it is not known whether it can cause fetal harm when administered during pregnancy or whether it can affect reproductive capacity. It is also not known whether BIG-IV is excreted in human breast milk during breast-feeding.
 
Botulism immune globulin, BIG-IV has been tested for safety and efficacy only in infants less than one year of age. BIG-IV has not been tested for safety or efficacy in other children or in adult or elderly populations.
 
The antibodies present in Botulism immune globulin, BIG-IV may interfere with the immune response to live virus vaccination, such as polio, measles, mumps, and rubella. Immunizations should be delayed until approximately 5 months after BIG-IV administration. If such vaccinations were given shortly before or after BIG-IV, a revaccination may be necessary.

    General Information
    Administer botulism immune globulin, BIG-IV with caution to patients predisposed to acute renal failure, which includes patients with any degree of pre-existing renal impairment, diabetes mellitus, hypovolemia (dehydration or volume depletion), sepsis, monoclonal gammopathy of undetermined significance (paraproteinemia), or who are receiving known nephrotoxic drugs.     Headline
    cardiovascular disease, chylomicronemia, cryoglobulinemia, geriatric, hypertriglyceridemia, hyperviscosity, immobility, thrombophilia
    General Information
    Thrombotic events may occur after IGIV receipt. Cautious use of IGIV is warranted in patients with a history of thromboembolic or cardiovascular disease. Patients at risk for thrombotic events are geriatric patients, those with a history of atherosclerosis (coronary artery disease), multiple cardiovascular risk factors, impaired cardiac output (heart failure), thrombophilia (coagulation disorders), immobility or prolonged periods of immobilization, and known or suspected hyperviscosity. Assessment of blood viscosity may be warranted for patients at risk for hyperviscosity such as those with cryoglobulinemia, fasting chylomicronemia, hypertriglyceridemia, or monoclonal gammopathies. Rapid infusion rates may increase potential risk factors for patients who are already at risk for thrombotic events. Administer BIG-IV at the slowest practical infusion rate in patients at risk for thromboembolic events.

  General Information
  Adverse reactions associated with botulism immune globulin were assessed in both controlled and open label studies. Minor clinical events that were not recorded as adverse events in the controlled study were recorded as adverse events in the open label study. As a result, a larger number of adverse events were reported in the open label study. The only adverse reaction possibly related to botulism immune globulin in controlled trials was a mild, transient erythematous rash (unspecified) of the face or trunk. When only treatment emergent adverse events are considered, 14% of the BIG-IV-treated patients experienced erythematous rash during or after study infusion in the controlled study; 8% of placebo-treated patients also experienced erythematous rash in the controlled study. A similar rash is known to occur both in infant botulism patients who have not received any IGIV products and in patients treated with other IGIVs. Therefore, it is difficult to determine the causality of the rash.     Serious events, such as transfusion-related acute lung injury (TRALI), may occur after treatment.     hemolysis, hemolytic anemia
    General Information
    Hemolysis and hemolytic anemia have been associated with administration of IGIV products, such as botulism immune globulin, BIG-IV. IGIV-induced hemolytic anemia occurs as a result of enhanced red blood cell sequestration. Hemolysis develops when blood group antibodies present in IGIV cause a positive direct antiglobulin reaction by acting as hemolysins and inducing immunoglobulin adherence to red blood cells. Monitor patients for signs and symptoms of hemolysis. If hemolysis is suspected, appropriate confirmatory tests are recommended.

    Hyperproteinemia, increased serum viscosity, and hyponatremia may occur in patients receiving IGIV therapy, such as botulism immune globulin, BIG-IV. Distinguish true hyponatremia from a pseudohyponatremia caused by decreased calculated serum osmolality or elevated osmolar gap. Treatment aimed at decreasing serum free water in patients with pseudohyponatremia may lead to volume depletion, a further increase in serum viscosity, and a possible predisposition to thrombotic events. Assessment of blood viscosity may be warranted for patients at risk for hyperviscosity such as those with cryoglobulins, fasting chylomicronemia, hypertriglyceridemia, or monoclonal gammopathies.

Description
  Text
  Botulism immune globulin IV (BIG-IV) is an intravenous solution of purified human immunoglobulin G (IgG), with trace amounts of IgA and IgM. The product is derived from pooled adult plasma from persons immunized with pentavalent botulinum toxoid. Donors for BIG-IV are selected based on high titers of neutralizing antibody against botulinum neurotoxins type A and B. All donors for the product are tested and found negative for antibodies against HIV, HBV, and HCV. Botulism immune globulin is used to treat infant botulism caused by toxin type A or B. Clinical studies show that BIG-IV reduces the overall hospital stay for infants as well as the time spent in the ICU, on mechanical ventilation, and on tube-feedings.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  The pooled IgG present in BIG-IV provides antibodies capable of opsonization and neutralization of botulinum toxins types A and B. The titer of antibodies in the reconstituted product against type A botulinum toxin is at least 15 international units/mL and against type B toxin is at least 4 international units/mL. The titers of antibody against botulinum neurotoxins C, D, and E have not been determined. In infants exposed to botulinum toxin types A or B, administration of BIG-IV provides sufficient levels of the relevant antibodies to neutralize the expected levels of circulating neurotoxin.

    Description
    Following intravenous infusion, peak titers occur rapidly. The distribution of botulinum immune globulin (BIG) is not known. The exact metabolic fate of BIG is unknown, but the half-life of infused BIG has been shown to be approximately 28 days in infants. A single IV infusion is expected to provide a protective level (more than 0.1 milli-international units/mL) of neutralizing antibodies for 6 months.

  Route Specific Administrations
    Administration Details
      Name
      Intravenous Administration
      Description
      Reconstitution
Reconstitute lyophilized powder with 2 mL of Sterile Water for Injection to obtain a 50 mg/mL solution. A double-ended transfer needle or large syringe is suitable for adding the water for reconstitution. When using a double-ended transfer needle, insert one end first into the vial of water. Since the lyophilized powder is contained in an evacuated vial, the water should transfer by suction; aim the jet of water to the side of the vial. After the water is transferred, the residual vacuum should be released to hasten the dissolution.
Rotate the container gently to wet all the powder. Allow an approximately 30-minute interval for dissolution. Do NOT shake the vial; this will cause foaming.
Botulinum immune globulin (BIG)-IV does not contain a preservative; only enter the vial twice: once for reconstitution and once for administration. Use the reconstituted solution within 2 hours; do not store in the reconstituted state.
Do NOT predilute BIG-IV before infusion.
 
Intravenous Infusion
Begin the infusion within 2 hours of reconstitution and complete within 4 hours of reconstitution unless the infusion is temporarily interrupted by an adverse reaction. Continuously monitor vital signs during the infusion.
Use low volume tubing and a constant infusion pump (i.e., an IVAC pump or equivalent). Administration through a separate intravenous line is recommended. If a separate line is not possible, the infusion may be piggybacked into a pre-existing line if that line contains either Sodium Chloride Injection or 1 of the following dextrose solutions (with or without NaCl added): 2.5% Dextrose in water, 5% Dextrose in water, 10% Dextrose in water, or 20% Dextrose in water. If a pre-existing line must be used, the solution should not be diluted more than 1:2 with any of the solutions mentioned. Admixtures with any other solutions or drugs have not been evaluated; it is recommended that BIG-IV be administered separately from other drugs or medications. Use of an in-line or syringe-tip sterile, disposable filter (18 micrometer) is recommended for administration.
Begin the infusion slowly at 0.5 mL/kg/hour (25 mg/kg/hour). If no untoward reactions occur after 15 minutes, the rate may be increased to 1 mL/kg/hour (50 mg/kg/hour); DO NOT EXCEED 1 mL/kg/hour; follow the infusion schedule closely. At the recommended rates, infusion of the indicated dose should take 67.5 minutes total elapsed time.
Monitor the patient's vital signs continuously and observe the patient closely during and after each rate change.
If anaphylaxis or a significant drop in blood pressure occurs, discontinue the infusion and administer epinephrine.
If a patient develops a minor infusion reaction like flushing, slow the rate of infusion immediately or temporarily interrupt the infusion.
Serious events, such as transfusion-related acute lung injury (TRALI), may occur 1 to 6 hours after the infusion. If TRALI is suspected, test for the presence of anti-neutrophil antibodies in both the product and the patient's serum. Treatment includes oxygen therapy and ventilatory support.

Dosage Adjustment Guidelines
  Hepatic Impairment
  No dosage adjustment required.
  Renal Impairment
  Reduction in dose, concentration, and/or rate of administration is recommended in order to reduce the risk of acute renal failure in at-risk patients. Because no prospective data are presently available to identify a maximal safe dose, concentration, or rate of infusion for botulinum immune globulin-IV products for patients at risk of acute renal failure, it is recommended that, for such patients, prescribers reconstitute/dilute the product in such a manner as to produce both the minimum concentration and rate of infusion practicable.
