Contraindications And Precaution
Drug Interactions

The coadministration of certain medications may lead to harm and require avoidance or therapy modification; review all drug interactions prior to concomitant use of other medications.

Hypersensitivity

This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components.

geriatric, hypovolemia


Use apremilast with caution in patients at increased risk for complications from severe diarrhea, nausea, or vomiting, such as geriatric adults, those with hypovolemia, or those taking medications that can lead to volume depletion or hypotension. Severe gastrointestinal adverse reactions have been reported, sometimes requiring hospitalization. Consider dose reduction or suspension of apremilast if patients develop severe diarrhea, nausea, or vomiting.


depression, suicidal ideation


Use apremilast with caution in patients with a history of depression or suicidal ideation, thoughts or behavior. Treatment with apremilast is associated with an increased incidence of depression. Advise patients, caregivers, and families to be alert for the emergence or worsening of depression, suicidal thoughts, or other mood changes, and to contact their health care provider if such changes occur. Carefully evaluate the risks and benefits of continuing treatment if such events arise.


renal failure, renal impairment


Apremilast dosage adjustments are recommended for both adult and pediatric patients with renal failure or severe renal impairment (CrCl less than 30 mL/minute), due to increased apremilast exposure in this population. No dosage adjustment is needed for mild or moderate renal impairment.


planned procedure


Guidelines recommend patients with psoriasis to continue to receive apremilast through low-risk surgery or a planned procedure. Expert opinion suggests that apremilast may also be safely continued through intermediate and high-risk surgery on a case-by-case basis.


pregnancy


Available data on apremilast use during pregnancy have not identified a drug-associated risk of major birth defects or adverse pregnancy outcomes. A pregnancy registry assessed pregnancy outcomes among individuals with psoriatic arthritis, psoriasis, or Behcet's disease exposed to apremilast during the first trimester (n = 15). No major birth defects or miscarriages were reported among liveborn infants in the apremilast-exposed group; 1 stillbirth occurred. Interpretation is limited by the small number of exposed pregnancies. Based on findings from animal reproduction studies, apremilast may increase the risk of fetal loss. Apremilast crosses the placenta in animals. In embryo-fetal development studies in cynomolgus monkeys, administration during organogenesis resulted in dose-related increases in abortion/embryo-fetal death at exposures 2-times the maximum recommended human therapeutic dose (MRHD) and greater; no adverse effects were observed at 1.4 times the MRHD. In mice, no drug-induced malformations were observed at exposures up to 4 times the MRHD; however, dystocia, reduced offspring viability, and decreased birth weights occurred at exposures greater than or equal to 4 times the MRHD. Advise pregnant individuals of the potential risk of fetal loss.


breast-feeding


Use apremilast with caution during breast-feeding. There are no data on the presence of apremilast in human milk, its effects on the breast-fed child, or its effect on milk production. Apremilast is present in animal milk. Consider the benefits of breast-feeding, the patient's clinical need for treatment, and any potential adverse effects on the breast-fed child from the medication or from the patient's underlying medical condition.

Adverse Reaction
abdominal pain, diarrhea, dyspepsia, gastroesophageal reflux, nausea, vomiting


Gastrointestinal adverse reactions, including diarrhea (8% to 41%), nausea (7% to 22%), vomiting (1% to 9%), abdominal pain (1% to 9%), frequent bowel movements (2%), dyspepsia (3%) and gastroesophageal reflux disease, were reported in adult patients receiving apremilast during clinical trials. In most cases, these gastrointestinal adverse reactions develop within the first few weeks of treatment. Hospitalization has been required in some cases. However, symptoms generally resolve quickly after apremilast dosage reduction or discontinuation of the drug. Consider dose reduction or treatment suspension for any patient who develops severe diarrhea, nausea, or vomiting.


cough, infection, pharyngitis


Upper respiratory tract infection (1% to 12%), naso-pharyngitis (3% or less), bronchitis (1%), and cough were reported in adult patients receiving apremilast during clinical trials.


anaphylactoid reactions, angioedema, rash


Hypersensitivity reactions including rash have been reported during clinical trials with apremilast. During postmarketing use of the drug, cases of angioedema and anaphylaxis or anaphylactoid reactions have been reported. If signs or symptoms of a serious hypersensitivity reaction develops during treatment, discontinue apremilast and institute appropriate therapy.


anorexia, growth inhibition, weight loss


Weight loss can occur with apremilast therapy. Body weight decreases of 5% to 10% were reported in 5% to 12% of adult patients treated with apremilast. Weight decreases of 10% or more of body weight occurred in 2% of adult patients. Anorexia was also reported during adult clinical trials. In pediatric patients, body weight decreases of 5% to 10% were reported in 12% of patients. Weight decreases of 10% or more occurred in 1% of patients. Monitor weight regularly in all drug recipients and watch for growth inhibition (height and weight) in pediatric patients. Patients with clinically significant weight loss, including children and adolescents who are not growing or gaining weight as expected, may need to have their treatment interrupted or discontinued. Guidelines recommend considering treatment discontinuation for any individual experiencing a more than 5% weight loss from baseline.


headache, migraine


Headache, including tension headache, migraine (2%), and sinus headache, was reported in 1% to 14% of adult patients receiving apremilast during clinical trials. Headache was among the most common reactions leading to discontinuation of therapy.


depression, suicidal ideation


Depression and suicidal ideation and behavior have been reported in patients receiving apremilast therapy. During clinical trials, depression or depressed mood was reported in approximately 1% of adult patients treated with apremilast. Depression was reported as serious in up to 0.2% of patients in the apremilast group and a reason for study discontinuation in up to 0.3% of patients. Suicidal ideation and behavior were reported in up to 0.2% of adult patients in the apremilast group. In all clinical trials, 1 patient treated with apremilast attempted suicide while 3 patients in the placebo groups committed suicide. If such events occur, carefully evaluate the risks and benefits of continuing treatment.


fatigue, insomnia


Fatigue (3%) and insomnia (2%) were reported in adult patients receiving apremilast during clinical trials.


arthralgia, back pain


Back pain (2% to 8%) and arthralgia (6%) were reported in adult patients receiving apremilast during clinical trials.


folliculitis


Folliculitis (1%) and tooth abscess (1%) were reported in adult patients receiving apremilast during clinical trials.


Description

Apremilast is a phosphodiesterase-4 (PDE4) inhibitor and is considered a targeted synthetic DMARD (tsDMARD). Apremilast is indicated for active psoriatic arthritis (PsA), moderate to severe plaque psoriasis in individuals who are candidates for phototherapy or systemic therapy, and oral ulcers associated with Behcet's Disease. Apremilast has also been shown to be effective in treating psoriasis of the nails, dactylitis, and enthesitis. Psoriasis treatment guidelines recommend the use of PDE4 inhibitors as an initial treatment option for plaque psoriasis in adults, and also recommend the use of apremilast for adults with plaque psoriasis in whom other standard DMARD treatments have been unsuccessful. In large clinical trials for PsA, apremilast has been shown to reduce the clinical manifestations of PsA, but the drug is not considered as efficacious as biologic therapies. Compared to biologic agents commonly used for PsA (e.g., TNF-blockers, interleukin-targeted treatments), definitive evidence of apremilast efficacy for reducing the progression of radiographic damage is lacking. Most PsA guidelines position apremilast as an alternative option for individuals who failed to reach the treatment target on conventional DMARDs and for whom biologic treatments may be contraindicated or inappropriate. In adults with active PsA and predominant enthesitis who are naive to conventional DMARDs and biologics, apremilast may be considered as an option. Compared to traditional DMARDs for PsA, apremilast does not require routine therapeutic drug monitoring. However, the drug is expensive and thus is often considered second line in comparison to conventional DMARD therapies. Apremilast is often used in conjunction with other conventional DMARDs to try to optimize treatment, given its relatively low evidence for efficacy when used alone. Use of apremilast has been associated with cases of severe diarrhea, nausea, and vomiting; some requiring hospitalization. If these symptoms develop during treatment, consider reducing the dosage or suspending therapy. Apremilast was FDA-approved in 2014.

Mechanism Of Action

Apremilast is a phosphodiesterase-4 (PDE4) inhibitor specific for cyclic adenosine monophosphate (cAMP). Inhibition of PDE4 results in an increase in the intracellular concentration of cAMP, resulting in partial inhibition of the production of many proinflammatory mediators and an increase in the production of some anti-inflammatory mediators. The specific mechanism by which apremilast exerts its therapeutic effect is not fully elucidated.
 
Pharmacodynamics
In clinical studies, apremilast decreased interleukin-17 (IL-17), interleukin-22 (IL-22), and tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-alpha) concentrations circulating in blood and reduced expression of these proinflammatory cytokine genes in the skin. The clinical implications of these responses are unknown.

Pharmacokinetics

Apremilast is administered orally. Apremilast is 68% bound to plasma proteins and has a mean volume of distribution of 87 L. Apremilast is a major circulating component (45%) followed by inactive metabolite M12 (39%), a glucuronide conjugate of O-demethylated apremilast. It is extensively metabolized in humans with up to 23 metabolites identified in plasma, urine, and feces. Apremilast is metabolized by both cytochrome (CYP) oxidative metabolism with subsequent glucuronidation and non-CYP mediated hydrolysis. In vitro, CYP metabolism of apremilast is primarily mediated by CYP3A4, with minor contributions from CYP1A2 and CYP2A6. After oral administration of radio-labeled apremilast, about 58% and 39% of the radioactivity is recovered in urine and feces, respectively, with about 3% and 7% of the radioactive dose recovered as apremilast in urine and feces, respectively. The terminal half-life is approximately 6 to 9 hours.
 
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes and drug transporters: CYP3A4, CYP1A2, CYP2A6, P-gp
Apremilast is a substrate of CYP3A4, CYP1A2, CYP2A6, and P-gp. Apremilast exposure is decreased when the drug is co-administered with strong CYP450 inducers (e.g., rifampin) and may result in loss of efficacy. Drug interaction studies indicate no significant pharmacokinetic interaction exists with CYP3A4 substrates or with CYP3A4 and P-gp inhibitors.

Route-Specific Pharmacokinetics
Oral Route

The absolute oral bioavailability of apremilast immediate-release tablets is 73%, with peak plasma concentrations (Cmax) occurring in approximately 2.5 hours. Food does not alter the extent of absorption of immediate-release apremilast. The extended-release tablets achieve Cmax after approximately 6 hours. At steady-state, the Cmax and systemic exposure (AUC) of the extended-release 75 mg once daily tablets are comparable to the immediate-release 30 mg twice daily tablets. Administration of the extended-release tablets with a high-fat meal delays the time to Cmax by 3 hours and the Cmax and AUC are increased by approximately 28% as compared to fasting conditions.

Hepatic Impairment

Moderate (Child Pugh B) or severe (Child Pugh C) hepatic impairment does not affect the pharmacokinetics of apremilast.

Renal Impairment

The pharmacokinetics of apremilast immediate-release tablets were studied in patients with mild, moderate, and severe renal impairment defined by a creatinine clearance of 60 to 89 mL/minute, 30 to 59 mL/minute, and less than 30 mL/minute, respectively. Pharmacokinetics are not affected by mild or moderate renal impairment. In a single-dose pharmacokinetic analysis (n = 8), the AUC and Cmax of apremilast were increased by 88% and 42%, respectively, in patients with severe renal impairment. The pharmacokinetics of the extended-release tablets have not been evaluated in patients with renal impairment.

Pediatrics

A population pharmacokinetic analysis in pediatric patients (age 6 to 17 years) receiving recommended dosing based on weight showed the AUC and Cmax of apremilast was similar to that of adult patients receiving 30 mg twice daily.

Geriatric

In a single-dose pharmacokinetic analysis, the AUC and Cmax were higher in older adult subjects compared to younger subjects (13% and 6%, respectively).

Gender Differences

The extent of apremilast exposure is about 31% higher in females compared to males; however, dosage adjustments are not recommended based on gender.

Ethnic Differences

No differences were seen in the pharmacokinetics of apremilast in terms of race and ethnicity.
