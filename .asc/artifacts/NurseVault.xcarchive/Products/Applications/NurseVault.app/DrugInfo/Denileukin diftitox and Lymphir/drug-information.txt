Drug Class: Denileukin diftitox-cxdl is a fusion protein designed to direct the cytocidal action of diphtheria toxin (DT) to cells which express the IL2 receptor.

Routes: injection (IV/IM/SC); Common adult doses: 9 mcg/kg/day IV.

Common Side Effects (excluding routine GI upset): capillary leak syndrome, dehydration, edema, hypoalbuminemia, hypotension, pericardial effusion, peripheral edema, pleural effusion, blurred vision, ocular toxicity, visual impairment, elevated hepatic enzymes, hepatotoxicity, hyperbilirubinemia, asthenia, fatigue, lethargy, arthralgia, back pain, bone pain, musculoskeletal pain, myalgia, dizziness, headache, migraine, amnesia, confusion, impaired cognition, memory impairment, hyperthyroidism

      Routes

          Heading

          Dosage Description
          9 mcg/kg (actual body weight) IV once daily on days 1 through 5, every 21 days until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity; administer recommended premedications in cycles 1, 2, and 3 to reduce the risk of infusion-related reactions. In a multicenter, open-label, single-arm trial, treatment with denileukin diftitox-cxdl resulted in an objective response rate of 36% (complete response, 9%) in patients with relapsed or refractory stage I to IV CTCL (n = 69); the median time to response was 1.4 months. Patients had received a median of 4 prior therapies including photodynamic therapy, total skin electron beam therapy, systemic retinoids, methotrexate/pralatrexate, histone deacetylase inhibitors, brentuximab vedotin, and mogamulizumab. The duration of response ranged from 3 to 23.5 months, with 52% of patients experiencing a duration of at least 6 months and 20% with a duration of at least 12 months.

    Headline
    capillary leak syndrome, hypoalbuminemia
    General Information
    Do not administer denileukin diftitox in patients with serum albumin levels less than 3 g/dL (hypoalbuminemia); delay administration until levels rise above 3 g/dL. Capillary leak syndrome (CLS) has occurred in patients treated with denileukin diftitox; some cases were life-threatening or fatal and most cases occurred during the first 2 cycles. Monitor patients for signs and symptoms of CLS during treatment, including regular assessments for weight gain, new or worsening edema, dyspnea, and hypotension (including orthostatic changes). Monitor serum albumin levels prior to each cycle and more often as clinically indicated. An interruption of therapy, dose reduction, or discontinuation of therapy may be necessary based on the severity of CLS.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Pregnancy should be avoided by females of reproductive potential during denileukin diftitox treatment and for 7 days after the last dose. Although there are no available data on the use of denileukin diftitox in pregnant women or animals, denileukin diftitox can cause fetal harm or death when administered during pregnancy based on its mechanism of action. Denileukin diftitox causes depletion of regulatory T lymphocytes, immune activation, and capillary leak syndrome; this may compromise the maintenance of a pregnancy. Women who are pregnant or who become pregnant while receiving denileukin diftitox should be apprised of the potential hazard to the fetus.
    Headline
    contraception requirements, infertility, pregnancy testing, reproductive risk
    General Information
    Counsel patients about the reproductive risk and contraception requirements during denileukin diftitox treatment. Denileukin diftitox can cause fetal harm if taken by the mother during pregnancy. Females of reproductive potential should avoid pregnancy and use effective contraception during and for 7 days after treatment with denileukin diftitox. Females of reproductive potential should undergo pregnancy testing prior to initiation of denileukin diftitox. Women who become pregnant while receiving denileukin diftitox should be apprised of the potential hazard to the fetus. Although there are no data regarding the effect of denileukin diftitox on human fertility, male infertility has been observed in animal studies; the reversibility is unknown.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in nursing infants from denileukin diftitox, advise women to discontinue breast-feeding during treatment and for 7 days after the final dose. It is not known whether denileukin diftitox is present in human milk, although many drugs are excreted in human milk.

    Headline
    capillary leak syndrome, dehydration, edema, hypoalbuminemia, hypotension, pericardial effusion, peripheral edema, pleural effusion
    General Information
    Capillary leak syndrome (CLS) occurred in 27% of patients treated with denileukin diftitox in a pooled safety population that included 3 clinical trials (grade 3, 8%); one patient died. In clinical trials, CLS was defined as having at least 2 of the following symptoms at any time during treatment (not required to be simultaneous): hypotension, edema, and serum albumin less than 3 g/dL. The most common symptoms included edema (33% to 35%; grade 3 or 4, 1.4%), hypoalbuminemia (43% to 53%; grade 3 or 4, 1.5%), and hypotension; pleural effusion, pericardial effusion, and dehydration also occurred. "Edema" was a grouped term that included peripheral edema, generalized edema, and facial edema. Most CLS events (81%) occurred within the first 2 cycles of treatment with a median time to onset of 6.5 days from Cycle 1, Day 1; the median duration of CLS was 14 days. The median duration of CLS was 14 days, with 75% of patients experiencing resolution of symptoms; however, of the patients who experienced CLS, 22% had a recurrence. Do not administer denileukin diftitox in patients with serum albumin levels less than 3 g/dL; hold treatment until levels rise above 3 g/dL. An interruption of therapy, dose reduction, or discontinuation of therapy may be necessary based on the severity of CLS.

    blurred vision, ocular toxicity, visual impairment
    General Information
    Grade 1 or 2 visual impairment occurred in 9% of patients who received denileukin diftitox in a pooled population across 3 clinical trials, including changes in visual acuity and changes in color vision; 67% of these patients experienced resolution. The most common symptom of visual impairment in clinical trials was blurred vision, which was reported in 13% of patients in one multicenter, single-arm trial. Refer patients for an ophthalmologic evaluation for symptoms of ocular toxicity including changes in visual acuity, changes in color vision, or blurred vision. An interruption of therapy or discontinuation of therapy may be necessary if visual impairment occurs, depending on the severity.

    infusion-related reactions
    General Information
    Infusion-related reactions occurred in 69% of patients treated with denileukin diftitox in a pooled population across 3 clinical trials (grade 3, 3.4%), with the most common symptoms including nausea, fatigue, chills, musculoskeletal pain, vomiting, fever, and arthralgia. Most reactions (83%) occurred in the first 2 cycles of therapy. Infusion-related reactions were reported in 25% (grade 3 or 4, 6%) of patients in a multicenter, single-arm trial. Premedicate patients with acetaminophen, diphenhydramine, and IV fluids for the first 3 cycles of therapy and monitor frequently during the infusion; add a corticosteroid to the premedication regimen for at least 3 cycles in patients who experience a grade 2 or higher infusion-reaction. An interruption or discontinuation of therapy may be necessary depending on the severity.

    elevated hepatic enzymes, hepatotoxicity, hyperbilirubinemia
    General Information
    Denileukin diftitox can cause hepatotoxicity including elevated hepatic enzymes and hyperbilirubinemia. Increased ALT (66% to 70%; grade 3 or 4, 13% to 22%) and AST (60% to 64%; grade 3 or 4, 4.5% to 9%) were reported in clinical trials. The median time to onset of transaminitis was 8 days with a median time to resolution of 15 days; resolution occurred in all cases. Additionally, elevated bilirubin levels occurred in 5% of patients in a pooled population across 3 clinical trials (grade 3, 0.9%); increased alkaline phosphatase was reported in 18% of patients in a multicenter, single-arm trial. Monitor liver function tests at baseline and during treatment as clinically indicated. An interruption of therapy, dose reduction, or discontinuation of therapy may be necessary depending on the severity.

    antibody formation
    General Information
    Seventy-four percent (74%) of patients with immunogenicity results at baseline (n = 90) had pre-existing antibodies to denileukin diftitox-cxdl and 7.8% had neutralizing anti-denileukin diftitox-cxdl antibodies at baseline in a multicenter, single-arm trial; this is likely due to the high prevalence of vaccination against diphtheria toxin. Anti-interleukin (IL)-2 antibodies were present at baseline in 5.6% of patients; no patients had neutralizing antibodies against IL-2. Anti-denileukin diftitox-cxdl and anti-IL-2 antibodies developed in 88% and 82% of patients who were evaluable for treatment-emergent antibody formation (n = 84). Neutralizing antibodies against the denileukin diftitox-cxdl and IL-2 domains developed in 80% and 13% of patients, respectively. Anti-denileukin diftitox-cxdl and anti-IL-2 antibody titers also increased after treatment. Description
  Text
  Denileukin diftitox-cxdl is a fusion protein designed to direct the cytocidal action of diphtheria toxin (DT) to cells which express the IL2 receptor. It is indicated for the treatment of relapsed or refractory Stage I to III cutaneous T-cell lymphoma (CTCL) after at least one prior systemic therapy. Denileukin diftitox was originally approved by the FDA in 1999 but was voluntarily removed from the market in 2014 due to manufacturing difficulties. Denileukin diftitox-cxdl uses a newer manufacturing process and has 1.2- to 2-times greater specific bioactivity in nonclinical assays compared with the original version of denileukin diftitox and was FDA-approved in August 2024. Denileukin diftitox-cxdl carries a black box warning for capillary leak syndrome; do not administer to patients with serum albumin levels less than 3 g/dL and monitor renal and hepatic function at baseline and during treatment.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Denileukin diftitox-cxdl is a fusion protein designed to direct the cytocidal action of diphtheria toxin (DT) to cells which express the IL2 receptor. It is composed of the amino acid sequences for diphtheria toxin (DT) fragments A and B-His and the sequence for human interleukin-1 (IL-2). The DT fragment is cleaved after cellular uptake, with the free DT fragments inhibiting protein synthesis and resulting in cell death. It has demonstrated the ability to deplete immunosuppressive regulatory T lymphocytes and has antitumor activity through direct cytocidal action on IL-2R-expressing tumors.

Anti-drug antibodies
The presence of anti-denileukin diftitox antibodies decreased measured serum concentrations of denileukin diftitox-cxdl. In patients with treatment-emergent antibodies, the geometric mean ratio for the Cmax and AUC of denileukin diftitox was 37% and 19%, respectively, in Cycle 3 compared to Cycle 1; the geometric mean ratio for the Cmax and AUC of denileukin diftitox was 30% and 15%, respectively, in Cycle 5 compared to Cycle 1. This does not appear to adversely affect safety or efficacy.
 
Pharmacodynamics
Higher exposure to denileukin diftitox-cxdl was associated with an increased incidence of capillary leak syndrome, hepatotoxicity, and grade 3 or higher adverse reactions. The time course of pharmacodynamic response is unknown.

Remove the appropriate number of vials from the refrigerator and allow to stand for approximately 30 minutes to reach room temperature.

Because denileukin diftitox does not contain preservatives, use reconstituted vials immediately.

Withdraw the calculated dose from the vial(s) and inject into an empty IV bag. Use only plastic syringes or soft plastic bags for dilution; do not use glass containers.

Protect from light.

Treatment-Related Hepatotoxicity
Grade 2 (ALT/AST 3 to 5 times the upper limit of normal [ULN]): Maintain the dose of denileukin diftitox. Monitor ALT and AST at least weekly until they return to less than 3 times ULN.
Grade 3 (ALT/AST greater than 5 to 20 times ULN): Hold denileukin diftitox and monitor ALT/AST at least weekly. When ALT/AST return to less than 3 times ULN, resume treatment at the same dose for the first occurrence, or at a reduced dose (50%) for any subsequent occurrence.
Grade 4 (ALT/AST greater than 20 times ULN): Permanently discontinue denileukin diftitox therapy.
