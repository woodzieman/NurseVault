Description
  Text
  Tapinarof is a topical aryl hydrocarbon receptor (AhR) agonist approved for the treatment of plaque psoriasis and atopic dermatitis (eczema). The drug is administered as a thin film once daily to the affected areas. Treatment with tapinarof has been associated with the development of headaches, dermatologic reactions (e.g., folliculitis, dermatitis, pruritus, urticaria), and respiratory tract reactions (e.g., congestion, infection, pharyngitis, rhinorrhea). Tapinarof was FDA-approved in 2022.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Tapinarof is a topical aryl hydrocarbon receptor (AhR) agonist. AhR is a ligand-dependent transcription factor that regulates gene expression in immune and epithelial cells, and thus, plays a part in maintaining skin homeostasis. The exact mechanism by which tapinarof exerts its therapeutic action is unknown; however, potential mechanisms of action include:
Immunomodulation: In a T-cell polarization assay, tapinarof decreased T-cell expansion and T helper cell (Th17) differentiation and reduced the production of interleukin-17 (IL-17). In a mouse model of psoriasis, tapinarof downregulated inflammatory cytokine expression in skin tissue, including IL-17A and IL-17F.
Reduce oxidative stress: Tapinarof induces the AhR-nuclear factor erythroid 2-related factor 2 (Nrf2) transcription factor pathway, causing expression of antioxidant enzyme genes (e.g., nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide phosphate quinone oxidoreductase 1 and heme oxygenase-1) to reduce reactive oxygen species (ROS). Tapinarof also directly scavenges ROS, including superoxide anions and hydroxyl radicals.
Skin barrier normalization: Tapinarof induces the expression of skin barrier genes related to keratinocyte differentiation, including filaggrin and loricrin.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Topical Route
    Description
    In a study evaluating the pharmacokinetics of tapinarof, 21 adult subjects with moderate to severe plaque psoriasis were administered topical tapinarof at a mean daily dose of 5.23 g applied to a mean body surface area involvement of 27% (range: 21% to 46%). The plasma concentrations of tapinarof were below the quantifiable limits (BQL) of the assay (i.e., 50 pg/mL) in 68% of the samples. On Day 1, the mean Cmax and AUC were 0.9 +/- 1.4 ng/mL and 4.1 +/- 6.3 ng x hour/mL, respectively. On Day 29, the mean Cmax and AUC were 0.12 +/- 0.15 ng/mL and 0.61 +/- 0.65 ng x hour/mL, respectively. No accumulation was observed with repeated topical application.
  Special Populations
    Name
    Pediatrics
    Description
    The plasma concentrations of tapinarof were higher in pediatric subjects 2 years and older with atopic dermatitis when compared to adult subjects with plaque psoriasis. In a study evaluating the pharmacokinetics of tapinarof, 36 pediatric subjects 2 years and older with moderate to severe atopic dermatitis were administered topical tapinarof 3.2 g/day applied to a mean body surface area involvement of 43% (range: 26% to 90%). The plasma concentrations of tapinarof were BQL in 25% of the samples on Day 1 and 76% on Day 28. On Day 1, the Cmax and AUC were 2.4 +/- 3.9 ng/mL and 4.7 +/- 5.6 ng x hour/mL, respectively. The concentration at the end of the 24-hour dosing interval on Day 28 in pediatric patients aged 2 to 6 years, 7 to 11 years, and 12 to 17 years was 0.046 +/- 0.107 ng/mL, 0.089 +/-0.13 ng/mL, and 0.125 +/- 0.413 ng/mL, respectively.
  Text
  Tapinarof is administered topically. According to in vitro data, approximately 99% of tapinarof binds to human plasma proteins. The drug is metabolized by the liver via multiple pathways including oxidation, glucuronidation, and sulfation.
