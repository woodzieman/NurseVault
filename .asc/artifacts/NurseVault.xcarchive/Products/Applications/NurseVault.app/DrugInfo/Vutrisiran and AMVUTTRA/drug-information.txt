    pregnancy
    General Information
    There are no data available on the use of vutrisiran during human pregnancy to determine medication-associated risks of adverse developmental outcomes. Vutrisiran can lead to a decrease in serum vitamin A concentrations. Vitamin A supplementation is recommended, as it is essential for normal embryo-fetal development; however, excessive vitamin A concentrations are associated with adverse developmental effects. The effects on the fetus from vitamin A supplementation or a reduction in maternal serum transthyretin (TTR) caused by vutrisiran are unknown. In animal reproductive studies, administration of vutrisiran to pregnant rats during the period of organogenesis at doses of 0, 3, 10, or 30 mg/kg/day, reduced fetal body weight was noted at mid and high doses and embryo-fetal mortality was noted at high dose. These doses were also associated with maternal toxicity. Subcutaneous administration of vutrisiran at doses of 0, 3, 10, or 30 mg/kg/day to pregnant rabbits resulted in no adverse effects on embryo-fetal development. Administration of doses of vutrisiran at doses of 0, 5, 10, or 20 mg/kg to pregnant rats every 6 days throughout pregnancy and lactation resulted in no adverse developmental effects on the offspring.

Adverse Reactions
  Headline
  Adverse Reactions
  Adverse Reactions
    Headline
    vitamin A deficiency
    General Information
    Vutrisiran treatment leads to a reduction in vitamin A concentrations. In a clinical study of 122 individuals with polyneuropathy of hereditary transthyretin-mediated amyloidosis receiving vutrisiran, subjects were instructed to take the recommended daily allowance of vitamin A. At baseline, 74% of these subjects had normal vitamin A concentrations. Following treatment with vutrisiran, 98% of these subjects developed low vitamin A concentrations. During this same clinical study, 7% of subjects reported reduced vitamin A concentration as an adverse reaction. In a clinical study of 654 individuals with cardiomyopathy of wild-type or hereditary transthyretin-mediated amyloidosis receiving vutrisiran, 82% of subjects had normal vitamin A concentrations at baseline and, of these subjects, 80% developed low vitamin A concentrations. Individuals who are receiving vutrisiran injections should supplement with the recommended daily allowance of vitamin A and should be referred to an ophthalmologist if they develop any ocular effects (such as night blindness) that may indicate vitamin A deficiency. Supplementation with doses higher than the recommended daily allowance of vitamin A should not be given in an attempt to achieve normal serum levels during treatment with vutrisiran, as serum levels do not reflect the total amount of vitamin A in the body.

Description
  Text
  Vutrisiran is a transthyretin-directed small interfering RNA (siRNA) that is given subcutaneously once every 3 months. Vutrisiran is indicated for the treatment of polyneuropathy of hereditary transthyretin-mediated amyloidosis. It is also used for treatment of cardiomyopathy of the wild-type or hereditary transthyretin-mediated amyloidosis to reduce cardiovascular mortality, cardiovascular-related hospitalizations, and urgent heart failure visits. In HELIOS-A, individuals with polyneuropathy of hereditary transthyretin-mediated amyloidosis receiving vutrisiran treatment showed statistically significant improvements from baseline to month 9 in modified Neuropathy Impairment Score +7 (mNIS+7), the Norfolk Quality of Life-Diabetic Neuropathy (QoL-ND) total score, and the 10-meter walk test (10MWT) compared to placebo. Change in modified body mass index from baseline to month 9 also showed improvement with the use of vutrisiran. In HELIOS-B, individuals with cardiomyopathy of the wild-type or hereditary transthyretin-mediated amyloidosis receiving vutrisiran treatment had a significant reduction in the risk of all-cause mortality (28%) and recurrent cardiovascular events (33%), consisting of cardiovascular hospitalizations and urgent heart failure visits, compared to placebo. As treatment with vutrisiran has been associated with a decrease in vitamin A concentrations, supplementation is recommended during therapy to avoid vitamin A deficiency. Individuals experiencing ocular symptoms suggestive of vitamin A deficiency should be referred to an ophthalmologist for assessment. Vutrisiran was FDA-approved in June 2022.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Vutrisiran is a double-stranded small interfering ribonucleic acid (siRNA) that targets mutant and wild-type transthyretin (TTR) messenger RNA (mRNA) conjugate that is covalently linked to a ligand containing three N-acetylgalactosamine (GalNAc) that causes degradation of mutant and wild-type TTR mRNA through RNA interference. As a result, serum TTR protein and TTR protein tissue deposits are reduced.
 
During clinical trials, following administration of the recommended dosage every 3 months to patients with hATTR amyloidosis, vutrisiran reduced mean serum TTR at steady state by 83%. Similar TTR reductions were observed regardless of Val30Met genotype status. Vutrisiran also reduced the mean steady-state serum vitamin A concentration by 62% over 9 months.

  Vutrisiran is administered subcutaneously. Plasma protein biding of vutrisiran is 80% and concentration dependent, with decreased binding at increased vutrisiran concentrations. The apparent volume of distribution is 10.1 L with a relative standard error of 5.8%. Following administration, vutrisiran distributes primarily to the liver, where it is metabolized by nucleases to short nucleotide fragments of varying sizes. Clearance at a rate of 21.4 L/hour (range: 19.8 to 30 L/hour). The mean fraction of unchanged vutrisiran in the urine was approximately 19.4% at the 25 mg dose. The terminal elimination half-life of vutrisiran is 5.2 hours (range: 2.2 to 6.4 hours).
 
Pharmacodynamics
In the HELIOS-A clinical trial, following administration of a 25 mg dose of vutrisiran every 3 months to individuals with polyneuropathy of hereditary transthyretin amyloidosis (hATTR), vutrisiran reduced mean serum TTR at steady state by 83%. Similar TTR reductions were observed regardless of Val30Met genotype status, weight, sex, age, or race. Vutrisiran also reduced the mean steady state serum vitamin A by 62% over 9 months. In the HELIOS-B clinical trial involving individuals with cardiomyopathy of wild-type (wtATTR) or hATTR, the mean serum TTR reduction profile was similar to that observed in HELIOS-A, and consistent across age, sex, race, body weight, anti-drug antibody (ADA) status, ATTR disease type (wtATTR versus hATTR), NYHA class, and baseline tafamidis use. Vutrisiran reduced the mean steady state serum vitamin A by 65% over 36 months.
 
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes and drug transporters: None
Vutrisiran is not a substrate of cytochrome P450 isoenzymes. It does not induce or inhibit cytochrome P450 isoenzymes or drug transporters.

      Subcutaneous Administration
      Description
      General information
Vutrisiran is for subcutaneous use only and should be administered by a healthcare professional.
Refer to the "Administration Instructions" for proper preparation and injection. The injection comes as a prefilled glass syringe.
Missed dose: If a dose is missed, administer as soon as possible and resume dosing every 3 months from the most recently administered dose.
Prepare the syringe
Ensure that the syringe is not damaged, such as cracked or leaking, the needle cap is attached to the syringe, and that the product is still within the expiration date. Do not use the syringe if any issues are found while inspecting the syringe.
If stored cold, warm to room temperature for 30 minutes prior to use.
Remove the syringe from the packaging by gripping the syringe body. Do not touch the plunger rod until ready to inject.
Hold the syringe body with one hand. Pull the needle cap straight off with the other hand and dispose of the needle cap immediately. It is normal to see a drop of liquid at the tip of the needle.
Do not touch the needle or let it touch any surface.
Do not recap the syringe.
Do not use the syringe if it is dropped.
Subcutaneous Administration
Choose and prepare the injection site:
Choose an injection site, such as the abdomen, thighs, or upper arms. Avoid the 5 centimeter area around the navel, scar tissue, or any areas that are reddened, inflamed, or swollen.
Clean the injection site as directed.
Pinch the cleansed skin and fully insert the needle into the pinched skin at a 45 to 90-degree angle.
Push the plunger rod as far as it will go to administer the dose and activate the needle shield.
Release the plunger rod to allow the needle shield to cover the needle. Do not block plunger rod movement.
Disposal: Immediately dispose of the used syringe into an approved sharps container.

Dosage Adjustment Guidelines
  Hepatic Impairment
  No dosage adjustment is recommended in individuals with mild hepatic impairment (bilirubin of 1 times the upper limit of normal (ULN) or less and AST more than 1 times the ULN, or bilirubin more than 1 to 1.5 times the ULN and any AST) or moderate hepatic impairment (bilirubin more than 1.5 to 3 times the ULN and any AST). Vutrisiran has not been studied in individuals with severe hepatic impairment.
  Renal Impairment
  Mild to moderate renal impairment (eGFR 30 to 89 mL/minute/1.73 m2): No dosage adjustment is recommended.
Severe renal impairment (eGFR less than 30 mL/minute/1.73 m2): Vutrisiran has not been studied in patients with severe renal impairment or end-stage renal disease.
