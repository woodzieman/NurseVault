          Step-up dosing schedule: dose 1, 0.06 mg/kg subcutaneously on day 1; dose 2, 0.3 mg/kg subcutaneously on day 4; and first treatment dose, 1.5 mg/kg subcutaneously on day 7. Administer with the recommended premedication and consider antiviral prophylaxis for herpes zoster reactivation. Allowable administration time windows as follows: step-up dose 2, may give 2 to 4 days after step-up dose 1 and up to 7 days after step-up dose 1 to allow for resolution of adverse reactions; first treatment dose, may give 2 to 4 days after step-up dose 2 and up to 7 days after step-up dose 2 to allow for resolution of adverse reactions; weekly doses, maintain a minimum of 5 days between doses; every 2 week doses, maintain a minimum of 12 days between doses. Additionally, 39.4% of patients achieved a complete response or better. The median duration of response was 18.4 months and the median progression-free survival time was 11.3 months.           Dosage Description
          Step-up dosing schedule: dose 1, 0.06 mg/kg subcutaneously on day 1 (administered 20 hours or more after the daratumumab; hyaluronidase dose on day 0); dose 2, 0.3 mg/kg subcutaneously on day 3; and first treatment dose, 1.5 mg/kg subcutaneously on day 7 (administered at least 3 hours after daratumumab; hyaluronidase dose). Begin the 1.5 mg/kg subcutaneously once weekly dosing schedule starting on week 2 (1 week after the first treatment dose) through week 8 (administered at least 15 minutes after daratumumab; hyaluronidase dose). Then start the 3 mg/kg subcutaneously biweekly (every 2 weeks) dosing schedule at week 9 through week 24 (administered at least 15 minutes after daratumumab; hyaluronidase dose) and continue every 4 weeks starting at week 25 onwards (administered at least 15 minutes after daratumumab; hyaluronidase dose). Daratumumab; hyaluronidase (1,800 mg/30,000 units) subcutaneously starts 1 day prior (on day 0) to initiation of teclistamab step-up dose 1 and continues once weekly from weeks 1 to 8, then every 2 weeks from weeks 9 to 24, and then every 4 weeks starting on week 25 until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity. Therapy interruption or discontinuation may be necessary in patients who develop toxicity. Administer teclistamab with the recommended premedication and consider antiviral prophylaxis for herpes zoster reactivation. Allowable administration time windows as follows: step-up dose 2, may give 2 to 4 days after step-up dose 1 and up to 7 days after step-up dose 1 to allow for resolution of adverse reactions; first treatment dose, may give 2 to 4 days after step-up dose 2 and up to 7 days after step-up dose 2 to allow for resolution of adverse reactions; weekly doses, maintain a minimum of 5 days between doses; every 2 week doses, maintain a minimum of 12 days between doses; every 4 week doses, maintain a minimum of 25 days between doses. In a randomized trial (MajesTEC-3) at a median follow-up of 34.5 months, median progression-free survival was not reached in the teclistamab plus daratumumab; hyaluronidase arm compared with 18.1 months in the investigator's choice control arm of either daratumumab; hyaluronidase, pomalidomide, plus dexamethasone [DPd] or daratumumab; hyaluronidase, bortezomib, plus dexamethasone [DVd]. Median overall survival was not reached in either arm. Additionally, 81.8% versus 32.1% of patients achieved a complete response or better in the respective treatment arms and of these patients, the minimal residual disease negativity rate was 70.8% versus 53.3%, respectively.

    Headline
    cytokine release syndrome
    General Information
    Cytokine release syndrome (CRS) is a potentially fatal reaction that has been reported with teclistamab therapy. To reduce the risk of CRS, administer teclistamab according to a step-up dosing schedule and give pretreatment medications. Hospitalization is recommended for 48 hours following both step-up dose 1 and 2; patients should remain within proximity of a healthcare facility for daily monitoring for 48 hours after the first treatment dose within the step-up dosing schedule. Interruption or discontinuation of therapy may be necessary in patients who develop CRS; evaluate the need for hospitalization, supportive care, and further management (using current practice guidelines) in patients with severe CRS.
    Headline
    activities requiring coordination and concentration, driving or operating machinery, neurotoxicity
    General Information
    Severe neurotoxicity (e.g., immune effector cell-associated neurotoxicity syndrome (ICANS), encephalopathy, seizures, and Guillain-Barre syndrome) has been reported with teclistamab therapy; some cases were fatal or life-threatening. Neurotoxicity/ICANS may occur concurrently with cytokine release syndrome (CRS), following CRS, or in the absence of CRS. Hospitalization is recommended for 48 hours following both step-up dose 1 and 2; patients should remain within proximity of a healthcare facility for daily monitoring for 48 hours after the first treatment dose within the step-up dosing schedule. Advise patients to avoid driving or operating machinery or performing other activities requiring coordination and concentration during and for 48 hours following the step-up dosing schedule and following the onset of any neurologic toxicity symptoms until toxicity resolves. Therapy interruption, corticosteroid administration, or permanent discontinuation of therapy may be necessary in patients who develop neurotoxicity; consider a neurology (or other applicable specialty) consult for further evaluation and management (e.g., non-sedating, anti-seizure medication for seizure prophylaxis). Evaluate the need for hospitalization, supportive care, and further management (using current practice guidelines) in patients with severe neurotoxicity.
    Headline
    infection
    General Information
    Do not administer teclistamab therapy to patients who have an active infection during the step-up dosing schedule. Severe and fatal infections have been reported with teclistamab therapy. Consider antiviral prophylaxis for herpes zoster reactivation prior to starting teclistamab therapy. Therapy interruption or permanent discontinuation may be necessary in patients who develop an infection.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Teclistamab may cause fetal harm if administered during pregnancy, based on its mechanism of action. There are no available human or animal data on the use of teclistamab during pregnancy. Teclistamab causes T-cell activation and cytokine release; immune activation may compromise pregnancy maintenance. Human immunoglobulin G (IgG) is known to cross into the placenta; therefore, teclistamab may be transmitted to the developing fetus.
    Headline
    people who may become pregnant, reproductive risk
    General Information
    Counsel people who may become pregnant about the reproductive risk associated with teclistamab. Teclistamab may cause fetal harm. Verify pregnancy status prior to treatment initiation. Advise people who may become pregnant to use effective contraception while taking teclistamab and for 5 months after the last dose.
    Headline
    neonates and infants exposed to this medication in utero
    General Information
    Teclistamab is associated with hypogammaglobulinemia. Consider monitoring immunoglobulin levels in neonates and infants exposed to this medication in utero.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Advise patients to avoid breast-feeding while taking teclistamab and for 5 months after the last dose because of the potential for serious adverse effects. There are no data on the presence of teclistamab in human milk, its effects on the breast-fed child, or its effects on milk production. Human immunoglobulin (IgG) is excreted in human milk; however, the effects of local gastrointestinal exposure and limited systemic exposure in the breastfed child to teclistamab are not known.

Interactions
Monitor for toxicity and adjust the dose as needed for CYP substrates, especially during the first 2 weeks of teclistamab therapy and during and after cytokine release syndrome. Teclistamab causes a cytokine release that may suppress CYP enzyme activity.

    infection, pharyngitis, rhinitis, sepsis, sinusitis, tracheitis
    General Information
    Infections, including opportunistic infections, have been reported with teclistamab therapy. Therapy interruption or permanent discontinuation may be necessary in patients who develop an infection. Severe (grade 3 or 4, 35%) or fatal (4.2%) infection was reported in patients with relapsed or refractory multiple myeloma who received teclistamab monotherapy in a clinical trial (n = 165). Specific infections reported in this trial were upper respiratory tract infection (26%; grade 3, 2.4%), pneumonia (24%; grade 3 or 4, 15%), and urinary tract infection (11%; grade 3, 5%). Sepsis and new onset or reactivated viral infections (including adenovirus, hepatitis B virus, cytomegalovirus, varicella zoster virus, and herpes simplex virus) occurred in less than 10% of patients. Additionally, fatal infection events included COVID-19 infection (1.8%), pneumonia (1.8%), and septic shock (0.6%). In another trial of multiple myeloma patients who received teclistamab in combination with daratumumab; hyaluronidase (n = 283), severe (grade 3 or 4, 54%) or fatal (4.6%) infection was reported. Specific infections reported in this trial were upper respiratory tract infection (79%; grade 3, 17%), COVID-19 (45%; grade 3 or 4, 6%), pneumonia (42%; grade 3 or 4, 33%), gastroenteritis (20%; grade 3 or 4, 4.9%), urinary tract infection (17%; grade 3 or 4, 2.8%), herpes virus infection (11%; grade 3 or 4, 2.1%), sepsis (less than 10%), and CMV infection (less than 10%). The term upper respiratory tract infection included bronchitis, bronchiolitis, nasopharyngitis/pharyngitis, rhinitis, sinusitis, and tracheitis.

    Headline
    cytokine release syndrome
    General Information
    Cytokine release syndrome (CRS) has been reported with teclistamab therapy. Signs and symptoms of CRS include fever, hypoxia, chills, hypotension, sinus tachycardia, headache, and elevated AST and ALT levels. Evaluate for and treat other causes of fever, hypoxia, and hypotension. Consider laboratory testing to monitor for disseminated intravascular coagulation (DIC), hematology parameters, as well as pulmonary, cardiac, renal, and hepatic function. Interruption or discontinuation of therapy may be necessary in patients who develop CRS; evaluate the need for hospitalization, supportive care, and further management (using current practice guideline) in patients with severe CRS. CRS was reported in 64% (grade 3, 0.2%) of patients with relapsed or refractory multiple myeloma who received teclistamab monotherapy and combination therapy in clinical trials (n = 448); recurrent CRS occurred in 27% of patients. Most patients experienced CRS during the step-up dosing period (step-up dose 1, 37%; step-up dose 2, 32%; first treatment dose, 20%); 2.5% of patients had CRS following subsequent treatment doses. The median time to CRS onset was 2 (range, 1 to 9) days after the most recent dose with a median duration of 2 (range, 1 to 22) days.

    Headline
    amnesia, confusion, delirium, encephalopathy, Guillain-Barre syndrome, headache, immune effector cell-associated neurotoxicity syndrome, neurotoxicity, peripheral neuropathy, progressive multifocal leukoencephalopathy, seizures
    General Information
    Serious, life-threatening, or fatal neurologic toxicity including immune effector cell-associated neurotoxicity syndrome (ICANS) can occur with teclistamab therapy. Rule out other causes of neurologic symptoms. Therapy interruption, corticosteroid administration, or permanent discontinuation of therapy may be necessary in patients who develop neurotoxicity; consider a neurology (or other applicable specialty) consult for further evaluation and management (e.g., non-sedating, anti-seizure medication for seizure prophylaxis). Evaluate the need for hospitalization, supportive care, and further management (using current practice guidelines) in patients with severe neurotoxicity. ICANS may occur concurrently with cytokine release syndrome (CRS) or after CRS resolves. The most frequent clinical manifestations of ICANS reported were confusion, dysgraphia, amnesia, encephalopathy, and delirium. Neurotoxicity occurred in 60% (grade 3 or 4, 6%) of patients with relapsed or refractory multiple myeloma who received teclistamab monotherapy and combination therapy in clinical trials (n = 448). Specific neurologic events reported were headache (27%; grade 3 or 4, 0.6% to 1.4%), sensory peripheral neuropathy (16%; grade 3 or 4, 0.7% to 1.2%), motor dysfunction (15%), insomnia (12%), encephalopathy (11%), and dizziness (8%); fatal events including Guillain-Barre syndrome and status epilepticus occurred in 0.4% of patients. In a teclistamab monotherapy trial (n = 165), ICANS occurred in 6% of patients with recurrent ICANS in 1.8% of patients. Most patients experienced ICANS during the step-up dosing period (step-up dose 1, 1.2%; step-up dose 2, 0.6%; first treatment dose, 1.8%). The median time to ICANS onset was 4 (range, 2 to 8) days after the most recent dose with a median duration of 3 (range, 1 to 20) days. Seizures, Guillain-Barre syndrome, and progressive multifocal leukoencephalopathy (PML) each occurred in fewer than 10% of patients. In another trial of multiple myeloma patients who received teclistamab in combination with daratumumab; hyaluronidase (n = 283), ICANS was reported in 1.1% (grade 4, 0.4%) of patients and all events occurred during the step-up dosing schedule. The median time to onset of ICANS was 2 (range, 1 to 3) days after the most recent dose with a median duration of 2 (range, 1 to 2) days.

Description
  Text
  Teclistamab-cqyv is a bispecific T-cell engaging (BiTE) antibody that binds the CD3 receptor expressed on the surface of T-cells with the B-cell maturation antigen (BCMA) expressed on the surface of multiple myeloma cells and some healthy B-lineage cells. It is indicated as monotherapy for the treatment of adults with relapsed or refractory multiple myeloma who have received at least 4 prior lines of therapy including a proteasome inhibitor, an immunomodulatory agent, and an anti-CD38 monoclonal antibody, and also in combination with daratumumab; hyaluronidase in adults who have received at least 1 prior line of therapy including a proteasome inhibitor and an immunomodulatory agent. Teclistamab has a black box warning for cytokine release syndrome and severe neurologic toxicity.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Teclistamab-cqyv is a bispecific T-cell engaging (BiTE) antibody that works by binding the CD3 receptor expressed on the surface of T-cells to the B-cell maturation antigen (BCMA) expressed on the surface of multiple myeloma cells. It is a humanized immunoglobulin G4-proline, alanine, alanine (IgG4-PAA) antibody produced from Chinese Hamster Ovary (CHO) cells using recombinant DNA technology. Teclistamab consists of an anti-BCMA heavy chain and light chain and an anti-CD3 heavy chain and light chain with 2 interchain disulfide bonds connecting the two arms. By binding the CD3 antigen and BCMA, teclistamab causes myeloma cell lysis directly via protein toxin (e.g., perforins and granzymes) release and increased cytokine (e.g., interferons, tumor necrosis factors, and interleukins) production.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Subcutaneous Route
    Description
    The mean bioavailability of teclistamab is 72% when administered subcutaneously. The pharmacokinetic parameters of teclistamab increase proportionally over a dosage range of 0.08 to 3 mg/kg (0.05 to 2 times the approved recommended treatment dosage). A 90% steady-state exposure rate was achieved after 12 weekly treatment doses. Following the thirteenth weekly 1.5 mg/kg subcutaneous dose, the teclistamab geometric mean Cmax level was 23.8 mcg/mL (coefficient of variation [CV], 55%), Cavg level was 22.8 mcg/mL (CV, 57%), and median Tmax was 72 (range, 24 to 168) hours. Discontinuation of teclistamab after the thirteenth weekly treatment dose is expected to result in a 50% decreased Cmax level at a median time of 15 (range, 7 to 33) days after Tmax and a 97% decreased Cmax level at a median time of 69 (range, 32 to 163) days after Tmax.

Using aseptic technique, withdraw the required injection volume from the vial(s) into a syringe using a transfer needle.

The appropriate injection volume is based on vial concentration, patient weight, and type of dose as described below.
