Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  General Information
  Intravenous administration of protamine has been associated with severe (some fatal) anaphylactic and anaphylactoid reactions (see Adverse Reactions). Potential risk factors for protamine reactions include insulin-dependent diabetes mellitus, vasectomy, fish hypersensitivity, prior exposure to protamine sulfate, and the rate of IV infusion. Patients who have demonstrated a fish hypersensitivity or those with previous protamine exposure through insulin products or protamine sulfate treatment are at increased risk for hypersensitivity to protamine. Antiprotamine antibodies have been found in the serum of vasectomized males (22—33%) or infertile males, thereby increasing the risk of hypersensitivity reactions in these patients. Fatal anaphylactoid-like reactions have been reported following protamine administration; therefore, these patient populations should be monitored closely if protamine therapy is indicated.Protamine is classified as pregnancy category C. Adequate studies on the risks during pregnancy have not been conducted; therefore, it is unknown whether protamine causes fetal harm or is distributed into breast milk. Protamine should be administered during pregnancy or breast-feeding only when clearly needed.

    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components. Because protamine is derived from salmon and certain other species of fish, individuals with a history of allergy to fish may develop hypersensitivity reactions to protamine, although to date no relationship has been established between allergic reactions to protamine and fish allergy.

    Do not administer protamine when bleeding occurs without prior heparin use.

    infertility, left ventricular dysfunction, serious hypersensitivity reactions or anaphylaxis, vasectomy
    General Information
    Protamine can cause serious hypersensitivity reactions or anaphylaxis. Risk factors for these adverse events include high protamine dose or overdose, rapid administration, repeated protamine doses, previous administration of protamine, and current or previous use of protamine-containing drugs (NPH insulin, protamine zinc insulin, and certain beta-blockers). Previous exposure to protamine can induce a humoral immune response and predispose susceptible individuals to the development of untoward reactions from the subsequent use of this medication. Individuals exposed to protamine through the use of protamine-containing insulin or during heparin neutralization may experience life-threatening reactions and fatal anaphylaxis upon receiving large doses of protamine intravenously. Severe reactions to intravenous protamine can occur in the absence of local or systemic allergic reactions to subcutaneous injection of protamine-containing insulin. Allergy to fish, previous vasectomy, severe left ventricular dysfunction, and abnormal preoperative pulmonary hemodynamics may also be risk factors. Reports of the presence of anti-protamine antibodies in the sera of men with infertility or post-vasectomy suggest that some of these individuals may react to the use of protamine. In individuals with any of these risk factors, carefully consider the risk to benefit of administration of protamine. Ensure vasopressors and resuscitation equipment are immediately available in case of a severe reaction to protamine.

Interactions
Heparin: (Contraindicated) Upon contact with heparin, protamine forms a salt, neutralizing the anticoagulant effect of both drugs. Protamine, a strongly basic compound, forms complexes with heparin sodium or heparin calcium, which are acidic compounds. Formation of this complex can result in disruption of the heparin-antithrombin III complex responsible for the anticoagulant activity of heparin. Protamine is used therapeutically to reverse the activity of heparins.

Adverse Reactions
  Headline
  Adverse Reactions
  General Information
  Severe hypotension, sinus bradycardia, pulmonary hypertension, dyspnea, and rash (unspecified) have been reported following protamine administration. These adverse reactions have been potentiated by rapid infusion of protamine and can be minimized by adherence to recommended dosing guidelines (see Dosage). Systemic hypertension and nausea/vomiting also have occurred during therapy with protamine. Intravenous administration of protamine has been associated with severe (some fatal) anaphylactic and anaphylactoid reactions (e.g., angioedema, urticaria, acute bronchospasm or wheezing, anaphylactic shock). Potential risk factors for protamine reactions include insulin-dependent diabetes mellitus, vasectomy, fish allergy, prior exposure to protamine sulfate, and the rate of IV infusion. Pulmonary edema has also been reported in patients on cardiopulmonary bypass undergoing cardiovascular surgery. The precise mechanism of protamine in potentiating this condition is unknown; however, multiple contributing factors have been implicated.Hyperheparinemia (heparin rebound) and bleeding have been reported 8—9 hours (up to 18 hours) after neutralization of heparin by protamine. This reaction results from release of heparin from the protamine/heparin complex. It has been observed most frequently when protamine is used following extracorporeal circulation in dialysis or arterial and cardiac surgery.

Description
  Text
  Protamine sulfate, a protein derived from fish sperm, is a chemical antagonist of heparin. Clinically, protamine is indicated for heparin overdose and is commonly used to neutralize heparin during extracorporeal circulation following dialysis, and arterial or cardiac surgery. Dosage is guided by blood coagulation studies, with each mg of protamine sulfate neutralizing not less than 100 USP heparin units. Protamine must be administered via slow intravenous injection at a rate not to exceed 50 mg over a 10 minute period. High doses and rapid administration may result in severe hypotension, cardiovascular collapse, pulmonary edema, pulmonary vasoconstriction, and pulmonary hypertension.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Protamine possesses weak anticoagulant effects when administered alone; however, upon contact with heparin, it forms a salt, neutralizing the anticoagulant effect of both drugs. Protamine, a strongly basic compound, forms complexes with heparin sodium or heparin calcium, which are acidic compounds. Formation of this complex can result in disruption of the heparin-antithrombin III complex responsible for the anticoagulant activity of heparin. The protamine-heparin complex does not possess anticoagulant properties. Protamine's anticoagulant effect usually is clinically insignificant and is likely the result of thromboplastin inhibition, which diminishes thrombin activity.

Pharmacokinetics
  Text
  Protamine is administered via intravenous infusion. The onset of action is rapid, with neutralization of heparin evident within 5 minutes. The duration of action can persist up to 2 hours, depending on body temperature. The fate of the heparin-protein complex is unknown, but it appears to be partially degraded, with the release of heparin.
 
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes: none
