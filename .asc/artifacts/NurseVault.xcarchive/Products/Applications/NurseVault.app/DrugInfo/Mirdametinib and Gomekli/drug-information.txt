    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Pregnancy should be avoided by people who may become pregnant during mirdametinib treatment and for at least 6 weeks after the last dose. Although there are no adequately controlled studies in people who may become pregnant, mirdametinib can cause fetal harm or death when administered during pregnancy based on its mechanism of action and animal studies. In the ReNeu clinical trial, a pregnancy reported 31 days after the last dose of mirdametinib resulted in a first trimester spontaneous abortion. In animal studies when administered to pregnant rats and rabbits at doses equivalent to the human clinical dose, embryo-fetal mortality, structural abnormalities, and alterations to growth occurred. Individuals who are pregnant or who may become pregnant while receiving mirdametinib should be apprised of the potential hazard to the fetus.
    Headline
    cardiac disease
    General Information
    Use mirdametinib with caution in patients with a history of cardiac disease; mirdametinib has not been studied in patients with a history of clinically significant cardiac disease or left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) less than 55% prior to treatment initiation. An interruption of therapy, dose reduction, or discontinuation of therapy may be necessary based on the severity of the adverse reaction.
    Headline
    people who can cause pregnancy in others, people who may become pregnant, reproductive risk
    General Information
    Counsel people about the reproductive risk associated with mirdametinib. People who may become pregnant should avoid pregnancy and use effective contraception during and for 6 weeks after treatment with mirdametinib. People who can cause pregnancy in others should use effective contraception during and for 3 months after treatment with mirdametinib. People who may become pregnant should undergo pregnancy testing prior to initiation of mirdametinib. Individuals who become pregnant while receiving mirdametinib should be apprised of the potential hazard to the fetus. It is not known whether mirdametinib is present in human milk, although many drugs are excreted in human milk.

  Adverse Reactions
    Headline
    blurred vision, ocular toxicity, retinal detachment
    General Information
    In a pooled safety population, ocular toxicity occurred in 28% (grade 3, 1.3%) of adults and 19% of pediatric patients treated with mirdametinib in clinical trials. Ocular toxicity in adults included blurred vision (9%), retinal vein occlusion (RVO) (2.7%), and retinal pigment epithelium detachment/retinal detachment (RPED) (1.3%). In a single-arm study (ReNeu) of adult and pediatric patients who received mirdametinib, RVO, RPED, and blurred vision each occurred in less than 20% of patients. Conduct comprehensive ophthalmic assessments prior to initiating mirdametinib, at regular intervals during treatment, and to evaluate any new or worsening visual changes. An interruption of therapy, dose reduction, or discontinuation of therapy may be necessary depending on the type and severity of toxicity.

    Headline
    left ventricular dysfunction
    General Information
    Left ventricular dysfunction has occurred in patients treated with mirdametinib. Mirdametinib has not been studied in patients with a history of clinically significant cardiac disease or left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) less than 55% prior to initiation. In a single-arm study (ReNeu) of adult and pediatric patients who received mirdametinib, decreased LVEF of 10% to less than 20% occurred in 16% of adults and 25% of pediatric patients; decreased LVEF of 20% or greater occurred in 1.8% pf pediatric patients. The median time to first onset of decreased LVEF in adults and pediatric patients was 70 days and 132 days, respectively. Assess ejection fraction by echocardiogram prior to initiating mirdametinib, every 3 months during the first year, and then as clinically indicated. An interruption of therapy, dose reduction, or discontinuation of therapy may be necessary depending on the severity of toxicity.
    Headline
    acneiform rash, alopecia, atopic dermatitis, exfoliative dermatitis, hair discoloration, maculopapular rash, palmar-plantar erythrodysesthesia (hand and foot syndrome), rash
    General Information
    Dermatologic toxicities have occurred in patients treated with mirdametinib. In a pooled safety population, rash occurred in 92% (grade 3, 8%) of adults and 72% (grade 3, 3.4%) of pediatric patients treated with mirdametinib in clinical trials. Acneiform rash/dermatitis occurred more often in patients 12 to 17 years than those aged 2 to 11 years (77% vs. 16%), while non-acneiform rashes occurred more often in patients 2 to 11 years than those aged 12 to 17 years (53% vs. 15%). In a single-arm study (ReNeu) of adult and pediatric patients who received mirdametinib, rash (90% and 73%; grade 3, 10% and 3.6%) was reported with mirdametinib treatment. Additionally, alopecia and hair discoloration each occurred in less than 20% of patients. The term rash included acneiform dermatitis, eczema, maculopapular rash, atopic dermatitis, erythematous rash, palmar-plantar erythrodysesthesia (hand and foot syndrome), exfoliative dermatitis/rash, and skin exfoliation. Initiate supportive care at first signs of dermatologic toxicity; an interruption of therapy, dose reduction, or discontinuation of therapy may be necessary depending on the severity.

Description
  Text
  Mirdametinib is an oral MEK 1/2 kinase inhibitor indicated for the treatment of adult and pediatric patients 2 years and older with neurofibromatosis type 1 who have symptomatic plexiform neurofibromas not amenable to complete resection. Mirdametinib may cause ocular toxicity and left ventricular dysfunction, which require monitoring at baseline and periodically during treatment. It was approved by the FDA in February 2025.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Mirdametinib is a kinase inhibitor of mitogen-activated protein kinases 1 and 2 (MEK1/2), which occupy pivotal positions in the MAPK pathway. The MAPK pathway is a key signaling network that regulates cell growth and survival and that plays a central role in multiple cancers and rare diseases when dysregulated. MEK1/2 proteins are upstream regulators of the extracellular signal-related kinase (ERK) pathway. In vitro, mirdametinib inhibited kinase activity of MEK1 and MEK2 and downstream phosphorylation of ERK. Oral administration of mirdametinib inhibited ERK phosphorylation and reduced neurofibroma tumor volume and proliferation in a mouse model of neurofibromatosis type 1.

  Text
  Mirdametinib is administered orally. It is greater than 99% bound to human plasma proteins and the mean apparent volume of distribution is 255 liters (CV, 13%). Mirdametinib has a mean terminal half-life of 28 hours (CV, 12%); the mean apparent clearance is 6.3 liters/hour (CV, 13%). Approximately 68% of a single radiolabeled dose of mirdametinib was recovered in urine (0.7% as unchanged drug) and 27% in the feces (9% as unchanged drug in both feces and urine).
 
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes and drug transporters: BCRP, P-gp
Mirdametinib is primarily metabolized by glucuronidation and oxidation via UGT (primarily UGT1A6 and UGT2B7) and CES enzymes. The effect of concomitant strong CYP3A4 inducers (that also co-induce UGTs, P-gp, and CES enzymes) on mirdametinib pharmacokinetics is unknown. Mirdametinib is a substrate of BCRP and P-gp transporters.

Dosage Adjustment Guidelines
  Hepatic Impairment
  No dosage adjustment is required in patients with mild hepatic impairment (total bilirubin upper limit of normal [ULN] or less and AST greater than ULN, or total bilirubin 1 to 1.5 times ULN). Mirdametinib has not been studied in patients with moderate (bilirubin greater than 1.5 to 3 times ULN and any AST) or severe (bilirubin greater than 3 times ULN and any AST) hepatic impairment.
  Renal Impairment
  No dosage adjustment is required in patients with mild (CrCl 60 to 89 mL/min) or moderate (CrCl 30 to 59 mL/min) renal impairment. Mirdametinib has not been studied in patients with severe (CrCl less than 30 mL/min) renal impairment.
