Drug Class: Paricalcitol is a synthetic, biologically active analog of calcitriol.

Routes: oral, injection (IV/IM/SC); Common adult doses: Specific maximum dosage information is not available for PO or IV formulations; however, doses up to 0.24 mcg/kg (16.8 mcg) IV have been administered safely.

Common Side Effects (excluding routine GI upset): arthralgia, back pain, muscle cramps, myalgia, acne vulgaris, alopecia, angioedema, hirsutism, laryngeal edema, night sweats, pruritus, rash, skin ulcer, urticaria, agitation, anxiety, confusion, delirium, depression, dizziness, headache, hypoesthesia, insomnia, myoclonia, paresthesias, restlessness, syncope, bowel ischemia, dysgeusia, gastroesophageal reflux

In an Overdose: Overdosage of paricalcitol may lead to hypervitaminosis D and hypercalcemia.

Key Nursing Considerations: Contraindications/precautions: Patients with hypercalcemia should not receive paricalcitol. Acute overdose of paricalcitol may cause hypercalcemia requiring emergent treatment.

Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  General Information
  Patients with hypercalcemia should not receive paricalcitol. Acute overdose of paricalcitol may cause hypercalcemia requiring emergent treatment. Close monitoring of calcium levels is recommended during dose adjustments of paricalcitol. If clinically significant hypercalcemia develops, the paricalcitol dose should be held or decreased. Chronic paricalcitol dosing may place the patient at risk for hypercalcemia, elevated calcium/phosphorus product and metastatic calcification.Since paricalcitol is a vitamin D analog, use of this agent should be avoided in patients with signs and symptoms of hypervitaminosis D (vitamin D toxicity). Patients should be instructed not to take vitamin D-related compounds with paricalcitol. Symptoms of vitamin D toxicity include hypercalcemia, hypercalciuria and hyperphosphatemia.Exercise caution when administering paricalcitol to women who are breast-feeding. Studies have shown that paricalcitol is present in the milk of lactating rats. It is not known if paricalcitol is excreted into breast milk, but vitamin D is excreted in limited amounts, and paricalcitol is probably also excreted in human breast milk, although exposure to the breast-feeding infant is expected to be minimal. In a woman given large doses of vitamin D, the infant developed hypercalcemia. In a woman who is breast-feeding her infant, a decision should be made whether to discontinue nursing or to discontinue the drug, taking into account the importance of the drug to the mother. However, if a woman who is breast-feeding her infant is taking paricalcitol, consideration should be given to monitoring calcium concentrations in the infant. Additionally, it may be prudent for women to avoid taking vitamins that contain vitamin D.There are no adequate and well-controlled studies of paricalcitol in pregnancy (FDA pregnancy category C). Paricalcitol (20 mcg/kg) has been shown to cross the placenta in rats. When administered at the highest dose tested (20 mcg/kg three times per week, 13 times the 14 mcg human dose), there was a significant increase in mortality of newborn rats at doses that were maternally toxic and are known to produce hypercalcemia in rats. Minimal decreases in fetal viability (5%) were seen when paricalcitol was administered at a dose 2 times the 0.24 mcg/kg human dose. Paricalcitol should only be used during pregnancy if the potential benefit justifies the potential risks to the fetus. Based on its pharmacokinetics, paricalcitol probably crosses the human placenta, although exposure to the fetus is expected to be low due to the extensive protein binding and metabolism of paricalcitol. If a pregnant woman is taking paricalcitol, she should not take additional vitamin D or its derivatives; furthermore, paricalcitol doses higher than those recommended by the manufacturer should not taken.The pharmacokinetics of unbound paricalcitol are similar in patients with normal, mild, or moderate hepatic disease. Total paricalcitol serum concentrations are lower in patients with moderate hepatic impairment; however, no dosage adjustment is required in patients with mild or moderate hepatic disease. Because the primary route of elimination of paricalcitol is hepatobiliary, it is prudent to use paricalcitol with caution in patients with severe hepatic disease; information is lacking in this subgroup of patients (see Pharmacokinetics section).The safety and efficacy of intravenous paricalcitol was studied in a 12 week, randomized, placebo-controlled trial of 29 children aged 5—19 years with end-stage renal disease and receiving hemodialysis. Sixty-seven percent of the paricalcitol-treated patients and 14% of the placebo-treated subjects completed the trial. Seventy-one percent of the placebo patients discontinued the study due to excessive elevations in iPTH concentrations (defined as 2 consecutive iPTH concentrations > 700 pg/mL which were greater than baseline after 4 weeks of treatment). In the primary efficacy analysis, 60% of the subjects in the paricalcitol group had 2 consecutive decreases of 30% from baseline iPTH compared to 21% of patients in the placebo group. Twenty-three percent of paricalcitol vs. 31% of placebo patients had at least one serum calcium concentration > 10.3 mg/dL; the overall percentage of serum calcium concentrations > 10.3 mg/dL was 7% in both groups. Forty percent of paricalcitol vs. 14% of placebo subjects had at least one Ca x P ion product > 72 mg2/dL2; the overall percentage of patients with Ca x P product > 72 mg2/dL2 was 8% in the paricalcitol group and 7% in the placebo group. No subjects in either group developed hypercalcemia (defined as at least one calcium serum concentrations > 11.2 mg/dL) during the study. The safety and efficacy of paricalcitol oral capsules in children has not been established.Of the 40 patients receiving intravenous paricalcitol in phase 3 chronic renal failure studies, ten were 65 years of age or older. Of the 220 patients receiving oral paricalcitol in clinical studies, 49% were 65 years or older, and 17% were 75 years or older. No overall differences in efficacy or safety was observed between elderly and younger patients.

    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    There are no adequate and well-controlled studies of paricalcitol use during human pregnancy. Paricalcitol should be used during pregnancy only if the potential benefit to the mother justifies the potential risk to the fetus. Paricalcitol has been shown to cause minimal decreases in fetal viability (5%) when administered daily to rabbits at a dose 0.5-times a human dose of 14 mcg or 0.24 mcg/kg (based on body surface area, mcg/m2), and when administered to rats at a dose 2-times the 0.24 mcg/kg human dose (based on body surface area, mcg/m2). At the highest dose tested, 20 mcg/kg administered 3 times per week in rats (13-times the 14 mcg human dose based on surface area, mcg/m2), there was a significant increase in the mortality of newborn rats at doses that were maternally toxic and are known to produce hypercalcemia in rats. No other effects on offspring development were observed. Paricalcitol was not teratogenic at the doses tested. Paricalcitol (20 mcg/kg) has been shown to cross the placental barrier in rats. Based on its pharmacokinetics, paricalcitol probably crosses the human placenta, although exposure to the fetus is expected to be low due to the extensive protein binding and metabolism of paricalcitol. If a pregnant woman is taking paricalcitol, she should not take additional vitamin D or its derivatives.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    There are no data available on the presence of paricalcitol in human breast milk or the effects of paricalcitol on the breast-fed infant or on milk production. Studies have shown that paricalcitol is present in the milk of lactating rats. Because of the potential for serious adverse reactions, including hypercalcemia in a breast-fed infant, breast-feeding is not recommended during treatment with paricalcitol.

Description
  Text
  Paricalcitol is a synthetic, biologically active analog of calcitriol. Intravenous paricalcitol is indicated for the treatment and prevention of secondary hyperparathyroidism associated with chronic kidney disease (CKD) stage 5 (chronic renal failure requiring dialysis). Oral paricalcitol is indicated for the treatment and prevention of secondary hyperparathyroidism associated with CKD stage 3 and 4 and CKD stage 5 patients requiring dialysis. Vitamin D is a fat-soluble vitamin, which is found in 2 forms, vitamin D3 or calcitriol, and vitamin D2. Both forms of vitamin D have effects on calcium absorption from the intestine and through bone resorption. Paricalcitol is approximately 10-fold less active than calcitriol in promoting resorption of calcium and phosphorus from the bone and does not increase absorption from the GI tract. Paricalcitol decreases parathyroid hormone (PTH) concentrations but has a lower incidence of hypercalcemia and hyperphosphatemia than calcitriol; the relative dosing of paricalcitol to calcitriol is 4:1. Chronic renal failure patients with secondary hyperparathyroidism treated with intravenous paricalcitol for 12 weeks achieved more than a 50% decrease in PTH concentrations. There was no significant change in serum calcium and phosphorus concentrations in the paricalcitol-treated patients compared to placebo. Similarly, 91% of patients treated with oral paricalcitol for 24 weeks achieved 2 consecutive reductions in PTH concentrations of 30% or more relative to baseline; there was no significant difference in the incidence of hypercalcemia or hyperphosphatemia between paricalcitol- and placebo-treated patients.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Paricalcitol reduces parathyroid hormone (PTH) concentrations with only small changes in serum calcium and phosphorus. This unique mechanism of action may be due to a different dose response of paricalcitol and calcitriol on the regulation of intestinal vitamin D receptor (VDR) content, parathyroid gland growth, and bone resorption. In animal studies, calcitriol is effective at suppressing PTH but only at doses which cause significant hypercalcemia and hyperphosphatemia. Paricalcitol, at all doses studied, decreases PTH concentrations with no significant difference in serum ionized calcium or phosphorus concentrations. In additional animal trials, paricalcitol reduced parathyroid gland weight compared to controls while calcitriol had no effect on parathyroid gland growth. Paricalcitol also does not upregulate intestinal VDR content as does calcitriol. Serum phosphorus, calcium, and calcium-phosphorus product may increase during paricalcitol administration; however, there is no significant difference in the incidence of hypercalcemia or hyperphosphatemia when compared to placebo. At higher doses, paricalcitol loses its selectivity for PTH suppression.

  Text
  Paricalcitol is administered orally and intravenously as a bolus infusion. It is highly bound to plasma proteins (>= 99.8%). After administration, it is metabolized, with approximately 2% of the dose eliminated unchanged in the feces, and no parent drug found in the urine. In vitro data suggest that paricalcitol is metabolized by multiple non-hepatic and hepatic enzymes including CYP3A4, UGT1A4, and mitochondrial CYP24. Only one metabolite, which is less active than paricalcitol, has been identified as 24-R-hydroxy paricalcitol. The average half-life is 4 to 6 hours in healthy subjects. The major route of elimination is hepatobiliary with approximately 63% to 70% and 18% to 19% of the dose recovered in feces and urine, respectively. There is no accumulation with repeated administration.
 
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes and drug transporters: CYP24, CYP3A4, UGT1A4
Paricalcitol is partially metabolized by CYP3A4 and CYP24; however, it is not expected to inhibit or induce the clearance of drugs metabolized by the CYP450 isozyme system. Paricalcitol is also a UGT1A4 substrate.

Dosage Adjustment Guidelines
  Hepatic Impairment
  No dosage adjustment is required in patients with mild or moderate hepatic impairment. Paricalcitol use has not been studied in patients with severe hepatic impairment.
  Renal Impairment
  Paricalcitol is indicated for patients with chronic renal failure; therefore, the recommended doses are appropriate.
