          Dosage Description
          9 mg PO once daily, in combination with palbociclib (125 mg PO once daily for 21 days followed by 7 days off every 28 days) and fulvestrant (500 mg IM on cycle 1, days 1 and 15, then 500 mg IM on day 1, every 28 days). Continue all treatments until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity. Treatment with inavolisib plus palbociclib and fulvestrant significantly improved the median progression-free survival (15 months vs. 7.3 months), overall survival (34 months vs. 27 months), and objective response rate (58% vs. 25%) compared with palbociclib, fulvestrant, and placebo in patients with endocrine resistant, PIK3CA-mutated, HR-positive, HER2-negative, locally advanced or metastatic breast cancer with disease progression during or within 12 months of completing adjuvant endocrine therapy and who had not received prior systemic therapy for metastatic disease in a multicenter, randomized, double-blind, phase 3 clinical trial (INAVO120). The median duration of response was 18.4 months versus 9.6 months, respectively.

    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Pregnancy should be avoided by females of reproductive potential during inavolisib treatment and for 1 week after the last dose. Although there are no adequately controlled studies in pregnant females, inavolisib can cause fetal harm or death when administered during pregnancy based on its mechanism of action and animal studies. Individuals who are pregnant or who become pregnant while receiving inavolisib should be apprised of the potential hazard to the fetus. Administration of inavolisib to pregnant rats at doses approximately equivalent to the human exposure at the recommended dose during organogenesis resulted in decreased fetal body weight and placental weight, post-implantation loss, lower fetal viability, fetal malformations (e.g., dilated renal pelvis, fused thoracic arch, kyphosis of vertebral column, microphthalmia, short supernumerary rib, wavy rib).
    Headline
    diabetes mellitus, diabetic ketoacidosis, hyperglycemia
    General Information
    Use inavolisib with caution in people with diabetes mellitus and/or risk factors for hyperglycemia. Severe or fatal hyperglycemia, including diabetic ketoacidosis, can occur with inavolisib treatment. Ketoacidosis with a fatal outcome has occurred in the postmarketing setting. The safety of inavolisib in people with type 1 or type 2 diabetes mellitus requiring ongoing antihyperglycemic treatment has not been studied. Test fasting plasma/blood glucose and hemoglobin A1C (HbA1C) at baseline and optimize levels. Monitor or have individuals self-monitor fasting glucose levels every 3 days for the first week of therapy (days 1 to 7), once weekly for the next 3 weeks (days 8 to 28), once every 2 weeks for the next 8 weeks, and then once every 4 weeks thereafter and as clinically indicated. Test HbA1C at baseline, every 3 months, and as clinically indicated. People with a history of well-controlled type 2 diabetes may require intensified antihyperglycemic treatment and close monitoring. Educate individuals on the signs and symptoms of hyperglycemia and lifestyle changes. An interruption of therapy, dose reduction, or discontinuation of therapy may be necessary depending on the severity of hyperglycemia.
    Headline
    contraception requirements, infertility, male-mediated teratogenicity, pregnancy testing, reproductive risk
    General Information
    Counsel patients about the reproductive risk and contraception requirements during inavolisib treatment. Inavolisib can be teratogenic if taken by the mother during pregnancy. Females of reproductive potential should avoid pregnancy and use effective nonhormonal contraception during and for 1 week after treatment with inavolisib; due to the risk of male-mediated teratogenicity, males with female partners of reproductive potential should also use effective contraception during treatment and for 1 week after the last dose. Females of reproductive potential should undergo pregnancy testing prior to initiation of inavolisib. Individuals who become pregnant while receiving inavolisib should be apprised of the potential hazard to the fetus. Although there are no data regarding the effect of inavolisib on human fertility, male and female infertility has been observed in animal studies.

    Headline

    General Information
    Reduce the dose of inavolisib in patients with moderate or severe renal impairment (eGFR less than 60 mL/min); no dosage adjustment is necessary for patients with mild renal impairment. Data are not available for patients with renal failure.

    diabetic ketoacidosis, hyperglycemia
    General Information
    Severe or fatal hyperglycemia, including diabetic ketoacidosis, can occur in people treated with inavolisib. Diabetic ketoacidosis with a fatal outcome has occurred in the postmarketing setting. Increased fasting glucose/hyperglycemia occurred in 85% of subjects treated with inavolisib (grade 3 or 4, 12.6%); the median time to first onset was 7 days. Higher inavolisib exposure was associated with a higher incidence of grade 2 or higher hyperglycemia. Treatment with oral antihyperglycemic medications was required in 46% of subjects, while 7% required treatment with insulin. Manage hyperglycemia with medications as clinically indicated; continue monitoring fasting glucose levels and hemoglobin A1C if antihyperglycemic medications are started. Consider consultation with a health care team experienced in the management of hyperglycemia. An interruption of therapy, dose reduction, or discontinuation of therapy may be necessary depending on the severity of hyperglycemia.

Description
  Text
  Inavolisib is an inhibitor of phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase (PI3K). It is indicated in combination with palbociclib and fulvestrant for the treatment of adults with endocrine-resistant, PIK3CA-mutated, hormone receptor (HR)-positive, human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2)-negative, locally advanced or metastatic breast cancer following recurrence on or after completing adjuvant endocrine therapy. Inavolisib can cause severe hyperglycemia; monitor blood glucose at baseline and periodically during treatment. Inavolisib was approved by the FDA in October 2024.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Inavolisib is a phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase (PI3K) inhibitor, predominantly targeted against PI3K-alpha. In vitro, inavolisib induced the degradation of mutated PI3K catalytic alpha subunit p110-alpha, which is encoded by the PIK3CA gene, inhibitor phosphorylation of the downstream target AKT, reduced cellular proliferation, and induced apoptosis in PIK3CA-mutated breast cancer cell lines. In vivo, it reduced tumor growth in PIK3CA-mutated, estrogen receptor-positive breast cancer xenograft models. Inavolisib in combination with palbociclib and fulvestrant increased the inhibition of tumor growth compared to each treatment alone or the doublet combinations.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics

    Oral Route
    Description
    The absolute oral bioavailability of inavolisib is 76%. At steady-state, the Cmax of inavolisib was 69 nanograms/mL (CV, 27%) and the AUC was 1,010 hours x nanograms/mL (CV, 25%). The median time to Cmax (Tmax) was 3 hours at steady-state, which was predicted to be attained by day 5. The steady-state AUC of inavolisib was proportional to dose from 6 mg to 12 mg (0.7 to 1.3 times the recommended dose), with accumulation of approximately 2-fold. A high-fat meal (approximately 1,000 calories, 50% fat) did not have a clinically significant effect on the pharmacokinetics of inavolisib.

    Pharmacodynamics
Higher inavolisib exposure was associated with an increased incidence of grade 2 or higher anemia and hyperglycemia, as well as dose modifications due to adverse reactions.
  Text
  Inavolisib is administered orally. It is 37% protein bound; protein binding is not concentration-depending in vitro. The blood-to-plasma ratio of inavolisib is 0.8. Inavolisib has an apparent volume of distribution of 155 liters (CV, 26%). The elimination half-life is 15 hours (CV, 24%) with a total clearance of 8.8 liters/hour (CV, 29%). After oral administration of a single radiolabeled dose, 49% was recovered in urine (unchanged, 40%) and 48% in feces (unchanged, 11%).
 
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes and transporters: None
Inavolisib is primarily metabolized by hydrolysis; in vitro, it was minimally metabolized by CYP3A. In vitro, inavolisib is a P-gp and BCRP substrate; it induces CYP3A and CYP2B6 and is also a time-dependent inhibitor of CYP3A.
