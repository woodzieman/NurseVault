Drug Class: Setmelanotide is a melanocortin 4 (MC4) receptor agonist approved for chronic weight management, including weight reduction and long-term maintenance, in individuals with genetic or hypothalamic causes of obesity.

Routes: injection (IV/IM/SC); Common adult doses: 3 mg/day subcutaneously.

Common Side Effects (excluding routine GI upset): ecchymosis, erythema, injection site reaction, pruritus, alopecia, nail discoloration, rash, skin discoloration, skin hyperpigmentation, striae, xerosis, arthralgia, asthenia, back pain, dizziness, fatigue, headache, insomnia, muscle cramps, musculoskeletal pain, vertigo, chills, cough, fever, infection, influenza, pharyngitis

In an Overdose: No overdose/antidote information is listed in this file.

Key Nursing Considerations: Contraindications/precautions: This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components. Use setmelanotide with caution in individuals with depression and/or suicidal ideation.

    Headline
    depression, suicidal ideation
    General Information
    Use setmelanotide with caution in individuals with depression and/or suicidal ideation. Individuals with a history of severe depression were excluded from setmelanotide clinical trials; however, depression and suicidal ideation were reported. Consider whether the benefits outweigh the risks of treatment in individuals with a history of suicidality or depression. Monitor individuals for depression and suicidal ideation throughout treatment; consider discontinuing setmelanotide if individuals develop suicidal thoughts or behaviors.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Discontinue setmelanotide during pregnancy unless the benefits of therapy outweigh the potential risks to the fetus. Setmelanotide contains 10 mg of benzyl alcohol per mL. Benzyl alcohol is metabolized quickly in pregnant women so fetal exposure is unlikely; however, intravenous benzyl alcohol has caused adverse reactions in premature neonates and low birth weight infants. There are no available data regarding setmelanotide use during pregnancy to inform a drug-associated risk of pregnancy-related outcomes. Animal data did not reveal evidence of harm to the fetus when administered at doses up to 11 times the maximum recommended human dose (MRHD).
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Treatment with setmelanotide is not recommended for use while breast-feeding. Setmelanotide contains 10 mg of benzyl alcohol per mL. Benzyl alcohol is metabolized quickly by a lactating woman so fetal exposure is unlikely; however, intravenous benzyl alcohol has caused adverse reactions in premature neonates and low birth weight infants. There are no data on the presence of setmelanotide in human milk, the effects on a breast-fed infant, or the effects on milk production. However, setmelanotide is present in the milk of rats. Consider the benefits of breast-feeding, the risk of potential infant drug exposure, and the risk of an untreated or inadequately treated condition. If a breast-feeding infant experiences an adverse effect related to a maternally administered drug, healthcare providers are encouraged to report the adverse effect to the FDA.
    Headline
    infants, neonates, premature neonates
    General Information
    Setmelanotide contains 10 mg of benzyl alcohol per mL and should not be used in infants, neonates, or premature neonates. Excessive amounts of benzyl alcohol in neonates and low birth weight infants have caused serious and fatal adverse reactions including "gasping syndrome." A "gasping syndrome" characterized by CNS depression, metabolic acidosis, and gasping respirations has been associated with benzyl alcohol dosages more than 99 mg/kg/day in neonates; however, the minimum amount of benzyl alcohol at which toxicity may occur is unknown.
    Headline
    renal failure, renal impairment
    General Information
    Setmelanotide is not recommended in individuals with end-stage renal failure (eGFR less than 15 mL/minute/1.73 m2). Setmelanotide is not recommended in individuals with severe renal impairment (eGFR 15 to 29 mL/minute/1.73m2) being treated for weight management with acquired hypothalamic obesity (HO). Dosage adjustments are necessary in individuals with severe renal impairment (eGFR 15 to 29 mL/minute/1.73 m2) with BBS or POMC, PCSK1, or LEPR deficiency. Setmelanotide is not recommended for use in pediatric patients 2 to 5 years weighing less than 20 kg with severe renal impairment.
    Headline
    adrenal insufficiency
    General Information
    Monitor for clinical signs of acute adrenal insufficiency in individuals with adrenal insufficiency.  In clinical trials of adult and pediatric patients with acquired hypothalamic obesity and secondary adrenal insufficiency, serious adverse reactions related to acute adrenal insufficiency were reported by 5% of individuals treated with setmelanotide and 0% for individuals treated with placebo.
    Headline
    arginine vasopressin disorder
    General Information
    Monitor serum sodium levels with changes in fluid intake and hydration status during setmelanotide therapy in individuals with central diabetes insipidus/arginine vasopressin disorder (AVP) and acquired hypothalamic obesity (HO). In clinical trials of adult and pediatric patients with acquired HO and AVP deficiency, hyponatremia and hypernatremia were reported. Adjust the doses of concomitant therapies for AVP deficiency as needed.

Description
  Text
  Setmelanotide is a melanocortin 4 (MC4) receptor agonist approved for chronic weight management, including weight reduction and long-term maintenance, in individuals with genetic or hypothalamic causes of obesity. These include acquired hypothalamic obesity (HO), Bardet-Biedl syndrome (BBS), and monogenic obesity due to proopiomelanocortin (POMC), proprotein convertase subtilisin/kexin type 1 (PCSK1), or leptin receptor (LEPR) deficiency confirmed by genetic testing and considered pathogenic, likely pathogenic, or of uncertain significance. Individuals with obesity due to POMC, PCSK1, or LEPR deficiency are severely obese at a young age. They are usually of normal weight at birth but gain excess weight due to a genetic defect that affects their level of hunger, ability to feel full, and energy output. The condition is rare, with only about 150 reported cases. BBS is a rare genetic disorder; symptoms may include obesity, retinal degeneration, reduced kidney function, or extra digits of the hands or feet. Obesity is progressive and usually begins by the time a child is 1 year old. HO refers to excess weight gain that may follow from an injury to the hypothalamus. When the hypothalamus is injured, the balance between how much energy a person takes in and how much they burn is disrupted. The most common adverse reactions reported in trials included injection site reactions, skin hyperpigmentation, headache, and gastrointestinal effects. Spontaneous penile erections in males, adverse sexual reactions in females, depression, and suicidal ideation have also been reported. Setmelanotide was FDA-approved in 2020.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Setmelanotide is a melanocortin 4 (MC4) receptor agonist with 20-fold less activity at the melanocortin 3 (MC3) and melanocortin 1 (MC1) receptors. MC4 receptors are located in the brain and are involved in the regulation of hunger, satiety, and energy expenditure. Setmelanotide may re-establish MC4 receptor pathway activity to reduce hunger and promote weight loss through decreased caloric intake and increased energy expenditure in patients with obesity due to acquired hypothalamic obesity (HO), Bardet-Biedl syndrome (BBS), proopiomelanocortin (POMC), proprotein convertase subtilisin/kexin type 1 (PCSK1), or leptin receptor (LEPR) deficiency associated with insufficient activation of the MC4 receptor. The MC1 receptor is expressed on melanocytes, and activation of this receptor leads to accumulation of melanin and increased skin pigmentation independently of ultraviolet light.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Subcutaneous Route
    Description
    After subcutaneous injection, the Tmax was reached 8 hours after dosing. At steady-state, the mean Cmax, AUC, and trough concentration were 31 ng/mL, 373 hour x ng/mL, and 5 ng/mL, respectively, for a 3 mg once daily dose in adult patients. Steady-state plasma concentrations were achieved within 2 days with daily dosing of 1 to 3 mg. Accumulation in the systemic circulation during once daily dosing over 12 weeks was approximately 30%. Setmelanotide AUC and Cmax increased proportionally after multiple-dose administration in the dosing range of 1 to 3 mg.
  Special Populations
    Name
    Renal Impairment
    Description
    AUC was approximately 13% to 15%, 34% to 35%, and 86% to 96% higher for patients with mild, moderate, and several renal impairment, respectively, compared to patients with normal renal function. Renal impairment did not appear to affect plasma protein binding; the average fraction unbound was approximately 0.2 and was independent of renal function.
    Name
    Pediatrics
    Description
    Simulations were performed using population pharmacokinetic analysis for pediatric patients 2 to 5 years receiving maximum recommended doses of 0.5 mg, 1 mg, 1.5 mg, and 2 mg for body weight groups of 15 to 19 kg, 20 to 29 kg, 30 to 39 kg, and 40 kg or more, respectively. The AUC and Cmax were 17% and 14% lower in patients weighing 15 to 19 kg, 24% and 38% higher in patients weighing 20 to 29 kg, 45% and 63% higher in patients weighing 30 to 39 kg, and 52% and 75% higher in patients weighing 40 kg or more, compared to adults. For patients 6 to 11 years, the AUC and Cmax were 88% and 89% higher compared to adults. For patients 12 to 17 years, the AUC and Cmax were both 26% higher compared to adults.
    Name
    Geriatric

  Text
  Setmelanotide is administered by subcutaneous injection. Plasma protein binding is 79.1% with an estimated apparent volume of distribution of 75.2 L after administration of setmelanotide 3 mg subcutaneously once daily. Setmelanotide is expected to be metabolized into small peptides by catabolic pathways. Approximately 39% of the 3 mg daily dose of setmelanotide is excreted unchanged into the urine. The clearance is estimated to be 7.15 L/hour for a typical adult male weighing 120 kg. The elimination half-life is approximately 11 hours.
 
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes and drug transporters: none
