Drug Class: Obinutuzumab is a CD20-directed cytolytic antibody.

Routes: injection (IV/IM/SC); Common adult doses: 1000 mg/dose IV.

Common Side Effects (excluding routine GI upset): elevated hepatic enzymes, hyperbilirubinemia, hypoalbuminemia, agranulocytosis, anemia, leukopenia, lymphopenia, neutropenia, thrombocytopenia, hyperkalemia, hypernatremia, hypocalcemia, hypokalemia, hyponatremia, hypophosphatemia, arthralgia, back pain, bone pain, musculoskeletal pain, infection, pharyngitis, rhinitis, sinusitis, gi perforation, pruritus, rash, urticaria, asthenia, fatigue, lethargy

In an Overdose: No overdose/antidote information is listed in this file.

Key Nursing Considerations: Contraindications/precautions: This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components. Obinutuzumab is contraindicated in patients with a known hypersensitivity or anaphylaxis to obinutuzumab, or serum sickness with prior use. Emetic risk: Minimal

          Dosage Description
          1,000 mg IV on Days 1, 8, and 15 of cycle 1; 1,000 mg IV on Day 1 of cycles 2, 3, 4, 5, and 6; and 1,000 mg IV every 8 weeks for up to 20 doses in combination with zanubrutinib 160 mg orally twice daily OR 320 mg orally once daily until disease progression. The complete response rate was also significantly higher in the combination therapy arm compared with the obinutuzumab alone arm (39% and 19%; p-value = 0.004). At a median follow-up time of 20.2 months, the median duration of response was not reached in the combination therapy arm and 14 months in the obinutuzumab alone arm. Although the OS improvement was not significant in the combination therapy arm, 35 of 72 subjects (48.6%) from the obinutuzumab only arm had crossed over. Subjects (median age, 64 years; range, 31 to 88 years) in this study had received a median of 3 (range, 2 to 11) prior lines of therapy that included an anti-CD20 antibody and an alkylator-based combination therapy; 21% of subjects had received a previous stem-cell transplantation.

Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  Contraindications And Precautions
    Headline
    Drug Interactions
    General Information
    The coadministration of certain medications may lead to harm and require avoidance or therapy modification; review all drug interactions prior to concomitant use of other medications.
    Headline
    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components. Obinutuzumab is contraindicated in patients with a known hypersensitivity or anaphylaxis to obinutuzumab, or serum sickness with prior use. Severe and life-threatening infusion-related reactions and hypersensitivity reactions have been reported with obinutuzumab therapy; late-onset hypersensitivity diagnosed as serum sickness has also occurred.
    Headline
    General Information
    General Information
    Immunization with live virus vaccines is not recommended during obinutuzumab treatment and until B-cell recovery. The safety and efficacy of immunization with live or attenuated viral vaccines during or following obinutuzumab therapy have not been studied.
    Headline
    hepatitis B exacerbation
    General Information
    Hepatitis B virus (HBV) reactivation resulting in hepatitis B exacerbation, fulminant hepatitis, hepatic failure, and death may occur during or following treatment with an anti-CD20 antibody, such as obinutuzumab. Screen all patients for HBV (e.g., hepatitis B surface antigen, hepatitis B core antibody) prior to starting obinutuzumab therapy. In patients who have evidence of HBV, consult with an expert in hepatitis management for monitoring and treatment recommendations. Monitor all patients with a history of prior hepatitis B during and for several months after obinutuzumab therapy for signs and symptoms of HBV reactivation. Discontinue therapy in patients who develop HBV reactivation and initiate treatment. The safety of resuming obinutuzumab therapy following HBV reactivation is not known.
    Headline
    progressive multifocal leukoencephalopathy
    General Information
    Progressive multifocal leukoencephalopathy (PML) resulting in death has been observed in individuals treated with obinutuzumab for chronic lymphocytic leukemia and non-Hodgkin lymphoma. Suspect PML in any individual presenting with new onset or changes to pre-existing neurological symptoms and perform an appropriate evaluation such as a neurology consultation, brain MRI, and lumbar puncture. Discontinue obinutuzumab in individuals who develop PML and consider discontinuing or reducing concomitant chemotherapy or immunosuppressive therapy.
    Headline
    cardiac disease, hypertension, pulmonary disease
    General Information
    Use obinutuzumab with caution in patients with pre-existing cardiac disease or pulmonary disease; severe and life-threatening infusion-related reactions and hypersensitivity reactions have been reported. Patients with pre-existing cardiac or pulmonary disease may be at greater risk of more severe infusion-related reactions; monitor these patients more frequently during and after the infusion. In patients with pre-existing hypertension, consider withholding antihypertensive medications for 12 hours prior to, during, and for the first hour after each infusion until blood pressure is stable; weigh the risk versus benefit of withholding antihypertensive medications in patients at high risk for hypertensive crisis. If a hypersensitivity reaction is suspected, hold the infusion; do not resume treatment in patients who have hypersensitivity reactions including serum sickness. Manage symptoms as appropriate with supportive care measures (e.g., glucocorticoids, epinephrine, bronchodilators, and/or oxygen).
    Headline
    infection
    General Information
    Do not administer obinutuzumab to individuals with an active infection. Serious and fatal bacterial infection, fungal infection, and viral infection have been reported during and after treatment with obinutuzumab. Individuals with a history of recurring or chronic infections may be at increased risk for developing a serious infection. Consider interrupting obinutuzumab treatment in individuals who develop an infection. Immediately discontinue obinutuzumab and institute appropriate treatment in individuals who develop a serious infection while receiving obinutuzumab. Consider the risk and benefit of resuming treatment with obinutuzumab after resolution of serious infections. Provide antimicrobial prophylaxis to individuals who experience grade 3 or 4 neutropenia lasting more than 1 week until the neutropenia resolves to grade 2 or less. Consider antiviral and antifungal prophylaxis in individuals with severe and prolonged (longer than 1 week) neutropenia.
    Headline
    history of significant adverse event(s) associated with previous use
    General Information
    Obinutuzumab is contraindicated in patients with a history of significant adverse event(s) associated with previous use (e.g., known hypersensitivity or anaphylaxis to obinutuzumab, or serum sickness with prior use). Severe and life-threatening infusion-related reactions and hypersensitivity reactions have been reported with obinutuzumab therapy; late-onset hypersensitivity diagnosed as serum sickness has also occurred.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Based on its mechanism of action and findings from animal studies, fetal B-cell depletion may occur if obinutuzumab is administered during pregnancy. There are no data on the use of obinutuzumab in human pregnancy to inform a drug-associated risk. Monoclonal antibodies are transferred across the placenta. No embryotoxic or teratogenic effects were observed in animal studies at exposures up to 2.4 times the clinical dose exposure in humans. Individuals with systemic lupus erythematosus are at increased risk of adverse outcomes during pregnancy including worsening of underlying disease, premature birth, miscarriage, and intrauterine growth restriction. Maternal lupus nephritis increases the risk of hypertension and preeclampsia/eclampsia. The transfer of monoclonal antibodies across the placenta may result in adverse neonatal outcomes (i.e., neonatal lupus and congenital heart block).
    Headline
    people who may become pregnant, reproductive risk
    General Information
    Counsel people who may become pregnant about the reproductive risk associated with obinutuzumab. Obinutuzumab may cause fetal harm. Advise people who may become pregnant to use effective contraception while taking obinutuzumab and for 6 months after the last dose.
    Headline
    neonates and infants exposed to this medication in utero
    General Information
    Fetal B-cell depletion is likely to occur when obinutuzumab is used during pregnancy. Avoid administration of live vaccines to neonates and infants exposed to this medication in utero until B-cell recovery occurs. Obinutuzumab was detected in the offspring of cynomolgus monkeys on day 28 postpartum, and B-cells were completely depleted. Immunologic function was restored and B-cell counts returned to normal concentrations within 6 months after birth.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in the breastfed child, avoid breast-feeding during obinutuzumab therapy and for 6 months after the last dose. There are no data on the presence of obinutuzumab in human milk, its effects on the breastfed child, or its effect on milk production. Low concentrations of obinutuzumab were present in animal milk (0.04% to 0.13% of concentrations in maternal serum). Human IgG is secreted in human milk.

    Hepatitis B virus (HBV) reactivation/hepatitis B exacerbation resulting in fulminant hepatitis, hepatic failure, and death may occur during or following treatment with an anti-CD20 antibody, such as obinutuzumab. Monitor patients who have had HBV for signs and symptoms of HBV reactivation during and for several months after obinutuzumab therapy. Discontinue obinutuzumab therapy (and concomitant chemotherapy if applicable) in patients who develop HBV reactivation and start appropriate treatment. There is insufficient data regarding the safety of resuming obinutuzumab in patients who had a reactivation of HBV during therapy.

    General Information
    Fatal and severe disseminated intravascular coagulation (DIC) was reported in 0.3% of obinutuzumab-treated subjects with chronic lymphocytic leukemia and non-Hodgkin lymphoma; all events occurred within 1 to 2 days after the first infusion. The majority of cases involved changes in platelet counts and coagulation parameters after the first infusion, with spontaneous resolution by day 8. Evaluate potential causes in individuals with suspected DIC and monitor coagulation parameters, platelet counts, and for signs and symptoms of bleeding or thrombosis. Manage according to standard guidelines for DIC; supportive care including transfusion of blood products and other medical management may be necessary.

Description
  Text
  Obinutuzumab is a CD20-directed cytolytic antibody. It is a monoclonal antibody that binds to the CD20 molecule found on pre B- and mature B-lymphocytes. Obinutuzumab is indicated for the treatment of previously untreated chronic lymphocytic leukemia (CLL) in combination with chlorambucil, for treating relapsed or refractory follicular lymphoma (FL) in combination with bendamustine, and for previously untreated bulky stage II, stage III, or IV FL in combination with chemotherapy. Individuals with FL who respond to combination therapy may also receive up to 2 years of obinutuzumab monotherapy. Obinutuzumab is also indicated for active lupus nephritis in individuals receiving standard therapy. Obinutuzumab helps improve kidney function, increases the likelihood of achieving a complete renal response, lowers the risk of flares, and reduces proteinuria in people with lupus nephritis. In a phase 3 trial, obinutuzumab was evaluated in adults with active systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE) without proliferative or membranous lupus nephritis, and appears to be effective when used off-label for SLE along with standard therapy. When added to standard therapy, obinutuzumab achieved a significantly higher Week 52 SLE Responder Index 4 (SRI-4) response than placebo (76.7% vs. 53.5%; p less than 0.001) with additional improvements across secondary end points. Hepatitis B virus reactivation resulting in fulminant hepatitis, hepatic failure, and death have been reported with obinutuzumab. Obinutuzumab was FDA-approved in November 2013.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Obinutuzumab is a humanized monoclonal antibody derived from the murine Bly-1 antibody that binds specifically to the CD20 antigen on pre B- and mature B-lymphocytes. Obinutuzumab is glycoengineered, which increases CD16A binding by 7 to 10 fold, improving antibody dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC). Anti-CD20 monoclonal antibodies have two subtypes: type I and type II agents. Type I agents (rituximab-like) redistribute CD20 into membrane lipid rafts and have high complement dependent cytotoxicity, while type II agents (tositumomab-like) do not activate complement and cause direct, programmed cell death. Obinutuzumab is a type II anti-CD20 antibody, indicating that cell death is nonapoptotic, independent of caspase activity, and correlated with homotypic adhesion. After binding to CD20, obinutuzumab causes redistribution of the actin cytoskeleton which triggers enlargement of the lysosomal compartment. This results in lysosomal membrane permeabilization (LMP), causing cathepsin B release into the cytosol and nonapoptotic cell death. This mechanism is independent of BCL-2 and caspase activation, which potentially circumvents resistance to chemotherapy-induced apoptosis. In summary, obinutuzumab causes lysis of B cells by engaging immune effector cell mechanisms (antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity and antibody-dependent cellular phagocytosis), activating intracellular death signaling pathways, and to a lesser extent, activation of the complement cascade.
Pharmacodynamics
CLL
Obinutuzumab causes CD19 B-cell depletion (CD19 B-cell counts less than 0.07 x 109 cells/L). Initial recovery occurred at approximately 9 months after the last obinutuzumab dose in some individuals; at 18 months of follow-up, B-cell depletion persists in some individuals. B-cell depletion in the peripheral blood is not directly correlated with B-cell depletion in solid organs or malignancies, and has not been directly correlated to clinical response.
Lupus Nephritis (LN)
In individuals with LN, treatment with obinutuzumab resulted in rapid and sustained B-cell depletion, with total peripheral CD19+ B-cell counts falling below 10 cells/mcL in 99% of subjects by Week 4 and remaining below this threshold in 95% at Week 76. Reductions in naive B cells, memory B cells, and plasmablasts/plasma cells were observed by Week 4 and persisted through Week 76. Treatment also led to early and sustained improvements in complement (C3 and C4) and anti-double-stranded DNA (anti-dsDNA) antibody concentrations. By Week 76, normalization of C3 and C4 occurred in 62% and 88% of individuals receiving obinutuzumab, respectively, compared with 29% and 55% in the placebo group; 56% of obinutuzumab-treated subjects seroconverted for anti-dsDNA compared with 16% in the placebo group. The clinical relevance of these pharmacodynamic changes has not been established.

  Text
  Obinutuzumab is given as an intravenous infusion. It has both linear and non-linear (time-dependent) clearance pathways; the impact of non-linear clearance becomes less significant as treatment continues. In chronic lymphocytic leukemia (CLL) and non-Hodgkin-lymphoma (NHL), the reduction in non-linear clearance over time is consistent with target-mediated drug disposition and CD20 target saturation. In lupus nephritis (LN), the decrease may also reflect reduced CD20 expression and improvement in proteinuria with treatment. Following obinutuzumab therapy in individuals with CLL or NHL, the geometric mean volume of distribution (Vd) at steady-state was approximately 4.1 to 4.3 L, clearance ranged from 0.08 to 0.11 L/day, and the terminal half-life was 25.5 to 35.3 days, based on population pharmacokinetic analyzes (coefficient of variance: Vd 20% to 21%, clearance 41% to 53%, and half-life 35% to 48%). Similar parameters were observed in individuals with lupus nephritis with a steady-state clearance of approximately 0.13 L/day and a median elimination half-life of 22.4 days.

Intermittent Infusion
Administer only as an intravenous infusion through a dedicated line; do not mix with other drugs.
Closely monitor individuals during the infusion for signs or symptoms of an infusion-related reaction; stop the infusion if an individual develops a reaction and institute medical management as needed.
If a grade 3 infusion-related reaction occurs, upon resolution of symptoms, consider restarting obinutuzumab infusion at no more than one-half the previous rate (the rate being used at the time that the infusion-related reaction occurred), and if not further infusion-related reaction symptoms, infusion rate escalation may resume at the increments and intervals as appropriate for the treatment dose. Permanently discontinue treatment if individuals experience a grade 3 or higher infusion-related reaction at rechallenge.
If a grade 4 (life-threatening) infusion-related reaction occurs, stop infusion immediately and permanently discontinue obinutuzumab.
