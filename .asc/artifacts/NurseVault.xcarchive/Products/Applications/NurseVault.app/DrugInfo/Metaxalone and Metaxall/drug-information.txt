Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  General Information
  According to the manufacturer, metaxalone is contraindicated in patients with significantly impaired renal or hepatic function such as renal failure, significant renal disease, renal impairment or hepatic dysfunction (hepatic disease). Great care is advised if metaxalone is used in a patient with preexisting liver damage; perform serial liver function studies in these patients.
 
Metaxalone is contraindicated in patients with a known tendency for drug-induced, hemolytic anemia, or other anemias.
 
Metaxalone is a CNS depressant. Patients should be cautioned about driving or operating machinery until the effects of the drug are known.
 
Metaxalone is not advised for use in the elderly due to the potential for anticholinergic side effects, sedation, and weakness. Elderly patients may be especially susceptible to an enhanced CNS depressant effect of metaxalone if it is taken with food (see Interactions and Pharmacokinetics). Also, the effectiveness of metaxalone at doses tolerated in the geriatric population is questionable.
 
No FDA pregnancy category is available for metaxalone. Reproduction studies in rats revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or harm to the fetus due to metaxalone. Adequate and well-controlled studies have not been conducted in pregnant women. Metaxalone should only be used during pregnancy if the potential benefits outweigh the possible risk to the fetus.
 
According to the manufacturer, as a general rule, breast-feeding should not be undertaken while a patient is on a drug since many drugs are excreted in human milk. It is not known if metaxalone is distributed into human milk. Consider the benefits of breast-feeding, the risk of potential infant drug exposure, and the risk of an untreated or inadequately treated condition. If a breast-feeding infant experiences an adverse effect related to a maternally ingested drug, healthcare providers are encouraged to report the adverse effect to the FDA.
 
Safety and effectiveness of metaxalone have not been established in children 12 years of age or younger.
 
False positive Benedict's test results (urine glucose test) have been noted among patients taking metaxalone. The false positive result is due to an unknown reducing substance. Use of a glucose-specific test is advised to confirm or reject false hyperglycemia.

    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components.
    Headline

    General Information
    Metaxalone is contraindicated in individuals with severe hepatic impairment, including hepatic failure (Child-Pugh class C). Use metaxalone with caution and consider additional follow-up in individuals with mild or moderate hepatic impairment.
    Headline
    anemia
    General Information
    Metaxalone is contraindicated in individuals with a known tendency for anemia, including drug-induced anemia and hemolytic anemia.
    Headline
    activities requiring coordination and concentration, driving or operating machinery
    General Information
    Advise individuals against driving or operating machinery or doing any activities requiring coordination and concentration until the effects of metaxalone are known. Metaxalone can cause sedation, which may interfere with everyday activity. Use metaxalone with caution in combination with ethanol ingestion or other CNS depressants.
    Headline
    geriatric
    General Information
    The Beers Criteria identifies skeletal muscle relaxants, such as metaxalone, as potentially inappropriate for geriatric adults. This is due to poor tolerance, potential anticholinergic effects, sedation, increased fracture risk, and questionable effectiveness at dosages that older adults can tolerate. Geriatric adults may also be especially susceptible to an enhanced CNS depressant effect of metaxalone if the drug is taken with food.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Metaxalone should only be used during pregnancy, particularly during early pregnancy, if the potential benefits outweigh the risks. Reproduction studies in rats have not revealed evidence of impaired fertility or harm to the fetus due to metaxalone. Postmarketing experience has not revealed evidence of fetal injury, but such experience is limited and cannot exclude the possibility of infrequent or subtle damage to the human fetus. Safe use of metaxalone has not been established with regard to possible adverse effects upon fetal development.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Use caution when administering metaxalone during breast-feeding. It is not known whether metaxalone is excreted in human breast milk. If this medication must be used during lactation, monitor the infant for sedation, gastrointestinal upset, and vomiting.

  Adverse Reactions
  General Information
  The most frequent reactions to metaxalone include nausea/vomiting, GI upset, drowsiness, dizziness, headache, and anxiety or irritability. Description
  Text
  Metaxalone is an oral CNS depressant used as adjunct therapy for acute, painful musculoskeletal conditions. Although the drug is classified as a skeletal muscle relaxant, it does not directly relax skeletal muscle. Most of the beneficial effects of metaxalone are thought to be due to its sedative properties. Metaxalone does not effectively treat skeletal muscle hyperactivity secondary to chronic neurologic disorders (e.g., cerebral palsy). Metaxalone was FDA-approved in 1962.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  The mechanism of action of metaxalone in humans has not been established, but may be due to general central nervous system (CNS) depression. Metaxalone has no direct action on the contractile mechanism of striated muscle, the motor end plate, or the nerve fiber.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Oral Route
    Description
    In healthy adult volunteers, a 400 mg dose of metaxalone reaches mean peak plasma concentrations in about 3 hours (Tmax) when given on an empty stomach and in about 4 hours when given with food. Administering 400 mg with a high-fat meal increases the mean Cmax and AUC to 177.5% and 123.5%, respectively. The terminal half-life is approximately 9 hours, decreasing to 2.4 hours when given with a high-fat meal. Increasing the dose to 800 mg in healthy volunteers resulted in a Cmax and systemic exposure roughly double that seen with the 400 mg dose. Dose proportionality at doses above 800 mg has not been evaluated. In healthy volunteers receiving 800 mg with a high-fat meal, the mean Cmax and AUC increase by 193.6% and 146.4%, respectively, compared to fasting conditions; the Tmax with a high-fat meal is delayed to 4.9 hours vs. 3 hours under fasting conditions. The terminal half-life was also decreased from 8 hours to about 4 hours.

    Description
    Metaxalone bioavailability appears to be higher in females compared to males when comparing Cmax and AUC values, and the mean half-life is longer in females than in males (11.1 hours vs. 7.6 hours, respectively). The apparent volume of distribution is higher in males compared to females, but when adjusted for body weight, the difference was not significant.
  Text
  Metaxalone is administered orally. Protein binding and absolute bioavailability are not known. However, based on apparent volume of distribution (V/F approximately 800 L) and lipophilicity (log P = 2.42), it appears metaxalone is extensively distributed throughout body tissues. Metabolism occurs in the liver. Excretion is via the urine as unidentified metabolites. The half-life of metaxalone is approximately 9 +/- 4.8 hours.
 
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes (Metaxalone): CYP1A2, CYP2C8, CYP2C9, CYP2C19, CYP2D6, CYP2E1, CYP3A4
