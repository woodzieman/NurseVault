Drug Class: Vimseltinib is an oral switch-control colony stimulating factor 1 receptor (CSF1R) kinase inhibitor approved for adult patients with symptomatic tenosynovial giant cell tumor (TGCT) for which surgical resection will potentially cause worsening functional limitation or severe morbidity.

Routes: oral; Common adult doses: 30 mg PO twice weekly.

Common Side Effects (excluding routine GI upset): elevated hepatic enzymes, hepatotoxicity, hypercholesterolemia, blurred vision, epiphora, xerophthalmia, elevated creatine kinase, hypermagnesemia, hypocalcemia

In an Overdose: No overdose/antidote information is listed in this file.

          Dosage Description
          30 mg PO twice weekly, with a minimum of 72 hours between doses, until disease progression. Coadministration of certain drugs may need to be avoided or dosage adjustments may be necessary; review drug interactions. In a phase 3 clinical trial (MOTION), patients were randomized to vimseltinib or placebo during the double-blind period for 24 weeks; during the open-label period, patients could continue vimseltinib and those receiving placebo could crossover to vimseltinib. Overall response rate at week 25 was 40% (complete response, 5%) versus 0% in the vimseltinib arm compared with placebo. Median duration of response (DOR) was not reached in the vimseltinib arm and based on an additional 18 months of follow-up, 85% of responders had a DOR of 6 months or greater and 73% had a DOR of 12 months or greater.

    Headline
    Drug Interactions
    General Information
    The coadministration of certain medications may lead to harm and require avoidance or therapy modification; review all drug interactions prior to concomitant use of other medications.
    Headline
    biliary tract disease, hepatic disease
    General Information
    Avoid vimseltinib in patients with pre-existing increased serum transaminases, total bilirubin or direct bilirubin (greater than the upper limit of normal), or active hepatic disease or biliary tract disease, including alkaline phosphatase (ALP). Monitor liver tests, including AST, ALT, total bilirubin, direct bilirubin, ALP, and gamma-glutamyl transferase, prior to initiation of vimseltinib, twice a month for the first 2 months and then once every 3 months for the first year of therapy, and as clinically indicated thereafter. An interruption of therapy, dose reduction, or discontinuation of therapy may be necessary based on the severity of hepatotoxicity.
    Headline
    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components. Because vimseltinib contains tartrazine dye (FD&C yellow no. 5), patients with tartrazine dye hypersensitivity who take vimseltinib may be at risk for developing allergic reactions (including bronchial asthma). Tartrazine sensitivity is frequently seen in patients who also have aspirin sensitivity. Vimseltinib also contains sunset yellow FCF (FD&C yellow no. 6), which may cause allergic reactions.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Pregnancy should be avoided by people who may become pregnant during vimseltinib treatment and for at least 1 month after the last dose. Although there are no adequately controlled studies in people who may become pregnant, vimseltinib can cause fetal harm or death when administered during pregnancy based on its mechanism of action and animal studies. In pregnant rats, fetal structural abnormalities occurred at exposures that were at least 3 times the recommended human dose. Individuals who are pregnant or who may become pregnant while receiving vimseltinib should be apprised of the potential hazard to the fetus.
    Headline
    people who can cause pregnancy in others, people who may become pregnant, reproductive risk
    General Information
    Counsel people about the reproductive risk associated with vimseltinib. People who may become pregnant should avoid pregnancy and use effective contraception during and for 1 month after treatment with vimseltinib. People who can cause pregnancy in others should use effective contraception during and for 1 month after treatment with vimseltinib. People who may become pregnant should undergo pregnancy testing prior to initiation of vimseltinib. Individuals who become pregnant while receiving vimseltinib should be apprised of the potential hazard to the fetus. Although there are no data regarding the effect of vimseltinib on human fertility, male and female infertility has been observed in animal studies.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in nursing infants from vimseltinib, advise individuals to discontinue breast-feeding during treatment and for 1 month after the final dose. It is not known whether vimseltinib is present in human milk, although many drugs are excreted in human milk.

Afatinib: (Moderate) If the concomitant use of vimseltinib and afatinib is necessary, monitor for afatinib-related adverse reactions. If the original dose of afatinib is not tolerated, consider reducing the daily dose of afatinib by 10 mg; resume the previous dose of afatinib as tolerated after discontinuation of vimseltinib. The manufacturer of afatinib recommends permanent discontinuation of therapy for severe or intolerant adverse drug reactions at a dose of 20 mg per day, but does not address a minimum dose otherwise. Administration with another P-gp inhibitor, given 1 hour before a single dose of afatinib, increased afatinib exposure by 48%; there was no change in afatinib exposure when the P-gp inhibitor was administered at the same time as afatinib or 6 hours later. In healthy subjects, the relative bioavailability for AUC of afatinib was 119% when coadministered with the same P-gp inhibitor, and 111% when the inhibitor was administered 6 hours after afatinib.

Description
  Text
  Vimseltinib is an oral switch-control colony stimulating factor 1 receptor (CSF1R) kinase inhibitor approved for adult patients with symptomatic tenosynovial giant cell tumor (TGCT) for which surgical resection will potentially cause worsening functional limitation or severe morbidity. Serious and fatal liver injury has occurred with other CSF1R kinase inhibitors; routine liver function monitoring is required. Vimseltinib was approved by the FDA in February 2025.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Vimseltinib is an oral switch-control kinase inhibitor specifically designed to inhibit colony stimulating factor 1 receptor (CSF1R). Tenosynovial giant cell tumor is a locally aggressive neoplasm of the joint or tendon sheath caused by dysregulation of the colony-stimulating factor 1 (CSF1) gene, leading to overproduction of CSF1. In vitro, vimseltinib inhibited CSF1R autophosphorylation, signaling induced by CSF1 ligand binding, and proliferation of cells expressing CSF1R.

  Text
  Vimseltinib is administered orally. It is 96.5% bound to human plasma protein and the mean volume of distribution is 90 liters (CV, 16%). The mean elimination half-life is approximately 6 days (CV, 32%) with a mean clearance of 0.5 liters/hour (CV, 23%). Approximately 43% of a single radiolabeled dose of vimseltinib was recovered in the feces (9.1% unchanged) and 38% in the urine (5.1% unchanged).
Pharmacodynamics: Higher vimseltinib exposure is associated with an increased risk of all grades of edema, rash, increased AST, and increased ALT.
 
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes and drug transporters: BCRP, OCT2, P-gp
Vimseltinib is primarily metabolized by oxidation, N-demethylation, and N-dealkylation. Vimseltinib is an inhibitor of BCRP, OCT2, and P-gp; avoid concomitant use with BCRP, OCT2, and P-gp substrates. If concomitant use of vimseltinib with P-gp substrates cannot be avoided, vimseltinib should be taken at least 4 hours prior to P-gp substrates. Dabigatran (a P-gp substrate) AUC and Cmax are predicted to increase 2- to 3-fold with concomitant vimseltinib use; the predicted increase may be up to 1.3-fold if administered 4 hours after vimseltinib administration. In vitro, vimseltinib is a P-gp substrate and inhibits P-gp, BCRP, BSEP, OATP1B1, OATP1B3, OCT2, BSEP, MATE1, and MATE2-K. Vimseltinib may increase serum creatinine by decreasing renal tubular secretion of creatinine; this may occur due to inhibition of renal transporters OCT2 and MATE1 and may not affect renal function.

Dosage Adjustment Guidelines
  Hepatic Impairment
  Baseline Hepatic Impairment
Avoid vimseltinib in patients with pre-existing increased serum transaminases, total bilirubin or direct bilirubin (greater than upper limit of normal), or active liver or biliary tract disease, including alkaline phosphatase.
Recommended Dose Reductions
First dose reduction: 20 mg PO twice weekly.
Second dose reduction: 14 mg PO twice weekly. Permanently discontinue in patients who are unable to tolerate 14 mg PO twice weekly.
Management of Treatment-Related Hepatotoxicity
AST and/or ALT increase greater than 3 to 5 times upper limit of normal (ULN) and total bilirubin increase up to 2 times ULN OR total bilirubin increase up to 2 times ULN: Hold vimseltinib until AST and ALT resolve to baseline or 3 times ULN or less and bilirubin resolves to baseline; resume at the next lower dose level once Hy's law have been definitively ruled out. Permanently discontinue if adverse reaction does not resolve within 4 weeks.
AST and/or ALT increase greater than 3 to 5 times ULN and total bilirubin increase greater than 2 times ULN or INR greater than 1.5 and ALP less than 2 times ULN OR total bilirubin increase greater than 2 times ULN: Hold vimseltinib until AST and ALT resolve to baseline or 3 times ULN or less and bilirubin resolves to baseline; resume at the next lower dose level once Hy's law has been definitively ruled out.
