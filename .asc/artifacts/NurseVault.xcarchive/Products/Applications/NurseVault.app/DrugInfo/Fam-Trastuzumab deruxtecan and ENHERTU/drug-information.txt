Drug Class: Fam-trastuzumab deruxtecan is a HER2-targeted antibody-drug conjugate (ADC) that contains the humanized anti-HER2 IgG1, trastuzumab, covalently linked to the topoisomerase I inhibitor drug, DXd (a derivative of exatecan) by a stable protease-cleavable maleimide tetrapeptide linker.

Routes: intravenous; Common adult doses: 6.4 mg/kg IV

In an Overdose: No overdose/antidote information is listed in this file.

    Headline
    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components.
    Headline
    interstitial lung disease, pulmonary disease
    General Information
    Use fam-trastuzumab deruxtecan with caution in patients who have a history of pre-existing pulmonary disease; severe pneumonitis/interstitial lung disease has been reported in patients treated with fam-trastuzumab deruxtecan. Monitor patients for new or worsening respiratory symptoms including fever, cough, or dyspnea. Immediately interrupt fam-trastuzumab deruxtecan therapy in patients with new or worsening respiratory symptoms. A dose reduction, interruption of therapy, or permanent discontinuation of therapy may be necessary depending on the severity.
    Headline
    coronary artery disease, heart failure, hypertension, thoracic radiation therapy
    General Information
    Use fam-trastuzumab deruxtecan with caution in patients with a history of heart failure, or in patients with conditions that could impair left ventricular function including coronary artery disease, uncontrolled hypertension, thoracic radiation therapy and previous anthracycline exposure. Decreased left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) has occurred in patients treated with fam-trastuzumab deruxtecan in clinical trials. Treatment with fam-trastuzumab deruxtecan has not been studied in patients with a history of clinically significant cardiac disease or LVEF less than 50% prior to initiation of treatment. Assess LVEF prior to initiation of therapy and at regular intervals during treatment as clinically indicated; an interruption or discontinuation of therapy may be necessary for decreases in LVEF. Permanently discontinue fam-trastuzumab deruxtecan in patients with symptomatic congestive heart failure (CHF).
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Pregnancy should be avoided by females of reproductive potential during fam-trastuzumab deruxtecan treatment and for at least 7 months after the last dose. Although there are no adequately controlled studies in pregnant animals or humans, fam-trastuzumab deruxtecan can cause fetal harm or death when administered during pregnancy based on its mechanism of action. Women who are pregnant or who become pregnant while receiving fam-trastuzumab deruxtecan should be apprised of the potential hazard to the fetus. If a woman receives fam-trastuzumab deruxtecan while pregnant or becomes pregnant within 7 months of receiving fam-trastuzumab deruxtecan, inform her of the fetal risks and monitor for oligohydramnios. In postmarketing reports, oligohydramnios (manifesting as pulmonary hypoplasia, skeletal abnormalities, and neonatal death) occurred in pregnant women who received trastuzumab. In addition, DXd, the cytotoxic component of fam-trastuzumab deruxtecan, can cause embryo-fetal toxicity because it is genotoxic and targets actively dividing cells.
    Headline
    Child-Pugh class B, Child-Pugh class C, hepatic failure
    General Information
    Closely monitor patients with moderate hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh class B; total bilirubin greater than 1.5 to 3 times the upper limit of normal [ULN] and any AST) for an increase in the incidence and severity of treatment-related adverse reactions due to increased exposure to the topoisomerase inhibitor, DXd. The recommended dosage of fam-trastuzumab deruxtecan has not been established for patients with severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh class C; total bilirubin greater than 3 times ULN and any AST) or hepatic failure.
    Headline
    contraception requirements, infertility, male-mediated teratogenicity, pregnancy testing, reproductive risk
    General Information
    Counsel patients about the reproductive risk and contraception requirements during fam-trastuzumab deruxtecan treatment. Fam-trastuzumab deruxtecan can be teratogenic if taken by the mother during pregnancy. Females of reproductive potential should avoid pregnancy and use effective contraception during and for 7 months after treatment with fam-trastuzumab deruxtecan. Due to the risk of male-mediated teratogenicity, males with female partners of reproductive potential should use effective contraception during and for 4 months after treatment with fam-trastuzumab deruxtecan. Females of reproductive potential should undergo pregnancy testing prior to initiation of fam-trastuzumab deruxtecan. Women who become pregnant while receiving fam-trastuzumab deruxtecan should be apprised of the potential hazard to the fetus. Although there are no data regarding the effect of fam-trastuzumab deruxtecan on human fertility, male infertility has been observed in animal studies.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in nursing infants from fam-trastuzumab deruxtecan, advise women to discontinue breast-feeding during treatment and for 7 months after the final dose. It is not known whether fam-trastuzumab deruxtecan is present in human milk, although many drugs are excreted in human milk.
    Headline
    renal failure, renal impairment
    General Information
    Monitor patients with moderate renal impairment (CrCl 30 to 59 mL/min) for signs and symptoms of interstitial lung disease (ILD)/pneumonitis; a higher incidence of ILD/pneumonitis has been reported in these patients. The recommended dose of fam-trastuzumab deruxtecan has not been established for patients with severe renal impairment (CrCl less than 30 mL/min) or renal failure.

  Adverse Reactions
    Headline
    cough, dyspnea, interstitial lung disease, pneumonitis, pulmonary fibrosis
    General Information
    Interstitial lung disease (ILD)/pneumonitis, including fatal cases, has occurred with fam-trastuzumab deruxtecan treatment. Higher systemic exposure was associated with an increased incidence of ILD, while an increased incidence of grade 1 or 2 ILD/pneumonitis was observed in patients with moderate renal impairment. In clinical trials, ILD occurred in 4.4% to 17% of patients treated with fam-trastuzumab deruxtecan as monotherapy, in combination with pertuzumab, or followed sequentially by taxane/trastuzumab/pertuzumab (THP) therapy. The median time to onset was reported to range from 2.7 to 5.5 months for patients receiving monotherapy (including monotherapy followed by THP) and 8 months for patients receiving fam-trastuzumab deruxtecan 5.4 mg/kg in combination with pertuzumab. Severe (grade 3 or 4) pneumonitis was reported in 2.6% or fewer patients across individual trials. Adjudicated ILD events included alveolitis, bronchiectasis, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, lung opacity, lymphangitis, organizing pneumonia, pulmonary fibrosis, pulmonary mass, radiation pneumonitis/pneumonitis, and respiratory failure. Radiation pneumonitis occurred with the same incidence in patients with residual invasive breast cancer after neoadjuvant therapy who received adjuvant fam-trastuzumab deruxtecan or ado-trastuzumab emtansine with radiotherapy (31% vs. 31%); the incidence was higher for patients who received sequential versus concurrent radiation, irrespective of the treatment arm (sequential, 34% vs. 37%; concurrent, 29% vs. 27%). For patients treated with fam-trastuzumab deruxtecan, the median time to onset was 4.1 months. Cough was reported in 20% or fewer patients (grade 3 or 4, 0.4% or less), and dyspnea in 4.5% to 13% (grade 3 or 4, 1.7% or less) of patients treated with fam-trastuzumab deruxtecan at the 5.4 mg/kg dosage. Promptly investigate suspected ILD/pneumonitis with radiographic imaging and consider pulmonology consultation; an interruption of therapy, treatment with systemic corticosteroids, or discontinuation of therapy may be necessary depending on the severity of confirmed ILD.

    Headline
    leukopenia, lymphopenia, neutropenia
    General Information
    Neutropenia, leukopenia, and lymphopenia have been reported in patients treated with fam-trastuzumab deruxtecan. Decreases from baseline in neutrophil occurred in 65% to 79% of patients treated with fam-trastuzumab as monotherapy or in combination with pertuzumab across clinical trials; grade 3 or 4 decreases from baseline were reported in 19% of patients receiving monotherapy at the 5.4 mg/kg dose level, in 29% of patients at the 5.4 mg/kg dose level in combination with pertuzumab, and in 51% of patients receiving monotherapy at the 6.4 mg/kg dose level. The median time to decreased neutrophil counts was 16 to 22 days. Febrile neutropenia occurred in 0.5% to 4.8% of patients treated with fam-trastuzumab deruxtecan. Decreases from baseline in leukocytes (60% to 87%; grade 3 or 4, 4% to 29%) and lymphocytes (43% to 70%; grade 3 or 4, 14% to 35%) were also reported in patients who received fam-trastuzumab deruxtecan. An interruption of therapy, dose reduction, or discontinuation of therapy may be necessary depending on the severity of myelosuppression.

Description
  Text
  Fam-trastuzumab deruxtecan is a HER2-targeted antibody-drug conjugate (ADC) that contains the humanized anti-HER2 IgG1, trastuzumab, covalently linked to the topoisomerase I inhibitor drug, DXd (a derivative of exatecan) by a stable protease-cleavable maleimide tetrapeptide linker. It is indicated as monotherapy for the neoadjuvant or adjuvant treatment of certain types of HER2-positive, HER2-low, and HER2-ultralow breast cancer as well as previously treated advanced HER2-positive non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), HER2-positive gastric or gastroesophageal junction (GEJ) cancer, and other HER2-positive solid tumors. Fam-trastuzumab deruxtecan is also indicated in combination with pertuzumab as first-line treatment of unresectable or metastatic HER2-positive breast cancer. Cases of interstitial lung disease and pneumonitis have been reported with fam-trastuzumab deruxtecan treatment; use with caution in patients with a history of lung disease. Do not substitute fam-trastuzumab deruxtecan for or with other trastuzumab-based products. Fam-trastuzumab deruxtecan was approved by the FDA in 2019.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Fam-trastuzumab deruxtecan is a HER2-targeted antibody-drug conjugate. Trastuzumab, a humanized anti-HER2 IgG1 antibody, is covalently linked to the topoisomerase I inhibitor DXd, which is an exatecan derivative. Following binding to HER2 on tumor cells, fam-trastuzumab deruxtecan-nxki undergoes internalization and cleavage of the tetrapeptide-based linker by lysosomal enzymes. Upon release, the membrane-permeable DXd causes DNA damage and apoptotic cell death.

Do not freeze. Discard any unused reconstituted fam-trastuzumab deruxtecan after 24 hours in the refrigerator.
 
Dilution
Fam-trastuzumab deruxtecan is compatible with infusion bags made of polyvinylchloride (PVC) or polyolefin (copolymer of ethylene and polypropylene).
Calculate the appropriate dose/volume of fam-trastuzumab deruxtecan and add to an IV bag containing 100 mL of Dextrose 5% Injection; do not dilute with Sodium Chloride Injection.
Mix the diluted solution by gentle inversion; do not shake.
Cover the infusion bag to protect from light.
Storage after dilution: Store at room temperature (20 to 25 degrees C; 68 to 77 degrees F) for up to 4 hours including preparation and infusion, or refrigerated (2 to 8 degrees C; 36 to 46 degrees F) for up to 24 hours, protected from light. The maximum time from reconstitution of the vial to the end of infusion should not exceed 24 hours. Do not freeze.
 
Intermittent IV Infusion
Allow diluted solutions that were stored in the refrigerator to warm to room temperature prior to administration.
Use an infusion set made of polyolefin or polybutadiene and a 0.2 or 0.22 micron in-line polyethersulfone or polysulfone filter.
First infusion: Administer IV over 90 minutes; slow or interrupt the infusion if the patient develops an infusion-related reaction. Permanently discontinue fam-trastuzumab deruxtecan therapy if a life-threatening infusion-related reaction occurs.
Subsequent infusions: Administer IV over 30 minutes if prior infusions were well tolerated; slow or interrupt the infusion if the patient develops an infusion-related reaction; permanently discontinue fam-trastuzumab deruxtecan therapy if a life-threatening infusion-related reaction occurs.

Dosage Adjustment Guidelines
  Hepatic Impairment
  Baseline Hepatic Impairment
Mild hepatic impairment (total bilirubin within normal limits and any AST above the upper limit of normal (ULN); or total bilirubin 1.1 to 1.5 times ULN and any AST): No dose adjustment is recommended.
Moderate hepatic impairment (total bilirubin 1.6 to 3 times ULN and any AST): No dose adjustment is recommended. Closely monitor for increased toxicities.
Severe hepatic impairment (total bilirubin 3.1 to 10 times ULN and any AST): Dosage not established.
  Renal Impairment
  Baseline Renal Impairment
Mild (CrCl 60 to 89 mL/min) or moderate (CrCl 30 to 59 mL/min) renal impairment: No dose adjustment is recommended.
Severe renal impairment (CrCl less than 30 mL/min): Dosage not established.
