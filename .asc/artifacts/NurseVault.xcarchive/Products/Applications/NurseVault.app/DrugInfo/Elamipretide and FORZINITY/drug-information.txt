    Headline
    neonates, premature neonates
    General Information
    Elamipretide is not approved for use in neonates. Elamipretide contains 20 mg of benzyl alcohol per mL. Serious adverse reactions, some fatal, have occurred in low-birth weight neonates (less than 2.5 kg) and premature neonates (gestational age younger than 34 weeks) who receive benzyl alcohol-containing medications intravenously. These reactions include new onset or worsening metabolic acidosis that progresses to neurotoxicity and, in some cases, gasping syndrome. Gasping syndrome is a life-threatening condition caused by benzyl alcohol toxicity and is primarily characterized by multiorgan dysfunction secondary to metabolic acidosis, which leads to gasping respirations and death. The minimum amount of benzyl alcohol at which these serious adverse reactions may occur is not known.
    Headline
    renal failure, renal impairment
    General Information
    Elamipretide is eliminated renally. Dose reduction of elamipretide is recommended in adults with severe renal impairment (eGFR less than 30 mL/minute) who are not on dialysis. Elamipretide has not been studied in adults with renal failure on dialysis. There is insufficient information to recommend a dosage regimen in pediatric patients weighing 30 kg or more with renal impairment.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Barth syndrome is not likely to affect females; therefore, there are no data on elamipretide during pregnancy to evaluate for a drug-related risk of major birth defects, miscarriage, or other adverse maternal or fetal outcomes. In animal reproduction studies, no adverse developmental outcomes occurred at any dose tested (range: 4 to 10 times the clinical exposure at the maximum recommended human dose (MRHD)). Elamipretide contains benzyl alcohol as a preservative. Benzyl alcohol is rapidly metabolized by a pregnant individual and benzyl alcohol exposure in the fetus is unlikely.

Adverse Reactions
  Headline
  Adverse Reactions
  Adverse Reactions
    Headline
    anaphylactoid reactions, cough, rash
    General Information
    Hypersensitivity reactions, including anaphylactoid reactions, have been reported with elamipretide administration. Reactions include rash, papular lesions, eczematous dermatitis, and cough. Reactions have occurred within minutes of administration up to months after treatment initiation. Monitor individuals for signs and symptoms of hypersensitivity reactions during treatment. If a reaction occurs, do not administer further doses of elamipretide and institute appropriate emergency treatment, including epinephrine, antihistamines, and corticosteroids. Individuals who experience a reaction should not be rechallenged with elamipretide. For mild to moderate skin reactions, consider treatment with corticosteroids and oral antihistamines. Discontinue elamipretide if persistent or severe skin reactions develop.

Description
  Text
  Elamipretide is a mitochondrial cardiolipin binder indicated to improve muscle strength in individuals with Barth syndrome. Barth syndrome is a genetic condition caused by mutations in the Tafazzin (TAZ) gene, which is involved in cardiolipin remodeling, and has an X-linked inheritance pattern mainly affecting males. Abnormal cardiolipin resulting from TAZ dysfunction results in mitochondrial deficiency. Symptoms include an enlarged heart, low blood cell count, muscle weakness, and fatigue. In a 12-week randomized, double-blind study and a long-term extension period of up to 192 weeks, individuals who received elamipretide showed improvement in the knee extensor muscle strength during the extension phase. Elamipretide was not superior to placebo for the study's primary endpoints (distance walked during 6-minute walk test and Total Fatigue Score on the Barth syndrome Symptom Assessment). The most common adverse reactions were injection site reactions. Elamipretide was FDA-approved in September 2025.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Elamipretide is a mitochondrial cardiolipin binder designed to treat mitochondrial dysfunction by binding to cardiolipin (CL) on the inner mitochondrial membrane. Cardiolipin is a critical phospholipid that maintains mitochondrial structure and electron transport chain function. As a result, elamipretide's interaction with cardiolipin helps stabilize the mitochondrial structure, decrease oxidative stress, and improve ATP production, collectively targeting critical components of mitochondrial dysfunction.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Subcutaneous Route
    Description
    Elamipretide exposure increases proportionally over a dose range of 2 to 80 mg after daily subcutaneous injections with minimal accumulation. After subcutaneous administration, maximum elamipretide concentrations were reached between 0.5 and 1 hour. The absolute bioavailability after subcutaneous administration is approximately 92%. Elamipretide exposure is comparable after subcutaneous injection to the thigh or to the abdomen.

    Hepatic Impairment

    Hepatic impairment is not expected to alter the pharmacokinetics of elamipretide.
    Name
    Renal Impairment
    Description
    Elamipretide AUC increased by 39% in individuals with creatinine clearance 60 to 89 mL/minute, 75% in individuals with creatinine clearance 30 to 59 mL/minute, and 125% in those with creatinine clearance less than 30 mL/minute not on dialysis. Accumulation of elamipretide was minimal regardless of the severity of renal impairment. There was a significant increase in exposure of the metabolites M1 and M2, up to 280% and 640%, in individuals with severe renal impairment not on dialysis (creatinine clearance less than 30 mL/minute).
  Text
  Elamipretide is administered subcutaneously. Approximately 39% binds to plasma protein. Elamipretide is distributed throughout total body water with an approximate Vd of 0.5 L/kg. It is metabolized via sequential C-terminal degradation to the M1 tripeptide and M2 dipeptide metabolites; neither metabolite has pharmacological activity. Elamipretide and its metabolites are excreted in the urine. At 48 hours after a dose, approximately 100% of the elamipretide dose was recovered in the urine as either elamipretide, M1, or M2 in individuals with normal renal function.
 
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes and drug transporters: MATE1
Elamipretide is an inhibitor of MATE1. Elamipretide does not inhibit CYP1A, CYP2D6, CYP2E1, CYP2B6, CYP2C8, CYP2C9, CYP2C19, CYP3A, OCT2, BCRP, OAT1, OAT3, OATP1B1, OATP1B3, P-gp, or MATE2-K. Elamipretide does not induce metabolism by CYP1A2, CYP2B6, or CYP3A4.

  Route Specific Administrations
    Administration Details
      Name
      Subcutaneous Administration
      Description
      Adult patients or caregivers may administer elamipretide after proper training.
Administer subcutaneously into the abdomen (at least 2 inches from the navel) or outer thigh.
Rotate the injection site daily.
Do not administer where the skin is tender, bruised, red, or hard. Avoid injecting into scars or stretch marks.
Do not mix with other products in the same syringe.
Missed dose: If you miss a dose, skip the dose and take the next dose of elamipretide at the scheduled time. Do not take a double dose of elamipretide.
Storage: Discard an open vial 8 days after opening. The opened vial can be stored either refrigerated between 2 and 8 degrees C (36 and 46 degrees F) or at room temperature between 20 and 25 degrees C (68 and 77 degrees F). Do not freeze.
