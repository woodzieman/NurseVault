    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Although no adverse effects are expected, there is no available data regarding pegunigalsidase alfa use during pregnancy to inform a drug associated risk of adverse maternal or fetal outcomes. Animal studies in pregnant rats did not result in adverse effects on embryo-fetal development during organogenesis at plasma exposures up to 3.6 times the maximum recommended human dose (MRHD) of pegunigalsidase alfa. During organogenesis in pregnant rabbits, maternal toxicity, maternal mortality, decreased body weight, and decreased feed consumption was observed at doses greater than 3.2 times the MRHD. Embryo-fetal effects such as abortion, increased late resorption, number of does with resorption, and increased post-implantation loss were reported at plasma exposures 6.5 times the MHRD. Decreased rabbit fetal body weight was observed at exposures greater than 3.2 times the MRHD. There is a pregnancy exposure registry that monitors outcomes in pregnant patients exposed to pegunigalsidase alfa; information about the registry can be obtained at https://chiesirarediseases.com/contact-us/medicalinformation-form or by calling 1-888-661-9260.

    Headline
    serious hypersensitivity reactions or anaphylaxis
    General Information
    Serious hypersensitivity reactions or anaphylaxis have been reported in individuals receiving pegunigalsidase alfa. Consider pretreatment with antihistamines, antipyretics, and/or corticosteroids prior to infusions. In clinical trials, 14% of pegunigalsidase alfa-treated individuals experienced hypersensitivity reactions. Anaphylaxis occurred within 5 to 40 minutes of infusion; signs and symptoms included headache, nausea, vomiting, throat tightness, facial and oral edema, truncal rash, tachycardia, hypotension, rigors, urticaria, intense pruritus, moderate upper airway obstructions, macroglossia, and mild lip edema. The risk of hypersensitivity reactions may be increased in individuals with antidrug antibodies (ADAs) from previous enzyme replacement therapy (ERT). Consider monitoring individuals who show signs and symptoms of hypersensitivity for the presence of IgG and IgE ADA. For mild to moderate hypersensitivity reactions, consider temporarily holding the infusion for 15 to 30 minutes or slowing the infusion rate by 25% to 50% until symptoms resolve. Initiate appropriate medical treatment. If symptoms subside after holding the infusion, resume infusion at 25% to 50% reduced rate as tolerated. If symptoms subside after slowing the infusion, complete infusion at the reduced rate as tolerated. If symptoms persist, discontinue the infusion, monitor the patient, and consider re-initiating within 7 to 14 days at 25% to 50% of the previous rate. Starting with the next infusion, increase the infusion rate by 25% every third infusion as tolerated until the infusion rate at which the reaction occurred is reached. For severe hypersensitivity reactions, discontinue pegunigalsidase alfa infusion immediately and provide appropriate treatment. Consider the risk and benefits of re-challenging treatment and use slower infusion rates to re-challenge until the patient can tolerate the infusion. If the patient tolerates the infusion, the rate may be increased to reach the recommended rate. Individuals with a severe hypersensitivity reaction may require desensitization measures.

    General Information
    During clinical trials, membranoproliferative glomerulonephritis was reported in a patient treated with pegunigalsidase alfa which led to a decline in renal function and treatment discontinuation. Renal function improved slowly following discontinuation; however, renal function did not return to baseline upon trial conclusion.     During an open label clinical trial, 29% of individuals treated with pegunigalsidase alfa experienced one or more infusion-related reactions. In a randomized, double-blind, active-controlled trial, 32% of individuals experienced infusion-related reactions in both pegunigalsidase alfa (n=52) and the control group, agalsidase beta (n=25). Infusion-related reactions during randomized active-controlled clinical trials included abdominal pain (12% vs. 0%, control), agitation, burning sensation, chest pain (non-cardiac), chills, diarrhea (19% vs. 24%, control), fatigue (17% vs. 16% control), flushing, increased body temperature, malaise, nausea (17% vs. 12 %, control), neuralgia (8% vs. 0%, control), throat irritation, pruritic rash, and vomiting. Infusion site extravasation occurred in 6% of individuals treated with pegunigalsidase alfa, compared to 0% from treatment with agalsidase beta. Individuals with pre-existing IgG anti-drug antibodies (ADA) from prior enzyme replacement therapy and individuals who develop ADAs from pegunigalsidase alfa use are at increased risk for infusion related reactions during pegunigalsidase alfa treatment. Prior to infusion, pretreatment with antihistamines, antipyretics, and/or corticosteroids may be used to reduce risk of infusion-related reactions, however, infusion-related reactions can still occur in pre-treated individuals. Individuals with advanced Fabry disease may have compromised cardiac function, which may predispose them to a higher risk of severe complications from infusion-related reactions; monitor cardiac function closely in these individuals and for infusion-related reactions.

    General Information
    Serious hypersensitivity reactions or anaphylaxis have been reported in 14% of patients treated with pegunigalsidase. During a randomized double-blind active-control trial, hypersensitivity, which included macular rash, pruritic rash, and face swelling occurred in 9% of patients treated with pegunigalsidase compared to 16% of patients treated with agalsidase beta.     Headline
    antibody formation
    General Information
    Pre-existing anti-drug antibodies (ADA) from prior enzyme replacement therapy (ERT) can cross-react with pegunigalsidase alfa and increase the risk of hypersensitivity and infusion related reactions. In clinical studies, infusion related reactions with pegunigalsidase alfa treatment occurred more frequently in patients that were ADA positive (51%) than those who were ADA negative (16%). Of the patients that experienced serious hypersensitivity reactions during the first pegunigalsidase alfa infusion and had pre-infusion samples, all but 1 had pre-existing IgE ADAs and all tested positive for IgE ADAs at the time of the reaction. Previous antibody formation from prior ERT and/or higher ADA titers may reduce the plasma concentrations and efficacy of pegunigalsidase alfa. In an open label trial, 3 previously negative patients developed new ADAs during pegunigalsidase alfa treatment, of which 2 patients experienced transient decreases in plasma pegunigalsidase alfa concentrations. In clinical trials, baseline and post-treatment plasma lyso-GL-3 metabolites were higher in pegunigalsidase treated patients who were positive for ADA, than in patients who were negative for ADA. During clinical trials, 94% of patients with previous ERT and positive ADA remained positive after switching to pegunigalsidase alfa, and 17% had boosted ADA titers during pegunigalsidase alfa treatment. Consider monitoring clinical or pharmacodynamic responses (e.g., plasma lyso-Gb3 levels) when switching from agalsidase beta to pegunigalsidase alfa.

Description
  Text
  Pegunigalsidase alfa is a hydrolytic lysosomal neutral glycosphingolipid-specific enzyme given by intravenous infusion for the treatment of confirmed Fabry disease in adults. Patients with Fabry disease have a deficiency of the lysosomal enzyme, ceramidetrihexosidase or alpha-galactosidase A, which breaks down glycosphingolipids (predominantly globotriaosylceramide or GL-3). Glycosphingolipids accumulate in the lining of blood vessels in the heart, kidney, and other organs in patients without an adequate presence of alpha-galactosidase A. Crises of severe pain in the extremities (acroparesthesias), hypohidrosis, corneal opacities, and dysfunction of several organs (kidney, brain, heart) are the primary manifestations, and patients often have decreased life expectancy and experience renal failure, cardiomyopathy, and cerebrovascular accidents. Replacement of the deficient enzyme may reduce concentrations of the accumulated glycolipids in various tissues and attenuate disease symptoms. Efficacy of pegunigalsidase alfa was demonstrated in a randomized double-blind active-control trial with agalsidase beta, where pegunigalsidase alfa treatment (n = 52) showed non-inferior efficacy through an estimated mean eGFR slope of -2.4 mL/minute/1.73m2/year, compared to -2.3 mL/minute/1.73m2/year with agalsidase beta treatment (n = 25). In an open-label trial, adults treated with pegunigalsidase alfa for 26 weeks (n = 14) had a mean change of -3.1 (-4.8 to -1.4, 95% CI) in renal biopsy BLISS score of GL-3 inclusions from baseline. Anaphylaxis and severe infusion-associated reactions have been reported with pegunigalsidase alfa. The most common adverse drug reactions are infusion-associated reactions, nasopharyngitis, headache, diarrhea, fatigue, nausea, and pain.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Pegunigalsidase alfa provides an exogenous source of the enzyme alpha-galactosidase A, which is deficient in patients with Fabry disease. Alpha-galactosidase is transported into lysosomes where it will catalyze the hydrolysis of globotriaosylceramide and reduce its accumulation within cells.

    Following intravenous infusion of 1 mg/kg pegunigalsidase alfa every 2 weeks, mean Cmax and AUC increases were seen with longer durations of treatment. In adult ERT-naive patients, the mean Cmax increased from 11.9 mcg/mL (+/- 2.4 mcg/mL) at 13 weeks to 17.3 mcg/mL (+/- 6.1 mcg/mL) at 52 weeks. Immunogenicity
The presence of treatment emergent anti-drug antibodies (ADAs) and preexisting ADAs appeared to reduce plasma concentrations of pegunigalsidase alfa in an inversely proportional manner. During clinical trials, 5 patients developed ADAs or had preexisting ADA following treatment and appeared to have lower plasma concentrations of pegunigalsidase alfa. Lower plasma concentration of pegunigalsidase alfa may result in reduced efficacy.
  Text
  Pegunigalsidase alfa is administered by intravenous infusion. At 13 weeks of intravenous infusion, the mean terminal volume of distribution was 271 mL/kg (+/- 89 mL/kg) at 13 weeks and decreased with longer duration of treatment to 186 mL/kg (+/- 91 mL/kg) at 52 weeks of treatment. Pegunigalsidase alfa is expected to be metabolized into small peptides via catabolic pathways. The mean elimination half-life of pegunigalsidase alfa increased with longer duration of treatment from 85.7 hours (+/- 28.4 hours) at 13 weeks to 121 hours (+/- 22 hours) at 52 weeks. The mean total body clearance decreased from 2.3 mL/hour/kg (+/- 0.8 mL/hour/kg) at 13 weeks to 1.1 mL/hour/kg (+/- 0.7 mL/hour/kg) at 52 weeks.

Hypersensitivity/Infusion Reaction Pre-treatment
In enzyme replacement therapy (ERT)-experienced patients with previous pre-treatment using antihistamines, antipyretics, and/or corticosteroids, consider initiation of the same pre-treatment.
After 4 to 6 infusions, stepwise decrease or discontinuation of pretreatment may be considered if pegunigalsidase alfa is tolerated.
In ERT- naive patients, consider pre-treatment with antihistamines, antipyretics, and/or corticosteroids.
Appropriate medical support measures including cardiopulmonary resuscitation equipment should be readily available during pegunigalsidase alfa administration.
Hypersensitivity/Infusion Reaction Administration Modifications
Severe hypersensitivity infusion-related reaction:
Discontinue pegunigalsidase immediately and initiate appropriate medical treatment
Mild to moderate hypersensitivity infusion-related reaction:
May temporarily hold infusion for 15 to 30 minutes and initiate appropriate medical treatment, if needed. If symptoms subside, resume infusion rate at a 25% to 50% reduced rate, as tolerated, with appropriate pretreatment. Infusion rates can be increased by 25% every third infusion until the previous rate has been reached, starting with the next infusion
May slow infusion rate by 25% to 50% and initiate appropriate medical treatment, if needed. If symptoms subside, complete infusion at the reduced rate as tolerated. Infusion rates can be increased by 25% every third infusion until the previous rate has been reached, starting with the next infusion.
If symptoms persists despite intervention, discontinue infusion and monitor patients. Infusion may be reinitiated after pretreatment within 7 to 14 days at 25% to 50% of the rate at which the reaction occurred with appropriate pretreatment.

      Dilution
Use aseptic technique when preparing pegunigalsidase alfa.
Determine the number of vials needed based on actual body weight and round the number of vials up to the next whole number.
Remove the vials from the refrigerator and allow them to sit at room temperature [20 to 25 degrees C (68 to 77 degrees F)] for 15 to 30 minutes before use. Do not use external heat sources.
Dilute pegunigalsidase alfa in 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection to a total volume based on actual body weight:
Actual body weight greater than 100 kg: 500 mL
Actual body weight 70 to 100 kg: 250 mL
Actual body weight less than 70 kg: 150 mL
Prior to adding the amount of pegunigalsidase alfa required for a dose to 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection, remove the equal volume of 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection from the infusion bag.
Withdraw the volume of pegunigalsidase alfa required for the dose from the vials; discard any unused solution remaining in the vial.
Inject pegunigalsidase alfa directly into the 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection solution through the port on the side of the infusion bag. Do not inject pegunigalsidase into the airspace within the infusion bag.
Mix by gentle inversion. Do not shake.
Storage after dilution: If the diluted pegunigalsidase solution is not used immediately, it may be stored refrigerated between 2 and 8 degrees C (36 and 46 degrees F) for up to 24 hours or at room temperature [20 to 25 degrees C (68 to 77 degrees F)] for up to 8 hours. Once at room temperature, the solution must be used within 8 hours, inclusive of infusion time, or discarded. Do not freeze or shake.
 
Intravenous Infusion:
Administer using a sterile, low protein-binding, 0.2 micron, in-line filter.
For initial 4 to 6 infusions in enzyme replacement therapy (ERT) naive patients, infuse at:
Actual body weight greater than 100 kg: 1.38 mL/minute (83 mL/h)
Actual body weight 70 to 100 kg: 1 mL/minute (60 mL/h)
Actual body weight less than 70 kg: 0.63 mL/minute (37.5 mL/h)
For the initial 4 to 6 infusions in ERT experienced patients, infusion at:
Actual body weight greater than 100 kg: 2.78 mL/minute (167 mL/h)
Actual body weight 70 to 100 kg: 1.39 mL/minute (83 mL/h)
Actual body weight less than 70 kg: 0.83 mL/minute (50 mL/h)
For the initial 4 to 6 infusions for patients with previous ERT infusion over 3 hours:
Use the same infusion rate from previous ERT for pegunigalsidase alfa.
After the initial 4 to 6 infusions, duration of every third infusion may be decreased by 30 minutes as tolerated. The minimum recommended infusion duration is 1.5 hours (based on patient tolerance). In the event of infusion-associated reactions or hypersensitivity, infusion rates may be slowed or temporarily stopped.
Home administration should only be considered for patients who have reached an infusion duration that is tolerated well. For infusions given at home, duration of infusion should only be decreased in a healthcare facility.
At the end of the infusion, flush the line with 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection using the same infusion rate.
Do not infuse other drugs through the same infusion line.
