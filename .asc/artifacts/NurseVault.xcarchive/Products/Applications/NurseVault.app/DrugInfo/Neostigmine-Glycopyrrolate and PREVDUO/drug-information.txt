Drug Class: Neostigmine; glycopyrrolate is an intravenous, fixed-dose combination of a cholinesterase inhibitor and antimuscarinic agent indicated in adults and pediatric patients 2 years and older for the reversal of the effects of nondepolarizing neuromuscular blocking agents (NMBAs) after surgery, while...

Routes: injection (IV/IM/SC); Common adult doses: 0.07 mg/kg (Max: 5 mg) IV.

Common Side Effects (excluding routine GI upset): confusion, diaphoresis, dizziness, drowsiness, dysarthria, headache, insomnia, loss of consciousness, oligohidrosis, seizures, shivering, blurred vision, cycloplegia, miosis, mydriasis, photophobia, av block, bradycardia, cardiac arrest, hypertension, hypotension, palpitations, qt prolongation, sinus tachycardia, syncope, ventricular fibrillation, ventricular tachycardia, bronchial secretions, bronchospasm, cholinergic crisis

In an Overdose: Cholinergic crisis may result from neostigmine overdosage; it is characterized by increasing muscle weakness, and through involvement of the respiratory muscles, may result in death.

Key Nursing Considerations: Contraindications/precautions: This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components. Glycopyrrolate is contraindicated in individuals with glaucoma because it may cause mydriasis and increased intraocular pressure.

    Headline
    activities requiring coordination and concentration, driving or operating machinery, glaucoma
    General Information
    Glycopyrrolate is contraindicated in individuals with glaucoma because it may cause mydriasis and increased intraocular pressure. Advise individuals with glaucoma to promptly seek medical care if they experience symptoms of acute angle closure glaucoma. Glycopyrrolate may also cause drowsiness or blurred vision. Advise individuals to avoid driving or operating machinery or participating in other activities requiring coordination and concentration until these issues are resolved.
    Headline
    cardiac arrhythmias, coronary artery disease, heart failure, hemorrhagic shock, hypertension, hyperthyroidism
    General Information
    Neostigmine; glycopyrrolate is contraindicated in individuals with unstable cardiovascular status in the setting of acute hemorrhagic shock. Use neostigmine; glycopyrrolate with caution in individuals with coronary artery disease (including recent acute coronary syndrome), cardiac arrhythmias, heart failure, hypertension, or hyperthyroidism. Blood pressure and heart rate complications may be increased in these individuals. Consider administration of glycopyrrolate prior to neostigmine as separate products in individuals with bradycardia or individuals in whom bradycardia, a known risk of neostigmine, may cause hemodynamic instability. Assess any tachycardia before administering neostigmine; glycopyrrolate, as glycopyrrolate may further increase heart rate.
    Headline
    colostomy, GI obstruction, ileostomy, peritonitis, toxic megacolon, ulcerative colitis
    General Information
    Neostigmine; glycopyrrolate is contraindicated in individuals with GI obstruction (e.g., achalasia, pyloroduodenal stenosis, paralytic ileus, intestinal atony), peritonitis, severe ulcerative colitis, or toxic megacolon complicating ulcerative colitis. Diarrhea may be an early symptom of incomplete intestinal obstruction, especially in individuals with ileostomy or colostomy; avoid glycopyrrolate use in individuals with these conditions due to risk for harm.
    Headline
    hepatic failure
    General Information
    Carefully monitor individuals with hepatic impairment or hepatic failure if a hepatically cleared neuromuscular blocking agent was used during their surgical procedure as the duration of action may be prolonged by hepatic insufficiency, whereas neostigmine, which undergoes renal elimination, will not likely be affected. This could result in the effects of the neuromuscular blocking agent outlasting those of neostigmine. This same situation may arise if the neuromuscular blocking agent has active metabolites. The interval for redosing the neuromuscular blocking agent during the surgical procedure may be useful in determining whether, and to what extent, post-operative monitoring needs to be extended.
    Headline
    renal failure, renal impairment
    General Information
    Closely monitor individuals with renal impairment or renal failure to assure the effects of the neuromuscular blocking agent, particularly a neuromuscular blocking agent that is cleared by the kidneys, do not persist beyond those of neostigmine and glycopyrrolate. The interval for redosing the neuromuscular blocking agent during the surgical procedure may be useful in determining whether, and to what extent, post-operative monitoring needs to be extended. Neostigmine elimination half-life is prolonged in anephric individuals, and the renal elimination of glycopyrrolate may be severely impaired in individuals with renal failure.
    Headline
    urinary tract obstruction
    General Information
    Neostigmine; glycopyrrolate is contraindicated in individuals with obstructive uropathy or urinary tract obstruction, such as bladder neck obstruction due to prostatic hypertrophy.
    Headline
    myasthenia gravis
    General Information
    Neostigmine; glycopyrrolate is contraindicated in individuals with myasthenia gravis. Blood pressure and heart rate complications may be increased in individuals with myasthenia gravis.
    Headline
    Down syndrome
    General Information
    Individuals with Down syndrome may exhibit increased sensitivity to anticholinergic agents, which may increase the risk of adverse effects with glycopyrrolate.
    Headline
    conditions contributing to an elevated core body temperature
    General Information
    Glycopyrrolate may cause heat prostration due to decreased sweating in the presence of conditions contributing to an elevated core body temperature (e.g., fever, high environmental temperature, or physical exercise), particularly in children and the elderly. Advise individuals to avoid physical exertion and exposure to high environmental temperatures after receiving neostigmine; glycopyrrolate.
    Headline
    brain injury, children, infants, neonates, spastic paralysis
    General Information
    Neostigmine; glycopyrrolate is not recommended in neonates, infants, and children younger than 2 years because blood pressure in pediatric populations, particularly infants and neonates, is sensitive to changes in heart rate. In these individuals, a formulation that allows glycopyrrolate to be administered prior to neostigmine to reduce the likelihood of bradycardia and hypotension is recommended. Recovery of neuromuscular activity occurs more rapidly with smaller doses of cholinesterase inhibitors in infants and children than in adults. However, infants and small children may be at greater risk of complications from incomplete reversal of neuromuscular blockade due to decreased respiratory reserve. The risks associated with incomplete reversal outweigh any risk from giving higher doses of neostigmine. Additionally, infants and any pediatric population with spastic paralysis or brain injury may also exhibit increased sensitivity to anticholinergic agents, which may increase the risk of adverse effects with glycopyrrolate. Infants and young children are especially susceptible to the toxic effects of anticholinergics. A paradoxical reaction characterized by hyperexcitability may occur in pediatric populations receiving large doses of anticholinergics, including glycopyrrolate.
    Headline
    geriatric
    General Information
    Use neostigmine; glycopyrrolate with caution and monitor geriatric adults for a longer period due to the increased likelihood of decreased renal function. The duration of action of neostigmine is prolonged in geriatric adults; however, geriatric adults may also experience slower spontaneous recovery from neuromuscular blocking agents. Monitor geriatric adults longer to ensure that additional doses of neostigmine are not needed. Determine the duration of monitoring based on the anticipated duration of action for the neuromuscular blocking agent used. Additionally, glycopyrrolate may cause heat prostration in the presence of fever, high environmental temperature, and/or during physical exercise due to decreased sweating, particularly in the elderly; advise geriatric adults to avoid exertion and a high environmental temperature after receiving neostigmine; glycopyrrolate.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    There are no adequate or well-controlled trials of neostigmine in pregnancy. It is not known whether neostigmine can cause fetal harm when administered during pregnancy or if it affects reproduction. The incidence of malformations in human pregnancies has not been established for neostigmine as the data are limited. No adverse effects were noted in rats or rabbits treated with human equivalent doses of neostigmine up to 8.1 and 13 mcg/kg/day, respectively, during organogenesis [0.1 to 0.2-times the maximum recommended human dose (MRHD) of 5 mg/60 kg person/day based on body surface area comparisons]. Anticholinesterase medications, including neostigmine, can cause uterine irritability and induce premature labor when administered in pregnancy near term. Limited data available with glycopyrrolate use in pregnancy have not identified a drug-associated risk of birth defects and miscarriage. Most of the reported glycopyrrolate exposures occurred after the first trimester and at the time of Cesarean section delivery. Randomized, controlled trials over several decades which compared the use of glycopyrrolate to another antimuscarinic agent in pregnancy during Cesarean section have not identified adverse maternal or infant outcomes, including infant Apgar scores. Teratogenic effects were not observed in animal reproduction studies with pregnant rats or rabbits administered glycopyrrolate orally (rats) and intramuscularly (rabbits) at 640-times and 10-times the MRHD of 1 mg (on a mg/m2 basis), respectively. Concentrations of glycopyrrolate in umbilical venous and arterial blood and in the amniotic fluid are low after intramuscular administration to parturients; glycopyrrolate does not appear to penetrate through the placental barrier in significant amounts. There are no studies on the safety of glycopyrrolate during organogenesis; therefore, the risk of birth defects after exposure to glycopyrrolate during pregnancy is unknown. Additionally, there are no data on the risk of miscarriage after fetal exposure to glycopyrrolate.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Consider the developmental and health benefits of breast-feeding along with the mother's clinical need for neostigmine; glycopyrrolate and any potential adverse effects on the breast-fed child from neostigmine; glycopyrrolate or the underlying maternal condition. It is not known whether neostigmine or glycopyrrolate are excreted in human milk. Glycopyrrolate may cause suppression of lactation like other anticholinergics.

Description
  Text
  Neostigmine; glycopyrrolate is an intravenous, fixed-dose combination of a cholinesterase inhibitor and antimuscarinic agent indicated in adults and pediatric patients 2 years and older for the reversal of the effects of nondepolarizing neuromuscular blocking agents (NMBAs) after surgery, while decreasing the peripheral muscarinic effects (e.g., bradycardia and excessive secretions) associated with cholinesterase inhibition after NMBA reversal administration. Consider administration of glycopyrrolate prior to neostigmine (i.e., as separate products) in persons with bradycardia or in persons in whom bradycardia, a known risk of neostigmine, may cause hemodynamic instability.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Neostigmine is a competitive cholinesterase inhibitor and induces an increase in acetylcholine in the synaptic cleft. Neostigmine-induced increases in acetylcholine concentrations result in the potentiation of both muscarinic and nicotinic cholinergic activity. The resulting increase of acetylcholine competes for the same binding site as nondepolarizing neuromuscular blocking agents and reverses the neuromuscular blockade. Glycopyrrolate inhibits the action of acetylcholine on structures innervated by postganglionic cholinergic nerves and on smooth muscles that respond to acetylcholine but lack cholinergic innervation. These peripheral cholinergic receptors are present in the autonomic effector cells of smooth muscle, cardiac muscle, the sinoatrial node, the atrioventricular node, exocrine glands, and in the autonomic ganglia (to a limited degree). Glycopyrrolate diminishes the volume and free acidity of gastric secretions and controls excessive pharyngeal, tracheal, and bronchial secretions. Glycopyrrolate antagonizes muscarinic symptoms (e.g., bronchorrhea, bronchospasm, bradycardia, and intestinal hypermotility) induced by cholinergic drugs such as the anticholinesterases.
