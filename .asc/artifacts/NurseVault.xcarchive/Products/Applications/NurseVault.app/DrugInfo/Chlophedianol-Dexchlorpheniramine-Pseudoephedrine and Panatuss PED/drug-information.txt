Drug Class: Chlophedianol, dexchlorpheniramine, and pseudoephedrine are used together in an oral preparation to relieve cough, nasal congestion, sneezing, itching of the nose or throat, and itchy, watery eyes due to the common cold, hay fever, or other respiratory allergies.

Routes: oral; Common adult doses: Chlophedianol 100 mg/day PO; dexchlorpheniramine 8 mg/day PO; pseudoephedrine 240 mg/day PO.

Common Side Effects (excluding routine GI upset): anxiety, ataxia, confusion, dizziness, drowsiness, euphoria, excitability, fatigue, hallucinations, headache, insomnia, neuritis, paresthesias, psychosis, restlessness, seizures, tinnitus, tremor, vertigo, colitis, dysuria, polyuria, nasal congestion, nasal dryness, wheezing, angina, arrhythmia exacerbation, hypertension, myocardial infarction, orthostatic hypotension

In an Overdose: Seizures may occur, but have more commonly occurred with excessive dosage, overdosage, or in patients with renal failure receiving the maximum dosage of pseudoephedrine.

Key Nursing Considerations: Contraindications/precautions: This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components. Dexchlorpheniramine is a first-generation antihistamine of the alkylamine class.

Contraindications And Precautions

  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  Contraindications And Precautions
    Headline
    Drug Interactions
    General Information
    The coadministration of certain medications may lead to harm and require avoidance or therapy modification; review all drug interactions prior to concomitant use of other medications.
    Headline
    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components. Dexchlorpheniramine is a first-generation antihistamine of the alkylamine class. Allergies to one antihistamine can sometimes lead to cross-reactivity with other chemically-related antihistamines.
    Headline
    asthma, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, tobacco smoking
    General Information
    People with a chronic cough that lasts such as occurs with tobacco smoking, asthma, or emphysema, or a cough that occurs with too much mucus, should consult their care team before use of chlophedianol; dexchlorpheniramine; pseudoephedrine. Use sedating antihistamines such as dexchlorpheniramine with caution in people with chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), including emphysema or chronic bronchitis. The anticholinergic activity of first-generation H1-antagonists may result in thickened bronchial secretions in the respiratory tract, thereby aggravating COPD. Individuals should discontinue use and contact their care team if the cough lasts more than 7 days, returns, or occurs with fever, rash or persistent headache since these could be signs of serious illness.
    Headline
    cardiac disease, hypertension
    General Information
    Sympathomimetics such as pseudoephedrine may raise heart rate and/or blood pressure in susceptible individuals. Use chlophedianol; dexchlorpheniramine; pseudoephedrine with caution in individuals with cardiac disease or hypertension. People with heart disease or high blood pressure should check with their care team before nonprescription (OTC) use.
    Headline
    prostatic hypertrophy
    General Information
    Use chlophedianol; dexchlorpheniramine; pseudoephedrine with caution in people with difficulty in urination due to prostatic hypertrophy, as sympathomimetic agents such as pseudoephedrine and anticholinergics such as dexchlorpheniramine may aggravate urinary retention.
    Headline
    glaucoma
    General Information
    Chlophedianol; dexchlorpheniramine; pseudoephedrine should be used with caution in people with glaucoma, as an increase in intraocular pressure may occur from the anticholinergic actions of the medication. Older adults are more susceptible to the anticholinergic effects of dexchlorpheniramine, including possible precipitation of undiagnosed glaucoma.
    Headline
    diabetes mellitus, thyroid disease
    General Information
    Use chlophedianol; dexchlorpheniramine; pseudoephedrine with caution in individuals with diabetes mellitus or thyroid disease. Sympathomimetic agents such as pseudoephedrine may aggravate these conditions.
    Headline
    children, infants, neonates
    General Information
    Due to the risk for serious adverse reactions, the FDA recommends against administration of over the counter (OTC) cough and cold products to neonates, infants and children younger than 2 years of age. When administering OTC medications to older pediatric patients, they advise caregivers to read product labels carefully, use caution when administering multiple products to avoid duplication of ingredients, and use only measuring devices specifically designed for use with medications. Care teams should thoroughly assess the use of similar products, both prescription and nonprescription, to avoid duplication of therapy and the potential for inadvertent overdose.
    Headline
    activities requiring coordination and concentration, driving or operating machinery
    General Information
    Dexchlorpheniramine can cause drowsiness. Advise individuals receiving chlophedianol; dexchlorpheniramine; pseudoephedrine to avoid driving or operating machinery and activities requiring coordination and concentration until the effects of the drug are known. Additive drowsiness may be seen during coadministration with alcohol and other CNS depressants.
    Headline
    renal failure, renal impairment
    General Information
    Pseudoephedrine should be used with caution in people with severe renal impairment (CrCl less than 30 mL/minute) and pseudoephedrine dosage reduction has been suggested for these individuals. Due to decreased elimination and potential pseudoephedrine drug accumulation, patients with renal failure may be at increased risk for drug-related toxicity. Many experts recommend against pseudoephedrine use in severe acute or chronic kidney disease (CKD) or renal failure.
    Headline
    geriatric
    General Information
    The Beers Criteria identifies first-generation antihistamines as potentially inappropriate medications (PIMs) for geriatric adults, recommending their avoidance due to their high anticholinergic properties, decreased clearance in older age, the development of tolerance when used as sleep aids, and an increased risk of anticholinergic effects and toxicity compared to younger individuals. These medications should particularly be avoided in patients with dementia or cognitive impairment (due to adverse CNS effects), those at high risk for delirium (which can worsen or trigger new-onset delirium), and men with lower urinary tract symptoms or benign prostatic hyperplasia (due to risks of urinary retention or hesitancy).
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Combination products containing chlophedianol; dexchlorpheniramine; pseudoephedrine are not recommended for use during pregnancy. Non-pharmacologic methods (e.g., fluids, rest) are recommended to be tried first for symptomatic relief of colds or allergies during pregnancy. Pregnant patients should see their health care professional for diagnosis and treatment recommendations. There are no reliable data regarding safe and effective chlophedianol use during pregnancy; no animal or human data are available. Pseudoephedrine may reduce blood flow to the placenta and the fetus, and there is some evidence use may be associated with birth defects if used during early pregnancy. Evidence from case-control studies in human pregnancy indicate there may be an increased risk of gastroschisis, small intestinal atresia, and hemifacial microsomia in babies exposed in utero to pseudoephedrine, particularly in the first trimester. No well controlled trials of dexchlorpheniramine use in human gestation are available; however, chlorpheniramine (parent drug) use as a single agent during pregnancy has been associated with reassuring animal and human data. However, second-generation antihistamines are generally preferred over first-generation antihistamines. Loratadine and cetirizine are acceptable antihistamine alternatives based on their excellent safety data and recommendation in multiple guidelines for use during pregnancy.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Chlophedianol; dexchlorpheniramine; pseudoephedrine should be used with caution during breast-feeding. Pseudoephedrine is excreted into breast milk. Peak milk concentrations occur 1 to 1.5 hours after a maternal oral dosage, and peak milk concentrations usually exceed those of maternal plasma. The total amount of pseudoephedrine (measured by AUC) in milk is 2 to 3 times that of plasma. However, only 0.5% of a maternal dose would probably be ingested by an infant during breast-feeding within any 24 hours. Pseudoephedrine may reduce milk production. Milk production over a 24-hour period was reduced by an average of 24% compared to placebo after a single 60 mg dose of pseudoephedrine. Based on concentrations in breast milk and assuming a maternal dose of 240 mg pseudoephedrine daily, it was estimated that an infant would receive 4.3% of the maternal weight-adjusted dose. It is suggested to avoid pseudoephedrine use  if lactation is not yet well established or if there are difficulties producing sufficient milk. Sympathomimetic adverse effects (irritability, excessive crying, and altered sleeping patterns) have been reported in a breast-fed infant following maternal administration of pseudoephedrine; symptoms resolved within 12 hours of drug discontinuation. Some experts consider dextromethorphan, at usual adult doses for cough and for limited duration of use for this purpose, to be compatible with breast-feeding and unlikely to be harmful to the breastfed infant. Based on dextromethorphan's relatively low molecular weight, some transfer into breast milk is expected. One study estimated infant exposure via breast milk to be less than 1% of the usual maternal dose. Side effects of sedating antihistamines, like dexchlorpheniramine, in breast-feeding infants such as irritability, disturbed sleeping patterns, drowsiness, hyperexcitability, and excessive crying have been reported in a number of cases.   Antihistamines can lower basal prolactin secretion and may interfere with the establishment of lactation.  Consider treatment alternatives to dexchlorpheniramine. Guidelines recommend loratadine or cetirizine at the lowest dose as preferred antihistamines in breast-feeding individuals because they are safer with only low levels found in breast milk.

Description
  Text
  Chlophedianol, dexchlorpheniramine, and pseudoephedrine are used together in an oral preparation to relieve cough, nasal congestion, sneezing, itching of the nose or throat, and itchy, watery eyes due to the common cold, hay fever, or other respiratory allergies. Chlophedianol is a centrally-acting non-opioid antitussive used to relieve dry, irritating coughs. Dexchlorpheniramine is a sedating antihistamine (H1-blocker) of the alkylamine class that dries the nasal and sinus passages secondary to its anticholinergic activity, and treats histamine-mediated symptoms such as rhinorrhea and sneezing. Pseudoephedrine is an adrenergic sympathomimetic agent that causes vasoconstriction and decreases nasal and sinus congestion.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Chlophedianol; dexchlorpheniramine; pseudoephedrine combination products provide antitussive, antihistaminic, and decongestant properties for the temporary relief of symptoms associated with the common cold, hay fever (allergic rhinitis), or other upper respiratory allergies.
Chlophedianol: Chlophedianol suppresses the cough reflex by a direct effect on the cough center in the medulla of the brain. Chlophedianol may also have local anesthetic and anticholinergic effects.
Dexchlorpheniramine: Dexchlorpheniramine, the d-isomer of the racemic compound chlorpheniramine, is two times more active than chlorpheniramine, although the therapeutic advantage, if any, is unknown. Dexchlorpheniramine does not prevent the release of histamine, as do cromolyn and nedocromil, but rather competes with free histamine for binding at the H1-receptor sites. Like other antihistamines, dexchlorpheniramine competitively antagonizes the effects of histamine on H1-receptors in the GI tract, uterus, large blood vessels, and bronchial muscle. Blockade of H1-receptors also suppresses the formation of edema, flare, and pruritus that result from histaminic activity. Unlike second generation antihistamines, such as loratadine and cetirizine, which selectively block peripheral H1-receptors, first generation antihistamines, such as dexchlorpheniramine, bind to H1-receptors centrally and peripherally. Thus, sedative effects are more likely to occur with first generation antihistamines. Following prolonged use, tolerance can occur which may be beneficial, dependent on the indication for use, because of reduced sedative effects. H1-antagonists are structurally similar to anticholinergic agents and therefore possess anticholinergic properties of varying degrees. They also have antipruritic effects. Dexchlorpheniramine has high antihistaminic activity, moderate anticholinergic effects, and minimal sedative effects. The drug does not possess antiemetic properties.
Pseudoephedrine: Pseudoephedrine acts directly on both alpha- and, to a lesser degree, beta-adrenergic receptors. Like ephedrine, pseudoephedrine also has an indirect effect by releasing norepinephrine from its storage sites. By acting directly on alpha-adrenergic receptors in the mucosa of the respiratory tract, pseudoephedrine produces vasoconstriction, which shrinks swollen nasal mucous membranes, reduces tissue hyperemia, edema, and nasal congestion, and increases nasal airway patency. Also, drainage of sinus secretions is increased, and obstructed eustachian ostia may be opened. Pseudoephedrine can relax bronchial smooth muscle by stimulating beta2-adrenergic receptors; however, bronchodilation has not been consistently demonstrated with oral administration. Dosages used in over-the-counter cold and flu products (e.g., 60 mg) may produce minimal changes in pulse and blood pressure. Pseudoephedrine may increase irritability of the heart muscle resulting in other cardiac effects.

  Chlophedianol; dexchlorpheniramine; pseudoephedrine combination products are administered orally as a liquid.
Chlophedianol: Metabolized in the liver and eliminated by the kidneys.
Dexchlorpheniramine: Pharmacokinetic data for dexchlorpheniramine, the primary active isomer of the racemic compound chlorpheniramine, are not specifically available but are likely similar to chlorpheniramine. Chlorpheniramine pharmacokinetics are primarily presented here. Protein binding is approximately 72%. Wide distribution occurs in body tissues and fluids, including across the placenta and excretion into breast milk. Metabolism is extensive and rapid, first occurring in the gastric mucosa and then on first-pass through the liver, which may be saturable. N-dealkylation produces several metabolites, which are excreted in the urine along with the parent compound. Plasma half-life is between 2 and 4 hours. Terminal elimination half-life varies with patient age; in healthy individuals, the half-life is 20 to 24 hours for adults and 10 to 13 hours for children. Excretion rates are dependent upon urine pH and flow rate, with the excretion decreasing as the pH increases and the flow rate decreases.
Pseudoephedrine: Pseudoephedrine is presumed to cross the placenta and the blood brain barrier, and may also be distributed into breast milk. Incomplete metabolism occurs in the liver to the primary active metabolite norpseudoephedrine. Parent drug and metabolite are excreted in the urine, with 55 to 75% excreted as unchanged drug. Elimination half-life ranges from 9-16 hours and is primarily dependent upon urinary pH. The urinary excretion rate is modifiable by altering urine pH; urinary acidification to a pH near 5 accelerates excretion and alkalinization to a pH of approximately 8 slows excretion as some of the drug is reabsorbed into the kidney tubule.
