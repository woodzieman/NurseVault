Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  General Information
  Dimercaprol is indicated for the treatment of poisoning due to arsenic, gold, lead (with edetate calcium disodium), and mercury. The efficacy in treating poisoning due to other heavy metals such as antimony and bismuth is questionable. Dimercaprol should not be used in iron, cadmium, or selenium poisoning because the resulting dimercaprol-metal complexes are more toxic than the metal alone, especially to the kidneys.
 
Dimercaprol injection should be used with caution in patients who have a history of peanut hypersensitivity or hypersensitivity to related foods and legumes. The injection in formulated in a peanut oil base. This injection is for deep intramuscular injection only; never administer via intravenous administration.
 
Dimercaprol-metal complexes can dissolve, especially in an acidic environment or as the concentration of dimercaprol decreases, thereby releasing the metal and increasing the drug's potential nephrotoxicity. This nephrotoxicity can be prevented by alkalizing the urine. Dosages of the drug should be decreased in patients with oliguria, and the drug should be discontinued or only used with extreme caution in patients who develop acute renal impairment or renal failure because the drug can accumulate, possibly leading to serum toxicity. Because the dimercaprol-metal complex is eliminated renally, this drug should be used cautiously in patients with preexisting renal disease. Medicinal iron therapy should not be given to patients receiving dimercaprol. The resulting dimercaprol-iron complex may be more toxic to the kidneys than iron alone.
 
Dimercaprol should be used with caution in patients with hypertension because this condition can be exacerbated by the drug.
 
When administering the drug to patients with rheumatoid arthritis, it should be kept in mind that dimercaprol can reverse the beneficial effects of gold therapy in these patients.
 
Dimercaprol is contraindicated for use in patients with hepatic disease, except in cases of post-arsenical jaundice when dosages may be reduced rather than discontinued. Hepatic disease can decrease the elimination of the drug, causing toxicity.
 
Dimercaprol is classified as FDA pregnancy risk category C. Although no adequate human studies have examined the effects of this drug on the fetus, animal reproduction studies have shown adverse fetal effects. Therefore, in making the decision to administer this drug during pregnancy, the potential risks to the fetus must be weighed against the potential benefits to the mother.
 
The indications necessitating the use of dimercaprol (e.g., heavy metal poisoning) often lead to a cessation of breast-feeding during maternal treatment of those toxicities. There are no data on the use of dimercaprol during lactation.
 
Dimercaprol may cause hemolysis in patients with G6PD deficiency. Use cautiously in these patients, especially during stressful situations (i.e., infection).

    General Information
    Dimercaprol should not be used for poisoning caused by certain heavy metals, including antimony and bismuth.     Headline
    dehydration, renal failure, renal impairment
    General Information
    Dimercaprol may cause renal toxicity, particularly with prolonged use or higher doses. Manifestations may include acute tubular necrosis, proteinuria, hematuria, increased BUN and serum creatinine, decreased creatinine clearance, and oliguria or anuria in severe cases. Individuals with pre-existing renal impairment or renal failure are at increased risk for worsening renal dysfunction during dimercaprol therapy. Dehydration, concomitant use of other nephrotoxic agents, and the severity of heavy metal poisoning may further increase this risk. Dimercaprol-metal complexes may dissociate in acidic conditions; therefore, maintain urine in an alkaline state to help protect renal function. Assess renal function (e.g., serum creatinine, BUN, urinalysis) prior to and during therapy, particularly with prolonged treatment or doses exceeding 3 mg/kg, and monitor for proteinuria, hematuria, and fluid status while maintaining adequate hydration and urine alkalinization. If acute renal insufficiency develops during dimercaprol treatment, consider discontinuing treatment.

    General Information

    G6PD deficiency
    General Information
    Dimercaprol may cause hemolysis, including severe or life-threatening hemolytic anemia, in individuals with G6PD deficiency. Screen high-risk individuals before treatment, particularly those of African, Mediterranean, or Southern Asian ancestry, and consider alternative chelators when appropriate. Adverse Reactions
  Headline
  Adverse Reactions
  General Information
  At dosages of 5 mg/kg, approximately half of patients receiving dimercaprol will experience mild, transitory adverse effects. At dosages greater than 5 mg/kg, most patients will experience seizures, vomiting, or stupor beginning within 30 minutes after administration and typically subsiding within 1—6 hours. The majority of the milder effects of the drug usually can be alleviated by administration of an antihistamine or ephedrine.Both systolic and diastolic hypertension can occur during dimercaprol therapy (typically within 15—30 minutes) and are dose-related. Sinus tachycardia also can occur. These symptoms generally resolve within 2 hours following administration.An injection site reaction can occur if dimercaprol is not administered deeply enough. Painful, sterile abscesses can develop at the injection site.Other adverse effects reported during dimercaprol therapy include muscle cramps, muscle spasms, tingling of extremities, nervousness, anxiety, tightness in the chest or throat, nausea/vomiting, headache, and diaphoresis.Dimercaprol itself has a disagreeable smell, and administration of the drug will cause a patient's breath to have an unpleasant, mercaptan-like odor. Dimercaprol also causes a burning sensation of the lips, mouth, throat, eyes, and penis, and may cause painful sensations in the teeth. Lacrimation, hypersalivation, rhinorrhea, conjunctivitis, and blepharal spasm also have been reported.Children receiving dimercaprol often develop a fever that persists throughout the remainder of treatment. Transient decreases in polymorphonuclear leukocytes (neutropenia) also can occur.Dimercaprol has been reported to cause hemolysis that can be severe, particularly in patients with glucose 6-phosphate dehydrogenase deficiency.Prolonged administration of dimercaprol can cause capillary damage and vascular collapse or hypotension due to a loss of protein from the blood. Studies show that the drug can precipitate metabolic acidosis associated with increases in plasma lactate concentrations. Very high doses have caused seizures and/or coma in animals.

Description
  Text
  Dimercaprol is a chelating agent approved for the treatment of arsenic or gold poisoning, acute mercury poisoning within 2 hours following ingestion, and acute lead poisoning (when given in combination with edetate calcium disodium). The drug contains 2 sulfhydryl, or thiol, groups which bind heavy metals; thereby rendering the metals inert until they are excreted. Dimercaprol should not be used for chronic mercury or lead poisoning or for poisoning caused by other heavy metals such as antimony and bismuth. Additionally, it is not indicated for iron, cadmium, or selenium poisoning because the resulting dimercaprol-metal complexes are more toxic than the metals alone, especially to the kidneys. Dimercaprol was FDA-approved in 1946.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Dimercaprol is a chelating agent that binds certain heavy metals in the body. The 2 sulfhydryl groups of dimercaprol form heterocyclic ring complexes with heavy metals, preventing them from reacting with or removing them from binding sites on sulfhydryl-containing enzymes. Once bound to dimercaprol, the metals are rendered inert and excreted by the kidneys. However, the dimercaprol-metal complexes can dissociate, especially in an acidic environment or as the concentration of dimercaprol decreases, thereby releasing the metal and increasing toxicity. To assure maximal excretion of the heavy metal, it is crucial to administer dosages of the drug that are in excess of the amounts of metal present.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Intramuscular Route
    Description
    Dimercaprol is rapidly absorbed after deep intramuscular injection, with a Tmax of 30 to 60 minutes.
  Text
  Dimercaprol is administered intramuscularly. Dimercaprol is highly lipophilic and distributes widely throughout the body, including the intracellular space. It reaches high concentrations in the liver, kidneys, and brain. Unbound dimercaprol is metabolized in the liver via glucuronidation into inactive metabolites. Both the metabolites and the metal-dimercaprol complexes are primarily excreted in the urine, with a small portion eliminated via bile. The dimercaprol-metal complex dissociates in acidic medium; alkalization of the urine can help prevent the release of the metal and protect the kidneys.
 
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes and drug transporters: none

Administration
  General Information
  For storage information, see the specific product information within the How Supplied section.
The injection is formulated with peanut oil which may cause allergic reactions in some individuals. Ensure medications and equipment necessary to treat allergic reactions are available if the product is administered to patients who have a history of peanut hypersensitivity.
Use other supportive measures in conjunction with dimercaprol therapy.
Keep the urine alkaline during dimercaprol therapy to help protect kidney function. The dimercaprol-metal complex can break apart in an acidic environment.
Pediatric patients may need premedication with an antihistamine to lower the risk of certain histamine-like reactions.
