Drug Class: Doxapram is a parenteral analeptic and respiratory stimulant indicated for postanesthesia respiratory depression or apnea other than that due to muscle relaxant drugs, drug-induced central nervous system (CNS) depression, and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) associated with acute...

Routes: injection (IV/IM/SC); Common adult doses: 2 mg/kg by IV injection or 4 mg/kg by continuous IV infusion for drug-induced postanesthesia respiratory depression; 3 g/day IV for drug-induced central nervous system depression; and 360 mg for chronic obstructive pulmonary disease with acute hypercapnia.

Common Side Effects (excluding routine GI upset): chest pain (unspecified), flushing, hypertension, qt prolongation, ventricular fibrillation, ventricular tachycardia, dizziness, fever, hallucinations, headache, hyperactivity, inconsolable crying, irritability, mydriasis, seizures, enterocolitis, bronchospasm, cough, dyspnea, hiccups, hyperventilation, hypoventilation, laryngospasm, tachypnea, paresthesias, phlebitis, pruritus, skin irritation

In an Overdose: No overdose/antidote information is listed in this file.

Key Nursing Considerations: Contraindications/precautions: This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components. Doxapram is contraindicated in individuals with cerebral edema, head trauma, seizure disorder, or stroke.

Contraindications And Precautions

  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  Contraindications And Precautions
    Headline
    Drug Interactions
    General Information
    The coadministration of certain medications may lead to harm and require avoidance or therapy modification; review all drug interactions prior to concomitant use of other medications.
    Headline
    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components.
    Headline
    agitated state, cerebral edema, head trauma, increased cerebrospinal fluid pressure, seizure disorder, stroke
    General Information
    Doxapram is contraindicated in individuals with cerebral edema, head trauma, seizure disorder, or stroke. Use doxapram with caution in individuals with CNS disorders including increased cerebrospinal fluid pressure or agitated state. Anticonvulsants, oxygen, and resuscitative equipment should be readily available during administration of doxapram to manage excessive central nervous system stimulation. Monitor deep tendon reflexes. Administer doxapram slowly, and carefully observe the individual for some time subsequently to ensure that protective reflexes have been restored and prevent post-hyperventilation or hypoventilation.
    Headline
    acute heart failure, cardiovascular disease, hyperthyroidism, hypovolemia, pheochromocytoma
    General Information
    Doxapram is contraindicated in individuals with significant cardiovascular disease, acute heart failure, severe coronary artery disease, or severe hypertension, including that associated with hyperthyroidism or pheochromocytoma. Use doxapram with caution in individuals with hypermetabolic states, such as hyperthyroidism and pheochromocytoma. A pressor response may result after doxapram administration. Provided there is no impairment of cardiac function, the pressor effect is more marked in hypovolemia than in normovolemic states. The pressor response is due to improved cardiac output rather than peripheral vasoconstriction. Monitor blood pressure, heart rate, and cardiac rhythm.
    Headline
    asthma exacerbation, flail chest, pneumothorax, pulmonary embolism, respiratory obstruction, restrictive lung disease
    General Information
    Doxapram is contraindicated in individuals with proven or suspected pulmonary embolism and mechanical disorders of ventilation, such as respiratory obstruction, respiratory muscle paresis (including neuromuscular blockade), flail chest, pneumothorax, asthma exacerbation, and restrictive lung disease (e.g., pulmonary fibrosis or other conditions resulting in restriction of the chest wall, muscles of respiration, or alveolar expansion).
    Headline
    hepatic failure, renal failure, renal impairment
    General Information
    Use doxapram with caution in individuals with significant hepatic impairment or hepatic failure or renal impairment or renal failure as a reduction in the metabolism or excretion of metabolites may alter the response.
    Headline
    infants, intracerebral hemorrhage, neonates, perinatal asphyxia, premature neonates, seizures
    General Information
    Excessive amounts of benzyl alcohol in neonates have been associated with hypotension, metabolic acidosis, and kernicterus. A "gasping syndrome" characterized by CNS depression, metabolic acidosis, and gasping respirations has been associated with benzyl alcohol dosages more than 99 mg/kg/day in neonates and low birth weight infants. The minimum amount of benzyl alcohol at which serious adverse reactions may occur is not known. Consider the daily metabolic load of benzyl alcohol from combined sources. Premature neonates and low birth weight infants may be more likely to develop toxicity. In premature neonates with risk factors such as a previous seizures, perinatal asphyxia, or intracerebral hemorrhage, seizures have occurred. In many instances, doxapram was given after administration of xanthine derivatives such as caffeine, aminophylline, or theophylline.
    Headline
    leukopenia
    General Information
    In the presence of pre-existing leukopenia, a further decrease in white blood cells has been observed after anesthesia and treatment with doxapram.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Use doxapram during pregnancy only if clearly needed. There are no adequate and well-controlled studies on the use of doxapram during human pregnancy. In animal studies, no fetal harm was observed in pregnant rats exposed to doxapram at 1.6 times the human dose.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Use doxapram with caution during breast-feeding. There are no data on the presence of doxapram in human milk.

    Headline
    chest pain (unspecified), flushing, hypertension, QT prolongation, ventricular fibrillation, ventricular tachycardia
    General Information
    Variations in heart rate, lowered T-waves, arrhythmias, including ventricular tachycardia and ventricular fibrillation, chest pain (unspecified), tightness in chest, sweating, and flushing have been reported with doxapram. Arrhythmias seen in some patients in acute respiratory failure secondary to chronic obstructive pulmonary disease are probably the result of hypoxia. A mild to moderate increase in blood pressure is commonly noted. A pressor response may result after doxapram administration. Provided there is no impairment of cardiac function, the pressor effect is more marked in hypovolemic than in normovolemic states. The pressor response is due to the improved cardiac output rather than peripheral vasoconstriction. An increased release of catecholamines has also been observed after doxapram administration. Premature neonates given doxapram have developed hypertension and QT prolongation that has resulted in heart block. Monitor blood pressure, heart rate, and cardiac rhythm. Discontinue doxapram if sudden hypotension develops.

Description
  Text
  Doxapram is a parenteral analeptic and respiratory stimulant indicated for postanesthesia respiratory depression or apnea other than that due to muscle relaxant drugs, drug-induced central nervous system (CNS) depression, and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) associated with acute hypercapnia. The respiratory stimulant action is manifested by an increase in tidal volume associated with a slight increase in respiratory rate. Doxapram is neither an antagonist to muscle relaxant drugs nor a specific opioid antagonist. Although opioid-induced respiratory depression is antagonized by doxapram, the analgesic effect is not affected. Modest to significant increases in blood pressure have been noted in some patients receiving doxapram. A pressor response may result after doxapram administration. Provided there is no impairment of cardiac function, the pressor effect is more marked in hypovolemia than in normovolemic states. The pressor response is due to improved cardiac output rather than peripheral vasoconstriction. An increased release of catecholamines has also been observed after doxapram administration. Doxapram has a limited role in COPD management and may be considered in selected individuals with acute hypercapnic respiratory failure when noninvasive ventilation is unavailable, contraindicated, or not tolerated.Doxapram was FDA-approved in 1965.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Doxapram is a central nervous system and respiratory stimulant and acts to stimulate respiration through both peripherally and centrally mediated mechanisms. Doxapram produces respiratory stimulation mediated through the peripheral carotid chemoreceptors. As the dosage level is increased, the central respiratory centers in the medulla are stimulated with progressive stimulation of other parts of the brain and spinal cord. At least some respiratory stimulant effects of doxapram are likely achieved through inhibition of acid sensitive potassium channels found in the carotid bodies and brain stem. The respiratory stimulant action is manifested by an increase in tidal volume associated with a slight increase in respiratory rate. Doxapram is neither an antagonist to muscle relaxant drugs nor a specific opioid antagonist. Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Intravenous Route
    Description
    The onset of respiratory stimulation after the recommended single intravenous injection of doxapram usually occurs in 20 to 40 seconds with peak effect at 1 to 2 minutes. The duration of effect may vary from 5 to 12 minutes.
  Special Populations
    Name
    Pediatrics
    Description
    Terminal elimination half-life estimation was determined in a subset of premature neonates receiving continuous IV infusion doxapram over a dose range of 0.5 to 2.5 mg/kg/hour (n = 10). The terminal elimination half-life was 9.9 +/- 2.9 hours, and there was no correlation between half-life and gestational, postnatal, or postconceptional age.
  Text
  Doxapram is administered intravenously. It is metabolized via ring hydroxylation to ketodoxapram, an active metabolite. After a single doxapram 1.5 mg/kg IV bolus and 6.5 mg/kg continuous IV infusion for 2 hours in healthy male volunteers, the mean terminal plasma half-lives were 5.9 and 7.5 hours, respectively. The mean Vd and clearance were 3,240 mL/kg and 5.93 mL/minute/kg after the bolus dose and 3,670 mL/kg and 5.61 mL/minute/kg after the 2-hour infusion.

      Intermittent IV Injection
Administer by single or single repeat IV injection.

Continuous IV Infusion
For the treatment of drug-induced post-anesthesia respiratory depression or drug-induced central nervous system depression, add doxapram 250 mg to 250 mL of 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection, 5% Dextrose Injection, or 10% Dextrose Injection to yield a concentration of approximately 1 mg/mL.
Discontinue doxapram if the individual begins to awaken or at end of 2 hours.
For the treatment of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) with acute hypercapnia, add doxapram 400 mg to 180 mL of 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection, 5% Dextrose Injection, or 10% Dextrose Injection to yield a concentration of 2 mg/mL.
Monitor arterial blood gases before starting doxapram and oxygen and at least every 30 minutes during the doxapram infusion to prevent development of carbon dioxide retention and acidosis in individuals with COPD and acute hypercapnia.
Because of the associated increase in work of breathing, do not increase the doxapram infusion rate in severely ill individuals with COPD in an attempt to lower pCO2.
Discontinue doxapram if arterial blood gases deteriorate and initiate mechanical ventilation. Do not use doxapram in conjunction with mechanical ventilation in the treatment of COPD with acute hypercapnia.
Additional infusions beyond the single maximum 2-hour administration period are not recommended.

Dosage Adjustment Guidelines
  Hepatic Impairment
  Specific guidelines for dosage adjustments in hepatic impairment are not available; it appears that no dosage adjustments are needed. Use doxapram with caution in patients with significantly impaired hepatic function.
  Renal Impairment
  Specific guidelines for dosage adjustments in renal impairment are not available; it appears that no dosage adjustments are needed. Use doxapram with caution in patients with significantly impaired renal function.
