Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  General Information
  Cyclopentolate is contraindicated for use in patients with untreated narrow angle glaucoma (closed-angle glaucoma). Use cautiously in those with Down's Syndrome and in those predisposed to increases in intraocular pressure.Cyclopentolate contains benzalkonium chloride, which may be absorbed by contact lenses. Users of soft contact lenses should not administer cyclopentolate while wearing these lenses.Use cautiously in patients with seizures or a seizure disorder; grand mal seizures have occurred following topical application of cyclopentolate in children. The use of cyclopentolate may be contraindicated in pediatric patients with a history of epilepsy. The risk of precipitating psychotic reactions in children is possible with the use of cyclopentolate when applied topically. This risk is greatest with the 2% solution.Infants are especially susceptible to CNS and cardiopulmonary effects of cyclopentolate. Feeding intolerance may also occur. Infant feedings should be held for 4 hours following administration. Observe infants closely for at least 30 minutes following ophthalmic instillation of cyclopentolate.Cyclopentolate is classified as FDA pregnancy risk category C. Although no adequate human studies have examined the effects of this drug on the fetus, animal reproduction studies have shown adverse fetal effects. Therefore, in making the decision to administer this drug during pregnancy, the potential risks to the fetus must be weighed against the potential benefits to the mother.Cyclopentolate is excreted in breast milk. Because of the potential risk for adverse reactions in nursing infants, cyclopentolate is not recommended for use in breast-feeding women.

    General Information
    No adequate human studies have examined the effects of cyclopentolate on the fetus during pregnancy. Use during the first trimester for an ophthalmic exam is often avoided when possible. Caution should be observed when considering use of cyclopentolate in the presence of Down syndrome.

Description
  Text
  Cyclopentolate is a cycloplegic, mydriatic ophthalmic agent with anticholinergic effects on the eye that are similar to atropine. It is used for cycloplegic refraction and for dilating the pupil for diagnostic purposes. This drug is useful as an aid to ophthalmic examination in a wide variety of populations, from newborns through the elderly. Cyclopentolate was approved by the FDA in 1953.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Cyclopentolate blocks the action of acetylcholine to produce relaxation of the sphincter muscle of the iris resulting in dilation of the pupil (mydriasis). This agent has an anticholinergic effect similar to atropine. Paralysis of accommodation (cycloplegia) is produced by blocking cholinergic stimulation of the ciliary muscle of the lens.
 
Pharmacodynamics
Cyclopentolate has a rapid onset of action, producing the maximum mydriatic and cycloplegic effects within 15 to 60 minutes following topical application to the eye. The duration of both effects is normally 24 hours. However, mydriasis may persist for several days in selected patients.

Pharmacokinetics
  Special Populations
    Name
    Pediatrics
    Description
    Neonates, Infants, and Children
Lacrimal occlusion after administration can help limit systemic absorption of cyclopentolate through the nasal mucosa in pediatric patients. To a much lesser extent, systemic absorption of eye drops may also occur through the conjunctiva, the nasolacrimal duct, the oropharynx, the digestive system, and the skin (due to overflow from the conjunctival sac). Absorbed ophthalmic medications are much more concentrated in neonates and infants because their average blood volume is one twentieth that of an adult. Additionally, immaturity of metabolic enzymes during the neonatal period may prolong the half-life of the drug.
  Text
  Cyclopentolate is administered as a topical ophthalmic solution. Specific pharmacokinetic data are not available from the manufacturer. Cyclopentolate, like other ophthalmic medications, can pass into the nasolacrimal system and thus have systemic absorption via the nasal mucosa; systemic absorption of cyclopentolate can be minimized by applying pressure to the lacrimal sac during and immediately after administration.

  Route Specific Administrations
    Name
    Ophthalmic Administration
    Description
    Ophthalmic Solution
Wash hands before and after use.
To prevent contamination or spread of infection, do not touch dropper tip to any surface. Do not use dropper for more than 1 person.
Tilt the patient's head back slightly and pull the lower eyelid down with the index finger to form a pouch.
Squeeze the prescribed number of drops into the pouch.
To avoid excessive systemic absorption, apply finger pressure on the lacrimal sac for 1 to 2 minutes (in adults and children) or 2 to 3 minutes (infants) following topical application to the eye.
In children, take care not to get into the mouth of the child or infant. Wash the hands of the child after administration.
Observe infants closely for at least 30 minutes after administration. Feeding intolerance may follow ophthalmic use of this product in infants. Withhold feeding for four (4) hours after examination.
If more than one topical ophthalmic drug product is being used, the drugs should be administered at least 5 minutes apart.
The patient's eyes will be sensitive to light and vision will be blurry approximately 30 to 60 minutes following dilation with this product.
Patients should not drive of perform hazardous activities while eyes are dilated.
