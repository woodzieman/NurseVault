Indication Specific Dosing
  General Information
  General dosage information:
Vaccinate persons according to immunization guidelines prior to vamorolone initiation. Administer live-attenuated or live vaccines at least 4 to 6 weeks prior to vamorolone initiation.
Persons may be switched from oral corticosteroid treatment to vamorolone without treatment interruption or a period of prior corticosteroid dosage reduction to minimize the risk of adrenal insufficiency.

  Contraindications And Precautions
    Headline
    Drug Interactions
    General Information
    The coadministration of certain medications may lead to harm and require avoidance or therapy modification; review all drug interactions prior to concomitant use of other medications.
    Headline
    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components.
    Headline
    infection
    General Information
    Systemic corticosteroids, including vamorolone, may cause dose-dependent immunosuppression and increase the risk of infection with any pathogen, including viral, bacterial, fungal, protozoan, or helminthic pathogens. Corticosteroids may mask symptoms and increase the risk of severe or fatal infectious complications, requiring close monitoring and potential dose adjustment or withdrawal. Systemic vamorolone and other corticosteroids may cause severe or fatal infections by reactivating or worsening TB, hepatitis B, amebiasis, Strongyloides, varicella, measles, herpes simplex, and other viral or herpes infections. Avoid vamorolone in cerebral malaria and systemic fungal infections. Screen for hepatitis B before prolonged immunosuppressive therapy; consult specialists and consider antiviral prophylaxis if infection is present. Rule out latent amebiasis in individuals with tropical exposure or unexplained diarrhea. Use caution in individuals with known or suspected Strongyloides infection; prophylactic therapy may be indicated. Avoid use in systemic fungal infections unless essential; discontinue or reduce corticosteroid if fungal infection develops. Avoid exposing nonimmune corticosteroid-treated individuals to varicella or measles. If exposure occurs, prophylaxis with varicella zoster immune globulin (VZIG) for varicella or immunoglobulin (IG) for measles may be needed. If infection develops, consider antiviral treatment.
    Headline
    diabetes mellitus, hyperglycemia
    General Information
    Monitor blood glucose at regular intervals and counsel individuals receiving vamorolone about the signs and symptoms of hyperglycemia. Initiate or adjust anti-diabetic treatment in individuals with hyperglycemia accordingly. Corticosteroids can increase blood glucose, worsen preexisting diabetes mellitus, reduce the effect of anti-diabetic medications, and predispose individuals on long term therapy to diabetes mellitus.
    Headline
    hyperthyroidism, hypothyroidism
    General Information
    Metabolic clearance of corticosteroids is decreased in hypothyroidism and increased in hyperthyroidism; changes in thyroid status may necessitate adjustment in the vamorolone dosage. When concomitant administration of corticosteroids and levothyroxine is required, administration of corticosteroid should precede the initiation of levothyroxine therapy to reduce the risk of adrenal crisis. Individuals with altered thyroid function may be at increased risk for adverse endocrine events.
    Headline
    pheochromocytoma
    General Information
    Consider the risk of pheochromocytoma crisis prior to administering vamorolone in individuals with suspected or identified pheochromocytoma. Pheochromocytoma crisis, which can be fatal, has been reported after administration of systemic corticosteroids.
    Headline
    heart failure, hypertension, renal failure, renal impairment
    General Information
    Exercise caution when using vamorolone in individuals with congestive heart failure, hypertension, renal impairment, or renal failure. Monitor blood pressure and serum potassium concentrations in individuals receiving vamorolone. Periodically assess for signs and symptoms of volume overload. Corticosteroids, including vamorolone, can cause sodium retention, fluid retention, potassium and calcium loss, and elevated blood pressure. Exacerbation of congestive heart failure or hypertension may occur.
    Headline
    myocardial infarction
    General Information
    Use vamorolone with caution in individuals with a history of recent myocardial infarction; corticosteroid therapy has been associated with left ventricular free-wall rupture in these individuals.
    Headline
    diverticulitis, GI abscess, GI perforation, intestinal anastomosis, peptic ulcer disease, ulcerative colitis
    General Information
    Avoid the use of vamorolone in persons with a probability of impending GI perforation, GI abscess, or other pyogenic infections; diverticulitis; fresh intestinal anastomoses; or active or latent peptic ulcer disease. Vamorolone increases the risk of gastrointestinal perforation in persons with certain types of GI disease, such as diverticulitis, nonspecific ulcerative colitis, active or latent peptic ulcer disease, and intestinal anastomosis. Signs of GI perforation, such as peritoneal irritation, may be masked in persons receiving corticosteroids.
    Headline
    mood disorder, psychotic disorder
    General Information
    Potentially severe psychiatric adverse reactions may occur with systemic corticosteroids, including vamorolone. Symptoms typically emerge within a few days or weeks of starting treatment and may be dose-related. These reactions may improve after either dose reduction or withdrawal, although pharmacologic treatment may be necessary. Inform individuals or caregivers of the potential for mood and behavioral changes and encourage them to seek medical attention if psychiatric symptoms develop, especially if depressed mood (depression) or suicidal ideation is suspected. In adults, psychiatric adverse reactions with corticosteroids usually involve hypomanic or manic symptoms (e.g., euphoria, insomnia, mood swings, psychosis) during treatment and depressive episodes after discontinuation of treatment. In children receiving corticosteroids, psychiatric adverse reactions usually involve hyperactivity symptoms (e.g., irritability, aggressive behavior, increased frequency of tantrums, and mood swings) and sleep disorder during treatment. Also, existing emotional instability or psychotic tendencies may be aggravated by corticosteroids, such as in individuals with a mood disorder or psychotic disorder.
    Headline
    increased risk for osteoporosis
    General Information
    Corticosteroids decrease bone formation and increase bone resorption both through their effect on calcium regulation (e.g., decreasing absorption and increasing excretion) and inhibition of osteoblast function. This, together with a decrease in the protein matrix of the bone secondary to an increase in protein catabolism, and reduced sex hormone production, may lead to the development of osteopenia and osteoporosis at any age. Special consideration should be given to people at increased risk for osteoporosis before initiating corticosteroid therapy.
    Headline
    myasthenia gravis
    General Information
    Use systemic corticosteroids, such as vamorolone, with caution in individuals with myasthenia gravis. A generalized acute myopathy has been reported with the use of corticosteroids in individuals with disorders of neuromuscular transmission, such as myasthenia gravis. Ocular and respiratory muscles may be involved, and the myopathy may result in quadriparesis. Recovery from the myopathy after discontinuation of corticosteroids may take weeks to years.f
    Headline
    thromboembolism, thrombophilia
    General Information
    Use systemic corticosteroids, like vamorolone, with caution in individuals who have thromboembolism or may be predisposed to thromboembolic disease (thrombophilia). Observational studies have shown an increased risk of thromboembolism (including venous thromboembolism) with corticosteroid use, particularly with higher cumulative doses. It is unclear if risk differs by daily dose or duration of use.
    Headline
    Child-Pugh class A, Child-Pugh class B, hepatic failure
    General Information
    Exercise caution during use of vamorolone in individuals with hepatic failure. Dosage adjustments are necessary in individuals with mild hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh class A) and moderate hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh class B). There is no clinical experience with vamorolone in individuals with severe hepatic impairment or hepatic failure. Vamorolone is hepatically metabolized through multiple metabolic pathways.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    There are no data on the use of vamorolone during pregnancy. In general, use corticosteroids during pregnancy only when the potential benefits outweigh the risks to the fetus. Evidence shows that corticosteroid use can cause orofacial clefts (cleft lip with or without palate), intrauterine growth restriction, and decreased birth weight. Some reports suggest that corticosteroid use during pregnancy may increase the risk of orofacial clefts from about 1 per 1,000 infants to 3 to 5 per 1,000 infants; however, an increased risk has not been observed in all clinical studies. Intrauterine growth restriction and decreased birth weight appear to be dose-related but may also be related to the patient's underlying medical condition. Two prospective case-control studies found infants exposed to corticosteroids in utero had lower birth weights. During animal reproduction studies, the incidence of cleft palate was increased in pregnant mice, rats, hamsters, and rabbits. Embryo-fetal death, intrauterine growth restriction, and constriction of the ductus arteriosus were observed in some species.
    Headline
    neonates and infants exposed to this medication in utero
    General Information
    Monitor neonates and infants exposed to this medication in utero for signs of hypoadrenalism, such as poor feeding, irritability, weakness, and vomiting. Hypoadrenalism may occur in infants born to patients receiving corticosteroids during pregnancy.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Use vamorolone with caution during breast-feeding. There are no data on the presence of vamorolone in human milk, its effects on the breast-fed child, or its effect on milk production. Systemically administered corticosteroids appear in human milk and could suppress growth, interfere with endogenous corticosteroid production, or cause other adverse effects. Consider the benefits of breast-feeding, the patient's clinical need for treatment, and any potential adverse effects on the breast-fed child from the medication or from the patient's underlying medical condition.

Adagrasib: (Major) Decrease the vamorolone dose to 4 mg/kg once daily (max: 200 mg) and monitor for adverse effects if concomitant use with adagrasib is necessary. Concomitant use may increase vamorolone exposure and the risk for vamorolone-related adverse effects. Vamorolone is a CYP3A substrate and adagrasib is a strong CYP3A inhibitor. Concomitant use may increase vamorolone exposure and the risk for vamorolone-related adverse effects. Vamorolone is a CYP3A substrate and voriconazole is a strong CYP3A inhibitor. Yellow Fever Vaccine, Live: (Contraindicated) Avoid the administration of live virus vaccines with high-dose corticosteroid therapy and for at least 1 month following treatment. High-dose corticosteroid therapy is generally defined as a dose of at least 20 mg/day of prednisone or equivalent (or 2 mg/kg/day for patients weighing less than 10 kg) for at least 14 consecutive days. When feasible, administer indicated live virus vaccines at least 4 weeks before planned high-dose corticosteroid therapy or wait at least 1 month after discontinuation. Patients with altered immunocompetence may be at increased risk for severe adverse reactions due to uninhibited growth of the attenuated live virus. Additionally, vaccine efficacy may be diminished in patients receiving any supraphysiologic dose of corticosteroid.

Description
  Text
  Vamorolone is a novel, oral corticosteroid indicated for the treatment of Duchenne muscular dystrophy (DMD) in persons 2 years of age and older. Vamorolone selectively targets the glucocorticoid receptor with a different pharmacologic profile compared to classic oral corticosteroids, such as prednisone and prednisolone. The pharmacological profile of vamorolone provides antiinflammatory efficacy and a potentially improved safety profile compared to classic corticosteroids, including a possible reduction in bone morbidities. The efficacy of vamorolone was assessed during a multicenter, randomized, double-blind study, during which male participants ages 4 to 7 years received vamorolone 6 mg/kg/day (n = 30), vamorolone 2 mg/kg/day (n = 30), prednisone 0.75 mg/kg/day (n = 31), or placebo (n = 30) for 24 weeks. The change from baseline to week 24 in Time to Stand Test (TTSTAND) velocity (rises/second) was significantly greater in participants treated with vamorolone 6 mg/kg/day compared to participants treated with placebo, with a difference from placebo of 0.06 (95% CI 0.023 to 0.098; p = 0.002). TTSTAND velocity is a measure of muscle function that measures the time required for the participant to stand to an erect position from a supine position (floor). The change from baseline to week 24 in TTSTAND velocity was also significantly greater in participants treated with vamorolone 2 mg/kg/day compared to participants treated with placebo, with a difference from placebo of 0.045 (95% CI 0.008 to 0.082; p = 0.017). Treatment with vamorolone did not produce significant linear growth delays and height percentile reductions that were associated with prednisone treatment. Vamorolone was also not associated with declines in bone turnover markers. Common adverse reactions reported during clinical trials of vamorolone include Cushingoid features, psychiatric disorders, weight gain, and vomiting.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Vamorolone exerts antiinflammatory and immunosuppressive effects by acting on the glucocorticoid receptor. Vamorolone inhibits nuclear factor kappa B proinflammatory pathways, and demonstrates less positive gene transcriptional activity than other corticosteroids. Vamorolone is a potent antagonist of the mineralocorticoid receptor, while most other corticosteroids are agonists. Modulatory 11 beta-hydroxysteroid dehydrogenase enzymes, which are known to be necessary for moderation of corticosteroid-associated bone morbidities in animals, cannot act on vamorolone. The precise mechanism by which vamorolone exerts its therapeutic effects in persons with Duchenne muscular dystrophy (DMD) is unknown.
