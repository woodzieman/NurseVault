Drug Class: Carbachol; brimonidine ophthalmic solution is a combination consisting of a cholinergic agonist (carbachol) and an alpha-adrenergic receptor agonist (brimonidine) indicated for the treatment of presbyopia in adults.

Routes: ophthalmic; Common adult doses: 1 drop/day in each eye.

Common Side Effects (excluding routine GI upset): application site reaction, ocular irritation, ocular pain, visual impairment

In an Overdose: No overdose/antidote information is listed in this file.

  Contraindications And Precautions
    Headline
    Drug Interactions
    General Information
    The coadministration of certain medications may lead to harm and require avoidance or therapy modification; review all drug interactions prior to concomitant use of other medications.
    Headline
    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components.
    Headline
    Treatment-Related Restrictions of Use
    General Information
    Presbyopia does not occur in the pediatric population, therefore this ophthalmic solution is not indicated in adolescents, children or infants.
    Headline
    retinal disease
    General Information
    Use carbachol; brimonidine with caution in people with preexisting retinal disease. Miotic agents have been associated with rare cases of retinal tear and retinal detachment. A retinal examination is recommended prior to initiation of therapy. Treated individuals should be instructed to seek immediate medical care if they experience sudden onset of flashes of light, floaters, or vision loss.
    Headline
    iritis
    General Information
    Carbachol; brimonidine is not recommended to be used when iritis is present, as sequelae of ocular inflammation (i.e., adhesions or synechiae) between the iris and the lens may be exacerbated with miotic use.
    Headline
    activities requiring coordination and concentration, driving or operating machinery
    General Information
    Carbachol; brimonidine may cause temporary blurred, dim, or dark vision. Patients should be cautioned against driving or operating machinery or engaging in activities requiring coordination and concentration, particularly under poor illumination, until visual effects resolve.
    Headline
    cerebrovascular insufficiency, coronary insufficiency, depression, orthostatic hypotension, Raynaud phenomenon, thromboangiitis obliterans
    General Information
    Use carbachol; brimonidine with caution in people with conditions associated with vascular insufficiency, including cerebrovascular insufficiency or coronary insufficiency, Raynaud phenomenon, orthostatic hypotension, thromboangiitis obliterans, or depression, as the systemic alpha-adrenergic effects of brimonidine may potentiate these conditions.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    There are no adequate and well-controlled studies of carbachol; brimonidine administration during pregnancy to inform a drug-associated risk. Topical ocular administration of carbachol; brimonidine to pregnant rats throughout organogenesis did not produce adverse effects up to 3 times the maximum recommended human ocular dose (MRHOD). Because animal reproductive studies are not always predictive of human response, carbachol; brimonidine should be administered during pregnancy only if the potential benefit justifies the potential risk to the fetus.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Use carbachol; brimonidine ophthalmic solution with caution during breast-feeding. There is no information regarding the presence of carbachol and brimonidine tartrate in human milk, the effects on breastfed infants, or the effects on milk production to inform risk of carbachol; brimonidine to an infant during lactation. There are low systemic levels of carbachol and brimonidine tartrate following topical ocular administration of carbachol; brimonidine, and it is not known whether measurable levels of carbachol and brimonidine tartrate would be present in human milk following maternal topical ocular administration. The developmental and health benefits of breast-feeding should be considered along with the clinical need for carbachol; brimonidine and any potential adverse effects on the breastfed child from carbachol; brimonidine. During ophthalmic administration in the lactating individual, systemic absorption can be minimized by applying pressure over the tear duct for at least 1 minute after ophthalmic administration and removing any excess solution.

Description
  Text
  Carbachol; brimonidine ophthalmic solution is a combination consisting of a cholinergic agonist (carbachol) and an alpha-adrenergic receptor agonist (brimonidine) indicated for the treatment of presbyopia in adults. The combination works by inducing pupillary constriction and creating a pinhole effect, which improves near visual acuity and depth of focus. In clinical trials, treatment demonstrated improvements in binocular uncorrected near visual acuity without clinically meaningful loss of distance vision under mesopic conditions. The combination product was first approved by the FDA in 2026.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Carbachol and brimonidine are used together to treat presbyopia. Pupil size was assessed using automated digital pupillometry under mesopic conditions during clinical studies. Average pupil diameter reduction following carbachol; brimonidine ophthalmic solution was significantly superior to both active agents alone from pre-dose baseline to Hour 0.5 through Hour 10 in the BRIO I study, and to vehicle from pre-dose baseline to Hour 0.5 through Hour 8 in the BRIO II study.
Carbachol: Carbachol is a direct-acting cholinergic agonist that stimulates muscarinic receptors in the iris sphincter and ciliary muscle, resulting in pupillary constriction (miosis) and increased depth of focus. This pupillary constriction produces a pinhole effect that improves near visual acuity in patients with presbyopia.
Brimonidine: Brimonidine tartrate is an alpha-adrenergic receptor agonist that inhibits contraction of the iris dilator muscle and relaxes tonic contraction of the ciliary muscle. In combination with carbachol, brimonidine enhances pupillary selectivity and increases the ocular bioavailability of carbachol within the aqueous humor, contributing to sustained miosis and improved near vision.

Pharmacokinetics
  Text
  Carbachol; brimonidine tartrate is administered via topical ophthalmic instillation. Following administration of 1 drop in each eye, systemic exposure is low. Brimonidine is approximately 29% bound to plasma proteins. The plasma protein-binding of carbachol has not been studied. Brimonidine is extensively metabolized by the liver. The metabolism of carbachol has not been studied. Brimonidine and its metabolites are primarily eliminated via the urine, with approximately 87% of an orally administered dose recovered within 120 hours, mainly in urine. The excretion pathway of carbachol has not been studied.
 
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes and drug transporters: None
