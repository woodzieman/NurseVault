    Headline
    Drug Interactions
    General Information
    The coadministration of certain medications may lead to harm and require avoidance or therapy modification; review all drug interactions prior to concomitant use of other medications.
    Headline
    infection
    General Information
    Spesolimab may increase the risk of infections. Do not administer to patients with any clinically important active infection, including active tuberculosis, until the infection resolves or is adequately treated; evaluate all potential recipients for tuberculosis infection before administering spesolimab. In patients with chronic infections or history of recurrent infections, consider the potential risks and expected benefits before initiating treatment, and in patients with latent tuberculosis or a history of tuberculosis in whom an adequate course of treatment cannot be confirmed, consider initiating antituberculosis therapy before initiating treatment. Instruct patients to seek medical advice if signs or symptoms of a clinically important infection occur during or after treatment. If a patient develops a clinically important active infection, discontinue therapy until the infection resolves or is adequately treated. Monitor patients closely for signs and symptoms of active tuberculosis during and after treatment with spesolimab.
    Headline
    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components. Hypersensitivity reactions to spesolimab may include immediate reactions such as anaphylaxis or delayed reactions such as drug reaction with eosinophilia and systemic symptoms (DRESS); cases of DRESS were reported during clinical trials in patients who received spesolimab. If a patient develops signs of anaphylaxis or other serious hypersensitivity, discontinue spesolimab immediately and initiate appropriate treatment. If mild or moderate hypersensitivity or other infusion-related reactions are observed during an infusion, stop the spesolimab infusion and consider appropriate medical therapy (e.g., systemic antihistamines or corticosteroids). Upon resolution of the reaction, the infusion may be restarted at a slower rate with gradual increases to complete the infusion. If the infusion is slowed or temporarily stopped, the total infusion time (including stop time) must not exceed 180 minutes.
    Headline
    General Information
    General Information
    Avoid vaccination with live vaccines during and for at least 16 weeks after treatment with spesolimab. No data are available on the ability of live vaccines to elicit an immune response in patients being treated with spesolimab. Consider completing all age appropriate immunizations prior to initiating spesolimab therapy.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Available data on the use of spesolimab during pregnancy are insufficient to evaluate a drug-associated risk of major birth defects, miscarriage, or other adverse outcomes. Human IgG crosses the placental barrier; therefore, spesolimab may be transmitted to the fetus. In animal studies, no embryo-fetal lethality or fetal malformations were observed in mice administered a surrogate mouse-specific IL36R antagonist monoclonal antibody intravenously at doses up to 50 mg/kg twice-weekly during organogenesis. In a pre- and postnatal study in mice, no treatment-related effects were observed on the postnatal developmental, neurobehavioral, or reproductive performance of offspring exposed to spesolimab at 50 mg/kg twice-weekly from gestation day 6 through lactation day 18.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Use spesolimab with caution during breast-feeding. There are no data on the presence of spesolimab in human milk, its effects on the breast-fed child, or its effect on milk production. Maternal antibodies are present in human milk; spesolimab is expected to pass into human milk. Consider the benefits of breast-feeding, the patient's clinical need for treatment, and any potential adverse effects on the breast-fed child from the medication or from the patient's underlying medical condition.

Adverse Reactions
  Headline
  Adverse Reactions
  Adverse Reactions
    Headline
    candidiasis, infection, influenza
    General Information
    Infections occurred in 14% of patients who received IV spesolimab in clinical trials (n = 35). Specific infections reported during the 1-week placebo-controlled period included urinary tract infection (6%), bacteruria (3%), bacteremia (3%), cellulitis (3%), herpes dermatitis and oral herpes (3%), and upper respiratory tract infection (3%). Most infections reported through week 1 were mild (29%) or moderate (71%) in severity, with only 1 serious infection (urinary tract infection) being reported in the spesolimab group. Additional mild to moderate infections or infectious complications that occurred in patients who received spesolimab during the open-label rescue period of the study (i.e., week 1 through week 17) included device-related infection (3%), subcutaneous abscess (3%), furuncle (3%), influenza (3%), otitis externa (7%), vulvovaginal candidiasis (4%), vulvovaginal mycotic infection (4%), and latent tuberculosis (4%). An increased incidence of urinary tract infection (18 per 100-patient years) was observed with subcutaneous spesolimab in clinical trials (n = 30).

Anaphylactoid reactions, including anaphylaxis, have been reported during postmarketing use with spesolimab.

    antibody formation
    General Information
    The development of immunogenicity (antibody formation) may occur with the use of spesolimab. During the IV clinical trial, patients who received spesolimab developed anti-drug antibodies (ADAs) at a median onset of 2.3 weeks; 24% of drug recipients had a maximum ADA titer greater than 4,000 and were neutralizing antibody-positive by the end of the trial (weeks 12 to 17). In the subcutaneous trial, ADAs developed at a median onset of 8 weeks; 24% of drug recipients had a maximum ADA titer greater than 4,000 and were neutralizing antibody-positive by the end of the trial (week 48). The data on immunogenicity response in males versus females were inconclusive.

Description
  Text
  Spesolimab is an interleukin-36 receptor (IL36R) antagonist indicated for acute and maintenance treatment of generalized pustular psoriasis (GPP) in adults and pediatric patients 12 years and older weighing at least 40 kg. Intravenous administration is indicated for the treatment of GPP flares while subcutaneous administration is indicated for maintenance treatment to reduce the occurrence of flares. In a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled study (n = 53), treatment with a single intravenous dose of spesolimab significantly improved signs and symptoms of GPP in patients experiencing a flare, including rapid pustular and skin clearance. In a 48-week clinical trial (n = 123) for subcutaneous use, high-dose spesolimab (600 mg loading dose followed by 300 mg every 4 weeks) significantly reduced the risk of GPP flares by 84%, compared with placebo (hazard ratio, 0.16; 95% CI: 0.05 to 0.54; p = 0.005). No flares were observed after week 4 of spesolimab subcutaneous treatment in the high-dose group (n = 30). Intravenous administration has been associated with significant hypersensitivity and infusion-related reactions. As with other interleukin inhibitors, spesolimab may increase the risk of infection.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Spesolimab is a humanized monoclonal immunoglobulin G1 (IgG1) antibody that inhibits interleukin-36 (IL-36) signaling by specifically binding to the IL-36 receptor (IL36R). By binding to IL36R, spesolimab prevents activation of IL36R by cognate ligands (IL-36 alpha, beta, and gamma) and downstream activation of proinflammatory and profibrotic pathways. The exact mechanism linking reduced IL36R activity with treatment of generalized pustular psoriasis flares is unclear.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Intravenous Route
    Description
    Based on a population pharmacokinetic model, the estimated exposure (AUC) and maximum plasma concentration (Cmax) in a typical anti-drug antibody-negative patient with generalized pustular psoriasis after a single intravenous dose of 900 mg spesolimab are 4,750 mcg x day/mL and 238 mcg/mL, respectively. The AUC increases dose-proportionally from 0.3 to 20 mg/kg.
    Name
    Subcutaneous Route
    Description
    After subcutaneous single dose administration of spesolimab in healthy volunteers, peak plasma concentrations were achieved between 5.5 to 7 days. After subcutaneous administration in the abdomen, absolute bioavailability was slightly higher at higher doses with estimated values of 58% (51% to 66%), 65% (60% to 69%), and 72% (65% to 79%) at 150 mg, 300 mg, and 600 mg, respectively. Based on limited data, absolute bioavailability in the thigh was approximately 85% (77% to 94%) after a subcutaneous dose of 300 mg. Spesolimab exposure increased slightly more than dose-proportionally across the dose range of 150 to 600 mg due to increased bioavailability at higher doses. After a 600 mg loading dose, followed by 300 mg every 4 weeks, the mean steady-state trough concentration of spesolimab ranged from 33.4 to 42.3 mcg/mL.

  Text
  Spesolimab is administered intravenously and subcutaneously. The typical total volume of distribution at steady state is 6.4 L. Although the metabolic pathway has not been characterized, spesolimab is expected to be degraded to small peptides and amino acids via catabolic pathways in the same manner as endogenous human IgG. Drug clearance in a typical generalized pustular psoriasis patient without anti-drug antibodies weighing 70 kg is 0.184 L/day. The terminal half-life is 25.5 days. Over a dosing rage of 0.3 to 20 mg/kg, the clearance and terminal half-life are independent of the dose.

      Dilution
Ensure aseptic technique is used when preparing the solution for infusion.

Intravenous Infusion
Administer as a continuous infusion over 90 minutes through an IV line containing a sterile, non-pyrogenic, low protein binding in-line filter (pore size of 0.2 micron). NOTE: no incompatibilities have been observed between spesolimab and:
Infusion sets composed of polyvinylchloride (PVC), polyethylene (PE), polypropylene (PP), or polybutadiene and polyurethane (PUR)
In-line filter membranes composed of polyethersulfone (PES, neutral and positively charged) or positively charged polyamide (PA)
A preexisting IV line may be used for administration of the infusion if the line is flushed with sterile 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection prior to and at the end of the infusion. No other infusion should be administered in parallel via the same IV access.
If the infusion is slowed or temporarily stopped, the total infusion time (including stop time) must not exceed 180 minutes.

      Subcutaneous Administration
      Description
      The product is intended for use under the guidance and supervision of a health care professional.
When using the 300 mg/2 mL prefilled syringe:
If appropriate, patients 12 years and older may self-inject or the caregiver may administer the loading dose and subsequent doses after proper training in subcutaneous injection technique. Administer injections under the supervision of an adult in pediatric patients 12 to 17 years.
When using the 150 mg/1 mL prefilled syringe:
If required, the 600 mg subcutaneous loading dose is to be administered by a health care professional.
For subsequent 300 mg doses, patients 12 years and older may self-inject or caregivers may give subcutaneous injections after proper training. Administer injections under the supervision of an adult in pediatric patients 12 to 17 years.
Before subcutaneous injection, remove the prefilled syringe(s) from the refrigerator and allow to reach room temperature (15 to 30 minutes) without removing the needle cap.
Administer subcutaneously in the upper thighs or abdomen. Do not inject into areas where the skin is tender, bruised, erythematous, indurated, or scarred.
If more than 1 injection is needed to achieve the recommended dose, administer each injection one right after the other. Choose a different injection site for each injection, at least 1 inch away from the other injection sites.
Alternate between the upper thigh and abdomen injection sites for each complete dose.
If a dose is missed, administer the dose as soon as possible. Thereafter, resume dosing at the regular scheduled time.
Storage: Store in the refrigerator between 2 and 8 degrees C (36 to 46 degrees F) in the original carton. Do not freeze and protect from light. If needed, the 300 mg/2 mL prefilled syringe may be stored at room temperature up to 25 degrees C (77 degrees F) for up to 14 days. Keep it in the original packaging and protect from light.
