Drug Class: Dabigatran is an oral direct thrombin inhibitor is indicated in adults for stroke and systemic embolism prophylaxis in nonvalvular atrial fibrillation.

Routes: oral; Common adult doses: 300 mg/day PO (oral capsules); safety and efficacy of oral pellets have not been established.

In an Overdose: No overdose/antidote information is listed in this file.

Indication Specific Dosing
  General Information
  General Dosing Information
There are differences between the dabigatran dosage forms with respect to dosing due to differences in bioavailability. Do not substitute different dosage forms (for example, capsules) for oral pellets on a milligram-to-milligram basis and do not combine more than 1 dosage form to achieve the total dose.
 
Switching from another anticoagulant to dabigatran
Conversion from warfarin to dabigatran: Discontinue warfarin and initiate dabigatran therapy when the INR is less than 2.
Conversion from a parenteral anticoagulant to dabigatran: Initiate dabigatran 0 to 2 hours before the time of the next scheduled anticoagulant dose or at the time of discontinuation of a continuously administered anticoagulant (e.g., intravenous unfractionated heparin).
 
Switching from dabigatran to another anticoagulant
Conversion from dabigatran to warfarin: If continuous anticoagulation is necessary, continue dabigatran and begin warfarin. Discontinue dabigatran when INR is therapeutic. Dabigatran use can contribute to an elevated INR; therefore, the INR will better reflect the effect of warfarin only after dabigatran has been discontinued for 2 days. Initiate warfarin based on creatinine clearance according to the recommendations below:
Adults
CrCl more than 50 mL/minute: Start warfarin 3 days before discontinuing dabigatran.
CrCl 31 to 50 mL/minute: Start warfarin 2 days before discontinuing dabigatran.
CrCl 15 to 30 mL/minute: Start warfarin 1 day before discontinuing dabigatran.
CrCl less than 15 mL/minute: No recommendations are available.
Pediatrics
eGFR 50 mL/minute/1.73 m2 or more: Start warfarin 3 days before discontinuing dabigatran
eGFR less than 50 mL/minute/1.73 m2: Avoid use as dabigatran has not been studied in these patients.
Conversion from dabigatran to a parenteral anticoagulant: When initiating a parenteral anticoagulant, discontinue dabigatran. Then, start the parenteral anticoagulant according to the recommendations below:
Adults
CrCl 30 mL/minute or more: Wait 12 hours after the last dabigatran dose.
CrCl less than 30 mL/minute: Wait 24 hours after the last dabigatran dose.
Pediatrics
Wait 12 hours after the last dabigatran dose.

        Dosage Description
        150 mg PO twice daily. Coadministration of certain drugs may need to be avoided or dosage adjustments may be necessary; review drug interactions. Data from the RE-LY trial (n = 18,113) indicate dabigatran 150 mg PO twice daily was associated with lower rates of stroke and systemic embolism (2.2% vs. 3.4%; HR 0.65, CI 0.52 to 0.81; p less than 0.0001) compared to warfarin. For eligible patients, direct oral anticoagulants are preferred over warfarin therapy.

          Dosage Description
          110 mg PO as a single dose starting 1 to 4 hours after surgery, then 220 mg PO once daily. If dabigatran is not started on the day of surgery, initiate treatment with 220 mg PO once daily. Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  Contraindications And Precautions
    Headline
    Drug Interactions
    General Information
    The coadministration of certain medications may lead to harm and require avoidance or therapy modification; review all drug interactions prior to concomitant use of other medications.
    Headline
    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components.
    Headline
    premature discontinuation
    General Information
    Avoid premature discontinuation of dabigatran, as stopping therapy without adequate alternative anticoagulation increases the risk of thrombotic events. If discontinuation is necessary for reasons other than pathological bleeding or completion of therapy, consider alternative anticoagulation and resume dabigatran as soon as clinically appropriate.
    Headline
    neuraxial procedure, spinal deformity, spinal hematoma, spinal surgery
    General Information
    Carefully consider the potential benefits and risks before performing a neuraxial procedure in individuals who are receiving or are expected to receive dabigatran. Epidural or spinal hematoma, which may result in long-term or permanent paralysis, can occur in anticoagulated individuals undergoing neuraxial anesthesia or spinal puncture. Risk factors for epidural or spinal hematoma include use of an indwelling epidural catheter, concomitant use of other agents that affect hemostasis (e.g., NSAIDs, platelet inhibitors, other anticoagulants), a history of spinal deformity or spinal surgery, and traumatic or repeated epidural or spinal puncture. To reduce the risk of bleeding, consider the pharmacokinetic profile of dabigatran and perform placement or removal of an epidural catheter or lumbar puncture when the anticoagulant effect is expected to be low; however, the exact timing to reach low anticoagulant effect is not known. If a neuraxial procedure (e.g., epidural or spinal anesthesia, lumbar puncture) is performed, monitor individuals undergoing these procedures frequently for neurological impairment and instruct them to report symptoms immediately. If an epidural or spinal hematoma is suspected, urgently diagnose and treat, including consideration of spinal cord decompression.
    Headline
    bleeding, bleeding disorder, major bleeding
    General Information
    Dabigatran is contraindicated in individuals with active major bleeding. It increases the risk of bleeding and can cause serious and potentially fatal bleeding. The risk of bleeding is higher in individuals with a history of bleeding or a bleeding disorder. Additionally, the risk may be increased with concomitant use of drugs affecting hemostasis, such as aspirin and other antiplatelet agents, other antithrombotic agents, thrombolytic agents, chronic use of nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs), selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors (SSRIs), and serotonin norepinephrine reuptake inhibitors (SNRIs).
    Headline
    bioprosthetic heart valves, mechanical heart valves, mitral stenosis
    General Information
    Dabigatran is contraindicated in individuals with mechanical heart valves. In the RE-ALIGN trial, which compared dabigatran (150, 220, or 300 mg twice daily) with dose-adjusted warfarin in adults with bileaflet mechanical heart valves implanted either within 3 days postoperatively or more than 3 months before enrollment, the study was terminated early due to higher rates of thromboembolic events (valve thrombosis, stroke, and myocardial infarction) and major bleeding in the dabigatran group compared with warfarin. All major bleeding events involved pericardial bleeding, and both thromboembolic complications and major bleeding occurred in those treated with dabigatran shortly after surgery as well as in those with longer-standing valve implants. Dabigatran has not been adequately studied in individuals with bioprosthetic heart valves or in those with moderate to severe mitral stenosis and therefore is not recommended.
    Headline
    planned procedure
    General Information
    In adults, discontinue dabigatran 1 to 2 days before a planned procedure or surgery if CrCl is 50 mL/minute or more, or 3 to 5 days before if CrCl is less than 50 mL/minute. In pediatric patients, discontinue dabigatran 24 hours before a planned procedure if eGFR is more than 80 mL/minute/1.73 m2, or 2 days before if eGFR is 50 to 80 mL/minute/1.73 m2. Consider a longer discontinuation for individuals undergoing major surgery, spinal puncture, or placement of a spinal or epidural catheter or port, in whom complete hemostasis may be required. If the procedure or surgery cannot be delayed, weigh the increased risk of bleeding against the urgency of the intervention. In adults requiring emergency surgery or urgent procedures, consider idarucizumab, a specific dabigatran reversal agent. The safety and efficacy of idarucizumab have not been established in pediatric patients. Restart dabigatran as soon as medically appropriate.
    Headline
    renal failure, renal impairment
    General Information
    Dabigatran dosage adjustments are required in adults with renal impairment or renal failure, and treatment avoidance may be necessary depending on the indication and concomitant use of P-glycoprotein (P-gp) inhibitors. Anticoagulant activity and half-life are increased in individuals with renal impairment, and renal impairment together with P-gp inhibition are major independent risk factors for increased dabigatran exposure and bleeding. In pediatric patients, avoid use of dabigatran with moderate to severe renal impairment (eGFR less than 50 mL/minute/1.73 m2), as safety and efficacy have not been established and there is a risk of increased exposure. The concomitant use of dabigatran and P-gp inhibitors has not been studied in pediatric patients but may further increase exposure. Assess renal function before initiating therapy and periodically during treatment, particularly in situations that may lead to renal decline. Discontinue dabigatran and consider alternative anticoagulants if acute renal failure develops.
    Headline
    antiphospholipid antibody syndrome
    General Information
    Dabigatran is not recommended in individuals with triple-positive antiphospholipid antibody syndrome (APS), defined as positivity for lupus anticoagulant, anticardiolipin, and anti-beta-2 glycoprotein I antibodies. Higher rates of recurrent thrombotic and bleeding events have occurred with use of direct oral anticoagulants (DOACs) in this population compared with vitamin K antagonists.
    Headline
    geriatric
    General Information
    According to the Beers Criteria, use caution when selecting dabigatran over other DOACs (e.g., apixaban) for long-term treatment of venous thromboembolism (VTE) or nonvalvular atrial fibrillation in geriatric adults, as dabigatran has been associated with an increased risk of gastrointestinal (GI) bleeding compared with warfarin (based on head-to-head clinical trials) and with increased risks of GI and major bleeding compared with apixaban (based on observational studies and meta-analyses). Avoid dabigatran in geriatric adults with creatinine clearance (CrCl) less than 30 mL/minute due to lack of evidence for safety and efficacy; the Beers panel notes that labeling recommendations for patients with CrCl 15 to 30 mL/minute are based only on pharmacokinetic data.
    Headline
    labor, obstetric delivery, pregnancy
    General Information
    Use of dabigatran during pregnancy and obstetric delivery may increase the risk of bleeding in both the pregnant person and the fetus or neonate. In addition, there is a risk of epidural or spinal hematoma associated with the use of dabigatran in patients receiving neuraxial anesthesia during labor and obstetric delivery. Consider the use of a shorter acting anticoagulant as delivery approaches. There are limited data on the use of dabigatran during human pregnancy to inform a drug-associated risk of adverse developmental outcomes. In animal studies, a decreased number of implantations was observed when male and female rats were given dabigatran doses resulting in 2.6 to 3 times the human exposure at the maximum recommended human dose (MRHD), based on AUC comparisons, prior to mating and up to gestation day 6. Treatment of pregnant rats after implantation at the same dose increased the number of dead offspring and caused vaginal/uterine bleeding close to parturition. Although major malformations were not observed, delayed or irregular ossification of fetal skull bones and vertebrae was seen when pregnant rats were given doses resulting in 8 and 13 times the human exposure at the MRHD during the period of organogenesis.
    Headline
    people who may become pregnant, reproductive risk
    General Information
    Counsel people who may become pregnant about the reproductive risk associated with dabigatran. Discuss pregnancy planning with people of reproductive potential requiring anticoagulation. Assess the risk of clinically significant uterine bleeding, potentially requiring gynecological surgical interventions, in people of reproductive potential and those with abnormal uterine bleeding.
    Headline
    neonates and infants exposed to this medication in utero
    General Information
    Monitor neonates and infants exposed to this medication in utero for bleeding.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Use dabigatran with caution during breast-feeding. FDA-approved labeling states that breast-feeding is not recommended during treatment with dabigatran; there are no data on its effects on the breastfed child or its effects on milk production. However, dabigatran has a large molecular weight and poor oral bioavailability (3% to 7% based on adult data); therefore, significant exposure in the breastfed child via human milk is not expected. Data from 2 lactating patients taking dabigatran 220 mg daily calculated a maternal weight-adjusted dosage of 0.01% and 0.07%. Due to limited data, if dabigatran is used during breast-feeding, monitor the breastfed child for signs of bleeding.

Intravenous Lipid Emulsions: (Moderate) Drug interactions with fish oil, omega-3 fatty acids (Dietary Supplements) or fish oil, omega-3 fatty acids (FDA-approved) are unclear at this time. However, because fish oil, omega-3 fatty acids inhibit platelet aggregation, caution is advised when fish oils are used concurrently with anticoagulants, platelet inhibitors, or thrombolytic agents. Theoretically, the risk of bleeding may be increased, but some studies that combined these agents did not produce clinically significant bleeding events. In one placebo-controlled, randomized, double-blinded, parallel study, patients receiving stable, chronic warfarin therapy were administered various doses of fish oil supplements to determine the effect on INR determinations. Patients were randomized to receive a 4-week treatment period of either placebo or 3 or 6 grams of fish oil daily. Patients were followed on a twice-weekly basis for INR determinations and adverse reactions. There was no statistically significant difference in INRs between the placebo or treatment period within each group. There was also no difference in INRs found between groups. One episode of ecchymosis was reported, but no major bleeding episodes occurred. The authors concluded that fish oil supplementation in doses of 3-6 grams per day does not have a statistically significant effect on the INR of patients receiving chronic warfarin therapy. However, an increase in INR from 2.8 to 4.3 in a patient stable on warfarin therapy has been reported when increasing the dose of fish oil, omega-3 fatty acids from 1 gram/day to 2 grams/day. The INR decreased once the patient decreased her dose of fish oil to 1 gram/day. This implies that a dose-related effect of fish oil on warfarin may be possible. Patients receiving warfarin that initiate concomitant fish oil therapy should have their INR monitored more closely and the dose of warfarin adjusted accordingly.

    bleeding, epistaxis, GI bleeding, intracranial bleeding, menorrhagia, pericardial effusion, spinal hematoma, vaginal bleeding
    General Information
    Bleeding (up to 20%) has been reported during clinical trials of dabigatran and cases of serious, sometimes fatal, bleeding have been reported. A study conducted by the FDA in Medicare patients age 65 years or older found the risk of intracranial bleeding was lower with dabigatran compared to warfarin [3.3 per 1,000 person-years vs. 9.6 per 1,000 person-years (HR 0.34, 95% CI 0.26 to 0.46)]. However, there was an increased risk of major GI bleeding with dabigatran compared to warfarin [34.2 per 1,000 person-years vs. 26.5 per 1,000 person-years (HR 1.28, 95% CI 1.14 to 1.44)]. In the RE-LY trial, which compared safety and efficacy of 110 mg and 150 mg dabigatran against warfarin, bleeding was reported in 16.6% of patients in the dabigatran 150 mg group compared to 18.4% in the warfarin group (HR 0.91, 95% CI 0.85 to 0.96). Intracranial bleeding was reported in 0.3% of patients taking 150 mg dabigatran compared to 0.8% of those taking warfarin (HR 0.41, 95% CI 0.28 to 0.6). Life-threatening bleed (defined as fatal, symptomatic intracranial bleed, reduction in hemoglobin of 5 g/dL or greater, transfusion of at least 4 units of blood, hypotension requiring inotropic agents, or necessitating surgical intervention) was reported in 1.5% of patients in the dabigatran 150 mg group compared to 1.9% in the warfarin group (HR 0.8, 95% CI 0.66 to 0.98). Major bleed (defined as bleeding associated with a reduction of hemoglobin of 2 g/dL or greater or symptomatic bleeding in a critical area or organ [intraocular, intracranial, intraspinal or intramuscular with compartment syndrome, retroperitoneal bleeding, intra-articular bleeding, or pericardial bleeding]) occurred in 3.3% of patients taking dabigatran 150 mg compared to 3.6% of those taking warfarin (HR 0.93, 95% CI 0.81 to 1.07). There was a higher risk of major bleeding for elderly patients aged 75 years or older (HR 1.2, 95% CI 1 to 1.4). GI bleeding was reported at a higher rate in patients taking dabigatran compared to warfarin (6.1% vs. 4%), and the rate of major GI bleeding was higher in patients in the 150 mg dabigatran arm compared to warfarin (1.6% vs. 1.1%, HR 1.5, 95% CI 1.2 to 1.9). In clinical trials comparing dabigatran to warfarin for treatment and reduction in the risk of recurrence of DVT and pulmonary embolism, bleeding was reported in 16.1% to 19.4% of patients in the dabigatran group compared to 22.7% to 26.2% in the warfarin group. Intracranial bleeding was reported in 0.1% of patients taking dabigatran compared to 0.2% to 0.3% of those taking warfarin. Major bleeding occurred in 0.9% to 1.4% of patients taking dabigatran compared to 1.8% to 2% of those taking warfarin. The rate of any GI bleeding was 3.1% in patients taking dabigatran 150 mg compared to 2.2% to 2.4% in patients taking warfarin. Data from the RE-ALIGN trial indicate that major bleeding occurs more often in patients with mechanical heart valves being treated with dabigatran compared to those treated with warfarin (4% vs. 2%). All patients with major bleeding had pericardial bleeding (pericardial effusion requiring intervention for hemodynamic compromise), which occurred within 2 weeks after surgery. Bleeding of any kind was reported in 45 patients (27%) receiving dabigatran compared to 10 patients (12%) receiving warfarin. The RE-ALIGN trial was terminated early due to significantly more thromboembolic events and major bleeding reported in patients receiving dabigatran. Although not reported in clinical trials, patients in whom epidural/spinal anesthesia or spinal/epidural puncture has been employed are at risk of developing spinal hematoma. Vaginal bleeding, including from abnormal/dysfunctional uterine bleeding, has been reported with use of dabigatran in patients of reproductive potential. In pediatric clinical trials (n = 379), major bleeding (defined as symptomatic bleeding in a critical area or organ (intraocular, intracranial, intraspinal or intramuscular with compartment syndrome, retroperitoneal bleeding, intra-articular bleeding, or pericardial bleeding), bleeding causing a fall in hemoglobin of 2 g/dL (1.24 mmol/L) or more, or leading to transfusion of 2 or more units of whole blood or red cells) was reported in approximately 2% of patients, clinically relevant non-major bleeding was reported in approximately 1% of patients, and minor bleeding was reported in approximately 20% of patients. Intracranial bleeding was reported in 1 patient receiving dabigatran. Bleeding rates were similar in the standard of care group (warfarin, low molecular weight heparin, or fondaparinux). Epistaxis (3% to 5%), GI bleeding (1%), and menorrhagia (3%) were also reported in pediatric patients in clinical trials (n = 379).

    Headline
    myocardial infarction, stroke, thrombosis
    General Information
    There are conflicting data regarding the rate of myocardial infarction in patients taking dabigatran compared to warfarin. The FDA conducted a large (n greater than 134,000) observational cohort study in Medicare patients 65 years or older with non-valvular atrial fibrillation. The findings from this study indicate the rate of myocardial infarction with dabigatran 75 mg or 150 mg was similar to warfarin [15.7 per 1000 person-years vs. 16.9 per 1000 person-years (HR 0.92, 95% CI 0.78 to 1.08)]. Dabigatran was also associated with a lower risk of ischemic stroke [11.3 per 1000 person-years vs. 13.9 per 1000 person-years (HR 0.8, 95% CI 0.67 to 0.96)] and death [32.6 per 1000 person-years vs. 37.8 per 1000 person-years (HR 0.86, 95% CI 0.77 to 0.96)] than warfarin. Data from the RE-LY trial indicate that the rate of clinical myocardial infarction was higher in the dabigatran 150 mg group compared to warfarin (0.74% per year vs. 0.53% per year, RR 1.38, CI 1.00 to 1.91; p = 0.048). In clinical trials comparing dabigatran to warfarin for treatment or prevention of recurrence of DVT or pulmonary embolism, a higher rate of clinical myocardial infarction was reported in patients who received dabigatran [20 (0.66 per 100 patient-years)] than in those who received warfarin [5 (0.17 per 100 patient-years)]. In the placebo-controlled study, the rates of non-fatal and fatal clinical myocardial infarction reported in patients who received dabigatran [1 (0.32 per 100 patient-years)] and in those who received placebo [1 (0.34 per 100 patient-years)] were similar. Data from the RE-ALIGN trial indicate that more thromboembolic events occur in patients with mechanical heart valves being treated with dabigatran compared to those treated with warfarin. Among patients receiving dabigatran, stroke was reported in 9 patients (5%), valve thrombosis was reported in 5 patients (3%), and myocardial infarction was reported in 3 patients (2%); none of these events were reported in patients receiving warfarin. Transient ischemic attack (TIA) occurred in 3 patients (2%) receiving dabigatran and in 2 patients (2%) receiving warfarin. The RE-ALIGN trial was terminated early due to significantly more thromboembolic events and major bleeding reported in patients receiving dabigatran.

Description
  Text
  Dabigatran is an oral direct thrombin inhibitor is indicated in adults for stroke and systemic embolism prophylaxis in nonvalvular atrial fibrillation. Dabigatran is also indicated for the treatment and prophylaxis of thromboembolism in several clinical contexts in adults and pediatric patients 3 months and older. Routine monitoring of dabigatran is not required. A reversal agent, idarucizumab, is available for adults when the reversal of the anticoagulant effect of dabigatran is needed for emergency surgery, urgent procedures, or in cases of life-threatening or uncontrolled bleeding. The safety and efficacy of idarucizumab have not been established in pediatric patients. Dabigatran was FDA-approved in 2010.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Dabigatran and its active metabolites are competitive, direct thrombin inhibitors that inhibit both free and clot-bound thrombin. Dabigatran prevents thrombin-induced platelet aggregation and the development of a thrombus by preventing the thrombin-mediated conversion of fibrinogen into fibrin during the coagulation cascade.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Oral Route
    Description
    Capsules: The absolute bioavailability after oral administration is 3% to 7%. The Tmax occurs 1 hour after administration in the fasted state. Administration of dabigatran after a high-fat meal delays the Tmax by 2 hours but has no effect on the bioavailability of dabigatran. Oral bioavailability increases by 75% when the pellets are taken without the capsule shell.
Oral pellets: Dabigatran oral pellets show 37% higher relative bioavailability in healthy adults compared to dabigatran capsules. In addition, the relative bioavailability between the 2 dosage forms is age dependent. The relative bioavailability observed in adult patients cannot be translated to pediatric patients. The capsule and oral pellet formulations are not interchangeable.
  Special Populations
    Name
    Hepatic Impairment
    Description
    Although a large inter-subject variability is observed when dabigatran is administered to adult patients with moderate hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh B), there is no evidence of a consistent change in the exposure or pharmacodynamics of dabigatran.
    Name
    Renal Impairment
    Description
    In a trial comparing healthy adult subjects and patients with mild to moderate renal impairment, the AUC of dabigatran increased to 1.5 times normal and the half-life increased to 15 hours after a single dose of 150 mg in patients with mild renal impairment (CrCl 50 to 80 mL/minute). Patients with moderate renal impairment (CrCl 30 to 50 mL/minute) had an increase in AUC of 3.2 times normal, an increase in Cmax of 1.7 times normal, and an increase in half-life to 18 hours after a single dose of 150 mg.
    Name
    Pediatrics
    Description
    In pharmacokinetic studies of dabigatran after multiple doses in pediatric patients from birth to 17 years, patients taking age- and weight-adjusted doses of dabigatran pellets (younger than 12 years of age), had an observed geometric mean steady-state trough concentration of 54.7 ng/mL (25.9 to 88.3 ng/mL, 10th to 90th percentile) compared to the geometric mean steady-state trough concentration of 59.7 ng/mL (26.3 to 146 ng/mL, 10th to 90th percentile) observed in adult patients with DVT/PE taking dabigatran capsules. In patients taking age- and weight-adjusted doses of dabigatran capsules (8 to 17 years), the observed geometric mean steady-state trough concentration was 97.9 ng/mL (63.7 to 151 ng/mL, 10th to 90th percentile) compared to the steady-state geometric mean trough concentration of 59.7 ng/mL (26.3 to 146 ng/mL, 10th to 90th percentile) observed in adult patients with DVT/PE. The elimination half-life of dabigatran in pediatric patients is 12 to 14 hours for capsules and approximately 9 to 11 hours for oral pellets.
 
As in adults, there is a correlation between plasma dabigatran concentrations and the degree of its anticoagulant effect in pediatric patients with venous thromboembolism. The parameters dTT and ECT increased in direct linear proportion to the plasma concentration of dabigatran, whereas aPTT prolongation increases in a nonlinear fashion with dabigatran plasma concentrations. Similar PK/PD relationships for aPTT, ECT and dTT were observed across age groups of pediatric patients (ages 26 days to 17 years) and between pediatric and adult patients with venous thromboembolism. This similarity in PK/PD relationship suggests that similar exposure-response relationship is expected for dabigatran treatment across the pediatric age groups and adult patients.
  Text
  Dabigatran etexilate mesylate is administered orally. It is absorbed as the dabigatran etexilate ester, which is then hydrolyzed forming dabigatran, the active moiety. Dabigatran is approximately 35% bound to plasma proteins with a volume of distribution of 50 to 70 L. Pharmacokinetics are proportional to dose. After oral absorption, dabigatran is metabolized by esterases to 4 active acyl glucuronides that have pharmacological activity similar to dabigatran; each acyl glucuronide accounts for less than 10% of total dabigatran in the plasma. It is eliminated primarily in the urine. The half-life is 12 to 17 hours in healthy adult subjects.
 
Dabigatran prolongs the activated partial thromboplastin time (aPTT), ecarin clotting time (ECT), and thrombin time (TT) at recommended therapeutic doses. The median peak aPTT is approximately 2 times control after an oral dose of 150 mg and is 1.5 times control 12 hours after the last dose, with less than 10% of patients exceeding 2 times control. In patients receiving the 150 mg dose in the RE-LY trial, the median trough aPTT was 52 (40 to 76) seconds and the median trough ECT was 63 (44 to 103) seconds. The INR may or may not be elevated in patients receiving dabigatran and is relatively insensitive to the activity of dabigatran.
 
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes and drug transporter: P-gp
Dabigatran is a substrate of the efflux transporter P-glycoprotein (P-gp) and plasma concentrations may be affected by P-gp inhibitors and inducers. Dabigatran is not an inhibitor, inducer, or substrate of CYP450 enzymes.

Administration
  General Information
  For storage information, see the specific product information within the How Supplied section.
  Route Specific Administrations
    Administration Details
      Name
      Oral Solid Formulations
      Description
      Capsules
Administer without regard to food. May administer with food if gastrointestinal distress occurs.
Swallow capsules whole with a full glass of water. Do not open, break, crush, or chew prior to or during administration. Breaking, chewing, or emptying the contents of the capsule could result in increased systemic exposure of dabigatran, which may increase the risk for bleeding.
Missed dose: If a dose is missed, administer as soon as possible on the same day. However, skip the missed dose if it cannot be taken at least 6 hours before the next scheduled dose.
 
Oral pellets
Administer the prepared medication before meals to ensure that the patient takes the full dose.
Remove the prescribed number of dabigatran oral pellet packets from the aluminum bag; keep unused packets in the aluminum bag. After opening the aluminum bag, remove the desiccant container and dispose in the trash.
Do not open the packets until ready for use.
Tap the packet of oral pellets on a flat surface to ensure that the contents settle to the bottom.
Administer with only specific soft foods or apple juice.
Administration with soft foods: Mix oral pellets in a small cup with 10 mL of soft foods at room temperature, such as mashed carrots, applesauce, or mashed bananas, and stir well. Administer the soft food and oral pellet mixture.
Administration with apple juice: The oral pellets may be spooned directly into the patient's mouth and swallowed with apple juice or added to a cup with approximately 30 to 60 mL of apple juice for drinking. If any pellets remain in the cup after drinking, add a little more apple juice and administer; repeat process until no pellets remain in the cup.
Do not administer via syringes or feeding tubes or with milk, milk products, or soft foods containing milk products.
If the patient does not take all the mixed dabigatran oral pellets, do not give another dose at that time. Give the next scheduled dose about 12 hours later.
Missed dose: If a dose is missed, administer as soon as possible on the same day. However, skip the missed dose if it cannot be taken at least 6 hours before the next scheduled dose.
Storage: Administer immediately after mixing or within 30 minutes after mixing. If the dabigatran dose is not administered within 30 minutes of mixing, discard the dose and prepare a new dose.

Dosage Adjustment Guidelines
  Hepatic Impairment
  Specific guidelines for dosage adjustments in hepatic impairment are not available; it appears that no dosage adjustments are needed.
  Renal Impairment
  Adults
For reduction in risk of stroke and systemic embolism in non-valvular atrial fibrillation
CrCl more than 30 mL/minute: No dosage adjustment is needed.
CrCl 15 to 30 mL/minute: 75 mg PO twice daily.
CrCl less than 15 mL/minute: Dosing recommendations are not available.
With concomitant use of P-glycoprotein (P-gp) inhibitors
CrCl 30 to 50 mL/minute: Reduce dose to 75 mg PO twice daily when coadministered with the P-gp inhibitors dronedarone or ketoconazole; dose adjustment is not necessary when coadministered with other P-gp inhibitors.
CrCl less than 30 mL/minute: Avoid coadministration.
 
For treatment or reduction in risk of recurrence of DVT and PE and prophylaxis of DVT and PE after hip replacement surgery
CrCl more than 30 mL/minute: No dosage adjustment is needed.
CrCl 30 mL/minute or less: Dosing recommendations are not available.
With concomitant use of P-gp inhibitors
CrCl less than 50 mL/minute: Avoid coadministration.
 
Pediatric patients
eGFR 50 mL/minute/1.73 m2 or more: No dosage adjustment is needed.
eGFR less than 50 mL/minute/1.73 m2: Avoid use; dabigatran has not been studied in pediatric patients with renal impairment and dosing recommendations cannot be provided for these patients.
 
Hemodialysis
Dabigatran is removed by dialysis; however, dosing recommendations are not available.
