Indication Specific Dosing
  General Information
  General Dosing Information
Perform testing for pre-existing antibodies to adeno-associated virus serotype 5 (AAV5) using the FDA-approved companion diagnostic. Do not administer valoctocogene roxaparvovec to persons with a positive test for antibodies to AAV5. Information on FDA-approved tests for the detection of antibodies to AAV5 is available at http://www.fda.gov/CompanionDiagnostics.
Perform factor VIII inhibitor titer testing. Do not administer valoctocogene roxaparvovec to persons with a positive test for factor VIII inhibitors.
Perform the following liver health assessments:
Liver function tests [alanine aminotransferase (ALT), aspartate aminotransferase (AST), gamma-glutamyl transferase (GGT), alkaline phosphatase, total bilirubin and international normalized ratio (INR)]
Ultrasound and elastography or laboratory assessments for liver fibrosis
If liver function and/or radiological liver tests are abnormal (ALT, AST, GGT, alkaline phosphatase, or total bilirubin more than 1.25 times the upper limit of normal (ULN) or INR of 1.4 or more), consider a consultation with a hepatologist to assess eligibility for valoctocogene roxaparvovec.
Assess the individual's ability to receive corticosteroids and/or other immunosuppressive therapy that may be required for an extended period. Ensure that the risks associated with immunosuppression are acceptable.
Ensure that vaccinations are up to date prior to administration of valoctocogene roxaparvovec. Individual vaccination schedules may need to be adjusted to accommodate concomitant immunosuppressive therapy; do not administer live vaccines to individuals receiving immunosuppressive therapy.

    Headline
    ethanol ingestion, geriatric, hepatic cirrhosis, hepatic fibrosis, infection, metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis, metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease
    General Information
    Valoctocogene roxaparvovec is contraindicated in individuals with active infection, either acute (e.g., acute respiratory infection or acute hepatitis) or uncontrolled chronic (e.g. chronic active hepatitis B) infection, and in individuals with known significant hepatic fibrosis, defined as stage 3 or 4 on the Batts-Ludwig scale or equivalent, or hepatic cirrhosis. The safety and effectiveness of valoctocogene roxaparvovec in individuals with hepatic impairment have not been established. Clinical studies excluded individuals with known hepatic cirrhosis, significant fibrosis (stage 3 or 4 on the Batts-Ludwig scale or equivalent), current hepatitis B or C, or history of hepatic malignancy. Intravenous administration of liver directed adeno-associated viral (AAV) vector could lead to transaminitis, especially alanine aminotransferase (ALT) elevation, due to immune-mediated injury of transduced hepatocytes, which may reduce therapeutic efficacy of AAV-vector based gene therapy. Prior to administration of valoctocogene roxaparvovec, perform liver health assessments, including measuring liver function tests [i.e., ALT, aspartate aminotransferase (AST), gamma-glutamyl transferase (GGT), alkaline phosphatase, total bilirubin, and international normalized ratio (INR)], and liver ultrasound and elastography or laboratory assessments for hepatic fibrosis. In the event of radiological liver abnormalities and/or liver function abnormalities, consider consultation with a hepatologist to assess eligibility for valoctocogene roxaparvovec. Some ALT elevations have been attributed to ethanol consumption; therefore, advise individuals to abstain from ethanol ingestion for at least 1 year after valoctocogene roxaparvovec administration and limit ethanol use thereafter. Integration of liver-targeting AAV vector DNA into the genome may carry the theoretical risk of developing hepatocellular carcinoma. Monitor individuals with risk factors for hepatocellular carcinoma, such as advanced (geriatric) age, chronic ethanol ingestion, hepatitis B or C, metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease, or metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis, with annual liver ultrasound and alpha-fetoprotein testing for 5 years after valoctocogene roxaparvovec administration.

    Headline
    elevated hepatic enzymes, hepatitis, hepatotoxicity
    General Information
    Intravenous administration of a liver-directed adeno-associated virus (AAV) vector could potentially lead to elevated hepatic enzymes (transaminitis), especially alanine aminotransferase (ALT) elevation. Transaminitis is presumed to occur due to immune-mediated injury of transduced hepatocytes and may reduce therapeutic efficacy of AAV-vector based gene therapy. Monitor ALT concentrations weekly for weeks 1 to 26, every 1 to 2 weeks for weeks 26 to 52, every 3 months for year 2, every 6 months for year 3 or more, and as clinically indicated during corticosteroid therapy. Monitor aspartate aminotransferase (AST) and creatine phosphokinase (CPK) as needed to rule out other causes for ALT elevations (i.e., potentially hepatotoxic medications or agents, alcohol consumption, or strenuous exercise). Consider repeating ALT testing within 24 to 48 hours to confirm ALT elevation prior to initiation of corticosteroid treatment; measure ALT concentrations using the same laboratory at baseline and over time to minimize the impact of inter-laboratory variability on test results. If ALT is 1.5 or more times baseline or above upper limit of normal (ULN), consider corticosteroid treatment. For persons who require corticosteroid therapy, the recommended dose is prednisone 60 mg PO once daily for 2 weeks, then 40 mg PO once daily for 3 weeks, then 30 mg PO once daily for 1 week, then 20 mg PO once daily for 1 week, and then 10 mg PO once daily for 1 week. If ALT continues to increase or has not improved after 2 weeks, increase the corticosteroid dose up to a maximum dose of 1.2 mg/kg/dose, after ruling out alternative causes for ALT elevation. Taper the corticosteroid after ALT concentrations normalize to baseline. The taper may be individualized based on the trend of ALT decline, the person's medical condition, corticosteroid tolerance, and adverse reactions to corticosteroid therapy. There is limited information on the benefit of using corticosteroid therapy after the first year of valoctocogene roxaparvovec administration. Other immunosuppressive medications, such as tacrolimus or mycophenolate mofetil, may be considered if corticosteroids are contraindicated, ineffective, or there are adverse reactions secondary to corticosteroid use requiring discontinuation. ALT elevations were observed in the majority of subjects treated with valoctocogene roxaparvovec in clinical trials. Most ALT elevations occurred within the first year after valoctocogene roxaparvovec administration, especially within the first 26 weeks, and were low-grade and resolved. ALT and AST concentration increases more than the ULN were observed in 81% and 69% of subjects treated with valoctocogene roxaparvovec in clinical trials, respectively. Additionally, gamma-glutamyl transferase (GGT) and bilirubin concentration increases above the ULN were observed in 18% and 13% of subjects treated with valoctocogene roxaparvovec in clinical trials, respectively. ALT, AST, and GGT elevations more than 5 to 20 times the ULN occurred in 8% to 9%, 7%, and 1% of subjects, respectively. Hepatotoxicity, defined as an ALT of 1.5 or more times baseline or ALT more than the ULN, occurred in 96% of subjects. The median time to the first ALT elevation (defined as ALT of 1.5 or more times baseline or above ULN) was 7 weeks and the median duration was 4 weeks. ALT elevations were observed in 89% of subjects within the first 26 weeks, 66% of subjects between weeks 27 to 52, and 64% of subjects beyond 52 weeks after valoctocogene roxaparvovec administration. ALT elevations were associated with a 30% or more decline in factor VIII activity in 30% of subjects. A single case of autoimmune hepatitis was reported during the third year of follow-up in a subject with a history of hepatitis C and steatohepatitis. Corticosteroid treatment was initiated in 82% of subjects for 1 or more episodes of ALT elevation, while 35% of subjects required alternate immunosuppression. Corticosteroids were required in 76%, 5%, and 1% of subjects during the first 26 weeks, weeks 27 to 52, and beyond 52 weeks after valoctocogene roxaparvovec administration, respectively. The median duration of corticosteroid use was 35 weeks, and the median duration of alternate immunosuppressive medication use was 26 weeks; the duration of immunosuppression was more than 1 year in 18% of subjects.

Description
  Text
  Valoctocogene roxaparvovec is an intravenous adeno-associated virus vector-based gene therapy indicated for the treatment of severe hemophilia A (congenital factor VIII deficiency with factor VIII activity less than 1 International Units/dL) without antibodies to adeno-associated virus serotype 5 (AAV5). Efficacy of valoctocogene roxaparvovec in persons with hemophilia A was evaluated in a prospective, phase 3, open-label, single-dose, single-arm, multinational clinical trial (n = 134). The main efficacy outcome was a non-interiority test of the difference in annualized bleeding rate (ABR) in the efficacy evaluation period after administration of valoctocogene roxaparvovec compared with ABR during the baseline period in the rollover population. The efficacy evaluation period ABR was 2.6 bleeds/year compared to a mean baseline ABR of 5.4 bleeds/year. The non-inferiority analysis met the pre-specified non-inferiority margin, indicating the effectiveness of valoctocogene roxaparvovec. Elevated hepatic enzymes have been reported with valoctocogene roxaparvovec, and integration of liver-targeting AAV vector DNA into the genome may carry the theoretical risk of hepatocellular carcinoma development.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Valoctocogene roxaparvovec is an adeno-associated virus serotype 5 (AAV5) based gene therapy vector that is designed to introduce a functional copy of a transgene encoding the B-domain deleted SQ form of human coagulation factor VIII (hFVIII-SQ). Transcription of this transgene occurs within the liver using a liver-specific promoter, which results in the expression of hFVIII-SQ. The expressed hFVIII-SQ replaces the missing coagulation factor VIII needed to achieve hemostasis. After administration of valoctocogene roxaparvovec, vector DNA is processed in vivo to form full-length, episomal transgenes that increase circulating hFVIII-SQ up to 5 years.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Intravenous Route
    Description
    Valoctocogene roxaparvovec biodistribution and shedding were investigated on samples from blood, saliva, semen, stool, and urine. After administration of valoctocogene roxaparvovec 6 x 1013 vector genomes (vg)/kg intravenously, vector DNA was detectable in blood and all shedding matrices evaluated; peak vector DNA concentrations were observed 1 to 9 days after administration. The peak vector DNA concentrations were observed in blood, followed by saliva, semen, stool, and urine. The peak vector DNA concentration observed in blood in clinical studies was 2 x 1011 vg/mL. The maximum concentration observed in any shedding matrix was 1 x 1010 vg/mL. After reaching the maximum in a matrix, the transgene DNA concentration declines rapidly. Potentially transmissible encapsidated vector DNA was detectable in plasma up to 10 weeks after valoctocogene roxaparvovec administration. All subjects in valoctocogene roxaparvovec clinical trials achieved the first of 3 consecutive measurements below the lower limit of quantification (LLOQ) for vector DNA in semen by 36 weeks, and all except 1 subject achieved 3 consecutive measurements below limit of detection (BLOD) or negative by the time of data analysis. The maximum time to the first 3 consecutive measurements BLOD for potentially transmissible encapsidated vector DNA in semen was 12 weeks. In valoctocogene roxaparvovec clinical trials, all subjects achieved 3 consecutive measurements below the LLOQ for vector DNA in urine and saliva, and 89% of subjects achieved 3 consecutive measurements below the LLOQ for vector DNA in stool at the time of data analysis. The maximum time to the first of 3 consecutive LLOQ measurements was 8 weeks for urine, 52 weeks for saliva, and 131 weeks for stool. In the first study, all subjects achieved 3 consecutive measurements BLOD or negative in urine, saliva, and stool by 5 years after administration of valoctocogene roxaparvovec. In another clinical trial, all subjects achieved 3 consecutive measurements BLOD or negative in urine and saliva and 63% of subjects achieved 3 consecutive measurements BLOD or negative in stool by 3 years after administration of valoctocogene roxaparvovec. Magnitude and duration of shedding appear to be independent of attained factor VIII activity.
  Text

The majority of subjects (95%) who reach factor VIII activity concentrations of 5 International Units/dL or more using the CSA do so within 5 months after administration of valoctocogene roxaparvovec.

      Preparation
General Precautions
Prepare valoctocogene roxaparvovec using aseptic technique. Wear gloves and safety glasses during preparation and administration.
Do not expose valoctocogene roxaparvovec to the light of an ultraviolet radiation disinfection lamp.
Do not use infusion system components other than those outlined by the manufacturer.
Do not mix or dilute valoctocogene roxaparvovec with infusion solutions.
Preparation of vial
Keep each vial in its carton until ready to thaw.
Thaw at room temperature; do not thaw or warm vials any other way. Thawing time is approximately 2 hours.
Remove the required number of vials from their cartons and inspect the vials for damage to the vial or cap. Do not use if damaged.
Set the vials upright and spread the vials out evenly or place them in racks that have been kept at room temperature.
Visually confirm that all vials have been thawed. Visible ice should not be present.
Invert each vial very gently 5 times to mix; minimize foaming.
Let the suspension settle for approximately 5 minutes before continuing with preparation of the vial.
Storage: Valoctocogene roxaparvovec does not contain preservatives. Keep the thawed suspension in the vial until it is time for infusion. If necessary, an intact vial (stopper not yet punctured) that has been thawed at room temperature can be stored refrigerated at 2 to 8 degrees C (36 to 46 degrees F) for up to 3 days, upright and protected from light (e.g., in the original carton). Thawed valoctocogene roxaparvovec in vials can be held at room temperature, up to 25 degrees C (77 degrees F), for a maximum of 10 hours including hold time in intact vial, preparation time into the syringes, and duration of infusion.
Extraction into Syringes
Slowly extract valoctocogene roxaparvovec from the vial(s) into the infusion-pump syringes using 18 to 21-guage sharp needles. Prepare all infusion-pump syringes prior to administering valoctocogene roxaparvovec. The contents of multiple vials may be combined into a single syringe.
Storage: Thawed valoctocogene roxaparvovec in syringes can be held at room temperature, up to 25 degrees C (77 degrees F), for a maximum of 10 hours including hold time in intact vial, preparation time into the syringes, and duration of infusion.
Intravenous (IV) Infusion
Administer valoctocogene roxaparvovec in a setting where personnel and equipment are immediately available to treat infusion-related reactions.
Do not infuse in the same IV line with any other products.
Infuse the suspension through a suitable peripheral vein using an infusion catheter with an in-line filter and a programmable syringe pump. Do not use a central line or port.
Insert an in-line filter (high volume, low protein binding, pore size of 0.22 microns) close to the infusion site; prime tubing and filter with valoctocogene roxaparvovec.
Use 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection for priming and flushing when replacing filters during the infusion.
Start the infusion at a rate of 1 mL/minute. If tolerated, the rate may be increased every 30 minutes by 1 mL/minute to a maximum of 4 mL/minute. The infusion time depends on infusion volume, rate, and patient response. The infusion time can be 2 to 5 hours or longer (e.g., for a patient weighing 100 kg).
After the last valoctocogene roxaparvovec-containing syringe is infused, flush the infusion line with a sufficient volume of 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection through the same tubing and filter and at the same infusion rate to ensure that the complete dose of valoctocogene roxaparvovec is administered.
In the event of an infusion-related reaction during administration:
Decrease the infusion rate or stop the infusion.
Administer treatment as needed to manage the infusion reaction.
If the infusion is stopped, restart the infusion at a rate of 1 mL/minute and consider maintaining it at a previously tolerated level for the remainder of the infusion. If the infusion needs to be restarted, complete the infusion within 10 hours of thawing the initial product.
Discontinue the infusion if anaphylaxis occurs.
Administer as a continuous IV infusion only; do not administer as an IV push or bolus.
Maintain venous access during the observation period after administration of valoctocogene roxaparvovec.
