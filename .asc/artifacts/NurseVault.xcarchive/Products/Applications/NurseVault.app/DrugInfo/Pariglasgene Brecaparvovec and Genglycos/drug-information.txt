    elevated hepatic enzymes, hepatotoxicity
    General Information
    Immune-mediated hepatotoxicity has occurred with pariglasgene brecaparvovec. In a clinical trial, elevated hepatic enzymes, including ALT and/or AST, were reported in 71% of participants receiving pariglasgene brecaparvovec compared with 12% of participants receiving placebo; one severe Grade 3 asymptomatic event was reported. ALT and AST elevations peaked 4 to 12 weeks after administration and resolved within 15 weeks. Monitor transaminase levels for the first 6 months after administration. In individuals who develop transaminase elevations, continue monitoring until transaminase levels return to baseline or as clinically indicated. Corticosteroids are administered after infusion to mitigate hepatic reactions, and the corticosteroid regimen may require adjustment if transaminase elevations occur. Signs and symptoms of hepatotoxicity may include fatigue, jaundice, dark urine, nausea, vomiting, and right upper quadrant pain.

    anaphylactoid reactions, infusion-related reactions
    General Information
    Infusion-related reactions, including hypersensitivity and anaphylactoid reactions, have occurred with pariglasgene brecaparvovec. Anaphylaxis occurred in 2 individuals (10%); both events were serious and began within minutes of administration. Symptoms of infusion-related reactions may include urticaria, flushing, hypotension, bronchospasm, dyspnea, chest tightness, nausea, vomiting, headache, abdominal pain, lightheadedness, flu-like symptoms, shivering, rash, and hypertension. Premedicate with acetaminophen and a non-sedating antihistamine 30 to 60 minutes before the infusion. Monitor patients during and after completion of the infusion as clinically indicated. If anaphylaxis or a severe infusion-related reaction occurs, immediately pause the infusion and initiate medical treatment as clinically indicated. For mild to moderate infusion-related reactions, consider slowing or temporarily interrupting the infusion and provide symptomatic treatment as clinically indicated. The infusion may be restarted at one-half the previous infusion rate after symptoms resolve. If the infusion is interrupted and restarted, complete the infusion within 24 hours of dose preparation. Cardiopulmonary resuscitation equipment and medications for the treatment of anaphylaxis, including epinephrine, antihistamines, and corticosteroids, should be available during administration.

    General Information
    Adrenocortical insufficiency (24%) has been reported with pariglasgene brecaparvovec during corticosteroid use and tapering; two serious events were reported. Cushingoid features (14%) and mild hyperglycemia (14%) were also reported in clinical trials. Signs and symptoms of adrenocortical insufficiency may include fatigue, weakness, anorexia, nausea, vomiting, hypotension, hyponatremia, and hypoglycemia; adrenal crisis may present with severe hypotension, acute abdominal pain, or loss of consciousness. Obtain a morning serum cortisol level and ACTH before starting the corticosteroid regimen. Monitor for adrenocortical insufficiency and adrenal crisis during and after corticosteroid therapy and tapering, and repeat laboratory testing as clinically indicated. Taper corticosteroid therapy gradually; do not abruptly discontinue corticosteroids. Consult an endocrinologist for an abnormal baseline or persistently low morning serum cortisol level. If adrenocortical insufficiency is suspected during corticosteroid tapering, increase the corticosteroid dose to the last tolerated dose and consult an endocrinologist.

Description
  Text
  Pariglasgene brecaparvovec is an adeno-associated virus (AAV) vector-based gene therapy indicated to reduce daily cornstarch intake as an adjunct to nutritional management in individuals with glycogen storage disease type Ia (GSDIa). It delivers a copy of the glucose-6-phosphatase (G6PC) gene to hepatocytes, with native human G6PC promotor and enhancer elements, resulting in production of functional glucose-6-phosphatase. The indication was approved under accelerated approval based on reduction in daily cornstarch intake. In a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled clinical trial, pariglasgene brecaparvovec reduced daily cornstarch intake by a mean of 31% from baseline compared with placebo after 48 weeks. Pariglasgene brecaparvovec is contraindicated in individuals with known severe hepatic fibrosis or cirrhosis. Safety considerations include hypersensitivity and infusion reactions, immune-mediated hepatotoxicity, adrenal insufficiency associated with corticosteroid therapy and tapering, and a theoretical risk of tumorigenicity from AAV vector integration. Pariglasgene brecaparvovec was FDA-approved in 2026.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Pariglasgene brecaparvovec is an adeno-associated virus serotype 8 (AAV8)-based gene therapy that delivers a copy of the human glucose-6-phosphatase (G6PC) gene to hepatocytes, together with native human G6PC promoter and enhancer elements. This delivery enables production of normally functioning glucose-6-phosphatase (G6Pase).
 
Pharmacodynamics
In a clinical trial, the least square mean reduction from baseline in daily cornstarch intake after 48 weeks was 41.1% with pariglasgene brecaparvovec versus 10.2% with placebo.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Intravenous Route
    Description
    In a clinical study of 12 participants, peak vector genome DNA levels occurred at the first post-dose assessment on Day 2 in blood and between Day 3 and Day 13 in stool, saliva, and urine. Blood vector genome DNA levels were below the limit of detection in most participants approximately 24 weeks after infusion. Clearance of vector genome DNA was defined as 3 consecutive measurements below the limit of detection. Based on this definition, vector genome DNA was cleared from saliva within 7 weeks and from urine and stool within 13 weeks after infusion. In a nonclinical study in mice, vector genome DNA was detected in all major organs analyzed 13 weeks after a single intravenous administration, with the highest quantities detected in the liver and lower levels in the kidney, lung, heart, gonads, and spleen. In this same nonclinical study, human G6PC mRNA expression was primarily detected in the liver, with levels at least 100-fold higher than those measured in extrahepatic tissues.

      Preparation
Use aseptic technique during preparation. Before thawing, verify the required weight-based dose for the patient and that the available kit contains the required number of vials.
Thaw the required vials at 20 to 25 degrees C (68 to 77 degrees F) for no longer than 30 minutes. After thawing, gently swirl each vial for 5 to 10 seconds to mix; do not shake.
Visually inspect the thawed suspension before withdrawal. Pariglasgene brecaparvovec is a clear to translucent suspension that may contain small white to off-white particles. Do not use a vial if the solution is discolored or contains matter other than small white to off-white particles.
Withdraw 2 mL from each vial using a polypropylene syringe and an 18-gauge needle. For doses requiring multiple vials, change the needle at least every third vial. Discard any unused portion left in the vials.
Storage: Do not refreeze after thawing. If necessary, an intact vial with an unpunctured stopper that has been thawed may be refrigerated at 2 to 8 degrees C (36 to 46 degrees F) for up to 12 days. Keep the vial upright and protected from light.
 
Dilution
Select a polyvinyl chloride or polypropylene/polyethylene infusion bag according to body weight:
For individuals weighing 15 to 39 kg: use a 100 mL infusion bag of 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection
For individuals weighing 40 kg or more: use a 250 mL infusion bag of 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection
Before adding the drug to the infusion bag, remove a volume of 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection equal to the pariglasgene brecaparvovec dose volume.
Using a sterile 0.2-micron PES or equivalent syringe filter, add the contents of the syringes to restore the infusion bag to the original volume (100 mL or 250 mL). To avoid foaming, do not add pariglasgene brecaparvovec into the airspace of the infusion bag.
Mix by gently inverting the infusion bag 4 times. To avoid foaming, do not shake.
Storage after dilution: Diluted pariglasgene brecaparvovec should be administered immediately. Do not freeze the diluted solution. If not administered immediately, the diluted solution may be stored as follows:
Room temperature: Store at 20 to 25 degrees C (68 to 77 degrees F) for up to 24 hours, including infusion time.
Refrigerated: Store at 2 to 8 degrees C (36 to 46 degrees F) and complete the infusion within 48 hours from the time of dilution.
 
Single-Dose Intravenous Infusion
Ensure cardiopulmonary resuscitation equipment and medications for the treatment of anaphylaxis, including epinephrine, antihistamines, and corticosteroids, are available during administration.
Connect the infusion bag to an infusion set equipped with a 0.2-micron filter and prime the tubing with diluted pariglasgene brecaparvovec to the catheter hub.
Administer pariglasgene brecaparvovec as a single-dose IV infusion through a peripheral venous catheter. Do not administer as an IV push or bolus.
To minimize the risk for infusion reactions, follow the recommended intervals and rates of infusion for each time interval after the start of infusion:
For individuals weighing less than 40 kg:
0 to 60 minutes: 3 mL/hour
61 to 80 minutes: 8 mL/hour
81 to 100 minutes: 15 mL/hour
101 to 120 minutes: 30 mL/hour
121 to 180 minutes: 80 mL/hour
For individuals weighing 40 kg or more:
0 to 60 minutes: 6 mL/hour
61 to 80 minutes: 18 mL/hour
81 to 100 minutes: 38 mL/hour
101 to 120 minutes: 75 mL/hour
121 to 180 minutes: 200 mL/hour
Infuse the entire contents of the infusion bag, even if the total infusion time exceeds 180 minutes. Complete the infusion within 24 hours of dose preparation.
Monitor for signs and symptoms of hypersensitivity and infusion reactions during and after the infusion as clinically indicated.
If anaphylaxis or a severe infusion reaction occurs, immediately pause the infusion and initiate supportive care as clinically indicated.
For a mild to moderate infusion reaction, consider slowing or temporarily interrupting the infusion and administer symptomatic treatment as clinically indicated. After symptoms of an infusion reaction have resolved, the infusion may be restarted at one-half of the previous infusion rate.
If the infusion is paused and restarted, complete the infusion within 24 hours of dose preparation.
After the entire contents of the infusion bag have been administered, flush the tubing with 50 mL of 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection at the same infusion rate used during the final portion of the infusion to ensure the entire dose of pariglasgene brecaparvovec is delivered. Do not administer the flush as a bolus.
Dispose of unused pariglasgene brecaparvovec and disposable materials that may have contacted the product according to local biosafety guidelines.
