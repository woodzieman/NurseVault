    Treatment-Related Restrictions of Use
    General Information
    Administration of onasemnogene abeparvovec IV infusion to premature neonates before reaching term gestational age is not recommended because concomitant treatment with corticosteroids may adversely affect neurological development. Delay onasemnogene abeparvovec infusion until the corresponding term gestational age is reached.
    Headline
    infection
    General Information
    The risk of serious systemic immune response is increased in patients with a concurrent infection receiving onasemnogene abeparvovec, potentially resulting in more severe clinical courses of the infection. Due to activation of humoral and cellular immunity after onasemnogene abeparvovec, patients with an active infection, either acute (e.g., respiratory, gastrointestinal) or chronic uncontrolled (e.g., chronic active hepatitis B) may be at an increased risk. Patients may present with high fever, hypotension, etc. To decrease the risk of serious and life-threatening systemic immune response, administer onasemnogene abeparvovec to patients who are clinically stable in their baseline health (e.g., hydration and nutritional status, absence of infection) prior to infusion. Postpone onasemnogene abeparvovec in patients with concurrent infections until the infection has resolved and the patient is clinically stable. Clinical signs or symptoms of infection should not be evident at the time of administration. Closely monitor patients to prevent, manage, and monitor for infection. Ensure that recommended seasonal prophylaxis against influenza and respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) and vaccination status are up to date prior to administration.
    Headline
    acute hepatic failure, hepatic disease, hepatotoxicity
    General Information
    Hepatotoxicity, aminotransferase elevations, and acute hepatic failure, including fatal cases, have been reported with the use of onasemnogene abeparvovec. Patients with pre-existing hepatic disease or acute hepatic viral infection may be at a higher risk of developing acute serious liver injury. Patients with alanine aminotransferase (ALT), aspartate aminotransferase (AST), or total bilirubin concentrations, excluding neonatal jaundice, more than 2 times the upper limit of normal (ULN) have not been studied in onasemnogene abeparvovec clinical trials. Use caution when administering onasemnogene abeparvovec to any patient with known hepatic disease. Weigh the risks and benefits of drug administration in patients with pre-existing hepatic impairment carefully against the risks of not treating the patient. Prior to onasemnogene abeparvovec administration, assess liver function by clinical examination and laboratory testing (e.g., AST, ALT, total bilirubin, albumin, prothrombin time, partial thromboplastin time [PTT], and international normalized ratio [INR]). Administer systemic corticosteroids before and after onasemnogene abeparvovec administration. Adjustment of the corticosteroid treatment regimen, including longer duration, increased dose, or prolongation of the corticosteroid taper may be necessary for immune-mediated hepatotoxicity. Monitor liver function (AST, ALT, total bilirubin, prothrombin time, INR) weekly for the first month after administration and during the corticosteroid taper period (28 days or longer if needed). For patients that are clinically stable with unremarkable findings at the end of the corticosteroid taper period, continue to monitor liver function every other week for another month. If acute serious hepatotoxicity or acute liver failure is suspected, consult a gastroenterologist or hepatologist. Closely monitor patients with worsening liver function tests and/or signs or symptoms of acute illness (e.g., vomiting, deterioration in health). If hepatic injury is suspected, further testing of albumin, PTT, and INR is recommended.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    There are no data on the use of onasemnogene abeparvovec during human pregnancy to evaluate for a drug-associated risk. It is not known whether onasemnogene abeparvovec has the potential to be transferred to the fetus during human pregnancy. In animal reproduction studies, administration of IV onasemnogene abeparvovec on gestational day 6 resulted in no evidence of maternal toxicity, embryo-fetal toxicity, teratogenicity, or reduced viability. Onasemnogene abeparvovec DNA was detected in the placenta at low concentrations but was not detected in any fetal tissue at gestational day 18. The IV dose used corresponds to at least 7 times the recommended clinical intrathecal dose based on body weight. Use onasemnogene abeparvovec during pregnancy only after a thorough benefit-risk evaluation.

Adverse Reactions
  Headline
  Adverse Reactions
  Adverse Reactions
    Headline
    elevated hepatic enzymes, hepatic failure, jaundice
    General Information
    Hepatotoxicity, generally manifested as aminotransferase elevations, has been commonly reported with onasemnogene abeparvovec. Elevated hepatic enzymes occurred in 27% of patients (12 of 44) receiving intravenous onasemnogene abeparvovec in clinical trials. Among these patients, 16% had alanine aminotransferase (ALT) elevations more than 3 times the upper limit of normal (ULN), and 9% had ALT elevations more than 20 times the ULN. In a postmarketing study with intravenous administration, aspartate aminotransferase (AST) or ALT elevations more than 2 times the ULN were observed in 23 of 24 patients, including 21 with ALT more than 3 times the ULN and 5 with ALT more than 20 times the ULN. Affected individuals were clinically asymptomatic without bilirubin elevation, and aminotransferase elevations were managed with corticosteroids, often requiring prolonged or higher-dose regimens. In clinical trials with intrathecal onasemnogene abeparvovec, elevated liver enzymes occurred in 8% of patients (6 of 75), and 2 patients experienced ALT elevations of 20 times the ULN. Cases of acute serious liver injury or hepatic failure, including death, have occurred with onasemnogene abeparvovec. Some patients experienced ALT and AST elevations greater than 20 times the ULN accompanied by prolonged prothrombin time and symptomatic presentations such as vomiting or jaundice. Administer systemic corticosteroids before and after onasemnogene abeparvovec treatment. The corticosteroid regimen may require adjustment, such as extended duration, increased dose, or prolonged taper, for immune-mediated hepatotoxicity. Liver function testing is recommended at baseline and weekly for the first month after administration, then during corticosteroid taper (28 days or longer if needed). If findings remain stable and unremarkable, continue monitoring every other week for 1 additional month. Consultation with a gastroenterologist or hepatologist is advised if acute serious hepatotoxicity or hepatic failure is suspected.

    thrombocytopenia, thrombotic microangiopathy
    General Information
    Transient decreases in platelet counts, including cases meeting criteria for thrombocytopenia, were observed at varying time points after intravenous onasemnogene abeparvovec infusion in clinical trials. In a postmarketing study, thrombocytopenia was reported in 20 of 24 patients, including 4 individuals with platelet counts below 50,000 cells/mm3. With intrathecal administration, thrombocytopenia occurred in 4% of patients (3 of 75). Thrombotic microangiopathy (TMA), characterized by thrombocytopenia, microangiopathic hemolytic anemia, and acute kidney injury, has also been reported postmarketing, typically within the first 2 weeks after treatment. Concurrent immune activation, such as infection or vaccination, was identified in some cases. Because TMA can be life-threatening or fatal, close monitoring for signs and symptoms such as hypertension, increased bruising, seizures, or decreased urine output is recommended. If these occur alongside thrombocytopenia, further evaluate for hemolytic anemia and renal dysfunction. When clinical or laboratory findings consistent with TMA are present, consult a hematologist and/or nephrologist promptly for appropriate management. Increases in cardiac troponin I concentrations (up to 0.176 mcg/L) have also been observed after onasemnogene abeparvovec administration, although the clinical significance is unknown. Cardiac toxicity has been observed in animal studies. Consider cardiac evaluation after onasemnogene abeparvovec administration and consult a cardiologist as needed.

    Headline
    tumorigenicity
    General Information
    There is a theoretical risk of tumorigenicity due to potential integration of adeno-associated virus (AAV) vector DNA into the host genome. Tumors have been reported in patients during postmarketing use of onasemnogene abeparvovec; however, available information is limited, and a causal relationship has not been established. If a tumor occurs in a patient who has received onasemnogene abeparvovec, health care providers should report the case to Novartis Gene Therapies, Inc. at 1-833-828-3947. Onasemnogene abeparvovec uses a recombinant, non-replicating AAV9 vector, and its DNA remains predominantly in episomal form. Random integration of recombinant AAV vector DNA into human DNA has been described in the literature with other AAV gene therapies. The clinical significance of these integration events is unknown, but they may theoretically contribute to tumorigenic risk.

Description
  Text
  Onasemnogene abeparvovec is an adeno-associated virus vector gene therapy indicated for the treatment of spinal muscular atrophy (SMA) with mutations in the survival motor neuron 1 (SMN1) gene. SMA is a rare and often fatal hereditary genetic disease affecting muscle strength and movement. It causes weakness and muscle wasting because of the loss of lower motor neurons controlling movement. The age of onset, symptoms, and rate of progression vary widely between patients. Onasemnogene abeparvovec is given as a single dose and carries a boxed warning for acute serious liver injury and acute liver failure. Efficacy of onasemnogene abeparvovec IV infusion was demonstrated in 2 open-label studies (n = 36) involving infants with SMA and bi-allelic SMN1 mutations. In the pivotal trial, 90% of treated individuals were alive without permanent ventilation, and nearly half achieved the ability to sit independently, milestones rarely reached in untreated SMA. Similar results were observed in a second study, where all participants in the higher-dose group survived without permanent ventilation, supporting approval for use in pediatric patients younger than 2 years of age with SMA. Efficacy of onasemnogene abeparvovec intrathecal injection was demonstrated in a randomized, double-blind, sham-controlled study of individuals with SMA. A single intrathecal dose of 1.2 x 10^14 vector genomes significantly improved motor function, with a mean Hammersmith Functional Motor Scale-Expanded (HFMSE) total score increase of 2.39 points versus 0.51 points for sham (difference 1.88; p = 0.0074), supporting its approval for individuals aged 2 years and older with SMA. The direct administration of onasemnogene abeparvovec into the cerebrospinal fluid surrounding the spinal cord (site of action) allows for delivery to motor neurons with a lower dose of vector, without the need to adjust for the patient's body weight. This provides a treatment with rapid onset and direct targeting of the genetic root cause of SMA. By addressing the root cause of SMA, onasemnogene abeparvovec restores SMN protein production and halts further disease progression. Because thrombocytopenia, hepatic injury, and potential cardiac toxicity can occur after onasemnogene abeparvovec administration, platelet count, liver function (by clinical examination and laboratory testing), and cardiac troponin-I concentrations are recommended at baseline and on a regular basis for at least 3 months after therapy.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Onasemnogene abeparvovec is a non-replicating recombinant adeno-associated virus (AAV) vector that uses an AAV9 capsid to deliver a functional copy of the human survival motor neuron 1 (SMN1) gene. The transgene DNA primarily persists in episomal form within the nucleus of transduced cells, and its expression is driven by a constitutive cytomegalovirus-enhanced chicken beta actin hybrid promoter, enabling continuous and sustained production of SMN protein. Spinal muscular atrophy (SMA) results from bi-allelic mutations in the SMN1 gene, leading to insufficient SMN protein expression. By providing an alternative source of SMN protein in motor neurons, onasemnogene abeparvovec is expected to enhance the survival and function of transduced motor neurons. Intravenous administration has been shown to result in cellular transduction and SMN protein expression in 2 documented human cases.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Intravenous Route
    Description
    Vector shedding after onasemnogene abeparvovec infusion was evaluated in a clinical trial in which saliva, urine, and stool samples from 5 patients were analyzed through 18 months. Vector DNA was detected in all 3 sample types, with substantially higher concentrations in stool than in saliva or urine. Saliva concentrations were low on Day 1 and declined to undetectable concentrations within 3 weeks, while urine concentrations were very low on Day 1 and declined to undetectable concentrations within 1 to 2 weeks. Stool concentrations remained higher for 1 to 2 weeks and declined to undetectable concentrations by 1 to 2 months after infusion. Biodistribution was also assessed in 2 patients who died 5.7 months and 1.7 months after infusion, with the highest vector DNA concentrations detected in the liver and additional distribution to the spleen, heart, pancreas, lymph nodes, skeletal muscle, peripheral nerves, kidney, lung, intestines, spinal cord, brain, and thymus. Immunostaining demonstrated generalized survival motor neuron (SMN) protein expression in spinal motor neurons, neuronal and glial cells of the brain, and in the heart, liver, skeletal muscle, and other evaluated tissues.

    Other Route(s)

    Intrathecal Route
Vector shedding studies of onasemnogene abeparvovec, which evaluated the elimination of vector DNA through saliva, urine, feces, and nasal secretions, were conducted in 134 individuals after intrathecal administration. Vector DNA was detectable in shedding samples from all participants, with feces identified as the primary route of elimination. Peak shedding occurred approximately 10, 3, 2, and 8 days post-dose for stool, urine, saliva, and nasal secretions, respectively, and more than 90% of the vector DNA was excreted within 2 weeks after administration.

Use polypropylene syringes and polypropylene, polyethylene, or methacrylate-acrylonitrile-butadiene-styrene caps; do not use syringes or caps made from polyvinylchloride (PVC), bisphenol-A (BPA), bis(2- ethylhexyl) phthalate (DEHP), or latex.
Storage: Once the dose is drawn into the syringe, it may be stored in the refrigerator for up to 24 hours, including a 5-hour maximum time out-of-refrigeration allowance within the 24-hour period. Discard the vector-containing syringe if not injected within this time period. Do not refreeze.
 
Procedural Preparation
Consider sedation and image-guided injection based on clinical judgment.
Evaluate the patient for any conditions that may contraindicate lumbar puncture or increase procedural risk before administration.
Prior to administration, remove 3 mL of cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) using a lumbar puncture needle to create space for the injection volume.
 
Intrathecal Injection
Administer only by health care professionals (e.g., interventional radiologist or neurologist) experienced in performing lumbar punctures.
Administer the dose as an intrathecal bolus injection over approximately 1 to 2 minutes through the lumbar puncture needle.
Place the patient in Trendelenburg position (head down at 30 degrees for 15 minutes). Adjust positioning and duration based on clinical status to enhance distribution.
Follow standard post-lumbar puncture care protocols.

Onasemnogene abeparvovec is given in conjunction with oral corticosteroids. When feasible, adjust an individual's vaccination schedule to accommodate concomitant corticosteroid administration prior to and after onasemnogene abeparvovec infusion. Seasonal RSV prophylaxis is not precluded.

Dosage Adjustment Guidelines
  Hepatic Impairment
  Specific guidelines for dosage adjustments in hepatic impairment are not available; it appears that no dosage adjustments are needed. Although dosage adjustments are not available, monitor liver function before and after onasemnogene abeparvovec therapy because acute serious liver injury and acute liver failure have occurred.
