Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  General Information
  Capsaicin should not be applied to irritated or broken skin, or to areas with skin abrasion. Due to the potential for enhanced absorption, capsaicin should not be used with a heating pad or applied after strenuous exercise. When used for self-medication, if a person's condition worsens or if symptoms persist for more than 7 days, or clear up and occur again within a few days, capsaicin therapy should be discontinued and a physician should be consulted.Capsaicin should not be used in patients who are known to be sensitive to the fruits of capsicum plants (e.g., hot peppers).Topical capsaicin has no specific precautions for use during pregnancy or breast-feeding.Use of capsaicin is not recommended in children or infants < 2 years of age, except under the direction of a physician. According to the manufacturer, Capzasin-P® is not recommended for use in children < 12 years of age unless directed by a physician.

    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components. Do not use capsaicin in individuals who are known to be sensitive to the fruits of capsicum plants (e.g., hot peppers).
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Capsaicin topical use appears to be of low risk during pregnancy. Capsaicin is negligibly absorbed systemically following topical administration, and maternal use is not expected to result in fetal exposure to capsaicin. In animal reproductive studies, no evidence of malformations was observed when capsaicin was administered by the topical route (patches or topical solution) to pregnant rats and rabbits during the period of organogenesis at doses of up to 11- and 37-times, respectively, the maximum recommended human dose (MRHD) of 716 mg/day capsaicin via skin patch.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Topical use of capsaicin appears to be compatible with breast-feeding. Capsaicin is negligibly absorbed systemically following topical administration, and breast-feeding is not expected to result in exposure of the infant to capsaicin. There are no data on the effects of capsaicin on milk production. To minimize potential direct exposure of capsaicin to the breastfed infant, avoid applying capsaicin directly to the nipple and areola.
    Headline
    cardiovascular disease
    General Information
    Use capsaicin 8% topical system with caution in individuals with cardiovascular disease. Individuals with unstable or poorly controlled hypertension, or a recent history of cardiovascular or cerebrovascular events, may be at an increased risk of adverse cardiovascular effects because of the associated transient increase in blood pressure. Blood pressure elevation is likely induced by procedural pain. Be prepared to treat acute pain with local cooling and/or appropriate analgesic medications.
    Headline
    skin hypoesthesia
    General Information
    Screen individuals with pre-existing sensory deficits (skin hypoesthesia) for signs of sensory deterioration or loss prior to each application of the capsaicin 8% topical system. Reductions in sensory function have been reported following the administration of the capsaicin 8% topical system. If sensory deterioration or loss is detected or pre-existing sensory deficit worsens, reconsider continued use.

Adverse Reactions
  Headline
  Adverse Reactions
  General Information
  The most common adverse effect associated with use of capsaicin is transient skin irritation (e.g., burning, stinging, warm sensation) at the site of application. Although this sensation usually disappears after the first several days of treatment, it may persist for 2—4 weeks or longer. Skin irritation is related to the initial excitatory effect of capsaicin on type C fibers and their release of substance P. The burning or stinging sensation produced during initial administration may be prolonged if capsaicin is applied less than 3—4 times per day. In addition, other factors such as heat or humidity, wrappings (e.g., clothing or bandages), bathing in warm water, or sweating may intensify the sensation. Patients with arthritis or postmastectomy pain generally experience less intense burning than do patients with peripheral neuropathies. Although the reason(s) for this difference is unclear, it may be related to the differences in etiology or pathogenesis of the pain syndromes.Cough has been reported rarely upon removal of clothing or bed sheets that have covered the site of application of capsaicin. This effect has been attributed to respiratory irritation from inhalation of the residue of the dried cream.

    Headline
    burns, contact dermatitis, drug-induced body odor, ecchymosis, edema, erythema, hyperesthesia, maculopapular rash, paresthesias, pruritus, skin irritation, urticaria, xerosis
    General Information
    The most common adverse effect associated with the use of capsaicin is transient skin irritation and/or paresthesias (e.g., burning, stinging, warm sensation) at the site of application; the severity of this effect appears to be dose-related. With the use of capsaicin 0.025% or 0.075% cream, this sensation usually disappears after the first several days of continuous treatment; however, it may persist for 2 to 4 weeks or longer. Factors such as exposure of the treated area to heat or humidity, direct sunlight, wrappings (e.g., clothing or bandages), bathing in warm water, or sweating may intensify the sensation. The burning or stinging sensation produced during initial administration may be prolonged if capsaicin cream is applied less than 3 to 4 times per day. During clinical trials, the use of the capsaicin 8% topical system in people with postherpetic neuralgia was associated with application site reactions including erythema (63%), pain (42%), pruritus (6%), papules or maculopapular rash (6%), edema (4%), swelling (2%), and dryness or xerosis (2%). In a clinical trial of the patch in people with diabetic neuropathy (n = 186), application site reactions included burning sensation (14%), pain (10%), and erythema (2%). The majority of reactions were considered mild to moderate. Increases in pain with capsaicin 8% topical system were considered transient, on average starting during application, beginning to resolve after the topical system removal, and returning to baseline by the end of treatment day. However, 3% of people reported a sustained increase in pain level. Reactions may necessitate local anesthetic pretreatment; even with such pretreatment, application site reactions may still occur. Cases of third-degree and second-degree burns with scarring have been reported following administration of the capsaicin 8% topical system. Some cases of third-degree burns required hospitalization and skin grafting and were reported in people who received the 8% topical system for an unapproved indication and/or received frequent dosing at an application site where there had been prior skin trauma. Ensure proper dosing and administration recommendations are followed. Other application site reactions reported following treatment with the capsaicin 8% topical system in postherpetic neuralgia trials include contact dermatitis, urticaria, ecchymosis (bruising), exfoliation, inflammation, anesthesia, site warmth, excoriation, and hyperesthesia. Excoriation (2%) and blisters (less than 1%) were reported in the diabetic neuropathy trial. Generalized pruritus (2%) and drug-induced body odor (abnormal skin odor) have also occurred. Skin irritation is related to the initial excitatory effect of capsaicin on type C fibers and their release of substance P. In clinical trials of capsaicin cream, people with arthritis or postmastectomy pain generally experience less intense burning than do people with peripheral neuropathies. Although the reason(s) for this difference is unclear, it may be related to the differences in etiology or pathogenesis of the pain syndromes. The FDA has warned healthcare providers and consumers about the rare risk of serious skin injuries, including first- to third-degree burns, with the use of over the counter topical analgesics used for mild muscle and joint pain. Postmarketing surveillance and medical literature review detected 43 cases of burns on application sites with the use of topical analgesic patches, balms, and creams. Few cases have been reported with capsaicin containing products. Many burns appeared in areas where the product was applied following 1 application, with severe burning or blistering occurring within 24 hours. Some burns resulted in hospitalization. People should not apply topical analgesics such as capsaicin to wounds or broken, irritated skin, and the area should not be tightly wrapped or bandaged. Contact with mucous membranes and application of heat to the affected area should be avoided. Items such as heating pads, lamps, or hot water bags and bottles may increase the risk of burns. Discuss the risk of burns, as warnings on over the counter topical analgesics for joint or muscle pain are not required. People who experience pain, skin blistering, or swelling should stop use and contact their health care provider for treatment.

    Capsaicin is a known respiratory irritant. Cough, sneezing, nasal irritation, and throat irritation have been reported in individuals treated with capsaicin.     hypertension
    General Information
    Use of capsaicin 8% topical system is associated with a transient increase in blood pressure related to treatment-induced increases in pain. Hypertension was reported in 2% of individuals with postherpetic neuralgia treated with capsaicin 8% topical system (n = 622) and 1% of individuals treated with a control patch (contained a low concentration of capsaicin 0.04% to maintain blinding) (n = 495). In a clinical trial of the capsaicin 8% topical system in individuals with diabetic neuropathy (n = 186), hypertension was reported in 2% of individuals treated with the topical system. In clinical trials, blood pressure elevations averaged less than 10 mmHg and returned to baseline within two hours of the removal of the topical system; no lasting effect was measured at the 4, 8, or 12-week follow-up visits. Appropriate pain management during treatment may minimize risk.

Description
  Text
  Capsaicin is a topical analgesic for external use. As the major pungent ingredient of hot chili peppers and other botanicals, capsicum preparations (e.g., capsaicin, capsicum, and capsicum oleoresin) can be derived from the fruit of various species of plants of the Solanaceae (i.e., nightshade) family or synthetically. Capsaicin is available as a topical solution, creams, and transdermal patches are available over-the-counter for the temporary relief of pain from arthritis, myalgias, arthralgias, and neuralgias. The FDA has granted capsaicin orphan drug status in the treatment of postherpetic neuralgia, intermetatarsal neuroma, erythromelalgia, and HIV-associated neuropathy. The prescription-only capsaicin 8% topical system is approved for the management of neuropathic pain associated with postherpetic neuralgia (PHN) and for the management of diabetic neuropathy of the feet.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Capsaicin's analgesic mechanism involves dose- and duration-dependent effects on transient receptor potential vanilloid 1 (TRPV1)-expressing nociceptors. At high concentrations, capsaicin activates TRPV1 receptors and produces a large calcium ion influx, which activates calpain and can lead to long-term defunctionalization through localized structural ablation of TRPV1-positive nociceptive nerve terminals. This mechanism can produce analgesia lasting for months, with pain potentially returning as nerve terminals reinnervate the treated area. TRPV1-mediated calcium ion influx and calpain activation are strongly supported as mechanisms for long-lasting analgesia in neuropathic pain models and are consistent with the proposed mechanism of high-concentration topical capsaicin. At lower concentrations, capsaicin can produce short-term, reversible defunctionalization without structural ablation, partly through calcium ion-dependent impairment of voltage-dependent sodium and calcium channels and inhibition of Piezo2 mechanosensitive ion channels, thereby reducing nociceptor excitability and pain signal transmission. Capsaicin also induces calcium ion-dependent TRPV1 receptor desensitization, diminishing responses to subsequent stimuli; this may reduce inflammatory hyperalgesia but is generally associated with more transient analgesia. Capsaicin has also historically been associated with depletion of substance P from sensory nerve terminals, which may contribute to reduced pain signaling as well. Capsaicin 8% topical system contains capsaicin solubilized in diethylene glycol monoethyl ether (DGME), facilitating localized delivery of capsaicin into the epidermis and dermis during administration. Capsaicin is negligibly absorbed systemically following topical administration.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics

    Description
    Following the topical application of capsaicin, systemic exposure is minimal. Application of a single capsaicin 8% topical system resulted in low systemic exposure to capsaicin in one-third of an unstated number of studied individuals; Cmax was less than 5 nanograms/mL in all individuals and occurred at 60 minutes after the topical system application. The capsaicin plasma concentration of most individuals was undetectable within 3 to 6 hours following the removal of the topical system. Use of a single 60 minute topical system results in a 30% or greater relief of postherpetic neuralgia in as soon as 1 week and persisting for up to 12 weeks following application.

Administration
  General Information
  For storage information, see the specific product information within the How Supplied section.
 
Accidental exposure to capsaicin can cause severe irritation of eyes, mucous membranes, respiratory tract, and skin. Avoid mucous membrane and ocular exposure to capsaicin. If capsaicin comes in contact with the eyes, rinse well with water; advise individuals to seek medical help if irritation persists. Ensure proper administration technique is followed for the capsaicin 8% patch to minimize the likelihood of accidental exposure.
 
Advise individuals using capsaicin to avoid conditions contributing to an elevated core body temperature, including the application of heat, the use of a heating pad or hot water bottle, or direct sunlight (UV) exposure to the area of product application immediately before, during, or after use. Do not use capsaicin within 1 hour before or after a bath, shower, hot tub, sauna, or strenuous exercise due to a potential for enhanced absorption. Treated areas may be sensitive to heat for a few days after treatment.

Use nitrile gloves at all times while handling and disposing of capsaicin topical system and while in contact with treatment area(s); do not use latex gloves as latex gloves do not provide full protection. It is advisable for providers to utilize a face mask and protective glasses.

Do not hold medicated topical system near eyes, nose, or mouth. Avoid application to the face, eyes, nose, or scalp to avoid exposure risk to the eyes or mucous membranes. If irritation of eyes or mucous membranes occurs, remove the affected individual from the vicinity of the topical system and flush eyes and mucous membranes with cool water.
