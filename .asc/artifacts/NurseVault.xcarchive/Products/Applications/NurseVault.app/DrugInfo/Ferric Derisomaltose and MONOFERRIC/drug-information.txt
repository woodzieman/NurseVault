    Headline
    iron overload disorder
    General Information
    Do not administer ferric derisomaltose to patients with iron overload disorder (e.g., hemochromatosis). Excessive therapy with parenteral iron can lead to excess iron storage and possibly iatrogenic hemosiderosis or hemochromatosis. Monitor the hematologic response (hemoglobin and hematocrit) and iron parameters (serum ferritin and transferrin saturation) during parenteral iron therapy.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    There are no data available on the use of ferric derisomaltose during pregnancy to evaluate for a drug-associated risk of major birth defects, miscarriage, or adverse outcomes. Studies on the use of intravenous iron products in pregnant individuals have not reported an association with adverse developmental outcomes. However, these data are insufficient to definitively exclude or establish the risk of adverse outcomes. Untreated iron deficiency in pregnancy is associated with adverse outcomes, including postpartum anemia, increased risk for preterm delivery, and low birth weight. However, serious adverse reactions, including circulatory failure, hypotension, in the context of an anaphylactic reaction, may occur in patients administered parenteral iron products. This may have serious consequences in pregnancy, such as fetal bradycardia, especially during the second and third trimester. Advise pregnant individuals of the potential fetal risk. In animal studies, iron complexes were teratogenic and embryocidal in non-iron-depleted pregnant animals; these findings may be due to iron overload and may not be applicable to patients with iron deficiency. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits administered ferric derisomaltose during organogenesis showed adverse developmental effects, including structural abnormalities and embryo-fetal mortality, at doses approximately 0.09 and 0.4 times the MRHD of 1,000 mg, respectively, based on body surface area.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Ferric derisomaltose is compatible with breast-feeding. Iron is a normal component of human milk; the concentration in human milk is not readily affected by dietary intake or the serum iron concentration. Limited data indicate that the iron concentration in human milk is increased 3 days after ferric derisomaltose administration, but that the concentration remains within the normal range. By 7 days after the dose, iron concentrations in human milk are similar to concentrations in lactating people using oral iron supplementation. It may be prudent to monitor the breastfed child for gastrointestinal side effects such as constipation and diarrhea.

Desogestrel: Ethinyl Estradiol; Ferrous Bisglycinate: (Major) Parenteral iron formulas are generally only indicated for use in patients with documented iron deficiency in whom oral administration is either impossible or unsatisfactory.     anaphylactoid reactions, angioedema, erythema, infusion-related reactions, pruritus, rash, skin discoloration, urticaria
    General Information
    Serious hypersensitivity reactions, including anaphylactoid reactions, some of which have been life-threatening or fatal, have been reported with ferric derisomaltose. Individuals may present with shock, clinically significant hypotension, loss of consciousness, and/or collapse. Monitor for signs and symptoms of hypersensitivity during and after ferric derisomaltose administration for at least 30 minutes and until clinically stable after completion of the infusion. In clinical trials, serious or severe hypersensitivity reactions were reported in 0.3% (n = 6/2,008) of subjects treated with ferric derisomaltose. Reactions included hypersensitivity in 3 subjects, infusion-related reactions in 2 subjects, and asthma in 1 subject. Rash was reported in 1% of subjects treated with ferric derisomaltose in clinical trials. Anaphylactic/anaphylactoid reactions, angioedema, Fishbane reaction, erythema, pruritus, urticaria, skin discoloration, skin exfoliation, and sweating have been reported with ferric derisomaltose in postmarketing experience.

Description
  Text
  Ferric derisomaltose is a parenteral iron replacement product indicated for the treatment of iron deficiency in adults who have an intolerance or unsatisfactory response to oral iron or who have non-hemodialysis dependent chronic kidney disease. Ferric derisomaltose was shown to be noninferior to IV iron sucrose for change in hemoglobin from baseline to week 8 in 2 clinical trials. Like other parenteral iron products, hypersensitivity may also occur with ferric derisomaltose; serious and fatal anaphylactic reactions have been reported.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Ferric derisomaltose is a complex of iron hydroxide and derisomaltose, an iron carbohydrate oligosaccharide that releases iron. Iron binds to transferrin for transport to erythroid precursor cells to be incorporated into hemoglobin.
 
Normal erythropoiesis is dependent on the concentration of iron and erythropoietin available in the plasma. Approximately two-thirds of total body iron is in the circulating erythrocytes as hemoglobin, the major factor in oxygen transport to the tissues. Stored iron, in the form of ferritin and hemosiderin, accumulates during periods of positive iron balance and can be mobilized to meet iron requirements when iron intake is low. Administration of iron does not stimulate the production of red blood cells, nor does it correct abnormalities not caused by iron deficiency. A therapeutic response to treatment with iron products is dependent on the patient's ability to absorb and use the iron. The response to iron therapy is also influenced by the cause of the deficiency as well as other illnesses that can affect normal erythropoiesis. A positive response to iron treatment can be noted by an increase in the hemoglobin concentration by 1 g/dL or more 4 weeks after initiating therapy, assuming that transfusion or other interventions cannot explain the improvements in the patient's clinical status.
 
Iron-containing proteins and enzymes are important in oxidation-reduction reactions, especially those of the mitochondria. Iron is a component of iron-containing heme proteins (e.g., hemoglobin, myoglobin), iron-sulfur enzymes (e.g., flavoproteins), proteins for iron storage and transport (e.g., transferrin) and other iron-containing enzymes. Iron deficiency not only causes anemia and decreased oxygen delivery, but it also reduces the metabolism of muscle and decreases mitochondrial activity. Thus, iron is important to several metabolic functions, which are independent of its importance to erythropoiesis.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Intravenous Route
    Description
    After a single IV dose of ferric derisomaltose, peak concentrations and total exposure of serum total iron increased approximately proportionally over the 100 to 1,000 mg dose range. The Cmax and AUC of serum total iron (geometric mean and CV%) after a single 1,000 mg dose were 408 (10.5) mcg/mL and 17,730 (22.1) mcg x hour/mL, respectively.
  Text
  Ferric derisomaltose is administered intravenously. Circulating iron is removed from the plasma by cells of the reticuloendothelial system. The iron is bound to the available protein moieties to form hemosiderin or ferritin, the physiological storage forms of iron, or to a lesser extent, to the transport molecule transferrin. Due to the size of the complex, ferric derisomaltose is not excreted via the kidneys; small quantities of iron are eliminated in the urine and feces. After a single 1,000 mg dose of ferric derisomaltose, the mean elimination half-life is 27 hours. Serum ferritin concentrations peak approximately 7 days after an IV dose of ferric derisomaltose and slowly return to stable concentrations after approximately 4 weeks.

  Route Specific Administrations
    Administration Details
      Name
      Intravenous Administration
      Description
      Dilution
Withdraw appropriate volume of ferric derisomaltose and dilute in 100 to 500 mL of 0.9% NaCl Injection. The final diluted concentration should be more than 1 mg/mL.
Each vial of ferric derisomaltose is for single-dose only; discard unused portion.
The compatibility of ferric derisomaltose with other drugs has not been established. Therefore, it should not be mixed with or physically added to solutions containing other drugs.
Storage: After dilution in 0.9% NaCl Injection, ferric derisomaltose may be stored at room temperature for up to 8 hours.
 
Intravenous infusion
Administer via IV infusion over 20 minutes.
Observe for signs and symptoms of hypersensitivity during and for at least 30 minutes after administration. If hypersensitivity reactions occur, stop ferric derisomaltose administration. Serious anaphylactoid reactions require appropriate resuscitation measures.
Extravasation of ferric derisomaltose may cause brown discoloration at the extravasation site, which may be long-lasting. Monitor for extravasation. If extravasation occurs, discontinue the ferric derisomaltose administration at that site.
