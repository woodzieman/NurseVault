Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  General Information
  Econazole cream is for external use only; it is not for ophthalmic administration or vaginal administration.Econazole is classified as pregnancy category C. Econazole has not been shown to be teratogenic when administered orally to mice, rabbits, or rats. Fetotoxic or embryotoxic effects were observed in oral studies with rats receiving 10—80 times the human dermal dose. Thus, econazole should be used during the first trimester of pregnancy only when considered essential to the welfare of the patient and during the second or third trimester of pregnancy only when clearly needed.It is not known whether econazole is excreted in human milk. Following oral administration to lactating rats, econazole and/or metabolites were excreted in milk and were found in nursing pups. Although systemic absorption following topical application is extremely low, caution is advised when econazole is administered to a woman who is breast-feeding.Topical econazole has been used in some children 3 months of age or older without unusual adverse effects.

    pregnancy
    General Information
    Use econazole cream during the first trimester of pregnancy only when considered essential to the welfare of the patient. Use during the second and third trimesters should be reserved for clear indications; however, fetal risk is likely to be minimal with appropriate use. There are no available data on econazole use during human pregnancy to evaluate a drug-associated risk of major birth defects, miscarriage, or adverse maternal or fetal outcomes. In animal reproduction studies, econazole did not cause fetal malformations at oral doses of 40 to 80 times the human dermal dose. However, fetotoxic or embryotoxic effects were observed in animals receiving oral doses 10 to 80 times the human dermal dose.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Econazole is compatible with breast-feeding. There is no information available on the presence of econazole in human milk, its effects on the breast-fed child, or its effects on milk production. Absorption after topical administration is minimal; hence, concentrations in breast milk are likely to be subclinical or even undetectable. The potential risk to the breastfed child is low. Alternatives to consider include clotrimazole and miconazole.

Adverse Reactions
  Headline
  Adverse Reactions
  General Information
  During clinical trials, approximately 3% of patients treated with econazole cream experienced adverse events thought possibly to be due to the drug. Adverse effects consisted mainly of pruritus, skin irritation (primarily burning or stinging) and erythema. One case of pruritic maculopapular rash has also been reported. These adverse events usually occur after 2—4 days of treatment and are often associated with application of the drug to the inguinal area. The majority of these reactions are transient and do not require drug discontinuation.Like other topical imidazole derivatives, contact dermatitis has been reported rarely with econazole. Cross-sensitization among the drugs (especially between miconazole and econazole) has been reported.

Description
  Text
  Econazole is an imidazole antifungal agent available for topical use. It differs structurally from miconazole in the absence of a chlorine atom at the 2 position of the phenylmethoxy group. Econazole is used primarily to treat cutaneous candidiasis and tinea infections, including tinea versicolor. In vitro, the activity of econazole against dermatophytes and Candida albicans appears to be similar to that of clotrimazole, miconazole, or tioconazole. Although econazole has been used effectively for the treatment of vulvovaginal candidiasis, an intravaginal preparation is not commercially available in the US. This drug was approved by the FDA in 1982.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Like other imidazoles, econazole exerts its effect by disrupting normal fungal cell membrane permeability. Ergosterol is an essential component of the fungal cell membrane. Econazole may inhibit ergosterol synthesis by interacting with 14-alpha-lanosterol demethylase, a cytochrome P-450 enzyme necessary for converting lanosterol to ergosterol. Inhibition of ergosterol synthesis results in increased cellular permeability, causing leakage of cellular contents such as phosphorous-containing compounds and potassium. Econazole is usually fungistatic.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Topical Route
    Description
    Following topical application of econazole, systemic absorption is extremely low. Although most of the applied drug remains on the skin surface, drug concentrations found in the stratum corneum greatly exceed the MIC for dermatophytes. Inhibitory concentrations are achieved in the epidermis and as deep as the middle region of the dermis. Use of an occlusive dressing appears to only slightly increase the extent of dermal penetration of the drug.
  Text
  Econazole is applied topically to the skin. It is not for ophthalmic use. Less than 1% of an applied dose is recovered in urine and feces.
