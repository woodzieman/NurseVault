Description
  Text
  Lunsotogene parvec is an adeno-associated virus vector-based gene therapy indicated for the treatment of severe-to-profound and profound sensorineural hearing loss (any frequency more than 90 dB HL) associated with molecularly confirmed biallelic variants in the OTOF gene, preserved outer hair cell function, and no prior cochlear implant in the same ear. It is administered as a single-dose intracochlear infusion. In an ongoing, multicenter, single-arm study, 24 pediatric patients (10 months to 16 years) received lunsotogene parvec. Among 20 patients with Week 24 assessments, 16 (80%) achieved the primary endpoint of hearing sensitivity threshold 70 dB HL or less by average pure tone audiometry (PTA), and 14 (70%) demonstrated an auditory brainstem response to a 90 dB normalized hearing level click stimulus. Lunsotogene parvec requires intracochlear surgery and close monitoring for procedure-related adverse reactions. It is not recommended in individuals in whom preoperative imaging demonstrates that access to the inner ear is not feasible, including those with abnormal mastoid pneumatization or clinically significant anatomic variations of the middle ear and inner ear. Lunsotogene parvec was FDA-approved in April 2026.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Intracochlear infusion of lunsotogene parvec results in expression of the OTOF gene primarily in inner hair cells, facilitating production of functional otoferlin protein and restoration of synaptic transmission to the auditory nerve.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Other Route(s)
    Description
    Intracochlear infusion
In a clinical study, peak concentration of lunsotogene parvec occurred on Day 1 and declined substantially thereafter. Vector DNA was detectable in plasma, urine, and saliva on Day 1 and undetectable by Week 2 (next sampling time point). In stool, vector DNA was detectable through Week 6 and became undetectable by Week 12. In cynomolgus monkeys and mice, the highest concentrations of vector DNA were detected in cochleae. Human OTOF messenger RNA expression in cochleae was sustained over study durations, with peak expression at 6 weeks in monkeys and 4 weeks in mice followed by plateauing. Distribution to non-otic tissues was limited (at least 10-fold lower than cochlear concentrations in monkeys and at least 3-fold lower in mice for vector DNA). In monkeys and mice, the highest non-otic concentrations were detected in selected neural/lymphatic tissues; no measurable vector DNA was detected in ovaries, and minimal concentrations were detected in testes (1 of 4 mouse samples near detection limit).

General administration information
Confirm the individual has biallelic, likely pathogenic or pathogenic OTOF variants.
For prophylaxis against inflammatory and immunological responses, administer systemic oral corticosteroids equivalent in strength to prednisone 1 mg/kg/day on the day of infusion, maintain the same dose for 2 weeks, then taper over the following 2 weeks (Max: 60 mg/day).
For prophylaxis against post-operative infection, administer antibiotics before incision.
Administer age-appropriate vaccination prior to treatment. Administer vaccines at least 1 month before the first corticosteroid dose and at least 1 month after the last dose. Vaccinate against microorganisms that cause meningitis before surgery.
 
Preparation
Prepare using sterile technique under aseptic conditions in a Class II biological safety cabinet.
For each treated ear, thaw 1 single-dose vial at room temperature (approximately 30 minutes). Do not thaw or warm any other way.
Gently invert the vial 5 times. Do not shake or vortex.
Visually inspect the vial after thaw to ensure the product is clear to slightly opalescent and colorless, and that the vial has no signs of damage. Do not use if cloudiness, particulates, or color are present.
Withdraw the entire contents of the vial using the smallest compatible syringe size in the supplied kit (1 or 3 mL); ensure that at least 0.6 mL is available in the syringe.
Storage: Administer within 4 hours of puncturing the vial. If not used immediately, store the unpunctured thawed vial in the refrigerator for up to 24 hours or at room temperature for up to 8 hours. Do not refreeze once thawed.
 
Administration
Administer in the surgical suite under general anesthesia with controlled aseptic conditions.
Use only the provided Administration Kit for use with the product.
Attach the syringe to the provided catheter. Do not flush the catheter.
Slowly prime the catheter manually until lunsotogene parvec is just visible at the catheter tip; the approximate priming volume is 0.11 mL.
Confirm priming of the catheter by initiating the pump and verifying the presence of lunsotogene parvec at the catheter tip; ensure that at least 0.3 mL of the suspension is available.
After surgical placement of the catheter, administer 0.24 mL per ear using a syringe pump set to deliver at an infusion rate of 0.9 mL/hour (infusion time approximately 16 minutes).
Monitor continuously to ensure no interruptions of the infusion.
If the infusion is interrupted for any reason, inspect the system to determine cause and resume if deemed medically appropriate based on length and cause of the interruption.
After the infusion is complete, leave the catheter in place for an additional 5-minute hold prior to removal.
After infusion, discard any remaining product and disposable materials according to local biosafety guidelines.
