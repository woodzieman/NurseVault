Drug Class: Filgrastim is a granulocyte colony-stimulating factor (G-CSF) involved in the regulation and production of neutrophils in response to host defense needs.

Routes: injection (IV/IM/SC); Common adult doses: 10 mcg/kg/day IV or subcutaneously.

Common Side Effects (excluding routine GI upset): anaphylactoid reactions, angioedema, wheezing, splenic rupture, splenomegaly, acute respiratory distress syndrome (ards), bleeding, cough, dyspnea, hemoptysis, hypoxia, azotemia, glomerulonephritis, hematuria, proteinuria, capillary leak syndrome, edema, hypoalbuminemia, hypotension, alopecia, erythema, maculopapular rash, rash, vasculitis, asthenia, fatigue, fever, malaise, peripheral edema, arthralgia

In an Overdose: No overdose/antidote information is listed in this file.

Key Nursing Considerations: Contraindications/precautions: The safe and effective use of filgrastim concurrently with cytotoxic chemotherapy has not been established. Rapidly dividing myeloid cells are potentially more sensitive to cytotoxic drugs.

          Dosage Description
          6 mcg/kg subcutaneously twice daily initially. Individualize the dosage to maintain clinical benefit based on patient clinical course and absolute neutrophil count (ANC). In a postmarketing surveillance study in 731 patients (adults, n = 302; pediatric patients, n = 429), the median filgrastim dose was 6 mcg/kg/day. Rarely, doses of 100 mcg/kg/day or more have been required. The starting dose for congenital neutropenia was 11.5 mcg/kg/day (based on actual body weight) given subcutaneously in 2 divided doses in a randomized trial in patients with chronic severe neutropenia (n = 123; median age of 12.1 years; range, 7 months to 76 years). In this study, the filgrastim dosage was adjusted to a level that maintained a median monthly ANC between 1,500 cells/mm3 and 10,000 cells/mm3. The dosage was increased if the median ANC was below 1,500 cells/mm3 for 2 weeks. The dosage was decreased if the median ANC was 10,000 cells/mm3 or more for 4 weeks. The median ANC was significantly improved in 62 patients who received 4 months of filgrastim compared with 60 patients who received 4 months of observation only (6.1 cells/mm3 vs. 0.21 cells/mm3). In patients with congenital neutropenia, the median ANC was 3.45 cells/mm3 in the filgrastim arm (n = 31; median age of 7.7 years; range, 1.1 to 32.9 years) and 0.15 cells/mm3 in the observation only arm (n = 29; median age of 8 years; range, 0.6 to 33.8 years).

Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  General Information
  The safe and effective use of filgrastim concurrently with cytotoxic chemotherapy has not been established. Rapidly dividing myeloid cells are potentially more sensitive to cytotoxic drugs. Filgrastim should not be used for 24 hours before, or in the 24 hours after administration of cytotoxic chemotherapy. Concurrent use with chemotherapeutic agents that have a delayed myelosuppressive effect (e.g., nitrosoureas), mitomycin C, or antimetabolites such as 5-fluorouracil or cytosine arabinoside have not been evaluated. Concurrent use should be avoided.
 
Filgrastim should be avoided in patients receiving concurrent chemotherapy and radiation therapy, especially radiation therapy involving the mediastinum. In the absence of chemotherapy, patients receiving radiation therapy involving large fields may be treated with filgrastim if prolonged delays in radiation therapy secondary to neutropenia are expected.
 
The administration of filgrastim for peripheral blood stem cell (PBSC) mobilization has been associated with rare cases of splenic rupture, some of which were fatal. Patients receiving filgrastim who report left upper abdominal pain or shoulder tip pain should be evaluated for an enlarged spleen or splenic rupture.
 
Adult respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS) has been reported in neutropenic patients with sepsis receiving filgrastim, and is postulated to be secondary to an influx of neutrophils to sites of inflammation in the lungs. Neutropenic patients who develop fever, pulmonary infiltrates, or respiratory insufficiency should be evaluated for the possibility of ARDS. In the event ARDS occurs, filgrastim therapy should be discontinued and/or withheld until resolution of ARDS.
 
Severe sickle cell crises have been reported in patients with sickle cell disease (specifically homozygous sickle cell anemia, sickle/hemoglobin C disease, and sickle/beta+ thalassemia) who receive filgrastim for peripheral stem cell mobilization or following chemotherapy. One of these cases was fatal. Only physicians qualified by specialized training or experience in the treatment of patients with sickle cell disorders should prescribe filgrastim for a patient with sickle cell disease. Filgrastim should be used with caution in patients with sickle cell disease, and only after careful consideration of the potential risks and benefits. Sickle cell patients who have received filgrastim should be kept well hydrated and monitored for the occurrence of sickle cell crisis.
 
Filgrastim is classified as FDA pregnancy risk category C. Although no adequate human studies have been conducted on the effects of this drug upon the fetus, animal reproduction studies have shown adverse fetal effects. Therefore, in making the decision to administer this drug during pregnancy, the potential risks to the fetus must be weighed against the potential benefits to the mother.
 
It is unknown whether filgrastim is excreted into breast milk. Because many drugs and proteins are excreted in breast milk, filgrastim should only be administered to woman who is breast-feeding her infant only if clearly needed.
 
Filgrastim increases leukopoiesis and can cause a dramatic leukocytosis. Complete blood and platelet counts should be performed before and during filgrastim therapy. The drug should be discontinued if the post-nadir absolute neutrophil count (ANC) rises above 10,000/mm3 due to an increased risk of toxicity. Following myelosuppressive chemotherapy or bone marrow transplantation, monitor the CBC 2—3 times weekly during filgrastim therapy. For patients with severe chronic neutropenia (SCN), during the first 4 weeks of filgrastim therapy and during the 2 weeks following any dosage adjustment, a CBC with differential and platelet count should be performed twice weekly. Once a patient with SCN is clinically stable, a CBC with differential and platelet count should be performed monthly during the first year of treatment. Thereafter, if clinically stable, quarterly monitoring of CBC is recommended.
 
The safety and efficacy of filgrastim in the treatment of neutropenia due to other hematological diseases (e.g., myelodysplastic syndrome or chronic myeloid leukemia) have not been established. Care should be taken to confirm the diagnosis of severe congenital neutropenia (SCN) prior to initiating filgrastim. If a patient with SCN develops abnormal cytogenetics or myelodysplasia, the risk and benefits of continuing filgrastim should be considered. The possibility that filgrastim can act as a growth factor for any neoplastic disease, including T-cell, lung, head and neck, and bladder tumor cell lines, cannot be excluded. However, in studies of patients receiving filgrastim for acute myeloid leukemia remission induction, there was no significant difference in remission rate, disease-free or overall survival as compared to placebo.
 
Filgrastim is contraindicated for use in patients who have E. coli protein hypersensitivity. Patients who may have reacted to E. coli asparaginase should not receive filgrastim.
 
Increased hematopoietic activity of the bone marrow in response to growth factor therapy (e.g., filgrastim, G-CSF) has been associated with transient positive bone imaging changes. This should be considered when interpreting bone-imaging results.

    The coadministration of certain medications may lead to harm and require avoidance or therapy modification; review all drug interactions prior to concomitant use of other medications.
    Headline
    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components. Filgrastim is contraindicated for use in patients with a history of serious allergic reactions to granulocyte colony-stimulating factors such as filgrastim or pegfilgrastim.
    Headline
    sickle cell disease
    General Information
    Use filgrastim with caution in patients with sickle cell disease or disorder because severe sickle cell crises have been reported in this patient population; some cases were fatal. Discontinue filgrastim if sickle cell crisis occurs.
    Headline
    radiation therapy
    General Information
    Due to the potential sensitivity of rapidly dividing myeloid cells, avoid the concurrent use of filgrastim with radiation therapy.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Available data from published studies, including several observational studies of outcomes in people exposed to filgrastim products and those who were unexposed, have not established an association with filgrastim use during pregnancy and major birth defects, miscarriage, or adverse outcomes. In animal reproduction studies, no fetal malformations were observed in rats or rabbits exposed to filgrastim during gestation. No maternal or fetal effects were observed in rats at doses up to 58 times the human dose. Reduced embryo-fetal survival and increased fetal abortion were observed in rabbits in the presence of maternal toxicity with filgrastim doses 2 to 10 times higher than the human dose.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Use filgrastim with caution during breast-feeding. Limited data suggest filgrastim is present in human milk in small amounts. Limited data from case reports observed no adverse effects in breast-fed children exposed to filgrastim via human milk. Filgrastim products are not orally bioavailable, further limiting total exposure in the breastfed child. There are no data on the effects of filgrastim on milk production. Consider the benefits of breast-feeding, the patient's clinical need for treatment, and any potential adverse effects on the breastfed child from the medication or from the patient's underlying medical condition.

Adverse Reactions
  Headline
  Adverse Reactions
  General Information
  Filgrastim usually does not cause serious or life-threatening adverse effects, and the drug only rarely requires discontinuation due to adverse reactions. Because most patients who receive filgrastim have recently undergone cytotoxic chemotherapy, many of the adverse effects reported with filgrastim therapy may actually result from the actions of the previously administered antineoplastic agents.
 
The most frequent adverse effect reported during filgrastim therapy is deep, throbbing medullary bone pain in up to 33% of patients. The pain occurs most often in the lower back, pelvis, ribs, sternum, or long bones (i.e., sites containing bone marrow). Sternal pain can imitate the chest pain (unspecified) of an acute myocardial infarction. Bone pain is usually mild to moderate in severity, and can be controlled in most patients with non-opiate analgesics; infrequently, bone pain is severe enough to require opiate analgesics. Bone pain is more frequent in patients treated with higher doses (i.e., >= 20 mcg/kg/day) given IV, and less frequent in patients given subcutaneous doses (i.e., 3—10 mcg/kg/day).
 
Due to the biological effect of filgrastim, leukocytosis (i.e., leukocyte count > 100,000 cells/mm3) can occur during therapy. White blood cell (WBC) counts of 100,000/mm3 or greater were observed in approximately 2% of patients receiving filgrastim at doses above 5 mcg/kg/day. White blood cell counts above 100,000 cells/mm3 represent a medical emergency because of the risk of serious adverse effects such as brain infarction, respiratory failure, intracranial hemorrhage, retinal hemorrhage, myocardial infarction, and acute limb ischemia. A complete blood count is recommended before filgrastim initiation and twice per week during filgrastim therapy. As leukocytosis may be caused by immature precursors (blasts) or expansion of mature leukocyte types (granulocytes, lymphocytes, monocytes), a WBC differential and peripheral blood smear will help determine the etiology of the WBC excess. Filgrastim should be discontinued if the post-nadir/post-chemotherapy absolute neutrophil count (ANC) rises above 10,000/mm3. An ANC greater than 10,000/mm3 is not associated with improved disease response but can lead to increased toxicity. Regular monitoring of WBC counts, particularly at the time of the recovery from the post chemotherapy nadir, is recommended in order to avoid excessive leukocytosis.
 
Mild to moderate hyperuricemia and elevated lactate dehydrogenase and alkaline phosphatase levels occur in 21—58% of patients receiving filgrastim. Transient hypotension (blood pressure < 90/60 mmHg), which does not require treatment, occurs in about 4% patients. Cardiac events (e.g., myocardial infarction, arrhythmias) have been reported following filgrastim; the relationship to filgrastim is not known.
 
One case of erythema nodosum has been reported in a patient receiving filgrastim following bone marrow transplantation.
 
In patients receiving filgrastim for peripheral blood stem cell mobilization, anemia and thrombocytopenia have been reported. Thrombocytopenia and anemia are associated with leukapheresis; however, the possibility that filgrastim mobilization contributed to these effects has not been ruled out. Thrombocytopenia (< 50,000/mm3) was noted in 12% of severe chronic neutropenia (SCN) patients with palpable spleens. In most cases, thrombocytopenia was managed by reducing or interrupting filgrastim therapy. Epistaxis was noted in 15% of SCN patients. Anemia was reported in 10% of SCN patients, but in most cases appeared to be due to frequent phlebotomy, chronic illness, or concurrent medications.
 
Splenomegaly has been observed in 30% of patients treated with filgrastim for severe chronic neutropenia (SCN). Abdominal pain or flank pain is infrequent. Left upper abdominal pain or shoulder tip pain accompanied by a rapid increase in spleen size should be carefully monitored due to the rare but serious risk of splenic rupture. Splenic rupture has been reported after the administration of filgrastim for peripheral stem cell mobilization in both health donors and cancer patients. Some of these cases were fatal.
 
Headache has been reported in 7% of patients receiving filgrastim for peripheral blood stem cell mobilization.
 
Anaphylactoid reactions have been reported on initial or subsequent treatment in < 1 in 4000 patients treated with filgrastim. The hypersensitivity reactions are characterized by systemic symptoms, most often dyspnea, facial edema, hypotension, rash (unspecified), tachycardia, urticaria, and wheezing. Reactions tend to occur within the first 30 minutes after administration and appear to occur more frequently in patients receiving intravenous filgrastim. Rapid resolution of symptoms occurred in most cases after administration of antihistamines, bronchodilators, and/or epinephrine. Symptoms recurred in more than half the patients who were rechallenged. Sweet's syndrome has been reported in patients receiving filgrastim.
 
Antibody formation or blunted or diminished response to filgrastim therapy has not been reported, including in those patients receiving daily filgrastim for almost 2 years.
 
Alveolar hemorrhage (bleeding) that manifested as pulmonary infiltrates and hemoptysis requiring hospitalization has been reported in healthy donors undergoing peripheral blood progenitor cell (PBPC) mobilization. Hemoptysis resolved with filgrastim, G-CSF discontinuation. The use of filgrastim, G-CSF for PBPC mobilization in healthy donors is not an approved indication.
 
Filgrastim may cause cutaneous vasculitis. Most cases were moderate or severe and involved patients with severe, chronic neutropenia receiving long-term filgrastim therapy. Symptoms of vasculitis generally developed simultaneously with an increase in the ANC and abated when the ANC decreased. Many patients were able to continue filgrastim at a reduced dose.
 
Sweet's syndrome (acute febrile neutrophilic dermatosis) has been reported post-marketing, but the relationship, if any, to filgrastim is unknown.
 
Acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS) has been reported in patients receiving filgrastim. The development of ARDS is postulated to be secondary to an influx of neutrophils to sites of inflammation in the lungs. Evaluate patients who develop fever, lung infiltrates, or respiratory distress for the possibility of ARDS. Discontinue filgrastim or withhold it until ARDS resolves. Patients should receive appropriate medical management in the event ARDS develops.

Description
  Text
  Filgrastim is a granulocyte colony-stimulating factor (G-CSF) involved in the regulation and production of neutrophils in response to host defense needs. It is indicated to reduce the incidence of infection in patients with nonmyeloid malignancies receiving myelosuppressive anticancer drugs associated with a significant incidence of febrile neutropenia; to reduce the time to neutrophil recovery and the duration of fever following induction or consolidation chemotherapy in patients with acute myeloid leukemia; to reduce the duration of neutropenia and related clinical sequelae in patients with nonmyeloid malignancies undergoing myeloablative chemotherapy followed by bone marrow transplantation; to reduce the incidence and duration of sequelae of neutropenia in symptomatic patients with severe chronic neutropenia; for the mobilization of peripheral blood progenitor cells for collection by leukapheresis; and to increase survival in patients acutely exposed to myelosuppressive doses of radiation. Acute respiratory distress syndrome, fatal splenic rupture, and glomerulonephritis have occurred with filgrastim.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Filgrastim is a granulocyte colony-stimulating factor (G-CSF). Colony-stimulating factors are glycoproteins which act on hematopoietic cells by binding to cell surface receptors and stimulating proliferation, differentiation commitment, and some end cell functional activation. Endogenous G-CSF is a lineage-specific colony-stimulating factor that is produced by monocytes, fibroblasts, and endothelial cells. G-CSF regulates the production of neutrophils within the bone marrow and affects neutrophil progenitor proliferation, differentiation, and selected end cell functions. G-CSF is not species-specific and has been shown to have minimal direct in vivo or in vitro effects on the production or activity of hematopoietic cell types other than the neutrophil lineage.

    Radiation Exposure
The pharmacokinetic parameters of filgrastim have not been evaluated in human subjects acutely exposed to myelosuppressive doses of radiation.   Text
  Filgrastim is administered intravenously (IV) or subcutaneously. The volume of distribution averaged 150 mL/kg. Clearance is nonlinear and is dependent on drug concentration and neutrophil count. G-CSF receptor-mediated clearance is saturated by a high concentration of filgrastim and clearance is diminished by neutropenia. Filgrastim is cleared by the kidney. The elimination half-life is approximately 3.5 hours, and the clearance is about 0.5 to 0.7 mL/minute/kg following IV filgrastim administration. The half-life of filgrastim is similar following IV (231 minutes) or subcutaneous (210 minutes) administration. Additionally, single IV doses or daily IV doses resulted in comparable half-life values.
Pharmacodynamics: A dose-dependent increase in circulating neutrophil counts occurred following filgrastim administration (dose range, 1 to 70 mcg/kg/day) in patients with nonmyeloid malignancies. This dose-dependent neutrophil increase occurred with IV (1 to 70 mcg/kg twice daily), subcutaneous (1 to 3 mcg/kg once daily), or continuous subcutaneous infusion (3 to 11 mcg/kg/day) administration. In most cases, the neutrophil count returned to baseline within 4 days of stopping filgrastim.

Dilution
After calculating/selecting the appropriate dose, dilute the filgrastim vial (Neupogen, Nivestym, Releuko, Zarxio; concentration of 300 mcg/mL) or prefilled syringe (Neupogen, Filkri, Nypozi, Zarxio) in 5% Dextrose Injection to a final concentration of 5 mcg/mL or more. Do NOT dilute with 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection; the product may precipitate.
Use a glass bottle, a polyvinyl chloride (PCV) or polyolefin IV bag, or a polypropylene syringe as a container for the diluted solution.
To protect the absorption of filgrastim to the plastic container in diluted solutions at concentrations of 5 to 15 mcg/mL, add albumin to a final concentration of 2 mg/mL.
Storage after dilution: Diluted filgrastim IV solutions from vial may be stored at room temperature for up to 24 hours (Neupogen, Nivestym) or at room temperature for up to 4 hours (Releuko, Zarxio) or refrigerated for up to 24 hours (Zarxio). Diluted filgrastim IV solutions from prefilled syringe may be stored at room temperature for up to 14 hours (Nypozi) or at room temperature for up to 24 hours (Neupogen, Filkri, Zarxio). The total storage time includes the infusion time.
IV Infusion
Administer the diluted IV infusion over 15 to 30 minutes or as a continuous IV infusion given over 24 hours.
