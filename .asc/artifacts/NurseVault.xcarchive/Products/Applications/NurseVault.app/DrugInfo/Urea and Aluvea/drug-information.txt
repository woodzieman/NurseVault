Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  General Information
  The majority of the adverse GI, CNS, and hematologic effects associated with urea administration can be alleviated or prevented by not exceeding an infusion rate of 4 ml of a 30% solution/minute. Patients also should be adequately hydrated and kept horizontal, if possible.Prolonged urea-induced diuresis can cause electrolyte imbalance, and patients should be observed for early warning signs. Hypovolemia may be masked and intensified by urea administration. Urea is contraindicated in patients with severe dehydration because the risk of urea-induced electrolyte depletion may be increased.Urea is contraindicated in patients with severe hepatic disease or impairment because blood ammonia concentrations can increase.Urea is contraindicated for use in patients with active intracranial bleeding, except when the drug is needed before surgical intervention to reduce bleeding (e.g., during craniotomy), because reduction of brain edema may increase bleeding.Transient increases in circulatory volume can cause circulatory overload, pulmonary edema, or exacerbation of congestive heart failure, so urea should be used cautiously in patients with cardiac disease.Urea should be used cautiously in patients with renal disease or impairment because these patients may not be able to excrete both the infused urea and endogenously produced urea. Urea is contraindicated in patients with severe renal impairment because accumulation of urea can lead to overexpansion of extracellular fluid and cause circulatory overload. Elderly patients are more likely to have age-related reduced renal function. Also, urea should not be infused into the lower extremities, especially of elderly patients, because of the danger of thrombophlebitis of superficial veins.Preparations of urea containing invert sugar should not be administered to patients with hereditary fructose intolerance due to aldolase deficiency.Systemic Urea is classified as FDA pregnancy risk category C; most topical urea formulations are rated pregnancy class B. It is not known whether systemic urea causes harm to the fetus of impair fertility. Systemic urea should only be used during pregnancy when the benefits outweigh any possible risk.Systemically administered urea should be used with caution in breast-feeding women, the extent of distribution of urea into breast milk is unknown.

    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    It is not known whether urea, in any formulation, can cause fetal harm when administered during pregnancy. Animal reproduction studies have not been conducted. Use urea in pregnant people only if clearly needed.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Use urea with caution during breast-feeding. There are no data on the presence of urea in human milk, its effects on the breast-fed child, or its effects on milk production. Consider the benefits of breast-feeding, the patient's clinical need for treatment, and any potential adverse effects on the breast-fed child from the medication or from the patient's underlying medical condition.

Acetaminophen; Ibuprofen: (Moderate) Nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) may reduce the natriuretic effect of diuretics in some patients. NSAIDS have been associated with an inhibition of prostaglandin synthesis, which may result in reduced renal blood flow leading to renal insufficiency and increases in blood pressure that are often accompanied by peripheral edema and weight gain. Patients taking diuretics and NSAIDS concurrently are at higher risk of developing renal insufficiency. If an NSAID and a diuretic are used concurrently, carefully monitor the patient for signs and symptoms of decreased renal function and diuretic efficacy.

Monitor serum potassium to determine the need for potassium supplementation and alteration in drug therapy.

If these drugs must be used together, it would be prudent to monitor renal function, serum electrolytes, and serum aminoglycoside concentrations. Adverse Reactions
  Headline
  Adverse Reactions
  General Information
  The majority of the adverse GI, CNS, and hematologic effects associated with urea administration can be alleviated or prevented by not exceeding an infusion rate of 4 ml of a 30% solution/minute. Patients also should be adequately hydrated and kept horizontal, if possible.The most frequent adverse effects of intravenously administered urea include headache, nausea/vomiting, syncope, disorientation, dizziness, mental confusion, hypotension, nervousness, hyperthermia, sinus tachycardia, and cardiotoxicity.Thrombosis can occur during administration of urea into the deep veins and is especially likely in older patients.Rapid administration of urea can cause hemolysis and ocular hemorrhage. Urea can affect the cerebral vasomotor centers, causing increased capillary bleeding.Prolonged urea-induced diuresis can cause tissue dehydration and electrolyte imbalance, so patients should be observed for early warning signs of hyponatremia or hypokalemia.Transient increases in circulatory volume can cause circulatory overload, pulmonary edema, or exacerbation of congestive heart failure, so urea should be used cautiously in patients with cardiac disease.Skin irritation (i.e., stinging, burning, or itching) following topical application of urea products is usually transient and normally subsides following discontinuation of the medication.

Description
  Text
  Urea (also known as carbamide) is available as topical formulations and a powder for oral solution. It is used topically to treat dry skin (xerosis) and for destruction and dissolution of dystrophic nails due to onychomycosis. As an oral agent, urea is an osmotic diuretic and is used for the treatment of hyponatremia, particularly due to SIADH.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Topically, urea promotes the uptake of water by the stratum corneum by allowing it to have a high water-binding capacity. This promotes hydration in dry skin and a mild keratolytic action in hyperkeratotic skin.
 
When used systemically, urea is an osmotic diuretic agent. Urea creates an osmotic gradient across the blood-brain barrier that promotes water flow out of the brain. As the urea gradient across the blood-brain barrier is diminished, it is replaced by an increase in serum sodium concentration that prevents plasma water from reentering the brain. This osmotic effect will persist as long as a gradient exists between the tissues and the blood; as urea diffuses into the tissues, the effect is diminished. Administration of large doses of urea will increase the osmotic pressure of the glomerular filtrate, inhibit the reabsorption of water and solutes in the renal tubule, and induce diuresis.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Oral Route
    Description
    Urea is hydrophilic and does not readily permeate artificial lipid bilayers. Urea enters the intracellular space rapidly (in less than 1 hour) throughout the body. Because urea penetrates into the CNS only about one-tenth as quickly as into muscle, a significant intravascular to CNS urea gradient occurs (during 4 to 10 hours). Urea is excreted in the urine, and with normal renal function, all of the administered dose is excreted within approximately 12 hours. Urea leads to a rapid and significant increase in serum sodium concentrations (approximately 7 mEq/L in 48 hours).

  Urea is used topically and orally. Specific pharmacokinetic parameters for topical application are not available.
