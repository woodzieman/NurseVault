Indication Specific Dosing
  General Information
  General Dosing Information
Switching between desmopressin formulations:
Always titrate dose individually according to diuresis (antidiuretic response) and electrolyte status (serum sodium).
Conversion from nasal spray to injection: The starting dose of the injection is one-tenth times the nasal spray dose.
Conversion from tablets to injection: Titrate dose individually according to diuresis and serum sodium concentrations due to the large variability in both pharmacodynamics and pharmacokinetics. Monitor closely during the initial dose titration period.
Conversion from tablets to nasal spray: Intranasal desmopressin is approximately 10 to 40-fold more potent than oral desmopressin; individualize dose titration.
Conversion from injection to nasal spray: Administer 10 times the amount of the injection dose as the nasal spray dose, rounding down to the nearest 10 mcg.

Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  General Information
  Desmopressin should not be used in patients with known desmopressin hypersensitivity. Severe allergic reactions, including anaphylaxis, have been reported.
 
Desmopressin is contraindicated in patients with moderate to severe renal impairment (CrCl 5%. Patients who are least likely to respond to desmopressin are those with severe homozygous von Willebrand's disease whose factor VIII and factor VIII/von Willebrand's factor activity are < 1%. In certain clinical situations it may be justified to try desmopressin therapy in patients with factor VIII levels of 2—5%; however, these patients should be monitored carefully. Desmopressin should not be used in severe classic von Willebrand's disease (type 1 disease), when there is evidence of an abnormal molecular form of factor VIII antigen, hemophilia B, or in patients with factor VIII antibodies. Desmopressin may be active in some, but not all, cases of type 2A, 2M, or 2N von Willebrand's disease. Although desmopressin has been effective in patients with type 2B disease, it is not usually used in these patients because of an increased risk of thromboembolism. Desmopressin is not active against type 3 von Willebrand's disease. Use of antihemophilic factor (factor VIII) products high in von Willebrand's factor (i.e., Alphanate, Humate P, and Koate-HP) is recommended in von Willebrand's disease patients who do not respond to desmopressin.
 
Desmopressin is contraindicated in patients with hyponatremia and in patients with a history of hyponatremia. Desmopressin should be used cautiously in patients with conditions that are associated with fluid and electrolyte imbalance such as cystic fibrosis, heart failure, and renal disease. Patients receiving desmopressin who have a preexisting fluid and electrolyte imbalance or who have no need of the antidiuretic effects are at an increased risk of developing water intoxication and hyponatremia. Patients with any other condition that causes fluid and electrolyte imbalance (e.g., habitual polydipsia, psychogenic polydipsia) or being treated with hypertonic fluids or drugs that cause SIADH should also be treated with caution (see Drug Interactions). Young children and the elderly are more prone to water intoxication and hyponatremia. Fluid intake should be adjusted downward in an effort to decrease the potential risk of developing water intoxication and hyponatremia with accompanying signs and symptoms (headache, nausea/vomiting, decreased serum sodium and weight gain). Excessive fluid intake should be avoided. Patients who do not have need of antidiuretic hormone for its antidiuretic effect should be cautioned to ingest only enough fluid to satisfy thirst, in an effort to decrease the potential for water intoxication and hyponatremia. Patients being treated for primary nocturnal enuresis (PNE) should not drink water at night, especially for the 1 hour before and 8 hours after tablet administration. Treatment with the tablets for PNE should be interrupted during acute illnesses (e.g., systemic infection, fever, recurrent vomiting, or diarrhea), during extremely hot weather, vigorous exercise, or other conditions associated with increased fluid intake. For all uses of desmopressin, particular attention should be paid to an extreme decrease in plasma osmolality. This is a rare occurrence, but can lead to seizures, possible coma, and death (see Adverse Reactions). In patients with diabetes insipidus or polyuria and polydipsia associated with head injury or surgery, urine volume and osmolality, and in some cases, plasma osmolality should be monitored periodically during therapy. In patients treated for nocturnal enuresis, serum electrolytes should be checked if therapy is continued beyond 7 days. Patients with polydipsia should not receive desmopressin for the treatment of bleeding abnormalities due to its antidiuretic effects.
 
Administration of intranasal desmopressin may be compromised by nasal mucosa changes (e.g., nasal trauma, nasal surgery, congestion, or severe atrophic rhinitis), cranial surgery, and nasal packing. Intravenous desmopressin should be considered. Although the manufacturer states that nasal congestion does not affect the absorption of intranasal desmopressin, some clinicians recommend a higher dose.
 
There are no adequate studies of the effects of this drug on the human fetus, but animal reproduction studies have shown no adverse fetal effects. Desmopressin is classified as FDA pregnancy risk category B. If given to a pregnant woman in conjunction with labor, this should not be done during obstetric delivery as the drug is probably passed to the fetus. Desmopressin may be given immediately following delivery. Desmopressin is not excreted in breast milk in significant amounts and is poorly absorbed orally, therefore, it is safe for a woman who is breast-feeding her infant while taking desmopressin.

    Headline
    Treatment-Related Restrictions of Use
    General Information
    Desmopressin is not effective in individuals with type 3 von Willebrand disease (vWD) and can cause platelet aggregation, thrombocytopenia, and possibly thrombosis in persons with type 2B vWD. Many individuals with type 2 vWD do not respond to desmopressin and require alternate treatment; however, a desmopressin trial can help confirm diagnosis and may be useful in some instances of mild bleeding in individuals with type 2 vWD. Confirm responsiveness before using desmopressin for therapeutic interventions. Individuals with vWD type 1 and von Willebrand factor (vWF) concentrations less than 0.3 International Units/mL or factor VIII activity equal to or less than 5% of normal may not respond to desmopressin. Desmopressin is not indicated for individuals with severe classic vWD (type 1), for the treatment of hemophilia B, or in individuals with factor VIII antibodies. In certain clinical situations, it may be justified to try desmopressin in individuals with factor VIII concentrations of 2% to 5%; however, carefully monitor these patients. Individuals with vWD subtype 1C, which is characterized by a shortened vWF half-life, may require alternative management in the setting of surgery. Do not use desmopressin as sole therapy in individuals with vWD undergoing major surgery.
    Headline
    adolescents, children, cystic fibrosis, electrolyte imbalance, females, gastroenteritis, hyponatremia, infants, polydipsia, salt-losing nephropathy, SIADH, systemic infection
    General Information
    Desmopressin is contraindicated in individuals with hyponatremia or a history of hyponatremia. When urine output is reduced by the antidiuretic effect of desmopressin, excessive fluid intake may lead to water intoxication with hyponatremia, which can be fatal if not promptly recognized and treated. The sublingual and injectable formulations are additionally contraindicated in individuals with known or suspected syndrome of inappropriate antidiuretic hormone (SIADH) secretion, polydipsia, or other conditions that may cause fluid or electrolyte imbalance, such as gastroenteritis, salt-losing nephropathy, or systemic infection. Close monitoring of fluid intake, volume status, and serum sodium and other electrolytes is required in all individuals receiving desmopressin, regardless of route of administration. Those at increased risk of hyponatremia include individuals with underlying conditions affecting fluid or electrolyte balance (e.g., cystic fibrosis, heart failure, renal disorders), those with habitual or psychogenic polydipsia, older adults, pediatric patients (e.g., infants, children, and adolescents), and those receiving concomitant medications that may cause hyponatremia. Females are more sensitive to the effects of desmopressin sublingual tablets and experienced a higher incidence of hyponatremia than males in clinical trials; therefore, the recommended dose for females is lower than for males. Confirm normal serum sodium before initiating or resuming therapy. Limit fluid intake from 1 hour before to 8 hours after each dose, and monitor serum sodium within 1 week, again at approximately 1 month, and periodically thereafter. Monitor serum sodium more frequently in individuals at increased risk of hyponatremia.
    Headline
    coronary artery disease, heart failure, hypertension, increased intracranial pressure, urinary retention
    General Information
    The sublingual and injectable formulations of desmopressin are contraindicated in individuals with heart failure or uncontrolled hypertension because fluid retention increases the risk for worsening of underlying conditions that are susceptible to volume status. Use other formulations of desmopressin with caution in individuals with heart failure. Desmopressin is not recommended for use in individuals with increased intracranial pressure or those with a history of urinary retention. Monitor blood pressure during desmopressin administration, particularly in those with coronary artery disease (insufficiency) and/or hypertension; desmopressin may cause hypotension (with compensatory increase in heart rate) or hypertension.
    Headline
    renal failure, renal impairment
    General Information
    Desmopressin is contraindicated in individuals with moderate to severe renal impairment (CrCl less than 50 mL/minute or eGFR less than 50 mL/minute/1.73 m2) and renal failure. Desmopressin acetate is substantially excreted by the kidneys, and the risk of adverse reactions may be greater in individuals with renal impairment than in those with normal renal function. However, desmopressin has been used off-label as a single injection for uremic bleeding in individuals with renal failure.
    Headline
    nasal disorder
    General Information
    Avoid the use of intranasal desmopressin in individuals with a nasal disorder or nasal mucosa abnormality, such as scarring or edema from chronic use, nasal blockage, nasal mucosal atrophy, severe atrophic rhinitis, or recent nasal surgery (e.g., transsphenoidal hypophysectomy), as these conditions may lead to erratic or unreliable absorption. Consider alternative desmopressin formulations in such cases.
    Headline
    geriatric
    General Information
    Geriatric adults treated with desmopressin are at increased risk for hyponatremia; therefore, monitor serum sodium closely and more frequently in these individuals. Restrict fluid intake to reduce the risk of hyponatremia and water intoxication. Because desmopressin is substantially excreted by the kidneys, adverse reactions may occur more frequently in individuals with renal impairment. As geriatric adults are more likely to have decreased renal function, use caution when selecting the dose and monitor renal function regularly. According to the Beers Criteria, desmopressin is considered a potentially inappropriate medication (PIM) in geriatric adults and avoidance is recommended for treating nocturia or nocturnal polyuria because there is a high risk of hyponatremia and safer alternatives are available.
    Headline
    neonates
    General Information
    Use desmopressin oral solution with caution in low-birth-weight neonates weighing less than 2,500 grams and in preterm neonates born before 34 weeks' gestation. Desmopressin oral solution contains benzoic acid, a metabolite of benzyl alcohol. Serious adverse reactions, including metabolic acidosis and death, have occurred in neonates exposed to intravenous products containing benzyl alcohol. Benzyl alcohol toxicity may cause gasping syndrome, a life-threatening neonatal condition characterized by new or worsening metabolic acidosis with progressive neurologic deterioration, seizures, intracranial hemorrhage, hematologic abnormalities, skin breakdown, hepatic and renal failure, hypotension, bradycardia, and gasping respirations that may progress to death. In reported cases, benzyl alcohol exposures of 99 to 234 mg/kg/day resulted in blood benzyl alcohol concentrations of 6.6 to 14.9 mg/dL. The minimum benzyl alcohol exposure associated with gasping syndrome in neonates is unknown.
    Headline
    labor, obstetric delivery, preeclampsia, pregnancy
    General Information
    Desmopressin has been used safely during pregnancy, including in patients with bleeding disorders and diabetes insipidus. Published literature and case reports spanning several decades have not identified a drug-associated risk of major birth defects, miscarriage, or other adverse pregnancy outcomes. In vitro data suggest poor placental transfer of desmopressin. Avoid desmopressin in patients with preeclampsia because its antidiuretic effect may worsen fluid retention and hyponatremia. The risk of desmopressin-induced hyponatremia may also be increased during labor and obstetric delivery, when intravenous fluids and oxytocic agents are commonly used. Careful fluid management is recommended during the peripartum and immediate postpartum periods to reduce the risk of hyponatremia. Pregnant patients with bleeding disorders may be at increased risk for bleeding during labor and delivery. In a systematic review of 30 studies evaluating desmopressin for treatment or prophylaxis of bleeding disorders during pregnancy (n = 216), desmopressin reduced pregnancy- and delivery-associated bleeding complications and demonstrated a favorable safety profile.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Desmopressin is compatible with breast-feeding. Desmopressin is present in human milk in negligible amounts, and clinically relevant exposure in the breastfed infant is not expected. Data are unavailable regarding the effects of desmopressin on milk production. In one study of postpartum patients given a single intranasal dose, the amount of desmopressin detected in human milk was 0.0001% to 0.0005% of the administered dose.

Description
  Text
  Desmopressin is a vasopressin analog indicated as an antidiuretic replacement therapy for the treatment of arginine vasopressin deficiency (AVD, formerly known as central diabetes insipidus) and also for the treatment of primary nocturnal enuresis, nocturia due to nocturnal polyuria, hemophilia A, and type 1 von Willebrand disease (vWD), with approved indications varying by formulation. Oral desmopressin is indicated for the treatment of AVD and primary nocturnal enuresis. Oral desmopressin is ineffective for the treatment of nephrogenic diabetes insipidus. Parenteral desmopressin is indicated for the treatment of AVD and hemophilia A with factor VIII concentrations more than 5% without factor VIII antibodies or mild to moderate type 1 vWD with factor VIII concentrations more than 5% to maintain hemostasis during surgical procedures and postoperatively and/or to reduce bleeding with episodes of spontaneous or traumatic injuries such as hemarthroses, intramuscular hematomas, or mucosal bleeding. Parenteral desmopressin is ineffective and not indicated for the treatment of nephrogenic diabetes insipidus and is also not indicated for the treatment of severe type 1 vWD or when there is evidence of an abnormal molecular form of factor VIII antigen. Intranasal desmopressin is indicated for the treatment of AVD. Intranasal desmopressin is not indicated for the treatment of nephrogenic diabetes insipidus or primary nocturnal enuresis, use in individuals with conditions that compromise the intranasal route of administration (e.g., severe nasal congestion and blockage, nasal mucosa atrophy, severe atrophic rhinitis, recent nasal surgery such as transsphenoidal hypophysectomy), impaired level of consciousness, or in individuals requiring doses less than 10 mcg or doses that are not multiples of 10 mcg. Intranasal desmopressin is not indicated for the treatment of primary nocturnal enuresis due to a higher risk of hyponatremia and hyponatremic convulsions with the use of intranasal desmopressin compared to oral desmopressin in postmarketing. Sublingual desmopressin is indicated for the treatment of nocturia due to nocturnal polyuria in adults who awaken at least 2 times per night to void. Desmopressin can cause hyponatremia and requires routine serum sodium monitoring. Desmopressin was FDA-approved in 1978.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Desmopressin's primary pharmacodynamic action involves its binding to vasopressin 2 (V2) receptors. V2 receptors are primarily located in the distal convoluted tubule and collecting ducts of the nephron in the kidneys. These receptors play a crucial role in regulating water reabsorption and urine concentration. By influencing the permeability of the renal tubules, V2 receptors help maintain the body's water balance and overall homeostasis.
