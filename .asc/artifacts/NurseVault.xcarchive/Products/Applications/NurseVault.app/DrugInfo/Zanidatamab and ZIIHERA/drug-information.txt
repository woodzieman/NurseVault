          Adults
          Dosage Description
          20 mg/kg IV every 2 weeks until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity. Administer recommended premedications (i.e., acetaminophen, an antihistamine, and a corticosteroid) 30 to 60 minutes prior to each infusion. Treatment with zanidatamab-hrii resulted in an objective response rate of 52% (complete response, 2%) for a median duration of 14.9 months in patients with unresectable or metastatic HER2-positive biliary tract cancer in Cohort 1 (n = 62) of a multicenter, open-label, single arm clinical trial (HERIZON-BTC-01). The duration of response was at least 6 months in 19% of patients, and was at least 12 months in 14% of patients. Patients were required to have received at least 1 prior gemcitabine-containing systemic chemotherapy regimen for advanced disease and have an ejection fraction of at least 50%.

          Heading
          Adults weighing 70 kg or more
          Dosage Description
          2,400 mg IV every 3 weeks OR 1,600 mg IV every 2 weeks in combination with fluoropyrimidine- and platinum-containing chemotherapy and tislelizumab (150 mg IV every 2 weeks OR 200 mg IV every 3 weeks OR 300 mg IV every 4 weeks OR 400 mg IV every 6 weeks) until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity. Administer zanidatamab first, followed by tislelizumab, and then chemotherapy; if chemotherapy is discontinued for reasons other than disease progression, treatment with zanidatamab and/or tislelizumab can continue until disease progression. Administer recommended premedications and antidiarrheal prophylaxis. In the HERIZON-GEA-01 trial, chemotherapy consisted of either CAPOX (oxaliplatin 130 mg/m2 IV on day 1 plus capecitabine 1,000 mg/m2 PO twice daily on days 1 to 14, followed by 7 days off, every 3 weeks) or FP (cisplatin 80 mg/m2 IV on day 1 plus fluorouracil 800 mg/m2/day by continuous IV infusion on days 1 through 5 [total infusional dose 4,000 mg/m2] every 3 weeks). If zanidatamab is administered in combination with fluorouracil-containing regimens, administer fluorouracil as an IV infusion; do NOT administer fluorouracil as an IV bolus due to the potential increase in diarrhea. Treatment with zanidatamab in combination with investigator's choice of CAPOX or FP plus tislelizumab significantly improved progression-free survival (PFS) (12.4 months vs. 8.1 months) and overall survival (26.4 months vs. 19.2 months) compared with trastuzumab plus either CAPOX or FP in patients with advanced or metastatic HER2-positive GEA, including those with gastric, gastroesophageal junction, and esophageal adenocarcinoma in a multicenter, randomized, 3-arm, open-label clinical trial (HERIZON-GEA-01). The objective response rate was 67% versus 63%, respectively (complete response, 20% vs. 12%) for a median duration of 18.9 months in the zanidatamab and tislelizumab arm compared with 8.3 months in the trastuzumab arm. In a prespecified exploratory analysis, the median PFS (16.1 months vs. 7.6 months) and OS (27.3 months vs. 19.8 months) were significantly improved in the HER2 IHC 3+ zanidatamab/tislelizumab arm. Median OS was numerically improved in the HER2 IHC2+/ISH+ population (21.5 months vs. 18.7 months); median PFS was 8.6 months versus 10 months, respectively.

    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components.
    Headline
    coronary artery disease, heart failure, hypertension, thoracic radiation therapy
    General Information
    Use zanidatamab with caution in patients with a history of heart failure, or in patients with conditions that could impair left ventricular function including coronary artery disease, uncontrolled hypertension, thoracic radiation therapy and previous anthracycline exposure. Zanidatamab can cause decreases in left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF). The safety of zanidatamab has not been established in patients with a baseline LVEF below 50% (heart failure). Assess LVEF at baseline and at regular intervals during treatment. An interruption or discontinuation of therapy may be necessary depending on the severity.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Zanidatamab use is not recommended during pregnancy. Based on its mechanism of action, zanidatamab can cause fetal harm. Although there are no human or animal data on the use of zanidatamab during pregnancy, there are case reports of oligohydramnios and oligohydramnios sequence manifesting as pulmonary hypoplasia, skeletal abnormalities, and neonatal death with the use of HER2-directed antibodies. Monitor for oligohydramnios in patients who received zanidatamab during pregnancy or within 4 months prior to conception. If oligohydramnios occurs, perform fetal testing appropriate for gestational age and consistent with local standards of care. People who are pregnant or who become pregnant while receiving zanidatamab should be apprised of the potential hazard to the fetus.
    Headline
    diarrhea
    General Information
    Severe, life-threatening, and fatal cases of diarrhea can occur when zanidatamab is administered in combination with fluoropyrimidine- and platinum-containing chemotherapy, with or without tislelizumab; the risk is higher when zanidatamab is administered with chemotherapy and tislelizumab in patients 65 years or older compared to younger patients. Administer antidiarrheal prophylaxis during cycle 1 for all patients when zanidatamab is given in combination with fluoropyrimidine- and platinum-containing chemotherapy (with or without tislelizumab). Manage with antidiarrheals and supportive measures as clinically indicated. Interrupt, reduce the dose or discontinue zanidatamab based on severity.
    Headline
    embryo-fetal toxicity, people who may become pregnant, reproductive risk
    General Information
    Counsel people who may become pregnant about the reproductive risk and embryo-fetal toxicity associated with zanidatamab. Zanidatamab may cause fetal harm. Verify pregnancy status prior to treatment initiation. Advise people who may become pregnant to use effective contraception while taking zanidatamab and for 4 months after the last dose.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Use zanidatamab with caution during breast-feeding. There are no data on the presence of zanidatamab in human milk, its effects on the breast-fed child, or its effect on milk production. Data suggest human IgG is present in human milk but does not enter neonatal or infant circulation in substantial amounts. Consider the benefits of breast-feeding, the patient's clinical need for zanidatamab, and any potential adverse effects on the breast-fed child from the medication or from the patient's underlying medical condition. This consideration should also take into account the drug half-life of approximately 21 days and a washout period of 4 months.

Interactions
Cholera Vaccine: (Moderate) Patients receiving immunosuppressant medications may have a diminished response to the live cholera vaccine. When feasible, administer indicated vaccines prior to initiating immunosuppressant medications. Counsel patients receiving immunosuppressant medications about the possibility of a diminished vaccine response and to continue to follow precautions to avoid exposure to cholera bacteria after receiving the vaccine.
Dengue Tetravalent Vaccine, Live: (Contraindicated) Avoid administration of the live dengue virus vaccine with immunosuppressive drug therapy and prior to immune recovery following treatment with immunosuppressive drug therapy. When feasible, administer indicated live virus vaccines at least 4 weeks before planned immunosuppression or wait until at least 3 months after discontinuation. The time to restoration of immune competence may be longer in some patients. Patients with altered immunocompetence may be at increased risk for severe adverse reactions due to uninhibited growth of the attenuated live virus. Additionally, vaccines may be less effective if administered during a period of altered immunocompetence.
SARS-CoV-2 (COVID-19) vaccines: (Moderate) Patients receiving immunosuppressant medications may have a diminished response to the SARS-CoV-2 virus vaccine. When feasible, administer indicated vaccines prior to initiating immunosuppressant medications. Counsel patients receiving immunosuppressant medications about the possibility of a diminished vaccine response and to continue to follow precautions to avoid exposure to SARS-CoV-2 virus after receiving the vaccine.
SARS-CoV-2 Virus (COVID-19) mRNA Vaccine: (Moderate) Patients receiving immunosuppressant medications may have a diminished response to the SARS-CoV-2 virus vaccine. When feasible, administer indicated vaccines prior to initiating immunosuppressant medications. Counsel patients receiving immunosuppressant medications about the possibility of a diminished vaccine response and to continue to follow precautions to avoid exposure to SARS-CoV-2 virus after receiving the vaccine.
SARS-CoV-2 Virus (COVID-19) Recombinant Spike Protein Nanoparticle Vaccine: (Moderate) Patients receiving immunosuppressant medications may have a diminished response to the SARS-CoV-2 virus vaccine. Adverse Reactions

    Headline
    left ventricular dysfunction
    General Information
    Zanidatamab can cause left ventricular dysfunction. A decrease in left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) to less than 50% and by greater than 10% was reported in 4.3% to 9% of patients who received zanidatamab as monotherapy or in combination with fluoropyrimidine- and platinum-based chemotherapy (with or without tislelizumab); grade 3 or 4 decreases in LVEF occurred in 2.3% to 3.1% of patients who received zanidatamab plus chemotherapy. Left ventricular dysfunction resolved in 70% of patients who received zanidatamab monotherapy. The median time to first occurrence of LV dysfunction was 5.6 to 6.9 months. An interruption or discontinuation of therapy may be necessary depending on the severity of LV dysfunction.

    Headline
    infusion-related reactions
    General Information
    Infusion-related reactions occurred in 22% to 35% (grade 3 or 4, 0.4% to 2.3%) of patients treated with zanidatamab in clinical trials, despite the use of premedication. Infusion-related reactions occurred on the first day of dosing in 17% to 28% of patients; most (60% to 97%) reactions resolved within 1 day. Administer premedications including acetaminophen, an antihistamine (e.g., diphenhydramine), and a corticosteroid (e.g., hydrocortisone) prior to each dose of zanidatamab to reduce this risk. Monitor patients during administration and as clinically indicated after completion of the infusion; have medications and emergency equipment available for immediate use if necessary. A decreased infusion rate, interruption of the infusion, or discontinuation of therapy may be necessary depending on the severity of the reaction.

    Headline
    abdominal pain, colitis, diarrhea, enterocolitis, GI obstruction
    General Information
    Diarrhea was reported in 48% to 50% of patients treated with zanidatamab monotherapy in 2 clinical trials (grade 3 or 4, 6% to 10%), including reports of enteritis; an interruption or discontinuation of therapy may be necessary depending on the severity. Advise patients to increase oral fluids, begin loperamide, and notify their healthcare provider immediately if diarrhea occurs. Diarrhea (including colitis, enteritis, enterocolitis, and hemorrhagic enterocolitis) was reported in 81% to 85% of patients treated with zanidatamab in combination with fluoropyrimidine- and platinum-containing chemotherapy, with or without tislelizumab (grade 3, 20% to 24%; grade 4, 1.3% to 2.1%; grade 5, 1.5% or less). In the first cycle of zanidatamab with fluoropyrimidine- and platinum-containing chemotherapy (with or without tislelizumab), grade 3 diarrhea occurred in 14% to 15% and grade 4 in 0.8% to 1.5% of patients despite the use of loperamide prophylaxis; the median time to onset of diarrhea in cycle 1 was 6 days and the median time to resolution was 18 days. The risk of diarrhea when zanidatamab is administered with fluoropyrimidine- and platinum-containing chemotherapy (with or without tislelizumab) is higher in patients aged 65 and older compared to younger patients (32% vs. 20%). Abdominal pain (29%; grade 3 or 4, 1%) and severe cases of GI obstruction (3.8%) have also been reported in patients with unresectable or metastatic biliary tract cancer in a single-arm clinical trial.

Decreases from baseline in sodium were reported in 35% of patients who received zanidatamab monotherapy (grade 3 or 4, 10%) and decreases in potassium occurred in 34% of patients (grade 3 or 4, 5%) in 1 cohort of a multicenter, open-label, single-arm clinical trial; the incidence of decreased potassium (56% to 61%; grade 3 or 4, 23% to 27%) and decreased sodium (35% to 43%; grade 3 or 4, 2% to 2.3%) were increased in patients who received zanidatamab in combination with CAPOX (capecitabine and oxaliplatin) or FP (fluorouracil and cisplatin), with or without tislelizumab, in another clinical trial. Additionally, an increase in lactate dehydrogenase was reported in 55% of patients who received zanidatamab monotherapy. Hypocalcemia and hypomagnesemia were also reported in patients treated with zanidatamab in combination with CAPOX or FP, with or without tislelizumab, including decreases from baseline in calcium levels (52% to 62%; grade 3 or 4, 1.6% to 1.7%) and magnesium levels (42% to 43%; grade 3 or 4, 0.3% to 3%).

Description
  Text
  Zanidatamab is a bispecific HER2-directed antibody. It is FDA-approved as monotherapy for previously treated HER2-positive unresectable or metastatic biliary tract cancer (BTC). It is also indicated in combination with fluoropyrimidine- and platinum-containing chemotherapy (with or without tislelizumab) for the treatment of HER2-positive unresectable locally advanced or metastatic gastroesophageal adenocarcinoma. It carries a black box warning regarding exposure during pregnancy and fetal risk; females of reproductive potential should use effective contraception and avoid pregnancy for 4 months after the last dose. It also carries a black box warning for diarrhea when used in combination with fluoropyrimidine- and platinum-containing chemotherapy; antidiarrheal prophylaxis is required in cycle 1 of combination therapy. Zanidatamab was approved by the FDA in November 2024.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Zanidatamab is a humanized, IgG1-like, bispecific HER2-directied antibody that binds to extracellular domains 2 (ECD2) and 4 (ECD4) on HER2. In vitro, this causes clustering and results in internalization, leading to a reduction of the receptor on the tumor cell surface. Zanidatamab inhibits tumor growth and causes cell death in vitro and in vivo by inducing complement-dependent cytotoxicity, antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC), and antibody-dependent cellular phagocytosis (ADCP).

    Description
    The Cmax of zanidatamab at steady-state in patients with gastroesophageal adenocarcinoma is 783 mcg/mL (CV, 20%) at a dose of 1,800 mg (less than 70kg) or 2,400 mg (70 kg or more) every 3 weeks and 609 mcg/mL (CV, 21%) at a dose of 1,200 mg/1,600 mg every 2 weeks; the Cmax in patients with biliary tract cancer who received a dose of 20 mg/kg every 2 weeks was 624 mcg/mL (CV, 26%). The Cmax of zanidatamab is dose-proportional and the AUC is greater than dose-proportional. Steady-state was approached after approximately 16 weeks. The average concentration at steady-state was 319 mcg/mL (CV, 27% to 36%) and the Cmin was 176 mcg/mL to 203 mcg/mL (CV, 36% to 52%) across dosage regimens. The median Ctrough accumulation of zanidatamab is 1.8-fold.

Emetic Potential
Low
Administer routine antiemetic prophylaxis prior to treatment.
Premedications
Administer acetaminophen, an antihistamine (e.g., diphenhydramine), and a corticosteroid (e.g., hydrocortisone) 30 to 60 minutes prior to each infusion to reduce the risk of infusion reactions.
When given in combination with fluoropyrimidine-containing chemotherapy: Administer loperamide 4 mg PO twice daily beginning on day 1 of cycle 1; continue loperamide for at least 7 days during the first cycle. If grade 1 or higher diarrhea occurs during the first cycle, administer loperamide prophylaxis for at least the first 7 days of each subsequent cycle.

      Zanidatamab is for intravenous administration only; do not administer as an intravenous push or bolus.

Reconstitution
Allow vial(s) to come to room temperature.
Compatible Closed System Transfer devices: Acrylonitrile-butadiene-styrene (ABS), acrylic copolymer, polycarbonate (PC), polyisoprene (PI), polyester, polypropylene (PP) polytetrafluoroethylene (PTFE), silicone, and stainless steel (SS).
Reconstitute each 300 mg vial with 5.7 mL of Sterile Water for Injection by slowly directing the stream toward the inside wall of the vial. The final concentration will be 50 mg/mL in an extractable volume of 6 mL.
Swirl the vial gently until completely dissolved. Do not shake or vigorously swirl. Allow the vial to settle and bubbles to dissipate.
Storage after reconstitution: Reconstituted solution may be stored for up to 4 hours at room temperature (18 to 24 degrees C or 64 to 75 degrees F) or refrigerated (2 to 8 degrees C or 36 to 46 degrees F).
 
Dilution
Add the appropriate volume of zanidatamab to a volume of 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection or 5% Dextrose Injection necessary to obtain a final concentration of 4 to 20 mg/mL. Use the least possible volume of diluent necessary, not to exceed 500 mL.
Gently invert the bag to mix; do not shake.
Storage after dilution: The diluted solution may be stored for up to 12 hours at room temperature (18 to 24 degrees C or 64 to 75 degrees F) or for up to 24 hours refrigerated (2 to 8 degrees C or 36 to 46 degrees F) including the beginning of reconstitution through the end of the infusion.
