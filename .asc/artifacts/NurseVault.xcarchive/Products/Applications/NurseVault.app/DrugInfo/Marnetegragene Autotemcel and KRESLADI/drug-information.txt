        Dosage Description
        2.8 x 10^6 CD34+ cells/kg IV as a single dose is the minimum recommended dose. The dose is calculated based on the patient's weight prior to first apheresis. In clinical trials, 4.3 x 10^6 CD34+ cells/kg IV was the median dose and 10 x 10^6 CD34+ cells/kg IV was the maximum dose.

    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components.
    Headline
    infection
    General Information
    Avoid the use of marnetegragene autotemcel in individuals with an active blood stream infection or another serious, untreated infection. Myeloablative conditioning prior to infusion may increase susceptibility to infection. Monitor patients for signs and symptoms of infection before and after marnetegragene autotemcel infusion and treat appropriately. Administer prophylactic antimicrobials according to institutional guidelines. Marnetegragene autotemcel has not been studied in individuals with human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) infection (i.e., HIV-1, HIV-2) or human T-lymphotrophic virus infection (i.e., HTLV-1, HTLV-2). A negative serology test for HIV is necessary to assure acceptance of apheresis material for marnetegragene autotemcel manufacturing. Apheresis material from individuals with a positive test for HIV will not be accepted for marnetegragene autotemcel manufacturing.
    Headline
    blood, organ, tissue, or cell donor
    General Information
    Advise individuals to avoid becoming a blood, organ, tissue, or cell donor after receiving treatment with marnetegragene autotemcel. Irradiate any blood products required within the first 3 months after marnetegragene autotemcel infusion.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Avoid administering marnetegragene autotemcel during pregnancy. Treatment requires myeloablative conditioning, which is associated with known risks. There are no data on the use of marnetegragene autotemcel during human pregnancy. Animal reproductive and developmental toxicity studies and nonclinical germline transmission studies have not been conducted.
    Headline
    people who can cause pregnancy in others, people who may become pregnant, reproductive risk
    General Information
    Counsel people who may become pregnant and people who can cause pregnancy in others about the reproductive risk associated with marnetegragene autotemcel. Verify negative pregnancy status prior to the start of mobilization and re-confirm prior to conditioning procedures and before administration of marnetegragene autotemcel. Insufficient data are available to recommend a duration of contraception following treatment. Follow the contraception recommendations provided for the mobilization and conditioning agents. There are no data on the effect of marnetegragene autotemcel on fertility; myeloablative conditioning may increase the risk of infertility in all people of reproductive potential. Inform individuals of options for fertility preservation prior to treatment, if appropriate.

Consider the benefits of breast-feeding, the individual's clinical need for marnetegragene autotemcel, and any potential adverse effects on the breastfed child from the medication or from the patient's underlying medical condition.     Headline
    General Information
    General Information
    Safety data are derived from 9 pediatric subjects treated in a clinical trial; adverse reactions were evaluated from initiation of myeloablative conditioning through 2 years after infusion.
    Headline
    candidiasis, infection
    General Information
    Serious infections were reported in 44% of pediatric patients receiving marnetegragene autotemcel in clinical trials. Infections reported in clinical trials included upper respiratory tract infection (89%, grade 3 or higher 56%), viral infection (78%, grade 3 or higher 11%), skin infection (33%), lower respiratory tract infection (22%, grade 3 or higher 11%), gastroenteritis (22%, grade 3 or higher 11%), skin candidiasis (22%), and urinary tract infection (22%). Device-related infections (33%, grade 3 or higher 22%), including vascular device infection, device related bacteremia, and bacteremia that occurred with a central line in place, were also reported. Myeloablative conditioning prior to infusion may increase susceptibility to infection. Monitor for signs and symptoms of infection before and during infusion and treat appropriately.

    elevated hepatic enzymes, veno-occlusive disease
    General Information
    Veno-occlusive disease was reported in 1 pediatric patient (11%) receiving marnetegragene autotemcel in clinical trials. Myeloablative conditioning prior to infusion may increase susceptibility to veno-occlusive disease. Monitor for signs and symptoms of veno-occlusive disease, including liver function tests, during the first month following treatment. Elevated hepatic enzymes, including increased aspartate aminotransferase (44%) and increased alanine aminotransferase (33%) were reported in pediatric patients receiving marnetegragene autotemcel in clinical trials.

    Headline
    anemia, leukopenia, neutropenia, thrombocytopenia
    General Information
    Hematologic abnormalities (grade 3 or higher) reported in pediatric patients receiving marnetegragene autotemcel in a clinical trial included decreased hemoglobin or anemia (100%), decreased platelet count or thrombocytopenia (100%), decreased neutrophil count or neutropenia (100%), febrile neutropenia (67%), and decreased leukocyte count or leukopenia (56%). Neutrophil engraftment failure, defined as failure to achieve 3 consecutive absolute neutrophil counts of 500 cells/microliter or more on different days by 43 days after infusion, may occur with marnetegragene autotemcel. Monitor neutrophil counts until engraftment is achieved; if engraftment failure occurs, administer rescue treatment with the back-up collection of CD34+ cells. Delayed platelet engraftment may also occur; monitor for thrombocytopenia and bleeding until platelet engraftment and recovery are achieved.

    new primary hematologic malignancy
    General Information
    Lentiviral vector (LVV)-mediated insertional ongogenesis is a potential risk after treatment with marnetegragene autotemcel. New primary hematologic malignancy may develop after treatment with marnetegragene autotemcel. Monitor for hematologic malignancies with a complete blood count with differential at least annually and integration site analysis as indicated for at least 15 years after treatment. If a malignancy occurs, contact the manufacturer at 1-800-982-2410 to report and obtain instructions on collection of samples for testing.

    laboratory test interference
    General Information
    Marnetegragene autotemcel may cause laboratory test interference. Individuals who have received marnetegragene autotemcel may test positive on polymerase chain reaction (PCR)-based assays for human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) due to the presence of integrated BB305 lentiviral vector (LVV) proviral DNA, potentially resulting in false-positive HIV PCR test results. Therefore, individuals who have received marnetegragene autotemcel should not be screened for HIV infection using PCR-based assays.

Description
  Text
  Marnetegragene autotemcel is an autologous hematopoietic stem cell-based gene therapy indicated for the treatment of severe leukocyte adhesion deficiency type 1 due to biallelic variants in ITGB2 in pediatric patients without an available human leukocyte antigen (HLA)-matched sibling donor for allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplant. Leukocyte adhesion deficiency type 1 is a rare, serious disorder of leukocyte adhesion and migration characterized by recurrent serious infections, umbilical cord complications, granulocytosis, and high mortality in early childhood. Marnetegragene autotemcel is prepared from the patient's hematopoietic stem cells collected via apheresis, with enrichment for CD34+ cells followed by ex vivo transduction with a lentiviral vector encoding the CD18 beta-subunit of human beta-2 integrins. After infusion, the transduced cells engraft in the bone marrow and differentiate into leukocytes capable of expressing functional CD18 protein. In an open-label, single-arm, multicenter study of 9 pediatric patients with severe leukocyte adhesion deficiency type 1, neutrophil CD18 and CD11a surface expression increased following marnetegragene autotemcel administration and was sustained through 24 months post-infusion. Serious infections, veno-occlusive disease, neutrophil engraftment failure, delayed platelet engraftment, and hypersensitivity reactions have been reported with marnetegragene autotemcel, and there is a potential risk for lentiviral vector-mediated insertional oncogenesis requiring long-term monitoring. Marnetegragene autotemcel was FDA-approved in March 2026.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Marnetegragene autotemcel is an autologous hematopoietic stem cell (HSC)-based gene therapy that adds functional copies of the ITGB2 gene into an individual's HSCs through ex vivo transduction of CD34+ cells with the LV-RP-201 lentiviral vector. Following infusion, transduced CD34+ HSCs engraft in the bone marrow and differentiate into multiple hematopoietic lineages, including leukocytes capable of expressing functional CD18 protein. CD18 pairs with CD11a to form leukocyte function-associated antigen-1 (LFA-1), thereby restoring leukocyte adhesion to endothelial surfaces and enabling migration to sites of infection and inflammation.

Pharmacokinetics
  Text
  Marnetegragene autotemcel is administered intravenously. Conventional pharmacokinetic parameters of absorption, distribution, metabolism, and elimination are not applicable to marnetegragene autotemcel because this medication is an autologous hematopoietic stem cell-based gene therapy composed of hematopoietic stem cells that have been genetically modified ex vivo.
 
Pharmacodynamics
Following intravenous infusion of marnetegragene autotemcel in a clinical trial (n = 9), neutrophil CD18 and CD11a surface expression increased and remained sustained for at least 42 months. Among 7 evaluable participants with baseline CD18 expression less than 2%, median CD18 expression after 12 and 24 months was 54% and 50%, respectively. Median CD11a expression after 12 and 24 months was 45% and 39%, respectively, in all 9 treated participants.
 
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes and drug transporters: none
