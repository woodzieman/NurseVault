Drug Class: Teprotumumab is an insulin-like growth factor 1 receptor (IGF-1R) inhibitor approved for the treatment of thyroid eye disease regardless of thyroid eye disease activity or duration.

Routes: injection (IV/IM/SC); Common adult doses: 20 mg/kg/dose via IV infusion.

Common Side Effects (excluding routine GI upset): alopecia, asthenia, dysgeusia, fatigue, headache, muscle cramps, otalgia, xerosis, inflammatory bowel disease, weight loss, dyspnea, hypertension, infusion-related reactions, musculoskeletal pain, sinus tachycardia, amenorrhea, dysmenorrhea, menstrual irregularity

In an Overdose: No overdose/antidote information is listed in this file.

    General Information
    Because teprotumumab may aggravate hyperglycemia, ensure patients with hyperglycemia or pre-existing diabetes mellitus are under appropriate glycemic control before and while receiving teprotumumab therapy. Monitor for elevated blood glucose and symptoms of hyperglycemia during treatment.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Teprotumumab may cause fetal harm during pregnancy as supported by animal reproductive study data and the drug's mechanism of action. During treatment, advise treated patients to contact their care team if pregnancy is suspected or confirmed. Discontinue treatment if pregnancy is confirmed and advise of the potential risk to the fetus. In an abridged animal embryofetal development study, adverse pregnancy outcomes were observed following once weekly administration of teprotumumab intravenously to pregnant cynomolgus monkeys (n = 7) from gestation day 20 through the end of gestation. Adverse effects included an increased incidence of abortion, decreased fetal growth during pregnancy, decreased fetal size and weight at cesarean section, decreased placental weight and size, and decreased amniotic fluid volume. Additionally, multiple external and skeletal abnormalities were observed in each exposed monkey fetus, including misshapen cranium, closely set eyes, micrognathia, pointing and narrowing of the nose, and ossification abnormalities of the skull, sternebrae, carpals, tarsals, and teeth.
    Headline
    people who may become pregnant, reproductive risk
    General Information
    Counsel people who may become pregnant about the reproductive risk associated with teprotumumab. Teprotumumab may cause fetal harm. Before starting treatment, people who may become pregnant should have a pregnancy test performed by their care team to confirm they are not pregnant. Advise people who may become pregnant to use effective contraception prior to treatment initiation, during treatment, and for 6 months after the last dose.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Use teprotumumab with caution during breast-feeding. There are no data regarding the presence of teprotumumab in human milk, or the drug's effect on a breastfed child or milk production. Consider the benefits of breast-feeding, the risk of potential infant drug exposure, and the risk of an untreated or inadequately treated maternal condition.

    General Information
    An infusion-related reaction was reported in approximately 4% of patients treated with teprotumumab during clinical trials. Signs and symptoms of infusion-related reactions include transient hypertension, feeling hot, sinus tachycardia, dyspnea, headache, and muscle or musculoskeletal pain. These reactions may occur during any of the infusions or within 1.5 hours after an infusion. Description
  Text
  Teprotumumab is an insulin-like growth factor 1 receptor (IGF-1R) inhibitor approved for the treatment of thyroid eye disease regardless of thyroid eye disease activity or duration. It is given via intravenous infusion every 3 weeks for a total of 8 doses. Teprotumumab may cause infusion-related reactions, hyperglycemia, and exacerbation of inflammatory bowel disease. In utero exposure to teprotumumab may cause fetal harm; therefore, use of the drug during pregnancy should be avoided. Instruct women of child-bearing potential to use effective contraception prior to, during, and for 6 months after treatment with teprotumumab.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Teprotumumab is a human monoclonal antibody that binds to and acts as an inhibitor of the insulin-like growth factor 1 receptor (IGF-1R); it is approved for the treatment of thyroid eye disease (TED). The pathogenesis of TED is as follows: autoantibodies [i.e., Graves' disease immunoglobulins (GD-IgG) and thyroid-stimulating immunoglobulins (TSI)] stimulate orbital fibroblasts causing an upregulation of the IGF-1R/thyroid-stimulating hormone receptor (TSH-R) signaling complex. Activation of the IGF-1R/TSH-R complex leads to the production of glycosaminoglycans (e.g., hyaluronan) and an increase in proinflammatory cytokines IL-6 and IL-8. This ultimately causes progressive inflammation and expansion of retrobulbar fat and muscle tissue. Teprotumumab prevents activation of the IGF-1R/TSH-R complex by blocking the stimulatory effects of the autoantibodies (GD-IgG and TSI) on IGF-1R. As a result, teprotumumab reduces inflammation and exessive expansion of fat and muscle in the orbital tissue, thereby decreasing the symptoms associated with TED (e.g., proptosis and diplopia).

  Route Specific Administrations
    Administration Details
      Name
      Intravenous Administration
      Description
      Reconstitution and Dilution
Calculate the dose (mg) and determine the number of 500 mg vials needed to produce the 10 or 20 mg/kg dose based on patient weight.
Using aseptic technique, reconstitute each vial with 10 mL of Sterile Water for Injection, USP. When adding the Sterile Water of Injection to the vial, ensure the stream of diluent is not applied directly onto the lyophilized powder. Do not shake the vial; instead, dissolve the lyophilized powder by gently swirling the solution. The reconstituted solution has a volume of 10.5 mL and a final concentration of 47.6 mg/mL.
Further dilute the reconstituted solution with 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection.
To prepare the diluted solution, use 100 mL infusion bags for a dose less than 1,800 mg, and 250 mL infusion bags for a dose of 1,800 mg or greater.
To maintain a constant volume in the infusion bag, a sterile syringe and needle should be used to remove the volume of 0.9% Sodium Chloride that is equivalent to the amount of reconstituted teprotumumab solution to be placed into the infusion bag. Discard the 0.9% Sodium Chloride that has been withdrawn.
Withdraw the required volume of reconstituted teprotumumab solution from the vial(s) and transfer into the intravenous bag containing 0.9% Sodium Chloride. Mix the diluted solution by gentle inversion; do not shake.
Discard any reconstituted solution that remains in the vial.
No incompatibilities have been observed between teprotumumab and polyethylene (PE), polyvinyl chloride (PVC), polyurethane (PUR), or polyolefin (PO) bags and intravenous administration sets.
Storage: The combined storage time of reconstituted solution in the vial and the diluted solution in the infusion bag is 4 hours at room temperature of 20 to 25 degrees C (68 to 77 degrees F) or up to 48 hours under refrigeration at 2 to 8 degrees C (36 to 46 degrees F). Protect from light. Do not freeze.
 
Intermittent IV Infusion
Administer the diluted solution intravenously over at least 90 minutes for the first two infusions. If well tolerated, the minimum infusion time for subsequent doses can be reduced to 60 minutes. If not well tolerated, the minimum infusion time for subsequent doses should remain at 90 minutes, and pre-medication is recommended for subsequent infusions.
DO NOT administer as an IV push or bolus.
Do not mix or infuse concomitantly with other medications.
Monitor for infusion-related reactions during administration. In most cases, an infusion-related reaction can be successfully managed with corticosteroids and antihistamines. Consider giving pre-medication to patients who experience an infusion-related reaction ; antihistamines, antipyretics, corticosteroids, may be used. Also, subsequent infusions can be administered at a slower rate.
