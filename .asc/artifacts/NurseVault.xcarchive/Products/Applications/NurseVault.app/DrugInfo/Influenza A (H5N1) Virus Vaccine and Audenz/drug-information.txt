    Treatment-Related Restrictions of Use
    General Information
    The decision to administer or to delay vaccination with the influenza A (H5N1) virus vaccine because of current or recent febrile illness depends on the severity of symptoms and on the etiology of the disease. Consider delaying vaccinations during the course of a moderate or severe acute illness with or without fever and administer after the acute phase of illness has resolved. All vaccines can be administered to people with mild illnesses. Vaccinate people with moderate or severe febrile illness as soon as they have recovered from the acute phase of the illness.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Data are insufficient to advise a vaccine-associated risk of influenza A (H5N1) virus vaccine use during pregnancy. No adequate and well-controlled studies have been conducted during human pregnancy. A developmental toxicity study performed in rabbits administered 0.5 mL of influenza A (H5N1) virus vaccine prior to mating and during gestation revealed no evidence of harm to the fetus or offspring. There is limited information on the risk of influenza A (H5N1) infection in pregnant individuals. However, pregnant people infected with pandemic H1N1 or with seasonal influenza are at increased risk of severe illness associated with influenza infection compared to non-pregnant individuals. Pregnant people with influenza may be at increased risk for adverse pregnancy outcomes, including preterm labor and delivery.
    Headline
    history of significant adverse event(s) associated with previous use
    General Information
    The influenza A (H5N1) virus vaccine should be used with caution in patients who have experienced a history of significant adverse event(s) associated with previous use. Carefully consider the potential benefits and risks of the influenza A (H5N1) virus vaccine for individuals who have developed acute inflammatory demyelinating polyneuropathy (Guillain-Barre syndrome) within 6 weeks of a previous influenza vaccination. Influenza immunization is generally not recommended in individuals who have developed acute inflammatory demyelinating polyneuropathy within 6 weeks of a previous influenza vaccination. However, for most people with a history of acute inflammatory demyelinating polyneuropathy who are at high risk for severe complications from influenza, many experts believe the benefits of the influenza virus vaccine justify vaccination.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    There are no data on the presence of influenza A (H5N1) virus vaccine in human milk, its effects on the breast-fed child, or its effects on milk production. Consider the developmental and health benefits of breast-feeding along with the clinical need for influenza A (H5N1) virus vaccine and any potential adverse effects on the breastfed child. According to the Advisory Committee on Immunization Practices (ACIP), inactivated vaccines pose no risk to breast-feeding individuals or their children.
    Headline
    immunosuppression
    General Information
    Immunocompromised individuals, including individuals with immunosuppression or receiving immunosuppressive therapy, may not have an adequate immune response to the influenza A (H5N1) virus vaccine.

Description
  Text
  Influenza A (H5N1) virus vaccine is an inactivated vaccine indicated for active immunization for the prevention of disease caused by influenza A virus H5N1. Since 2003, H5N1 viruses have disseminated in large waves worldwide, increasing the number of outbreaks affecting birds. It was reported that more than 400 million domestic poultry died because of H5N1 from 2003 to March 2012. Non-human mammals have also been infected; however, human infections were initially sporadic and restricted to bird contacts. In 1997, the virus jumped to humans, with 18 total cases and 6 fatal cases reported in Hong Kong. Outbreaks occurred in China and across Asia in 2003, and since then, it has evolved and spread throughout several continents. From 2003 to June 2024, 892 human cases of influenza A (H5N1) infections with 463 deaths were reported across 24 countries. There is no evidence of sustained human-to-human H5N1 virus transmission. Illness in individuals infected with the virus range from mild (e.g., upper respiratory symptoms) to severe illness (e.g., pneumonia, multi-organ failure) resulting in death. Due to the high number of deaths and the severity of the disease, this virus would be of great concern in the event that it becomes pandemic. The influenza A (H5N1) virus vaccine was FDA-approved in 2020 and, although it is not commercially available, will be used at the discretion of the government during prepandemic or pandemic situations.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Many viruses bind to hemagglutinins, molecules at the surface of red blood cells (RBC) of various animal species, causing RBC aggregation in suitable conditions. The hemagglutination inhibition test is used most notably with influenza viruses. A specific post-vaccination hemagglutination-inhibition (HI) antibody titer has not been correlated with protection from H5N1 influenza illness; however, HI titers have been used as a measure of influenza vaccine activity. In some human challenge studies with other influenza virus strains, antibody titers of greater than or equal to 1:40 have been associated with protection from influenza illness in up to 50% of subjects.

    The proportion of subjects with seroconversion or a hemagglutination-inhibiting (HI) titer greater than or equal to 1:40 after vaccination was assessed by age group (18 to 64 years and 65 years and older) after the second dose. Administration
  General Information
  For storage information, see the specific product information within the How Supplied section.
The health care professional should have immediate availability of epinephrine injection and other agents used in the treatment of anaphylaxis in the event of a serious allergic reaction.
Inform the parent, guardian, or responsible adult of the benefits and risks of the vaccine. Provide the Vaccine Information Statements from the manufacturer to the parent or guardian before each immunization. This action is required by the National Childhood Vaccine Injury Act of 1986.
Record the manufacturer, lot number, administration date, and the name and address of the person administering the vaccine in the patient's permanent record. These actions are required by the National Childhood Vaccine Injury Act of 1986.
Complete a Vaccine Adverse Event Reporting System (VAERS) report form if an adverse event occurs. The reporting of events is required by the National Childhood Vaccine Injury Act of 1986. The toll-free number for VAERS is 1-800-822-7967. Depending on the adverse reaction, subsequent vaccination, if needed, may be contraindicated.

    Administration Details
      Name
      Intramuscular Administration
      Description
      Shake syringe gently before use and shake the multi-dose vial gently each time before withdrawing a vaccine dose.
A maximum of 10 doses can be withdrawn from the multi-dose vial.
Return the multi-dose vial to the recommended storage conditions of 2 to 8 degrees C (36 to 46 degrees F) between uses.
Inject into the anterolateral aspect of the mid-thigh (for pediatric patients aged 6 to 11 months) or the deltoid muscle of the upper arm (for patients aged 12 months and older). Do not administer in the gluteal muscle or areas where there may be a major nerve trunk.
    Name
    Injectable Administration
    Description
    Administer intramuscularly. Do not administer intravenously, intradermally, or subcutaneously.
Visually inspect parenteral products for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration whenever solution and container permit. The vaccine has a milky-white appearance.
Do not mix with any other vaccine.
When concomitant administration of other vaccines is required, administer with different syringes and at different injection sites.
