Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  General Information
  No formal studies have been performed to assess the impact of renal impairment on the pharmacokinetics of bendamustine. In vitro data indicate that 90% of drug is recovered unchanged, primarily in the feces. Use bendamustine with caution in patients with mild or moderate renal impairment, and do not use it in patients with creatinine clearance 3 xULN) hepatic impairment.Bendamustine may cause fetal harm if administered to a woman during pregnancy. Single intraperitoneal doses of bendamustine produced malformations and embryo lethality in animal studies. There are no adequate and well-controlled studies in pregnant women. If bendamustine is used during pregnancy, or if the patient becomes pregnant during treatment, the patient should be informed of the potential hazard to the fetus.It is not known if bendamustine is excreted into breast milk. Due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in nursing infants and due to the tumorigenicity displayed by bendamustine in animal studies, a decision should be made whether to discontinue breast-feeding or to discontinue the drug, taking into account the importance of the drug to the mother.Do not administer to children. The safety and efficacy of bendamustine have not been established in this population.Patients with a known hypersensitivity to bendamustine should not receive this medication. In addition, bendamustine vials contain mannitol. Patients with mannitol hypersensitivity should not receive bendamustine.Patients may develop infusion-related reactions to bendamustine. Symptoms typically include fever, chills, pruritus, and rash, but may include severe anaphylaxis (see Adverse Reactions). Discontinue the drug in patients who have severe reactions to bendamustine. In the CLL trial, patients who developed Grade 3 or 4 infusion reactions were not typically rechallenged. Antihistamine, antipyretic, and corticosteroid premedications should be considered prior to subsequent cycles of bendamustine in patients who have previously experienced a Grade 1 or 2 infusion-related reaction.Hyperuricemia and tumor-lysis syndrome (TLS) may develop during bendamustine therapy (see Adverse Reactions). Onset is typically with the first treatment cycle. Preventive measures including maintaining adequate volume status, routinely monitoring blood chemistries, and administration of allopurinol during the first one to two weeks of therapy should be considered in patients at high risk for developing TLS.

    Headline
    renal failure, renal impairment
    General Information
    Do not use bendamustine in patients with severe renal impairment including renal failure (CrCl less than 30 mL/minute).
    Headline
    Child-Pugh class A, Child-Pugh class B, Child-Pugh class C, hepatic failure
    General Information
    Use bendamustine with caution in patients with hepatic impairment (e.g., Child-Pugh class A, Child-Pugh class B, Child-Pugh class C). Fatal and serious hepatic impairment has been reported with bendamustine use; most cases occurred within the first 3 months of starting therapy. Do not use bendamustine in patients with AST or ALT level of 2.5 to 10 times the ULN and total bilirubin level of 1.5 to 3 times the ULN OR total bilirubin more than 3 times the ULN, including hepatic failure. Patients with progressive disease, receiving combination therapy, or hepatitis B virus reactivation may be at increased risk of developing hepatic dysfunction. Monitor liver function tests prior to and during bendamustine therapy.
    Headline
    infection
    General Information
    Prior to starting bendamustine therapy, treat active infections and/or administer appropriate prophylactic treatment in patients who have a history of infection; reactivation of infections may occur. Serious and fatal infection (i.e., pneumonia, sepsis, septic shock, hepatitis) has been reported with bendamustine therapy. Patients who experience myelosuppression are at increased risk for developing an infection. Monitor patients for signs and symptoms of infection prior to and during therapy.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Bendamustine may cause fetal harm when administered during pregnancy, based on animal data. Females of reproductive potential should avoid becoming pregnant during bendamustine therapy. Advise pregnant people of the potential fetal risk. In animal studies, skeletal and visceral malformations (e.g., exencephaly and cleft palates, accessory rib, and spinal deformities), hydronephrosis, hydrocephalus, and decreased fetal body weights were reported when intraperitoneal bendamustine was given to pregnant mice and rats at doses that were 0.6 to 1.8 times the maximum recommended human dose.
    Headline
    people who can cause pregnancy in others, people who may become pregnant, reproductive risk
    General Information
    Counsel people who may become pregnant and people who can cause pregnancy in others about the reproductive risk associated with bendamustine. Bendamustine can cause fetal harm. Verify pregnancy status prior to treatment initiation. Advise people who may become pregnant to use effective contraception while taking bendamustine and for at least 6 months after the last dose. For those that can cause pregnancy in others, contraception should be used during bendamustine treatment and for at least 3 months after the last dose. Based on animal data, bendamustine may cause male infertility. Impaired spermatogenesis, azoospermia, and total germinal aplasia have been reported in individuals who received other alkylating agents, particularly in combination with other chemotherapy drugs. Spermatogenesis may return to some individuals in remission, but this may occur only several years after intensive chemotherapy has been discontinued.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Advise individuals to avoid breast-feeding while taking bendamustine and for 1 week after the last dose, due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in the breast-fed child. There are no data on the presence of bendamustine or its metabolites in human milk, their effects on the breast-fed child, or their effects on milk production.

Acyclovir: (Major) Consider the use of an alternative therapy if acyclovir treatment is needed in patients receiving bendamustine. Concomitant use of acyclovir may increase bendamustine exposure, which may increase the risk of adverse reactions (e.g., myelosuppression, infection, hepatotoxicity). Bendamustine is a CYP1A2 substrate and acyclovir is a CYP1A2 inhibitor.

Adverse Reactions
  Headline
  Adverse Reactions
  General Information
  Hematologic toxicities were the most frequently observed adverse events in clinical trials of bendamustine. In a clinical trial of 153 treatment naive chronic lymphocytic leukemia (CLL) patients, leukopenia (all grades: 18%, grades 3 or 4: 15%), neutropenia (28%, 24%), lymphopenia (7%, 7%), anemia (19%, 3%), and thrombocytopenia (23%, 13%) were observed. Hematologic toxicity rates were more pronounced in patients in trials of non-Hodgkin's lymphoma (NHL) receiving bendamustine. Grade 3 or 4 myelosuppression occurred in 98% of patients. Three patients (2%) died from myelosuppression-related adverse events including neutropenic sepsis, diffuse alveolar hemmorhage, and pneumonia from an opportunistic infection with CMV.
 
Infection, including pneumonia and sepsis, has been reported in patients receiving bendamustine. Infection has been associated with septic shock and death. In a phase III trial of bendamustine/rituximab as front-line treatment for indolent and mantle-cell lymphoma, 19/139 patients experienced infectious complications.
 
Infusion reactions, including anaphylactoid reactions, have occurred during therapy with bendamustine, particularly in the second and subsequent courses of therapy. Fever, chills, pruritus, and rash have been reported. Rarely severe, or anaphylactoid reactions have occurred. Patients should be questioned about the development of allergic symptoms after their first course of therapy. For patients who develop grade 1 or 2 reactions, premedication with antihistamines, corticosteroids, and antipyretics should be considered prior to subsequent treatments. Discontinuation of therapy should be considered for patients who develop grade 3 or 4 reactions. Patients in the CLL trial were not typically rechallenged after presentation of a grade 3 or 4 infusion reaction.
 
Gastrointestinal adverse reactions reported in clinical trials include nausea/vomiting and diarrhea. In a trial of CLL patients receiving bendamustine monotherapy, nausea and vomiting occurred in 20% and 16%, respectively, with < 1% of patients developing grade 3 or 4 toxicity. Diarrhea was reported in 9% of patients with 1% reporting toxicity of grade 3 or 4. In a clinical trial of lymphoma patients receiving moderately higher doses of bendamustine, nausea occurred in 68% of patients and vomiting in 42%. Grade 3 or 4 nausea was reported in 5% of patients while no patients reported grade 3 or 4 vomiting.
 
Tumor lysis syndrome (TLS) has been reported in clinical trials of bendamustine and in post-marketing reports. Onset of TLS typically occurs within the first treatment cycle and lack of intervention may lead to acute renal failure (unspecified) or death. Adequate measures should be taken to prevent hyperuricemia and TLS. These may include hydration to maintain adequate volume status, monitoring of blood chemistry (particularly potassium and uric acid), and the use of allopurinol during the first one to two weeks of bendamustine therapy in patients at high-risk for TLS. Hyperuricemia occurred in 7% of patients in the CLL trial. The concomitant use of allopurinol and bendamustine may result in an increased risk of severe skin toxicities.
 
Skin reactions have been reported in clinical trials of bendamustine and in post-marketing reports. Rash (unspecified), bullous rash, and toxic skin reactions have occurred. Some events occurred when bendamustine was given in combination with other antineoplastic agents and therefore the relationship to bendamustine is uncertain in those cases. In a clinical trial of bendamustine with rituximab, one case of toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN) occurred. TEN has previously been reported as a potential adverse reaction of rituximab. Cases of Stevens-Johnson syndrome (SJS) and TEN, some fatal, have been reported when bendamustine is administered with allopurinol and other medications known to cause these conditions. The relationship to bendamustine cannot be determined. When skin reactions occur, they may be progressive and get worse with additional bendamustine treatment. If skin reactions are severe or progressive, bendamustine should be withheld or discontinued.
 
Hyperbilirubinemia occurred in 34% of CLL patients given bendamustine in the clinical trial, with grade 3 or 4 elevations observed in 3%. Elevated hepatic enzymes were not always associated with increases in bilirubin and grade 3 or 4 increases in AST and ALT were limited to 1% and 3% of patients, respectively.
 
In a trial of 153 patients with CLL who received bendamustine, 4 patients developed worsening hypertension. Three of these patients developed a hypertensive crisis. Patients with hypertensive crisis were treated with oral medications and the crisis resolved.
 
Pre-malignant and malignant diseases (i.e., secondary malignancy) have developed in patients who have been treated with bendamustine, including in patients with myelodysplastic syndrome, myeloproliferative disorders, acute myeloid leukemia and bronchial carcinoma. The association with bendamustine therapy has not been determined in these cases.
 
Spermatogenesis, azoospermia, and total germinal aplasia have been reported in male patients receiving alkylating agents, especially when given in combination with other antineoplastic drugs. Spermatogenesis has returned in some patients in remission several years after intensive chemotherapy was discontinued. Patients should be advised of the potential risk of infertility prior to beginning treatment with bendamustine.
 
Other adverse reactions occurring commonly in clinical trials of bendamustine include pyrexia (24%), fatigue (9%), asthenia (8%) and weight loss (7%).

Description
  Text
  Bendamustine is a mechlorethamine-derivative alkylating agent. It is indicated for the treatment of adults with chronic lymphocytic leukemia or adults with indolent B-cell non-Hodgkin lymphoma that has progressed during or within 6 months of treatment with rituximab or a rituximab-containing regimen. Severe myelosuppression, infection, and anaphylactic reactions have been reported with bendamustine therapy.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Bendamustine has a unique chemical structure that provides antimetabolite and alkylating properties. It consists of three primary structural elements, a 2-chloroethylamine group, a benzimidazole ring, and a butyric acid side chain. The 2-chloroethylamine group is responsible for the alkylating properties of bendamustine and is shared with other alkylators in the nitrogen mustard family. The benzimidazole ring is unique to bendamustine and gives the drug antimetabolite function. The butyric acid side-chain is shared with chlorambucil, however the addition of a hydrochloride residue confers water solubility.
 
The mechanism of action of bendamustine is not completely understood, but is thought to consist of several components. Similar to other alkylators, bendamustine is a DNA cross-linking agent which causes DNA single-strand and double-strand breaks. The DNA damage caused by bendamustine however, is more extensive and more durable than damage produced by cisplatin, cyclophosphamide, or carmustine. Bendamustine increases the activation of p53 and p53-dependent genes, a pathway resulting in strong activation of intrinsic apoptosis and previously known to be induced by nitrogen mustard alkylating agents. The activation of this pathway appears to be stronger and more rapidly induced by bendamustine than by either chlorambucil or phosphoramide mustard (metabolite of cyclophosphamide). Bendamustine is also known to inhibit several mitotic checkpoints, which results in mitotic catastrophe, a necrotic form of cell death morphologically distinct from apoptosis that occurs during metaphase. Bendamustine prevents physiologic arrest and repair of DNA damage created by alkylation, causing cells to enter mitosis with DNA damage. Mitotic catastrophe may occur in the presence or absence of the functional pro-apoptotic gene p53, including pre-treated cells refractory to conventional apoptosis mechanisms. The combination of p53 activation and mitotic catastrophe may account for the increase in bendamustine sensitivity in previously chemo-refractory cells. In addition, bendamustine does not appear to induce an alkyltransferase mechanism of DNA repair, a limitation previously shown with chlorambucil, melphalan, and phosphoramide mustard. This suggests that bendamustine is less susceptible to drug resistance based on alkylguanyl transferase expression.

  Text
  Bendamustine is administered intravenously. It is 94% to 96% bound to protein. Bendamustine has a blood to plasma concentration ratio ranging from 0.84 to 0.86 over a concentration range of 10 to 100 micrograms/mL. The mean steady-state volume of distribution is about 20 to 25 L, the intermediate half-life is about 40 minutes, and the clearance is about 700 mL/min following a single dose of bendamustine 120 mg/m2 IV given over 1 hour. Bendamustine is metabolized via hydrolytic, oxidative, and conjugative pathways. In vitro, metabolism mostly occurs via hydrolysis to monohydroxy (HP1) and dihydroxy (HP2) bendamustine metabolites that have low cytotoxic activity and to 2 active but minor metabolites, gamma-hydroxybendamustine (M3) and N-desmethylbendamustine (M4) that are primarily formed via CYP1A2. Plasma concentrations of M3 and M4 are 1/10th and 1/100th that of the parent drug, respectively. The mean terminal elimination half-lives of M3 and M4 are approximately 3 hours and 30 minutes, respectively. Following a radiolabeled bendamustine dose, 76% of the dose was recovered (50% in the urine, 25% in the feces) in cancer patients. In the urine, 3.3% of the dose was parent drug, less than 1% of the dose was M3 and M4 metabolites, and less than 5% of the dose was the HP2 metabolite.
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes and drug transporters: CYP1A2, P-gp, BCRP
Bendamustine is a substrate of CYP1A2, P-glycoprotein (P-gp), and breast cancer resistance protein (BCRP). Minor involvement of CYP1A2 in bendamustine elimination suggests a low likelihood of drug-drug interactions with CYP1A2 inhibitors.

Reconstitution of Lyophilized Powder Vials
Add 5 mL Sterile Water for Injection to the 25-mg vial and 20 mL Sterile Water for Injection to the 100-mg vial for a final vial concentration of 5 mg/mL.
Shake well; the lyophilized powder should completely dissolve in 5 minutes.
Further dilute the reconstituted vial(s) within 30 minutes.
Dilution
If applicable, allow the refrigerated vials to warm to room temperature (15 to 30 degrees C; 59 to 86 degrees F); do not use if solid or particulate matter is present.
Withdraw the calculated dose/volume from the reconstituted vial (5 mg/mL) or the solution (25 mg/mL) and transfer to an infusion bag containing the recommended volume of 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection or 2.5% Dextrose/0.45% Sodium Chloride Injection. Bendeka is also compatible with 5% Dextrose injection.
Treanda: 500 mL; final admixture concentration of 0.2 to 0.6 mg/mL.

Thoroughly mix the contents of the infusion bag.
