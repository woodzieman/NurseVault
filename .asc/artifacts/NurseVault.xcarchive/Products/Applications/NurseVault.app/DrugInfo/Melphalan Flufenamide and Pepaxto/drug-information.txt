    Headline
    anemia, infection, neutropenia, thrombocytopenia
    General Information
    Myelosuppression (e.g., anemia, neutropenia, and thrombocytopenia) occurs frequently with melphalan flufenamide therapy. Monitor complete blood counts (CBC) at baseline, during therapy (including more frequently during the first 2 months), and as clinically indicated. Fatal infection has also been reported. Therapy interruption, a dose reduction, or discontinuation may be necessary in patients who develop bleeding, infection, or severe hematologic toxicity. Do not administer melphalan flufenamide on day 1 if the platelet count is less than 50 x 109 cells/L OR the absolute neutrophil count is less than 1 x 109 cells/L. Monitor CBC weekly until blood counts recover. Administer growth factor support and antimicrobial agents and treat anemia as clinically indicated per institutional guidelines.
    Headline
    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components.
    Headline
    new primary malignancy
    General Information
    New primary malignancy (i.e., myelodysplastic syndromes and acute leukemia) has been reported with melphalan flufenamide therapy. Monitor patients for the development of new primary malignancy during and after therapy.
    Headline
    treatment outside of a clinical trial
    General Information
    In patients requiring a stem-cell transplant, do not use melphalan flufenamide as high-dose conditioning treatment outside of a clinical trial. Death was reported in dogs who received dosages of melphalan flufenamide that exceeded the recommended dosage for relapsed or refractory multiple myeloma in humans.
    Headline
    ensure correct formulation selection
    General Information
    Melphalan flufenamide has different dosage and administration recommendations than other melphalan-based products. Ensure correct formulation selection and dose before preparation and administration.
    Headline
    mortality
    General Information
    Because of an increased risk of mortality in multiple myeloma patients treated with melphalan flufenamide and dexamethasone compared with pomalidomide in the OCEAN trial, health care professionals should review each patient's progress on treatment and discuss the risks of continued administration with the patient in the context of other treatments. Patients with relapsed or refractory multiple myeloma following 2 to 4 lines of prior therapy who were resistant to lenalidomide in the last line of therapy were randomized to treatment with melphalan flufenamide plus low-dose dexamethasone or pomalidomide plus low-dose dexamethasone in a multicenter, randomized, open label, clinical trial (OCEAN; n = 495). In an efficacy and safety evaluation conducted by the FDA, the median overall survival for patients treated with melphalan flufenamide was 19.7 months (95% CI, 15.1 to 25.6) compared with 25 months (95% CI, 18.1 to 31.9) for patients who received pomalidomide after a median follow-up of 19.1 months (HR, 1.104; 95% CI, 0.846 to 1.441). The FDA is requiring the manufacturer to suspend enrollment in the OCEAN trial as well as other ongoing melphalan flufenamide clinical trials. Patients receiving clinical benefit from melphalan flufenamide may continue treatment in the OCEAN trial after signing a revised written informed consent.

    General Information
    Melphalan flufenamide may cause fetal harm if used during pregnancy, based on its mechanism of action. Pregnant women should be advised of the potential risk to the fetus.
    Headline
    contraception requirements, infertility, male-mediated teratogenicity, pregnancy testing, reproductive risk
    General Information
    Counsel patients about the reproductive risk and contraception requirements during melphalan flufenamide treatment. Pregnancy testing should be performed prior to starting melphalan flufenamide in female patients of reproductive potential. These patients should use effective contraception and avoid pregnancy during and for 6 months after melphalan flufenamide therapy. Additionally, male patients with a female partner of reproductive potential should use effective contraception during therapy and for 3 months after therapy due to the risk of male-mediated teratogenicity. Melphalan flufenamide may cause amenorrhea in premenopausal women and irreversible testicular suppression in males resulting in infertility.

Description
  Text
  NOTE: All products associated with this monograph are off-market. The content is retained for historical reference only and is no longer actively maintained.
 
Melphalan flufenamide is a peptide-drug conjugate that releases an alkylating agent (melphalan) into tumor cells. It was approved under the accelerated approval process by the FDA in February 2021 in combination with dexamethasone for the treatment of relapsed or refractory multiple myeloma in patients who had received at least 4 prior lines of therapy and had refractory disease to at least 1 proteasome inhibitor, 1 immunomodulatory agent, and 1 CD38-directed monoclonal antibody. Melphalan flufenamide was voluntarily removed from the US market by the manufacturer in October 2021 due to an increased risk of mortality reported in multiple myeloma patients treated with melphalan flufenamide plus dexamethasone compared with pomalidomide plus dexamethasone in the OCEAN trial. In February 2024, FDA approval was removed for this indication due to failure to confirm a clinical benefit in the HORIZON trial.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  NOTE: All products associated with this monograph are off-market. The content is retained for historical reference only and is no longer actively maintained.
 
Melphalan flufenamide is a peptide-drug conjugate. It is highly lipophilic and diffuses through the tumor cell membrane where it is cleaved by aminopeptidases (proteolytic enzymes located in the tumor cell) to release the alkylating moiety, melphalan. Additionally, the alkylating moiety is highly hydrophobic which increases the concentration gradient across the tumor cell membrane causing increased diffusion of melphalan flufenamide into the myeloma cell. Once inside the tumor cell nucleus, melphalan induces DNA damage. In vitro, melphalan flufenamide inhibits cell proliferation and causes apoptosis of hematopoietic and solid tumor cells. It has also demonstrated cytotoxicity in multiple myeloma cells that had resistance to melphalan, bortezomib, and dexamethasone as well as synergistic cytotoxicity with dexamethasone in melphalan resistant and non-resistant multiple myeloma cell lines. In contrast with melphalan hydrochloride, melphalan flufenamide induces DNA damage that is not dependent on activation of p53, achieves higher intracellular concentrations, and exhibits anti-angiogenic activity.

Melphalan flufenamide is administered intravenously. Its distribution from plasma to peripheral tissues is rapid. After a single dose, the mean volume of distribution is 35 L (coefficient of variation (CV), 71%) for melphalan flufenamide and 76 L (CV, 32%) for melphalan. At the end of the melphalan flufenamide 40-mg infusion, the mean clearance is 692 L/hour (CV, 49%) for melphalan flufenamide and 23 L/hour (CV, 23%) for melphalan; the mean elimination half-life is 2.1 minutes (CV, 34%) and 70 minutes (CV, 21%), respectively. Melphalan flufenamide is metabolized in tissues to desethyl-melphalan flufenamide and melphalan. Melphalan is metabolized via hydrolysis to monohydroxy-melphalan and dihydroxy-melphalan.

      Do not substitute melphalan flufenamide for or with other melphalan-based products.
Melphalan flufenamide degrades in solution, especially at room temperature.
Drug reconstitution and dilution need to be completed within 30 minutes.
Reconstitution:
Remove the correct number of melphalan flufenamide vials from the refrigerator to make the desired dose; let vial(s) stand for at least 30 minutes to allow them to reach room temperature (20 to 25 degrees C or 68 to 77 degrees F).
Shake the lyophilized vial(s) vigorously (or vortex) to disintegrate the powder cake into a loose powder.
Reconstitute each 20-mg vial with 40 mL of room temperature 5% Dextrose injection for a final vial concentration of 0.5 mg/mL.
Shake the vial(s) vigorously until the reconstituted solution is clear; let vial(s) stand to allow air bubbles to dissipate to confirm a clear solution.
Dilution:
Withdraw and discard 80 mL from a refrigerated (2 to 8 degrees C or 36 to 46 degrees F) 250-mL infusion bag of 0.9% Sodium Chloride injection.
Transfer the calculated dose/volume from the reconstituted vial(s) to the cold 0.9% Sodium Chloride injection infusion bag to achieve a final diluted solution concentration of 0.1 mg/mL to 0.16 mg/mL.
Discard any unused reconstituted solution left in the vial(s).
Mix by gently inverting the infusion bag; do not shake.
The diluted solution should be clear and colorless to pale yellow.
Storage following dilution: If used immediately, infuse the diluted admixture within 60 minutes from the start of the vial reconstitution. Otherwise, place the diluted admixture in the refrigerator within 30 minutes of vial reconstitution and store for up to 6 hours.
Intravenous (IV) Infusion:
Allow the diluted admixture to acclimate to room temperature (if applicable) prior to administration; start the infusion within 30 minutes of removing the diluted admixture from the refrigerator.
Administer melphalan flufenamide as an IV infusion over 30 minutes via a central venous access device (e.g., mediport, PICC line, or tunneled central venous catheter).
Flush the central catheter after the infusion is complete per institutional guidelines.
