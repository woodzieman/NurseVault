        Heading
        Children and Adolescents 2.5 to 17 years
        Dosage Description
        0.16 mg/kg/dose subcutaneously once weekly, initially. Adjust dose based on growth response. When switching from daily human growth hormone to once-weekly somapacitan, choose the preferred day for the weekly dose. Take the final dose of daily treatment on the day before (or at least 8 hours before) the first dose of somapacitan. The failure to increase growth rate, especially during the first year of therapy, necessitates close assessment of compliance and evaluation for underlying causes of growth failure, such as hypothyroidism, undernutrition, advanced bone age, and antibodies to recombinant human growth hormone. Reevaluate children who were treated with somapacitan for growth hormone deficiency in childhood and whose epiphyses are closed before continuing somapacitan treatment.

Contraindications And Precautions

  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  Contraindications And Precautions
    Headline
    Drug Interactions
    General Information
    The coadministration of certain medications may lead to harm and require avoidance or therapy modification; review all drug interactions prior to concomitant use of other medications.
    Headline
    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components.
    Headline
    critical illness, respiratory failure, trauma
    General Information
    Somapacitan is contraindicated in individuals with acute critical illness due to complications following open-heart surgery such as coronary artery bypass graft surgery (CABG), abdominal surgery, multiple accidental trauma, or acute severe respiratory insufficiency (acute respiratory failure) because of the risk of increased mortality. The safety of continuing somapacitan replacement treatment for approved indications in individuals who develop acute critical illnesses has not been established.
    Headline
    malignancy
    General Information
    Somapacitan is contraindicated in individuals with active malignancy. There is an increased risk of malignancy progression with growth hormone treatment in individuals with active malignancy. Any pre-existing neoplastic disease must be inactive, and treatment complete, prior to beginning somapacitan therapy. Discontinue somapacitan treatment in individuals who have recurrent cancer. In childhood cancer survivors who were treated with radiation therapy to the brain/head for their first neoplasm and who developed subsequent growth hormone deficiency and were treated with somatropin, an increased risk of a new primary malignancy has been reported. Intracranial tumors, in particular meningiomas, were the most common of these second neoplasms. Monitor all individuals with a history of growth hormone deficiency secondary to an intracranial neoplasm routinely while on somapacitan therapy for progression or recurrence of the tumor. Because children with certain rare genetic causes of short stature have an increased risk of developing malignancies, consider the risks and benefits of starting somapacitan in these individuals. If treatment with somapacitan is initiated, monitor these individuals carefully for development of neoplasms. There is risk of malignant changes of preexisting nevi (i.e., skin cancer) in individuals treated with somatropin. Monitor individuals on somapacitan therapy carefully for increased growth, or potential malignant changes, of preexisting nevi. Advise individuals and caregivers to report significant changes in behavior, onset of headaches, vision disturbances, and/or changes in skin pigmentation or the appearance of preexisting nevi. Discontinue somapacitan therapy if evidence of neoplasia develops.
    Headline
    diabetes mellitus, proliferative diabetic retinopathy, severe non-proliferative diabetic retinopathy
    General Information
    Somapacitan is contraindicated in individuals with active proliferative diabetic retinopathy and severe non-proliferative diabetic retinopathy as growth hormone (GH) appears to influence the progression of this disease. Use somapacitan cautiously in individuals with diabetes mellitus or risk factors for glucose intolerance as somapacitan may decrease insulin sensitivity, especially at higher doses. New onset type 2 diabetes mellitus has been reported in individuals taking GH products. Individuals with pre-diabetes or undiagnosed diabetes may experience worsening glycemic control and develop symptoms. Monitor glucose concentrations in all individuals receiving somapacitan, especially those with risk factors for diabetes mellitus (e.g., obesity, Turner syndrome, family history). Monitor individuals with type 1 or type 2 diabetes or pre-diabetes closely; dose adjustments of antidiabetic medications may be necessary.
    Headline
    adrenal insufficiency, hypothyroidism, pituitary insufficiency
    General Information
    Individuals who have or at risk for pituitary insufficiency (corticotropin deficiency), and are receiving somapacitan, may be at risk for reduced serum cortisol concentrations and/or unmasking of central (secondary) adrenal insufficiency. Individuals treated with glucocorticoid replacement for previously diagnosed adrenal insufficiency may require an increase in their maintenance or stress doses following initiation of somapacitan treatment. In addition, individuals with untreated hypothyroidism will have an inadequate response to somapacitan therapy. In individuals with growth hormone deficiency, central (secondary) hypothyroidism may first be detected or become worse during treatment with growth hormone therapy. Periodic thyroid function tests should be performed and treatment with thyroid hormone initiated when indicated.
    Headline
    hepatic failure
    General Information
    Avoid the use of somapacitan in individuals with hepatic failure due to significantly increased somapacitan exposure. Dosage adjustment with slow dose titration is recommended in adult patients with moderate hepatic impairment. Somapacitan is not recommended in pediatric patients with moderate hepatic impairment. No initial dosage adjustment is necessary for individuals with mild hepatic impairment.
    Headline
    geriatric
    General Information
    Geriatric patients receiving somapacitan appear to have higher exposure than younger patients at the same dose. Geriatric patients may also be more sensitive to the actions of somapacitan and may be at an increased risk of adverse reactions. Dose selection for older individuals should be cautious, usually starting at the low end of the dosing range. Geriatric individuals are more at risk for the adverse effects of therapy. Growth hormone should only be prescribed to individuals with clinical features suggestive of adult growth hormone deficiency (GHD) and biochemically proven evidence of adult GHD. There are no data available to suggest that somatropin has beneficial effects in treating aging and age-related conditions and the enhancement of sporting performance; therefore, non-approved uses of the drug are not recommended. According to the Beers Criteria, growth hormone is considered a potentially inappropriate medication in geriatric individuals.
    Headline
    scoliosis
    General Information
    Monitor pediatric patients with scoliosis receiving somapacitan for disease progression, which can occur in patients who experience rapid growth. Somatropin has not been shown to increase the occurrence of scoliosis.
    Headline
    epiphyseal closure
    General Information
    Somapacitan is contraindicated in individuals with epiphyseal closure. Slipped capital femoral epiphysis may occur more frequently during periods of rapid growth and can lead to osteonecrosis. Cases of slipped capital femoral epiphysis, with or without osteonecrosis, have been reported in pediatric patients with short stature receiving somapacitan. Evaluate pediatric patients presenting with a new limp or complaints of hip or knee pain.
    Headline
    obesity, obstructive sleep apnea, respiratory insufficiency
    General Information
    Somapacitan is contraindicated in individuals with Prader-Willi syndrome who are severely obese or have severe respiratory impairment. Unless individuals with Prader-Willi syndrome also have a diagnosis of growth hormone deficiency, somapacitan is not indicated for long-term treatment of pediatric patients who have growth failure due to genetically confirmed Prader-Willi syndrome. There have been reports of fatalities with the use of growth hormone in pediatric patients with Prader-Willi syndrome who had one or more of the following risk factors: severe obesity, history of respiratory insufficiency or sleep apnea, or unidentified respiratory infection. Male individuals with one or more of these factors may be at increased risk. Individuals with Prader-Willi syndrome should be evaluated for upper airway obstruction before initiation of treatment with growth hormone. If during treatment with growth hormone, individuals show signs of upper airway obstruction (including onset of or increased snoring), treatment should be interrupted. All individuals with Prader-Willi syndrome should be evaluated for obstructive sleep apnea and monitored if sleep apnea is suspected.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Available data on the use of somapacitan during pregnancy are insufficient to evaluate a drug-associated risk. However, published studies with short-acting recombinant growth hormone use during pregnancy over several decades have not identified any drug-associated risk of major birth defects, miscarriage, or adverse outcomes. In animal studies, doses exceeding the regular human dose revealed no evidence of fetal harm.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Use somapacitan with caution during breast-feeding. There are no data on the presence of somapacitan in human milk, its effects on the breast-fed child, or its effect on milk production. Somapacitan is present in animal milk. When other short-acting recombinant growth hormone products were administered over a period of 7 days in lactating individuals, there was no change in the concentration of growth hormone in human milk and there were no adverse effects reported in the breast-fed infants. Consider the benefits of breast-feeding, the patient's clinical need for treatment, and any potential adverse effects on the breast-fed child from the medication or from the patient's underlying medical condition.

Description
  Text
  Somapacitan is a subcutaneously administered human growth hormone (GH) analog. It is indicated for the once-weekly replacement of endogenous growth hormone in adults and pediatric patients 2.5 years and older with growth hormone deficiency. Growth hormone deficiency occurs when there is inadequate growth hormone production from the anterior pituitary gland. In a double-blind, placebo-controlled study in adult patients (n = 300), somapacitan efficacy was determined by the percentage change of truncal fat. At the end of the 34-week treatment period, truncal fat decreased by 1.06%, on average, in patients taking weekly somapacitan, increased by 0.47% in placebo patients, and decreased by 2.23% in daily somatropin patients. Patients in the weekly somapacitan and daily somapacitan groups had similar improvements in other clinical endpoints. The efficacy of somapacitan in treatment-naive pediatric patients was established in a randomized, open-label, active-controlled, parallel-group phase 3 study (n = 200). At week 52, treatment with once-weekly somapacitan resulted in an annualized height velocity of 11.2 cm/year compared with an annualized height velocity of 11.7 cm/year for daily somatropin treatment (treatment difference -0.5; 95% CI: -1.1 to 0.2). The mean increase in height standard deviation score over the 52-week period was 1.25 and 1.3 in the once weekly somapacitan and daily somatropin groups, respectively. Prior to starting somapacitan therapy, a funduscopic eye examination should be performed, and periodically thereafter, to exclude papilledema. Papilledema may be a symptom of intracranial hypertension and growth hormones may induce or worsen intracranial hypertension.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Somapacitan is a long-acting albumin-binding human growth hormone (hGH) derivative that binds to a dimeric GH receptor in the cell membrane of target cells. This results in intracellular signal transduction and multiple pharmacodynamic effects. Some of the pharmacodynamic effects are primarily mediated by insulin-like growth factor 1 (IGF-1) produced in the liver. Other actions are primarily a consequence of the direct effects of somapacitian.

  Text
  Somapacitan is administered by subcutaneous injection. Somapacitan displays non-linear pharmacokinetics overall, but somapacitan pharmacokinetics are approximately linear in adults with growth hormone deficiency (GHD) receiving clinically relevant doses. Somapacitan is extensively bound (greater than 99% to plasma proteins). The estimated volume of distribution (V/F) in adult GHD patients is approximately 14.6 L and the plasma elimination half-life is 2 to 3 days. Somapacitan is metabolized via proteolytic cleavage of the linker sequence between the peptide backbone and albumin binder sidechain. Somapacitan is primarily eliminated by the urine and feces. Approximately 81% of the dose is excreted in the urine and approximately 13% is excreted in the feces. No intact somapacitan is excreted indicating full breakdown of somapacitan prior to excretion.
 
IGF-1 was measured in treatment-naive adults with GHD treated with somapacitan; use of the drug normalized the mean IGF-1 standard deviation score (SDS) level from a baseline value below -2 to a value within the reference range (-2 to +2). Somapacitan induces a less than dose-proportional IGF-1 response at steady-state. Maximum IGF-1 concentrations were observed within 2 to 4 days after dosing and a steady-state IGF-1 response was reached after 1 to 2 weekly doses. There was a limited cumulative IGF-1 response.
 
Affected cytochrome P450 (CYP450) isoenzymes and drug transporters: Unknown
Limited published data indicate that growth hormone treatment increases CYP450-mediated antipyrine clearance. Thus, somapacitan may alter the clearance of compounds known to be metabolized by CYP450 enzymes. No specific data are available. Careful monitoring is advisable when somapacitan is used with drugs metabolized by CYP450 enzymes, particularly those drugs with narrow therapeutic index.
