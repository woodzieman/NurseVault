Drug Class: Milrinone is a parenteral positive inotrope and vasodilator with phosphodiesterase inhibitor activity indicated for the short-term treatment of patients with acute decompensated heart failure.

Routes: injection (IV/IM/SC); Common adult doses: 0.75 mcg/kg/minute continuous IV infusion.

Common Side Effects (excluding routine GI upset): angina, atrial fibrillation, atrial flutter, atrial tachycardia, chest pain (unspecified), premature ventricular contractions (pvcs), supraventricular tachycardia (svt), torsade de pointes, ventricular fibrillation, ventricular tachycardia, anaphylactic shock, bronchospasm, rash, tremor

In an Overdose: No overdose/antidote information is listed in this file.

Key Nursing Considerations: Contraindications/precautions: Milrinone is contraindicated in those patients with milrinone hypersensitivity. Milrinone should not be used in patients with severe valvular heart disease such as severe obstructive aortic valvular disease or obstructive pulmonary valvular disease.

Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  General Information
  Milrinone is contraindicated in those patients with milrinone hypersensitivity.
 
Milrinone should not be used in patients with severe valvular heart disease such as severe obstructive aortic valvular disease or obstructive pulmonary valvular disease. Surgical relief of the obstruction in aortic or pulmonic valvular disease is required. Due to the drug's inotropic activity, exacerbation of outflow tract obstruction can occur in patients with idiopathic hypertrophic subaortic stenosis.
 
Patients with atrial fibrillation or atrial flutter can exhibit an increased ventricular response to milrinone due to its effect of shortening the AV node conduction time. In addition, milrinone has precipitated supraventricular and ventricular arrhythmias including ventricular tachycardia. Due to the potential for arrhythmias occurring in the high-risk population treated with this drug, patients should be monitored by ECG during milrinone infusion. Correct underlying fluid and electrolyte imbalance to minimize arrhythmia potential during milrinone administration. Milrinone may lead to hypotension (syncope, orthostatic hypotension), particularly in patients with hypovolemia.
 
The effects of milrinone in the acute phase following acute myocardial infarction are currently unknown, so milrinone should be avoided in this circumstance.
 
The dosage of milrinone requires adjustment in patients with creatinine clearance (CrCl) < 50 ml/min (renal impairment, renal failure). The half-life of milrinone may be prolonged in patients with renal disease.
 
Milrinone is classified as FDA pregnancy risk category C. Adequate studies on risks during pregnancy have not been conducted, so milrinone should be used during pregnancy only when clearly needed.
 
It is unknown if milrinone is excreted into breast milk. Caution should be exercised when administering milrinone to breast-feeding mothers.

    Headline
    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components. Dextrose-containing milrinone solutions may be contraindicated in individuals with a known allergy to corn or corn products.
    Headline
    hypertrophic obstructive cardiomyopathy, severe aortic stenosis, severe pulmonary valve stenosis
    General Information
    Do not use milrinone in individuals with severe aortic stenosis or severe pulmonary valve stenosis in lieu of surgical relief of the obstruction. Inotropes may aggravate outflow tract obstruction in individuals with hypertrophic obstructive cardiomyopathy.
    Headline
    cardiac arrhythmias, hypokalemia
    General Information
    Milrinone may exacerbate pre-existing cardiac arrhythmias. Milrinone produces a slight shortening of atrioventricular conduction time, indicating a potential for an increased ventricular response rate in individuals with atrial flutter or atrial fibrillation which is not controlled with digoxin therapy. Monitor electrocardiogram continuously during milrinone therapy. Correct hypokalemia prior to or during milrinone administration due to risk for arrhythmias.
    Headline
    acute myocardial infarction
    General Information
    Milrinone use is not recommended during acute myocardial infarction. The effects of milrinone during the acute phase after myocardial infarction are unknown.
    Headline
    hypovolemia
    General Information
    Do not administer milrinone to individuals with uncorrected hypovolemia; correct hypovolemia with volume expanders before initiating milrinone.
    Headline
    renal failure, renal impairment
    General Information
    Infusion rate reductions may be necessary for milrinone in individuals with renal impairment or renal failure (CrCl 50 mL/minute or less). Monitor renal function carefully during milrinone therapy. Severe renal impairment significantly increases the elimination half-life of milrinone.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Use milrinone during pregnancy only if the potential benefit justifies the potential risk for the fetus. There are no adequate and well-controlled studies of milrinone use during human pregnancy. No evidence of teratogenicity was observed when milrinone was administered intravenously at doses up to 2.5 times the maximum recommended clinical dose to pregnant rats and rabbits during organogenesis, although an increased resorption rate was seen in rabbits.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Use milrinone with caution during breast-feeding. There are no data on the presence of milrinone in human milk, its effects on the breastfed child, or its effects on milk production. Milrinone is well absorbed with oral administration, but has a short half-life (2.3 hours). Waiting 8 hours after the end of infusion prior to restarting breast-feeding may eliminate the risk of exposure in the breastfed child. Consider the benefits of breast-feeding, the patient's clinical need for treatment, and any potential adverse effects on the breast-fed child from the medication or from the patient's underlying medical condition.

Adverse Reactions
  Headline
  Adverse Reactions
  General Information
  The use of IV and oral milrinone has been associated with an increased frequency of ventricular arrhythmias, including nonsustained ventricular tachycardia. Long-term oral use has been associated with an increased risk of sudden death. Hence, patients receiving milrinone should be observed closely with the use of continuous electrocardiographic monitoring to allow the prompt detection and management of ventricular arrhythmias. Ventricular arrhythmias are the most frequent adverse reactions that occur during milrinone therapy, reported in 12.1% of patients. Specifically, premature ventricular contractions (PVCs) (8.5%), ventricular tachycardia (2.8%), sustained ventricular tachycardia (1%), and ventricular fibrillation (0.2%) have been observed. During post-marketing experience, rare cases of torsades de pointes have been reported. Potentially fatal arrhythmias have been reported infrequently and are associated primarily with other precipitating factors such as preexisting arrhythmias, metabolic abnormalities such as hypokalemia, abnormal digoxin serum concentrations, and catheter insertion. Improved cardiac function following administration of milrinone can induce diuresis, predisposing the patient to hypokalemia and arrhythmias. Supraventricular tachycardia (SVT) has been observed in 3.8% of patients. Other atrial arrhythmias may include atrial tachycardia, atrial fibrillation, and atrial flutter. Patients with preexisting atrial fibrillation or atrial flutter can exhibit an increased ventricular response to milrinone due to its effect of shortening the AV node conduction time. Arrhythmias associated with milrinone do not appear to be related to dose or milrinone plasma concentrations. Other cardiovascular-related effects that have been reported include hypotension (2.9%), palpitations, syncope, and chest pain/angina (1.2%). Short-term milrinone infusions did not improve morbidity or mortality outcomes, but resulted in increased adverse effects (hypotension and new onset atrial arrhythmias) when compared to placebo in patients hospitalized with worsened systolic heart failure; the study excluded patients who required continuous IV inotropic support. In this study, sustained hypotension requiring intervention (10.7% vs. 3.2% placebo) and new atrial arrhythmias (4.6% vs. 1.5% placebo) occurred more frequently in patients receiving milrinone. Due to the potential for arrhythmias occurring in the high-risk population treated with this drug, patients should have blood pressure and ECG monitored closely during milrinone infusion. Correct underlying fluid and electrolyte imbalances to minimize proarrhythmic and hypotensive potential during milrinone administration.Headache, usually mild to moderate, has been reported in 2.9% of patients. Nausea/vomiting may also occur. Thrombocytopenia occurs infrequently (0.4%). Hypokalemia (0.6%) and tremor (0.4%) have also been reported infrequently. During post-marketing experience, the following reactions have been reported: isolated spontaneous reports of bronchospasm and anaphylactoid reactions (anaphylactic shock), elevated hepatic enzymes, and rash (unspecified).The safety of milrinone infusions lasting longer than 48 hours has not been evaluated in clinical trials. Injection site reactions have been reported with intravenous milrinone therapy. Therefore, careful monitoring of the infusion site should be maintained during therapy to avoid possible extravasation or injection site reaction.

Description
  Text
  Milrinone is a parenteral positive inotrope and vasodilator with phosphodiesterase inhibitor activity indicated for the short-term treatment of patients with acute decompensated heart failure. It has minimal chronotropic activity. Milrinone is a preferred parenteral inotrope for patients with chronic heart failure, especially in the presence of significant right ventricular dysfunction, pulmonary hypertension, or concomitant beta-blocker therapy. Heart failure guidelines recommend inotropic support to maintain systemic perfusion and preserve end-organ performance until definitive therapy is initiated or the acute precipitating problem resolves. Short-term inotropic support may be reasonable in hospitalized patients presenting with documented severe systolic dysfunction with low blood pressure and significantly depressed cardiac output. Long-term palliative inotropic therapy may be considered for symptom control in patients with stage D heart failure refractory to optimal medical and device therapy who are awaiting mechanical circulatory support (MCS) or cardiac transplant or are not eligible for MCS or transplant. Milrinone may also be used to maintain hemodynamic stability postoperatively in heart transplant recipients. Dobutamine and milrinone have similar clinical effects in patients with severe heart failure. Both dobutamine and milrinone were used successfully as a pharmacologic bridge to heart transplantation (n = 36) with no significant differences in right heart hemodynamics, need for additional vasodilator/inotropic therapy, need for mechanical cardiac support, or death. In another study, there was no difference in mortality between chronic dobutamine or milrinone in patients with stage D heart failure being discharged from the hospital (n = 112). Inotropes can induce hypotension and arrhythmias. Milrinone is more likely to cause hypotension and less likely to cause tachyarrhythmias when compared to dobutamine and low-dose dopamine; however, milrinone may cause reflex tachycardia. Milrinone is primarily eliminated by the kidneys and requires dosage adjustment in patients with renal insufficiency.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Milrinone is a positive inotrope and vasodilator with little chronotropic activity. Milrinone selectively inhibits phosphodiesterase III, preventing breakdown of cyclic adenosine monophosphate (cAMP) in cardiac and vascular muscle cells. This results in increased myocardial contractility and vasodilation. Milrinone also improves diastolic relaxation (lusitropy), thus reducing preload, afterload, and systemic vascular resistance.

    Description
    Children and Adolescents
Compared to adults, children have a larger Vd, faster clearance, and similar half-life of milrinone. Although Vd is similar, clearance is significantly faster in children compared to infants. Vd was 0.7 +/- 0.2 L/kg, clearance was 5.9 +/- 2 mL/kg/minute, and half-life was 1.86 +/- 2 hours in a prospective, open-label study in children after open-heart surgery (n = 7, age: 1 to 13 years). In pediatric patients with septic shock (n = 11, age: 8 months to 15 years), Vd was 1.47 +/- 1.03 L/kg, mean clearance was 11 +/- 9.6 mL/kg/minute, and median half-life was 1.47 hours (0.62 to 10.85 hours).
 
Infants
Compared to adults, infants have a larger Vd, faster clearance, and prolonged half-life of milrinone. Although Vd is similar, clearance is significantly faster in children compared to infants. Vd was 0.9 +/- 0.4 L/kg, clearance was 3.8 +/- 1 mL/kg/minute, and half-life was 3.15 +/- 2 hours in a prospective, open-label study in infants after open-heart surgery (n = 12). In pediatric patients with septic shock (n = 11, age: 8 months to 15 years), Vd was 1.47 +/- 1.03 L/kg, mean clearance was 11 +/- 9.6 mL/kg/minute, and median half-life was 1.47 hours (0.62 to 10.85 hours).
 
Premature Neonates
Estimated half-life is approximately 5-times longer in premature neonates compared to children or adults. In a prospective, open-label pilot study in 29 very preterm neonates (gestational age younger than 29 weeks, postnatal age younger than 12 hours), the population mean (95% CI) clearance estimate was 0.64 mL/kg/minute, the population mean Vd was 0.576 L/kg, and the half-life estimate was 10.3 hours.
  Text
  Milrinone is administered by intravenous infusion. Milrinone has a Vd of about 0.45 L/kg, a mean elimination half-life of 2.4 hours, and a clearance of 0.14 L/kg/hour after continuous infusion in adult patients with congestive heart failure. Milrinone is approximately 70% bound to human plasma protein. It is primarily eliminated via the urine, with 83% excreted as the parent drug and 12% as its O-glucuronide metabolite. Elimination is rapid; approximately 60% is recovered in the urine within 2 hours of dosing and 90% is recovered within 8 hours. The mean renal clearance of milrinone is approximately 0.3 L/minute, indicating active secretion.
