Drug Class: Rozanolixizumab is a monoclonal antibody neonatal Fc receptor blocker indicated for the treatment of generalized myasthenia gravis (gMG) in adults who are anti-acetylcholine receptor (AChR) or anti-muscle-specific tyrosine kinase (MuSK) antibody positive.

Routes: subcutaneous; Common adult doses: Less than 50 kg: 420 mg subcutaneous infusion once weekly for 6 weeks.

Common Side Effects (excluding routine GI upset): angioedema, injection site reaction, rash, headache, photophobia, vertigo

In an Overdose: No overdose/antidote information is listed in this file.

    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    There are limited data on rozanolixizumab use during pregnancy to inform a drug-associated risk of major birth defects, miscarriage, or adverse maternal or fetal outcomes. Subcutaneous administration to pregnant monkeys every 3 days throughout pregnancy at doses 10 to 30 times the maximum recommended human dose (MRHD, approximately 10 mg/kg) resulted in an increase in embryonic death, reduced body weight, and impaired immune function in offspring. A no-effect dose for adverse developmental effects was not identified. There is a pregnancy registry that monitors pregnancy outcomes in people exposed to rozanolixizumab during pregnancy and/or breast-feeding.   Adverse Reactions
  Adverse Reactions
    Headline
    angioedema, injection site reaction, rash
    General Information
    In clinical trials, hypersensitivity reactions (including angioedema and rash) were observed in 11% of individuals treated with rozanolixizumab. Hypersensitivity reactions occurred within 1 day to 2 weeks of administration. One individual discontinued use of rozanolixizumab due to a hypersensitivity reaction. Injection site reactions also occurred in 8% of individuals within 1 to 3 days after the most recent infusion. Management of hypersensitivity reactions depend on the type and severity of the reaction. Monitor individuals during treatment and for 15 minutes after the rozanolixizumab infusion is complete for clinical signs and symptoms of hypersensitivity reactions. If a hypersensitivity reaction occurs during administration, discontinue the infusion and institute appropriate supportive measures. Inform individuals about the signs and symptoms of hypersensitivity reactions and advise them to contact their care team right away if hypersensitivity reactions occur.

    infection
    General Information
    People receiving rozanolixizumab are at increased risk of infection. If serious infections occur, appropriate treatment should be administered and additional infusions of rozanolixizumab should be withheld until the infection has resolved. Infections were reported in 23% of subjects during a placebo-controlled trial. Across the clinical trial and extension studies, 94 of 196 subjects (48%) receiving rozanolixizumab reported infections. The most common infections reported included upper respiratory infections (17%), COVID-19 (14%), urinary tract infections (9%), and herpes simplex (6%). Serious infections were reported in 4% of subjects treated with rozanolixizumab. Three fatal cases of pneumonia were identified; COVID-19 was identified in 2 of these subjects, with an unknown pathogen in 1 subject. Six cases of infections led to discontinuation of the medication. Herpes zoster has been identified during postmarketing use. However, a causal relationship has not been established.

Description
  Text
  Rozanolixizumab is a monoclonal antibody neonatal Fc receptor blocker indicated for the treatment of generalized myasthenia gravis (gMG) in adults who are anti-acetylcholine receptor (AChR) or anti-muscle-specific tyrosine kinase (MuSK) antibody positive. Rozanolixizumab was shown to be effective in multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled studies of patients who were Myasthenia Gravis Foundation of America Clinical Classification Class II to IVa, had a Myasthenia Gravis-Activities of Daily Living (MG-ADL) total score of at least 3, presence of autoantibodies against AChR or MuSK, and were on a stable dose of myasthenia gravis therapy prior to screening. Following 6 weeks of weekly subcutaneous infusions and an observation period of up to 8 weeks, a statistically significant difference with rozanolixizumab treatment was noted in both the MG-ADL total score (-3.4 points for medication vs -0.8 points on placebo) and in the Quantitative Myasthenia Gravis (QMG) score (-5.4 points for 7 mg/kg dose level and -6.7 points for 10 mg/kg dose level vs -1.9 points in placebo-treated group) when compared to baseline assessments. The most common adverse events noted in trials were headache, infections, diarrhea, fever, hypersensitivity reactions, and nausea. Because rozanolixizumab causes a transient reduction in IgG levels, patients are at increased risk of infection; those with an active infection should not receive the medication. Cases of aseptic meningitis have also been reported in patients receiving rozanolixizumab. Rozanolixizumab was FDA-approved in June 2023.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Rozanolixizumab-noli is a humanized IgG4 monoclonal antibody that binds to the neonatal Fc receptor (FcRn), resulting in reduction of circulating IgG. When IgG is bound to FcRn, it is saved from lysosomal degradation and is recycled into the circulation. In patients with myasthenia gravis, the presence of pathogenic IgG autoantibodies impair synaptic transmission at the neuromuscular junction, leading to musculoskeletal symptoms. By blocking IgG from binding to FcRn and inhibiting IgG recycling, the unbound IgG and pathogenic autoantibodies can be eliminated via the natural lysosomal degradation pathway.

    Description
    Rozanolixizumab exhibits nonlinear pharmacokinetics. Following subcutaneous administration, peak plasma levels were achieved after approximately 2 days in healthy subjects. Rozanolixizumab exposure increased in a greater than dose-proportional manner over a dose range from 1 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (2 times the maximum recommended dose).

      Name
      Subcutaneous Administration
      Description
      Rozanolixizumab should only be prepared and infused by a health care provider.
Must be administered with a subcutaneous infusion pump. Refer to the infusion pump manufacturer's instructions.
Missed doses: The missed dose may be administered up to 4 days after the scheduled time point. After that, resume the original dosing schedule until the treatment cycle is completed.
 
Preparation of subcutaneous infusion:
Use aseptic technique when preparing rozanolixizumab.
Prior to use, allow vials to reach room temperature in the original carton for approximately 30 minutes. Do not use heating devices. Do not shake.
Infuse within 4 hours of puncturing the vial. Administer immediately after priming the infusion set.
Use transfer needles to fill the syringe.
Remove the needle from the syringe and attach the infusion set to the syringe.
Follow the device manufacturer's instructions to prepare the infusion pump and prime the tubing. The following criteria are recommended:
It is recommended to use pumps where the administered volume can be pre-set as each vial contains excess volume for priming of the infusion line.
Syringe pump occlusion alarm limits should be at the maximum setting.
Tubing length should be 61 cm or shorter.
An infusion set with a needle of 26 gauge or larger should be used.
Storage: Once removed from refrigeration, unopened vials may be stored at room temperature up to 77 degrees F (25 degrees C) for up to 30 days in the original carton to protect from light until ready to use. Each vial is for one-time use only and does not contain preservatives. Once opened, discard any remaining solution.
Subcutaneous infusion administration:
Choose an infusion site in the lower right or lower left part of the abdomen below the navel and clean with an alcohol wipe. Do not infuse where the skin is tender, bruised, red, or hard. Avoid infusing into tattoos, scars, or stretch marks. Rotate infusion sites for subsequent injections.
Insert the infusion set needle into the infusion site and secure the needle to the skin with sterile gauze and tape or a transparent dressing.
Infuse at a constant flow rate up to 20 mL/hour.
Monitor patients during administration and for 15 minutes after completion for clinical signs and symptoms of hypersensitivity reactions. If a hypersensitivity reaction occurs during administration, discontinue administration and institute appropriate supportive measures.
When the infusion is complete, do not flush the infusion line as the volume of infusion has been adjusted taking into account the losses in the line.
