Drug Class: Macitentan; tadalafil is an oral combination of an endothelin receptor antagonist and phosphodiesterase 5 inhibitor indicated for the treatment of pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH, WHO Group I) in adults of WHO functional class II to III.

Routes: oral; Common adult doses: 10 mg/day macitentan; 40 mg/day tadalafil PO.

Common Side Effects (excluding routine GI upset): amnesia, headache, migraine, seizures, edema, fluid retention, flushing, heart failure, hypotension, myocardial infarction, palpitations, peripheral edema, sinus tachycardia, stroke, angioedema, exfoliative dermatitis, pruritus, rash, stevens-johnson syndrome, urticaria, anemia, bleeding, epistaxis

In an Overdose: No overdose/antidote information is listed in this file.

    Drug Interactions
    General Information
    The coadministration of certain medications may lead to harm and require avoidance or therapy modification; review all drug interactions prior to concomitant use of other medications.
    Headline
    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components.
    Headline
    Child-Pugh class C, hepatic cirrhosis, hepatic failure
    General Information
    Avoid use of macitentan; tadalafil in individuals with severe hepatic cirrhosis (Child-Pugh class C) or hepatic failure. Do not initiate macitentan; tadalafil in individuals with elevated aminotransferases more than 3 times the upper limit of normal. Obtain liver function tests prior to initiation of macitentan; tadalafil and repeat during treatment as clinically indicated.
    Headline
    renal failure, renal impairment
    General Information
    Avoid use of macitentan; tadalafil in individuals with severe renal impairment (CrCl less than 30 mL/minute) or renal failure, including dialysis-dependence. There is limited experience in this population and renal impairment increases tadalafil exposure. Dialysis does not influence the clearance of tadalafil.
    Headline
    anemia
    General Information
    Initiation of macitentan; tadalafil is not recommended in individuals with severe anemia. Monitor hemoglobin prior to starting macitentan; tadalafil treatment and repeat during treatment as clinically indicated. Decreases in hemoglobin and hematocrit have been observed with macitentan; tadalafil.
    Headline
    crowded optic disc, inherited retinal dystrophy, non-arteritic anterior ischemic optic neuropathy
    General Information
    Individuals with inherited retinal dystrophy, including retinitis pigmentosa, were not included in clinical trials with macitentan; tadalafil and use in this population is not recommended. Non-arteritic anterior ischemic optic neuropathy (NAION) has been reported postmarketing in temporal association with the use of phosphodiesterase 5 inhibitors, including tadalafil. In most cases, underlying anatomic or vascular risk factors for the development of NAION were present, including low cup to disc ratio (crowded optic disc), age older than 50 years, diabetes, hypertension, coronary artery disease, hyperlipidemia, and smoking. There is an increased risk of NAION in individuals who have already experienced NAION in 1 eye.
    Headline
    cardiovascular disease, hypotension, left ventricular outflow tract obstruction
    General Information
    Before initiating macitentan; tadalafil therapy, carefully consider whether individuals with underlying cardiovascular disease may be adversely affected by its vasodilatory properties. Individuals with pre-existing hypotension, autonomic dysfunction, or left ventricular outflow tract obstruction may be particularly sensitive to the actions of vasodilators. Additionally, peripheral edema and fluid retention are known clinical consequences of pulmonary arterial hypertension and known effects of endothelin receptor antagonists (ERAs). Individuals with underlying left ventricular dysfunction may be at particular risk of developing significant fluid retention after initiation of ERA treatment.
    Headline
    pulmonary veno-occlusive disease
    General Information
    Macitentan; tadalafil use is not recommended in individuals with pulmonary veno-occlusive disease (PVOD) since there are no clinical data on use in this population. Pulmonary vasodilators may significantly worsen the cardiovascular status of individuals with PVOD. Should signs of pulmonary edema occur during macitentan; tadalafil therapy, consider the possibility of associated pulmonary PVOD. If confirmed, discontinue macitentan; tadalafil.
    Headline
    leukemia, multiple myeloma, penile angulation, Peyronie disease, priapism, sickle cell disease
    General Information
    Prolonged erections more than 4 hours and priapism (painful erections more than 6 hours) have been reported with phosphodiesterase 5 inhibitors like tadalafil. Individuals with an anatomical deformation of the penis, such as penile angulation, cavernosal fibrosis, or Peyronie disease, or with conditions predisposing to priapism, such as sickle cell disease, leukemia, or multiple myeloma, are at an increased risk.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Macitentan; tadalafil is contraindicated in pregnancy. Based on animal reproduction studies, macitentan may cause fetal harm, including birth defects and fetal death. Macitentan was teratogenic in rats and rabbits at all doses tested, with findings including cardiovascular and mandibular arch abnormalities, reduced pup survival, and impaired fertility in male offspring. Human pregnancy data for macitentan and other endothelin receptor antagonists are limited and are insufficient to establish the risk of adverse fetal or neonatal outcomes. Available human pregnancy data for tadalafil have not identified a drug-associated risk of major birth defects, miscarriage, or other adverse pregnancy or fetal outcomes. In animal studies, tadalafil did not cause adverse developmental effects in rats or mice at exposures up to 7 times the maximum recommended human dose. Pulmonary arterial hypertension during pregnancy is associated with increased morbidity and mortality for the pregnant patient and fetus, including heart failure, stroke, spontaneous abortion, intrauterine growth restriction, premature labor, and preterm birth. Advise patients of the potential fetal risks associated with macitentan; tadalafil. If pregnancy occurs, discontinue macitentan; tadalafil as soon as possible.
    Headline
    embryo-fetal toxicity, people who can cause pregnancy in others, people who may become pregnant, reproductive risk
    General Information
    Counsel people who may become pregnant and people who can cause pregnancy in others about the reproductive risk and embryo-fetal toxicity associated with macitentan; tadalafil. Macitentan; tadalafil may cause fetal harm. Verify pregnancy status prior to treatment initiation. People who may become pregnant should use effective contraception prior to treatment initiation, during treatment, and for 1 month after discontinuation of treatment. Advise individuals to contact their care team for pregnancy testing immediately if menses is delayed or pregnancy is suspected. If pregnancy occurs, discontinue macitentan; tadalafil and discuss the potential risks to the pregnancy and fetus. Based on animal data, macitentan may impair male fertility. It is unknown whether effects on fertility are reversible.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Advise patients to avoid breast-feeding while taking macitentan; tadalafil due to the potential for serious adverse reactions. There are no data on the presence of macitentan, tadalafil, and their metabolites in human milk, the effects on the breastfed child, or the effects on milk production. Macitentan is more than 99% bound to plasma proteins, so the amount in milk is likely to be low. Macitentan and its active metabolites should be completely eliminated from milk in 10 days. Tadalafil should be completely eliminated from milk in 4 days.

Acetaminophen; Chlorpheniramine; Dextromethorphan; Phenylephrine: (Major) Avoid use of sympathomimetic agents with macitentan. Sympathomimetics counteract the medications used to stabilize pulmonary hypertension, including macitentan. Sympathomimetics can increase blood pressure, increase heart rate, and may cause vasoconstriction resulting in chest pain and shortness of breath in these patients. Adagrasib: (Major) Avoid coadministration of macitentan with adagrasib due to the risk of increased macitentan exposure and adverse effects. Consider alternative treatment options for pulmonary hypertension if treatment with adagrasib is necessary. Macitentan is a CYP3A substrate and adagrasib is a strong CYP3A inhibitor. Coadministration with another strong CYP3A inhibitor increased macitentan exposure by approximately 2-fold. (Major) Avoid coadministration of tadalafil and adagrasib for the treatment of pulmonary hypertension. For the treatment of erectile dysfunction, do not exceed 10 mg tadalafil within 72 hours of adagrasib for the 'as needed' dose or 2.5 mg daily for the 'once-daily' dose. Tadalafil is metabolized predominantly by CYP3A. Potent inhibitors of CYP3A, such as adagrasib, may reduce tadalafil clearance. Increased systemic exposure to tadalafil may result in increased associated adverse events including hypotension, syncope, visual changes, and prolonged erection.
Alfuzosin: (Moderate) Due to the potential for symptomatic hypotension, patients should be stable on alfuzosin therapy before initiating therapy with the lowest recommended dose of tadalafil. Conversely, patients already receiving an optimized dose of tadalafil should be started on the lowest dose of alfuzosin; increases in the alpha-blocker dose should be done in a stepwise fashion. When tadalafil is used for benign prostatic hypertrophy (BPH), discontinue alfuzosin therapy at least 1 day prior to initiating tadalafil therapy. Other variables, such as intravascular volume depletion, concurrent antihypertensive therapy, or evidence of hemodynamic instability with alpha-blocker monotherapy, may affect the safety of concomitant use of tadalafil and alfuzosin.
Alpha-blockers: (Moderate) Concomitant use of tadalafil and an alpha-blocker may increase the risk for symptomatic hypotension. To minimize the risk for harm if concomitant use is necessary, optimize the dose of one medication before starting the other, initiate therapy at the lowest recommended dose, and monitor blood pressure. Both medications are vasodilators and may have an additive blood pressure lowering effect when used together. In healthy volunteer studies, maximal placebo-subtracted decreases in systolic blood pressure with combination therapy ranged from 1 mmHg to 9.8 mmHg.

Aprepitant, Fosaprepitant: (Moderate) Use caution if tadalafil and aprepitant, fosaprepitant are used concurrently and monitor for an increase in tadalafil-related adverse effects for several days after administration of a multi-day aprepitant regimen. Tadalafil is a CYP3A4 substrate. Aprepitant, when administered as a 3-day oral regimen (125 mg/80 mg/80 mg), is a moderate CYP3A4 inhibitor and inducer and may increase plasma concentrations of tadalafil. For example, a 5-day oral aprepitant regimen increased the AUC of another CYP3A4 substrate, midazolam (single dose), by 2.3-fold on day 1 and by 3.3-fold on day 5. After a 3-day oral aprepitant regimen, the AUC of midazolam (given on days 1, 4, 8, and 15) increased by 25% on day 4, and then decreased by 19% and 4% on days 8 and 15, respectively. As a single 125 mg or 40 mg oral dose, the inhibitory effect of aprepitant on CYP3A4 is weak, with the AUC of midazolam increased by 1.5-fold and 1.2-fold, respectively. After administration, fosaprepitant is rapidly converted to aprepitant and shares many of the same drug interactions. However, as a single 150 mg intravenous dose, fosaprepitant only weakly inhibits CYP3A4 for a duration of 2 days; there is no evidence of CYP3A4 induction. Fosaprepitant 150 mg IV as a single dose increased the AUC of midazolam (given on days 1 and 4) by approximately 1.8-fold on day 1; there was no effect on day 4. Less than a 2-fold increase in the midazolam AUC is not considered clinically important.

Description
  Text
  Macitentan; tadalafil is an oral combination of an endothelin receptor antagonist and phosphodiesterase 5 inhibitor indicated for the treatment of pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH, WHO Group I) in adults of WHO functional class II to III. Individually, macitentan reduces the risk of clinical worsening events and hospitalization, and tadalafil improves exercise ability. Macitentan; tadalafil may cause embryo-fetal toxicity. For females of childbearing potential who are receiving macitentan; tadalafil, routine pregnancy testing and effective contraception are required. Additionally, like other endothelin receptor antagonists, macitentan may have an adverse effect on spermatogenesis and adversely affect male fertility. Macitentan; tadalafil has vasodilatory properties that may result in transient decreases in blood pressure. Macitentan; tadalafil is contraindicated in persons who are using nitrate/nitrite therapy, either regularly or intermittently, or guanylate cyclase (GC) stimulators therapy, such as riociguat. Do not use nitrates within 48 hours of the last macitentan; tadalafil dose. Tadalafil may potentiate the hypotensive effects of nitrates or GC stimulators.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Macitentan; tadalafil reduces the risk of clinical worsening events and hospitalizations and improves exercise capacity in persons with pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH) via 2 distinct mechanisms.
Macitentan: Macitentan is a dual endothelin (ET) receptor antagonist. In persons with PAH, plasma ET-1 concentrations are elevated and involved in the production of vasoconstriction, fibrosis, proliferation, vascular hypertrophy, inflammation, and organ damage. Macitentan prevents the binding of ET-1 to ETA and ETB receptors on human pulmonary arterial smooth muscle cells and does so with high affinity and sustained occupancy. The active metabolite of macitentan is estimated to be approximately 20% as potent in antagonizing the ET receptors as macitentan in vitro. In clinical trials, subjects treated with macitentan monotherapy achieved a median reduction of 37% in pulmonary vascular resistance and an increase of 0.6 L/minute/m2 in cardiac index compared to placebo.
Tadalafil: Tadalafil is a selective inhibitor of phosphodiesterase (PDE) 5, the enzyme responsible for the degradation of cyclic guanosine monophosphate (cGMP). PAH is associated with impaired release of nitric oxide by the vascular endothelium and consequent reduction of cGMP concentrations in the pulmonary vascular smooth muscle. PDE5 is the predominant phosphodiesterase in the pulmonary vasculature. Inhibition of PDE5 by tadalafil increases the concentrations of cGMP, resulting in relaxation of pulmonary vascular smooth muscle cells and vasodilation of the pulmonary vascular bed. PDE5 is an enzyme found in corpus cavernosum, vascular smooth muscle, visceral smooth muscle, skeletal muscle, platelets, kidney, lung, and cerebellum. The effect of tadalafil is more potent on PDE5 than on other phosphodiesterases. Tadalafil is more than 10,000-fold more potent for PDE5 than for PDE1, PDE2, PDE4, and PDE7, enzymes which are found in the heart, brain, blood vessels, liver, and other organs. Tadalafil is more than 10,000-fold more potent for PDE5 than for PDE3, an enzyme found in the heart and blood vessels. This selectivity for PDE5 over PDE3 is important because PDE3 is an enzyme involved in cardiac contractility. Additionally, tadalafil is approximately 700-fold more potent for PDE5 than for PDE6, an enzyme which is found in the retina and is responsible for phototransduction. Tadalafil is also more than 9,000-fold more potent for PDE5 than for PDE8, PDE9, and PDE10.

  Macitentan; tadalafil is administered orally.
Macitentan: Macitentan and its active metabolite are highly bound to plasma proteins (more than 99%), primarily to albumin and to a lesser extent to alpha-1-acid glycoprotein. In healthy subjects, the apparent Vd for macitentan and its active metabolite was 50 L and 40 L, respectively. Macitentan is metabolized primarily by oxidative depropylation of the sulfamide to form the pharmacologically active metabolite. This reaction is dependent on the cytochrome P450 (CYP) system, mainly CYP3A with minor contributions of CYP2C8, CYP2C9, and CYP2C19. At steady-state in persons with pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH), the systemic exposure to the active metabolite is 3-times the exposure to macitentan and is expected to contribute approximately 40% of the total pharmacologic activity. Approximately 50% of radiolabeled macitentan was eliminated in the urine, but none was in the form of unchanged drug or the active metabolite; about 24% was recovered from feces. The elimination half-life of macitentan and its active metabolite is 16 hours and 48 hours, respectively. The pharmacokinetics of macitentan are dose proportional over a range from 1 to 30 mg after once daily administration.
Tadalafil: At therapeutic concentrations, 94% of tadalafil in plasma is bound to proteins. The mean apparent Vd of tadalafil after oral administration is 77 L. Over a dose range of 2.5 to 20 mg, tadalafil exposure increases proportionally with dose in healthy subjects. In PAH subjects administered between 20 and 40 mg of tadalafil, an approximately 50% higher AUC was observed, indicating a less than proportional increase in exposure over the entire dose range of 2.5 to 40 mg. During tadalafil 20 and 40 mg once daily dosing, steady-state plasma concentrations were attained within 5 days, and exposure was approximately 30% higher than after a single dose. The average tadalafil exposure at steady-state after 40 mg was 26% higher in subjects with PAH compared to healthy subjects, suggesting reduced clearance in individuals with PAH. Tadalafil is predominately metabolized by CYP3A to a catechol metabolite. The catechol metabolite undergoes extensive methylation and glucuronidation to form the methylcatechol and methylcatechol glucuronide conjugate, respectively. The major circulating metabolite is the methylcatechol glucuronide. Methylcatechol concentrations are less than 10% of glucuronide concentrations. In vitro data suggests that metabolites are not expected to be pharmacologically active at observed metabolite concentrations. After a 40 mg dose, the mean oral clearance for tadalafil is 3.4 L/hour. In persons with PAH not receiving concomitant bosentan, the mean oral clearance for tadalafil is 1.6 L/hour. Tadalafil is excreted predominantly as metabolites, mainly in the feces (approximately 61% of the dose) and to a lesser extent in the urine (approximately 36% of the dose). The mean effective half-life of tadalafil is 11 hours in healthy subjects.
 
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes and drug transporters: CYP2C8, CYP2C9, CYP2C19, CYP3A
Macitentan and tadalafil are CYP3A substrates. The metabolism of macitentan to its active metabolite is catalyzed mainly by CYP3A, with minor contributions by CYP2C8, CYP2C9, and CYP2C19. Tadalafil is predominantly metabolized by CYP3A.

Dosage Adjustment Guidelines
  Hepatic Impairment
  Mild to moderate hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh Class A or B): No dose adjustment needed.
Elevated aminotransferases more than 3 times the upper limit of normal at baseline: Do not initiate macitentan; tadalafil therapy.
Severe hepatic cirrhosis (Child-Pugh Class C): Avoid use.
  Renal Impairment
  CrCl 30 mL/minute or more: No dosage adjustment needed.
CrCl 15 to 29 mL/minute: Avoid use.
 
Hemodialysis
Macitentan; tadalafil use is not recommended in persons undergoing dialysis.
