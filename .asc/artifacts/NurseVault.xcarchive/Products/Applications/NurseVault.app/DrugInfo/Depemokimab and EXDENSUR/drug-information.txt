    helminth infection
    General Information
    Treat individuals with a pre-existing helminth infection prior to initiating therapy with depemokimab. If an individual becomes infected while receiving depemokimab therapy and does not respond to anti-helminth treatment, discontinue depemokimab therapy until the infection resolves. It is unknown if depemokimab will influence the body's response against parasitic infections. Eosinophils may be involved in the immunological response to some helminth infections. Individuals with known parasitic infections were excluded from clinical trials.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    There are no data on the use of depemokimab during pregnancy to evaluate for a drug-associated risk of major birth defects, miscarriage, or adverse outcomes. Endogenous IgG antibodies and monoclonal antibodies cross the placenta as pregnancy progresses and peak in the third trimester. The presence of the YTE modification on placental transfer is unknown. The YTE modification may lead to prolonged and increased exposure to the neonate exposed in utero, and the clinical impact is unknown and should be considered. Animal studies have shown no treatment-related embryo-fetal or postnatal development effects. There are risks to the pregnant individual and fetus associated with poorly controlled asthma during pregnancy, including increased risk of preeclampsia for the pregnant individual, prematurity, low birth weight, and small gestational age for the neonate. Asthma control should be monitored during pregnancy and treatment adjusted as necessary to maintain optimal control. Pregnant individuals exposed to depemokimab or their health care providers should report exposure by calling 1-888-825-5249.
    Headline

    General Information
    There is no data on the presence of depemokimab in human or animal milk, its effects on the breast-fed child, or its effects on milk production. Human IgG is present in human milk.  If depemokimab is present in human milk, the effects of local gastrointestinal exposure and the extent of systemic exposure in the breast-fed child are unknown. Consider the benefits of breast-feeding, the patient's clinical need for treatment, and any potential adverse effects on the breast-fed child from the medication or from the patient's underlying medical condition.

Description
  Text
  Depemokimab is an ultra long-acting interleukin-5 (IL-5) antagonist monoclonal antibody allowing for twice yearly dosing. It is used as add-on maintenance treatment of severe asthma characterized by an eosinophilic phenotype. Guidelines identify depemokimab as an add-on anti-IL-5 option for severe eosinophilic asthma in individuals 12 years and older whose asthma is uncontrolled despite optimized high-dose inhaled corticosteroids (ICS) and long acting beta-agonists (LABA) therapy, or who require maintenance oral corticosteroids (OCS), after modifiable factors are addressed. During clinical trials, individuals receiving depemokimab demonstrated sustained exacerbation reduction with 2 doses per year versus placebo. Depemokimab resulted in significant reductions of 58% and 48% in the annualized rate of clinically significant asthma exacerbations over 52 weeks from the SWIFT-1 and SWIFT-2 trials, respectively. Additionally, fewer individuals experienced exacerbations requiring hospitalization or ED visits in the depemokimab groups (1.1% vs. 8%; 4% vs. 10%). There was no clear difference in the secondary end points of lung function (pre-bronchodilator FEV1) or patient-reported outcomes in the clinical trials. The most common adverse effects were upper respiratory tract infection, allergic rhinitis, influenza, arthralgia, and pharyngitis. Depemokimab was FDA-approved in December 2025.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Depemokimab is an IL-5 antagonist monoclonal antibody. It binds to IL-5 with high affinity and inhibits IL-5 activity by binding to the alpha chain of the IL-5 receptor complex expressed on the cell surface. Depemokimab contains a triple amino acid substitution (YTE) in the fragment crystallizable (Fc) region that increases binding to the neonatal Fc receptor, thereby prolonging its elimination half-life. These properties allow for every 6-month dosing. IL-5 is a major cytokine for the growth and differentiation, recruitment, activation, and survival of eosinophils. Inflammation is an important component in the pathogenesis of asthma. Multiple cell types and mediators are involved in inflammation. Depemokimab, by blocking IL-5 signaling, reduces the production and survival of eosinophils; however, the mechanism of action in asthma has not been definitively established.
Pharmacodynamics
In clinical trials, individuals 12 and older, receiving a 100 mg dose of depemokimab subcutaneously every 6 months for 52 weeks had a reduction in blood eosinophils to a geometric mean count of 57 cells/microL (95% CI: 51, 63). This corresponded to a geometric mean reduction of 79% (95% CI: 75.8, 81.8) compared to placebo. The reduction was observed within 2 weeks of treatment and was maintained throughout the treatment period.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Subcutaneous Route
    Description
    The pharmacokinetics of depemokimab were approximately dose-proportional in individuals with asthma following subcutaneous administration over a dose range of 0.1-times the recommended dose to 3-times the recommended dose. The mean (SD) model-based estimated trough concentration of depemokimab at Week 26 was approximately 1.3 (0.44) mcg/mL. The mean (SD) model-based estimated average steady-state plasma concentration over a single dosing interval was 6.16 (1.62) mcg/mL. Depemokimab median Tmax was approximately 14 days. There was no accumulation following repeated subcutaneous administration once every 6 months.

Administration
  General Information

  Route Specific Administrations
    Administration Details
      Name
      Subcutaneous Administration
      Description
      For subcutaneous administration only.
Depemokimab should be administered by a health care provider.
Inject into the upper arm, thigh, or abdomen, avoiding 2 inches around the navel. Do not give injections into areas where the skin is tender, bruised, red, or hard.
Missed dose: If a dose is missed, administer the missed dose as soon as possible and resume every 6 month injection schedule from the date of when the missed dose was given.
 
Preparation
Allow the depemokimab prefilled syringe to sit at room temperature for 30 minutes prior to injection. Do not warm any other way.
Do not shake.
Do not expel the air bubble prior to administration.
Storage: Depemokimab may be left at room temperature out of the carton for up to 8 hours. Depemokimab may be removed from the refrigerator and kept in the unopened carton, protected from light for up to 7 days at room temperature up to 30 degrees C (86 degrees F).
 
Administration of single-dose prefilled syringe
Remove the gray needle cap from the syringe. Do not handle the syringe by the white plunger.
Inject within 5 minutes after removing the gray needle cap.
Gently pinch the skin around the clean injection site.
Hold the middle of the syringe and insert the entire needle into the pinched skin at a 45-degree angle.
Slowly push down on the white plunger with your thumb to inject the full dose.
Slowly lift your thumb. This will allow the white plunger to come up and the needle to automatically retract into the needle guard.
Dispose of the used syringe.
