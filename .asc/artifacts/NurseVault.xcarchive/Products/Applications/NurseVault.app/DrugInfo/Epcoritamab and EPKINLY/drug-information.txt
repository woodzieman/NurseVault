          Cycle 1: 0.16 mg on day 1 (step-up dose 1), 0.8 mg on day 8 (step-up dose 2), and 48 mg on days 15 and 22 as a subcutaneous injection. Cycles 2 and 3: 48 mg subcutaneously on days 1, 8, 15, and 22 repeated every 28 days. Cycles 4 to 9: 48 mg subcutaneously on days 1 and 15 repeated every 28 days. Cycles 10 and beyond: 48 mg subcutaneously once every 28 days until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity. Ensure patients are well-hydrated, provide Pneumocystis jirovecii pneumonia (PJP) prophylaxis, and consider antiviral prophylaxis to prevent herpes simplex and herpes zoster during treatment. Premedicate with corticosteroids (dexamethasone 15 mg [preferred], prednisolone 100 mg, or equivalent) PO or IV, diphenhydramine 50 mg PO or IV or equivalent, and acetaminophen 650 to 1,000 mg PO at 30 to 120 minutes prior to each dose in cycle 1; continue corticosteroids daily for 3 days after each dose. In cycles 2 and beyond, premedicate with corticosteroids prior to the dose and continuing daily for 3 days after the dose (as in cycle 1) if the patient experienced grade 2 or 3 cytokine release syndrome (CRS) with the previous dose; continue this until epcoritamab is given without grade 2 or higher CRS. Restart premedications in patients who repeat doses within the step-up dosing schedule following a dose delay. The overall response rate was 63.1% (complete response, 38.9%) in patients with relapsed, progressive, and/or refractory mature B-cell lymphoma who received a median of 5 treatment cycles of epcoritamab in a dose expansion cohort of a phase 1/2 (EPCORE NHL-1) trial (n = 157). At a median follow-up time of 10.7 months, the median time to response was 1.4 months and the median duration of response was 12 months. Additionally, the median progression-free survival time was 4.4 months and the median overall survival time was not reached (range, 0.3 to 17.91 months). In this trial, patients had received a median of 3 prior systemic therapies; 19.7% of patients had received a prior autologous stem-cell transplant. Most patients had DLBCL (88.5%) including transformed DLBCL (28.8%); 5.7% had high-grade B-cell lymphoma, not otherwise specified.

    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components.
    Headline
    cytokine release syndrome
    General Information
    Cytokine release syndrome (CRS) is a potentially fatal reaction that has been reported with epcoritamab use. Assess whether hospitalization or outpatient monitoring is appropriate based on comorbidities or other situational factors for the first full 48 mg dosage on cycle 1 day 15 for patients with diffuse large B-cell lymphoma or high-grade B-cell lymphoma and on cycle 1 day 22 for patients with follicular lymphoma. During outpatient monitoring, patients should remain in proximity to a healthcare facility that can assess and manage CRS. To reduce the risk of CRS, ensure adequate hydration, administer epcoritamab according to a step-up dosing schedule, and give pretreatment medications. Hold epcoritamab until CRS resolves or permanently discontinue therapy based on toxicity severity; evaluate the need for hospitalization, supportive care, and further management (using current practice guidelines) in patients with severe CRS. Advise patients who experience CRS or other adverse reactions that impair consciousness during epcoritamab therapy to refrain from driving or operating machinery or performing other dangerous duties or activities requiring coordination and concentration until the toxicity resolves.
    Headline
    activities requiring coordination and concentration, driving or operating machinery, immune effector cell-associated neurotoxicity syndrome
    General Information
    Neurotoxicity including immune effector cell-associated neurotoxicity syndrome (ICANS) has been reported with epcoritamab therapy; serious or fatal cases may occur. Neurotoxicity/ICANS may occur concurrently with cytokine release syndrome (CRS), following CRS, or in the absence of CRS. Advise patients who experience CRS, neurotoxicity, or other adverse reactions that impair consciousness during epcoritamab therapy to refrain from driving or operating machinery or performing other dangerous duties or activities requiring coordination and concentration until the toxicity resolves. Hold epcoritamab until ICANS resolves or permanently discontinue therapy based on toxicity severity; evaluate the need for hospitalization, supportive care, and further management (using current practice guidelines) in patients with severe neurotoxicity or ICANS. Consider seizure prophylaxis with a non-sedating anti-seizure medication for patients who develop any grade neurotoxicity.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Epcoritamab may cause fetal harm if administered during pregnancy based on its mechanism of action. Pregnant patients should be apprised of the potential hazard to the fetus. There are no available data with epcoritamab use during human pregnancy; animal reproductive and developmental toxicity studies have not been conducted. Epcoritamab causes T-cell activation and cytokine release and immune activation may compromise pregnancy maintenance. Human immunoglobulin (IgG) is known to cross the placenta; therefore, epcoritamab may be transmitted from the mother to the developing fetus. It may also cause B-cell depletion and lymphopenia in neonates and infants exposed to epcoritamab in-utero.
    Headline
    contraception requirements, pregnancy testing, reproductive risk
    General Information
    Counsel patients about the reproductive risk and contraception requirements during epcoritamab treatment. Pregnancy testing should be performed prior to starting epcoritamab in patients of reproductive potential. These patients should use effective contraception during and for 4 months after the last epcoritamab dose.
    Headline
    infection
    General Information
    Do not administer epcoritamab in patients who have an active infection. Severe and fatal infections including opportunistic infections have been reported with epcoritamab therapy. Provide Pneumocystis jirovecii pneumonia (PJP) prophylaxis during epcoritamab therapy and consider antiviral prophylaxis to prevent herpes simplex and herpes zoster. Consider monitoring immunoglobulin levels during treatment and administering immunoglobulin replacement treatment as appropriate. Therapy interruption or permanent discontinuation may be necessary in patients who develop an infection.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    There is no information regarding the presence of epcoritamab in human milk or the effect on the breastfed child or milk production. Because human IgG is present in human milk, and there is potential for epcoritamab absorption leading to B-cell depletion in the breastfed child, advise patients to avoid breast-feeding during treatment and for 4 months after the last dose.

Interactions
Monitor for toxicity and adjust the dose as needed for CYP substrates, especially during the first month of epcoritamab therapy and during and after cytokine release syndrome. Epcoritamab causes a cytokine release that may suppress CYP enzyme activity.

    Headline
    hypogammaglobulinemia, infection, laryngitis, pharyngitis, progressive multifocal leukoencephalopathy, rhinitis, sepsis, sinusitis
    General Information
    Serious cases of infection including opportunistic infections occurred in 15% of patients with relapsed or refractory large B-cell lymphoma (LBCL) and in 40% of those with follicular lymphoma (FL) treated with epcoritamab monotherapy in 1 clinical trial; some cases were fatal. The incidence of serious infections in patients with FL treated with epcoritamab, lenalidomide, and rituximab was 28% in a separate clinical trial. The most common serious infections in epcoritamab-treated patients with LBCL were sepsis (4.5%) and pneumonia (3.2%); COVID-19 infection, urinary tract infection, and upper respiratory tract infection were each reported in less than 10% of these patients. COVID-19 (40%; grade 3 or 4, 19%), upper respiratory infection (29%; grade 3, 2%), pneumonia (17%; grade 3, 13%), urinary tract infection (13%; grade 3, 5%), and herpesvirus infection (12%; grade 3, 1.6%) were reported in patients with FL who received epcoritamab; cellulitis, cytomegalovirus infection, and sepsis were each reported in less than 10% of patients in this cohort. The most common serious infections in patients treated with epcoritamab, lenalidomide, and rituximab were pneumonia (15%), COVI-19 infection (7%), opportunistic infection including CMV and herpesvirus infection (5%), and upper respiratory infections (3.3%). Cases of sinusitis, laryngitis, nasopharyngitis, pharyngitis, rhinitis, rhinovirus infection, and tonsillitis were included with upper respiratory infections in some trials. The term pneumonia included infectious pleural effusion and infective exacerbation of bronchiectasis in 1 clinical trial. Progressive multifocal leukoencephalopathy (PML) has occurred in patients treated with epcoritamab, including fatal cases. PML was reported in 0.4% of patients across clinical trials treated with epcoritamab (n = 3,072), including in the first-line treatment setting. Of the 11 cases of PML, 6 resulted in fatal outcomes; one case was unresolved at the time of death. Avoid epcoritamab with active infection; provide Pneumocystis jirovecii pneumonia (PJP) prophylaxis and consider prophylaxis against herpes virus to prevent herpes zoster reactivation prior to initiating epcoritamab. Hypogammaglobulinemia has been reported in other clinical trial experiences; consider monitoring immunoglobulin levels during treatment and administration of immunoglobulin treatment as appropriate. If infection occurs, therapy interruption or permanent discontinuation may be necessary based on severity.

    Headline
    anemia, leukopenia, lymphopenia, neutropenia, thrombocytopenia
    General Information
    Myelosuppression has been reported with epcoritamab therapy including lymphopenia, anemia, leukopenia, neutropenia, and thrombocytopenia. A decrease from baseline in lymphocytes (86% to 94%; grade 3 or 4, 62% to 82%), hemoglobin (59% to 66%; grade 3 or 4, 7% to 12%), leukocytes (58% or less; grade 3 or 4, 22% or less), and platelets (48% to 50%; grade 3 or 4, 8% to 12%) were reported in patients who received epcoritamab as monotherapy or in combination with lenalidomide and rituximab in 2 clinical trials. Decreases from baseline in neutrophils occurred in 50% to 55% (grade 3 or 4, 30% to 32%) of patients treated with epcoritamab monotherapy compared to 86% (grade 3 or 4, 67%) of patients treated with epcoritamab in combination with lenalidomide and rituximab. Similarly, febrile neutropenia was reported in 2.5% (grade 4, 0.6%) to 3.1% of patients treated with epcoritamab monotherapy while the incidence was increased (6%; grade 4, 2.1%) in patients treated with the combination of epcoritamab, lenalidomide, and rituximab. Interruption or discontinuation of therapy may be necessary in patients who develop severe myelosuppression. Consider the prophylactic use of granulocyte colony-stimulating factors.

    hyperkalemia, hypokalemia, hypomagnesemia, hyponatremia, hypophosphatemia
    General Information
    Electrolyte abnormalities including hyperkalemia, hypokalemia, hypomagnesemia, hyponatremia, and hypophosphatemia have been reported in patients treated with epcoritamab. In 2 clinical trials, patients who received epcoritamab as monotherapy or in combination with lenalidomide and rituximab reported a decrease from baseline in sodium (43% to 56%; grade 3 or 4, 1.6% to 4.1%), potassium (20% to 41%; grade 3 or 4, 3.1% to 9%), and magnesium (20% to 31%; grade 3 or 4, 0.8% or less). A decrease from baseline in serum phosphate (49% to 56%) occurred in patients treated with epcoritamab monotherapy or in combination with lenalidomide and rituximab in 2 clinical trials. An increase from baseline in potassium (21%; grade 3 or 4, 1.3%) was also reported in single agent epcoritamab-treated patients in 1 of these trials.

Description
  Text
  Epcoritamab-bysp is a bispecific CD20-directed CD3 T-cell engager indicated for the treatment of adult patients with relapsed or refractory diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL) not otherwise specified (including DLBCL arising from indolent lymphoma), high-grade B-cell lymphoma after 2 or more lines of systemic therapy, as monotherapy for the treatment of relapsed or refractory follicular lymphoma after 2 or more lines of systemic therapy, and in combination with lenalidomide and rituximab fo the treatment of relapsed or refractory follicular lymphoma. Epcoritamab has black box warnings for cytokine release syndrome and immune effector cell-associated neurotoxicity syndrome.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Epcoritamab is a bispecific T-cell engaging (BiTE) monoclonal antibody that binds to CD20 expressed on lymphoma cells and CD3 expressed on the surface of T-cells. It is a humanized immunoglobulin G1 (IgG1) antibody produced from Chinese Hamster Ovary cells using recombinant DNA technology. The binding of T-cells to lymphoma cells triggers a signaling cascade leading to the release of pro-inflammatory cytokines and proliferation of T-cells ultimately resulting in the lysis of CD20-positive B-cells. The combination of epcoritamab and rituximab resulted in natural killer (NK) cell mediated antibody-dependent cytotoxicity (ADCC) and T-cell mediated cytotoxicity in nonclinical studies.

    The exposure of epcoritamab increases more than proportionally over a dosage range of 1.5 to 60 mg (0.03125 to 1.25 times the recommended dosage).     Pharmacodynamics
Circulating B-cells decreased to undetectable levels (less than 10 cells/microliter) by cycle 1, day 15 after administration of the approved dose of epcoritamab in patients with detectable B-cells at baseline, which was sustained throughout treatment. Plasma cytokine concentrations (IL-2, IL-6, IL-10, TNF-alpha, and interferon-gamma) increased within 24 hours after the first dose of epcoritamab on cycle 1, day 1; maximum cytokine levels were generally reached after the first 48-mg dose, and returned to baseline prior to the second 48-mg dose.
  Text
  Epcoritamab is administered subcutaneously. It has an apparent total volume of distribution of 25.6 L (coefficient of variation (CV), 82%). Following the administration of epcoritamab 48 mg subcutaneously, the mean elimination half-life was about 22 days (CV, 58%) and the mean apparent total clearance was about 0.53 L/day (CV, 40%) at the end of cycle 3. Epcoritamab is metabolized into small peptides via catabolic pathways.
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes and drug transporters: CYP substrates
Epcoritamab may cause a cytokine release that suppresses CYP enzyme activity and which may increase the exposure of CYP substrates. Increased exposure of CYP450 substrates is more likely to occur after the first dose and for up to 14 days after the first 48-mg dose on day 15 of cycle 1, or if cytokine release syndrome occurs during or after treatment.

  For storage information, see the specific product information within the How Supplied section.
