      Routes

        Route Age Groups
          Heading
          Adults
          Dosage Description
          6 mg/kg adjusted body weight IV every 3 weeks until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity. Premedicate with dexamethasone 10 mg IV or equivalent, diphenhydramine 25 to 50 mg IV or PO, acetaminophen 325 mg to 650 mg IV or PO, and a 5HT3 antagonist IV or PO at least 30 minutes prior to each dose of mirvetuximab soravtansine. Topical ophthalmic steroids and lubricating eye drops should be used. Coadministration of certain drugs may need to be avoided or dosage adjustments may be necessary; review drug interactions. In a multicenter, randomized, open-label phase 3 trial (MIRASOL), treatment with mirvetuximab soravtansine significantly improved median progression-free survival (5.4 months vs. 4 months), overall survival (16.5 months vs. 12.7 months), and overall response rate (ORR) (42% vs. 16%; complete response [CR], 5% vs. 0%) compared with investigator's choice of chemotherapy (paclitaxel, pegylated liposomal doxorubicin, or topotecan) in patients with FR alpha positive, platinum-resistant epithelial ovarian, fallopian tube, or primary peritoneal cancer. In a single-arm study (SORAYA), treatment with mirvetuximab soravtansine resulted in an ORR of 32% (CR, 5%) in patients with FR-alpha positive, platinum resistant epithelial ovarian, fallopian tube, or primary peritoneal cancer for a median duration of 6.9 months.

    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components.
    Headline
    peripheral neuropathy
    General Information
    Use mirvetuximab soravtansine with caution in patients with a history of neuropathy. Peripheral neuropathy has been commonly reported in patients treated with mirvetuximab soravtansine. Monitor patients for signs and symptoms of peripheral neuropathy including paresthesias, tingling/burning sensations, neuropathic pain, muscle weakness, or dysesthesia. An interruption of therapy, dose reduction, or discontinuation of therapy may be necessary depending on the severity of peripheral neuropathy.
    Headline
    contact lenses, dry eye disease, keratitis, ocular toxicity
    General Information
    Use mirvetuximab soravtansine with caution in patients with a history of keratitis, ulcerative keratitis, or severe dry eye disease. Mirvetuximab soravtansine can cause ocular toxicity including visual impairment, keratopathy/keratitis, corneal disorders, dry eye, photophobia, eye pain, and uveitis. Patients should avoid the use of contact lenses during treatment with mirvetuximab soravtansine unless directed by a healthcare provider as this increases the risk for keratitis/keratopathy. Refer patients to an eye care professional for an ophthalmic exam including visual acuity and slit lamp exam at baseline, every other cycle for the first 8 cycles, and as clinically indicated. Patients who develop an onset of new eye symptoms or visual disturbance such as ocular pain, inflammation, lacrimation, light sensitivity, blurred vision, and/or red eye should be evaluated and managed appropriately; an interruption or discontinuation of therapy may be necessary. Premedication and use of lubricating and ophthalmic topical steroid eye drops during treatment are recommended. An interruption of therapy, dose reduction, or discontinuation of therapy may be necessary if ocular toxicity occurs, depending on the severity and persistence.
    Headline
    pulmonary disease
    General Information
    Use mirvetuximab soravtansine with caution in patients who have a history of pre-existing pulmonary disease; severe pneumonitis/interstitial lung disease has been reported in patients treated with mirvetuximab soravtansine. Monitor patients for new or worsening respiratory symptoms including fever, cough, or dyspnea. Immediately interrupt mirvetuximab soravtansine therapy in patients with new or worsening respiratory symptoms. A dose reduction or permanent discontinuation of therapy may be necessary for recurrent or severe pneumonitis.
    Headline
    Child-Pugh class B, Child-Pugh class C, hepatic failure
    General Information
    Avoid the use of mirvetuximab soravtansine in patients with moderate or severe hepatic disease (Child-Pugh class B; Child-Pugh class C; total bilirubin greater than 1.5 times the upper limit of normal [ULN]) or hepatic failure; the pharmacokinetics of mirvetuximab soravtansine are unknown in this population.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Based on its mechanism of action, mirvetuximab soravtansine can cause embryo-fetal harm when administered during pregnancy. No reproductive or developmental animal toxicity studies have been conducted, and there are no data evaluating its use during human pregnancy to inform drug-associated risk. Mirvetuximab soravtansine contains a genotoxic compound, DM4, that affects actively dividing cells. Human immunoglobulin G (IgG) crosses the placenta; mirvetuximab soravtansine may be transmitted from the pregnant person to the fetus. People who are pregnant or who become pregnant while receiving mirvetuximab soravtansine should be apprised of the potential risk to the fetus.
    Headline
    people who may become pregnant, reproductive risk
    General Information
    Counsel people who may become pregnant about the reproductive risk associated with mirvetuximab soravtansine. Mirvetuximab soravtansine can cause fetal harm. Verify pregnancy status prior to treatment initiation. Advise people who may become pregnant to use effective contraception while taking mirvetuximab soravtansine and for 7 months after the last dose. If a person becomes pregnant while receiving mirvetuximab soravtansine, apprise them of the potential hazard to the fetus.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Due to the risk of serious adverse reactions in the breast-fed child, counsel patients to avoid breast-feeding during mirvetuximab soravtansine treatment and for 1 month after the last dose. There are no data on the presence of mirvetuximab soravtansine in human milk, its effects on the breast-fed child, or its effect on milk production.

Adverse Reactions

  Adverse Reactions
    Headline
    blurred vision, cataracts, corneal deposits, diplopia, keratitis, keratopathy, lacrimation, ocular pain, ocular toxicity, photophobia, uveitis, visual impairment, xerophthalmia
    General Information
    Mirvetuximab soravtansine caused ocular toxicity in 59% of patients with ovarian cancer who received it as treatment for platinum-resistant disease (grade 3, 11%), including blurred vision (45% to 50%; grade 3 or 4, 7% to 9%), keratopathy (37%; grade 3 or 4, 9% to 11%) (corneal disorders), xerophthalmia including increased lacrimation (27% to 29%; grade 3 or 4, 2% to 3%), cataracts (16% to 18%; grade 3 or 4, 3%), photophobia (17% to 18%; grade 3 or 4, 0.5% or less), and ocular pain (10% or less); uveitis occurred in up to 1% of patients. Higher exposure to mirvetuximab soravtansine was associated with a higher incidence of ocular adverse reactions. The median time to onset for first ocular adverse reaction was 5.1 weeks (range, 0.1 months to 68.6 weeks). Of those who experienced ocular events, 53% had complete resolution and 38% had partial improvement at last follow-up. The term blurred vision included vitreous floaters, reduced visual acuity, diplopia, presbyopia, accommodation disorder, refraction disorder, and visual impairment; keratopathy included corneal disorder, corneal epithelial microcysts, corneal epithelial defect, keratitis, keratopathy, corneal deposits, punctate keratitis, and corneal opacity. Patients should avoid the use of contact lenses during treatment with mirvetuximab soravtansine unless directed by a healthcare provider. Conduct an ophthalmic exam including visual acuity and slit lamp exam prior to initiation of therapy, every other cycle for the first 8 cycles, and as clinically indicated thereafter. After the slit lamp exam, instill prophylactic artificial tears and ophthalmic topical steroids with each cycle. An interruption of therapy, dose reduction, and discontinuation of therapy may be necessary for ocular toxicity depending on the level of severity.

    Headline
    dyspnea, interstitial lung disease, pneumonitis, respiratory failure
    General Information
    Pneumonitis/interstitial lung disease (ILD) occurred in 10% of patients treated with mirvetuximab soravtansine (grade 3, 1%; grade 4, 0.1%). One patient (0.1%) died due to respiratory failure in the setting of pneumonitis and lung metastases, and 1 patient (0.1%) died due to respiratory failure of unknown etiology. Monitor patients for new or worsening pulmonary symptoms indicative of ILD/pneumonitis including hypoxia, cough, dyspnea, or interstitial infiltrates on radiologic exams; rule out infectious, neoplastic, and other causes for such symptoms if they occur. An interruption of therapy, dose reduction, or discontinuation of therapy may be necessary for symptoms of ILD/pneumonitis. Dyspnea was reported in 12% of patients with platinum-resistant ovarian, fallopian tube, or primary peritoneal cancer treated with mirvetuximab soravtansine in 1 clinical trial.

Description
  Text
  Mirvetuximab soravtansine is a folate receptor alpha (FR alpha)-directed IgG1 antibody and microtubule inhibitor conjugate. It is indicated for adult patients with FR alpha positive, platinum-resistant epithelial ovarian, fallopian tube, or primary peritoneal cancer, who have received one to three prior systemic treatment regimens. Mirvetuximab soravtansine can cause severe ocular toxicities including visual impairment, dry eye, photophobia, eye pain, and uveitis; prophylactic lubricating eye drops and topical ophthalmic steroids are recommended during treatment. Conduct an ophthalmic exam including visual acuity and slit lamp exam prior to initiation of treatment as well as during treatment.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Mirvetuximab soravtansine is a folate receptor alpha (FR-alpha)-directed antibody-drug conjugate: the antibody is a chimeric IgG1 directed against FR-alpha; the small molecule, DM4, is a maytansine-derived microtubule inhibitor attached to the antibody via a cleavable linker. After binding to FR-alpha, mirvetuximab soravtansine is internalized. DM4 is released intracellularly via proteolytic cleavage, disrupting the microtubule network within the cell; this results in cell cycle arrest and apoptotic cell death.

    Description
    After IV administration of a dose of 6 mg/kg, the mean Cmax of mirvetuximab soravtansine, unconjugated DM4, and S-methyl-DM4 was 137.3 mcg/mL (+/- 62.3 mcg/mL), 4.1 ng/mL (+/- 2.3 ng/mL), and 7 ng/mL (+/- 6.8 ng/mL), respectively. Accumulation was minimal following repeat administration.
 
Higher exposure to mirvetuximab soravtansine was associated with higher overall response rates and longer median progression-free survival and overall survival as well as an increase in the incidence of ocular adverse reactions and peripheral neuropathy.

Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes and transporters: CYP3A4
The monoclonal antibody portion of mirvetuximab soravtansine is metabolized into small peptides by catabolic pathways. Unconjugated DM4 and its main metabolite S-methyl-DM4 are metabolized by CYP3A4. Unconjugated DM4 is a time-dependent inhibitor of CYP3A4 and a substrate of P-gp; S-methyl-DM4 is also a P-gp substrate.

Dilution:
Remove the necessary number of mirvetuximab soravtansine vials from the refrigerator and allow to warm to room temperature.
Gently swirl each vial prior to withdrawing the calculated dose; do not shake.
Withdraw the calculated dose from the vial and discard any unused drug.
Add the calculated volume of mirvetuximab soravtansine to the appropriate volume of 5% Dextrose Injection to attain a final concentration of 1 mg/mL to 2 mg/mL; do NOT dilute with any other IV solution.
Slowly mix by gentle inversion; do not shake or agitate.
Storage following dilution: Diluted mirvetuximab soravtansine solutions may be stored at ambient room temperature (18 to 25 degrees C; 64.4 to 77 degrees F) for no more than 8 hours including infusion time, or under refrigeration for no more than 12 hours including infusion time. Do not freeze.
Intravenous Infusion:
If refrigerated, allow the solution to come to room temperature prior to administration.
Administer using a 0.2 or 0.22 micromolar polyethersulfone (PES) in-line filter. Do not substitute other membrane materials.
First dose: Begin with an initial infusion rate of 1 mg per minute. If well-tolerated after 30 minutes, increase the infusion rate to 3 mg per minute. If well-tolerated after another 30 minutes, increase the infusion rate to 5 mg per minute.
Subsequent infusions: If no infusion-related reactions occurred with the previous dose, subsequent infusions may be started at the highest tolerated infusion rate and may be increased as tolerated to a maximum infusion rate of 5 mg per minute.
After completion of the infusion, flush the IV line with 5% Dextrose Injection to ensure delivery of the full dose. Do not use other IV fluids for flushing.
