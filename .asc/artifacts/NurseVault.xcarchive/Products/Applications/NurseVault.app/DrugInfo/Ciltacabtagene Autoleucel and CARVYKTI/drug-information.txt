Drug Class: Ciltacabtagene autoleucel is a chimeric antigen receptor (CAR) T-cell gene therapy.

Routes: oral; Common adult doses: 1 X 108 CAR-positive viable T-cells as a single IV dose.

Common Side Effects (excluding routine GI upset): angina, atrial fibrillation, atrial flutter, av block, chest pain (unspecified), sinus tachycardia, supraventricular tachycardia (svt), ventricular tachycardia, cough, dyspnea, hypoxia, nasal congestion, respiratory failure, tachypnea, colitis, gi perforation, immune effector cell-associated enterocolitis, delirium, depression, insomnia, psychomotor impairment, edema, peripheral edema, pulmonary edema, coagulopathy, disseminated intravascular coagulation (dic), hypoalbuminemia, hypofibrinogenemia, hypocalcemia, hypokalemia

In an Overdose: No overdose/antidote information is listed in this file.

Key Nursing Considerations: Contraindications/precautions: This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components. Hypersensitivity reactions including anaphylaxis may occur with ciltacabtagene autoleucel therapy; reactions may be due to dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) in the product.

  Indications
    Related Indications
      Heading
      for the treatment of relapsed or refractory multiple myeloma in patients who have received 1 or more prior lines of therapy, including a proteasome inhibitor and an immunomodulatory agent, and are refractory to lenalidomide
      Routes
        Heading
        Intravenous dosage
        Route Age Groups
          Heading
          Adults
          Dosage Description
          0.5 to 1 x 10^6 CAR-positive viable T-cells per kg of body weight (maximum dose, 1 x 10^8 CAR-positive viable T-cells) infused as a single dose from a patient-specific infusion bag. Administer with recommended premedication. Additionally, ensure that 2 doses of tocilizumab and emergency equipment are available for each patient prior to the infusion and during the recovery period. The primary endpoint of overall response rate was 97.9% in 97 evaluable patients with relapsed or refractory multiple myeloma who received a single dose of ciltacabtagene autoleucel in a multicenter, single-arm, phase 1b/2 (CARTITUDE-1) trial. The stringent complete response rate was 82.5%. At a median follow-up time of 27.7 months, the median duration of response, median progression-free survival (PFS) time, and median overall survival (OS) time was not reached. Bridging chemotherapy between apheresis and lymphodepleting chemotherapy was given in 75% of patients. At a median follow-up time of 33.6 months, the median PFS time was significantly improved in patients with relapsed or refractory multiple myeloma who received a single dose of ciltacabtagene autoleucel compared with standard multiple myeloma therapy (not reached vs. 11.8 months) in a randomized, phase 3 (CARTITUDE-4) trial (n = 419). A statistically significant improvement in OS was seen in the ciltacabtagene autoleucel arm compared with the standard therapy arm. All patients received bridging chemotherapy between leukapheresis and lymphodepleting chemotherapy in the ciltacabtagene autoleucel arm. Following apheresis and T-cell collection, all patients had lymphocyte depletion with fludarabine (30 mg/m2 IV daily for 3 doses) and cyclophosphamide (300 mg/m2 IV daily for 3 doses) starting 5 to 7 days prior to the ciltacabtagene autoleucel infusion.

    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components. Hypersensitivity reactions including anaphylaxis may occur with ciltacabtagene autoleucel therapy; reactions may be due to dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) in the product.
    Headline
    Treatment-Related Restrictions of Use
    General Information
    Treatment-related mortality occurred more often within 10 months after randomization in patients who received ciltacabtagene autoleucel compared with standard therapy in a randomized trial.
    Headline
    General Information
    General Information
    Vaccination with live viral vaccines during or following treatment with ciltacabtagene autoleucel has not been studied. Live virus vaccination is not recommended for at least 6 weeks prior to the start of lymphodepleting chemotherapy, during ciltacabtagene autoleucel therapy, and until immune recovery after ciltacabtagene autoleucel therapy.
    Headline
    cytokine release syndrome
    General Information
    Cytokine release syndrome (CRS) is a potentially fatal reaction that has been reported with ciltacabtagene autoleucel. Do not administer ciltacabtagene autoleucel in patients with active infection or inflammatory disorder. Promptly treat CRS with supportive care, tocilizumab, or tocilizumab and corticosteroids as indicated. Consider starting levetiracetam for seizure prophylaxis in patients who experience CRS. Intensive care unit monitoring and supportive care may be required in patients who have severe or life-threatening CRS.
    Headline
    activities requiring coordination and concentration, driving or operating machinery, neurological disease, neurotoxicity
    General Information
    Use ciltacabtagene autoleucel with caution in patients with a history of neurological disease (e.g., seizures, stroke, or memory loss). Severe and fatal neurotoxicity (e.g., immune effector cell-associated neurotoxicity syndrome [ICANS], parkinsonism, Guillain-Barre syndrome [GBS], peripheral neuropathy, cranial nerve palsies, immune-mediated myelitis) has been reported with ciltacabtagene autoleucel therapy. Geriatric patients 65 years or older may be at increased risk of developing severe neurotoxicity. Neurologic toxicity may occur before or concurrently with cytokine release syndrome (CRS) or after CRS resolves. Advise patients to avoid driving or operating machinery or performing activities requiring coordination and concentration for at least 2 weeks after the ciltacabtagene autoleucel infusion and for any new onset neurologic toxicity due to the risk of decreased consciousness or coordination. Administer supportive care and/or corticosteroids as indicated. Consider seizure prophylaxis with a non-sedating anti-seizure medication (e.g., levetiracetam) for patients who develop any grade neurotoxicity. Provide care unit monitoring and supportive care in patients who develop severe or life-threatening neurologic toxicities
    Headline
    hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis
    General Information
    Fatal hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis/macrophage activation syndrome (HLH/MAS) has been reported with ciltacabtagene autoleucel therapy. Symptoms of HLH/MAS may include hyperferritinemia, bleeding, respiratory failure, hypotension, hypoxia with diffuse alveolar damage, coagulopathy, multiple organ dysfunction, renal dysfunction, and cytopenias. HLH/MAS may occur concurrently with neurotoxicity or cytokine release syndrome (CRS). Evaluate for HLH/MAS in patients with progressive symptoms of CRS or refractory CRS despite first-line treatment. Treat patients with HLH/MAS per institutional guidelines.
    Headline
    inflammatory disorder
    General Information
    Do not administer ciltacabtagene autoleucel in patients with active infection or inflammatory disorder. Serious and fatal infections including bacterial infection, fungal infection, viral infection, and opportunistic infections have been reported with ciltacabtagene autoleucel therapy. Administer prophylactic and/or therapeutic antimicrobials according to standard institutional guidelines. Reactivation of JC virus leading to potentially fatal progressive multifocal leukoencephalopathy (PML) has been reported following ciltacabtagene autoleucel treatment; consider a diagnosis of PML in immunosuppressed patients with neurologic events and perform a diagnostic evaluation. Hepatitis B virus (HBV) reactivation resulting in fulminant hepatitis, hepatic failure, and death may occur in patients with hypogammaglobulinemia. Screen for viral infection before collection of cells for manufacturing (leukapheresis); consider antiviral therapy to prevent viral reactivation per local institutional guidelines/clinical practice.
    Headline
    prolonged and/or recurrent cytopenias
    General Information
    Prolonged and/or recurrent cytopenias (e.g., neutropenia and thrombocytopenia) resulting in fatal complications have been reported following lymphodepleting chemotherapy and ciltacabtagene autoleucel infusion; the need for stem-cell transplantation for hematopoietic recovery due to prolonged cytopenias has also occurred.
    Headline
    new primary hematologic malignancy
    General Information
    New primary hematologic malignancy including myelodysplastic syndrome and acute myeloid leukemia has occurred following treatment with ciltacabtagene autoleucel. T cell malignancies have occurred following treatment with BCMA- and CD19-directed genetically modified autologous T cell immunotherapies, including ciltacabtagene autoleucel. Mature T cell malignancies, including CAR-positive tumors, may present as soon as weeks following infusion, and may include fatal outcomes; life-long monitoring is recommended following ciltacabtagene autoleucel therapy.
    Headline
    blood, organ, tissue, or cell donor
    General Information
    Patients must avoid being a blood, organ, tissue, or cell donor during treatment with ciltacabtagene autoleucel.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Ciltacabtagene autoleucel is not recommended for individuals who are pregnant, and pregnancy after administration should be discussed with the treating physician. There are no available human or animal data on the use of ciltacabtagene autoleucel during pregnancy. It is not known if ciltacabtagene autoleucel has the potential to be transferred to the fetus. However, based on its mechanism of action, fetal toxicity, including B-cell lymphocytopenia or hypogammaglobulinemia, may occur if the transduced cells cross the placenta.
    Headline
    people who can cause pregnancy in others, people who may become pregnant, reproductive risk
    General Information
    Counsel people who may become pregnant and people who can cause pregnancy in others about the reproductive risk associated with ciltacabtagene autoleucel. Based on its mechanism of action, ciltacabtagene autoleucel has the potential to cause fetal harm. Verify pregnancy status prior to treatment. Follow contraceptive guidance specific to the agents used in patients who receive lymphodepleting chemotherapy. There is insufficient exposure data to provide a recommendation concerning the duration of contraception required after treatment with ciltacabtagene autoleucel. In clinical trials, patients of childbearing potential were advised to practice a highly effective form of contraception for 1 year after ciltacabtagene autoleucel infusion. Patients with partners who were pregnant or of childbearing potential were instructed to use a barrier method of contraception for 1 year after ciltacabtagene autoleucel infusion. There are no data on the effects of ciltacabtagene autoleucel on fertility.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Use ciltacabtagene autoleucel with caution during breast-feeding. There is no information on the presence of ciltacabtagene autoleucel in human milk, its effects on the breastfed child, or its effects on milk production. A risk to the breastfed child cannot be excluded. Consider the developmental and health benefits of breast-feeding along with the clinical need for ciltacabtagene autoleucel and any potential adverse effects on the breastfed child from the medication or from the patient's underlying medical condition.

Description
  Text
  Ciltacabtagene autoleucel is a chimeric antigen receptor (CAR) T-cell gene therapy. It is B-cell maturation antigen (BCMA)-directed immunotherapy that works by using a patient's own genetically altered immune cells to kill BCMA-expressing cancer cells in the blood. Ciltacabtagene autoleucel is indicated for use in adult patients with relapsed or refractory multiple myeloma who have received at least 1 prior line of therapy, including a proteasome inhibitor and an immunomodulatory agent, and are refractory to lenalidomide. It has black box warnings for cytokine release syndrome, severe neurotoxicity, parkinsonism, Guillain-Barre syndrome, hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis/macrophage activation syndrome, prolonged and/or recurrent cytopenias, and new primary malignancy, including new T-cell malignancies.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Ciltacabtagene autoleucel is a chimeric antigen receptor (CAR) T-cell therapy that works by redirecting T-cells to target the B-cell maturation antigen (BCMA) antigen on plasma cells. It has 2 BCMA-targeting domains. The domains, 4-1BB and CD3-zeta, activate downstream signaling cascades that lead to T-cell activation, proliferation, secretion of chemokines, and lysis of BCMA-expressing cells. This immunotherapy involves removing, genetically modifying, and then re-infusing a patient's own T-cells. During the manufacturing process, the cells are genetically modified using lentiviral vector transduction to express a CAR comprised of a bispecific variable fragment that targets 2 BCMA epitopes linked to 4-1BB and CD3-zeta co-stimulatory domains.
To produce CAR T-cell therapy, peripheral mononuclear cells are collected from the blood by leukapheresis; enriched for T-cells; transduced with the replication incompetent lentiviral vector containing the anti-BCMA CAR transgene; expanded to large numbers in a cell culture; and then washed, formulated into a suspension, and cryopreserved.

The ciltacabtagene autoleucel Cmax and AUC(0-28 days) values were increased by 168% and 209%, respectively, in patients who received tocilizumab to treat cytokine release syndrome (CRS; n = 68) compared with patients who did not receive tocilizumab (n = 29).   Text
  Ciltacabtagene autoleucel is administered intravenously. Post infusion, it exhibits an initial expansion phase followed by a rapid decline and then a slower decline. Ciltacabtagene autoleucel is distributed from the systemic circulation into the bone marrow; blood and bone marrow transgene levels decline over time. Following a single infusion of ciltacabtagene autoleucel (median dose, 0.71 X 106 CAR-positive viable T cells/kg; range, 0.41 X 106 to 1.08 X 106 cells/kg), the median half-lives were 15.3 (range, 3 to 95.4) and 11.7 (range, 4.1 to 179.6) days in 42 and 49 patients with relapsed/refractory multiple myeloma, respectively.
Pharmacodynamics: Peak levels of IL-6, IL-10, IFN-gamma and IL-2 receptor alpha were observed at 7 to 14 days after the ciltacabtagene autoleucel infusion; serum cytokine levels typically returned to baseline within 2 to 3 months.

Administration
  General Information
  For storage information, see the specific product information within the How Supplied section.
NOTE: Ciltacabtagene autoleucel may carry the risk of transmitting infectious diseases to health care professionals handling the product. Follow universal precautions and local biosafety guidelines in the handling and disposal of ciltacabtagene autoleucel.
Premedication
Premedicate patients with acetaminophen 650 to 1000 mg PO or IV and diphenhydramine 25 to 50 mg PO or IV (or another H1-antihistamine) approximately 30 to 60 minutes prior to the infusion.
Avoid the prophylactic use of systemic corticosteroids.

      Ciltacabtagene autoleucel is for autologous and intravenous use.

Preparation
Match the patient's identity with the patient identifiers on the cassette; do not remove the product bag from the cassette if the patient-specific label does not match the intended recipient.
Remove the product bag from the cassette; verify that the patient information on the cassette label matches the bag label.
Put the infusion bag inside a sealable and preferably sterile bag.
Thaw the infusion bag(s) at 37 +/- 2 degrees C using either a water bath or dry thaw method.
Once there is no visible ice in the infusion bag, gently mix the contents of the bag to allow the clumps of cellular material to disperse.
The total time from start to finish of thaw should be no longer than 15 minutes.
Remove the infusion bag from the sealable plastic bag.
If visible cell clumps remain, continue to gently mix the contents of the bag; small clumps of cellular material should disperse with gentle manual mixing.
Do not pre-filter into a different container; do not wash, spin down, and/or resuspend ciltacabtagene autoleucel in new media prior to infusion.
Storage: After thawing, the product may be stored at room temperature (20 to 25 degrees C); do not refreeze or refrigerate. Complete the ciltacabtagene autoleucel infusion within 2.5 hours from when the infusion bag was thawed.
Intravenous (IV) Infusion
Confirm the patient's identity with the patient identifiers on the infusion bag.
Use of a central access line is encouraged in patients with poor peripheral IV access.
Prime the tubing with normal saline prior to the infusion; do not use a leukocyte-depleting filter.
Administer as an IV infusion (using an infusion set fitted with an in-line filter) over approximately 30 to 60 minutes; gently mix the product bag during the infusion to prevent cell clumping.
Observe patients for 2 hours after the infusion for signs or symptoms of hypersensitivity.
To ensure all the product is delivered, flush the line (including the in-line filter) with normal saline at a volume that is at least the total hold up volume of the primary administration set used inclusive of the drip tube.
