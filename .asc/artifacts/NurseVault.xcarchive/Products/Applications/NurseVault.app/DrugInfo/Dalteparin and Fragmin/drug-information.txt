Drug Class: Dalteparin is a subcutaneously administered low molecular weight heparin used for deep vein thrombosis (DVT) prophylaxis, treatment of venous thromboembolism (VTE), and prophylaxis of ischemic complications in unstable angina and non-Q-wave myocardial infarction (MI).

Routes: injection (IV/IM/SC); Common adult doses: Dosage Description

Common Side Effects (excluding routine GI upset): bleeding, epistaxis, gi bleeding, hematoma, hematuria, intracranial bleeding, spinal hematoma, anaphylactoid reactions, bullous rash, ecchymosis, fever, injection site reaction, pruritus, rash

In an Overdose: In cases of severe bleeding or overdose, an initial dose of protamine 1 mg for every 100 anti-Xa IU of dalteparin is given. In cases of severe bleeding or overdose, give an initial dose of protamine 1 mg for every 100 units of dalteparin given.

Key Nursing Considerations: Contraindications/precautions: Dalteparin is contraindicated in patients with porcine protein hypersensitivity, dalteparin hypersensitivity, or heparin hypersensitivity. Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  General Information
  Dalteparin is contraindicated in patients with porcine protein hypersensitivity, dalteparin hypersensitivity, or heparin hypersensitivity.
 
Dalteparin is contraindicated in patients with thrombocytopenia associated with positive in vitro tests for antiplatelet antibodies in the presence of dalteparin. Cautious use of dalteparin is recommended for patients with thrombocytopenia; the platelet count should be monitored in patients receiving dalteparin. Cases of thrombocytopenia with disseminated thrombosis have been observed in clinical practice with heparins and low-molecular weight heparins. Low molecular weight heparins (LMWHs) such as dalteparin should not be used for the treatment of acute heparin-induced thrombocytopenia (HIT); dalteparin should be used with extreme caution in patients with a history of heparin-induced thrombocytopenia. Although LMWHs are less likely to trigger formation of HIT antibodies than unfractionated heparin (UFH), LMWHs are just as effective as UFH at triggering platelet activation by HIT antibodies (i.e., LMWHs have essentially 100% in vitro cross-reactivity).
 
The manufacturer advises caution for the use of dalteparin in patients with severe renal disease or renal impairment. Patients with renal disease have a higher risk of hemorrhage during anticoagulant therapy. In addition, dalteparin is eliminated renally; the half-life is prolonged to about 5.7 hours in renal failure patients receiving hemodialysis. Monitor anti-factor Xa serum concentrations to guide dosage adjustment in patients with cancer and a CrCl < 30 ml/minute who are receiving dalteparin for the extended treatment of acute, symptomatic venous thromboembolism; monitoring of anti-factor Xa serum concentrations in other patients with renal impairment may be advisable (see Dosage).
 
Bleeding is the major risk associated with use of dalteparin. Before initiating therapy, coagulopathy should be ruled out. Dalteparin should not be used in patients with severe bleeding disorders such as hemophilia or idiopathic thrombocytopenic purpura (ITP). Dalteparin is contraindicated in patients with active major bleeding, such as GI bleeding or hemorrhagic stroke in the acute phase. Dalteparin should be used with caution in any disease state in which there is an increased risk of hemorrhage such as acute infective endocarditis, dissecting aneurysm, peptic ulcer disease, recent GI bleeding, non-hemorrhagic stroke, diverticulitis, inflammatory bowel disease, decreased platelets, menstruation, threatened abortion or other abnormal vaginal bleeding, severe hepatic disease, severe renal disease or renal impairment, uncontrolled hypertension, and hypertensive or diabetic retinopathy. Caution should also be used in patients who have undergone recent brain, spinal, or ophthalmological surgery. Dalteparin is contraindicated in patients undergoing regional anesthesia when dalteparin is used in combination with aspirin for the prophylaxis of ischemic complications in unstable angina or non-Q wave myocardial infarction or for the extended treatment of symptomatic proximal DVT or pulmonary embolism in patients with cancer to reduce the recurrence of venous thromboembolism. The recommended dalteparin dosage for both indications puts the patient at an increased risk of bleeding. If dalteparin is used for other indications, consider the potential benefit versus risk before neuraxial intervention such as epidural anesthesia, spinal anesthesia, or spinal puncture in patients anticoagulated or to be anticoagulated for thromboprophylaxis; these patients are at risk of developing an epidural or spinal hematoma, which can result in long-term or permanent paralysis. The risk of an epidural or spinal hematoma is increased by the use of indwelling epidural catheters for analgesia administration or by the concomitant use of drugs affecting hemostasis such as nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs, platelet inhibitors, or other anticoagulants. The risk also appears to be increased by traumatic or repeated epidural or spinal puncture. If dalteparin is administered during or immediately following diagnostic lumbar puncture, epidural anesthesia, or spinal anesthesia, patients should be observed closely for bleeding and monitored frequently for signs and symptoms of neurological impairment. If neurological compromise is noted, urgent treatment is necessary. In addition, patients should be closely monitored for bleeding after tube drainage of the stomach or small intestine (see Adverse Reactions). Patients should also be monitored closely for a fall in hematocrit and/or a fall in blood pressure, hematuria, hematemesis, and other signs or symptoms of bleeding. Periodic complete blood counts and stool occult blood tests are recommended.
 
Intramuscular injections should not be administered to patients receiving dalteparin. IM injections may cause bleeding, bruising, or hematoma formation in patients who are anticoagulated.
 
No overall differences in efficacy have been identified between older and younger patients during clinical trials of dalteparin. Elderly patients generally have a higher risk of bleeding associated with anticoagulants. Post-marketing experience has not revealed any differences in the safety profile of dalteparin in elderly patients. The manufacturer advises careful attention to the dosing interval of dalteparin, and concomitant medications (e.g., platelet inhibitors) in elderly patients, especially patients with low body weight (< 45 kg) and/or reduced renal function.
 
LMWHs have not been adequately studied for thromboprophylaxis in patients with prosthetic heart valves, including pregnant women, and have not been adequately studied for long-term use in this patient population. Isolated cases of prosthetic heart valve thrombosis have been reported in patients with mechanical prosthetic heart valves who have received LMWHs for thromboprophylaxis. Some of these cases were pregnant women in whom thrombosis led to maternal and fetal deaths (see precautions for pregnant patients below). Insufficient data, the underlying disease, and the possibility of inadequate anticoagulation complicate the evaluation of these cases. Regardless, the ACCP recommends the use of LMWHs for thromboprophylaxis in patients with prosthetic heart valves after prosthetic valve insertion or when vitamin K antagonists must be discontinued (see Dosage).
 
Dalteparin is classified as FDA pregnancy risk category B; it does not appear that LMWHs cross the placenta. No adequate and well controlled studies exist in pregnant women, and dalteparin should be used during pregnancy only if clearly needed. Pregnant women receiving anticoagulants are at increased risk for bleeding. Hemorrhage can occur at any site and may lead to death of the mother and/or fetus. Pregnant women receiving dalteparin should be carefully monitored, especially women with lupus anticoagulant or antiphospholipid antibody syndrome who have a history of fetal loss. Compared with unfractionated heparin, LMWHs may have advantages in pregnant women because they cause less heparin-induced thrombocytopenia and may be less likely to produce osteoporosis; the ACCP guidelines indicate that because of these reasons, LMWHs are the preferred drugs for the treatment of acute venous thromboembolism (VTE) in pregnancy. The treatment of acute VTE in pregnancy requires weight-adjusted dosing. Because the half-life of dalteparin is decreased in pregnancy, twice daily dosing is preferred for acute VTE treatment. Anticoagulant needs may change during pregnancy; clinicians can increase the dose to reflect increases in weight or monitor anti-factor Xa concentrations and adjust the dose accordingly (goal concentration 0.5—1.2 IU/ml). Some data indicate that once a therapeutic anti-factor Xa concentration is achieved, dose modifications are not common, and monitoring may be necessary. In a clinical study of pregnant women with prosthetic heart valves given enoxaparin (1 mg/kg twice daily) to reduce the risk of thromboembolism, 2 of 8 women developed clots resulting in blockage of the valve and leading to maternal and fetal death. Furthermore, prosthetic valve thrombosis in pregnant women with prosthetic heart valves receiving enoxaparin for thromboprophylaxis have been reported. These events resulted in maternal death or surgical interventions. LMWHs have not been adequately studied for thromboprophylaxis in pregnant women with prosthetic heart valves; however, the ACCP guidelines do recommend using twice-daily LMWH in pregnant women with prosthetic heart valves requiring anticoagulation therapy (see Dosage). Pregnant women with prosthetic heart valves may be at higher risk for thromboembolism, and have a higher rate of fetal loss from stillbirth, spontaneous abortion, and premature delivery. Frequent monitoring of peak and trough anti-factor Xa levels, and dalteparin dosage adjustments to ensure adequate anti-Xa concentrations (goal concentration 1—1.2 IU/ml) are necessary for patients with mechanical valves, especially pregnant women. The multi-dose vial of Fragmin® contains benzyl alcohol as a preservative. The use of this formulation should be avoided in patients with benzyl alcohol hypersensitivity and in neonates. Benzyl alcohol has been reported to be associated with a fatal 'gasping syndrome' in premature neonates. Benzyl alcohol also crosses the placenta. Products preserved with benzyl alcohol should not be used in pregnant women.
 
It is unknown if dalteparin is excreted into breast milk. Use caution when administering dalteparin in women who are breast-feeding their infants. However, because of the relatively high molecular weight of dalteparin, excretion is expected to be minimal. Also, because of inactivation by the GI tract on oral ingestion, any potential risk to a nursing infant posed by dalteparin should be negligible.
 
Safe and effective use of dalteparin in children have not been established.
 
Dalteparin should be used with caution in patients receiving other anticoagulant therapy (e.g., warfarin), thrombolytic therapy (e.g., alteplase, reteplase, streptokinase), and/or platelet inhibitors (e.g., aspirin, ticlopidine) due to the potential increased risk of bleeding.
 
Patients who are anticoagulated are at increased the risk of bleeding during dental procedures. Patient should inform health care providers of anticoagulant therapy prior to having dental work performed. Patients, especially those with dental disease, should be instructed in proper oral hygiene, including caution in use of regular toothbrushes, dental floss, and toothpicks.

    Headline
    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components. Dalteparin is also contraindicated in individuals with prior hypersensitivity to heparin or pork products. The needle shield for the prefilled syringe may contain natural rubber latex; individuals with a latex hypersensitivity should not handle or administer this product.
    Headline
    neuraxial anesthesia, spinal deformity, spinal hematoma, spinal surgery
    General Information
    Dalteparin is contraindicated in individuals undergoing neuraxial anesthesia (i.e., spinal or epidural anesthesia). Epidural or spinal hematoma, which may result in long-term or permanent paralysis, can occur in anticoagulated individuals undergoing neuraxial anesthesia or spinal puncture. Risk factors for epidural or spinal hematoma include use of an indwelling epidural catheter, concomitant use of other agents that affect hemostasis (e.g., NSAIDs, platelet inhibitors, other anticoagulants), a history of spinal deformity or spinal surgery, and traumatic or repeated puncture. To reduce the risk of bleeding, consider the pharmacokinetic profile of dalteparin and perform any neuraxial procedure (e.g, placement or removal of an epidural catheter, lumbar puncture) when the anticoagulant effect is expected to be low; however, the optimal timing is not known. For those who have received preoperative dalteparin thromboprophylaxis, give the first postoperative dose 6 to 8 hours after surgery and the second dose no sooner than 24 hours after the first dose. Delay catheter placement or removal for at least 12 hours after administration of 2,500 units once daily, at least 15 hours after administration of 5,000 units once daily, and at least 24 hours after administration of higher doses (200 units/kg once daily or 120 units/kg twice daily). Although a specific recommendation for timing of a subsequent dalteparin dose after catheter removal cannot be made, consider delaying this next dose for at least 4 hours. For individuals with CrCl less than 30 mL/minute, consider doubling the timing of removal of a catheter to at least 24 hours for the lower prescribed doses (2,500 or 5,000 units/day) and at least 48 hours for the higher doses (200 units/kg once daily or 120 units/kg twice daily). Monitor individuals undergoing these procedures frequently for neurological impairment and instruct them to report symptoms immediately. If an epidural or spinal hematoma is suspected, urgently diagnose and treat, including consideration of spinal cord decompression.
    Headline
    bleeding disorder, diabetic retinopathy, GI angiodysplasia, hepatic failure, hypertension, infective endocarditis, major bleeding, neurosurgical procedure, ocular surgery, peptic ulcer disease
    General Information
    Dalteparin is contraindicated in individuals with active major bleeding. Use dalteparin with extreme caution in individuals who have an increased risk of hemorrhage, including those with severe uncontrolled hypertension, infective endocarditis, congenital or acquired bleeding disorder, active peptic ulcer disease, GI angiodysplasia, hemorrhagic stroke, or those who have recently undergone ocular surgery or neurosurgical procedure, such as brain or spinal surgery. Dalteparin may further increase bleeding risk in individuals with thrombocytopenia or platelet function defects, severe hepatic impairment or hepatic failure, hypertensive or diabetic retinopathy, or recent GI bleeding.
    Headline
    heparin-induced thrombocytopenia (HIT)
    General Information
    Dalteparin is contraindicated in individuals with a history of heparin-induced thrombocytopenia (HIT) or heparin-induced thrombocytopenia with thrombosis (HITT). Although low molecular weight heparins (LMWHs) are less likely than unfractionated heparin (UFH) to induce the formation of HIT antibodies, LMWHs can activate platelets in the presence of these antibodies and demonstrate nearly complete in vitro cross-reactivity with HIT antibodies.
    Headline
    renal failure, renal impairment
    General Information
    Monitor the anticoagulant effect of dalteparin using anti-Xa concentrations in individuals with severe renal impairment or renal failure. In these individuals, dalteparin exposure is increased, which may lead to a higher risk of bleeding.
    Headline
    geriatric
    General Information
    Geriatric adults have an increased risk of bleeding with anticoagulant therapy. Postmarketing experience has not identified any differences in the safety profile of dalteparin in geriatric adults compared with younger adults. Use caution with dosing intervals and concomitant medications (e.g., platelet inhibitors) in geriatric adults, particularly those with low body weight (less than 45 kg) and/or reduced renal function.
    Headline
    infants, neonates, premature neonates
    General Information
    Use preservative-free dalteparin formulations (single-dose vials) in neonates and infants. Benzyl alcohol, used as a preservative in multiple-dose vials of dalteparin, has been associated with "gasping syndrome", a potentially fatal condition characterized by metabolic acidosis and central nervous system, respiratory, circulatory, and renal dysfunction in premature neonates and low-birth-weight infants. When administering multiple-dose vials of dalteparin in infants, consider the total daily metabolic load of benzyl alcohol from all sources; multiple-dose vials of dalteparin contain 14 mg/mL of benzyl alcohol. The minimum amount of benzyl alcohol that may cause serious adverse reactions is not known.
    Headline
    labor, obstetric delivery, pregnancy
    General Information
    Dalteparin may be used during pregnancy when anticoagulation is indicated, but careful risk stratification is essential, particularly in patients with mechanical heart valves, where thrombotic events may be catastrophic. Dalteparin does not cross the placenta and has not been associated with fetal anticoagulation or developmental abnormalities. Physiologic changes in pregnancy increase drug clearance and volume of distribution, and dose adjustments during gestation may be required; the impact of anti-factor Xa monitoring on clinical outcomes is uncertain. Pregnancy itself confers an increased risk of thromboembolism, which is further amplified in patients with prior thromboembolic disease and certain high-risk conditions. Pregnant patients with thromboembolic disease or thrombophilia have an increased risk of pregnancy-related complications and fetal loss, regardless of the anticoagulant used. Use of low molecular weight heparins (LMWH) for thromboprophylaxis in pregnant patients with mechanical valves has been associated with valve thrombosis, including cases causing maternal and fetal death. If dalteparin is used in this population, frequent anti-factor Xa monitoring and dose adjustment are recommended. Additionally, anticoagulants increase the risk of bleeding in all patients, which can result in both fetal and maternal harm during pregnancy. Heparins are associated with a higher rate of maternal complications, including hemorrhage, during pregnancy when compared to warfarin. Monitor pregnant patients receiving dalteparin for clinical evidence of bleeding or excessive anticoagulation. In a retrospective review of 624 pregnancies (693 live births) exposed to another LMWH (enoxaparin), adverse outcomes included 72 hemorrhagic events (11 serious) in 63 patients and 14 cases of neonatal hemorrhage. Dalteparin should be discontinued prior to planned labor and obstetric delivery to minimize risks related to bleeding. This includes the risk of hematoma in patients receiving neuraxial anesthesia. Consider transitioning to a shorter-acting anticoagulant, such as unfractionated heparin, near term. Preservative-free formulations should be used during pregnancy, as benzyl alcohol may cross the placenta and has been associated with serious toxicity when given directly to neonates.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Dalteparin is compatible with breast-feeding. Limited data show that only small amounts are excreted into human milk. Because dalteparin has poor oral bioavailability, clinically relevant absorption by the breastfed child is unlikely. No adverse effects in breastfed children have been reported. Low levels of anti-Xa activity (less than 0.005 to 0.037 units/mL) have been detected in human milk after a single prophylactic postpartum dose; the clinical significance of this finding is unknown.

Adverse Reactions
  Headline
  Adverse Reactions
  General Information
  The most serious adverse reaction associated with dalteparin therapy is bleeding, which may occur at any site. This can involve minor bleeding, such as bleeding from the gums or hematuria, or frank hemorrhage. In a study comparing dalteparin 5000 IU once daily to 2500 IU once daily in patients undergoing surgery for malignancy, the incidence of bleeding was 4.6% and 3.6%, respectively. The most common bleeding event associated with dalteparin use is hematoma; wound hematoma occurs in 0.1—3.4% of patients and injection site hematoma occurs in 0.2—7.1% of patients. Major bleeding events, which include those requiring a transfusion, a decrease in hemoglobin > 2 mg/dl, bleeding leading to interruption of treatment or death, intracranial bleeding or retroperitoneal bleeding, occurred in about 1% and 2.6% of unstable angina and hip replacement patients, respectively. Major bleeding due to GI bleeding, subarachnoid hemorrhage, and subdural hematoma have also been reported. Cases of bleeding or hematoma within the spinal column have been reported with concurrent use dalteparin and epidural/spinal anesthesia or spinal puncture. Many of the epidural or spinal hematomas caused neurologic injury, including long-term or permanent paralysis. The risk of epidural or spinal hematoma is higher with the use of postoperative indwelling epidural catheters, traumatic or repeated spinal or epidural punctures, or in patients treated concomitantly with NSAIDs, platelet inhibitors, or other anticoagulants. Frequently monitor patients for signs and symptoms of neurological impairment. If serious bleeding occurs in a patient receiving dalteparin, the drug should be discontinued. In cases of severe bleeding or overdose, an initial dose of protamine 1 mg for every 100 anti-Xa IU of dalteparin is given. A second infusion of protamine 0.5 mg per 100 anti-Xa IU of dalteparin may be administered if the aPTT measured 2—4 hours after the first infusion remains prolonged.Other adverse reactions associated with use of dalteparin include injection site reaction, pain at the injection site (0—4.5%), skin necrosis, and allergic reactions (pruritus, maculopapular rash, fever, bullous rash, and rash (unspecified)). Anaphylactoid reactions have been reported rarely.Thrombocytopenia with platelets 3 times the upper limit of normal have been reported in 1.7—4.7% of patients treated with dalteparin for non-cancer indications. Such elevations are fully reversible and are rarely associated with hyperbilirubinemia. However, in patients with cancer and an acute thromboembolism receiving dalteparin for up to 6 months, asymptomatic increases in AST and ALT to > 3 times the upper limit of normal have been reported in 8.9% and 9.5% of patients, respectively. In animal studies, LMWH appears to only decrease the rate of bone formation, while UFH has been found to stimulate bone resorption in addition to decreasing bone formation.Alopecia has been infrequently reported during post-marketing surveillance. The alopecia improved upon drug discontinuation.

Description
  Text
  Dalteparin is a subcutaneously administered low molecular weight heparin used for deep vein thrombosis (DVT) prophylaxis, treatment of venous thromboembolism (VTE), and prophylaxis of ischemic complications in unstable angina and non-Q-wave myocardial infarction (MI). Like enoxaparin, dalteparin consists of smaller heparin molecules than unfractionated heparin (UFH), with the majority of molecules ranging in size from 2,000 to 9,000 daltons (14% to 26% are more than 8,000 daltons). Compared with UFH, dalteparin has an improved bioavailability after subcutaneous injection and a more predictable anticoagulant response. Dalteparin also has a longer half-life than UFH and therefore, can be dosed less frequently. Dalteparin has been shown to be superior to warfarin in preventing DVT after total hip replacement surgery. Dalteparin has also been shown to be effective in treating unstable coronary artery disease. The FRISC trial (the low-Molecular Weight Heparin (Fragmin) During Instability in Coronary Artery Disease Trial) showed that dalteparin decreased the risk of death or acute myocardial infarction (AMI) by 36% as compared to aspirin alone. In an additional trial (The Fragmin in Unstable Coronary Artery Disease Trial, FRIC), dalteparin was as effective as intravenous heparin in preventing death or AMI in the acute phase after unstable angina or non-Q wave MI. Dalteparin was FDA-approved in 1994.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Dalteparin exerts its antithrombotic activity by binding to and accelerating the activity of antithrombin III (ATIII). The interaction with antithrombin is mediated by a unique pentasaccharide sequence distributed along the heparin chains. Only 15% to 25% of the chains of dalteparin contain this pentasaccharide sequence, compared with about one-third of the chains of unfractionated heparin (UFH). By activating antithrombin, coagulation factor Xa and factor IIa (thrombin) are inhibited. Dalteparin and other LMWHs are more selective inhibitors of factor Xa than UFH. Although any pentasaccharide chain can inhibit factor Xa, only those at least 18 saccharide units long can inactivate thrombin, since a long chain is required to form a ternary complex between heparin, antithrombin, and thrombin. Although most of the chains of UFH are at least 18 saccharide units long, less than 50% of those of dalteparin and other LMWHs are of adequate length to bind both antithrombin and thrombin (factor IIa). Therefore, the anti-factor Xa:antifactor IIa inhibitory ratio of dalteparin is 2.7:1, compared to 1:1 for UFH. Ultimately, thrombin inhibition prevents the formation of fibrin clots. At recommended doses, dalteparin does not significantly affect platelet activity, prothrombin time (PT), or activated partial thromboplastin time (aPTT).

    Description
    The absolute bioavailability of dalteparin is about 87% after subcutaneous administration. Preparation
Do not mix with other injections or infusions unless specific compatibility data are available that support such mixing.
If using a fixed-dose prefilled syringe, do not expel the air bubble prior to injecting the dose. This ensures administration of the proper dose.
If using a graduated syringe assembly, hold the syringe assembly by the open sides of the device. Remove the needle shield. With the needle pointing up, prepare the syringe by expelling the air bubble and then continuing to push the plunger to the desired dose or volume. Discard the extra solution appropriately.
Storage (multiple-dose vial): After first penetration of the rubber stopper, store the multiple-dose vials at room temperature for up to 2 weeks. Discard any unused solution after 2 weeks.
 
Subcutaneous injection
Patients should be sitting or lying down and dalteparin should be administered by deep subcutaneous injection. Dalteparin may be injected in a U-shape area around the navel, the upper outer side of the thigh, or the upper outer quadrangle of the buttock. When the area around the navel or the thigh is used, use the thumb and forefinger to lift a fold of skin while giving the injection. The entire length of the needle should be inserted at a 45- to 90-degree angle.
Rotate the injection site daily.

Maximum Dosage Limits
  Text
  Dalteparin has a narrow therapeutic index, specific for the patient population and indication. The maximum dosage is individualized based on anti-Xa concentrations and assessment of efficacy and safety parameters.

Dosage Adjustment Guidelines
  Hepatic Impairment
  Specific guidelines for dosage adjustments in hepatic impairment are not available; it appears that no dosage adjustments are needed.
  Renal Impairment
  CrCl less than 30 mL/minute: FDA-approved labeling recommends monitoring anti-Xa concentrations to determine the appropriate dose in adult cancer patients with severely impaired renal function. Some experts recommend switching to an alternative anticoagulant with lower renal clearance.
