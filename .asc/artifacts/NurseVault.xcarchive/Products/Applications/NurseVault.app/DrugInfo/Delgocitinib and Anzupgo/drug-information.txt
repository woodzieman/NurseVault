    Treatment-Related Restrictions of Use
    General Information
    Delgocitinib is not for use in people with rheumatoid arthritis. It is not known whether using delgocitinib carries the same risks as taking other systemic or topical JAK inhibitors. In a large, randomized, postmarketing safety study in rheumatoid arthritis patients 50 years and older with at least 1 cardiovascular risk factor, a higher rate of all-cause mortality, including sudden cardiovascular death, major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE), thrombosis, and malignancies was observed with the use of an oral JAK inhibitor as compared to those treated with a tumor necrosis factor (TNF) blocker.
    Headline
    General Information
    General Information
    Complete all age-appropriate vaccinations, including herpes zoster vaccinations, as recommended by current immunization guidelines prior to treatment initiation. Avoid vaccination with live vaccines immediately prior to, during, and immediately after delgocitinib treatment.
    Headline
    infection
    General Information
    Avoid use of delgocitinib in patients with an active or serious infection. Consider the risks and benefits before initiating delgocitinib in people who have been exposed to tuberculosis or who have chronic or recurrent infections, a history of serious or opportunistic infections, or underlying health conditions that may predispose them to an infection. Closely monitor patients for the development of signs and symptoms of infection during and after treatment. If a patient develops a new infection during treatment, perform prompt and comprehensive diagnostic testing, initiate appropriate antimicrobial therapy, and monitor the patient closely. Discontinue delgocitinib in patients with a serious infection and do not resume treatment until the infection resolves or is adequately treated. Viral reactivation (e.g., herpes zoster) has been reported with delgocitinib. If a patient develops herpes zoster, consider interrupting treatment until the episode resolves. The impact of delgocitinib on chronic viral reactivation is unknown. Use in people with active hepatitis B or hepatitis C infection is not recommended. Consider viral hepatitis screening and monitoring for reactivation in accordance with clinical guidelines before initiation and during therapy. If signs of reactivation occur, consult a specialist.
    Headline
    malignancy
    General Information
    Use delgocitinib with caution in individuals with a history of malignancy. Delgocitinib may increase the risk of non-melanoma skin cancers including basal cell carcinoma. Periodic skin examinations of the application sites are recommended for all patients, particularly those with risk factors for skin cancer. Advise patients to avoid sunlamps and minimize exposure to sunlight by wearing sun-protective clothing or using broad-spectrum sunscreen.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Available data on the use of topical delgocitinib during pregnancy is insufficient to evaluate for a drug-associated risk of major birth defects, miscarriage, or other adverse outcomes. In animal reproduction studies, oral administration to pregnant rats and rabbits during the period of organogenesis did not result in adverse developmental effects at doses 120 or 193 times, respectively, the maximum recommended human dose (MRHD) based on AUC comparison.
    Headline
    diabetes mellitus
    General Information
    Use delgocitinib with caution in people with diabetes mellitus. Hypoglycemia, including severe hypoglycemia, has been reported in individuals with diabetes mellitus who have started other JAK inhibitors. During treatment with delgocitinib, consider increased blood glucose monitoring as clinically indicated in individuals with diabetes. Advise individuals to notify their health care provider if they develop signs or symptoms of hypoglycemia.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Use topical delgocitinib with caution during breast-feeding. There are no data on the presence of delgocitinib in human milk, its effects on the breastfed child, or its effects on milk production. Delgocitinib was present in the milk of lactating rats after oral administration. Consider the developmental and health benefits of breast-feeding along with the patient's clinical need for delgocitinib and any potential adverse effects on the breastfed child from delgocitinib or from the patient's underlying condition. To minimize potential infant exposure, advise the lactating individual to avoid direct contact with the nipple and surrounding area immediately after applying delgocitinib to the hands and/or wrists.

Description
  Text
  Delgocitinib is a topical Janus associated kinase (JAK) inhibitor with broad-spectrum activity, targeting multiple JAK enzymes involved in inflammatory processes. It is indicated for the treatment of moderate to severe chronic hand eczema (CHE) in people with an inadequate response to, or for whom topical corticosteroids are not recommended. It is not known whether using delgocitinib is associated with the same risks as other systemic or topical JAK inhibitors. Overall, delgocitinib is well-tolerated with low systemic exposure after topical application. It was approved by the FDA in July 2025.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Delgocitinib is a Janus kinase (JAK) inhibitor that targets JAK1, JAK2, JAK3, and tyrosine kinase 2 (TYK2). JAK signaling involves the recruitment of signal transducers and activators of transcription (STATs) to cytokine receptors, and their activation and subsequent localization to the nucleus, where they promote the expression of cytokine-responsive genes that induce specific biological responses in target cells. The precise mechanism by which delgocitinib exerts its therapeutic effect in chronic hand eczema remains to be fully elucidated.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Topical Route
    Description
    Cmax and AUC were 1.53 nanograms/mL and 6.1 nanograms x hour/mL, respectively, on Day 1 after topical administration of 0.87 g of delgocitinib to the hands and wrist twice daily. Systemic exposure was similar on Day 8. There was no evidence of accumulation.
  Text
  Delgocitinib is administered topically. Plasma protein binding is 22% to 29%. Delgocitinib does not undergo extensive metabolism and the main plasma component is unchanged delgocitinib. With oral administration, 4 metabolites (formed via oxidation and glucuronide conjugation) were detected at less than 2% of the average unchanged delgocitinib plasma concentrations. The limited metabolism of delgocitinib occurs primarily through CYP3A4/5 and, to a lesser extent, through CYP1A1, CYP2C9, CYP2C19, and CYP2D6. Delgocitinib is renally eliminated; approximately 75% of the total dose after oral administration was found unchanged in the urine. After repeated topical application, the average half-life is estimated to be approximately 21 hours.
