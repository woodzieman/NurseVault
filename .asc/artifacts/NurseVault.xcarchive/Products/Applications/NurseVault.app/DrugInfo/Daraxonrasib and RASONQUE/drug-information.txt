    Headline
    pulmonary disease
    General Information
    Use daraxonrasib with caution in patients who have a history of pre-existing pulmonary disease; severe pneumonitis/interstitial lung disease has been reported in patients treated with daraxonrasib. Monitor patients for new or worsening respiratory symptoms including fever, cough, or dyspnea. Immediately interrupt daraxonrasib therapy in patients with new or worsening respiratory symptoms. A dose reduction or permanent discontinuation of therapy may be necessary for recurrent or severe pneumonitis.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Avoid the use of daraxonrasib during pregnancy. Daraxonrasib can cause fetal harm, based on its mechanism of action and data from animal studies. There are no adequately controlled studies during human pregnancy. Administration of daraxonrasib to pregnant female mice during organogenesis resulted in adverse developmental outcomes including post-implantation loss, reduced number of live fetuses, decreased fetal body weights, and malformations including eyes, limbs, palate, gonads, great vessels, kidneys, and bones at exposures 2.5 times the recommended dose based on AUC or higher. Apprise individuals receiving daraxonrasib of the potential hazard to the fetus.
    Headline
    people who can cause pregnancy in others, people who may become pregnant, reproductive risk
    General Information
    Counsel people who may become pregnant and people who can cause pregnancy in others about the reproductive risk associated with daraxonrasib. Daraxonrasib may cause fetal harm. Verify pregnancy status prior to treatment initiation. Advise people who may become pregnant to use effective contraception while taking daraxonrasib and for 1 week after the last dose. For those that can cause pregnancy in others, contraception should also be used during daraxonrasib treatment and for 1 week after the last dose.

    Headline
    pruritus, rash, xerosis
    General Information
    Daraxonrasib caused dermatologic toxicity in 86% of patients with pancreatic cancer in a pooled safety population from 2 clinical trials (grade 3, 10%), including rash, pruritus, paronychia, dry skin, and skin fissures. The median time to first onset was 13 days, and the median time to improvement from grade 3 to grade 1 or resolution was 16 days. Rash (87% vs. 9%; grade 3 or 4, 13% vs. 0%), paronychia (17% vs. 0%), xerosis (14% vs. 3%), and pruritus (11% vs. 4%; grade 3 or 4, 0.4% vs. 0%) were more common in patients treated with daraxonrasib compared with physician's choice of chemotherapy (mFOLFIRINOX, gemcitabine/nab-paclitaxel, FOLFOX, or liposomal irinotecan/fluorouracil/LV) in a randomized clinical trial. Based on exposure and safety data from patients with pancreatic cancer, higher daraxonrasib exposure was associated with a higher incidence of grade 2 or higher dermatologic reactions. Monitor patients who develop dermatologic or soft tissue toxicities during treatment and initiate supportive measures (e.g., oral corticosteroids) as clinically indicated; consider dermatologic consultation. An interruption of therapy, dose reduction, or discontinuation of therapy may be necessary depending on the severity.

Description
  Text
  Daraxonrasib is a first-in-class, oral inhibitor of the RAS GTPase family. It is indicated for adults with metastatic pancreatic adenocarcinoma who have received at least one prior systemic therapy or who are not candidates for multiagent systemic therapy. It can cause dermatologic or soft tissue toxicities including rash, pruritus, and dry skin. Advise patients to limit sun exposure, use sunscreen, and use emollient creams. Daraxonrasib was approved by the FDA in August 2026.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Daraxonrasib is an inhibitor of the RAS GTPase family. Daraxonrasib binds to cyclophilin A, resulting in a binary complex that binds to the active, GTP-bound state of RAS. The tri-complex inhibits RAS signaling by blocking interactions with downstream effectors and promoting GTP hydrolysis to the inactive GDP-bound state of RAS. Daraxonrasib inhibition of wild-type and mutant variants of KRAS, NRAS, and HRAS induces tumor growth suppression and apoptosis. In RAS-dependent models of pancreatic adenocarcinoma, daraxonrasib treatment led to tumor growth inhibition and regression and is associated with antitumor immunity.

    Description
    The geometric mean Cmax of daraxonrasib is 365 ng/mL (CV, 50%) and the geometric mean AUC is 3,760 nanograms x hour/mL (CV, 46%). The AUC of daraxonrasib increases in an approximately dose proportional manner; the Cmax increases in a less than dose proportional manner over the dose range of 80 mg (0.27 times the recommended dose) to 300 mg.   Text
  Daraxonrasib is administered orally. It is approximately 98% protein bound in vitro; plasma protein binding is not concentration-dependent. The daraxonrasib blood to plasma ratio is concentration-dependent and ranges from 1.7 to 2.6 in healthy subjects. Daraxonrasib has an apparent (oral) volume of distribution during the terminal elimination phase of 1,060 L (CV, 48%). Daraxonrasib has a mean terminal elimination half-life of 9.2 hours (+/- 2.7 hours) with an apparent clearance (CL/F) of 80.4 L/hour (CV, 44%). Daraxonrasib is primarily metabolized by CYP3A. Approximately 93% of a single radiolabeled oral dose of daraxonrasib was recovered in feces (unchanged, 51%) and approximately 1% was recovered in urine (unchanged, 1%).
 
Pharmacodynamics
Daraxonrasib exposure-response relationships and time course of pharmacodynamic response have not been fully characterized. Based on exposure and safety data from patients with pancreatic cancer, higher daraxonrasib exposure was associated with higher incidence of dose interruption/reduction/discontinuation, grade 3 or higher adverse reactions, and grade 2 or higher dermatologic reactions, mucositis/stomatitis, nausea/vomiting, and diarrhea.
 
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes and drug transporters: CYP3A, P-gp
Daraxonrasib is a CYP3A and P-gp substrate. Coadministration with a strong CYP3A inhibitor with P-gp inhibition increased daraxonrasib exposure 5.1-fold; coadministration with a strong CYP3A inhibitor without P-gp inhibition increased daraxonrasib exposure 2-fold. Coadministration with a moderate CYP3A inhibitor with P-gp inhibition increased daraxonrasib exposure 2.6-fold; coadministration with a moderate CYP3A inhibitor without P-gp inhibition increased daraxonrasib exposure 1.5-fold. Coadministration with a P-gp inhibitor without CYP3A inhibition increased the AUC of daraxonrasib by 1.8-fold. Moderate or strong CYP3A inducers decreased daraxonrasib exposure by 30% to 84% in vivo. Daraxonrasib is also a P-gp inhibitor. Concomitant use increased the Cmax and AUC of a P-gp substrate by 2.5-fold and 2.1-fold, respectively; when doses were separated by 4 hours, no clinically significant effect on the P-gp substrate exposure was observed.
