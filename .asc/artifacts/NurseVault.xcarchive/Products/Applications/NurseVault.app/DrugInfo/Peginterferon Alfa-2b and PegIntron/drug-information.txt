Initiation of therapy for HCV infection:
HCV screening (i.e., HCV-antibody for initial screening, HCV-RNA for confirmation of active infection) recommended for:
All persons 18 years and older (a 1-time, routine, opt-out test)
All persons younger than 18 years with any of the below activities, exposures, or conditions and circumstances associated with an increased risk of HCV infection (a 1-time test)
Each pregnancy (testing as part of routine prenatal care)
All persons with any of the below activities, exposures, or conditions and circumstances associated with an increased risk of HCV infection (periodic repeat testing)
All persons who inject drugs (annual testing)
Men with HIV who have unprotected sex with men, and men who have sex with men taking HIV pre-exposure prophylaxis (annual testing)
HCV risk-associated activities, exposures, or conditions and circumstances include:
Activities: injection drug use (current or ever, including those who injected only once); intranasal illicit drug use; use of glass crack pipes; engagement in chem sex (i.e., combining sex with nonprescription drugs to facilitate or enhance sexual encounter); men who have sex with men.
Exposures: persons on long-term hemodialysis (ever); persons with percutaneous or parenteral exposures in an unregulated setting; healthcare, emergency medical, and public safety workers after needlestick, sharps, or mucosal exposure to HCV-infected blood; children born to mothers with HCV; persons who were ever incarcerated; certain recipients of a prior transfusion or organ transplant (i.e., received blood from a donor who later tested positive for HCV; received a blood transfusion or blood component, or underwent an organ transplant before July 1992; received clotting factor concentrates produced before 1987).
Conditions and Circumstances: HIV infection; sexually active persons about to start pre-exposure prophylaxis for HIV; chronic hepatic disease or chronic hepatitis, including unexplained elevated ALT concentrations; solid organ donors and recipients.
Treatment is recommended for all patients with acute or chronic HCV infection, except those with short life expectancy.
Laboratory tests recommended prior to initiating antiviral therapy: quantitative HCV-RNA test to document baseline viral load; HCV genotype and subtype test (if starting a non-pan-genotypic direct-acting antiviral [DAA]); HBsAg test for active HBV coinfection, and anti-HBs and anti-HBc test for prior HBV coinfection (if starting DAA); test for HIV coinfection (if starting DAA).
 
Place in therapy for HCV infection:
Interferon-based therapies are no longer recommended as part of preferred or alternative treatment regimens.

          Dosage Description
          1.5 mcg/kg/dose subcutaneously once weekly for 12 weeks led to undetectable HCV RNA concentrations 24 weeks after drug cessation (sustained virologic response) among most adults whose infection did not spontaneously resolve by week 8. Specifically, a sustained virologic response (SVR) was obtained by 95% of 43 patients who had drug started at week 8, by 94% of 33 patients who had drug started at week 12, and by 70% of 33 patients who had drug started at week 20. Early (week 8) peginterferon alfa-2b initiation appeared to be particularly important in regard to an SVR for patients with genotypes 1 or 4. In another study, an SVR was obtained by 67% of 34 patients who got 8 weeks of therapy, by 82% of 34 patients who got 12 weeks of therapy, and by 91% of 34 patients who got 24 weeks of therapy; peginterferon alfa-2b 1.5 mcg/kg/dose once weekly was started at week 12 after HCV infection. A treatment duration of 24 weeks may be necessary for patients with genotype 1; 88% of 16 had an SVR with 24 weeks of therapy whereas 38% of 16 had an SVR with 8 weeks of therapy. In both studies, all patients with an SVR had undetectable HCV RNA 48 weeks after peginterferon alfa-2b cessation.

Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  General Information
  Peginterferon alfa-2b is contraindicated in persons with known hypersensitivity to alfa interferon or any component of the product, including E. coli protein hypersensitivity. Serious acute hypersensitivity reactions (e.g., anaphylaxis, angioedema, acute bronchospasm, and urticaria) have been rarely observed during alfa interferon therapy. If such a reaction develops during treatment with peginterferon alfa-2b, discontinue treatment and begin appropriate medical therapy immediately. Transient rashes do not necessitate interruption of therapy.
 
Patients should be warned that depression, homicidal ideation, or suicidal ideation might occur during peginterferon alfa-2b therapy and to report any of these symptoms to their health care provider. Any patient with a history of substance abuse (e.g., alcoholism), depression or severe psychiatric disorder (e.g., bipolar disorder, mania, psychosis) should receive peginterferon alfa-2b with extreme caution. Former drug addicts may fall back into drug addiction or overdose. Patients should be closely monitored for depressive symptoms and other psychiatric symptoms. Development or exacerbation of neuropsychiatric disorders has been observed in patients receiving alpha interferons. Patients should be monitored closely with baseline and periodic neuropsychiatric monitoring and clinical and laboratory evaluations. Peginterferon alfa-2b should be discontinued in patients with persistently severe or worsening signs or symptoms of these conditions. In many, but not all cases, these disorders resolve after stopping peginterferon alfa-2b. Cessation of therapy should be considered in patients experiencing depression and is recommended for patients with severe depression (see Dosage). Although, dose reduction or cessation of therapy may lead to resolution of the symptoms, depression may persist and suicides have occurred after withdrawing therapy. Institution of psychiatric intervention is recommended. Full resolution of symptoms may take up to 3 weeks in severe cases. EEG abnormalities can develop during alfa interferon therapy. Any patient with a seizure disorder or compromised nervous system function should receive peginterferon alfa-2b with caution. Patients should be warned against driving or operating machinery until they know how peginterferon alfa-2b therapy will affect them.
 
Safe use of peginterferon alfa-2b during pregnancy has not been established. Peginterferon alfa-2b is classified as FDA pregnancy risk category C. Only use during pregnancy if the potential benefit justifies the potential risk to the fetus. Dose-related menstrual abnormalities and an increased incidence of spontaneous abortions have occurred in primate studies. Decreased serum estradiol and progesterone concentrations have been reported in women treated with human leukocyte interferon; the impact on female fertility has not been fully evaluated. Therefore, females of childbearing age should not receive peginterferon alfa-2b unless they are using effective contraception during treatment. Use with ribavirin: Peginterferon alfa-2b may be used with ribavirin, and ribavirin may cause birth defects or fetal death; ribavirin is genotoxic and mutagenic and should be considered a potential carcinogen. Pregnancy must be avoided in female patients and in female partners of male patients. A negative pregnancy test obtained immediately before ribavirin initiation is needed; do not start therapy until the pregnancy test result is obtained. Both men and women of childbearing potential must use 2 forms of effective contraception during treatment and for 6 months after treatment discontinuation, and monthly pregnancy testing during this time is needed.
 
According to the manufacturer, peginterferon alfa-2b or breast-feeding should be discontinued because of the potential for serious adverse reactions in nursing infants from peginterferon alfa-2b. It is not known if peginterferon alfa-2b is excreted into human breast milk. Studies in mice have shown that mouse interferons are excreted in breast milk. Consider the benefits of breast-feeding, the risk of potential infant drug exposure, and the risk of an untreated or inadequately treated condition. If a breast-feeding infant experiences an adverse effect related to a maternally ingested drug, healthcare providers are encouraged to report the adverse effect to the FDA.
 
Peginterferon alfa-2b should be used cautiously in patients with debilitating conditions such as those with a history of cardiac disease (e.g., angina or congestive heart failure), cerebrovascular disease, or diabetes mellitus prone to diabetic ketoacidosis. Patients with diabetes mellitus may require adjustment of their antidiabetic medications; do not start peginterferon alfa-2b in a patient whose diabetes mellitus is not effectively treated. Development or exacerbation of ischemic disorders has been observed in patients receiving alpha interferons. Ischemic and hemorrhagic cerebrovascular events, hypotension, arrhythmias, sinus tachycardia, chest pain, cardiomyopathy, and myocardial infarction have been observed in patients who received peginterferon alfa-2b; some events occurred in patients with few or no reported risk factors. Common adverse effects of peginterferon alfa-2b such as fever and chills may exacerbate preexisting cardiac conditions. Patients such as those with a history of myocardial infarction and/or previous or current cardiac arrhythmias should be monitored closely with baseline and periodic monitoring and clinical and laboratory evaluations. Patients with preexisting cardiac abnormalities should have electrocardiograms prior to beginning therapy with peginterferon alfa-2b. Patients with hypertension or diabetes mellitus may be at increased risk for retinal adverse events. Baseline and periodic ocular exams are recommended (see Adverse Reactions). Baseline chest X-rays are suggested and should be repeated, if clinically needed. Peginterferon alfa-2b should be discontinued in patients with persistently severe or worsening signs or symptoms of these conditions. Discontinue peginterferon alfa-2b if diabetes mellitus develops and is unable to be controlled with medication. In many, but not all, cases, ischemic disorders resolve after stopping peginterferon alfa-2b. Use with ribavirin: Peginterferon alfa-2b may be used with ribavirin, and ribavirin causes hemolytic anemia, which may worsen cardiac disease. Ribavirin is not recommended for patients with a history of significant or unstable cardiac disease.
 
Peginterferon alfa-2b should be used cautiously in patients with pulmonary disease like chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD). Peginterferon alfa-2b may induce or aggravate dyspnea, pulmonary infiltrates, pneumonia, bronchiolitis obliterans, interstitial pneumonitis, and sarcoidosis; respiratory failure or death may occur. Discontinue peginterferon alfa-2b in patients who develop pulmonary infiltrates or pulmonary function impairment. Closely monitor patients if peginterferon alfa-2b is reinstituted; recurrence of respiratory failure has occurred.
 
Elderly patients may have difficulty tolerating the adverse reactions to peginterferon alfa-2b therapy. These patients may require lower doses and close monitoring during treatment.
 
Peginterferon alfa-2b is contraindicated for use by patients with autoimmune hepatitis or a Child-Pugh score > 6 in cirrhotic chronic hepatitis C patients before or during treatment. Patients with cirrhosis may be at risk of hepatic decompensation and death when treated with alpha interferons including peginterferon alfa-2b. Of note, patients with chronic hepatitis C, HIV, and cirrhosis receiving highly active antiretroviral therapy (HAART) and alpha interferons with or without ribavirin appear to be at increased risk for the development of hepatic decompensation as compared with patients not receiving HAART. Closely monitor patients' clinical status and hepatic function during treatment. Determination of the Child-Pugh score is based on 5 parameters: ascites, encephalopathy, bilirubin, albumin, and prothrombin time. Patients with a Child-Pugh score > 6 may have jaundice, ascites, or a coagulopathy. Immediately discontinue peginterferon alfa-2b in a patient with cirrhosis if decompensation defined as a Child-Pugh score > 6 develops. Closely monitor patients without cirrhosis but with a Child-Pugh score > 6; peginterferon dose reduction or discontinuation may be needed. Peginterferon alfa-2b is only indicated for the treatment of chronic hepatitis C in patients with compensated hepatic disease.
 
Patients who develop signs and symptoms of colitis or pancreatitis during peginterferon alfa-2b therapy should discontinue therapy. Use cautiously in those patients with a history of pancreatitis, colitis or ulcerative colitis, as these conditions may be exacerbated.
 
When used in combination with ribavirin, peginterferon alfa-2b is contraindicated in patients with hemoglobinopathies, including thalassemia major and sickle cell disease.
 
Peginterferon alfa-2b should be used with caution in patients with a history of autoimmune disease. Peginterferon alfa-2b is contraindicated for use in patients with autoimmune hepatitis. Development or exacerbation of autoimmune disease (e.g., thyroiditis, thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura (TTP), idiopathic thrombocytopenic purpura (ITP), rheumatoid arthritis, interstitial nephritis, systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE), or psoriasis) has been observed in patients receiving peginterferon alfa-2b. Patients should be monitored closely with periodic clinical and laboratory evaluations. Peginterferon alfa-2b should be discontinued in patients with persistently severe or worsening signs or symptoms of these conditions. In many, but not all cases, these disorders resolve after stopping peginterferon alfa-2b.
 
Peginterferon alfa-2b suppresses bone marrow function and can sometimes result in severe cytopenias. Development or exacerbation of infectious disorders has been observed in patients receiving alpha interferons. Caution should be exercised when administering peginterferon alfa-2b to patients with bone marrow suppression or immunosuppression. For example, caution is advised when peginterferon alfa-2b is used in combination with other myelosuppressive therapy including radiation therapy. Patients should be monitored closely for infection with baseline and periodic monitoring and clinical and laboratory evaluations. Monitoring of hematologic parameters is recommended at baseline and periodically during peginterferon alfa-2b treatment. During clinical trials, a CBC was measured at weeks 2, 4, 8, 12, and then at 6-week intervals or more frequently if abnormalities developed. Peginterferon alfa-2a should be discontinued in patients with persistently severe or worsening signs or symptoms of infectious processes. In many, but not all, cases, these disorders resolve after stopping peginterferon alfa-2a. Patients with neutropenia (neutrophils 2 mg/dl. When used in conjunction with ribavirin, peginterferon alfa-2b should not be used in patients with CrCl < 50 ml/min. In a single-center, uncontrolled case experience, renal failure in 16 renal allograft recipients receiving interferon alpha and ribavirin was more frequent than expected from the center's previous experience with renal allograft recipients not receiving combination therapy. The relationship of the renal failure to renal allograft rejection is not clear. The safety and efficacy of peginterferon alfa-2b alone or in combination with ribavirin for the treatment of hepatitis C in liver or other organ transplant recipients have not been studied.
 
Peginterferon alfa-2b is for subcutaneous administration only; do not administer via intravenous administration or intramuscular administration. Intramuscular injections of other medications should be avoided in patients with platelet counts < 50,000/mm3.
 
Peginterferon alfa-2b should be used with caution in patients with dental disease. Myelosuppressive effects of peginterferon alfa-2b increase the risk of infection and bleeding. Dental work should be performed prior to initiating peginterferon alfa-2b therapy, or deferred until blood counts return to normal. Patients should be instructed in proper oral hygiene, including caution in use of regular toothbrushes, dental floss, and toothpicks.
 
Patients with thyroid disease (e.g., hyperthyroidism or hypothyroidism) whose thyroid function cannot be maintained in the normal range by medication should not be treated with peginterferon alfa-2b. Testing of TSH levels in these patients is recommended at baseline and every 3 months following initiation of peginterferon alfa-2b therapy. Also, the development or exacerbation of autoimmune thyroid diseases (e.g., Graves' disease, thyroiditis) has been observed in patients receiving interferons. Peginterferon alfa-2b should be used with caution in these patients and only if the potential benefit justifies the risk.
 
Safety and efficacy of peginterferon alfa-2b in children < 3 years of age with chronic hepatitis C have not been established. Combination therapy with peginterferon alfa-2b and ribavirin is indicated for the treatment of chronic hepatitis C in previously untreated patients 3—17 years of age with compensated disease. Of 80 children and adolescents with HCV genotype 1, 4, or 3 and a HCV RNA of at least 600,000 International Units/ml who got 48 weeks of peginterferon alfa-2b and ribavirin, 55% achieved a sustained viral response (SVR). Most children and adolescents with HCV genotype 2 or 3 and a HCV RNA less than 600,000 International Units/ml achieved a SVR with 24 weeks of therapy: 26 of 27 patients. Patients who reach their 18th birthday while receiving peginterferon alfa-2b and ribavirin should remain on the pediatric dosing regimen (see Dosage).
 
All patients should receive an ophthalmic exam prior to beginning therapy with peginterferon alfa-2b. Patients with ocular disease, including hypertensive or diabetic retinopathy, should receive periodic ophthalmic exams during the use of peginterferon alfa-2b. Decreased vision or loss of vision has been associated with interferon alfa therapy (see Adverse Reactions). Any patient who experiences changes in vision or other visual disturbance during peginterferon alfa-2b therapy should have a complete eye exam promptly. Peginterferon alfa-2b should be discontinued in any patient who develops new or worsening ophthalmologic disorders.
 
The safety and efficacy of peginterferon alfa-2b have not been studied in patients with hepatitis C virus (HCV) co-infected with either hepatitis B virus (HBV) or human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) infection.

Closely monitor patients for treatment-associated toxicities, especially hepatic decompensation. Most protease inhibitors have been associated with episodes of liver toxicity. Cirrhotic chronic HCV infected patients co-infected with HIV receiving HAART and alpha interferons appear to be at increased risk for hepatic decompensation (e.g., Childs-Pugh score 6 or more) compared to patients not receiving HAART. The HCV-HIV International Panel recommends the management of hepatotoxicity should be based on the knowledge of the mechanisms involved for each drug. Description
  Text
  NOTE: All products associated with this monograph are off-market. The content is retained for historical reference only and is no longer actively maintained.
 
Peginterferon alfa-2b is a covalent conjugate of recombinant interferon alfa-2b with monomethoxy polyethylene glycol (PEG). It is indicated for the treatment of chronic hepatitis C virus infections and for the adjuvant treatment of melanoma with microscopic or gross nodal involvement within 84 days of definitive surgical resection including complete lymphadenectomy. Interferon alfa-2b represents one specific subtype of alpha interferon (i.e., interferon alpha-2). It differs from interferon alfa-2a by only one amino acid at position 23. 'Pegylated' interferon alfa-2b results in a molecule with a longer half-life than traditional interferon alfa-2b. The longer duration of action prevents viral replication between doses. Peginterferon alfa-2b has significantly higher sustained virologic response rates than interferon alfa-2b. Peginterferon alfa-2b and regular interferon alfa-2b cause similar adverse reactions; however, peginterferon is more likely to result in mild bone marrow depression.
 
The efficacy of peginterferon alfa-2b has been compared with peginterferon alfa-2a. In the study, similar sustained response rates were obtained by 3,070 patients with genotype 1 who received peginterferon alfa-2b 1.5 mcg/kg/week and ribavirin 800 to 1,400 mg/day (40%), peginterferon alfa-2b 1 mcg/kg/week and ribavirin 800 to 1,400 mg/day (38%), or peginterferon alfa-2a 180 mcg/week and ribavirin 1,000 to 1,200 mg/day (41%) for up to 48 weeks. Although sustained virologic responses were similar, fewer patients treated with peginterferon alfa-2b 1.5 mcg/kg/week and ribavirin 800 to 1,400 mg/day (24%) or peginterferon alfa-2b 1 mcg/kg/week and ribavirin 800 to 1,400 mg/day (20%) had a relapse after the end of treatment as compared with peginterferon alfa-2a 180 mcg/week and ribavirin 1,000 to 1,200 mg/day recipients (32%). Relapse was defined as undetectable HCV RNA at the end of treatment but not 24 weeks after treatment cessation. Ribavirin was dosed by weight categories in all 3 treatment arms. As compared with peginterferon alfa-2a recipients, an equal ribavirin dose was given to 52% of peginterferon alfa-2b recipients; 10% had a higher ribavirin dose and 38% had a lower ribavirin dose. All 3 treatment regimens were similarly well tolerated.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  NOTE: All products associated with this monograph are off-market. The content is retained for historical reference only and is no longer actively maintained.
 
Peginterferon alfa-2b is indicated for the treatment of chronic hepatitis C virus infections and for the adjuvant treatment of melanoma. The active component of peginterferon alfa-2b is the interferon alfa-2b moiety. Interferon alfa-2b acts similarly to endogenous alfa interferons, which are cytokines with immunomodulatory, antiviral, antiangiogenic, antiproliferative, and antitumor activities. Interferons produce these therapeutic effects by directly binding to cell surface receptors (i.e., type I or type II interferon receptors) and by indirect inducting host antitumor responses. Of the 2 receptors, interferon alfa selectively binds to the type 1 interferon receptor. By binding to this receptor, interferon alfa causes autophosphorylation of tyrosine kinase 2 (Tyk2) and Janus kinase 1 (Jak1), which leads to activation of the following signal pathways: (1) phosphorylation and activation of the signal transducer and activator transcription (Stat) proteins (i.e., Jak-Stat pathway); (2) activation of CrkL, resulting in downstream activation of small GTPase Rap1 (Crk-pathway); (3) phosphorylation of insulin receptor substrates (IRS-1 and IRS-2), resulting in downstream activation of phosphatidylinositol and serine kinase activities of the phosphatidylinositol 3'kinase (IRS/PI3'-kinase pathway); (4) activation of p38 mitogen-activated protein (MAP) kinase which is involved in many signal transduction pathways in mammalian cells; (5) activation of nuclear factor kappa-light-chain-enhancer of activated B cells (NFkB) which produces anti-inflammatory effects by inhibiting expression of proinflammatory genes and manipulating activity of anti-inflammatory cytokines. In addition to receptor-mediated effects, data suggest interferons may induce apoptosis in certain malignant cells. This effect appears to be cell type-dependent and interferon species-specific.

Peginterferon alfa-2b is given subcutaneously. The mean elimination half-life is 40 hours (range, 22 to 60 hours) in patients with hepatitis C infection. Renal elimination accounts for about 30% of the clearance. When compared to interferon alfa-2b (Intron A), peginterferon alfa-2b has approximately a 7-fold lower mean apparent clearance and a 5-fold greater mean half-life allowing for a reduced dosing frequency. At effective therapeutic doses, peginterferon alfa-2b has about a 10-fold greater Cmax and 50-fold greater AUC than interferon alfa-2b.
