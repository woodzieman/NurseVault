Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  General Information
  Mexiletine is contraindicated in patients with preexisting second- or third-degree AV block, unless an artificial pacemaker is in place. Mexiletine can increase the risk of arrhythmias or complete heart block in patients with sinus bradycardia or incomplete heart block and should be used with caution in these patients. Mexiletine also is contraindicated in patients in cardiogenic shock.
 
Mexiletine should be used with extreme caution in patients with hypotension because the drug can worsen this condition. Mexiletine increases the risk for further blood pressure reductions.
 
Like other antiarrhythmics, mexiletine is proarrhythmic and can worsen or induce cardiac arrhythmias. Increased ventricular arrhythmias are of particular concern for patients with sustained ventricular tachycardia. Other patients at risk for proarrhythmic effects include patients with a history of myocardial infarction or acute myocardial infarction. In the CAST trial, a randomized double-blind study in patients with asymptomatic non-life-threatening ventricular arrhythmias, has demonstrated an excessive rate of mortality or non-fatal cardiac arrest in patients treated with encainide or flecainide (7.7%) compared to placebo (3%). The enrolled patients had a history of myocardial infarction (more than 6 days but less than 2 years); the average duration of antiarrhythmic therapy was 10 months. The applicability of the CAST results to other populations (e.g., those without recent myocardial infarction) or other antiarrhythmics is uncertain. Considering the known proarrhythmic properties of mexiletine and the lack of evidence of improved survival for antiarrhythmic drugs in patients without life-threatening arrhythmias, the use of mexiletine should be reserved for patients with life-threatening ventricular arrhythmias. Mexiletine should be used with caution in patients with severe congestive heart failure because of the potential to aggravating this condition; exacerbation of congestive heart failure may occur in some patients with pre-existing ventricular dysfunction. Mexiletine should also be used with caution in patients with congestive heart failure, acute myocardial infarction, or hepatic disease. These conditions may reduce hepatic blood flow, therefore reduce hepatic metabolism, resulting in prolongation of effect. Cirrhosis markedly impairs the elimination of mexiletine; therefore, dosage should be reduced in patients with hepatic impairment with close monitoring for therapeutic effectiveness and safety (see Dosage - hepatic impairment).
 
Severe renal disease, renal failure, or renal impairment has been shown to prolong the half-life of mexiletine. Dosage reduction may be needed in patients with CrCl 10%), which could exacerbate pre-existing Parkinson's disease.
 
Although less than 1% of patients treated with mexiletine presented with blood dyscrasias and the causal relationship is unknown due to coexisting factors, there have been reports of agranulocytosis or marked leukopenia (0.06%), mild leukopenia (0.08%), and thrombocytopenia (0.15%). If drug-related hematologic abnormalities occur, mexiletine should be discontinued.
 
Mexiletine is classified as FDA pregnancy risk category C. The safe use of mexiletine during pregnancy has not been established. Mexiletine should not be used during pregnancy unless the potential benefits to the mother outweigh the potential risks to the fetus.
 
Mexiletine is excreted into breast milk in concentrations similar to those in human plasma. If mexiletine therapy is essential to the mother, an alternative to breast-feeding should be considered.
 
Tobacco smoking has been noted to significantly enhance the elimination rate of mexiletine. Conversely, because the effect on hepatic microsomal enzymes is not related to the nicotine component of tobacco, sudden smoking cessation may result in a reduced clearance of mexiletine, despite the initiation of nicotine replacement. Monitor patients carefully for the desired clinical effects when changes in tobacco smoking status occur.

    cardiac conduction disorder, cardiogenic shock
    General Information
    Mexiletine is contraindicated in the presence of cardiogenic shock or preexisting second or third-degree AV block (if no pacemaker is present). If a ventricular pacemaker is operative, individuals with second or third-degree heart block may be treated with mexiletine if continuously monitored.     Headline
    mortality, myocardial infarction
    General Information
    Like other antiarrhythmics, mexiletine can cause worsening of arrhythmias, which is of greater concern in individuals with life-threatening arrhythmias such as sustained ventricular tachycardia; worsening of arrhythmias has been uncommon in individuals with less serious arrhythmias (e.g., frequent premature beats or nonsustained ventricular tachycardia). In the National Heart, Lung, and Blood Institute's Cardiac Arrhythmia Suppression Trial (CAST), a long-term, multicentered, randomized, double-blind study in subjects with asymptomatic non-life-threatening ventricular arrhythmias who had a myocardial infarction more than 6 days but less than 2 years previously, an excessive mortality or non-fatal cardiac arrest rate was seen in subjects treated with encainide or flecainide compared with that seen in subjects assigned to carefully matched placebo-treated groups. The applicability of the CAST results to other populations (e.g., those without recent myocardial infarction) is uncertain. Considering the known proarrhythmic properties of mexiletine and the lack of evidence of improved survival for any antiarrhythmic drug in individuals without life-threatening arrhythmias, reserve the use of mexiletine for individuals with life-threatening ventricular arrhythmias.
    Headline
    heart failure, hepatic disease, hepatotoxicity, hypotension
    General Information
    Use mexiletine with caution in individuals with hypotension or heart failure because of the potential for aggravating these conditions. Since mexiletine is metabolized in the liver, and hepatic impairment has been reported to prolong the elimination half-life of mexiletine, follow individuals with hepatic disease carefully while receiving mexiletine, including individuals with hepatic dysfunction secondary to heart failure. Individuals with severe liver disease may require lower mexiletine doses. Similarly, marked right-sided heart failure may reduce hepatic metabolism and the necessary mexiletine dose. In postmarketing experience, acute liver injury (hepatotoxicity), defined as abnormal liver function tests, has been reported, some in the first few weeks of therapy with mexiletine. Most of these cases of acute liver injury have been observed in the setting of heart failure or ischemia and their relationship to mexiletine has not been established.
    Headline
    seizure disorder
    General Information
    Use mexiletine with caution in individuals with a known seizure disorder. Convulsions have been reported with mexiletine in individuals with and without a prior history of seizures.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Use mexiletine during pregnancy only if the potential benefits outweigh the potential risks to the fetus. There are no adequate and well-controlled studies to assess the safety of mexiletine use during human pregnancy. In reproduction studies involving rats, mice, and rabbits, an increase in fetal resorption was observed at doses up to 4 times the maximum human oral dose; however, there was no evidence of teratogenicity.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    In general, advise patients to avoid mexiletine during breast-feeding. Mexiletine is excreted into human milk at concentrations similar to those in human plasma. In one case report, a patient receiving mexiletine 200 mg 3 times daily during pregnancy and postpartum had a mean milk-to-plasma ratio of 1.45 on postpartum days 2 and 5. In another report, a 17-day-old breastfed child exposed to mexiletine during pregnancy and breastfeeding had suboptimal growth and poor feeding; after breastfeeding was discontinued, weight gain improved and growth returned to an acceptable curve.

Adverse Reactions
  Headline
  Adverse Reactions
  General Information
  The most frequently reported adverse events with mexiletine affect the GI tract or CNS. Upper gastrointestinal distress was recorded in 41% of patients. This includes nausea/vomiting, dyspepsia and, less frequently, dysphagia. The most frequent CNS effects are lightheadedness or dizziness (10.5% of patients), tremor (12.6%), and coordination difficulties or ataxia (10.2%). Nervousness, paresthesias, hallucinations, blurred vision, and rarely seizures have also been attributed to mexiletine therapy. The discontinuation rate due to adverse effects in controlled clinical trials was 40%.Like other antiarrhythmics, mexiletine can worsen some arrhythmias (arrhythmia exacerbation). Increased ventricular arrhythmias are of particular concern for patients with sustained ventricular tachycardia. Less than 1% of patients have suffered bradycardia, AV block, or atrial arrhythmias. Angina or unspecified chest pain can occur.Although less than 1% of patients treated with mexiletine presented with blood dyscrasias, there have been reports of agranulocytosis or marked leukopenia (0.06%), mild leukopenia (0.08%), and thrombocytopenia (0.15%). In addition, two patients experienced myelofibrosis One patient was receiving long-term thiotepa therapy and the other had pre-treatment myeloid abnormalities. The causal relationship is questionable because many of these patients were also receiving other medications with known hematologic adverse effects. If drug-related hematologic abnormalities occur, mexiletine should be discontinued. Blood counts usually return to normal within one month of drug discontinuation.Elevated hepatic enzymes can occur during mexiletine therapy and may warrant discontinuation of the drug. Less than 1% of patients are affected.Unspecified rashes have been reported from mexiletine therapy, with rare instances of exfoliative dermatitis and Stevens-Johnson syndrome.Less than 1% of patients treated with mexiletine have reported syncope, edema, hot flashes, short-term amnesia, diaphoresis, urinary retention/hesitance, changes in libido, pharyngitis, and congestive heart failure. Cases of exacerbation of congestive heart failure in patients with pre-existing compromised ventricular function, have been reported.In post-marketing experience, isolated and spontaneous reports of pulmonary changes including pulmonary infiltrates and pulmonary fibrosis have been reported. These changes have been occurred in patients using mexiletine with or without drugs or diseases known to produce pulmonary toxicity. A causal relationship has not been established. In addition, nystagmus has been reported.

Description
  Text
  Description: Mexiletine is an oral, local anesthetic-type antiarrhythmic agent similar to lidocaine and tocainide. Mexiletine is a class Ib antiarrhythmic according to the Vaughan-Williams classification and is used to treat life-threatening ventricular arrhythmias. It also has been used successfully in the treatment of pain associated with diabetic neuropathy. Mexiletine was approved by the FDA in December 1985, and the patent expired in June 1994.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Mechanism of Action: Mexiletine inhibits the fast sodium channels of the myocardial cell membrane, decreasing the rate of rise and amplitude of the action potential, which increases the recovery period following repolarization. The drug decreases automaticity (increases the excitability threshold) in the His-Purkinje system, but it does not affect conduction velocity. Mexiletine decreases both the effective refractory period and the action potential duration of the His-Purkinje system. The effective refractory period is decreased less than the action potential duration, so the myocardium remains refractory even after the resting membrane potential has been restored. In summary, its antiarrhythmic effect may be related to the ability of the drug to increase the ratio of the effective refractory period to the action potential duration.

  Text
  Mexiletine is administered orally. It is 50—60% protein-bound and is distributed into breast milk. Mexiletine is metabolized in the liver primarily to inactive metabolites (85%), although a minority of the metabolites are pharmacologically active. Elimination is primarily via biliary routes, with approximately 10% of the drug excreted unchanged in the urine. The plasma half-life is 10—12 hours.
 
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes and drug transporters: CYP2D6, CYP1A2
Mexiletine is primarily metabolized by CYP2D6 isoenzymes, with a secondary pathway by CYP1A2. Mexiletine is an inhibitor of CYP1A2 isoenzymes.
