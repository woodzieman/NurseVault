    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    There are no available data regarding the use of omidenepag during human pregnancy. In animal reproduction studies, subcutaneous administration to pregnant rabbits throughout the period of organogenesis resulted in fetal skeletal anomalies (i.e., thoracic misaligned centrum and hemivertebra, fused sternebra, absent rib) at doses 24-times the maximum recommended human ocular dose (MRHOD). Additionally, increases in preimplantation losses and post-implantation losses were observed in rabbits at doses 3,696-times the MRHOD. In rats, no teratogenicity or adverse effects on male or female fertility were observed after administration of subcutaneous doses 2,452-times the MRHOD.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Use omidenepag with caution during breast-feeding. There are no data on the presence of omidenepag in human milk, its effects on the breast-fed child, or its effects on milk production. Systemic exposure to omidenepag after topical ophthalmic administration is low, and it is not known whether measurable drug concentrations would be present in human milk. Consider the benefits of breast-feeding, the patient's clinical need for treatment, and any potential adverse effects on the breast-fed child from the medication or from the patient's underlying medical condition.

    Headline
    iridal discoloration, skin discoloration, skin hyperpigmentation
    General Information
    Administration of omidenepag isopropyl ophthalmic solution is expected to result in pigmentation changes that increase as long as treatment continues. These changes in pigmentation are due to increased melanin content in the melanocytes rather than an increase in number of melanocytes. After discontinuation of omidenepag isopropyl, periorbital skin discoloration (i.e., skin hyperpigmentation) and eyelash changes are expected to be reversible in most patients; however, pigmentation of the iris is likely to be permanent. Iridal discoloration occurs slowly and may not be noticeable for several months to years. Typically, the brown pigmentation around the pupil spreads concentrically towards the periphery of the iris, and the iris (entire iris or parts of the iris) become more brownish. Neither iris nevi nor freckles of the iris appear to be affected by treatment. Although treatment may continue in patients who develop noticeably increased iris pigmentation, these patients should be examined regularly as the long-term effects of increased pigmentation are not known.

Description
  Text
  NOTE: All products associated with this monograph are off-market. The content is retained for historical reference only and is no longer actively maintained.
 
Omidenepag isopropyl ophthalmic solution is approved for the reduction of elevated intraocular pressure (IOP) in patients with open-angle glaucoma or ocular hypertension. The drug is a relatively selective prostaglandin E2 (EP2) receptor agonist, that lowers IOP by increasing the outflow of aqueous humor through both the trabecular and uveoscleral pathways. Omidenepag isopropyl has been associated with increased pigmentation of eyelashes, the iris, and periorbital tissue; inform drug recipients that changes in iridal pigmentation may be permanent. Additionally, cases of ocular inflammation and macular edema (including cystoid macular edema) have been reported during treatment. Use with caution in patients with active ocular inflammation, aphakic patients, pseudophakic patients, and patients with known risk factors for macular edema.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  NOTE: All products associated with this monograph are off-market. The content is retained for historical reference only and is no longer actively maintained.
 
Omidenepag isopropyl is a prodrug for omidenepag, the biologically active metabolite. Omidenepag is a relatively selective prostaglandin E2 (EP2) receptor agonist. By binding to an EP2 receptor, omidenepag increases aqueous humor outflow via the trabecular and uveoscleral pathways; thereby reducing intraocular pressure.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Other Route(s)

    Ophthalmic Route
The pharmacokinetics of omidenepag were evaluated in patients who received 1 drop of omidenepag isopropyl ophthalmic solution 0.0025% in both eyes for 7 days. After dosing, the time to reach maximum plasma concentration (Tmax) was 10 to 15 minutes. Systemic exposures (AUC) were similar between Days 1 and 7, indicated no systemic accumulation.
  Text
  NOTE: All products associated with this monograph are off-market. The content is retained for historical reference only and is no longer actively maintained.
 
Omidenepag isopropyl ophthalmic solution is applied topically to the eye. Once absorbed through the cornea, omidenepag isopropyl is rapidly hydrolyzed in the eye by carboxylesterase-1 to become omidenepag, the biologically active metabolite. Omidenepag is further metabolized by the liver through oxidation, N-dealkylation, glucuronidation, sulfate conjugation, or taurine conjugation. Excretion was evaluated in rats using radiolabeled omidenepag isopropyl instilled in both eyes as a single dose. At 168 hours after ocular instillation, 89% of the administered radioactive dose had been excreted (83% in the feces, 4% in the urine, radioactivity in expired air below lower limits of quantitation).

    Care should be taken to avoid contamination by using proper aseptic techniques. Inadvertent contamination of the multidose container may increase the risk of ocular infection.
