        Adults
        Dosage Description
        20 mg subcutaneously once per chemotherapy cycle; administer at least 24 hours after cytotoxic chemotherapy. Do not administer efbemalenograstim alfa within 14 days before and less than 24 hours after administration of cytotoxic chemotherapy. The mean duration of grade 4 neutropenia (1.4 vs, 4.3 days; p less than 0.001) and the incidence of febrile neutropenia (4.8% vs. 25.6%; p = 0.0016) in cycle 1 were significantly lower in breast cancer patients who received a single subcutaneous injection of efbemalenograstim alfa compared with placebo on day 2 following chemotherapy with doxorubicin and docetaxel in a randomized, double-blind trial (Study GC-627-04; n = 122). Additionally, the mean duration of grade 4 neutropenia was 0.2 days in cycle 1 following a single subcutaneous injection of efbemalenograstim alfa or pegfilgrastim on day 2 in breast cancer patients who were receiving chemotherapy with docetaxel and cyclophosphamide in a randomized trial (Study GC-627-05; n = 393).
    Heading
    For chemotherapy-induced neutropenia prophylaxis, to decrease the incidence of febrile neutropenia, in patients with nonmyeloid malignancies receiving myelosuppressive anti-cancer drugs associated with a clinically significant incidence of febrile neutropenia

    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components. Efbemalenograstim alfa is contraindicated for use in patients with a history of serious allergic reactions to granulocyte colony-stimulating factors such as efbemalenograstim alfa, eflapegrastim, pegfilgrastim, or filgrastim products.
    Headline
    sickle cell disease
    General Information
    Use efbemalenograstim alfa with caution in patients with sickle cell disease or disorder because severe sickle cell crises have been reported in this patient population; some cases were fatal. Discontinue efbemalenograstim alfa if sickle cell crisis occurs.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    There are no data regarding the use of efbemalenograstim alfa during pregnancy. However, data from published studies evaluating other recombinant human granulocyte colony-stimulating factors have not established a drug-associated risk of major birth defects, miscarriage, or adverse outcomes. In pregnant rabbits, dosing every other day during organogenesis at cumulative doses up to 0.7 times the human dose showed no signs of fetal loss or structural malformations. Additionally, no fetal loss, malformations, or effects on growth and development were observed in studies in which pregnant rats received efbemalenograstim at cumulative doses up to approximately 2.6 times the recommended human dose during various gestation stages, including organogenesis, from mating through the first half of pregnancy, and from the first trimester through delivery and lactation.
    Headline

There are no data on the presence of efbemalenograstim alfa in human milk, its effects on the breast-fed child, or its effects on milk production. Consider the benefits of breast-feeding, the patient's clinical need for treatment, and any potential adverse effects on the breast-fed child from the medication or from the patient's underlying medical condition.

Description
  Text
  Efbemalenograstim alfa-vuxw is a leukocyte growth factor that binds to cell surface receptors on hematopoietic cells stimulating proliferation, differentiation, commitment, and end cell functional activation. It is indicated to decrease the incidence of infection, as manifested by febrile neutropenia, in patients with non-myeloid malignancies receiving myelosuppressive anticancer drugs associated with a clinically significant incidence of febrile neutropenia. Efbemalenograstim alfa is not indicated for the mobilization of peripheral blood progenitor cells for hematopoietic stem-cell transplantation. Acute respiratory distress syndrome, fatal splenic rupture, and glomerulonephritis may occur with efbemalenograstim alfa.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Efbemalenograstim alfa is a long-acting recombinant human granulocyte colony-stimulating factor (G-CSF) dimer that is linked to an immunoglobulin G2 (IgG2)-Fc fragment produced by covalent coupling of a human G-CSF analog and a Fc fragment of human immunoglobulin G2 via a 16 amino acid linker. It binds to G-CSF receptors on hematopoietic cells and triggers signaling pathways that stimulate cell differentiation, proliferation, commitment, and end cell functional activation.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Subcutaneous Route
    Description
    The pharmacokinetic parameters of efbemalenograstim alfa were nonlinear and time dependent over the dose range of 30 to 360 micrograms/kg in healthy male subjects and female patients with breast cancer who received subcutaneous efbemalenograstim alfa. At the approved recommended dose of efbemalenograstim alfa, the geometric mean Cmax and AUC(0-inf) values were 525 nanograms (ng)/mL (CV, 163%) and 26,217 ng/mL x hour (CV, 167%), respectively, in cycle 3 in patients with breast cancer. Following subcutaneous efbemalenograstim alfa doses of 80 to 320 micrograms/kg in female patients with breast cancer receiving chemotherapy, the median Tmax ranged from 9 to 30 hours in cycle 3.

  Text
  Efbemalenograstim alfa is administered subcutaneously. In pharmacokinetics studies in female breast cancer patients, the geometric mean apparent volume of distribution was 40.7 L (coefficient of variation (CV), 387%), elimination half-life was 36.9 hours (CV, 120%), and clearance was 0.76 L/hour (CV, 167%) in cycle 3 of efbemalenograstim alfa therapy. Neutrophil receptor binding is an important component of the clearance, and serum clearance is directly related to the number of neutrophils. Efbemalenograstim alfa appears to be metabolized via catabolic pathways into small peptides.

    Administration Details
      Name
      Subcutaneous Administration
      Description
      Efbemalenograstim alfa is available as a single-dose 20-mg/mL prefilled syringe; it should be administered by a health care professional.
Do not administer efbemalenograstim alfa less than 24 hours after or within 14 days before administration of cytotoxic chemotherapy.
Store prefilled syringes in the refrigerator at 2 to 8 degrees C (36 and 46 degrees F) in the carton to protect from light; do not shake.
Allow the prefilled syringe to reach room temperature for a minimum of 30 minutes before administering; discard syringes kept at room temperature longer than 48 hours.
Do not freeze the efbemalenograstim alfa prefilled syringe; discard syringe if frozen.
Subcutaneous Injection:
Administer efbemalenograstim alfa subcutaneously; give the entire contents of the prefilled syringe.
Inject into the abdomen, the back or side of the upper arms, or the thighs; rotate injection sites.
Do not inject into scar tissue or areas that are reddened, inflamed, or swollen.
After injecting the entire dose, the needle safety device will be triggered and the needle will be covered by the needle guard.
If a dose is missed, instruct the patient or caregiver to contact their health care provider.
