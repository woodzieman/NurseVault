          180 mg PO once daily until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity. Treatment with zongertinib resulted in an objective response rate (ORR) of 76% (complete response [CR], 11%) in patients with unresectable or metastatic, nonsquamous NSCLC with HER2 (ERBB2) tyrosine kinase domain (TKD) mutations who had not received prior systemic therapy for advanced disease in 1 cohort of a multicenter, open-label, single-arm, multicohort trial (Beamion LUNG-1); the duration of response was at least 6 months in 64% of patients, and at least 12 months in 44% of patients. Treatment with zongertinib resulted in an ORR of 44% (CR, 2.9%) for a median response of 5.4 months in patients with unresectable or metastatic HER2 (ERBB2)-positive nonsquamous NSCLC in patients with a median of 3 prior treatments, including a previous HER2-targeted tyrosine kinase inhibitor in 2.9% of patients (n = 34).

    Headline
    Drug Interactions
    General Information
    The coadministration of certain medications may lead to harm and require avoidance or therapy modification; review all drug interactions prior to concomitant use of other medications.
    Headline
    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components.
    Headline
    hepatic failure
    General Information
    Use zongertinib with caution in patients with hepatic impairment including hepatic failure as treatment with zongertinib has been associated with severe and life-threatening hepatotoxicity, including drug induced liver injury (DILI). Monitor liver function tests including ALT, AST, and total bilirubin at baseline, every 2 weeks for the first 12 weeks, and then monthly thereafter as clinically indicated; monitor more frequently in patients who develop transaminitis. An interruption of therapy, dose reduction, or discontinuation of therapy may be necessary if hepatotoxicity occurs.
    Headline
    coronary artery disease, heart failure, hypertension, thoracic radiation therapy
    General Information
    Use zongertinib with caution in patients with a history of heart failure, or in patients with conditions that could impair left ventricular function including coronary artery disease, uncontrolled hypertension, thoracic radiation therapy and previous anthracycline exposure. Decreases in left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) have occurred with anti-HER2 therapies, including zongertinib; zongertinib has not been studied in patients with a history of clinically significant cardiac disease or LVEF less than 50% at baseline. Monitor the LVEF at baseline, regularly during treatment, and as clinically indicated. An interruption of therapy, dose reduction, or discontinuation of therapy may be necessary if a decrease in LVEF occurs.
    Headline
    pulmonary disease
    General Information
    Use zongertinib with caution in patients who have a history of pre-existing pulmonary disease; severe pneumonitis/interstitial lung disease has been reported in patients treated with zongertinib. Monitor patients for new or worsening respiratory symptoms including fever, cough, or dyspnea. Immediately interrupt zongertinib therapy in patients with new or worsening respiratory symptoms. A dose reduction or permanent discontinuation of therapy may be necessary for recurrent or severe pneumonitis.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Avoid the use of zongertinib during pregnancy. Zongertinib can cause fetal harm, based on its mechanism of action and data from animal studies. There are no adequately controlled studies during human pregnancy. In an embryo-fetal development study, pregnant rats given zongertinib during organogenesis at maternal exposures approximately 19 times human exposure based on AUC at the recommended dose caused decreased fetal weights, delayed development of the urinary system, and kidney hydronephrosis. There were no adverse embryo-fetal findings when zongertinib was administered to pregnant rabbits during organogenesis at exposures up to 2.4 times the human exposure based on AUC at the recommended dose. A literature-based assessment of the effects on reproduction demonstrated that mice expressing catalytically inactive HER2 die at mid-gestation due to cardiac dysfunction. Apprise individuals receiving zongertinib of the potential hazard to the fetus.
    Headline
    people who can cause pregnancy in others, people who may become pregnant, reproductive risk
    General Information
    Counsel people who may become pregnant and people who can cause pregnancy in others about the reproductive risk associated with zongertinib. Zongertinib may cause fetal harm. Verify pregnancy status prior to treatment initiation. Advise people who may become pregnant to use effective contraception while taking zongertinib and for 2 weeks after the last dose. Male and female infertility has been observed in animal studies. The effects in female animals were reversible. However, the effect of zongertinib on testes was not reversible within a 4-week recovery period. Human data are not available.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Advise individuals to avoid breast-feeding during zongertinib treatment and for 2 weeks after the final dose due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in the breastfed child. There are no data on the presence of zongertinib or its metabolites in human milk, the effects on the breastfed child, or the effects on milk production.

Adverse Reactions

  Adverse Reactions
    Headline
    elevated hepatic enzymes, hepatic failure, hepatotoxicity, hyperbilirubinemia
    General Information
    Hepatotoxicity including drug induced liver injury (grade 3, 1.4%; grade 4, 0.3%) and hepatic failure (0.3%) was reported in 27% of patients treated with zongertinib in a pooled safety population. Elevated hepatic enzymes including increased ALT (37%; grade 3 or 4, 5.9%) and increased AST (31%; grade 3 or 4, 4.5%), as well as hyperbilirubinemia or increases from baseline in bilirubin (20%; grade 3 or 4, 1.3%) were reported in the same population. Increases from baseline in alkaline phosphatase occurred in 20% (grade 3 or 4, 1.7%) of patients treated with zongertinib in the Beamion LUNG-1 trial. If hepatotoxicity occurs, an interruption in therapy, dose reduction, or permanent discontinuation of therapy may be necessary depending on severity.

Description
  Text
  Zongertinib is an oral tyrosine kinase inhibitor of human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2). It is indicated for the treatment of adult patients with unresectable or metastatic non-squamous non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) whose tumors have HER2 (ERBB2) tyrosine kinase domain activating mutations, as detected by an FDA-authorized test. Hepatotoxicity, left ventricular dysfunction, and interstitial lung disease/pneumonitis have been reported with zongertinib therapy. Zongertinib was approved by the FDA in August 2025.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Zongertinib is a tyrosine kinase inhibitor of human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2). It inhibits phosphorylation of HER2, downstream signaling of HER2 (phosphorylation of ERK), and proliferation of lung cancer cells harboring HER2 tyrosine kinase domain activating mutations in vitro. Zongertinib also demonstrated antitumor activity in mouse xenograft models of NSCLC harboring HER2 tyrosine kinase domain activating mutations in vivo.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Oral Route
    Description
    Following zongertinib 120 mg PO daily, the Cmax is 3 micromol/L (CV, 37%) and the AUC is 34 micromol x hour/L (CV, 34%). The AUC and Cmax of zongertinib increase in an approximately dose proportional manner across the dose range of 60 mg (0.5 times the approved recommended dose) to 360 mg (3 times the approved recommended dose). Zongertinib accumulation is approximately 1.3-fold for Cmax and 1.5-fold for AUC at the approved recommended dosage; steady-state is achieved within 2.5 days. The absolute bioavailability of zongertinib is 76% with a median Tmax of approximately 2 hours (range, 2 to 6 hours).
Effect of food: Following administration of a single 240 mg dose (2 times the approved recommended dose) with a high-fat meal (approximately 1,000 calories, approximately 50% fat), no clinically significant differences in the AUC or Cmax of zongertinib were observed.

  Text
  Zongertinib is administered orally. Zongertinib plasma protein binding is more than 99% with a mean apparent volume of distribution of 118 L (CV, 29%). CYP-mediated oxidation pathways (primarily CYP3A4 and CYP3A5) represent 48% to 62%, glucuronidation (mainly UGT1A4) 13% to 25%, and glutathione conjugation 13% to 26% of total hepatic metabolism of zongertinib based on in vitro metabolite profiling. Unchanged zongertinib represented the majority (75%) of total radioactivity in the plasma. The mean effective half-life of zongertinib is 12 hours (CV, 21%) with a mean apparent clearance of 115 mL/min (CV, 31%). Following a single oral dose of radiolabeled zongertinib 60 mg, approximately 93% of the dose was recovered in the feces (31% as unchanged drug) and 1.3% in the urine (0.2% as unchanged drug).
Pharmcodynamics
A mean increase in the QTc interval of more than 20 msec was not observed at 2.6 times the mean maximal concentration provided by the recommended dose of 120 mg.
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes and drug transporters: CYP3A, BCRP
Zongertinib is metabolized by CYP3A4, CYP3A5, and UGT1A4. However, no clinically significant differences in zongertinib pharmacokinetics were observed when used concomitantly with a strong CYP3A inhibitor. Coadministration with carbamazepine, a strong CYP3A4 inducer, decreased the zongertinib AUC by 63% and Cmax by 43%; the effect of concomitant use of a moderate CYP3A inducer on zongertinib exposure is unknown. Zongertinib is a BCRP inhibitor. Following concomitant use of zongertinib 120 mg daily for 12 days with rosuvastatin (BCRP substrate), the Cmax of rosuvastatin increased by 3-fold and the AUC increased by 2.3-fold.

Dosage Adjustment Guidelines
  Hepatic Impairment
  Baseline Hepatic Impairment
Specific guidelines for dosage adjustments in hepatic impairment are not available; it appears that no dosage adjustments are needed.
Treatment-Related Hepatotoxicity
Grade 3 or 4 increased ALT and/or AST without increased bilirubin: Hold zongertinib. When the ALT and/or AST recovers to grade 1 or baseline, reduce the dose of zongertinib by 1 dose level and resume therapy.
Grade 3 increased bilirubin: Hold zongertinib. When ALT/AST recover to grade 1 or baseline, reduce the dose of zongertinib by 1 dose level and resume therapy.
Grade 4 increased bilirubin: Permanently discontinue zongertinib therapy.
AST or ALT 3 times the upper limit of normal (ULN) or more with total bilirubin 2 times ULN or more: Permanently discontinue zongertinib therapy.
