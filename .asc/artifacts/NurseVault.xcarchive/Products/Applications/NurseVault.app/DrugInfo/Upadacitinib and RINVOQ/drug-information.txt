Indication Specific Dosing
  General Information
  Reserve upadacitinib for patients who have an inadequate response or intolerance to other therapies (i.e., TNF inhibitors). Consider the benefits and risks for the individual patient before initiation or continuation of therapy, particular in those with risk factors such as smoking, cardiovascular risk factors, those who develop malignancy while receiving upadacitinib therapy, and those with a known malignancy other than successfully treated non-melanoma skin cancer (NMSC). A large randomized safety clinical trial showed an increased risk of malignancy, all-cause morality (including sudden cardiac death), major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE; defined as cardiovascular death, non-fatal myocardial infarction, non-fatal stroke), and thrombosis (including deep venous thrombosis (DVT), pulmonary embolism (PE), and arterial thrombosis) with use of tofacitinib, another JAK inhibitor, compared to tumor necrosis factor (TNF) inhibitors in patients with RA. Upadacitinib has not been studied in similar trials; however, the risks may be similar for all JAK inhibitors used for inflammatory conditions.

    General Information
    Update immunizations in agreement with current immunization guidelines prior to initiating upadacitinib therapy. Avoid the use of live vaccines during or immediately prior to upadacitinib therapy.
    Headline
    infection
    General Information
    Treatment with upadacitinib increases the risk of serious infections that may lead to hospitalization or death. Avoid use in people with an active, serious infection, including localized infections. Use upadacitinib with caution in patients at increased risk for serious infections, including those with chronic or recurrent infections, a history of serious or opportunistic infections, underlying conditions that may predispose to infection, and those receiving concomitant immunosuppressants (e.g., corticosteroids, methotrexate). Patients residing in or with travel history to regions where mycoses are endemic (e.g., Ohio and Mississippi River valleys, Southwest) are at increased risk for invasive fungal infections. Viral reactivation (herpes zoster, hepatitis B) has occurred with upadacitinib treatment, with the risk of herpes zoster being higher in certain geographic regions (e.g., Japan). Consider temporarily interrupting therapy in patients who develop herpes zoster until the episode resolves. Perform screening for viral hepatitis in accordance with clinical guidelines before starting upadacitinib. Treatment with upadacitinib is not recommended in patients with active hepatitis B infection or hepatitis C infection. Upadacitinib also increases the risk of tuberculosis (TB) infection. Screen all patients for active or latent TB infection prior to starting upadacitinib. If positive for active TB, do not give upadacitinib. If positive for latent TB, treat with standard antimycobacterial therapy before starting upadacitinib. Consider anti-tuberculosis therapy in patients with a history of latent or active TB in whom an adequate course of treatment cannot be confirmed, and in those with risk factors for TB infection. Consult a physician with expertise in treating TB about whether starting anti-tuberculosis therapy is appropriate for the individual patient. Monitor for signs and symptoms of TB and other infections during and after therapy, even in patients who tested negative for latent TB prior to initiation. For patients who develop an infection during treatment with upadacitinib, perform a prompt and complete diagnostic workup appropriate for immunocompromised patients and initiate appropriate antimicrobial therapy. If a patient develops a serious or opportunistic infection or has an infection that is not responding to antimicrobial therapy, interrupt treatment with upadacitinib until the infection is controlled.
    Headline
    cardiovascular disease, mortality, serious cardiovascular events, thromboembolism, thrombophilia, tobacco smoking
    General Information
    Due to the risk for serious cardiovascular events, reserve upadacitinib for patients who have had an inadequate response or intolerance to one or more TNF inhibitors. Avoid the use of upadacitinib in people at increased risk of thrombosis and thromboembolism. A current or past history of tobacco smoking adds to the increased risk of these events. Consider the benefits and risks for the individual patient before starting or continuing upadacitinib therapy, particularly in those with thrombophilia, risk factors for cardiovascular disease, or who are current or past smokers. Serious cardiovascular events have been reported with the Janus kinase (JAK) inhibitors class of medications. A higher rate of all-cause mortality (including sudden cardiovascular death), major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE; defined as cardiovascular death, non-fatal myocardial infarction (MI), and non-fatal stroke), and thrombosis [including deep venous thrombosis (DVT), pulmonary embolism (PE)] were observed in rheumatoid arthritis patients taking tofacitinib, another JAK inhibitor, compared to patients taking tumor necrosis factor (TNF) inhibitors. Upadacitinib has not been studied in similar trials; however, the risks may be similar for all JAK inhibitors used for inflammatory conditions. Thrombosis, including DVT, PE, and arterial thrombosis, have been observed at an increased incidence in upadacitinib recipients; many events were serious and some resulted in death. Advise patients to seek emergency medical attention if they experience signs and symptoms of serious cardiovascular events. If a patient develops a thromboembolic event, discontinue use of the drug.
    Headline
    malignancy
    General Information
    Upadacitinib may increase the risk of malignancy; reserve upadacitinib for patients who have an inadequate response or intolerance to one or more TNF inhibitors. In a large, postmarketing, randomized safety clinical trial in rheumatoid arthritis patients, higher rates of lymphomas and malignancies [excluding non-melanoma skin cancer (NMSC)] were observed with the use of tofacitinib, another JAK inhibitor, compared to tumor necrosis factor (TNF) inhibitors. A higher rate of lung cancer was also observed in patients with a history of current or past tobacco smoking treated with the JAK inhibitor compared to those treated with TNF inhibitors. Patients with a history of current or past tobacco smoking were also at an increased risk of overall cancer. Upadacitinib has not been studied in similar trials; however, the risks may be similar for all JAK inhibitors used for inflammatory conditions. Consider the benefits and risks for the individual patient before starting or continuing upadacitinib therapy, particularly in those with risk factors such as smoking, those who develop malignancy, and those with a known malignancy other than successfully treated NMCS. NMSCs have been observed in patients taking upadacitinib. Patients at increased risk of skin cancer should undergo periodic skin examinations. Instruct patients to limit sunlight (UV) exposure by wearing protective clothing and using broad-spectrum sunscreen.
    Headline
    diverticulitis, peptic ulcer disease
    General Information
    Use upadacitinib with caution in people who may be at increased risk for gastrointestinal (GI) perforation, such as those with a history of diverticulitis or peptic ulcer disease, or those taking nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) or corticosteroids. Promptly evaluate treated patients presenting with new onset abdominal symptoms for early identification of GI perforation.
    Headline
    Child-Pugh class A, Child-Pugh class B, Child-Pugh class C, hepatic failure
    General Information
    Upadacitinib is not recommended for use in people with severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh class C) or hepatic failure. For patients with ulcerative colitis or Crohn disease who have mild (Child-Pugh class A) or moderate (Child-Pugh class B) hepatic impairment, dosage adjustments may be needed. No dosage adjustments are required for other patients with mild to moderate hepatic impairment.
    Headline
    renal failure, renal impairment
    General Information
    Upadacitinib is not recommended for use in people with end stage renal failure (eGFR less than 15 mL/minute/1.73 m2). Dosage adjustments may be needed in atopic dermatitis, ulcerative colitis, and Crohn's disease patients with severe renal impairment (eGFR 15 to 29 mL/minute/1.73 m2). No dosage adjustments are needed in these patients with mild or moderate renal impairment (eGFR 30 mL/minute/1.73 m2 or greater). In other treated patient populations, no upadacitinib dosage adjustments are needed for mild, moderate, or severe renal impairment (eGFR 15 mL/minute/1.73 m2 or greater).
    Headline
    anemia, lymphopenia, neutropenia
    General Information
    Determine the hemoglobin concentration, absolute neutrophil count (ANC), and absolute lymphocyte count (ALC) before and during treatment with upadacitinib. Avoid or interrupt upadacitinib treatment in patients with anemia (hemoglobin less than 8 grams/dL), neutropenia (ANC less than 1,000 cells/mm3), or lymphopenia (ALC less than 500 cells/mm3).
    Headline
    planned procedure
    General Information
    Patients who undergo surgery while taking biologic therapy, such as upadacitinib, may be at greater risk for postoperative infections. For a planned procedure, balance the risk of postoperative infection against the risk of developing a severe or unstable disease by stopping the biologic therapy. The American College of Rheumatology recommends holding upadacitinib for 3 days before undergoing total knee or total hip arthroplasties.
    Headline
    geriatric
    General Information
    Geriatric patients (65 years and older) may be at increased risk for upadacitinib-associated adverse reactions, including serious infections and malignancies. Use upadacitinib with caution in older adults, and monitor closely for adverse effects.
    Headline
    diabetes mellitus
    General Information
    Use upadacitinib with caution in people with diabetes mellitus. Hypoglycemia, including severe hypoglycemia, has been reported in individuals with diabetes mellitus who started upadacitinib and other JAK inhibitors. During treatment with upadacitinib, consider increased blood glucose monitoring as clinically indicated in individuals with diabetes. Advise individuals to notify their health care provider if they develop signs or symptoms of hypoglycemia.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Upadacitinib may cause fetal harm, based on data from animal studies. Data are insufficient to evaluate a drug-associated risk of major birth defects or miscarriage. Increased disease activity in patients with rheumatoid arthritis or inflammatory bowel disease during pregnancy is associated with an increased risk for adverse outcomes such as preterm delivery, low birth weight, and small for gestational age at birth. In animal studies, upadacitinib exposure in rats during organogenesis at doses as low as 0.6 times the maximum recommended human dose (MRHD) based on AUC resulted in skeletal malformations (e.g., misshapen humerus, bent scapula). Bent forelimbs, bent hindlimbs, rib defects, vertebral defects, and decreased fetal body weights occurred with exposures approximately 31 times the MRHD. In rabbits, embryolethality, decreased fetal body weights, and cardiovascular malformations were observed at doses approximately 5.6 times the MRHD. No developmental toxicity was observed at exposures approximately 0.11 and 0.82 times the MRHD in rats and rabbits, respectively. Report exposures during pregnancy to the pregnancy surveillance program at 1-800-633-9110.
    Headline
    people who may become pregnant, reproductive risk
    General Information
    Counsel people who may become pregnant about the reproductive risk associated with upadacitinib. Upadacitinib may cause fetal harm. Verify pregnancy status prior to treatment initiation. Advise people who may become pregnant to use effective contraception while taking upadacitinib and for 4 weeks after the last dose.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Avoid breast-feeding during upadacitinib therapy and for 6 days after the last dose. Upadacitinib has the potential to cause serious side effects in the breastfed child. There are no data on the presence of upadacitinib in human milk, its effects on the breastfed child, or the effects on milk production. Upadacitinib is present in animal milk.

Interactions
Abatacept: (Major) Concomitant use of upadacitinib with biologic DMARDs, such as abatacept, is not recommended because of the possibility of increased immunosuppression and increased infection risk. Upadacitinib may be used as monotherapy or concomitantly with methotrexate or other nonbiologic DMARDs.
Adagrasib: (Major) During concomitant use of upadacitinib and adagrasib reduce the upadacitinib dosage to 15 mg once daily. During induction for ulcerative colitis and Crohn's disease reduce the upadacitinib dosage to 30 mg once daily. Concomitant use may increase upadacitinib exposure and risk for adverse effects. Concomitant use with another strong CYP3A inhibitor increased upadacitinib overall exposure 1.75-fold.

Adverse Reactions
  Headline
  Adverse Reactions
  Adverse Reactions
    Headline
    candidiasis, cough, fever, infection, influenza, laryngitis, pharyngitis, rhinitis, sinusitis
    General Information
    Closely monitor patients for the development of signs and symptoms of infection during and after treatment with upadacitinib. Interrupt therapy with upadacitinib if a patient develops a serious infection, an opportunistic infection, or sepsis. Perform a prompt and complete diagnostic evaluation appropriate for an immunocompromised patient, initiate antimicrobial therapy, and closely monitor patients who develop a new infection. Serious and sometimes fatal bacterial infection, mycobacterial infection, invasive fungal infection, viral infection, or infections due to other opportunistic pathogens have been reported in patients receiving upadacitinib. In clinical trials evaluating upadacitinib for the treatment of ulcerative colitis, upper respiratory infections were reported in 9% to 20% of adult patients receiving upadacitinib and 18% of patients receiving placebo. In a study involving patients with giant cell arteritis, 21% of upadacitinib recipients and 20% of those who received a placebo developed an upper respiratory tract infection, defined as acute sinusitis, laryngitis, nasopharyngitis, oropharyngeal pain, pharyngitis, viral pharyngitis, pharyngotonsillitis, rhinitis, tonsillitis, and viral infection. Upadacitinib-treated giant cell arteritis patients also reported cough (7%) and cases of herpes zoster (5%), which included herpes zoster, herpes zoster oticus, and ophthalmic herpes zoster. In the 52-week placebo-controlled study period, opportunistic infections were reported in 4 patients receiving 15 mg of upadacitinib (2.3 per 100 patient-years) and 1 placebo patient (1.1 per 100 patient-years). In clinical trials evaluating use of upadacitinib for the treatment of atopic dermatitis, upper respiratory tract infections were reported in 23% to 25% of upadacitinib recipients (age 12 and older) and 17% of patients receiving a placebo. In clinical trials evaluating upadacitinib for the treatment of Crohn's disease, upper respiratory infections were reported in 14% of adult patients receiving upadacitinib 15 mg, 12% receiving upadacitinib 30 mg, and 13% receiving upadacitinib 45 mg. Upper respiratory tract infections included the following: laryngitis, nasopharyngitis, pharyngitis, oropharyngeal pain, pharyngeal abscess, pharyngotonsillitis, rhinitis, rhinolaryngitis, sinusitis, tonsillitis, and viral upper respiratory tract infection. Other infections reported in upadacitinib treated patients included herpes simplex (2% to 8%), herpes zoster and varicella (2% to 4%), influenza (2% to 3%), oral candidiasis (less than 1%), and pneumonia (less than 1%). Cough was reported in 3% and fever in 2% of patients receiving upadacitinib and at incidences higher than with placebo. During the 16 week placebo-controlled period of the atopic dermatitis trials, eczema herpeticum was reported in 6 patients (2.2 events per 100 patient-years) treated with 15 mg upadacitinib, 7 patients (2.6 events per 100 patient-years) treated with 30 mg upadacitinib, and 4 patients (1.6 events per 100 patient-years) treated with placebo. Description
  Text
  Upadacitinib is an oral Janus kinase (JAK) inhibitor and a target specific disease-modifying antirheumatic drug (tsDMARD). Upadacitinib has been shown useful for a variety of inflammatory conditions, including moderately to severely active ulcerative colitis (UC) or Crohn Disease (CD), moderately to severely active rheumatoid arthritis (RA), active non-radiographic axial spondyloarthritis, active ankylosing spondylitis (AS), giant cell arteritis (temporal arteritis), refractory, moderate-to-severe atopic dermatitis, active psoriatic arthritis (PsA), and polyarticular juvenile idiopathic arthritis (pJIA). In people with RA, upadacitinib also improves symptoms and inhibits the radiographic progression of structural joint damage. For inflammatory arthritis, upadacitinib may be used as monotherapy or in combination with methotrexate or other non-biologic disease-modifying antirheumatic drugs (DMARDs) that are not potent immunosuppressant agents. The ideal combination of therapy for individuals with inflammatory arthritis conditions is determined by treat-to-target strategies, as well as factors such as severity of disease and comorbid conditions. Upadacitinib can induce and maintain remission in adults with moderate-to-severe UC and CD. Adult treatment guidelines support upadacitinib as an option for adults with UC or CD who have had an inadequate response, loss of response, or intolerance to anti-TNF therapy. JAK inhibitors carry a boxed warning regarding the increased risk for serious infections, malignancy, thrombosis and related major cardiovascular events. Do not use upadacitinib concomitantly with other JAK inhibitors, other biologic DMARDs or biologic immunomodulators, or with potent immunosuppressants such as azathioprine or cyclosporine. Upadacitinib was FDA-approved in 2019.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Upadacitinib is an oral Janus kinase (JAK) inhibitor. Janus kinases, part of the tyrosine kinase enzyme family, are intracellular enzymes that transmit signals arising from cytokine or growth factor-receptor interactions on the cellular membrane to influence cellular processes of immune cell function and hematopoiesis. JAK-mediated signaling is pivotal in immune activation, as cytokine receptors are expressed on most immune cells. JAKs are responsible for the intracellular signaling in type I and type II cytokine receptors located on over 50 cytokines, interleukins, interferons, colony-stimulating factors, and hormones. Upon ligand and receptor interaction, JAKs phosphorylate and activate signal transducers and activators of transcription proteins (STATs), which modulate intracellular activity including gene expression. Upadacitinib modulates the signaling pathway by inhibiting JAKs and preventing phosphorylation and activation of STATs preventing intracellular signal transduction. Cytokine signaling is transmitted through the pairing of JAKs, such as JAK1/JAK3, JAK1/JAK2, and JAK2/JAK2. Upadacitinib has a greater affinity for JAK1 and JAK2 compared to JAK3 and TYK2. In cellular assays, upadacitinib had a 74-fold higher selectivity for JAK1 compared to JAK2 and, in biochemical assays, a 58-fold higher selectivity for JAK1 compared to JAK3. In human leukocytes, upadacitinib exhibited greater inhibition of cytokine-induced STAT phosphorylation mediated by JAK1 and JAK1/JAK3 compared to JAK2/JAK2. The selectivity for JAK1 may be advantageous due to the potential decreased risk of adverse effects. However, the relevance of specific JAK combinations to therapeutic effectiveness is not known.

  Upadacitinib is administered orally. Plasma protein binding is 52%. Upadacitinib distributes equally between red blood cells and plasma with a blood to plasma ratio of 1. Affected cytochrome P450 (CYP450) isoenzymes and drug transporters: CYP3A4, CYP2C19
Upadacitinib is primarily a substrate of CYP3A4, with minor contributions from CYP2C19. The Cmax and AUC of upadacitinib are increased when coadministered with strong CYP3A4 inhibitors; caution is recommended. Due to a reduction in Cmax and AUC, there is a potential for reduced therapeutic effect when upadacitinib is coadministered with strong CYP3A4 inducers and concomitant use is not recommended.
 
In vitro studies demonstrated that upadacitinib does not inhibit or induce the activity CYP1A2, CYP2B6, CYP2C8, CYP2C9, CYP2C19, CYP2D6, and CYP3A4 at clinically relevant concentrations. Upadactinib was not found to inhibit the transporters P-glycoprotein (P-gp), BCRP, OATP1B1, OATP1B3, OCT1, OCT2, OAT1, OAT3, MATE1, and MATE2K at clinically relevant concentrations during these studies.

Dosage Adjustment Guidelines
  Hepatic Impairment
  Rheumatoid arthritis, psoriatic arthritis, atopic dermatitis, ankylosing spondylitis, non-radiographic axial spondyloarthritis, giant cell arteritis, and polyarticular juvenile idiopathic arthritis:
Mild (Child Pugh A) or moderate (Child Pugh B) impairment: No dosage adjustments are required.
Severe (Child Pugh C) impairment: Use is not recommended.
 
Ulcerative colitis:
Mild (Child Pugh A) or moderate (Child Pugh B) impairment: The recommended dose is 30 mg PO once daily for 8 weeks for induction. Then, 15 mg PO daily for maintenance.
Severe (Child Pugh C) impairment: Use is not recommended.
 
Crohn disease:
Mild (Child Pugh A) or moderate (Child Pugh B) impairment: The recommended dose is 30 mg PO once daily for 12 weeks for induction. Then, 15 mg PO daily for maintenance.
Severe (Child Pugh C) impairment: Use is not recommended.
 
Abnormalities in hepatic transaminases that occur during treatment
If increases in ALT or AST are observed during routine patient management and drug-induced liver injury is suspected, interrupt upadacitinib treatment until this diagnosis is excluded. Prompt investigation of the cause of liver enzyme elevation is recommended.
  Renal Impairment
  Rheumatoid arthritis, psoriatic arthritis, ankylosing spondylitis, non-radiographic axial spondyloarthritis, giant cell arteritis, and polyarticular juvenile idiopathic arthritis:
Mild, moderate, or severe renal impairment (eGFR 15 to less than 90 mL/min/1.73 m2}.: No dosage adjustments are needed.
 
Atopic dermatitis:
Mild or moderate renal impairment (eGFR 30 mL/minute/1.73 m2 or more): No dosage adjustments are needed.
Severe renal impairment (eGFR 15 to less than 30 mL/minute/1.73 m2): The recommended dose is 15 mg PO once daily.
End stage renal disease (eGFR less than 15 mL/minute/1.73 m2): Use is not recommended.
 
Ulcerative colitis:
Mild or moderate renal impairment (eGFR 30 mL/minute/1.73 m2 or more): No dosage adjustments are needed.
Severe renal impairment (eGFR 15 to less than 30 mL/minute/1.73 m2): The recommended dose is 30 mg PO once daily for 8 weeks for induction. Then, 15 mg PO daily for maintenance.
End stage renal disease (eGFR less than 15 mL/minute/1.73 m2): Use is not recommended.
 
Crohn disease:
Mild or moderate renal impairment (eGFR 30 mL/minute/1.73 m2 or more): No dosage adjustments are needed.
Severe renal impairment (eGFR 15 to less than 30 mL/minute/1.73 m2): The recommended dose is 30 mg PO once daily for 12 weeks for induction. Then, 15 mg PO daily for maintenance.
End stage renal disease (eGFR less than 15 mL/minute/1.73 m2): Use is not recommended.
 
Intermittent hemodialysis
Upadacitinib has not been studied in people with end-stage renal disease (ESRD) (eGFR less than 15 mL/minute/1.73 m2):; use not recommended.
