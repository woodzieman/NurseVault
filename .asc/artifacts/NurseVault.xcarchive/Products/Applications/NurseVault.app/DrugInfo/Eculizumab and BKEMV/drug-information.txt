Indication Specific Dosing
  General Information
  General Dosing Information
Vaccinate persons against meningococcal infection (serogroups A, C, W, Y, and B) at least 2 weeks prior to initiation of eculizumab according to the most current Advisory Committee on Immunization Practices (ACIP) recommendations for persons receiving a complement inhibitor.
If urgent eculizumab therapy is indicated in persons who are not up to date with meningococcal vaccines according to ACIP recommendations, administer meningococcal vaccine(s) as soon as possible and provide antibacterial medication prophylaxis.
A supplemental dose of eculizumab is required in the setting of plasmapheresis or plasma exchange and in persons receiving fresh frozen plasma infusions.
Monitor individuals being treated for paroxysmal nocturnal hemoglobinuria after discontinuing eculizumab for at least 8 weeks to detect hemolysis.
Monitor individuals being treated for atypical hemolytic-uremic syndrome after discontinuing eculizumab for at least 12 weeks for signs and symptoms of thrombotic microangiopathy (TMA) complications.
If TMA complications occur after eculizumab discontinuation, consider reinstitution of eculizumab treatment, plasma therapy [plasmapheresis, plasma exchange, or fresh frozen plasma infusion (PE/PI)], or appropriate organ-specific supportive measures.

Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  General Information
  Use eculizumab with caution in any patient with a systemic infection; eculizumab is contraindicated for use by patients with unresolved, serious Neisseria meningitidis infection. Eculizumab blocks terminal complement and increases the risk of serious meningococcal infections such as septicemia and meningitis. Patients with a genetic deficiency of terminal complement proteins are at increased risk of Neisseria meningitidis infections, and eculizumab has a similar mechanism of action. Do not administer eculizumab to patients who are not currently vaccinated against Neisseria meningitidis. In patients without a history of prior meningococcal vaccination, the vaccine must be administered at least 2 weeks before the first dose of eculizumab is given. Consult current vaccination schedules for revaccination information. Use of the quadrivalent, conjugated meningococcal vaccine is strongly recommended; of note, vaccination may not prevent infection. During clinical trials, 2 of 196 patients who had been previously vaccinated against Neisseria meningitidis developed serious meningitis infections. Monitor patients for signs of meningococcal infections. If an infection is suspected, the patients should be evaluated immediately and treated with antibiotics, if necessary. Strongly consider discontinuing eculizumab during the treatment of such infections.
 
The safe and effective use of eculizumab has not been established in children < 18 years of age.
 
Eculizumab is classified as FDA pregnancy risk category C. There are no clinical trials describing the use of eculizumab in pregnant women. Because eculizumab is a recombinant IgG molecule, it is expected to cross the placenta. However, pregnant women with paroxysmal nocturnal hemoglobinuria (PNH) and their fetuses are at high risk for morbidity and mortality during pregnancy and the postpartum period. Eculizumab should only be used during pregnancy if the potential benefit justifies the potential risk to the fetus.
 
According to the manufacturer, weigh the unknown risks to the infant from gastrointestinal or limited systemic exposure to eculizumab against the known benefits of breastfeeding. Eculizumab excretion into human milk is unknown but is expected, as IgG is excreted in human milk. Data suggest that breast milk antibodies do not enter the neonatal and infant circulation in substantial amounts. Consider the benefits of breast-feeding, the risk of infant drug exposure, and the risk of an untreated or inadequately treated condition. If a breast-feeding infant experiences an adverse effect related to a maternally ingested drug, healthcare providers are encouraged to report the adverse effect to the FDA.

    children, immunosuppression, infants, meningococcal infection, neutropenia
    General Information
    Eculizumab is contraindicated in individuals with unresolved meningococcal infection. Eculizumab increases the risk of serious, life-threatening, or fatal infections caused by meningococcal bacteria in any serogroup, including non-groupable strains. Complete or update meningococcal vaccination (for serogroups A, C, W, Y, and B) at least 2 weeks prior to administration of the first dose of eculizumab, according to current Advisory Committee on Immunization Practices (ACIP) recommendations for individuals receiving a complement inhibitor. Revaccinate considering the duration of eculizumab therapy. Note that ACIP recommends an administration schedule in individuals receiving complement inhibitors that differs from the administration schedule in the vaccine prescribing information. If urgent eculizumab therapy is indicated in individuals who are not up to date with meningococcal vaccines according to ACIP recommendations, administer meningococcal vaccine(s) as soon as possible and provide antibacterial medication prophylaxis. The optimal durations and medication regimens for antibacterial medication prophylaxis and their efficacy have not been studied in individuals receiving complement inhibitors, including eculizumab. Consider the benefits and risks of treatment with eculizumab in addition to the benefits and risks of antibacterial medication prophylaxis in unvaccinated or vaccinated individuals against the known risks for serious infections caused by N. meningitidis. Closely monitor for early signs and symptoms of meningococcal infection and evaluate immediately if infection is suspected. Instruct individuals to seek immediate medical care if these signs and symptoms occur. Promptly treat known infections. Meningococcal infection may become rapidly life-threatening or fatal if not recognized and treated early. Consider interruption of eculizumab in individuals who are being treated for a serious meningococcal infection, depending on the risks of interrupting treatment in the disease being treated. The use of eculizumab increases susceptibility to encapsulated bacteria infection, especially those caused by N. meningitidis but also S. pneumoniae, H. influenzae, and to a lesser extent, N. gonorrhoeae. Additionally, fungal infection with Aspergillus sp. has occurred in individuals with immunosuppression and neutropenia. Infants and children treated with eculizumab may be at increased risk of developing serious S. pneumoniae and H. influenzae type b (Hib) infections; administer vaccinations for the prevention of pneumococcal infection and Hib infection according to ACIP guidelines. Vaccination does not eliminate the risk for infections due to these organisms, despite development of antibodies after vaccination.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    There are insufficient data on eculizumab use in human pregnancy to inform a drug-associated risk of major birth defects and miscarriage. Eculizumab, a recombinant IgG molecule (humanized anti-C5 antibody), is expected to cross the placenta. A pooled analysis of prospectively and retrospectively collected data of more than 300 women with live births following exposure to eculizumab have not raised safety concerns. However, due to the limited sample size, these data cannot definitively exclude any drug-associated rusk during pregnancy. Animal studies using a mouse analogue of the eculizumab molecule (murine anti-C5 antibody) showed increased rates of developmental abnormalities and an increased rate of dead and moribund offspring at doses 2 to 8 times the human dose. Advise pregnant women of the potential risk to the fetus. The manufacturer's disease registries collect pregnancy outcomes in women exposed to eculizumab during pregnancy. To enroll or obtain information, contact www.pnhregistry.com or www.ahusregistry.com, or call 215-616-3558. A healthy male infant was delivered at 38 weeks gestation to a woman who had taken eculizumab for paroxysmal nocturnal hemoglobinuria (PNH) throughout the entire pregnancy. The infant was developing normally at 9 months of age. Two additional healthy babies were born to women who received eculizumab throughout their pregnancies. In both women, therapeutic serum eculizumab concentrations were noted at delivery, and no eculizumab was detected in the cord blood. Three additional women delivered healthy babies after eculizumab receipt during early pregnancy: eculizumab was stopped at gestation weeks 5, 14, and 4. Lastly, 4 healthy babies (2 sets of twins) were born to women who received eculizumab later in pregnancy (week 30 in 1 mother and week 27 in the other). In 1 mother, therapeutic serum eculizumab concentrations were noted at delivery, and low concentrations were noted in the cord blood of both twins, but cord drug concentrations were within the background concentrations for the assay and insufficient to block complement.

    breast-feeding
    General Information
    There is no information regarding the presence of eculizumab in human milk, the effects on the breast-fed infant, or the effects on milk production. IgG is excreted in human milk, so it is expected that eculizumab will be present in human milk. However, published data suggest that antibodies in human milk do not enter the neonatal and infant circulation in substantial amounts. Consider the developmental and health benefits of breast-feeding along with the mother's clinical need for eculizumab and any potential adverse effects on the breast-fed child from eculizumab or the underlying maternal condition. In a small study, excretion of the drug in breast milk was evaluated in a eculizumab-treated lactating mother. The drug was not detected during the analysis on postpartum days 1, 2, 3, 9, and 10.

Interactions
Cholera Vaccine: (Moderate) Patients receiving immunosuppressant medications may have a diminished response to the live cholera vaccine. When feasible, administer indicated vaccines prior to initiating immunosuppressant medications. Counsel patients receiving immunosuppressant medications about the possibility of a diminished vaccine response and to continue to follow precautions to avoid exposure to cholera bacteria after receiving the vaccine.
Crovalimab: (Major) Avoid concomitant use of crovalimab with eculizumab. When switching C5 inhibitors, a washout period is required; administer the first loading dose of the new medication no sooner than the time of the next scheduled C5 inhibitor administration. Concomitant use or an inadequate washout duration increases the risk for Type III hypersensitivity reactions related to the formation of drug-target-drug complexes. Allowing at least 5.5 medication half-lives to elapse between medication administrations minimizes the risk of adverse effects.
Dengue Tetravalent Vaccine, Live: (Contraindicated) Avoid administration of the live dengue virus vaccine with immunosuppressive drug therapy and prior to immune recovery following treatment with immunosuppressive drug therapy. When feasible, administer indicated live virus vaccines at least 4 weeks before planned immunosuppression or wait until at least 3 months after discontinuation. The time to restoration of immune competence may be longer in some patients. Patients with altered immunocompetence may be at increased risk for severe adverse reactions due to uninhibited growth of the attenuated live virus. Additionally, vaccines may be less effective if administered during a period of altered immunocompetence.
Efgartigimod Alfa: (Moderate) Monitor for a decrease in eculizumab efficacy during concomitant use with neonatal Fc receptor (FcRn) blockers; avoid long-term concomitant use. Concomitant use may decrease eculizumab overall exposure.
Efgartigimod Alfa; Hyaluronidase: (Moderate) Monitor for a decrease in eculizumab efficacy during concomitant use with neonatal Fc receptor (FcRn) blockers; avoid long-term concomitant use. Concomitant use may decrease eculizumab overall exposure.
Hyaluronidase, Recombinant; Immune Globulin: (Major) A supplemental dose of eculizumab is required for patients with generalized myasthenia gravis who are also receiving treatment with intravenous immune globulin (IVIG). Supplemental dosing specifics are based on the most recent dose of eculizumab received: administer an additional 600 mg of eculizumab if the most recent eculizumab dose was 900 mg or more or an additional 300 mg of eculizumab if the most recent eculizumab dose was 600 mg or less. For patients receiving IVIG at least every 4 weeks, administer the supplemental dose at the same time as the scheduled eculizumab dose. For patients receiving IVIG less often than every 4 weeks, administer the supplemental dose at the next scheduled eculizumab dose after the final IVIG cycle. Adverse Reactions
  Headline
  Adverse Reactions
  General Information
  The most commonly reported adverse reactions during clinical trials include headache (44% eculizumab vs. 27% placebo), nasopharyngitis (23% vs. 18%), back pain (19% vs. 9%), nausea/vomiting (16% vs. 11%), fatigue (12% vs. 2%), cough (12% vs. 9%), constipation (7% vs. 5%), myalgia (7% vs. 2%), and pain in extremity (7% vs. 2%). Anemia and pyrexia (fever) were both reported in 2% of patients in a single-arm study of patients receiving eculizumab.
 
Low-titers of antibodies to eculizumab were detected in 2% of all patients receiving eculizumab. A correlation between response to treatment and antibody formation was not found.
 
Hypersensitivity reactions, including anaphylactoid reactions, are possible. During clinical trials, no patients experienced infusion related reactions requiring drug discontinuation; however, eculizumab should be stopped in patients experiencing severe infusion reactions. During clinical trials, one patient experienced nausea/vomiting and a headache during the infusion with dizziness and shivering occurring the next day. Appropriate supportive care should be instituted.
 
Herpes simplex infection (7% eculizumab vs. 0% placebo), sinusitis (7% vs. 0%), and respiratory tract infection (7% vs. 2%) were reported more commonly in patients receiving eculizumab vs. placebo during clinical trials. In addition, symptoms consistent with an influenza-like illness were also reported more commonly (5% eculizumab vs. 2% placebo). During clinical trials, 2 of 196 patients with paroxysmal nocturnal hemoglobinuria (PNH) who had been previously vaccinated against Neisseria meningitidis developed serious meningitis infections. In addition, 1 patient without PNH who was not previously vaccinated developed a serious meningitis infection. Do not administer eculizumab to patients who are not currently vaccinated against Neisseria meningitidis (see Contraindications). Monitor patients for early signs of meningococcal infections. If an infection is suspected, immediately evaluate and, if necessary, treat the patient. Strongly consider discontinuing eculizumab during the treatment of such infections; eculizumab initiation is contraindicated for use in patients with an unresolved serious Neisseria meningitidis infection.
 
Eculizumab increases the number of paroxysmal nocturnal hemoglobinuria (PNH) cells. Accordingly, patients who discontinue treatment with eculizumab may be at increased risk for serious hemolysis. For at least 8 weeks after eculizumab discontinuation, monitor patients for LDH concentrations higher than pretreatment concentrations plus one of the following: a > 25% decrease in PNH clone size in 4 g/dl in 50% of the total RBCs), corticosteroids, anticoagulation, or eculizumab reinitiation. During clinical studies, 16 of 196 patients discontinued therapy; serious hemolysis was not observed.

Description
  Text
  Eculizumab is a complement inhibitor indicated for the treatment of paroxysmal nocturnal hemoglobinuria (PNH), atypical hemolytic uremic syndrome (aHUS), anti-acetylcholine receptor antibody positive generalized myasthenia gravis, and anti-aquaporin-4 (AQP4) antibody positive neuromyelitis optica spectrum disorder (NMOSD). Eculizumab is not indicated for the treatment of Shiga toxin E. coli-related hemolytic uremic syndrome (STEC-HUS). Eculizumab increases the risk of serious, life-threatening, or fatal infections caused by meningococcal bacteria in any serogroup. Life-threatening and fatal meningococcal infections have occurred in both vaccinated and unvaccinated persons treated with complement inhibitors; vaccination for meningococcal disease is recommended to be completed at least 2 weeks before starting eculizumab to reduce the risk of meningococcal infection. Eculizumab was FDA-approved in March 2007.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Eculizumab is a monoclonal antibody designed to selectively block terminal complement activation. Eculizumab binds to the terminal complement protein C5 with high affinity, which inhibits its cleavage into C5a and C5b and prevents the generation of the terminal complement complex C5b-9. A genetic mutation in individuals with paroxysmal nocturnal hemoglobinuria (PNH) causes the formation of abnormal red blood cells, which are deficient in terminal complement inhibitors. The deficiency in terminal complement inhibitors causes the abnormal cells to be sensitive to lysis by terminal complements leading to intravascular hemolysis. In individuals with PNH, eculizumab inhibits intravascular hemolysis mediated by terminal complements. Eculizumab inhibits complement-mediated thrombotic microangiopathy in individuals with atypical hemolytic uremic syndrome. The presumed mechanism of action of eculizumab in generalized myasthenia gravis is the reduction of the terminal complement complex C5b-9 deposition at the neuromuscular junction. Eculizumab may inhibit aquaporin-4-antibody induced terminal complement C5b-9 deposition in neuromyelitis optica spectrum disorder.

Preparation
Withdraw the required amount of eculizumab from the vial into a sterile syringe. Transfer the dose to an infusion bag made of polyolefin (PO).
Dilute to a final concentration of 5 mg/mL. The volume of diluent required will be equal to the volume of drug.
Dilute Soliris or Bkemv by adding the appropriate amount of 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection, 0.45% Sodium Chloride Injection, 5% Dextrose Injection, or Lactated Ringer's Injection to the infusion bag.
Dilute Epysqli by adding the appropriate amount of 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection, 0.45% Sodium Chloride Injection, or 5% Dextrose Injection to the infusion bag.
The diluted solution should be clear to slightly opalescent, colorless to light brown.
Final infusion volumes:
Eculizumab 300 mg dose = 60 mL.
Eculizumab 600 mg dose = 120 mL.
Eculizumab 900 mg dose = 180 mL.
Eculizumab 1,200 mg dose = 240 mL.
Once the diluent is added, gently invert the infusion bag to ensure thorough mixing of eculizumab and the diluent. Do not shake.
Discard any amount of eculizumab that is remaining in the vial; eculizumab does not contain a preservative.
Allow the admixture to come to room temperature (18 to 25 degrees C or 64 to 77 degrees F) prior to administration. Do not heat in a microwave or with any other heat source other than ambient air temperature.
Storage: Once admixed, Soliris is table for 24 hours under refrigeration (2 to 8 degrees C or 36 to 46 degrees F) or at room temperature (18 to 25 degrees C or 64 to 77 degrees F). Epysqli admixed in 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection or 0.45% Sodium Chloride Injection is stable for 14 days under refrigeration (2 to 8 degrees C or 36 to 46 degrees F) or for 24 hours at room temperature (18 to 25 degrees C or 64 to 77 degrees F). Epysqli admixed in 5% Dextrose Injection is stable for 24 hours under refrigeration (2 to 8 degrees C or 36 to 46 degrees F) or at room temperature (18 to 25 degrees C or 64 to 77 degrees F). Bkemv is stable for 64 hours under refrigeration (2 to 8 degrees C or 36 to 46 degrees F) or for 24 hours at room temperature (18 to 25 degrees C or 64 to 77 degrees F). Do not freeze.
 
Intravenous Infusion
Infuse IV over 35 minutes in adults and over 1 to 4 hours in children using gravity feed, a syringe-type pump, or an infusion pump.
Infuse repeat doses at the recommended intervals, or within 2 days of these intervals.
Monitor for allergic reactions during the infusion and for 1 hour after the completion of the infusion. If an allergic reaction occurs, the infusion can be stopped or slowed. If it is slowed, the total infusion time should not exceed 2 hours for adults.
