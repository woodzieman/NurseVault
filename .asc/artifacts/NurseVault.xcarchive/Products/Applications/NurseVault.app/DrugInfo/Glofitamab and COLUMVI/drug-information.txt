          Dosage Description
          Cycle 1: 2.5 mg IV on day 8 (step-up dose 1) and 10 mg IV on day 15 (step-up dose 2). Cycles 2 to 12: 30 mg IV on day 1 repeated every 21 days. Continue therapy until disease progression, unacceptable toxicity, or a maximum of 12 cycles. Therapy interruption or discontinuation may be necessary in patients who develop toxicity. Ensure patients are well hydrated prior to starting glofitamab therapy; consider Pneumocystis jirovecii pneumonia and antiviral prophylaxis. Premedications: Complete the administration of a corticosteroid (dexamethasone 20 mg IV; or alternatively, prednisolone 100 mg IV or methylprednisolone 80 mg IV) at least 1 hour prior, antihistamine (diphenhydramine 50 mg PO or IV or equivalent) at least 30 minutes prior, and acetaminophen 500 to 1,000 mg PO at least 30 minutes prior to each dose in cycles 1, 2, and 3. In cycles 4 to 12, complete the administration of an antihistamine and acetaminophen at least 30 minutes prior to the glofitamab dose; complete the administration of a corticosteroid at least 1 hour prior to the glofitamab dose in patients who experienced any grade cytokine release syndrome with the previous dose. Restart premedications in patients who repeat doses within the step-up dosing schedule following a dose delay. The complete response (CR) rate (evaluated by an independent review committee) was 39% (overall response rate (ORR), 52%) in patients with relapsed or refractory DLBCL (not otherwise specified), transformed follicular lymphoma (FL), high-grade B-cell lymphoma (BCL), or primary mediastinal large BCL who received a median of 5 (range, 1 to 13) treatment cycles of glofitamab in the phase 2 cohort of a phase 1/2 trial (Study NP30179; n = 155). At a median follow-up time of 12.6 (range, 0.1 to 22.1) months, the median time to response to first CR was 42 (range, 31 to 308) days and the median duration of CR was not reached. Additionally, the median progression-free survival (PFS) time was 4.9 months; the 12-month PFS rate was 37%. In this trial, patients (median age, 66 years; range, 21 to 90 years) with DLBCL (n = 110; 71%), transformed FL (n = 27; 18%), high-grade BCL (n = 11; 7%), and primary mediastinal large BCL (n = 6; 4%) had received a median of 3 (range, 2 to 7) prior systemic therapies. In some cases, prior therapy included an autologous stem-cell transplant (18%) and CAR T-cell therapy (33%). CR and ORR rates were 43% and 56%, respectively, in a subgroup of 134 patients (de novo DLBCL, 80%; large BCL arising from FL, 20%) who received the recommended dosage of glofitamab.

    Headline
    cytokine release syndrome
    General Information
    Cytokine release syndrome (CRS) is a potentially fatal reaction that has been reported with glofitamab use. Administer glofitamab in a facility with medical personnel and equipment capable of monitoring and managing severe reactions. Hospitalize patients during glofitamab administration and for 24 hours after the completion of step-up dose 1, step-up dose 2 in patients who had any grade CRS with step-up dose 1, or the full dose in patients who had grade 2 or higher CRS with the previous infusion. To reduce the risk of CRS, ensure adequate hydration, administer glofitamab according to a step-up dosing schedule, and give pretreatment medications. Therapy may need to be interrupted or permanently discontinued based on toxicity severity; evaluate the need for hospitalization, supportive care, and further management (using current practice guidelines) in patients with severe CRS. If CRS is refractory to management, consider other causes including hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Glofitamab may cause fetal harm if administered during pregnancy based on its mechanism of action. Pregnant patients should be apprised of the potential hazard to the fetus. There are no available data with glofitamab use during human pregnancy; animal reproductive and developmental toxicity studies have not been conducted. Glofitamab causes T-cell activation and cytokine release and immune activation may compromise pregnancy maintenance. Human immunoglobulin (IgG) is known to cross the placenta; therefore, glofitamab may be transmitted from the mother to the developing fetus. It may also cause B-cell lymphopenia in neonates and infants exposed to glofitamab in-utero.
    Headline
    contraception requirements, pregnancy testing, reproductive risk
    General Information
    Counsel patients about the reproductive risk and contraception requirements during glofitamab treatment. Pregnancy testing should be performed prior to starting glofitamab in patients of reproductive potential. These patients should use effective contraception during and for 1 month after the last glofitamab dose.
    Headline
    infection
    General Information
    Do not administer glofitamab to patients who have an active infection. Severe or fatal infection including progressive multifocal leukoencephalopathy (PML) may occur with glofitamab therapy. Prior to initiating glofitamab therapy, start antimicrobial prophylaxis according to guidelines; consider prophylaxis for Pneumocystis jirovecii pneumonia and antiviral infection (e.g., herpes virus reactivation, cytomegalovirus infection) in patients at increased risk. Monitor patients before and during treatment for infection, including neurologic signs and symptoms of PML, and treat appropriately. Therapy interruption or permanent discontinuation may be necessary in patients who develop an infection.

Because human IgG is present in human milk, and there is potential for glofitamab absorption leading to B-cell depletion in the breastfed child, advise patients to avoid breast-feeding during treatment and for 1 month after the last dose.

Description
  Text
  Glofitamab-gxbm is a bispecific CD20-directed CD3 T-cell engager indicated for the treatment of adult patients with relapsed or refractory diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (not otherwise specified) or large B-cell lymphoma arising from follicular lymphoma after 2 or more lines of systemic therapy. Glofitamab has black box warnings for cytokine release syndrome.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Glofitamab is a bispecific T-cell engaging (BiTE) monoclonal antibody that binds to CD20 expressed on lymphoma cells and CD3 expressed on the surface of T-cells. It is a recombinant humanized immunoglobulin G1 (IgG1) antibody produced from Chinese Hamster Ovary cells. The binding of T-cells to lymphoma cells triggers a signaling cascade leading to the release of pro-inflammatory cytokines and proliferation of T-cells ultimately resulting in the lysis of CD20-positive B-cells.

  Text
  Glofitamab is administered intravenously. It has a geometric mean total volume of distribution of 5.6 L (coefficient of variation (CV), 24%). At steady state, the terminal half-life was 7.6 days (CV, 24%) and the clearance was about 0.617 L/day (CV, 33%). Glofitamab is metabolized into small peptides via catabolic pathways. Peripheral B-cell counts decreased to undetectable levels in 88.2% of patients by day 10 of cycle 1. A transient elevation of cytokines (IL-2, IL-6, IL-10, TNF-alpha, and IFN-gamma) occurred at doses of 0.045 mg or more. Peak cytokine levels were observed within 6 hours after the first 2.5-mg dose on day 8 of cycle 1; cytokine levels returned to baseline within 48 hours after the first 30-mg dose on day 1 of cycle 2.
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes and drug transporters: CYP substratesGlofitamab may cause a cytokine release that suppresses CYP enzyme activity resulting in an increase in the exposure of CYP substrates. Increased exposure of CYP450 substrates is more likely to occur after the first dose and for up to 14 days after the first 30-mg dose on day 1 of cycle 2, or if cytokine release syndrome occurs during or after treatment.

      Description
      Glofitamab is prepared and administered by a health care professional in a facility with medical personnel and equipment capable of managing severe reactions.
Hospitalize patients during glofitamab administration and for 24 hours after the completion of step-up dose 1 (2.5 mg on day 8 in cycle 1), step-up dose 2 (10 mg on day 15 in cycle 1) in patients who had any grade cytokine release syndrome (CRS) with step-up dose 1, or the full dose (30 mg on day 1 in cycles 2 to 12) in patients who had grade 2 or higher CRS with the previous infusion.
Dilution (for Intravenous Bag infusion)
2.5-mg dose
Remove and discard 27.5 mL from a 50-mL infusion bag.
Add 2.5 mL of glofitamab to the infusion bag.
10-mg dose
Remove and discard 10 mL from a 50-mL or 100-mL infusion bag.
Add 10 mL of glofitamab to the infusion bag.
30-mg dose
Remove and discard 30 mL from a 50-mL or 100-mL infusion bag.
Add 30 mL of glofitamab to the infusion bag.
After withdrawing and discarding a specified volume from an infusion bag containing 0.9% Sodium Chloride injection or 0.45% Sodium Chloride injection, add the appropriate volume of glofitamab to the infusion bag for a final concentration of 0.1 to 0.6 mg/mL as described above.
Glofitamab diluted with 0.9% Sodium Chloride injection is compatible with infusion bags composed of polyvinyl chloride (PVC), polyethylene (PE), polypropylene (PP) or polyolefin; glofitamab diluted with 0.45% Sodium Chloride injection is compatible with infusion bags composed of PVC.
Gently invert the infusion bag to mix the solution to avoid excessive foaming; do not shake.
Discard any unused drug in the vial(s).
Storage of diluted solution: Use immediately (preferred) or store in the refrigerator (2 to 8 degrees C; 36 to 46 degrees F) for up to 64 hours or at room temperature (up to 25 degrees C; up to 77 degrees F) for up to 4 hours; do not freeze. Discard the diluted infusion solution if storage time exceeds these limits.
Dilution (for Intravenous Syringe infusion)
Alternative preparation method for 2.5 mg dose only.
Add 22.5 mL of 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection or 0.45% Sodium Chloride Injection to a 30 mL syringe composed of polypropylene (PP).
Withdraw 2.5 mL of glofitamab into a second syringe; discard any unused glofitamab in the vial.
Attach a connector to the 2 syringes and transfer the glofitamab into the 30 mL syringe. The final concentration of glofitamab should be 0.1 mg/mL.
Disconnect the syringes. Draw air into the syringe containing glofitamab dilution and close.
Gently invert the syringe to mix the solution to avoid foaming; do not shake.
Remove air bubbles from syringe before administration.
Storage of diluted solution: Use immediately (preferred) or store in the refrigerator (2 to 8 degrees C; 36 to 46 degrees F) for up to 64 hours or at room temperature (up to 25 degrees C; up to 77 degrees F) for up to 4 hours; do not freeze. Discard the diluted infusion solution if storage time exceeds these limits.
Intravenous Infusion
2.5-mg or 10-mg dose: Infuse over 4 hours or up to 8 hours in patients who experienced CRS with the previous dose.
30-mg dose, cycle 2: Infuse over 4 hours or up to 8 hours in patients who experienced CRS with the previous dose.
30-mg dose, cycles 3 to 12: Infuse over 2 hours or maintain the infusion time of 4 hours in patients who experienced CRS with the previous dose.
Administer the glofitamab dose as an IV infusion through a dedicated infusion line with a 0.2 micron in-line filter using an IV infusion pump or syringe pump as described above. Prime the infusion line with diluted glofitamab solution.
No incompatibilities have been observed with infusion sets with product-contacting surfaces of polyurethane (PUR), PVC, PE, polybutadiene (PBD), polyetherurethane (PEU), polycarbonate (PC), silicone, polytetrafluoroethylene (PTFE), or acrylonitrile butadiene styrene (ABS) or with in-line filter membranes composed of polyethersulfone (PES) or polysulfone.
To ensure the entire dose of glofitamab is administered, replace the empty infusion bag or syringe with an infusion bag or syringe containing 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection or 0.45% Sodium Chloride Injection connected to the same infusion line. Continue the infusion at the same rate until the recommended infusion duration is reached as described above.
Do not mix glofitamab with other drugs.
