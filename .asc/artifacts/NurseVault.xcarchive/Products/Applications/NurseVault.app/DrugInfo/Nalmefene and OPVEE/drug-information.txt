        Titrate to reverse the undesired opioid effects with a partial reversing dose of 1 mcg/kg or a full reversal dose of 1 mg/70 kg. Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  General Information
  NOTE: Revex™ is available in two (2) concentrations, 100 mcg/ml (blue label) and 1 mg/ml (green label). The 100 mcg/ml is for postoperative use and the 1 mg/ml is for management of opiate agonist overdosage. Care should be taken to avoid using the incorrect dosage form.Seizures have been associated with nalmefene use in animals. Although they have not been reported in humans, use cautiously in patients with a history of a seizure disorder and/or receiving drugs known to induce seizures.Nalmefene should be used with caution in patients with cardiac arrhythmias or other cardiac disease, or in patients receiving cardioactive drugs. Cardiovascular adverse effects can occur, especially in postoperative patients. Clinicians should note that onset can be delayed if nalmefene is administered via subcutaneous or intramuscular routes in patients with impaired peripheral circulation.Nalmefene should be used with caution in patients with head trauma, brain tumor, or increased intracranial pressure because the drug can obscure neurological parameters.Clinical studies of nalmefene did not include sufficient number of elderly patients (>= 65 years old) to determine whether they respond differently from younger subjects. Other reported clinical experience has not identified differences in responses between the elderly and younger patients. In general, dose selection for an elderly patient should be cautious, usually starting at the low end of the dosing range, reflecting the greater frequency of decreased hepatic, renal, or cardiac function, and of concomitant disease or other drug therapy.Caution should be exercised in giving nalmefene to neonates whose mothers are known to have been dependent on opiates. Sudden reversal of opioid dependency can induce tachycardia, nausea/vomiting, and sweating. Nalmefene should be used in pregnancy or during breast-feeding only when clearly needed (FDA pregnancy risk category C).Patients known to have substance abuse habits should receive nalmefene cautiously; nalmefene can precipitate opiate withdrawal in these patients. Although this withdrawal is rarely serious, clinicians should be prepared to manage its symptoms.Nalmefene is contraindicated in patients with hypersensitivity to nalmefene or any components of the commercially available product. Also, nalmefene should not be used in patients with a known hypersensitivity to naloxone or naltrexone because these three drugs are all structurally similar.Rapid administration of nalmefene to patients with renal impairment can cause dizziness and hypotension. Administer nalmefene slowly to these patients. The duration of action of nalmefene may also be prolonged.

    cardiovascular disease
    General Information
    The use of nalmefene can precipitate adverse cardiovascular events, particularly during abrupt opioid reversal. These complications are most likely to occur in patients with preexisting cardiovascular disease or those receiving medications with similar adverse cardiovascular effects. Following nalmefene administration, closely monitor patients at high cardiovascular risk in an appropriate health care setting.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Because nalmefene is indicated for reversal of narcotic depressant effects or acute opioid overdose, treatment should not be withheld in a patient who is breast-feeding. There is no clinical information available on the use of nalmefene during breast-feeding or its presence in human milk. Nalmefene and its metabolites are excreted into rat milk, with peak concentrations of about 3-times those in plasma at 1 hour after administration. Naloxone, an alternate treatment for reversal of narcotic toxicity, has been administered to newborns following in utero exposure to narcotics with no adverse effects reported.
    Headline
    neonates
    General Information
    In neonates with in utero opioid exposure, opioid withdrawal may be life-threatening if not recognized and properly treated. Monitor for the development of opioid withdrawal manifesting as seizures, excessive crying, and hyperactive reflexes. There may be clinical settings, particularly the postpartum period in neonates with known or suspected exposure to maternal opioid use, where it is preferable to avoid the abrupt precipitation of opioid withdrawal symptoms. In these settings, use an alternative opioid antagonist that can be titrated to effect and dosed according to weight. The nalmefene Solution for Injection may be used for resuscitation ONLY WHEN, in the opinion of the treating physician, the expected benefits outweigh the risks.
    Headline
    increased risk for opioid withdrawal
    General Information
    Use nalmefene with caution in individuals with increased risk for opioid withdrawal, including those with opiod dependence. Withdrawal symptoms include agitation, hypertension, tachycardia, GI events (cramps, diarrhea, nausea, vomiting), shivering, and diaphoresis. In neonates with in utero opioid exposure, withdrawal may be life-threatening, manifesting as seizures, excessive crying, and hyperactive reflexes. In opioid-dependent individuals, titrate to effect where possible, and monitor closely for withdrawal complications. Individuals who experience a withdrawal reaction after receiving nalmefene should receive symptomatic and supportive care. Administration of large amounts of opioids in an attempt to overcome a full blockade has resulted in adverse respiratory and circulatory reactions. Inform individuals of the potential consequences of trying to overcome the opioid blockage.
    Headline
    renal failure, renal impairment
    General Information
    Although a nalmefene dose adjustment is not needed for single-use emergency reversal, patients with renal impairment and renal failure may be at increased risk for hypertension and dizziness with rapid dosing. Thus, in patients with renal failure, administer incremental doses slowly (e.g., over 60 seconds for injection).

Adverse Reactions
  Headline
  Adverse Reactions
  General Information
  Although not reported in humans, seizures have been associated with nalmefene use in animals. Use cautiously in patients with a history of seizures.Based on data from patients in controlled clinical trials (n=1127) and use in normal volunteers (n=152), the manufacturer reports that nalmefene therapy is well-tolerated, and no serious toxicity was observed even at 15 times the normal human dose. The most common adverse effects reported include fever (3%), dizziness (3%), sinus tachycardia (5%), hypertension (5%), and nausea/vomiting (18%/9%). Other adverse effects (<= 1% incidence) include sinus bradycardia, cardiac arrhythmias, headache, diarrhea, xerostomia, drowsiness, depression, agitation, nervousness, confusion, myoclonus, pharyngitis, pruritus, and urinary retention. The incidence of adverse reactions increases with doses higher than recommended.Nalmefene will produce symptoms of opiate withdrawal if administered to opiate-dependent subjects. Although opiate withdrawal is generally not life-threatening, acute reversal of opiate effects has been associated with restlessness, diaphoresis, tremor, pulmonary edema, hypotension or hypertension, ventricular arrhythmias, and cardiovascular instability. Also, nalmefene at doses greater than recommended produced symptoms of endogenous opioid antagonism, which included nausea, chills, myalgia, dysphoria, abdominal cramps, and joint pain. These effects were usually transient and were infrequently reported.

    arthralgia, dysphoria, myalgia, withdrawal
    General Information
    Nalmefene will produce symptoms of opioid withdrawal if administered to an opioid-dependent person. Signs and symptoms of withdrawal in persons who are physically dependent on opioids have included body aches, fever, sweating, runny nose, sneezing, piloerection, yawning, weakness, shivering or trembling, nervousness, restlessness or irritability, diarrhea, nausea or vomiting, abdominal cramps, increased blood pressure, tachycardia, and aggressive behavior. Also, nalmefene at doses greater than recommended produced symptoms of endogenous opioid antagonism, which included nausea, chills, myalgia, dysphoria, abdominal cramps, and arthralgia.

  Text
  Nalmefene is an opioid antagonist, indicated for the complete or partial reversal of opioid drug effects (i.e., respiratory depression, sedation, hypotension) and for the management of known or suspected opioid overdose. The drug is a 6-methylene analog of naltrexone, that has a longer duration of action than naloxone at fully reversing doses. Nalmefene has no opioid agonist activity and does not produce tolerance, physical dependency, or abuse potential. The drug is available in a unit-dose nasal spray device, prefilled autoinjector, and as a solution for injection.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Nalmefene, like naloxone, is essentially a pure opioid receptor antagonist with little or no agonistic activity. In patients who have not recently received opiate agonists, nalmefene shows little or no pharmacological effects, even at high doses. Opioid receptors include mu, kappa, and delta, which have been reclassified by an International Union of Pharmacology subcommittee as OP1 (delta), OP2 (kappa), and OP3 (mu). Mu receptors are responsible for analgesia, euphoria, respiratory depression, and miosis; kappa-receptors, are responsible for analgesia, dysphoria and some psychomimetic effects (i.e., disorientation and/or depersonalization) and sedation; and delta-receptors mediate analgesia, sedation, and possibly hormonal and neurotransmitter release. Since nalmefene has essentially the same effects as naloxone, it can be assumed that nalmefene antagonizes mu-, kappa-, and delta-receptors, however, unlike naloxone, this antagonism lasts longer. Thus, repeat doses of nalmefene may only be required when long-acting opioids (i.e., methadone) are involved. Nalmefene itself produces no physical or psychological dependence and will not worsen respiratory depression if administered for non-opioid overdose.
 
Based on pre-clinical trials, the manufacturer reports that nalmefene in doses of up to 10 mg/kg (437-times the recommended maximum human dosage) does not completely reverse butorphanol-induced analgesia in animal models. This occurs because of butorphanol's high affinity and slow displacement from opioid receptors. Therefore, nalmefene may not completely reverse the respiratory depression produced by butorphanol.
 
Nalmefene antagonizes both the toxic and clinical effects of opioids. Thus, not only are respiratory depression, hypotension, and sedation reversed, but so is analgesia. Clinicians should use discretion when considering administering nalmefene to patients who are sedated from opioids but do not exhibit respiratory depression. Reversal of analgesia is undesirable in patients known to be in severe pain. Nalmefene should not be used for drowsiness unless opioid-induced respiratory depression coexists.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Intravenous Route
    Description
    After a single 1 mg IV dose to 18 adult males, nalmefene concentrations were 3.7 ng/mL at 5 minutes post-dose and systemic exposure (AUC) was 16.6 ng/mL x hour.
    Name
    Intramuscular Route
    Description
    Nalmefene is completely bioavailable after IM administration, with the Solution for Injection having a relative bioavailability of 101.5% +/- 8.1%. Although the time to reach maximum plasma concentrations (Cmax) after IM administration was 2.3 +/- 1.1 hours, therapeutic concentrations were achieved within 5 to 15 minutes. The relative bioavailability of a single 1.5 mg IM dose from the autoinjector was compared to a single 1 mg IM dose from the Solution for Injection in a study involving 24 healthy adults. Based on the dose-normalization AUC, the mean bioavailability of the autoinjector was 1.13 (Frel) relative to the IM Solution for Injection. The median Tmax for the autoinjector was 0.25 hours compared to 0.5 hours for the IM Solution for Injection.
    Name
    Subcutaneous Route
    Description
    Nalmefene is completely bioavailable after subcutaneous administration, with a relative bioavailability of 99.7% +/- 6.9%. Although the time to reach maximum plasma concentrations (Cmax) after subcutaneous administration is 1.5 +/- 1.2 hours, therapeutic concentrations are achieved within 5 to 15 minutes.
    Name
    Other Route(s)
    Description
    Intranasal Route
A study compared the pharmacokinetics of one 2.7 mg nalmefene nasal spray to a single 1 mg IM nalmefene injection in 68 healthy subjects. For the nasal spray, a maximum plasma concentration (Cmax) of 10.4 ng/mL was achieved in 0.25 hours (range: 0.08 to 2 hours). The Cmax for the IM injection was 1.5 ng/mL which was reached in 0.33 hours (range: 0.117 to 18 hours). Systemic exposures (AUC) for the nasal spray and IM injection were 40.6 ng x hour/mL and 16.8 ng x hour/mL, respectively. The half-live was 11.4 hours for the nasal spray and 10.6 hours for the IM injection.
 
A second study of 24 healthy subjects compared the pharmacokinetics of the following nalmefene doses: 2.7 mg dose (1 spray in a single nostril); 5.4 mg dose (1 spray in each nostril); 5.4 mg dose (2 sprays in a single nostril). The Cmax for the 2.7 mg dose, 5.4 mg dose (1 spray each nostril), and 5.4 mg dose (2 sprays single nostril) were 9.75 ng/mL, 18.9 ng/mL, and 16.1 ng/mL, respectively. The time to reach maximum plasma concentration was approximately 0.25 hours for each dose. The AUC for the 2.7 mg dose, 5.4 mg dose (1 spray each nostril), and 5.4 mg dose (2 sprays single nostril) were 45.8 ng x hour/mL, 88.1 ng x hour/mL, and 83.8 ng x hour/mL, respectively. The half-life was 11 hours for each dose.
  Special Populations
    Name
    Hepatic Impairment
    Description
    After a 1 mg dose, the plasma clearance of nalmefene is decreased by 28.3% (0.56 +/- 0.21 L/kg/hour/ vs. 0.78 +/- 0.24 L/kg/hour) and the terminal elimination half-life is increased to 11.9 +/- 2.0 hours in patients with hepatic impairment.
    Name
    Renal Impairment
    Description
    There is a statistically significant 27% decrease in the plasma clearance of nalmefene in patients with end-stage renal disease (ESRD) during interdialysis (0.57 +/- 0.2 L/kg/hour) and a 25% decreased plasma clearance in ESRD patients during intradialysis (0.59 +/- 0.18 L/kg/hour) as compared to normal controls (0.79 +/- 0.24 L/kg/hour). The terminal elimination half-life after a 1 mg dose is increased to 26.1 +/- 9.9 hours in end-stage renal disease patients.
    Name
    Pediatrics
    Description
    Nalmefene pharmacokinetic studies have not been conducted in pediatric patients. However, based on population pharmacokinetic simulations for nalmefene nasal spray, 12-year-old subjects (median weight: 50.6 kg, range: 27.6 to 126.8 kg) are expected to have 7.6% higher mean maximum plasma concentration (Cmax) and 25.5% higher exposure (AUC) as compared to the adult population (mean weight: 75.42 kg). Based on population pharmacokinetic simulations for 1.5 mg nalmefene IM administration via the autoinjector, 12-year-old subjects (median weight: 49 kg, range: 33 to 71 kg) are expected to have a 27% higher mean Cmax and 15% higher mean AUC compared to the adult population.
    Name
    Geriatric
    Description
    No significant differences between younger adults (19 to 32 years) and older patients (62 to 80 years) were observed with respect to nalmefene plasma clearance, steady-state volume of distribution, or half-life. There was an apparent age-related decrease in the central volume of distribution (young: 3.9 +/- 1.1 L/kg vs. elderly: 2.8 +/- 1.1 L/kg) that resulted in a greater initial nalmefene concentration in the elderly group. Despite this greater initial drug concentration, no dosage adjustments are required.
    Name
    Other
    Description
    Body Weight
The influence of body weight on nalmefene pharmacokinetics was assessed using population pharmacokinetic simulations. Based on the pharmacokinetic simulation for a single 2.7 mg intranasal dose, the first quartile of body weight (50.4 to 64.8 kg) had a 5.2% and 15.7% higher Cmax and AUC, respectively, while the fourth quartile of body weight (91 to 106.8 kg) had a 4.4% and 11.6% lower Cmax and AUC, respectively, when compared to the mean pharmacokinetic values across the full population in the pharmacokinetic dataset (median weight 74.7 kg). In the pharmacokinetic simulation for a single 1.5 mg IM dose administered via the autoinjector, the first quartile of body weight (54 to 67.3 kg) had a 17.6% and 13.4% higher Cmax and AUC, respectively, while the fourth quartile of body weight (86.8 to 117.1 kg) had a 15.3% and 11.8% lower Cmax and AUC, respectively, when compared to the mean pharmacokinetic values across the full population in the pharmacokinetic dataset (median weight 76 kg).
  Text
  Nalmefene is administered intravenously, subcutaneously, intramuscularly, or intranasally. Once absorbed, nalmefene is rapidly distributed with over 80% of brain opioid receptors occupied within 5 minutes of administration. The apparent volumes of distribution centrally and at steady-state are 3.9 +/- 1.1 L/kg and 8.6 +/- 1.7 L/kg, respectively. Nalmefene distributes 67% into red blood cells and 39% into plasma, with a plasma protein binding of approximately 45% over a concentration range of 0.1 to 2 mcg/mL. The whole blood to plasma ratio is 1.3. Metabolism occurs via the liver to a primary glucuronide conjugate and trace amounts of a N-dealkylated metabolite. These metabolites have minimal or no pharmacological activity. Less than 5% of nalmefene is excreted in the urine unchanged, and 17% of the nalmefene dose is excreted in the feces. Based on plasma concentration-time profiles in some patients, it is suggested that nalmefene undergoes enterohepatic recirculation. The redistribution and terminal elimination half-life after a 1 mg IV dose is 41 +/- 34 minutes and 10.8 +/- 5.2 hours, respectively. The terminal half-life after a 1.5 mg dose from the autoinjector is 9.07 hours.
 
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes and/or drug transporters: none

Administration
  General Information
  For storage information, see the specific product information within the How Supplied section.
 
NOTE: Information for Caregivers and Family
Alert others about the presence of nalmefene autoinjector or nasal spray, as administration must be performed by someone other than the patient. Family members, caregivers, or other people who may have to use nalmefene in an opioid emergency should know the signs and symptoms of opioid overdose, where the autoinjector or nasal spray is stored, and how to administer the drug before an opioid emergency occurs. Once obtained, become familiar with how to use the autoinjector or nasal spray by reading the Instructions for Use.
Those who administer nalmefene should be aware that its use in patients who are opioid dependent may precipitate opioid withdrawal. In neonates, opioid withdrawal can be life-threatening and must be treated immediately.
Recurrence of respiratory depression is possible, even after an adequate initial response to treatment. Therefore, emergency medical assistance must be sought immediately after delivering the first dose of nalmefene. The patient must remain under continued surveillance, and repeated doses of nalmefene should be administered as necessary. Additional supportive and resuscitative measures may be helpful while awaiting emergency medical assistance.
Instruct patients and their family members or caregivers that the reversal of respiratory depression caused by partial agonists or mixed agonist/antagonists such as buprenorphine and pentazocine, may be incomplete. Repeated administration of nalmefene may be needed.
NOTE: All FDA-approved nalmefene products achieve plasma concentrations sufficient to reverse the effects of opioid overdose. Comparing products on a mg-for-mg basis may be misleading.

      Name
      Intravenous Administration
      Description
      Intravenous Bolus Injection (Solution for Injection):
IV bolus injection is the primary route of administration.
Incremental IV doses in patients with renal failure should be administered slowly (over 60 seconds) to minimize hypertension and dizziness.
      Name
      Intramuscular Administration
      Description
      Zurnai autoinjector:
Administer as quickly as possible if a patient is unresponsive and an opioid overdose is suspected, even when in doubt. Prolonged respiratory depression may result in central nervous system damage or death.
The autoinjector is ready to use; no assembly is required. Do not prime or test prior to administration.
Once the safety seal is broken and the blue cap is removed, the autoinjector must be used immediately or disposed of properly.
The injection is administered into the anterolateral aspect of the thigh and may be given through clothing if necessary.
Place the needle guard of the autoinjector against the outer thigh. Press the autoinjector firmly into the thigh until a click is heard and hold in place for 3 seconds. After 3 seconds, the viewing window should turn completely orange. If viewing window is not completely orange, administer a new autoinjector.
Place the patient in the recovery position on their side. Monitor until emergency medical assistance arrives.
Each autoinjector contains a single dose and cannot be reused.
Using a new autoinjector, readminister nalmefene every 2 to 5 minutes if the patient does not respond or responds and then relapses into respiratory depression. The requirement for repeat doses depends on the amount, type, and route of administration of the opioid being antagonized.
 
Solution for Injection:
Intramuscular injections can be given if IV access is lost or not readily obtainable.
Inject into a large muscle mass.
Therapeutic effects of a single IM dose should occur within 5 to 15 minutes.
Due to the variable speed of drug absorption and the inability to titrate to effect, great care is advised if repeated IM doses must be administered.

    Administration Details
      Name
      Intranasal Inhalation Administration
      Description
      Administer nalmefene nasal spray as quickly as possible if a patient is unresponsive and an opioid overdose is suspected, even when in doubt. Prolonged respiratory depression may result in central nervous system damage or death.
Hold the device with your thumb on the white plunger and your fingers on either side of the nozzle. DO NOT prime or test the device prior to administration.
Place the patient on their back. Assure that the device nozzle is inserted into 1 of the patient's nostrils and provide support to the back of the neck to allow the head to tilt back.
Press firmly on the device plunger to administer the dose. The dose has been delivered when the white plunger has been pushed all the way down until it stops.
Remove the device nozzle from the nostril.
Turn the patient on their side (recovery position) and seek immediate medical assistance after the first dose of nalmefene has been administered. Additional supportive and resuscitative measures may be helpful while awaiting emergency medical assistance. Keep the patient under continued surveillance until emergency personnel arrive and administer repeated nalmefene doses as necessary.
Do NOT attempt to reuse the nalmefene nasal spray device; each device contains a single dose.
Using a new nasal spray device, readminister nalmefene every 2 to 5 minutes if the patient does not respond or responds and then relapses into respiratory depression. The requirement for repeat doses depends on the amount, type, and route of administration of the opioid being antagonized.
Administer the nasal spray in alternate nostrils with each dose.
After use, put the device back into its box and dispose of properly.
Replace the product before its expiration date.

Dosage Adjustment Guidelines
  Hepatic Impairment
  No dosage adjustments are required for single episodes of opioid antagonism.
  Renal Impairment
  No dosage adjustments are required for single episodes of opioid antagonism. However, in patients with renal failure, incremental intravenous doses should be delivered slowly (i.e., over 60 seconds) to minimize hypertension and dizziness.
