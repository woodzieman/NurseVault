  General Information
  General Dosing Information
Withdraw ganaxolone gradually if discontinuing therapy because of the risk of increased seizure frequency and status epilepticus. If withdrawal is needed because of a serious adverse reaction, rapid discontinuation of ganaxolone can be considered.

    Headline
    Drug Interactions
    General Information

    Headline
    mood disorder, suicidal ideation
    General Information
    Monitor for worsening of depression, suicidal thoughts or behavior, or any unusual changes in behavior in individuals with mood disorder or suicidal ideation; antiepileptic drugs (AEDs), including ganaxolone, increase the risk of suicidal thoughts or behavior in individuals taking these medications for any indication. When considering AED use, balance the risk of suicidal thoughts or behaviors with the risk of untreated illness. The relative risk for suicidal thoughts or behavior was higher in clinical trials in subjects with epilepsy than in clinical trials in subjects with psychiatric or other conditions, but the absolute risk differences were similar for the epilepsy and psychiatric indications. Epilepsy and many other illnesses for which AEDs are prescribed are themselves associated with morbidity and mortality and an increased risk of suicidal thoughts and behavior. If suicidal thoughts and behavior emerge during treatment, consider whether the emergence of these symptoms may be related to the illness being treated.
    Headline
    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components.
    Headline
    Child-Pugh class C, hepatic failure
    General Information
    Ganaxolone dosage adjustment is required in individuals with severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh class C) or hepatic failure. Ganaxolone plasma concentrations are increased in individuals with severe hepatic impairment.
    Headline
    activities requiring coordination and concentration, driving or operating machinery
    General Information
    Advise individuals against driving or operating machinery or doing activities requiring coordination and concentration until they have gained sufficient experience on ganaxolone to gauge whether it adversely affects their ability to do so. Ganaxolone can cause somnolence and sedation. Coadministration with other CNS depressants, including opioids, antidepressants, and ethanol, could potentiate somnolence and sedation in individuals receiving ganaxolone.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Available data on the use of ganaxolone during pregnancy are insufficient to evaluate a drug-associated risk of major birth defects, miscarriage, or other adverse outcomes. In animal studies, fetal malformations were observed in mice after ganaxolone exposure during organogenesis. Neurobehavioral and growth impairment were observed in rats after ganaxolone exposure throughout gestation and lactation. These outcomes occurred at maternal exposures lower than the human exposure at the maximum recommended human dose. Additionally, neuronal death was observed in rats exposed to ganaxolone during a period of brain development that, in humans, begins during the third trimester of pregnancy and continues during the first few years after birth. Seizures, with or without the use of antiseizure medications, have been associated with several adverse outcomes during pregnancy, including preeclampsia, preterm labor, antepartum and postpartum hemorrhage, placental abruption, poor fetal growth, and fetal and maternal death. The risk of adverse outcomes may be greatest for individuals with untreated or poorly controlled seizures. Individuals with seizures who become pregnant should not abruptly discontinue antiseizure medications due to the risk of status epilepticus or severe seizures, which may be life-threatening. There is a pregnancy exposure registry that monitors pregnancy outcomes in people exposed to antiepileptic drugs during pregnancy. People taking ganaxolone during pregnancy are encouraged to enroll by contacting the North American Antiepileptic Drug (NAAED) Pregnancy Registry online at http://www.aedpregnancyregistry.org/ or by calling 1-888-233-2334.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Use ganaxolone with caution during breast-feeding. Ganaxolone is present in human milk. After a single 300 mg oral dose of ganaxolone, exposure was approximately 4 times higher in breast milk compared to the lactating individual's plasma. The calculated maximum relative infant dose was approximately 0.157 mg/kg/day (based on human milk intake of 150 mL/kg/day), which is less than 1% of the adult dose and approximately 0.24% of the labeled pediatric dose of 63 mg/kg/day. The effect of ganaxolone on milk production and its effects on the breast-fed child are unknown. Consider the benefits of breast-feeding, the patient's clinical need for treatment, and any potential adverse effects on the breast-fed child from the medication or from the patient's underlying medical condition.

Description
  Text
  Ganaxolone is a neuroactive steroid gamma-aminobutyric acid (GABA) A receptor positive modulator indicated for the treatment of seizures associated with cyclin-dependent kinase-like 5 (CDKL5) deficiency disorder (CDD) in patients 2 years and older. CDD is a rare developmental epileptic encephalopathy (DEE) that causes both epileptic activity and severe developmental impairment, impacting cognitive, motor, speech, and visual function. Seizures start at a very early age (about 3 months, but can occur during the first week of life). A randomized, placebo-controlled trial of pediatric patients 2 to 19 years of age with CDD was conducted to evaluate the efficacy of ganaxolone. The percentage change in the 28-day frequency of major motor seizures from a 6-week baseline phase (during the 17-week double-blind treatment phase) was evaluated. Patients treated with ganaxolone had a 31% median reduction in the 28-day frequency of major motor seizures compared to a 7% reduction in placebo-patients. The most commonly reported adverse reactions were somnolence, fever, salivary hypersecretion, and seasonal allergy. Antiepileptic drugs, including ganaxolone, are associated with an increased risk of suicidal thoughts and behaviors; monitor patients for new or worsening depression, suicidal thoughts and/or behaviors, and changes in mood or behavior.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  The exact mechanism of action of ganaxolone is unknown, but its anticonvulsant effects are thought to result from positive allosteric modulation of the gamma-aminobutyric acid type A (GABAA) receptor in the CNS.
