Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  General Information
  Normal undamaged epithelial surfaces inhibit the absorption of polyethylene glycol, the vehicle for mupirocin skin ointment. Damaged epithelial surfaces allow absorption of polyethylene glycol. Mupirocin skin ointment should not be used on burns (especially extensive burns), open wounds, or other damaged skin. Increased absorption of polyethylene glycol should be avoided, especially if there is evidence of moderate to severe renal impairment.
 
Prolonged use of mupirocin is not recommended because of possible overgrowth of resistant organisms including fungi. This overgrowth can cause superinfection.
 
The intranasal form of mupirocin is not for use in neonates or children < 12 years old. Safe use and pharmacokinetic properties have not been studied in these groups.
 
Mupirocin is not for ophthalmic administration. Avoid ocular exposure of mupirocin as ocular contact produces severe burning and tearing.
 
Mupirocin is classified as FDA pregnancy risk category B. No adequate studies have been conducted in pregnant women and mupirocin should only be used if clearly needed.
 
Mupirocin is not absorbed when applied topically to the nasal passages or to the skin; detectable systemic concentrations of mupirocin have not been observed. Distribution of mupirocin into breast milk is not likely because systemic absorption essentially does not occur. There are no specific precautions for use during lactation; however, mupirocin should not be applied to the breast of breast-feeding mothers.

    General Information
    Avoid mupirocin topical ointment containing polyethylene glycol in conditions where absorption of large quantities of polyethylene glycol is possible, especially in individuals with moderate or severe renal impairment or renal failure.     Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Limited data over decades of use of mupirocin topical cream and ointment during pregnancy have not identified a drug-associated risk of major birth defects, miscarriage or other adverse maternal or fetal outcomes. Systemic absorption of mupirocin through intact skin is minimal after topical administration. No developmental toxicity was observed in rats or rabbits exposed to mupirocin subcutaneously during organogenesis at doses 11 and 22 times the human topical dose, respectively. There are insufficient data to evaluate a drug-associated risk with the use of mupirocin nasal ointment during pregnancy. Mupirocin absorption was negligible following repeated intranasal administration of mupirocin nasal ointment, but the dosage regimen used in the study did not mimic recommended clinical use. No developmental toxicity was observed in rats or rabbits exposed to mupirocin subcutaneously during organogenesis at doses 65 times and 32 times the human intranasal dose, respectively.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Mupirocin is compatible with breast-feeding. There are no data on the presence of mupirocin in human milk, its effects on the breast-fed child, or its effects on milk production. However, due to limited systemic exposure after topical or intranasal use, no mupirocin exposure in the breast-fed child is expected. To minimize oral exposure of the drug to the breast-fed child, thoroughly wash the breast and/or nipple if those areas are treated with mupirocin prior to breast-feeding.

Adverse Reactions
  Headline
  Adverse Reactions
  General Information
  Adverse effects are uncommon and generally mild during therapy with mupirocin. The most frequently reported adverse reactions to topical use are skin irritation (burning, stinging, or pain) and pruritus. Some reactions can be attributed to the polyethylene glycol (PEG) vehicle used in the topical preparation. Systemic absorption of PEG can cause renal failure, which is potentially fatal.     C. difficile-associated diarrhea, infection, pseudomembranous colitis, superinfection
    General Information
    Microbial overgrowth and superinfection can occur with antibiotic use. C. difficile-associated diarrhea (CDAD) or pseudomembranous colitis has been reported for nearly all systemic antibacterial drugs, which may range in severity from mild to life-threatening. Treatment with antibacterial agents alters the normal flora of the colon leading to overgrowth of C. difficile. If pseudomembranous colitis is suspected or confirmed, ongoing antibacterial therapy not directed against C. difficile may need to be discontinued. Institute appropriate fluid and electrolyte management, protein supplementation, C. difficile-directed antibacterial therapy, and surgical evaluation as clinically appropriate. Careful medical history is necessary as pseudomembranous colitis has been reported to occur over 2 months after the administration of antibacterial agents. Secondary wound infection has also been reported with mupirocin use.

Description
  Text
  Description: Mupirocin is an antibiotic produced from Pseudomonas fluorescens that is structurally unrelated to any other topical or systemic antibiotics. Mupirocin is used topically in the treatment of impetigo caused by Staphylococcus aureus and beta-hemolytic streptococci including Streptococcus pyogenes. Mupirocin cream was shown to be at least as effective as oral cephalexin for the treatment of secondarily infected traumatic skin lesions. Mupirocin is also applied intranasally to eradicate nasal colonization with methicillin-resistant strains of Staphylococcus aureus (MRSA) in adult patients and health care workers. Intranasal application is usually done to reduce the risk of infection in patients with high risk during institutional outbreaks of MRSA. Because of its unique mechanism, this antibiotic has little, if any, potential for cross-resistance with other antibiotics. Mupirocin topical ointment was approved by the FDA in December 1987. An intranasal dosage form was subsequently approved October 1995, and the topical cream was approved December 1997.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Mupirocin is bacteriostatic at low concentrations and bactericidal at high concentrations. Bacterial protein and RNA synthesis are inhibited when mupirocin reversibly binds to bacterial isoleucyl-tRNA synthetase. This enzyme normally promotes the conversion of isoleucine and tRNA to isoleucyl-tRNA. The epoxide side chain of mupirocin is similar to isoleucine and competes with it for binding sites on the synthetase enzyme. The cellular concentration of isoleucyl-tRNA becomes depleted, subsequently inhibiting bacterial protein and RNA synthesis. Unlike many other antibiotics, mupirocin has little effect on bacterial DNA synthesis and cell wall peptidoglycan formation.

Children
One pharmacokinetic study found percutaneous absorption of mupirocin topical cream occurred more frequently in pediatric patients (90% of patients) than in adults (44% of patients). In this study, the cream was applied multiple times per day to various skin lesions on 10 pediatric patients (ages 3—12 years) and 16 adults. Systemic absorption was determined via urinary detection of the inactive metabolite, monic acid. Although the percentage of pediatric patients with monic acid in the urine was higher than in adults, the observed urinary concentration was comparable (0.07—1.3 mcg/ml vs 0.08—10.03 mcg/ml, respectively).
  Text
  Mupirocin is administered topically to the skin or the nares. Once in systemic circulation, it is rapidly metabolized to inactive monic acid and is excreted via the kidneys. The elimination half-life is 20—40 minutes for mupirocin and 30—80 minutes for monic acid.
