          Dosage Description
          1 mg IV on cycle 1, day 1, followed by 10 mg IV on cycle 1, days 8 and 15. For all subsequent cycles, administer tarlatamab 10 mg IV on days 1 and 15, every 28 days until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity. For cycle 1, days 1 and 8, premedicate with dexamethasone 8 mg IV (or equivalent) within 1 hour prior to tarlatamab administration; for cycle 1, days 1, 8, and 15, administer 1 liter of 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection IV over 4 to 5 hours beginning immediately after completion of the infusion. Treatment with tarlatamab significantly improved the median overall survival time (13.6 months vs. 8.3 months) and median progression-free survival (4.2 months vs. 3.2 months) compared with the investigator's choice standard of care (topotecan 73%; lurbinectedin 18%; amrubicin 9%) in patients with SCLC and disease progression after platinum-based chemotherapy in a multicenter, randomized, open-label clinical trial (DeLLphi-304). In a multicenter, multicohort, open-label phase 2 clinical trial (DeLLphi-301), treatment with tarlatamab resulted in an overall response rate (ORR) of 40% (complete response, 2%) in patients with relapsed or refractory ES-SCLC with disease progression after platinum-based chemotherapy and at least 1 other line of prior therapy (n = 99); 74% of patients had received prior anti-PD1/PDL1 therapy. The median duration of response was 9.7 months, with 68% of patients having a response of at least 6 months and 40% of patients having a response of at least 12 months. The ORR was 52% in the 27 patients with known platinum-resistant SCLC and 31% in the 42 patients with known platinum-sensitive disease.

    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components.
    Headline
    cytokine release syndrome
    General Information
    Cytokine release syndrome (CRS) is a potentially fatal reaction that has been reported in patients receiving tarlatamab therapy. Symptoms include fever, headache, hypotension, hypoxia, nausea, cardiac dysfunction, neurologic toxicity, renal or hepatic failure and disseminated intravascular coagulation (DIC). To reduce the risk of CRS, ensure adequate hydration, administer tarlatamab according to a step-up dosing schedule, and give concomitant medications before and after cycle 1. An interruption or discontinuation of therapy may be necessary in patients who develop CRS; hospitalization and supportive therapy may be required based on the severity of CRS.
    Headline
    activities requiring coordination and concentration, driving or operating machinery, neurotoxicity
    General Information
    Neurotoxicity has been reported with tarlatamab therapy including immune effector cell-associated neurotoxicity syndrome (ICANS); some cases were life-threatening. Neurotoxicity/ICANS may occur concurrently with cytokine release syndrome (CRS), following CRS, or in the absence of CRS. Therapy interruption or permanent discontinuation may be necessary in patients who develop severe neurological toxicity. Advise patients who experience neurotoxicity during tarlatamab therapy to refrain from driving or operating machinery or performing other dangerous duties or activities requiring coordination and concentration until the toxicity resolves.
    Headline
    infection
    General Information
    Do not administer tarlatamab in patients who have an active infection during the step-up dosing schedule. Serious and life-threatening cases of infection have occurred in patients treated with tarlatamab in a pooled safety population, including opportunistic infections. An interruption or discontinuation of therapy may be necessary depending on the severity.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Tarlatamab may cause fetal harm when administered during pregnancy, based on its mechanism of action. There are no available data on the use of tarlatamab during pregnancy to inform a drug-associated risk. Human immunoglobulin G (IgG) and proteins with IgG-derived Fc domains are known to cross the placental barrier; hence, tarlatamab has the potential to be transmitted to the developing fetus. In an animal reproduction study, a murine surrogate molecule administered IV to pregnant mice crossed the placental barrier. Tarlatamab causes T-cell activation and cytokine release, which may compromise the maintenance of pregnancy.
    Headline

    General Information
    Counsel people who may become pregnant about the reproductive risk associated with tarlatamab. Tarlatamab may cause fetal harm. Verify pregnancy status prior to treatment initiation. Advise people who may become pregnant to use effective contraception while taking tarlatamab and for 2 months after the last dose.
    Headline

    General Information
    Advise patients to avoid breast-feeding while taking tarlatamab and for 2 months after the last dose due to the potential for serious adverse reactions. There are no data on the presence of tarlatamab in human milk, its effects on the breast-fed child, or its effects on milk production. Interactions
Monitor for toxicity and adjust the dose as needed for CYP substrates, especially during the first month of tarlatamab therapy and during and after cytokine release syndrome. Tarlatamab causes a cytokine release that may suppress CYP enzyme activity.

    cytokine release syndrome
    General Information
    Cytokine release syndrome (CRS) occurred in 57% (grade 3 or 4, 1.9%) of patients with extensive-stage small cell lung cancer treated with tarlatamab in a pooled safety population; recurrent CRS occurred in 24% (grade 3, 1 case) of patients treated with tarlatamab. Most CRS events (73%) occurred after the first dose, with 60% of events after the second dose and 15% after third dose or later. The median time to onset of CRS was 16 hours after administration. Adverse events related to CRS may include fever, hypotension, fatigue, tachycardia, headache, hypoxia, nausea, and vomiting. Cardiac dysfunction, acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS), neurologic toxicity, renal failure, hepatic failure, and disseminated intravascular coagulation (DIC) may also be indicative of CRS. Administer pretreatment medications and initiate therapy according to the step-up dosing schedule to reduce the risk of CRS. At the first sign of CRS, immediately discontinue tarlatamab, evaluate the need for hospitalization, and institute supportive care and corticosteroids as clinically appropriate based on severity. An interruption in therapy or permanent discontinuation may be necessary based on the severity of CRS.

    anemia, bone marrow suppression, leukopenia, lymphopenia, neutropenia, thrombocytopenia
    General Information
    Bone marrow suppression including anemia, leukopenia, lymphopenia, neutropenia, and thrombocytopenia have occurred in patients who received tarlatamab in clinical trials. Description
  Text
  Tarlatamab is a bispecific T-cell engaging (BiTE) monoclonal antibody that binds to delta-like ligand 3 (DLL3) expressed on small cell lung cancer (SCLC) cells and CD3 expressed on the surface of T-cells. It is indicated for the treatment of extensive stage SCLC with disease progression on or after platinum-based chemotherapy. Tarlatamab has a black box warning for cytokine release syndrome and neurotoxicity; some cases were life-threatening or fatal.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Tarlatamab is a bispecific T-cell engaging (BiTE) monoclonal antibody. BiTE molecules consist of 2 single-chain variable fragment (scFv) binding domains - one which always targets CD3 on T-cells, and one which can be modified to target any surface antigen - joined by a flexible peptide linker. For tarlatamab, the modifiable scFv binding domain targets delta-like protein 3 (DLL3) which is expressed on the surface of small cell lung cancer (SCLC) cells, but not on normal lung tissue. The linking of DLL3 on SCLC cells with the CD3 receptor on T-cells via the BiTE molecule causes T-cell activation, release of inflammatory cytokines, and cell lysis independent of normal CD3/major histocompatibility complex (MHC) recognition. Tarlatamab has shown antitumor activity in mouse models of SCLC.

Pharmacodynamics
Transient elevation of IL-2, IL-6, IL-8, IL-10, and interferon gamma were observed at tarlatamab doses greater than or equal to 0.3 mg. Peak cytokine elevation was generally 24 hours after the 1-mg tarlatamab dose given on cycle 1, day 1; cytokine levels generally returned to baseline before cycle 1, day 8.
 
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes and drug transporters: None
Tarlatamab is expected to be metabolized into small peptides by catabolic pathways. If cytokine release syndrome (CRS) occurs, the resultant cytokine release may suppress CYP450 enzymes and may result in an increased exposure of concomitant CYP substrates during and for up to 14 days afterwards.

Premedication
For cycle 1, day 1 and 8, premedicate with dexamethasone 8 mg IV (or equivalent) within 1 hour prior to tarlatamab administration.
For cycle 1, days 1 and 8, administer 1 liter of 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection IV over 2 to 4 hours beginning immediately after completion of the infusion.

Reconstitution
Inject the appropriate amount of Sterile Water for Injection (below) against the wall of the glass vial; avoid injecting directly onto the powder to prevent foaming. Do NOT use the IV Solution Stabilizer (IVSS) for reconstitution.
1 mg vial: Inject 1.3 mL of Sterile Water for Injection. Each vial contains overfill to compensate for liquid loss during preparation and after reconstitution, with the total resulting volume after dilution delivering a final concentration of 0.9 mg/mL.
10 mg vial: Inject 4.4 mL of Sterile Water for Injection. Each vial contains overfill to compensate for liquid loss during preparation and after reconstitution, with the total resulting volume after dilution delivering a final concentration of 2.4 mg/mL.
NOTE: the final concentrations for the 1 mg and 10 mg vials are NOT the same following reconstitution.
Gently swirl; do not shake.
Storage after reconstitution: Reconstituted tarlatamab must be diluted within 4 hours of reconstitution or discarded.
 
Dilution
1 mg dose: Withdraw 14 mL of 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection from a 250 mL IV bag.
10 mg dose: Withdraw 17 mL of 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection from a 250 mL IV bag.
Inject 13 mL of the IVSS into the 250 mL bag of 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection and gently mix; do not shake to avoid foaming. The IVSS prevents the adsorption of tarlatamab to IV bags and tubing.
Add 1.1 mL for the 1 mg dose/vial, or 4.2 mL for the 10 mg dose/vial, to the bag of 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection with IVSS. The final concentrations for the different strength vials are NOT the same following reconstitution and further dilution.
Gently mix; do not shake.
Remove air from the IV bag using an empty syringe to avoid foaming.
Prime the IV tubing with either 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection or with the final prepared product.
Storage after dilution: Tarlatamab may be stored at room temperature (20 to 25 degrees C; 68 to 77 degrees F) for up to 8 hours from the time of reconstitution to the end of the infusion, or refrigerated (2 to 8 degrees C; 36 to 46 degrees F) for up to 7 days from the time of reconstitution to the end of the infusion. Do not re-refrigerate a diluted tarlatamab bag.
 
Intravenous Infusion
Tarlatamab may be administered via the same IV line as dexamethasone when administered as a premedication.
Regardless, flush the IV catheter over 3 to 5 minutes using 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection.
If refrigerated, allow prepared tarlatamab infusion bag to come to room temperature prior to administration; complete infusion within 8 hours (including preparation and infusion time).
Using a programmable, lockable, non-elastomeric infusion pump with an alarm, infuse tarlatamab over 1 hour, at a constant flow rate of 250 mL/hour; flush IV line following completion of infusion.
 
Post-Infusion Monitoring
Cycle 1, Days 1 and 8: Monitor patients in an appropriate healthcare setting for 22 to 24 hours after the start of the tarlatamab infusion. It is recommended that patients stay with a caregiver, within 1 hour of an appropriate healthcare setting, for a total of 48 hours from the start of the tarlatamab infusion.
Cycle 1, Day 15 and Cycle 2: Observe patients for 6 to 8 hours after the tarlatamab infusion.
Cycles 3 and 4: Observe patients for 3 to 4 hours after the tarlatamab infusion.
Cycle 5 and beyond: Observe patients for 2 hours after the tarlatamab infusion.
Extended monitoring in a healthcare setting is not required after cycle 1, day 8, unless the patient experiences grade 2 or higher cytokine release syndrome (CRS), immune effector-cell associated neurotoxicity syndrome (ICANS), or neurological toxicity during prior infusions.
