Drug Class: Ibritumomab tiuxetan is a radiotherapeutic monoclonal antibody that binds to the CD20 molecule found on pre B- and mature B-lymphocytes.

          0.4 millicurie (mCi) (14.8 Megabecquerels (MBq)) per kg of actual body weight (maximum of 32 mCi) IV over 10 minutes given once on day 7, 8, or 9 within 4 hours following the completion of the second rituximab infusion. Immediately stop the infusion if signs or symptoms of extravasation occurs. The therapeutic regimen includes 2 total doses of rituximab 250 mg/m2 given on day 1 (initial rate of 50 mg/hour increased by 50 mg/hour increments every 30 minutes to a maximum of 400 mg/hour in the absence of infusion reactions) and then on days 7, 8, or 9 (initial rate of 100 mg/hour increased by 100 mg/hour increments every 30 minutes to a maximum of 400 mg/hour in the absence of current or prior infusion reactions). All patients require premedication with acetaminophen 650 mg PO and diphenhydramine 50 mg PO prior to rituximab; the rituximab infusion may need to be slowed, interrupted, or discontinued if an infusion reaction occurs. Y-90-ibritumomab tiuxetan plus rituximab following first-line chemotherapy with cyclophosphamide, hydroxydaunorubicin, vincristine, prednisone (CHOP) (31.4%); a CHOP-like regimen (14.7%); cyclophosphamide, vincristine, prednisone (CVP/COP) (25.5%); a rituximab-containing regimen (13.2%); chlorambucil (9.8%); or a fludarabine-containing regimen (5.4%) was evaluated in 208 patients with previously untreated stage III or IV follicular lymphoma (age range, 27 to 78 years) in a multicenter, randomized, phase 3 trial (n = 414). In patients who achieved a complete or partial response to first-line therapy, the median progression-free survival (PFS) time was significantly improved with Y-90-ibritumomab tiuxetan compared with no further treatment (36.5 months vs. 13.3 months; hazard ratio (HR) = 0.465; 95% CI, 0.357 to 0.605; p less than 0.0001) at a median follow-up time of 3.5 years. The median PFS time was 4.1 years in the Y-90-ibritumomab tiuxetan arm compared with 1.1 years in the control arm (p less than 0.001) at a median follow-up of 7.3 years; additionally, the 8-year PFS rates were 41% and 22% in the Y90-ibritumomab tiuxetan and control arms, respectively (HR = 0.47; 95% CI, 0.37 to 0.6; p less than 0.001).

Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  General Information
  NOTE: The ibritumomab tiuxetan therapeutic regimen includes the use of rituximab (Rituxan). All contraindications and precautions to rituximab also apply (see separate Rituximab monograph).
 
Ibritumomab tiuxetan therapeutic regimen contains indium 111 (In-111) and yttrium 90 (Y-90), which are radiopharmaceuticals and should be used only by physicians and other professionals qualified by training and experienced in the safe use and handling of radionuclides. The ibritumomab tiuxetan therapeutic regimen may be inappropriate for patients with known murine protein hypersensitivity, albumin hypersensitivity, rituximab hypersensitivity, Y-90 hypersensitivity, In-111 hypersensitivity, or ibritumomab tiuxetan hypersensitivity. Hypersensitivity and anaphylactic reactions have been reported and appropriate medications and supportive equipment should be available during administration of the ibritumomab tiuxetan therapeutic regimen. Patients who develop human anti-murine antibody (HAMA) or human anti-chimeric antibody (HACA) titers may be at increased risk for hypersensitivity reactions during the ibritumomab tiuxetan therapeutic regimen administration or treatment with other murine or chimeric monoclonal antibodies. HAMA and HACA data are available for 446 patients enrolled in 8 clinical trials. Overall, 11 (2.5%) patients developed HAMA and/or HACA formation during the ibritumomab tiuxetan regimen, 6 of which were positive for HAMA and/or HACA at baseline. In 6 of these 11 patients, HAMA/HACA reverted to negative within 2 weeks to 3 months, and no patients had increasing levels of HAMA/HACA. In addition, severe cutaneous and sometimes fatal reactions including Stevens-Johnson syndrome, toxic epidermal necrolysis, erythema multiforme, bullous dermatitis, and exfoliative dermatitis have been reported in association with the ibritumomab tiuxetan therapeutic regimen. Patients experiencing a severe cutaneous reaction should not receive any further component of the ibritumomab tiuxetan therapeutic regimen; prompt medical attention should be sought. Rituximab has also been associated with hypersensitivity reactions (non-IgE mediated reactions) that may respond to adjustments in the infusion rate and medical management. Hypotension, acute bronchospasm, and angioedema have occurred during rituximab infusions (see Adverse Reactions). For severe hypersensitivity reactions, the rituximab, In-111 ibritumomab tiuxetan or Y-90 ibritumomab tiuxetan infusions may be interrupted.
 
Ibritumomab tiuxetan therapeutic regimen contains indium 111 (In-111) and yttrium 90 (Y-90), which are radiopharmaceuticals and should be used only by physicians and other professionals qualified by training and experienced in the safe use and handling of radionuclides. Patients should not receive more than the maximum allowable dose of Y-90 ibritumomab tiuxetan (see Dosage Limits). Additionally, Y-90 ibritumomab tiuxetan should not be administered to patients with an altered biodistribution of In-111 ibritumomab tiuxetan. Imaging with In-111 ibritumomab tiuxetan is used as a safety measure to ensure appropriate biodistribution of the radioimmunoconjugate before proceeding with the therapeutic dose of Y-90 ibritumomab tiuxetan. The biodistribution of In-111 ibritumomab tiuxetan is assessed by a visual evaluation of whole-body, planar view, anterior and posterior gamma images at 2—24 hours and 48—72 hours after injection. To resolve ambiguities, a third image at 90—120 hours may be obtained. Using a 256 x 1024 computer acquisition matrix, the scan speed should be 10 cm/min for the first scan, 7 cm/min for the second, and 5 cm/min for the optional third scan. The expected biodistribution of In-111 ibritumomab includes easily detectable uptake in the blood pool area on the first day image, with less activity in the blood pool on the second or third day image; moderately high uptake in normal liver and spleen during the first day and second or third day image; and moderately low or very low uptake in normal kidneys, urinary bladder, and normal bowel on the first day image and second or third day image. If visual inspection of gamma images reveals a markedly altered biodistribution, the patient should not proceed to the therapeutic dose of Y-90 ibritumomab tiuxetan. The patient may be considered to have an altered biodistribution if the blood pool is not visualized on the first image indicating rapid clearance of the radiopharmaceutical by the reticuloendothelial system to the liver, spleen and/or bone marrow. Other potential examples of altered biodistribution of In-111 ibritumomab tiuxetan can be characterized by diffuse uptake in normal lung more intense than the cardiac blood pool on the first day image or more intense than the liver on the second or third day image; uptake in kidneys greater intensity than in the liver on the posterior view of the second or third day image; or intense areas of uptake throughout normal bowel comparable to uptake by the liver on the second or third day images. Localization to lymphoid aggregates in the bowel wall has been reported. Tumor uptake may be visualized as areas of increased intensity, and tumor-bearing areas in normal organs may be seen as areas of increased or decreased intensity. Although solid organ toxicity has not been directly attributed to radiation from adjacent tumors, careful consideration should be applied before proceeding with treatment in patients with very high radiation uptake in tumors adjacent to critical organs or structures.
 
Ibritumomab tiuxetan therapeutic regimen contains indium 111 (In-111) and yttrium 90 (Y-90), which are radiopharmaceuticals and should be used only by physicians and other professionals qualified by training and experienced in the safe use and handling of radionuclides. Patients should not receive more than the maximum allowable dose of Y-90 ibritumomab tiuxetan (see Dosage Limits). Close monitoring of hematologic parameters during the ibritumomab tiuxetan therapeutic regimen is required due to severe bone marrow suppression, including anemia, leukopenia, and especially neutropenia and thrombocytopenia. The ibritumomab tiuxetan therapeutic regimen should not be administered to patients with >= 25% lymphoma bone marrow involvement and/or impaired bone marrow reserve (e.g., prior myeloablative therapy), thrombocytopenia (platelet count = 25,000/mm3) with or without evidence of high tumor burden. Patients with preexisting cardiac arrhythmias and angina have had recurrences during rituximab therapy; monitor patients closely during the infusion and the immediate post-infusion period. Those patients who develop cardiac arrhythmias or angina during rituximab infusion should have the infusion stopped and undergo cardiac monitoring during and after subsequent infusions. Severe hypotension has occurred during rituximab infusions. Patients with underlying hypotension may be at increased risk for severe reactions. Patients being treated for hypertension should be instructed not to take their antihypertensive medication 12 hours before rituximab infusions.
 
Intramuscular injections should not be given to patients with a platelet count = 2 weeks. Those undergoing chemotherapy should not be exposed to others who have recently received the oral poliovirus vaccine (OPV). Measles-mumps-rubella (MMR) vaccination is not contraindicated for the close contacts, including health care professionals, of immunocompromised patients. Passive immunoprophylaxis with immune globulins may be indicated for immunocompromised persons instead of, or in addition to, vaccination. When exposed to a vaccine-preventable disease such as measles, severely immunocompromised children should be considered susceptible regardless of their vaccination history.

    Headline
    prolonged and/or recurrent cytopenias
    General Information
    Prolonged and/or recurrent cytopenias resulting in bleeding and severe infection have occurred with Yttrium (Y)-90 ibritumomab tiuxetan therapy. Do not administer Y-90 ibritumomab tiuxetan in patients with 25% or greater lymphoma marrow involvement and/or impaired bone marrow reserve or in patients who have a platelet count less than 100,000 cells/mm3. Obtain complete blood counts including platelets once weekly until counts recover or as clinically indicated. Monitor patients for signs and symptoms of bleeding or febrile neutropenia for up to 3 months after Y-90 ibritumomab tiuxetan therapy. During and after therapy, avoid concomitant use with other drugs that interfere with platelet function or coagulation (e.g., antiplatelet or anticoagulant therapy).
    Headline
    infusion-related reactions
    General Information
    Severe infusion-related reactions have been reported with rituximab and Y-90 ibritumomab tiuxetan administration. Fatalities have occurred within 24 hours of the rituximab infusion; 80% of deaths occurred with the first infusion. Therefore, rituximab and Y-90 ibritumomab tiuxetan should be administered in facilities that have immediate access to resuscitative measures. Symptoms including urticaria, hypotension, angioedema, bronchospasm, hypoxia, pulmonary infiltrates, acute respiratory distress syndrome, myocardial infarction, ventricular fibrillation, or cardiogenic shock typically occurred within 30 to 120 minutes from the start of the first rituximab infusion. Immediately discontinue rituximab and Y-90 ibritumomab tiuxetan administration in patients who develop a severe infusion reaction.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Y-90 ibritumomab tiuxetan may cause fetal harm when administered during pregnancy, based on its radioactivity. Although there are no data on the use of Y-90 ibritumomab tiuxetan in pregnant women or in animals, some immunoglobulins are known to cross the placenta. Females of reproductive potential should avoid pregnancy during and following ibritumomab tiuxetan therapy. If Y-90 ibritumomab tiuxetan is used during pregnancy, the woman should be apprised of the potential hazard to the fetus.
    Headline
    contraception requirements, infertility, male-mediated teratogenicity, pregnancy testing, reproductive risk
    General Information
    Counsel patients about the reproductive risk and contraception requirements during ibritumomab tiuxetan treatment. Pregnancy testing should be performed prior to starting ibritumomab tiuxetan in females of reproductive potential. These patients should avoid pregnancy and use effective contraception during and for 12 months after ibritumomab tiuxetan therapy. Patients who become pregnant while receiving ibritumomab tiuxetan should be apprised of the potential hazard to the fetus. There have been no studies to evaluate if ibritumomab tiuxetan causes infertility; however, a significant amount of radiation to the testes has been demonstrated in clinical studies. There is a potential risk that ibritumomab tiuxetan may cause toxic effects on the male and female gonads. Due to a risk of male-mediated teratogenicity, men with female partners of reproductive potential should avoid fathering a child and use effective contraception during and for 12 months following ibritumomab tiuxetan therapy.
    Headline
    serious mucocutaneous reactions
    General Information
    Serious mucocutaneous reactions, such as erythema multiforme, Stevens-Johnson syndrome, toxic epidermal necrolysis, bullous dermatitis, and exfoliative dermatitis have been reported with the ibritumomab tiuxetan therapeutic regimen; some cases were fatal. Discontinue ibritumomab tiuxetan infusion in patients experiencing severe cutaneous or mucocutaneous reactions.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    It is not known if ibritumomab tiuxetan or it's metabolites are secreted in human milk or have effects on the breast-fed infant or on milk production. The presence of ibritumomab tiuxetan is expected in human breast milk, as human IgG is excreted in human milk. Due to the risk of serious adverse reactions in nursing infants, women should discontinue breast-feeding during Y-90 ibritumomab tiuxetan therapy and for 6 months after the last dose.

Calcium Acetate: (Moderate) The oral absorption of phosphorus is reduced by ingestion of pharmacologic doses of calcium carbonate or other phosphate-lowering calcium salts (e.g., calcium acetate). There is, however, no significant interference with phosphorus absorption by oral dietary calcium at intakes within the typical adult range. If the patient requires multiple calcium supplements or a calcium-containing antacid, it may be wise to separate the administration of phosphorus salts from calcium-containing products. In some instances the administration of calcium salts or calcium carbonate is used therapeutically (e.g., uremia) to decrease serum phosphorus levels, so the administration of phosphorus supplements would dynamically counteract the intended use of calcium in these settings, assuming hypophosphatemia is not present. Appropriate calcium-phosphorus ratios in vivo are important for proper calcium homeostasis in tissues and bone; if the serum ionized calcium concentration is elevated, the concomitant use of calcium salts and phosphorus salts may increase the risk of calcium deposition in soft tissue.

Chromium: (Moderate) The oral absorption of phosphorus is reduced by ingestion of pharmacologic doses of calcium carbonate or other phosphate-lowering calcium salts (e.g., calcium acetate). There is, however, no significant interference with phosphorus absorption by oral dietary calcium at intakes within the typical adult range. If the patient requires multiple calcium supplements or a calcium-containing antacid, it may be wise to separate the administration of phosphorus salts from calcium-containing products. In some instances the administration of calcium salts or calcium carbonate is used therapeutically (e.g., uremia) to decrease serum phosphorus levels, so the administration of phosphorus supplements would dynamically counteract the intended use of calcium in these settings, assuming hypophosphatemia is not present. Appropriate calcium-phosphorus ratios in vivo are important for proper calcium homeostasis in tissues and bone; if the serum ionized calcium concentration is elevated, the concomitant use of calcium salts and phosphorus salts may increase the risk of calcium deposition in soft tissue.

  General Information
  NOTE: The ibritumomab tiuxetan therapeutic regimen includes the use of rituximab (Rituxan). All adverse reactions to rituximab also apply (see separate Rituximab monograph).
 
Administration of the ibritumomab tiuxetan therapeutic regimen results in sustained lymphopenia, primarily depletion of circulating B-cells. Hematologic toxicity consisting of thrombocytopenia (95%), neutropenia (77%), and anemia (61%), which can be prolonged and severe, is the most frequently observed adverse event of the ibritumomab tiuxetan therapeutic regimen. The median platelet nadir was 41,000/mm3 (median duration 24 days) in patients with baseline platelet counts >= 150,000/mm3 and 24,000/mm3 (median duration 35 days) in patients with baseline platelet counts of 100,000—149,000/mm3 who were treated with a modified ibritumomab tiuxetan therapeutic regimen. The median time to platelet nadir was 53 days. The median absolute neutrophil count (ANC) nadir was 800/mm3 (median duration 22 days) in patients with baseline platelet counts >= 150,000/mm3 and 600/mm3 (median duration 29 days) in patients with baseline platelet counts of 100,000—149,000/mm3 who were treated with a modified ibritumomab tiuxetan therapeutic regimen. The median time to ANC nadir was 62 days. Median time to hemoglobin nadir was 68 days. In < 5% of cases, patients experienced severe myelosuppression that extended beyond the prospectively defined protocol treatment period of 12 weeks following the administration of the ibritumomab tiuxetan therapeutic regimen. Intracranial bleeding, including fatal cerebral hemorrhage, occurred in a minority of patients. Other bleeding-related adverse reactions reported include ecchymosis (7%), petechiae (3%), epistaxis (3%), and melena (2%). Severe or life-threatening adverse hematologic events consisted of pancytopenia (2%), GI bleeding (1%), melena (1%), and in < 1% of patients, subdural hematoma, pulmonary embolism, hematemesis, or vaginal bleeding. Up to 20% of patients treated received supportive care consisting of filgrastim, erythropoietin, and/or platelet or red blood cell transfusions.
 
Infection (29%), primarily bacterial in origin, is another common adverse reaction to the ibritumomab tiuxetan therapeutic regimen during the first 3 months following initiation of treatment. Three percent of patients developed serious infections comprising urinary tract infections, febrile neutropenia, sepsis, pneumonia, cellulitis, colitis, diarrhea, osteomyelitis, and upper respiratory tract infections. Life threatening infections were reported in 2% of patients and included sepsis, empyema, pneumonia, febrile neutropenia, fever, and biliary stent-associated cholangitis. During follow-up from 3 months to 4 years after the start of ibritumomab tiuxetan therapy, 6% of patients developed infections.
 
Infusion reactions, which are sometimes severe and/or fatal, are associated with rituximab, an integral component of the ibritumomab tiuxetan therapeutic regimen. During the first infusion of rituximab, about 80% of patients experience rituximab infusion-related reactions; the incidence decreases with subsequent rituximab infusions (30% with the fourth, 14% with the eighth). Infusion-related reactions generally occur within 30 minutes to 2 hours of the start of the first rituximab infusion. These reactions may reflect hypersensitivity reactions in some patients. Fever and chills are the most common infusion-related reactions, occurring in 53% and 33% of patients, respectively. A cytokine release syndrome resulting in severe pulmonary and cardiovascular infusion-related events has been reported within 24 hours of rituximab infusions and has resulted in fatal outcomes in some situations. These fatal reactions followed an infusion reaction complex that included hypoxia, pulmonary infiltrates, acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS), myocardial infarction, ventricular fibrillation, or cardiogenic shock. Approximately 80% of fatal infusion reactions occurred with the first rituximab infusion. (See Rituximab monograph for more information regarding serious adverse reactions.)
 
Allergic reactions (2%) including anaphylactoid reactions and anaphylactic shock may occur during the ibritumomab tiuxetan therapeutic regimen. Hypersensitivity reactions have included symptoms of bronchospasm (5%), wheezing, angioedema (5%), pruritus (9%), rash (unspecified) (8%), hypotension (6%), and urticaria (4%). There have been post-marketing reports of severe cutaneous and mucocutaneous reactions such as erythema multiforme, Stevens-Johnson syndrome, toxic epidermal necrolysis, bullous rash (bullous dermatitis), and exfoliative dermatitis in patients who received the ibritumomab tiuxetan therapeutic regimen. Some of these events were fatal. The onset of the reactions varied from days to months after drug initiation. If a severe cutaneous or mucocutaneous reaction occurs, do not administer any further components of the ibritumomab tiuxetan therapeutic regimen to the patient. The patient should be promptly evaluated.
 
Gastrointestinal symptoms (48%) were also commonly reported during the ibritumomab tiuxetan therapeutic regimen and were usually mild. Commonly reported GI adverse reactions included nausea/vomiting (31%/12%), abdominal pain (16%), diarrhea (9%), anorexia (8%), abdominal enlargement (5%), constipation (5%), and dyspepsia (4%).
 
A secondary malignancy [myelodysplastic syndrome (MDS) and/or acute myelogenous leukemia (AML)] occurred in 11 of the 211 patients enrolled in clinical studies; median follow-up was 6.5 years, and the median time to development of MDS/AML was 2.9 years. The cumulative Kaplan-Meier estimated incidence of MDS/secondary leukemia was 2.2% at 2 years and 5.9% at 5 years. Among 535 patients included in the expanded-access trial, 8 developed MDS or AML; median follow-up was 4.4 years, and the median time to development of MDS/AML was 1.5 years. Among the 19 reported cases, the median time to the diagnosis of MDS or AML was 1.9 years following ibritumomab tiuxetan treatment; however, the cumulative incidence continues to increase. Multiple cytogenetic abnormalities were described; chromosomes 5 and/or 7 were most commonly involved. The risk of MDS/AML was not associated with the number of prior treatments (0—1 versus 2—10). Among 204 patients receiving the ibritumomab tiuxetan therapeutic regimen after first-line chemotherapy, 2 (1%) were diagnosed with AML within 3 years of receiving the regimen.
 
In clinical trials, antibody formation (2.5%, 11 of 446 patients) was reported during the ibritumomab tiuxetan therapeutic regimen as evidenced by human anti-murine antibody (HAMA) or human anti-chimeric antibody (HACA). Six of the patients had evidence of HAMA and/or HACA prior to initiation of ibritumomab. HAMA/HACA reverted to negative within 2 weeks to 3 months in 6 of the 11 patients with positive formation. No patients had increasing levels of HAMA/HACA.
 
In post-marketing surveillance, patients receiving the ibritumomab tiuxetan therapeutic regimen have developed skin ulcer and erythema after extravasation of yttrium-90 ibritumomab tiuxetan. If signs or symptoms of extravasation occur, immediately terminate the infusion and restarted in another limb. If extravasation occurs, contact the facility radiation safety officer immediately. A case of radiation-induced skin necrosis occurred in a 64-year old patient after an yttrium-90 ibritumomab tiuxetan extravasation. Erythema developed within 24 hours of extravasation and progressed to desquamation, requiring skin-graft, by 28 days post-infusion. In addition, radiation-induced tissue damage near areas of lymphomatous involvement has been reported in post-marketing surveillance in patients receiving the ibritumomab tiuxetan therapeutic regimen. Damage typically occurs within a month of administration of the ibritumomab tiuxetan therapeutic regimen.
 
Additional adverse reactions occurring in patients treated with the ibritumomab tiuxetan therapeutic regimen, regardless of causation, include asthenia (weakness) (43%), chills (24%), fever (17%), dyspnea (14%), headache (12%), increased cough (10%), dizziness (10%), throat irritation (10%), back pain (8%), peripheral edema (8%), arthralgia (7%), myalgia (7%), flushing (6%), rhinitis (6%), insomnia (5%), and anxiety (4%). The following severe or life-threatening events occurred in <= 1% of patients: apnea, angioedema, tachycardia, urticaria, arthritis, pulmonary edema, and encephalopathy.

A theoretical risk for transmission of Creutzfeldt-Jakob disease (CJD) also is considered extremely remote. No cases of transmission of viral diseases or CJD have ever been identified for albumin, a derivative of human blood.

Description
  Text
  Ibritumomab tiuxetan is a radiotherapeutic monoclonal antibody that binds to the CD20 molecule found on pre B- and mature B-lymphocytes. It is indicated in combination with rituximab for relapsed or refractory, low-grade or follicular B-cell non-Hodgkin lymphoma (NHL) and previously untreated follicular NHL in patients who achieve a partial or complete response to first-line chemotherapy. Serious infusion reactions, prolonged and severe cytopenias, and severe cutaneous and mucocutaneous reactions have been reported with ibritumomab tiuxetan therapy.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  The therapeutic component of the ibritumomab tiuxetan therapeutic regimen is Y-90 ibritumomab tiuxetan. The beta emission from the chelated radionuclide, Y-90, induces cellular damage by the formation of free radicals in the target and neighboring cells. In vitro studies have shown that the ibritumomab antibody induces apoptosis (programmed cell death) in CD20+ B-cell lines. In vivo studies have shown that ibritumomab may also promote weak Fc-mediated phagocytosis of CD20+ cells (Note: The Fc fragment is composed of the C-terminal halves of the immunoglobulin heavy chains and is responsible for secondary activities of immunoglobulins such as complement fixation). Ibritumomab tiuxetan binding is only observed on lymphoid cells of the bone marrow, lymph node, thymus, red and white pulp of the spleen, and lymphoid follicles of the tonsil, as well as lymphoid nodules of other organs such as the large and small intestines. No binding has been observed in non-lymphoid tissues. During clinical development, the estimates of the amount of radiation absorbed by an individual tumor were as high as 778 cGy/mCi. In clinical studies, the ibritumomab tiuxetan therapeutic regimen results in sustained depletion of circulating B-cells. At 4 weeks, the median number of circulating B-cells is zero (range, 0—1084/mm3). B-cell recovery begins at approximately 12 weeks following treatment. The median number of B-cells is within the normal range by 9 months after treatment.

Administration
  General Information
  For storage information, see the specific product information within the How Supplied section.
CAUTION: Yttrium-90-ibritumomab tiuxetan is a radiopharmaceutical. Minimize radiation exposure in patients, medical personnel, and household contact in accordance with good radiation safety practices.
  Route Specific Administrations
    Administration Details
      Name
      Intravenous Administration
      Description
      The kit contains 1) a single-dose vial of 3.2 mg ibritumomab tiuxetan in 2 mL of 0.9% sodium chloride; 2) a sodium acetate 50 milliMolar vial; 3) a formulation buffer vial; and 4) an empty reaction vial.
Components required to make the final product that are not included in the kit include a Yttrium (Y)-90 chloride sterile solution; ITLC silica gel strips; 0.9% sodium chloride aqueous solution (for the chromatography solvent); and a 0.22 micrometer, low-protein-binding filter.
Additionally, a developing chamber for chromatography, a suitable radioactivity counting apparatus, and appropriate acrylic shielding for the reaction vial and Y-90 syringe are required for dose preparation.
Dose Preparation:
Allow the ibritumomab tiuxetan vial, sodium acetate vial, and formulation buffer vial to reach room temperature; place the empty reaction vial in an appropriate acrylic shield.
Calculate the amount of each component as follows: 1) the volume of Y-90 chloride is equivalent to 40 millicuries (mCi) based on the activity concentration of the Y-90 chloride stock; 2) the volume of sodium acetate solution needed is 1.2-times the volume of the Y-90 chloride solution; 3) add enough formulation buffer to produce a total volume of 10 mL.
Transfer the calculated volume of sodium acetate solution to the empty reaction vial; coat the entire inner surface of the reaction vial by gentle inversion or rolling.
Transfer 40 mCi of Y-90 chloride to the reaction vial using an acrylic shielded syringe; mix the two solutions by gentle inversion or rolling.
Transfer 1.3 mL of ibritumomab tiuxetan to the reaction vial; do not shake or agitate the vial contents.
Allow the labeling reaction to proceed at room temperature for 5 minutes; a shorter or longer reaction time may adversely alter the final labeled product.
Immediately after the 5-minute incubation period, gently add the formulation buffer down the side of the reaction vial; if necessary, withdraw an equal volume of air to normalize the pressure.
Measure the final product for total activity; record the date and time of preparation, the total activity and volume, and the date and time of expiration using the supplied labels and affix these labels to the shielded reaction vial container.
Calculate the volume required for a patient-specific Y-90 ibritumomab tiuxetan dose; withdraw the required volume from the reaction vial into a syringe.
Assay the syringe in the dose calibrator; the measured dose must be within 10% of the prescribed dose and must not exceed 32 mCi (1,184 Megabecquerels); record the patient identifier, total activity and volume, and the date and time of expiration using the supplied labels and affix these labels to the syringe and shielded unit dose container.
Determine the radiochemical purity by placing a small drop of Y-90 ibritumomab tiuxetan at the origin of an ITLC silica gel strip. Place the ITLC silica gel strip into a chromatography chamber with the origin at the bottom and the solvent front at the top. Allow the 0.9% sodium chloride solvent to migrate at least 5 cm from the bottom of the strip. Remove the strip from the chamber and cut the strip in half. Count each half of the ITLC silica gel strip for one minute (CPM) with a suitable counting apparatus. Calculate the percent radiochemical purity by dividing the CPM bottom half by the sum of the CPM bottom half plus CPM top half and multiplying the result by 100. Repeat the ITLC procedure if the radiochemical purity is less than 95%. If repeat testing confirms that radiochemical purity is less than 95%, do not administer the Y-90 ibritumomab tiuxetan dose.
Store the Y-90 ibritumomab tiuxetan syringe at 2 to 8 degrees C (36 to 46 degrees F) until use; administer within 8 hours of radiolabeling. Assay the syringe and contents immediately before administration to the patient.
Intravenous (IV) infusion:
Administer as an IV infusion over 10 minutes through a free flowing IV line; use a 0.22-micron low-protein-binding in-line filter between the syringe and the infusion port.
If signs or symptoms of extravasation occur, immediately terminate the infusion and restart in another limb.
Flush the IV line with at least 10 mL of 0.9% sodium chloride injection after the infusion.
