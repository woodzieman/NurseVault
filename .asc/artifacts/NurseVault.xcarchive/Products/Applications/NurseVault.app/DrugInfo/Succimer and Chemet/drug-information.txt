Drug Class: Succimer is an oral chelating agent approved for the treatment of lead toxicity or poisoning in pediatric patients with blood lead concentrations greater than 45 mcg/dL.

Routes: oral; Common adult doses: Safety and efficacy have not been established; however, doses up to 30 mg/kg/day PO have been used off-label. FDA-approved maximum in pediatric patients weighing 45 kg or more is 1,500 mg/day PO.

Common Side Effects (excluding routine GI upset): hemorrhoids, metallic taste, dizziness, drowsiness, paresthesias, peripheral neuropathy, eosinophilia, infection, neutropenia, thrombocytosis, angioedema, maculopapular rash, pruritus, rash, urticaria, vesicular rash, back pain, candidiasis, fatigue, flank pain, headache, musculoskeletal pain, chills, cough, fever, influenza, nasal congestion, rhinorrhea, throat irritation

In an Overdose: No overdose/antidote information is listed in this file.

Key Nursing Considerations: Contraindications/precautions: Assess renal function prior to and periodically during prolonged succimer therapy and monitor patients with a history of renal impairment or renal failure more frequently during therapy. Most of a succimer dose is excreted renally, including the water-soluble lead chelates.

Indication Specific Dosing
  General Information
  General Dosing Information
Identify the source of lead in the pediatric patient's environment and eliminate the source prior to beginning treatment with succimer. Succimer is not indicated for prophylaxis of lead poisoning in a lead-containing environment.
Elevated blood lead concentrations and associated symptoms may return rapidly after succimer discontinuation because of redistribution of lead from bone stores to soft tissues and blood.
In patients who have received edetate calcium disodium with or without dimercaprol, succimer can be used for subsequent treatment after an interval of 4 weeks.

    Headline
    hepatic failure
    General Information
    Monitor serum AST and ALT at baseline and at least weekly during treatment in all patients; monitor patients with a history of hepatic failure more frequently. Elevated transaminases (ALT/AST) have occurred in patients treated with succimer. Dose reduction or temporary cessation of treatment is recommended for serum aminotransferase elevations above 5 times the upper limit of normal (if confirmed).
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Use succimer during pregnancy only if the potential benefit justifies the potential risk to the fetus. There are no studies with the use of succimer during human pregnancy to inform drug-associated risk. However, based on findings from animal reproduction studies, succimer may cause fetal harm. Administration of succimer to pregnant mice during organogenesis at a dose exposure of 11 times the human exposure at the maximum recommended human dose (MRHD) of 700 mg based on body surface area (BSA) resulted in maternal toxicity and mortality and impaired reflex development in offspring. There are adverse effects on outcomes associated with lead poisoning in pregnancy. Lead exposure may increase the risk of gestational hypertension. Prenatal lead exposure may be associated with spontaneous abortion, preterm delivery, decreased birth weight, and impaired neurodevelopment. For pregnant people with blood lead concentrations of 5 mcg/dL or more, guidelines recommend removal of the lead source, disruption of the route of exposure, and avoidance of the lead-containing substance or activity. In circumstances where the blood lead concentration is 45 mcg/dL or more, chelation therapy may be warranted after consultation with an expert in lead poisoning; for lower lead concentrations, data are insufficient to advise the use of chelation during pregnancy. Avoid chelation during the first trimester in all pregnant people unless the lead intoxication is severe and life-threatening, due to the general concerns about drug use during organogenesis. Although no chelation-attributable toxicities have been reported in the existing published case reports of use during pregnancy, very limited information is available to understand any potential short- or long-term effects on the fetus.
    Headline
    people who may become pregnant, reproductive risk
    General Information
    Counsel people who may become pregnant about the reproductive risk associated with succimer. Succimer may cause fetal harm. Verify pregnancy status prior to treatment initiation. Advise people who may become pregnant to use effective contraception while taking succimer and for 14 days after the last dose.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Use succimer with caution during breast-feeding. There are no data on the presence of succimer or its metabolite in human milk, its effects on the breast-fed child, or its effects on milk production. When used for the treatment of lead poisoning, the amount of lead in breast milk may range from 0.6% to 3% of the maternal serum concentration. Those with confirmed blood lead concentrations of 40 mcg/dL or more should not initiate breast-feeding. Pumping and discarding breast milk is recommended until blood lead concentrations are less than 40 mcg/dL, at which point breast-feeding may resume. Calcium supplementation may reduce the amount of lead in breast milk.

Description
  Text
  Succimer is an oral chelating agent approved for the treatment of lead toxicity or poisoning in pediatric patients with blood lead concentrations greater than 45 mcg/dL. Treatment must always be used in conjunction with identification and removal of the source of the lead exposure. The drug is not intended to be used as prophylaxis of lead poisoning in a lead-containing environment. Succimer works by forming water-soluble chelates with lead, which are excreted in the urine. Succimer does not cross the blood-brain barrier and is not indicated to treat encephalopathy associated with lead toxicity. Succimer was FDA-approved in 1982.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Succimer is an orally administered lead chelator. It forms water-soluble chelates and, consequently, increases the urinary excretion of lead, thereby decreasing blood lead concentrations. Among children 2 to 7 years who received 10 mg/kg/dose every 8 hours for 5 days, the mean blood lead concentration decreased 78%. However, patients experienced a rebound in blood lead concentrations after succimer discontinuation. The mean lead concentration rebounded and plateaued at 60% to 85% of pretreatment concentrations 2 weeks after therapy. Succimer receipt beyond 5 days was studied to examine the effect of longer dosing on the rebound of blood lead concentrations. Children 1 to 7 years with blood lead concentrations of 42 to 67 mcg/dL who received 10 mg/kg/dose every 12 hours for 2 weeks after the initial 5 days of 10 mg/kg/dose every 8 hours did not experience rebound of blood lead concentrations after the initial 5-day treatment period. Further, less rebound was noted after succimer discontinuation. In another study, 10 children aged 21 to 72 months with blood lead concentrations of 30 to 57 mcg/dL received succimer 10 mg/kg/dose every 8 hours for 5 days, followed by 10 mg/kg/dose PO every 12 hours for an additional 19 to 22 days. Blood lead concentrations decreased and remained stable under 15 mcg/dL during the extended dosing period.
 
Succimer is an effective chelator with minimal effect on the excretion of essential minerals when compared to edetate calcium disodium, calcium EDTA (another medication used for the treatment of lead toxicity). Treatment with succimer results in a doubling in zinc excretion but does not have a significant effect on the urinary elimination of iron, calcium, or magnesium.

  Text
  Succimer is administered orally. Approximately 90% of the absorbed dose is metabolized to mixed succimer-cysteine disulfides. The majority of mixed disulfides consist of succimer in disulfide linkages with 2 molecules of L-cysteine, and the remaining disulfides contain 1 L-cysteine per succimer molecule. After administration of a single 10 mg/kg oral dose of succimer to healthy adults, approximately 25% of the dose was excreted in the urine, with peak blood concentration and urinary excretion occurring between 2 and 4 hours. Of the total amount of drug eliminated in the urine, the majority was excreted as mixed succimer-cysteine disulfides; only 10% was excreted as unchanged drug. After administration of a radiolabeled succimer dose in adult patients, the apparent elimination half-life in the blood was approximately 2 days.
