Drug Class: Pseudoephedrine; triprolidine is an oral combination of a nasal decongestant and antihistamine indicated for the temporary relief of symptoms, such as runny nose, itchy, watery eyes, nasal congestion, sneezing, or itching of the nose and throat, due to allergic rhinitis or other respiratory...

Routes: oral; Common adult doses: 240 mg/day PO pseudoephedrine; 10 mg/day PO triprolidine.

Common Side Effects (excluding routine GI upset): agitation, anxiety, confusion, dizziness, drowsiness, dyskinesia, hallucinations, headache, impaired cognition, insomnia, irritability, lethargy, psychosis, restlessness, seizures, tremor, angina, arrhythmia exacerbation, hypertension, hypotension, orthostatic hypotension, palpitations, peripheral vasodilation, premature ventricular contractions (pvcs), sinus tachycardia, supraventricular tachycardia (svt), blurred vision, diplopia, mydriasis, ocular hypertension

In an Overdose: Seizures may occur, but have more commonly occurred with excessive dosage, overdosage, or in patients with renal failure receiving the maximum dosage (without dosage adjustment).

Key Nursing Considerations: Contraindications/precautions: The anticholinergic activity of first-generation H1-antagonists such as triprolidine may result in thickened bronchial secretions in the respiratory tract, thereby aggravating COPD. This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components.

Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  Contraindications And Precautions
    Headline
    Drug Interactions
    General Information
    The coadministration of certain medications may lead to harm and require avoidance or therapy modification; review all drug interactions prior to concomitant use of other medications.
    Headline
    chronic obstructive pulmonary disease
    General Information
    Use pseudoephedrine; triprolidine with caution in people with chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), including emphysema or chronic bronchitis. The anticholinergic activity of first-generation H1-antagonists such as triprolidine may result in thickened bronchial secretions in the respiratory tract, thereby aggravating COPD.
    Headline
    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components. Triprolidine is a first-generation antihistamine of the alkylamine class. Allergies to one antihistamine can sometimes lead to cross-reactivity with other chemically-related antihistamines.
    Headline
    cardiac disease, hypertension
    General Information
    Sympathomimetics such as pseudoephedrine may raise heart rate and/or blood pressure in susceptible individuals. Use pseudoephedrine; triprolidine with caution in individuals with cardiac disease or hypertension. People with heart disease or high blood pressure should check with their care team before nonprescription (OTC) use.
    Headline
    prostatic hypertrophy
    General Information
    Use pseudoephedrine; triprolidine with caution in people with difficulty in urination due to prostatic hypertrophy, as sympathomimetic agents such as pseudoephedrine and anticholinergics such as triprolidine may aggravate urinary retention.
    Headline
    glaucoma
    General Information
    Pseudoephedrine; triprolidine should be used with caution in people with glaucoma, as an increase in intraocular pressure may occur from the anticholinergic actions of the medication. Older adults are more susceptible to the anticholinergic effects of triprolidine, including possible precipitation of undiagnosed glaucoma.
    Headline
    diabetes mellitus, thyroid disease
    General Information
    Use pseudoephedrine; triprolidine with caution in individuals with diabetes mellitus or thyroid disease. Sympathomimetic agents such as pseudoephedrine may aggravate these conditions.
    Headline
    renal failure, renal impairment
    General Information
    Pseudoephedrine; triprolidine should be used with caution in people with severe renal impairment (CrCl less than 30 mL/minute) and a pseudoephedrine dosage reduction has been suggested for these individuals. Due to decreased elimination and potential pseudoephedrine drug accumulation, patients with renal failure may be at increased risk for drug-related toxicity. Many experts recommend against pseudoephedrine use in severe acute or chronic kidney disease (CKD) or renal failure.
    Headline
    activities requiring coordination and concentration, driving or operating machinery
    General Information
    Triprolidine can cause drowsiness. Advise individuals receiving pseudoephedrine; triprolidine to avoid driving or operating machinery and activities requiring coordination and concentration until the effects of the drug are known. Additive drowsiness may be seen during coadministration with alcohol and other CNS depressants.
    Headline
    children, infants, neonates
    General Information
    Due to the risk for serious adverse reactions, the FDA recommends against administration of over the counter (OTC) cough and cold products to neonates, infants and children younger than 2 years of age. When administering OTC medications to older pediatric patients, they advise caregivers to read product labels carefully, use caution when administering multiple products to avoid duplication of ingredients, and use only measuring devices specifically designed for use with medications. Care teams should thoroughly assess the use of similar products, both prescription and nonprescription, to avoid duplication of therapy and the potential for inadvertent overdose.
    Headline
    geriatric
    General Information
    The Beers Criteria identifies first-generation antihistamines as potentially inappropriate medications (PIMs) for geriatric adults, recommending their avoidance due to their high anticholinergic properties, decreased clearance in older age, the development of tolerance when used as sleep aids, and an increased risk of anticholinergic effects and toxicity compared to younger individuals. These medications should particularly be avoided in patients with dementia or cognitive impairment (due to adverse CNS effects), those at high risk for delirium (which can worsen or trigger new-onset delirium), and men with lower urinary tract symptoms or benign prostatic hyperplasia (due to risks of urinary retention or hesitancy).
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Combination products containing pseudoephedrine; triprolidine should be avoided during the first trimester of pregnancy. Non-pharmacologic methods (e.g., fluids, rest) are recommended to be tried first for symptomatic relief of colds or allergies during pregnancy. Pregnant patients should see their health care professional for diagnosis and for treatment recommendations. Evidence from case-control studies in human pregnancy indicate there may be an increased risk of gastroschisis, small intestinal atresia, and hemifacial microsomia in babies exposed in utero to pseudoephedrine, particularly in the first trimester. Adequate well controlled trials of triprolidine use in human gestation are not available. Two retrospective analyses found that of 628 women who ingested triprolidine; pseudoephedrine combination products during the first trimeter, 9 infants (1.4%) had congenital disorders (types not listed). Baseline congenital disorder rates were 1.2% and 1.6% for study populations of 6,837 and 6,509 women whose pregnancies resulted in live births, respectively. Alternative antihistamines may be considered. Loratadine and cetirizine are acceptable antihistamine alternatives based on their excellent safety data and recommendation in multiple guidelines for use during pregnancy.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Use pseudoephedrine; triprolidine combination products with caution during breast-feeding. Pseudoephedrine is excreted into breast milk. Peak milk concentrations occur 1 to 1.5 hours after a maternal oral dosage, and peak milk concentrations usually exceed those of maternal plasma. The total amount of pseudoephedrine (measured by AUC) in milk is 2 to 3 times that of plasma. However, only 0.5% of a maternal dose would probably be ingested by an infant during breast-feeding within any 24 hours. Pseudoephedrine may reduce milk production. Milk production over a 24-hour period was reduced by an average of 24% compared to placebo after a single 60 mg dose of pseudoephedrine. Based on concentrations in breast milk and assuming a maternal dose of 240 mg pseudoephedrine daily, it was estimated that an infant would receive 4.3% of the maternal weight-adjusted dose. It is suggested to avoid pseudoephedrine use; if lactation is not yet well established or if there are difficulties producing sufficient milk. Sympathomimetic adverse effects (irritability, excessive crying, and altered sleeping patterns) have been reported in a breastfed infant following maternal administration of pseudoephedrine; symptoms resolved within 12 hours of drug discontinuation. Small, occasional doses of triprolidine would not be expected to cause adverse effects in breastfed infants as very low amounts of triprolidine are secreted in the breast milk. Prolonged antihistamine use may decrease the milk supply. Consider treatment alternatives to pseudoephedrine; triprolidine. Guidelines recommend loratadine or cetirizine at the lowest dose as preferred antihistamines because they are safer with only low levels found in breast milk.

    General Information
    Central nervous system (CNS) effects such as restlessness, headache, lightheadedness, dizziness, and insomnia have been infrequently reported during therapy with pseudoephedrine at usual doses. Description
  Text
  Pseudoephedrine; triprolidine is an oral combination of a nasal decongestant and antihistamine indicated for the temporary relief of symptoms, such as runny nose, itchy, watery eyes, nasal congestion, sneezing, or itching of the nose and throat, due to allergic rhinitis or other respiratory allergies or the common cold. Sympathomimetics, such as pseudoephedrine, may cause increased blood pressure. However, well-controlled hypertensive adults receiving pseudoephedrine at recommended doses (240 mg/day PO) generally do not appear at risk for significant elevations in blood pressure, although small increases in blood pressure and heart rate may occur. Triprolidine is a first-generation antihistamine with anticholinergic and sedative effects; excitability may occur, especially in children.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Pseudoephedrine is a sympathomimetic amine that exerts a decongestant action on the nasal mucosa via alpha adrenergic activity. The vasoconstrictor effects of pseudoephedrine reduce nasal blood flow. Pseudoephedrine produces peripheral effects similar to those of ephedrine and central effects similar to, but less intense than, amphetamines. While its vasoconstrictor action is similar to that of ephedrine, pseudoephedrine has less pressor effect in normotensive adults. It has the potential for excitatory side effects.   Triprolidine is a first-generation H1-antagonist that competes with histamine for binding at H1-receptor sites to lessen symptoms caused by histamine release. H1-antagonists also produce anticholinergic effects through antagonism of neuronal and neuromuscular muscarinic receptors. First-generation antihistamines do cross the blood-brain barrier to bind to receptors in the central nervous system.

  Text
  Pseudoephedrine; triprolidine is administered orally. Pseudoephedrine is rapidly and almost completely absorbed from the gastrointestinal tract. Dosage Adjustment Guidelines
  Hepatic Impairment
  Specific guidelines for dosage adjustments in hepatic impairment are not available. No specific hepatic impairment precautions are noted.
  Renal Impairment
  Use triprolidine; pseudoephedrine with caution in people with severe renal impairment (CrCl less than 30 mL/minute) and pseudoephedrine dosage reduction has been suggested for these individuals. Accumulation is expected in people with renal failure and an increased risk for pseudoephedrine toxicity. Many experts recommend against pseudoephedrine use in severe acute or chronic kidney disease (CKD) or renal failure. 26183 No data are available for triprolidine but it is minimally excreted unchanged in the urine.
 
Intermittent hemodialysis
No data are available for triprolidine. Hemodialysis is only minimally effective at removing pseudoephedrine from the circulation. Do not give supplemental doses.
