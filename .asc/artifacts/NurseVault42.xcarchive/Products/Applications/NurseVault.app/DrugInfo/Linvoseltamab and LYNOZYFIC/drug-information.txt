          Dosage Description
          Step-up dosing schedule: dose 1, 5 mg IV on day 1; dose 2, 25 mg IV on day 8; and first treatment dose, 200 mg IV on day 15. One week after day 15 treatment dose, give 200 mg IV once weekly from week 4 to week 13 for 10 treatment doses. Weekly doses should be at least 5 days apart. Starting on week 14, give 200 mg IV every 2 weeks (at least 10 days apart) until disease progression. In patients who achieve and maintain a very good partial response (VGPR) or better at or after week 24 and received at least 17 doses of 200 mg, the dosing frequency may be decreased to 200 mg every 4 weeks (at least 24 days apart) thereafter. Therapy interruption, a modification of the next dose, or discontinuation of therapy may be necessary in patients who develop toxicity. Administer premedication 30 to 60 minutes prior to infusion with acetaminophen 650 mg to 1,000 mg PO (or equivalent) and diphenhydramine 25 mg PO/IV (or equivalent). Also administer dexamethasone 40 mg IV (or equivalent) 1 to 3 hours prior to step-up dose 1, step-up dose 2, and the first full treatment dose; dexamethasone dose may be reduced to 10 mg prior to subsequent treatment doses once a treatment dose is tolerated without cytokine release syndrome (CRS) and/or an infusion-related reaction (IRR). Premedication may be discontinued once a treatment dose is tolerated without CRS and/or IRR following pre-treatment with 10 mg dexamethasone (or equivalent), acetaminophen (or equivalent), and diphenhydramine (or equivalent) as described; restart premedication in patients who restart therapy with the step-up dosing schedule following a dose delay. Following treatment with linvoseltamab, the objective response rate was 70% (complete response or better, 45%) in an open-label, multicohort clinical trial (LINKER-MM-1) in patients with relapsed or refractory multiple myeloma. At a median follow-up of 11.3 months, the estimated duration of response rate was 89% at 9 months and 72% at 12 months.

To reduce the risk of CRS, administer linvoseltamab according to a step-up dosing schedule and give pretreatment medications.     Headline
    activities requiring coordination and concentration, driving or operating machinery, neurotoxicity
    General Information
    Neurotoxicity including immune effector cell-associated neurotoxicity syndrome (ICANS) has been reported with linvoseltamab therapy; life-threatening or fatal cases may occur. Neurotoxicity/ICANS may occur concurrently with cytokine release syndrome (CRS), following CRS, or in the absence of CRS. Advise patients who experience neurotoxicity during linvoseltamab therapy to refrain from driving or operating machinery or performing activities requiring coordination and concentration for 48 hours after completion of each step-up dose and in the event of new onset of any neurological symptoms until the toxicity resolves. Interruption or modification of the next dose, or discontinuation of therapy may be necessary in patients who develop neurotoxicity. Consider a neurology (or other applicable specialty) consult for further evaluation and management (e.g., non-sedating, anti-seizure medication for seizure prophylaxis). Evaluate the need for hospitalization, supportive care, and further management (using current practice guidelines) based on severity.

    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Pregnancy should be avoided during linvoseltamab treatment and for at least 3 months after the last dose. Although there are no adequately controlled human studies in pregnancy, linvoseltamab can cause fetal harm or death when administered during pregnancy based on its mechanism of action and animal studies. Linvoseltamab causes T-cell activation and cytokine release; immune activation may compromise pregnancy maintenance. Based on findings of B-cell depletion in non-pregnant animals, linvoseltamab can cause B-cell lymphocytopenia in infants exposed to linvoseltamab in-utero. Individuals who are pregnant or who may become pregnant while receiving linvoseltamab should be apprised of the potential hazard to the fetus.

    Headline
    cytokine release syndrome
    General Information
    Linvoseltamab can cause cytokine release syndrome (CRS), which can be severe or life-threatening. In the LINKER-MMI trial, CRS occurred in 46% (grade 3, 0.9%) of patients treated with linvoseltamab; 38% of these patients experienced CRS following step-up dose 1, including 1 patient who experienced Grade 3 CRS, while 8% of these patients had an initial CRS event following a subsequent dose. Seventeen percent (17%) developed CRS after step-up dose 2, 10% after the first full 200 mg dose, and 3.6% developed CRS after the second full dose. Recurrent CRS occurred in 20% of patients. The median time to onset of CRS from the end of infusion was 11 (-1 to 184) hours after the most recent dose with a median duration of 15 (1 to 76) hours. Administer pretreatment medications and initiate therapy according to the step-up dosing schedule to reduce the incidence and severity of CRS. At the first sign of CRS, evaluate for hospitalization, manage per current practice guidelines, and administer supportive care. An interruption of therapy, dose modification, or permanent discontinuation of therapy may be necessary depending on the severity of CRS.

    Neurologic toxicity, including immune effector cell-associated neurotoxicity syndrome (ICANS) has been reported with linvoseltamab therapy. Specific neurologic events reported in the same clinical trial included headache (22%; grade 3, 0.9%) and encephalopathy, including fatal cases, in 18% (grade 3 or 4, 3.4%) of patients. The term encephalopathy included agitation, amnesia, cognitive disorder, confusional state, delirium, depressed level of consciousness, irritability, lethargy, memory impairment, mental status changes, somnolence, and excluded ICANS. In the LINKER-MM1 trial, ICANS occurred in 8% (grade 3, 2.6%) of patients who received linvoseltamab; 5% of these patients experienced ICANS following step-up dose 1, 1.8% experienced initial ICANS following step-up dose 2 and 1 patient experienced initial ICANS following a subsequent full dose of linvoseltamab. The onset of ICANS can be concurrent with CRS, follow the resolution of CRS, or occur in the absence of CRS; the most common symptoms of ICANS include confusion, depressed consciousness, and lethargy. At first sign of neurologic toxicity, including ICANS, immediately evaluate the patient, including the need for hospitalization, supportive therapy, and further management per current practice guidelines. An interruption of therapy, dose modification, or permanent discontinuation of therapy may be necessary depending on the severity of ICANS; consider a neurology (or other applicable specialty) consult for further evaluation and management (e.g. non-sedating, anti-seizure medication for seizure prophylaxis).

    infection, progressive multifocal leukoencephalopathy, sepsis
    General Information
    Linvoseltamab can cause serious, life-threatening, or fatal infection. Serious infections, including opportunistic infections, occurred in 42% (grade 3 or 4, 38%) of patients with relapsed or refractory multiple myeloma treated with linvoseltamab in the LINKER-MM1 trial; fatal infections occurred in 4% of patients in the same population. Description
  Text
  Linvoseltamab-gcpt is a bispecific T-cell engaging (BiTE) antibody that binds the CD3 receptor expressed on the surface of T-cells with the B-cell maturation antigen (BCMA) expressed on the surface of multiple myeloma cells and some healthy B-lineage cells. It is indicated for the treatment of adult patients with relapsed or refractory multiple myeloma who have received at least 4 prior lines of therapy, including a proteasome inhibitor, an immunomodulatory agent, and an anti-CD38 monoclonal antibody. Linvoseltamab has a black box warning for cytokine release syndrome and severe neurological toxicity. Linvoseltamab was approved by the FDA in July 2025.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Linvoseltamab is a bispecific T-cell engaging (BiTE) antibody that works by binding the CD3 receptor expressed on the surface of T-cells to the B-cell maturation antigen (BCMA) expressed on the surface of multiple myeloma cells. It is a humanized immunoglobulin G4 antibody (IgG4) produced from Chinese hamster ovary (CHO) cells using recombinant DNA technology. By binding the CD3 antigen and BCMA, linvoseltamab causes the release of various proinflammatory cytokines and results in the lysis of multiple myeloma cells. Linvoseltamab demonstrated anti-tumor activity in mouse models of multiple myeloma.

    Linvoseltamab Cmax (127 mg/L; CV, 51%) is achieved after the first dose of the every 2-week dosing regimen (i.e. the 12th dose of 200 mg).   Linvoseltamab is administered intravenously. It has a mean volume of distribution of 7.05 L (CV, 33.6%). Linvoseltamab is expected to be metabolized into small peptides by catabolic pathways; its clearance decreases over time because its elimination is mediated by two parallel processes, including a non-linear, saturable, target-mediated pathway and a linear, non-saturable catabolic process. Pharmacodynamics
Transient elevation of circulating cytokines (i.e. IL-2, IL-6, and IFN-y) was primarily observed during the step-up dose regimen and the first full 200 mg dose. The highest elevation was generally observed 4 hours after each infusion and returned to baseline prior to the next dose; limited cytokine release was observed following subsequent doses.
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes and drug transporters: CYP substrates
Linvoseltamab causes the release of cytokines that may suppress cytochrome P450 (CYP) enzyme activity. Concomitant use with linvoseltamab increases CYP substrate exposure which may increase the risk of adverse reactions related to CYP substrates. Increased CYP substrate exposure is more likely to occur from the initiation of linvoseltamab step-up dosing up to 14 days after the first 200 mg dose, and during or after cytokine release syndrome (CRS).

To reduce the risk of cytokine release syndrome (CRS) and/or infusion-related reaction (IRR), administer the following pre-treatment medications before each dose of the linvoseltamab step-up dosing schedule, which includes step-up dose 1, step-up dose 2, and the first treatment dose, the second treatment dose, and if indicated, subsequent treatment doses:
Acetaminophen (or equivalent) 650 mg to 1000 mg PO 30 to 60 minutes prior to infusion
Diphenhydramine (or equivalent) 25 mg IV/PO 30 to 60 minutes prior to infusion
Dexamethasone (or equivalent) IV 1 to 3 hours prior to infusion:
Dexamethasone 40 mg prior to step-up dose 1, step-up dose 2, and the first full treatment dose
Once a treatment dose of linvoseltamab is tolerated without CRS and/or IRR with 40 mg dexamethasone, administer 10 mg dexamethasone prior to the subsequent linvoseltamab treatment dose
Pre-treatment medications may be discontinued once a treatment dose of linvoseltamab is tolerated without CRS and/or IRR following premedication with 10 mg dexamethasone (or equivalent), acetaminophen (or equivalent), and diphenhydramine (or equivalent).

Administer pre-treatment medications.
Due to risk of CRS and neurologic toxicity, including ICANS, patients should be hospitalized for 24 hours after administration of Step-up dose 1, and for 24 hours after administration of Step-up dose 2.
Linvoseltamab should be administered by a provider with immediate access to emergency equipment and appropriate medical support to manage severe reactions such as CRS, infusion-related reactions (IRR), and neurologic toxicity, including ICANS.
Administer as an intravenous infusion only
Do not mix or administer with other drugs through the same IV line.
Dilution
2.5 mg dose
Add 2.5 mg of linvoseltamab to a 50 mL infusion bag made of polyvinyl chloride (PVC) or polyolefin (PO) containing 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection.
Mix by gentle inversion; do not shake.
Discard any unused drug remaining in the vial.
5 mg dose, 25 mg dose, and 200 mg dose
Add appropriate dose of linvoseltamab to a 50 mL or 100 mL infusion bag made of polyvinyl chloride (PVC) or polyolefin (PO) containing 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection.
Mix by gentle inversion; do not shake.
Discard any unused drug remaining in the vial.
Storage of diluted solution: Use immediately (preferred) or store at room temperature up to 25 degrees C (77 degrees F) for no more than 8 hours from preparation to the start of the infusion or under refrigeration at 2 degrees C (36 to 46 degrees F) for no more than 48 hours from preparation to the start of the infusion. Do not freeze.
Intravenous infusion
Administer linvoseltamab through a dedicated infusion line made of PVC, polyethylene (PE)-line PVC, or polyurethane (PU) containing a 0.2 to 5 micron polyethersulfone (PES) filter.
Prime the intravenous tubing with linvoseltamab.
Administer linvoseltamab according to the dosing schedule below:
Step-up Dosing Schedule
Step-up dose 1 (Day 1): Administer 5 mg IV over 4 hours
Step-up dose 2 (Day 8): Administer 25 mg IV over 4 hours
First treatment dose (Day 15): Administer 200 mg IV over 4 hours
Weekly Dosing Schedule
Second treatment dose (One week after Day 15 treatment): Administer 200 mg IV over 1 hour or maintain the infusion time of 4 hours for patients who experienced CRS with a previous dose
Subsequent treatment doses (Week 4 to Week 13 for 10 treatment doses): Administer 200 mg IV over 30 minutes or maintain the infusion time of 1 hour for patients who experienced CRS with a previous dose
Biweekly (Every 2 Weeks) Dosing Schedule
Subsequent treatment doses (Week 14 and every 2 weeks thereafter): Administer 200 mg IV over 30 minutes or maintain the infusion duration of the previous infusion for patients who experience CRS. Reduce the duration of infusion sequentially in subsequent doses in patients who do not experience CRS (e.g., 4 hours, 1 hour, then 30 minutes).
Every 4 Weeks Dosing Schedule (Week 24 or after and every 4 weeks thereafter)
NOTE: For patients achieving and maintaining a very good partial response (VGPR) or better at or after Week 24 and who have received at least 17 doses of linvoseltamab 200 mg.
Administer 200 mg IV over 30 minutes or maintain the infusion duration of the previous infusion for patients who experience CRS. Reduce the duration of infusion sequentially in subsequent doses in patients who do not experience CRS (e.g., 4 hours, 1 hour, then 30 minutes).
Following linvoseltamab administration, flush the IV line with an adequate volume of 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection.
