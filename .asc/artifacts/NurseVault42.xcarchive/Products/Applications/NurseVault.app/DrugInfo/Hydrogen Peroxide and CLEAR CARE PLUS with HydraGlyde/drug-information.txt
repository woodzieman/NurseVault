  Adverse Reactions
    Headline
    edema, erythema, pruritus, skin erosion, skin hyperpigmentation, skin hypopigmentation, skin ulcer
    General Information
    Dermatologic adverse reactions were observed in patients receiving treatment with hydrogen peroxide 40% topical solution during clinical trials. Application site reaction that occurred within 10 minutes of treatment included erythema (98%), stinging (93%), edema (85%), pruritus (32%), and vesiculation (18%). Other common local skin reactions that developed 1 week after treatment included scaling (72%), erythema (66%), crusting (67%), pruritus (18%), skin erosion (9%), and skin ulcer (4%). Delayed reactions (i.e., 15 weeks after the initial treatment) were also observed and included erythema (21%), skin hyperpigmentation (18%), scaling (16%), crusting (12%), and skin hypopigmentation (7%). A few patients also developed eyelid edema (0.6%) and herpes zoster infection (0.6%). Cases of crepitus have been reported during postmarketing use. Due to the voluntary nature of postmarketing reports, neither a frequency nor a definitive causal relationship can be established. Instruct recipients of any hydrogen peroxide product (i.e., 3% and 40% solution or 1.8% gel) to discontinue use of the drug and seek medical attention if any of the above adverse reactions develop during treatment.

Description
  Text
  Hydrogen peroxide is used as an antiseptic and debriding agent for topical first aid and wound management. It is also used as an oral rinse for minor mouth irritations, wounds, gum irritation, or canker sores or for following dental procedures. Hydrogen peroxide is also available in contact lens cleansing solution used to clean and disinfect contact lenses. Hydrogen peroxide solutions have been in clinical use for decades. A 40% hydrogen peroxide solution was previously approved for the treatment of seborrheic keratoses, but the product was discontinued by the manufacturer in 2019.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Hydrogen peroxide (H2O2) exerts antiseptic and debridement effects via chemical oxidation. The exact mechanism by which hydrogen peroxide treats seborrheic keratoses is unknown.

Pharmacokinetics
  Text
  Hydrogen peroxide solution is administered topically or is used as an oral rinse. Pharmacokinetic data are not readily available, but systemic absorption from topical or oral rinse use is likely low.  Hydrogen peroxide rapidly dissociates into water and reactive oxygen species. An indirect assessment of reactive oxygen species did not demonstrate any systemic absorption of hydrogen peroxide after use of a 40% topical solution.
 
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes and drug transporters: None

Directions for use
Place each contact lens into the appropriately marked L/R dome for the lens holder. Rinse each lens while in the lens holder for 5 seconds with hydrogen peroxide solution.
Fill the lens case up to the marked line and place the lens holder into the case.
Allow lenses to soak for at least 6 hours. Keep the lens case upright and do not shake during the neutralization period. Once the 6-hour neutralization period is over, you can wear your lenses again. Do not rinse lenses with solution prior to inserting in the eye.
Always discard the remaining solution from the lens case after each use.
