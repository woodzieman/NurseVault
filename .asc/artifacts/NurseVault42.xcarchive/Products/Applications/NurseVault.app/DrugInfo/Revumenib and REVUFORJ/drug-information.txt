Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  Contraindications And Precautions
    Headline
    Drug Interactions
    General Information
    The coadministration of certain medications may lead to harm and require avoidance or therapy modification; review all drug interactions prior to concomitant use of other medications.
    Headline
    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Pregnancy should be avoided by individuals of reproductive potential during revumenib treatment. Although there are no adequately controlled studies in pregnant people, revumenib can cause fetal harm or death when administered during pregnancy based on its mechanism of action and animal studies. Individuals who are pregnant or who become pregnant while receiving revumenib should be apprised of the potential hazard to the fetus.
    Headline
    differentiation syndrome
    General Information
    Differentiation syndrome has been reported with revumenib therapy; some cases were life-threatening or fatal. Reduce the white cell count to less than 25 Gi/L prior to starting revumenib. Monitor patients for signs and symptoms such as fever, dyspnea, hypoxia, peripheral edema, pleuropericardial effusion, acute renal failure, and/or hypotension. If differentiation syndrome is suspected, hold revumenib therapy, start corticosteroid therapy and closely monitor hemodynamic parameters until symptom resolution. Discontinuation of therapy may be necessary.
    Headline
    contraception requirements, infertility, pregnancy testing, reproductive risk
    General Information
    Counsel people about the reproductive risk and contraception requirements during revumenib treatment. Revumenib can be teratogenic if taken by the mother during pregnancy. Females and males with female partners of reproductive potential should avoid pregnancy and use effective contraception during and for at least 4 months after treatment with revumenib. Individuals of reproductive potential should undergo pregnancy testing prior to initiation of revumenib. Individuals who become pregnant while receiving revumenib should be apprised of the potential hazard to the fetus. Although there are no data regarding the effect of revumenib on human fertility, male and female infertility has been observed in animal studies.
    Headline
    bradycardia, cardiomyopathy, congenital long QT syndrome, coronary artery disease, females, geriatric, hypocalcemia, hypokalemia, hypomagnesemia, QT prolongation, torsade de pointes
    General Information
    Avoid use of revumenib in people with baseline QT prolongation or who have conditions that may increase the risk of QT prolongation or torsade de pointes, including bradycardia, congenital long QT syndrome, hypocalcemia, hypokalemia, hypomagnesemia, geriatric adults, females, structural abnormalities that interfere with electrical conduction (e.g., cardiomyopathy, coronary artery disease, ischemic heart disease), or in those who have other additional risk factors for QT prolongation or torsade de pointes. The use of other medications that have been associated with QT prolongation or torsade de pointes may further increase risk. Do not initiate treatment in patients with heart-rate corrected QT interval using Fridericia's method (QTcF) greater than 450 milliseconds. Perform an ECG at baseline, at least once a week for the first 4 weeks on treatment, and at least monthly thereafter. Correct electrolyte abnormalities prior to starting revumenib and monitor monthly thereafter.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in nursing infants from revumenib, advise individuals to discontinue breast-feeding during treatment and for 1 week after the final dose. It is not known whether revumenib is present in human milk, although many drugs are excreted in human milk.

Interactions
Adagrasib: (Major) Avoid concomitant use of revumenib with adagrasib due to the risk for increased revumenib exposure and additive risk for QT/QTc prolongation and torsade de pointes (TdP). If concomitant use is necessary, a revumenib dosage decrease is required; dosing recommendations vary by weight. For patients weighing 40 kg or more, reduce the starting dose to 160 mg PO twice daily or 110 mg PO twice daily for those requiring a dosage reduction for adverse reactions. For patients weighing less than 40 kg, reduce the starting dose to 95 mg/m2 PO twice daily or 65 mg/m2 PO twice daily for those requiring a dosage reduction for adverse reactions. See prescribing information for additional details regarding dosage form rounding with body surface area (BSA) based dosing. Additionally, consider taking steps to minimize the risk for QT/QTc interval prolongation and TdP, such as electrolyte monitoring and repletion and ECG monitoring. Revumenib is a CYP3A substrate, adagrasib is a strong CYP3A inhibitor, and both medications have been associated with QT/QTc prolongation. Concomitant use with other strong CYP3A inhibitors increased revumenib overall exposure by 2- to 2.5-fold.

    Headline
    differentiation syndrome
    General Information
    Revumenib can cause fatal or life-threatening differentiation syndrome (DS). Reduce the white blood cell count to less than 25 Gi/L prior to starting revumenib. Symptoms of DS include fever, dyspnea, hypoxia, peripheral edema, pleuropericardial effusion, acute renal failure, rash, and/or hypotension. Across clinical trials, DS occurred in 25% (grade 3 or 4, 12%) of patients treated with revumenib in clinical trials; two fatal cases of DS occurred in patients in the same population. The median time to onset of DS was 9 days (3 to 41 days); some patients experienced more than 1 DS event. Among those with KMT2A translocation, DS occurred in 33% of patients with acute myeloid leukemia (AML), 33% of patients with mixed-phenotype acute leukemia (MPAL), and 9% of patients with acute lymphoblastic leukemia (ALL); DS occurred in 18% of patients with NPM1 mutated AML. If DS is suspected, immediately initiate treatment with corticosteroids (e.g., dexamethasone 10 mg IV every 12 hours in adults or dexamethasone 0.25 mg/kg/dose IV every 12 hours in pediatric patients weighing less than 40 kg) for a minimum of 3 days and until resolution; institute supportive measures and hemodynamic monitoring until improvement. An interruption or discontinuation of revumenib therapy may be necessary.

    Headline
    QT prolongation, torsade de pointes
    General Information
    QT prolongation and torsade de pointes (TdP) have occurred in patients treated with revueminb. QTc interval prolongation occurred in 36% (grade 3, 15%; grade 4, 2%) of patients (86 out of 241) with relapsed or refractory acute leukemia treated with revumenib in clinical trials. The heart-rate corrected QT interval using Fridericia's method (QTcF) was greater than 500 msec in 10% of patients, and the increase from baseline QTcF was greater than 60 msec in 24% of patients. One case of nonsustained TdP and one fatal case of cardiac arrest were reported in patients in the same population. Perform an ECG prior to initiation of revumenib, at least once a week for the first 4 weeks on treatment, and at least monthly thereafter; more frequent ECG monitoring may be necessary in patients with congenital long QTc syndrome, congestive heart failure, electrolyte abnormalities, or those taking medications known to prolong the QTc interval. Correct electrolyte abnormalities, including hypokalemia and hypomagnesemia, prior to and during treatment. Do not initiate revumenib in patients with a QTcF of more than 450 msec. If QTc interval prolongation occurs, an interruption of therapy, dose reduction, or permanent discontinuation of therapy may be necessary based on severity; permanently discontinue revumenib for ventricular arrhythmias and QTc interval prolongation with signs or symptoms of life-threatening arrhythmia.

Description
  Text
  Revumenib is a menin inhibitor indicated for the treatment of relapsed or refractory acute leukemia with a lysine methyltransferase 2A gene (KMT2A) translocation in adults and pediatric patients 1 year and older and for the treatment of relapsed or refractory acute myeloid leukemia (AML) with a susceptible nucleophosmin 1 (NPM1) mutation in adults and pediatric patients 1 year and older who have no satisfactory alternative treatment options. Acute leukemia with KMT2A rearrangements (KMT2A-r) was previously known as mixed lineage leukemia. Revumenib has a black box warning for differentiation syndrome, QTc prolongation, and torsade de pointes (TdP). Revumenib was approved by the FDA in November 2024.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Revumenib is a menin inhibitor and blocks the interaction of both wild-type lysine methyltransferase 2A (KMT2A) and KMT2A fusion proteins with menin. The binding of wild-type KMT2A or KMT2A fusion proteins with menin is involved in NPM1 mutated acute myeloid leukemias (AML) and KMT2A-rearranged (KMT2Ar) acute leukemias through activation of a leukemogenic transcriptional pathway. Susceptible NPM1 mutations are defined as those resulting in the loss of the nucleolar localization signal and the insertion of a new nuclear export signal leading to the accumulation of mutant NPM1 in the cytoplasm of AML cells; the most common of these NPM1 mutations in patients with AML are Types A, B, and D. In pre-clinical studies, menin was found to be crucial for KMT2A activity and the maintenance of homebox (HOXA) expression, in addition to being necessary for ongoing leukemogenesis in KMT2Ar models.

  Text
  Revumenib is administered orally. It is 90% protein bound; protein binding is not concentration dependent. The blood to plasma ratio of revumenib is 0.8. Revumenib has an apparent volume of distribution of 63 liters (CV, 84%). When administered with strong CYP3A4 inhibitors, the elimination half-life is 6.4 hours (CV, 52%) with an apparent clearance of 7 liters/hour (CV, 64%). When administered without strong CYP3A4 inhibitors, the elimination half-life is 3 hours (CV, 49%) with an apparent clearance of 20 liters/hour (CV, 112%). Revumenib is primarily metabolized by CYP3A4; its active metabolite, M1, contributes to the effects of revumenib on QTc but does not contribute to its efficacy at the approved recommended dosage.After oral administration of a single radiolabeled dose, approximately 52% was recovered in feces (unchanged, 7%) and approximately 25% in urine (unchanged, 6%).
Pharmacodynamics: A concentration-dependent increase in the QTc interval with a predicted increase of 23 milliseconds (msec) was observed in patients at the highest approved recommended dose without strong CYP3A4 inhibitors. The increase in QTc interval was predicted to be 20 msec after administration of 160 mg twice daily with strong CP3A4 inhibitors.
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes and transporters: CYP3A4, OCT1, OCT2, OAT1, OAT3, MATE1
Revumenib is a CYP3A4 substrate. Revumenib AUC and Cmax were increased by 2-fold to 2.5-fold following concomitant use with strong CYP3A4 inhibitors. Revumenib exposure is expected to decrease and its active metabolite (M1) exposure is expected to increase with strong and moderate CYP3A4 inducers which may increase the risk of QT prolongation associated with the M1 metabolite. In vitro, revumenib is a substrate of OCT1, OCT2, OAT1, OAT3, and MATE1 and an inhibitor of CYP3A4 and MATE1. In vitro, M1 is a substrate of OATP1B1 and an inhibitor of MATE1.

Administration

    Administration Details
      Name
      Oral Solid Formulations
      Description

      Name
      Extemporaneous Compounding-Oral
      Description
      For oral administration in patients unable to swallow whole tablets:
Tablets may be crushed and dispersed in water and taken within 2 hours of preparation.
Verify the prescribed dose and confirm the strength(s) and number of tablets needed for dose administration.
Crush tablet(s) in a clean and dry pill crusher until all tablet pieces are broken up; the crushed tablet(s) should be like the consistency of flour.
Measure 10 mL (2 teaspoons) of room temperature water and add to a small cup.
Add the crushed revumenib tablets to the small cup that contains 10 mL of water.
Carefully swirl the cup every 30 seconds to 1 minute for a total of 5 minutes; the mixture will look cloudy.
Immediately draw up the mixture into a 20 mL oral syringe.
Place the tip of the oral syringe into the patient's mouth against the inside of the cheek and gently squirt the mixture into the mouth; the patient should remain sitting up straight or standing for 2 to 3 minutes.
Rinse the small cup with 10 mL (2 teaspoons) of room temperature water to make sure the full dose is received. Swirl the water around the sides of the cup to capture any remaining crushed revumenib tablets.
Draw up the rinsed mixture into the oral syringe and gently squirt the mixture into the patient's mouth; repeat until no more mixture is left in the cup.
If the mixture is vomited or not fully swallowed, do not give another dose; wait until the next scheduled dose.
