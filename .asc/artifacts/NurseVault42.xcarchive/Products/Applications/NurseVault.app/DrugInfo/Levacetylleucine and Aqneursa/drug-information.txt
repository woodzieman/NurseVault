    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Based on animal data, levacetylleucine may cause embryo-fetal harm when administered during pregnancy. There are no data on the use of levacetylleucine during human pregnancy to evaluate for a drug-associated risk of major birth defects, miscarriage, or other adverse outcomes. Administration of levacetylleucine to rats and rabbits during organogenesis resulted in an increase in embryo-fetal death (post-implantation loss/resorption), a decrease in fetal body weight, and an increase in external and skeletal malformations at doses approximately 1.4 times and 6 times the maximum recommended human dose based on body surface area, respectively. When making a decision regarding use in pregnancy, consider the patient's clinical need for treatment and potential fetal adverse effects from the medication or from the patient's underlying medical condition.

Abacavir; Dolutegravir; lamiVUDine: (Moderate) Monitor for increased toxicity of dolutegravir if coadministered with levacetylleucine. Concurrent use may increase the plasma concentrations of dolutegravir. Dolutegravir is a P-gp substrate and levacetylleucine is a P-gp inhibitor.
Afatinib: (Moderate) If the concomitant use of levacetylleucine and afatinib is necessary, monitor for afatinib-related adverse reactions. If the original dose of afatinib is not tolerated, consider reducing the daily dose of afatinib by 10 mg; resume the previous dose of afatinib as tolerated after discontinuation of levacetylleucine. The manufacturer of afatinib recommends permanent discontinuation of therapy for severe or intolerant adverse drug reactions at a dose of 20 mg per day, but does not address a minimum dose otherwise. Afatinib is a P-gp substrate and levacetylleucine is a P-gp inhibitor. Administration with another P-gp inhibitor, given 1 hour before a single dose of afatinib, increased afatinib exposure by 48%; there was no change in afatinib exposure when the P-gp inhibitor was administered at the same time as afatinib or 6 hours later. In healthy subjects, the relative bioavailability for AUC of afatinib was 119% when coadministered with the same P-gp inhibitor, and 111% when the inhibitor was administered 6 hours after afatinib.

Oral administration within 4 hours of another P-gp inhibitor increased the dose-normalized AUC of topotecan lactone and total topotecan 2-fold to 3-fold compared to oral topotecan alone.
Ubrogepant: (Major) Limit the initial and second dose of ubrogepant to 50 mg if coadministered with levacetylleucine. Concurrent use may increase ubrogepant exposure and the risk of adverse effects. Ubrogepant is a P-gp substrate and levacetylleucine is a P-gp inhibitor.
Venetoclax: (Major) Reduce the dose of venetoclax by at least 50% and monitor for venetoclax toxicity (e.g., hematologic toxicity, GI toxicity, and tumor lysis syndrome) if coadministered with levacetylleucine due to the potential for increased venetoclax exposure. Resume the original venetoclax dose 2 to 3 days after discontinuation of levacetylleucine. Venetoclax is a P-gp substrate; levacetylleucine is a P-gp inhibitor. Coadministration with a single dose of another P-gp inhibitor increased venetoclax exposure by 78% in a drug interaction study.

Description
  Text
  Levacetylleucine is a modified amino acid (N-acetyl-L-leucine) that is indicated for the treatment of neurological manifestations of Niemann-Pick disease type C (NPC) in adult and pediatric patients weighing 15 kg or more. NPC is a rare inherited autosomal recessive genetic disorder where the body is unable to transport cholesterol and other lipids within a cell. This leads to the accumulation of cholesterol and other lipids inside various tissues of the body, including brain tissue. In a clinical trial, patients receiving levacetylleucine showed greater improvement in the fSARA (modified version of the Scale for Assessment and Rating of Ataxia (SARA)) score compared to placebo. The mean change from baseline was -0.5 (+/- 1.2) during levacetylleucine treatment and 0 (+/- 1.5) during placebo when patients received levacetylleucine first for 12 weeks followed by placebo for 12 weeks. In Treatment Sequence 2 (patients received placebo first for 12 weeks, then levacetylleucine for 12 weeks), the mean change in fSARA was -0.7 (+/- 0.9) during levacetylleucine treatment and -0.3 (+/- 0.9) during placebo therapy. Patients who are pregnant or plan to become pregnant should not use levacetylleucine. Levacetylleucine was FDA-approved in September 2024.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  The molecular target for levacetylleucine in the treatment of Niemann-Pick disease type C is unknown. Levacetylleucine is a modified amino acid (N-acetyl-L-leucine) that targets patterns of neurological dysfunction and degeneration. Levacetylleucine uses monocarboxylate transporters to cross the blood-brain barrier and reach the central nervous system. Once levacetylleucine enters enzyme-controlled pathways, it corrects metabolic dysfunction by normalizing mitochondrial dysfunction by improving ATP production and reducing oxidative stress. It has been shown to normalize lysosomal function, resulting in reduction of substrate accumulation. It also enhances cerebral glucose metabolism in the cerebellum, leading to improved cerebellar activity.

      Name

      Description
      One packet contains 1 gram of levacetylleucine.
Take with or without food.
Missed dose: Skip the missed dose and take the next dose at the scheduled time. Do not take 2 doses at the same time to make up for a missed dose.
Oral administration:
Empty the entire contents of one packet into 40 mL of water, orange juice, or almond milk. Do not use hot liquid.
Stir the mixture to form a suspension.
Swallow the suspension within 30 minutes.
Repeat the steps if 2 packets are required.
Discard any unused suspension if not used within 30 minutes.
Feeding tube administration (G-tube):
Prepare levacetylleucine suspension immediately prior to administration.
Open the packet and empty into 40 mL of water ONLY. Do not use hot liquid.
Stir the mixture to form a suspension.
Draw the suspension into a catheter tip syringe.
Administer the suspension immediately through the G-tube.
Flush any residual suspension in the catheter tip syringe with an additional 20 mL of water.
Flush the G-tube again, as needed, until no residual suspension is left in the syringe or feeding tube.
For doses that require 2 packets, repeat steps for the second packet.
Discard an unused suspension if not administered immediately.
