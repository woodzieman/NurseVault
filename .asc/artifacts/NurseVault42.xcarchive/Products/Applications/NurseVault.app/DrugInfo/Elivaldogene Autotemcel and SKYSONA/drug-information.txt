Indication Specific Dosing
  General Information
  General Dosing Information
Elivaldogene autotemcel does not prevent the development of or treat adrenal insufficiency due to adrenoleukodystrophy.
An immune response to elivaldogene autotemcel may cause rapid loss of efficacy in patients with full deletions of the human ABCD1 gene.
It has not been studied in cerebral adrenoleukodystrophy (CALD) secondary to head trauma.
Due to the risk of hematologic malignancy with elivaldogene autotemcel, and unclear long-term durability of elivaldogene autotemcel and human adrenoleukodystrophy protein (ALDP) expression, careful consideration should be given to the timing and appropriateness of treatment for each patient, especially those with isolated pyramidal tract disease as clinical manifestations do not usually occur until adulthood.

    Headline
    infection
    General Information
    Do not administer elivaldogene autotemcel in patients with active infection. Serious infections including bacterial infection, viral infection, and opportunistic infections have been reported with elivaldogene autotemcel therapy; some cases were life-threatening or fatal. Monitor patients for signs and symptoms of infection prior to and after the elivaldogene autotemcel infusion and treat appropriately; administer prophylactic antimicrobial therapy as medically indicated.
    Headline
    General Information
    General Information
    During elivaldogene autotemcel clinical trials, 1 patient had a full ABCD1 deletion. Radiologic disease progression in the setting of declining peripheral blood vector copy number occurred, suggesting loss of product efficacy, which may have been immune mediated. The patient was subsequently treated with allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplant.
    Headline
    hematologic malignancy
    General Information
    Hematologic malignancy, including myelodysplastic syndrome (MDS) and acute myeloid leukemia, has been reported after elivaldogene autotemcel therapy. Malignancies developed in clinical trials between 14 months and 10 years after elivaldogene autotemcel therapy. Due to the risk of hematologic malignancy, reserve the use of elivaldogene autotemcel for individuals in whom alternative treatment options are not available. Consult hematology experts prior to treatment to inform benefit-risk treatment decision and ensure proper monitoring for hematologic malignancy. Consider preforming the following baseline hematologic assessments: complete blood count with differential, hematopathology review of peripheral blood smear, and bone marrow biopsy (core and aspirate) with flow cytometry, conventional karyotyping, and next generation sequencing (NGS) with a molecular panel appropriate for age and including coverage for gene mutations expected in myeloid and lymphoid malignancies; and testing for germline mutations that are associated with hematologic malignancy. Monitor patients treated with elivaldogene autotemcel lifelong for hematologic malignancy. For the first 15 years after treatment, monitor complete blood count (with differential) at least every 3 months and integration site analysis or other testing for evidence of clonal expansion and predominance at least twice in the first year and then annually. In certain patients, consider expert consultation and additional testing such as more frequent complete blood count (with differential) and integration site analysis, bone marrow studies, and gene expression studies. These patients include those with delayed or failed engraftment of platelets or other cell lines (patients who do not achieve unsupported platelet counts of 20 x 10^9 L or more on or after Day 60 appear to be at particularly high risk for developing malignancy), new or prolonged cytopenias, or presence of clonal expansion or predominance (e.g., increasing relative frequency of an integration site, especially if 10% or more and present in MECOM or another proto-oncogene known to be involved in hematologic malignancy). If a hematologic malignancy is detected, contact bluebird bio at 1-833-999-6378 for reporting and to obtain instructions on collection of samples for further testing. Elivaldogene autotemcel Lenti-D lentiviral vector integration into proto-oncogenes appears to have mediated the reported cases of hematologic malignancy. All patients treated with elivaldogene autotemcel in clinical studies have integrations into MECOM; it is unknown which integrations into MECOM or other genes are likely to lead to malignancy. Patients who intend to receive elivaldogene autotemcel therapy are encouraged to enroll in a follow-up study to assess the long term safety and the risk of malignancies occurring after treatment. Call bluebird bio, Inc. at 1-833-999-6378 to enroll in the study.

    Headline
    human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) infection
    General Information
    Elivaldogene autotemcel has not been studied in patients with human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) infection (HIV-1, HIV-2) or human T-lymphotrophic virus (HTLV-1, HTLV-2). Perform screening for HIV-1, HIV-2, HTLV-1, HTLV-2, hepatitis B virus, and hepatitis C virus prior to collection of cells for manufacturing. Patients must have a negative serology test for HIV to ensure acceptance of apheresis material for elivaldogene autotemcel manufacturing. Apheresis material from patients with a positive HIV test will not be accepted for elivaldogene autotemcel manufacturing.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    There are no available data evaluating the use of elivaldogene autotemcel during pregnancy. Animal reproductive and developmental toxicity studies have not been conducted. No nonclinical germline transmission studies have been conducted. Consider the risks associated with mobilization and conditioning agents during pregnancy.
    Headline
    people who can cause pregnancy in others, reproductive risk
    General Information
    Counsel people who can cause pregnancy in others about the reproductive risk associated with elivaldogene autotemcel treatment. People who can cause pregnancy and their partners of childbearing potential should use an effective method of contraception (intrauterine device or a combination of hormonal and barrier contraception) from the start of mobilization through at least 6 months after administration of elivaldogene autotemcel. There are insufficient data to recommend a duration of contraception after treatment with elivaldogene autotemcel. There are no data on the effect of elivaldogene autotemcel on fertility; however, myeloablative conditioning may increase the risk of infertility in people of reproductive potential. Inform patients of the option to cryopreserve semen before treatment, if appropriate.

Adverse Reactions
  Headline
  Adverse Reactions
  Adverse Reactions
    Headline
    abdominal pain, colitis, constipation, diarrhea, nausea, proctitis, stomatitis, vomiting
    General Information
    Gastrointestinal (GI) toxicity, including mucositis (92%; grade 3 or higher, 51%), nausea (84%; grade 3 or higher, 25%), vomiting (76%; grade 3 or higher, 18%), decreased appetite (64%; grade 3 or higher, 40%), abdominal pain (45%; grade 3 or higher, 3%), constipation (42%), and diarrhea (28%; grade 3 or higher, 1%), was reported between the start of conditioning and 24 months after treatment with elivaldogene autotemcel. The term mucositis included anal inflammation, colitis, gastrointestinal inflammation, mucosal inflammation, proctitis, and stomatitis; the term abdominal pain included abdominal discomfort and abdominal upper pain. Nausea (79%), vomiting (72%), decreased appetite (42%), constipation (30%) and abdominal pain (21%) were reported during mobilization and conditioning; after elivaldogene autotemcel treatment, the highest incidence of GI adverse reactions was reported in the first 60 days. If nausea and vomiting occur on the day of infusion, premedication with antiemetics, antipyretics, and/or antihistamines may be considered.

Description
  Text
  Elivaldogene autotemcel is an autologous hematopoietic stem cell-based gene therapy indicated to slow the progression of neurologic dysfunction in individuals with early, active cerebral adrenoleukodystrophy (CALD) without an available human leukocyte antigen (HLA)-matched donor for allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplant. CALD is a rare neurologic disease caused by mutations in the ABCD1 gene that lead to a buildup of very long-chain fatty acids (VLCFA) causing inflammation and damage in the brain. The condition only affects males; 35% of affected individuals develop neurological symptoms between the ages of 3 and 10. Affected individuals develop normally, but start to show signs of regression. They may have behavioral problems including attention deficit and hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) and learning disabilities. Individuals may later develop additional symptoms including diminished visual acuity, hearing loss, gait difficulty, weakness and stiffness of limbs, and convulsions or seizures. Eventually, affected individuals will lose most neurological function; death typically occurs within 2 to 3 years from onset of symptoms. Elivaldogene autotemcel works by inserting functional copies of the ABCD1 gene into a individual's hematopoietic stem cells. In clinical trials, major functional disability-free survival at 24 months was 72% (95% CI: 35%, 90%) in symptomatic CALD patients treated with elivaldogene autotemcel compared to 43% (95% CI: 10%, 73%) in an untreated natural history control group. New hematologic malignancies, including myelodysplastic syndrome and acute myeloid leukemia, have been reported in individuals who received elivaldogene autotemcel treatment. Due to the risk of hematologic malignancy, reserve the use of elivaldogene autotemcel for individuals in whom alternative treatment options are not available. Monitor individuals closely for evidence of malignancy through complete blood counts every 3 months. Elivaldogene autotemcel was FDA-approved in 2022.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Elivaldogene autotemcel is an autologous hematopoietic stem cell (HSC)-based gene therapy that works by adding functional copies of the adenosine triphosphate binding cassette, sub family D, member 1 (ABCD1) cDNA into patients' HSCs through transduction of autologous CD34 positive cells with Lenti-D lentiviral vector (LVV). Lenti-D LVV is a replication-incompetent, self-inactivating LVV carrying ABCD1 cDNA that encodes normal adrenoleukodystrophy protein (ALDP). The ABCD1 gene is controlled by an internal MNDU3 promotor, which is a modified viral promoter and has been shown to control expression of the transgene in HSCs and their progeny in all lineages. After infusion, transduced CD34 positive HSCs engraft in the bone marrow and differentiate into various cell types, including monocytes (CD14 positive) capable of producing functional ALDP. Functional ALDP can then participate in the local degradation of very long chain fatty acids (VLCFAs), which is believed to slow or possibly prevent further inflammation and demyelination.
 
To produce this autologous HSC-based gene therapy, HSCs are collected from the blood via apheresis procedure(s); enriched for CD34 positive cells; transduced ex vivo with Lenti-D LVV; cultured with growth factors; and then washed, formulated into a suspension, and cryopreserved.

Pharmacokinetics
  Text
  Elivaldogene autotemcel is administered intravenously.
 
All patients who received elivaldogene autotemcel with at least 1 month of follow-up produced ALDP in CD14 positive cells, demonstrating early expression of the transgene. The %ALDP positive cell counts stabilized at 6 months after elivaldogene autotemcel infusion. In peripheral blood, the ALDP in CD14 positive cells remained generally stable through month 24. ALDP expression was quantifiable in 43% of the patients who had follow-up through month 60 (n = 7), indicating long-term expression of transgenic ALDP in the progeny of hematopoietic stem cells.

Preparation
Remove each metal cassette from liquid nitrogen storage.
Remove the product bag from the cassette; verify that the patient information on the cassette label matches the bag label.
Confirm that patient identity matches the unique patient identification information located on the elivaldogene autotemcel infusion bag(s). Do NOT infuse if the information on the patient-specific label on the infusion bag does not match the intended patient, and contact bluebird bio at 1-833-999-6378.
Use the enclosed lot information sheet to confirm that each infusion bag is within the expiration date prior to preparation of elivaldogene autotemcel for infusion.
If more than 1 infusion bag is part of the treatment dose, thaw each infusion bag 1 at a time; do not start to thaw the next bag until the previous bag has been infused. Maintain the second infusion bag within the cold-storage dewar to maintain temperatures less than or equal to -140 degrees C (-220 degrees F) until ready to thaw.
Thaw the infusion bag(s) at approximately 37 degrees C (98.6 degrees F) using an approved thaw device or water bath. Thawing of each infusion bag takes approximately 2 to 4 minutes. Promptly remove the infusion bag from the bath once thawed. Do not leave unattended and do not submerge the infusion ports if thawed in a water bath.
After thawing, gently massage the contents of the bag to allow the clumps of cellular material to disperse until all the contents are uniform.
If visible cell clumps remain, continue to gently mix the contents of the bag; most small clumps of cellular material should disperse with gentle manual mixing.
Do not filter, wash, spin down, and/or resuspend elivaldogene autotemcel in new media prior to infusion.
Do not sample, alter, irradiate, or refreeze elivaldogene autotemcel.
Storage: Administer as soon as possible after thawing. Complete the IV infusion no more than 4 hours after thawing.
 
Intravenous (IV) Infusion
Do NOT use an in-line blood filter or an infusion pump.
Prime the tubing with 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection prior to the infusion.
Administer as an IV infusion via gravity flow over a period of less than 60 minutes.
Once the infusion is complete, flush all elivaldogene autotemcel remaining in the infusion bag and the tubing with at least 50 mL of 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection to ensure as many cells as possible are delivered.
Repeat steps for each infusion bag.
