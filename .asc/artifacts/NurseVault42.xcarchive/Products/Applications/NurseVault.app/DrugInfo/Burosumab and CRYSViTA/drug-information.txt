In a single-arm, phase 2 trial, the mean serum phosphorus concentrations increased to above the LLN after 24 weeks of burosumab therapy in 50% of adult patients (n = 14; median age, 59.5 years; range, 33 to 68 years) with FGF23-related hypophosphatemia due to an underlying tumor (including TIO) that was not amenable to surgical excision or could not be located. Patients had increased fasting mean serum phosphorus concentrations from 1.62 +/- 0.49 mg/dL at baseline to 2.63 +/- 0.87 mg/dL at week 24. The increased mean serum phosphorus concentrations were sustained above the LLN through week 88 of treatment. The target range was 2.5 to 4 mg/dL for fasting serum phosphorus concentrations in these trials.

    Headline
    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components.
    Headline
    hyperphosphatemia
    General Information
    Burosumab is contraindicated in patients with hyperphosphatemia or with serum phosphorus concentrations within the normal range for age. Measure fasting serum phosphorus concentrations at baseline, monthly for the first 3 months of treatment, and then periodically thereafter as appropriate to maintain phosphorus concentrations within the normal range based on age. A temporary interruption of therapy and/or dose reduction may be required based on serum phosphorus concentrations. The risk of nephrocalcinosis is increased when serum phosphorus increases to above the upper limit of normal.
    Headline
    renal failure, renal impairment
    General Information
    Burosumab is contraindicated in patients with severe renal impairment or renal failure since these conditions are associated with abnormal mineral metabolism.
    Headline
    dehydration, hypercalcemia, hyperparathyroidism, hypervitaminosis D, immobility
    General Information
    Increases in serum calcium have been reported in patients treated with burosumab. In patients at high risk for hypercalcemia, such as those with pre-existing hyperparathyroidism, prolonged immobility, dehydration, hypervitaminosis D, or renal impairment, assess serum calcium and parathyroid hormone concentrations before and during treatment. Do not administer burosumab in patients with moderate to severe hypercalcemia until the condition is adequately controlled.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Human pregnancy data are insufficient to assess the drug-associated risk of burosumab. Animal studies showed no teratogenic effects. In pregnant cynomolgus monkeys, late fetal loss and preterm birth occurred at exposures approximately 15 times the maximum human exposure, based on AUC, at a dose of 2 mg/kg every 2 weeks; these findings were accompanied by maternal hyperphosphatemia and placental mineralization. If burosumab is used during pregnancy, monitor serum phosphorus concentrations throughout pregnancy. Report pregnancy exposures to the manufacturer at 1-844-768-3544.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Use burosumab with caution during breast-feeding. There are no data on the presence of burosumab in human milk, its effects on the breastfed child, or its effects on milk production. Maternal IgG is present in human milk. However, the effects of local gastrointestinal exposure and limited systemic exposure to burosumab in the breastfed child are unknown. Consider the benefits of breast-feeding, the potential risk of drug exposure to the child, and the risks of untreated or inadequately treated disease.

    maculopapular rash, rash, urticaria
    General Information
    Hypersensitivity reactions (e.g., rash, urticaria) have been reported with burosumab. In adults, mild to moderate hypersensitivity occurred in 6% to 22% of patients in clinical trials; reported events included eczema (11%) and rash (11% to 15%). In pediatric patients, the most frequent potential hypersensitivity reactions were rash (10% to 22%), injection-site rash (6% to 10%), and urticaria (5% to 7%). Reported rashes included pruritic rash, maculopapular rash, erythematous rash, and pustular rash. Discontinue burosumab and institute appropriate medical management if serious hypersensitivity reactions occur.

    General Information
    Subcutaneous administration of burosumab can cause an injection site reaction. If severe injection site reactions occur, discontinue burosumab and administer appropriate medical treatment. Symptoms of a reaction may include erythema, pruritus, swelling, pain, rash, bruising, discoloration, discomfort, hematoma, hemorrhage, induration, macule, and urticaria. Description
  Text
  Burosumab is a fibroblast growth factor 23 (FGF23) blocking antibody indicated for the treatment of X-linked hypophosphatemia (XLH) and tumor-induced osteomalacia (TIO). In a placebo-controlled study involving 134 adult patients with XLH, 94% of adults receiving burosumab once a month achieved normal phosphorus concentrations compared to 8% of those receiving placebo. In pediatric studies, 94% to 100% of patients treated with burosumab every 2 weeks achieved normal phosphorus concentrations. Additionally, there was an improvement in X-ray findings associated with XLH in both children and adults receiving burosumab therapy. In 2 single-arm, phase 2 trials (n = 14 and n = 13), the mean serum phosphorus concentrations increased to above the lower limit of normal (LLN) following 24 weeks of burosumab therapy in 50% and 69% of adult patients with TIO. The use of oral phosphate and active vitamin D analogs is contraindicated during burosumab treatment; discontinue these products 1 week prior to the initiation of burosumab. Ensure serum phosphorous concentrations are below the reference range for age prior to treatment initiation; monitor serum phosphorus monthly for the first 3 months of treatment and periodically thereafter.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  X-linked hypophosphatemia (XLH) is caused by excess fibroblast growth factor 23 (FGF23), which suppresses renal tubular phosphate reabsorption and the renal production of 1,25 dihydroxy vitamin D. Burosumab, a human monoclonal immunoglobulin G1 antibody, binds to and inhibits the biological activity of FGF23 restoring renal phosphate reabsorption and increasing the serum concentration of 1,25 dihydroxy vitamin D.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Subcutaneous Route
    Description
    Burosumab exhibits linear pharmacokinetics across the dose range of 0.1 to 1 mg/kg subcutaneously. The mean steady state trough concentration is 5.8 (+/- 3.4) mcg/mL in adult patients with X-linked hypophosphatemia (XLH). The mean Tmax values range from 8 to 11 days. Based on a population pharmacokinetic analysis (PK), the PK parameters of burosumab were similar between patients with XLH and tumor-induced osteomalacia.

    Hepatic Impairment
    Description
    The effect of hepatic impairment on burosumab pharmacokinetics is unknown.
    Name
    Renal Impairment
    Description
    The effect of renal impairment on burosumab pharmacokinetics is unknown. However, renal impairment can induce abnormal mineral metabolism which will increase phosphate concentrations greater than expected with burosumab alone. This increase may result in hyperphosphatemia which can induce nephrocalcinosis.
    Name
    Pediatrics
    Description
    The burosumab steady state trough concentration is 15.8 (+/- 0.4) mcg/mL and 11.2 (+/- 4.6) mcg/mL in pediatric patients with X-linked hypophosphatemia aged 5 to 12 years and 1 to 4 years, respectively.
  Text
  Burosumab is administered as a subcutaneous injection. It exhibits linear pharmacokinetics across the dose range of 0.1 to 1 mg/kg. The elimination half-life after subcutaneous administration is approximately 19 days and the apparent clearance is 0.29 L/day. The apparent volume of distribution is 8 liters. Clearance and volume of distribution of burosumab increases with body weight. No significant difference in burosumab pharmacokinetics has been observed based on age. The exact pathway for burosumab metabolism has not been characterized, but it is expected to be degraded into small peptides and amino acids via catabolic pathways.

      Description
      Administer burosumab by subcutaneous injection only. Do not administer intravenously or intramuscularly.
If the dose requires using multiple burosumab vials, contents from 2 vials can be combined in the same syringe.

Dosage Adjustment Guidelines
  Hepatic Impairment
  Specific guidelines for dosage adjustments in hepatic impairment are not available; it appears that no dosage adjustments are needed.
