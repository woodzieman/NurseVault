Drug Class: Crovalimab is a complement C5 inhibitor indicated for the treatment of paroxysmal nocturnal hemoglobinuria (PNH).

Routes: injection (IV/IM/SC); Common adult doses: Weighing 100 kg or more: 1,500 mg IV load and 340 mg/dose subcutaneously load; 1,020 mg/dose subcutaneously every 4 weeks maintenance dose.

Common Side Effects (excluding routine GI upset): infusion-related reactions, injection site reaction, rash, infection, influenza, pharyngitis, rhinitis, asthenia, fatigue, malaise

In an Overdose: No overdose/antidote information is listed in this file.

Key Nursing Considerations: Contraindications/precautions: Crovalimab is contraindicated in individuals with unresolved meningococcal infection. Crovalimab increases the risk of serious, life-threatening, or fatal infections caused by meningococcal bacteria in any serogroup, including non-groupable strains.

Indication Specific Dosing
  General Information
  General Dosing Information
Vaccinate individuals against meningococcal infection (serogroups A, C, W, Y, and B) at least 2 weeks prior to initiation of crovalimab according to the most current Advisory Committee on Immunization Practices (ACIP) recommendations for individuals receiving a complement inhibitor.
If urgent crovalimab therapy is indicated in individuals who are not up to date with meningococcal vaccines according to ACIP recommendations, administer meningococcal vaccine(s) as soon as possible and provide antibacterial medication prophylaxis.
The dosing schedule is allowed to occasionally vary within 2 days of the scheduled administration day (except Days 1 and 2). If this occurs, administer the subsequent dose according to the regular schedule.
Switching from another C5 inhibitor to crovalimab or from crovalimab to another C5 inhibitor:
Conversion from another C5 inhibitor to crovalimab: Administer the first intravenous loading dose of crovalimab no sooner than the time of the next scheduled complement inhibitor administration. Follow the recommended schedule for the administration of the additional crovalimab subcutaneous loading doses and maintenance doses.
Individuals switching from another C5 inhibitor to crovalimab or from crovalimab to another C5 inhibitor are at risk of serious Type III hypersensitivity reactions. Consider the benefits of the timing of switching C5 inhibitors vs. the risks of Type III hypersensitivity reactions.
Initiating crovalimab sooner than 5.5 half-lives from the last dose of a C5 inhibitor or initiating a C5 inhibitor sooner than 5.5 half-lives from the last dose of crovalimab increases the risk of Type III hypersensitivity reactions.
Monitor for the first 30 days of the new therapy for the occurrence of symptoms of Type III hypersensitivity reactions.
For mild or moderate Type III hypersensitivity reactions, administer symptomatic treatment, such as topical corticosteroids, antihistamines, antipyretics, and/or analgesics.
For severe reactions, initiate and taper oral or systemic corticosteroid therapy as clinically indicated.
When discontinuing crovalimab in individuals who do not switch to another treatment for paroxysmal nocturnal hemoglobinuria (PNH), monitor for at least 20 weeks for signs and symptoms of serious hemolysis, identified by elevated lactate dehydrogenase concentrations, along with a sudden decrease in hemoglobin, or reappearance of symptoms such as fatigue, hemoglobinuria, abdominal pain, dyspnea, major adverse vascular events (including thrombosis), dysphagia, erectile dysfunction, or renal impairment. If signs and symptoms of hemolysis occur after discontinuation of crovalimab, consider restarting treatment with crovalimab, if appropriate, or initiating another treatment for PNH.

    Headline
    children, meningococcal infection
    General Information
    Crovalimab is contraindicated in individuals with unresolved meningococcal infection. Crovalimab increases the risk of serious, life-threatening, or fatal infections caused by meningococcal bacteria in any serogroup, including non-groupable strains. Complete or update meningococcal vaccination (for serogroups A, C, W, Y, and B) at least 2 weeks prior to administration of the first dose of crovalimab, according to current Advisory Committee on Immunization Practices (ACIP) recommendations for individuals receiving a complement inhibitor. Revaccinate considering the duration of crovalimab therapy. Note that ACIP recommends an administration schedule in individuals receiving complement inhibitors that differs from the administration schedule in the vaccine prescribing information. If urgent crovalimab therapy is indicated in individuals who are not up to date with meningococcal vaccines according to ACIP recommendations, administer meningococcal vaccine(s) as soon as possible and provide antibacterial medication prophylaxis. The optimal durations and medication regimens for antibacterial medication prophylaxis and their efficacy have not been studied in individuals receiving complement inhibitors, including crovalimab. Consider the benefits and risks of treatment with crovalimab in addition to the benefits and risks of antibacterial medication prophylaxis in unvaccinated or vaccinated individuals against the known risks for serious infections caused by N. meningitidis. Closely monitor for early signs and symptoms of meningococcal infection and evaluate immediately if infection is suspected. Instruct individuals to seek immediate medical care if these signs and symptoms occur. Promptly treat known infections. Meningococcal infection may become rapidly life-threatening or fatal if not recognized and treated early. Consider interruption of crovalimab therapy in individuals who are being treated for a serious meningococcal infection. The use of crovalimab increases susceptibility to encapsulated bacteria infection, especially those caused by N. meningitidis but also S. pneumoniae, H. influenzae, and to a lesser extent, N. gonorrhoeae. Children treated with crovalimab may be at increased risk of developing serious S. pneumoniae and H. influenzae type b (Hib) infections; administer vaccinations for the prevention of pneumococcal infection and Hib infection according to ACIP guidelines. Vaccination does not eliminate the risk for infections due to these organisms, despite development of antibodies after vaccination.
    Headline
    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components. Individuals who are switching from another C5 inhibitor to crovalimab or from crovalimab to another C5 inhibitor are at risk of serious Type III hypersensitivity reactions related to the formation of drug-target-drug-complexes (DTDCs), because crovalimab and these other C5 inhibitors bind different epitopes of C5. Consider the benefits of the timing of switching C5 inhibitors vs. the risks of Type III hypersensitivity reactions. Individuals are expected to no longer be at risk of Type III hypersensitivity reactions if the prior C5 inhibitor has been cleared from the body prior to starting crovalimab or if crovalimab has been cleared from the body prior to starting another C5 inhibitor. Therefore, initiating crovalimab sooner than 5.5 half-lives from the last dose of a C5 inhibitor or initiating a C5 inhibitor sooner than 5.5 half-lives from the last dose of crovalimab increases the risk of Type III hypersensitivity reactions. Monitor for the first 30 days of the new therapy for the occurrence of symptoms of Type III hypersensitivity reactions.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Available data on crovalimab use during human pregnancy are insufficient to evaluate for a drug-associated risk of major birth defects, miscarriage, or adverse outcomes. Human IgG antibodies cross the placenta; transport increases as pregnancy progresses and peaks during the third trimester. Therefore, crovalimab may be transmitted from the pregnant person to the fetus. No adverse developmental outcomes were observed in monkeys exposed to crovalimab during the period of organogenesis through parturition at doses that produced exposures 14 times the maximum recommended human (MRHD) dose based on AUC. There are risk associated with untreated paroxysmal nocturnal hemoglobinuria (PNH). PNH in pregnancy is associated with worsening cytopenias, thrombosis, infection, bleeding, miscarriage, and increased mortality in the pregnant person. It is also associated with fetal death and premature delivery.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Advise patients to avoid breast-feeding while taking crovalimab and for 9 months after the last dose. Crovalimab has the potential to cause serious side effects in the breast-fed child. There are no data on the presence of crovalimab in human or animal milk, its effects on the breast-fed child, or its effect on milk production. Endogenous IgG and monoclonal antibodies are transferred into human milk. The effects of local gastrointestinal exposure and limited systemic exposure of crovalimab in a breast-fed child are unknown.

Interactions
Cholera Vaccine: (Moderate) Patients receiving immunosuppressant medications may have a diminished response to the live cholera vaccine. When feasible, administer indicated vaccines prior to initiating immunosuppressant medications. Counsel patients receiving immunosuppressant medications about the possibility of a diminished vaccine response and to continue to follow precautions to avoid exposure to cholera bacteria after receiving the vaccine.
Dengue Tetravalent Vaccine, Live: (Contraindicated) Avoid administration of the live dengue virus vaccine with immunosuppressive drug therapy and prior to immune recovery following treatment with immunosuppressive drug therapy. When feasible, administer indicated live virus vaccines at least 4 weeks before planned immunosuppression or wait until at least 3 months after discontinuation. The time to restoration of immune competence may be longer in some patients. Patients with altered immunocompetence may be at increased risk for severe adverse reactions due to uninhibited growth of the attenuated live virus. Additionally, vaccines may be less effective if administered during a period of altered immunocompetence.
Eculizumab: (Major) Avoid concomitant use of crovalimab with eculizumab. When switching C5 inhibitors, a washout period is required; administer the first loading dose of the new medication no sooner than the time of the next scheduled C5 inhibitor administration. Concomitant use or an inadequate washout duration increases the risk for Type III hypersensitivity reactions related to the formation of drug-target-drug complexes. Allowing at least 5.5 medication half-lives to elapse between medication administrations minimizes the risk of adverse effects.
Ravulizumab: (Major) Avoid concomitant use of crovalimab with ravulizumab. When switching C5 inhibitors, a washout period is required; administer the first loading dose of the new medication no sooner than the time of the next scheduled C5 inhibitor administration. Concomitant use or an inadequate washout duration increases the risk for Type III hypersensitivity reactions related to the formation of drug-target-drug complexes. Allowing at least 5.5 medication half-lives to elapse between medications minimizes the risk of adverse effects.

Adverse Reactions
  Headline
  Adverse Reactions
  Adverse Reactions
    Headline
    infusion-related reactions, injection site reaction, rash
    General Information
    Administration of crovalimab may cause infusion-related reactions (14% to 16%) or systemic injection site reaction (6% to 9%), depending on the route of administration. These may include hypersensitivity reactions (including anaphylaxis) but also a range of other symptoms such as injection site pain, erythema, headache, or myalgia. A single subject experienced a serious infusion-related reaction that resolved 4 days after interruption of crovalimab infusion. Rash, including skin exfoliation, was reported in 9% of subjects receiving crovalimab during clinical trials. Instruct individuals/caregivers to seek immediate medical attention if symptoms of a serious hypersensitivity reaction develop and to report this reaction to their health care provider. If a serious hypersensitivity reaction occurs, discontinue crovalimab immediately, institute appropriate treatment per standard of care, and monitor until signs and symptoms are resolved. Persons who are switching from another C5 inhibitor to crovalimab or from crovalimab to another C5 inhibitor are at risk of serious Type III hypersensitivity reactions. In clinical trials, Type III hypersensitivity reactions were reported in 39 of 201 subjects (19%) who switched from eculizumab or ravulizumab to crovalimab. The majority of events were Grade 1 and 2. Grade 3 Type III hypersensitivity reaction occurred in 8% of subjects who switched from eculizumab or ravulizumab to crovalimab. A total of 6 subjects had switched 2 times, and of the 6 subjects, 2 subjects experienced a second episode of Type III hypersensitivity reaction after discontinuing crovalimab and switching to ravulizumab, including 1 subject who developed Grade 3 axonal neuropathy and another subject who developed Grade 2 arthralgia and myalgia. These events remained unresolved at the last follow-up visit of the clinical studies (313 days for the Grade 3 axonal neuropathy and 142 days for the Grade 2 arthralgia and myalgia). Grade 3 rash and Grade 3 arthralgia were experienced by 2 additional subjects, respectively, with unresolved Type III hypersensitivity reaction at the last follow-up visit. Symptoms of Type III hypersensitivity reactions that occurred in more than 2 subjects were arthralgia, rash, pyrexia, myalgia, headache, fatigue, petechiae, and abdominal pain. Among subjects who experienced Type III hypersensitivity reactions, 8 (21%) had events that were considered serious due to hospitalization. Symptoms of serious Type III hypersensitivity reactions included pyrexia and arthralgia. Type III hypersensitivity reactions can also cause renal abnormalities. The median time to onset of Type III hypersensitivity reaction in subjects who switched from eculizumab or ravulizumab to crovalimab was 1.6 weeks (range: 0.7 to 4.4 weeks), and the median duration of Type III hypersensitivity reactions was 1.9 weeks (range: 0.4 to 34.1 weeks). Out of 42 Type III hypersensitivity reactions, 37 (88%) resolved, including 1 (2.4%) that resolved with crovalimab discontinuation, 2 (4.8%) that resolved with crovalimab interruption, and 34 (81%) that resolved without discontinuation, interruption, or dose change in crovalimab therapy. Risk of Type III hypersensitivity reactions are no longer expected if the prior C5 inhibitor has been cleared from the body prior to starting crovalimab or if crovalimab has been cleared from the body prior to starting another C5 inhibitor. Based on time-to-onset of Type III hypersensitivity reactions observed in clinical trials, monitor for the first 30 days of the new therapy for the occurrence of symptoms of Type III hypersensitivity reactions. For mild or moderate Type III hypersensitivity reactions, administer symptomatic treatment, such as topical corticosteroids, antihistamines, antipyretics, and/or analgesics. For severe reactions, initiate and taper oral or systemic corticosteroid therapy as clinically indicated.

    antibody formation
    General Information
    Across clinical trials, treatment-emergent antidrug antibody formation was observed in 31% (60/191) of treatment-naive subjects after a median crovalimab treatment duration of 52 weeks (range: 0.1 to 108 weeks) and 23% (43/184) of subjects that switched from another C5 inhibitor to crovalimab after a median crovalimab treatment duration of 32 weeks (range: 0.3 to 108 weeks). Approximately 3% (11/375) of antidrug antibody-positive subjects had a loss of pharmacological activity (based on CH50 or free C5) coinciding with loss of exposure and efficacy, manifesting as a sustained loss of hemolysis control in 1.6% (6/375) of subjects with paroxysmal nocturnal hemoglobinuria. There was no evidence for a clinical impact of antidrug antibody status on the safety profile of crovalimab.

Description
  Text
  Crovalimab is a complement C5 inhibitor indicated for the treatment of paroxysmal nocturnal hemoglobinuria (PNH). Crovalimab increases the risk of serious, life-threatening, or fatal infections caused by meningococcal bacteria in any serogroup. Life-threatening and fatal meningococcal infections have occurred in both vaccinated and unvaccinated persons treated with complement inhibitors; vaccination for meningococcal disease is recommended to be completed at least 2 weeks before starting crovalimab to reduce the risk of meningococcal infection. Crovalimab was FDA-approved in June 2024.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Crovalimab is a monoclonal antibody that binds with high affinity to the complement protein C5, inhibiting its cleavage into C5a and C5b, which prevents the formation of the membrane attack complex. Crovalimab inhibits terminal complement-mediated intravascular hemolysis in a concentration-dependent manner in persons with paroxysmal nocturnal hemoglobinuria naive to complement inhibitor therapy as well as switching from another complement C5 inhibitor therapy.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Intravenous Route
    Description
    Crovalimab exhibits dose proportional pharmacokinetics over the dose range from 75 to 1,500 mg when given as a single intravenous infusion. After the first intravenous loading dose, crovalimab concentrations exceeded the targeted threshold for complete terminal complement inhibition (100 mcg/mL). Terminal complement activity (CH50 as measured by Liposome Immunoassay) inhibition was achieved by the end of the initial crovalimab infusion.
    Name
    Subcutaneous Route
    Description
    After subcutaneous administration, the bioavailability of crovalimab is 83%. Crovalimab exhibits dose proportional pharmacokinetics over the dose range of 100 to 1,020 mg when given as a subcutaneous injection. After 12 weeks, crovalimab attained steady-state exposure. Mean Cmax and AUC were 292 mcg/mL and 7,478 mcg x day/mL, respectively, in subjects weighing 40 to 99 kg and 265 mcg/mL and 6,748 mcg x day/mL, respectively, in subjects weighing 100 kg or more. Terminal complement activity (CH50 as measured by Liposome Immunoassay) inhibition was sustained through the duration of crovalimab treatment. Similarly, mean free C5 concentrations declined to low concentrations (less than 0.0001 g/L) in comparison to baseline and remained low throughout the treatment period.

    Individuals Switching from Another C5 Inhibitor
A transient increase in clearance is observed in individuals switching from another C5 inhibitor (e.g., eculizumab or ravulizumab) due to the formation of transient immune complexes, leading to a faster elimination of crovalimab.   Text
  Crovalimab is administered intravenously and subcutaneously. The mean central and peripheral Vd is 3.23 L and 2.32 L, respectively. Crovalimab is expected to be catabolized by lysosomal proteolysis into small peptides and amino acids. Crovalimab is not eliminated via renal or hepatic pathways. The mean clearance is 0.0791 L/day in paroxysmal nocturnal hemoglobinuria treatment-naive subjects. The mean estimated terminal half-life is 53.1 days.

      Preparation of Intravenous Infusion
Crovalimab for intravenous infusion administration must be diluted in 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection prior to administration.
Withdraw the required volume of crovalimab from the vial and dilute into a 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection infusion bag. Dilution of crovalimab in infusion bags containing 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection must be in the range of 4 to 15 mg/mL as the final concentration after dilution.
Dilute 5.9 mL of crovalimab (1,000 mg) in a 250 mL 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection bag to yield 4 mg/mL.
Dilute 5.9 mL of crovalimab (1,000 mg) in a 100 mL 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection bag to yield 10 mg/mL.

Dilute 8.8 mL of crovalimab (1,500 mg) in a 100 mL 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection bag to yield 15 mg/mL.
Gently mix the infusion bag by inverting the bag. Do not shake.
Storage: If immediate use is not possible, the prepared solution for infusion may be stored under specific conditions depending on the type of infusion bag used. Protect from direct sunlight.
Refrigerate crovalimab solution for infusion in polyolefin, polyethylene, and polypropylene infusion bags at 2 to 8 degrees C (36 to 46 degrees F) for up to 64 hours protected from light or store at room temperature up to 30 degrees C (86 degrees F) for up to 6 hours including infusion time under ambient light conditions.
Refrigerate crovalimab solution for infusion in polyvinyl chloride infusion bags at 2 to 8 degrees C (36 to 46 degrees F) for up to 12 hours protected from light or store at room temperature up to 30 degrees C (86 degrees F) for up to 6 hours including infusion time under ambient light conditions.
 
Intravenous Infusion
Use a 0.2 micron in-line filter during administration.
Use a dedicated infusion line.
Administer 1,000 mg IV over 60 minutes (+/- 10 minutes) and 1,500 mg IV over 90 minutes (+/- 10 minutes).
Flush the infusion line after administering the dose to ensure complete administration of the entire dose.
The infusion may be slowed or interrupted if an infusion-related reaction occurs. Discontinue the infusion immediately if a serious hypersensitivity reaction occurs.

      Description
      Administer crovalimab subcutaneously undiluted.
Remove the vial cap and clean the vial rubber stopper.
Attach the transfer needle on the syringe.
A transfer needle without a filter fulfilling the following criteria may be used: Stainless steel, sterile, preferably 18 gauge with single bevel at approximately 45 degrees to reduce risk of needle stick injury (or 21 gauge standard needle as an alternative), single-use, latex-free and non-pyrogenic, and commercially available.

Remove air bubbles if any.
Remove the transfer needle from the vial and recap the transfer needle using a 1-handed scoop technique.

Inject crovalimab subcutaneously in the abdomen region.
Rotate abdominal injection sites with every injection.

Do not inject into moles, scars, or areas where the skin is tender, bruised, red, hard, or not intact.
If the dose requires multiple injections, perform a new injection using a new crovalimab vial.
Storage: If immediate use is not possible, a capped syringe can be refrigerated at 2 to 8 degrees C (36 to 46 degrees F) for up to 64 hours protected from light or stored at room temperature up to 30 degrees C (86 degrees F) for up to 5 hours under ambient light conditions. Protect from direct sunlight.

    Injectable Administration
    Description
    Visually inspect parenteral products for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration whenever solution and container permit. Crovalimab is a clear to opalescent and almost colorless to brownish-yellow solution; discard if the medication looks cloudy, discolored, or has particles.
Each vial is for single use only in a single individual.
Crovalimab is administered as an intravenous infusion for the first dose and as subcutaneous injections for subsequent doses.
Only health care providers should administer crovalimab.
Missed dose: If the entire dose or part of a planned dose is missed, administer the missed dose or remainder of the missed dose as soon as possible before the day of the next scheduled dose. Administer the next dose on the regularly scheduled dosing day. Do not administer 2 doses or more than the prescribed dose on the same day to make up for a missed dose.
