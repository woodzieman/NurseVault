Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  General Information
  Some formulations of nystatin, such as the nystatin oral suspension, contain methylparaben and propylparaben. These products should be used with caution in patients with paraben hypersensitivity.Nystatin oral suspension is classified as pregnancy category C. The effects on the fetus from administration during pregnancy are unknown. Nystatin oral suspension should be used during pregnancy only when clearly needed. Nystatin vaginal tablets are classified as pregnancy category A.The use of topical nystatin is not a problem during breast-feeding; topical nystatin is commonly used as a first line therapy on affected areas of the breast when thrush (C. albicans) infection in the neonate gets transferred to the maternal breast tissue. It is not known whether orally-administered nystatin is excreted in human breast milk; however, nystatin is poorly absorbed systemically and minimal serum and milk concentrations would be expected; problems have not been reported in nursing infants.Because nystatin suspension contains significant amounts of sucrose, it should be used cautiously in patients with diabetes mellitus.

Description
  Text
  Nystatin is an oral and topical polyene antifungal indicated for the treatment of oropharyngeal, gastrointestinal, cutaneous, and mucocutaneous candidiasis. Nystatin is not indicated for the treatment of invasive candidiasis due to negligible absorption from the gastrointestinal tract. Nystatin possesses fungistatic and fungicidal activity against a wide variety of yeast and yeast-like fungi.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Nystatin is a polyene antifungal that acts by binding to sterols in the cell membranes of both fungal and human cells, which results in a change in membrane permeability allowing leakage of intracellular components with subsequent cell death. Nystatin has greater affinity for ergosterol, the sterol found in fungal cell membranes, than for cholesterol, the sterol found in human cell membranes; however, nystatin is too toxic to be used parenterally. Nystatin is both fungistatic and fungicidal.
