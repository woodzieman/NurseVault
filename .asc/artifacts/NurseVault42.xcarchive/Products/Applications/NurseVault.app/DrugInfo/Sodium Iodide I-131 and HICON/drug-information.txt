Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  General Information
  Radiopharmaceuticals should be used only by nuclear physicians and/or radiopharmacists who are qualified by training and experience in the safe use and handling of radionuclides and whose experience and training have been approved by the appropriate governmental agency authorized to license the use of radionuclides. Care should be taken to assure minimum accidental exposure of radiation to occupational workers. Furthermore, care should be taken to minimize radiation exposure to the patient by proper patient management before, during, and after the procedure. When using sodium iodide I-131 at therapeutic doses, advise patients by both written and oral instruction to follow radiation safety precautions in an effort to minimize radiation contamination of other persons or the environment (see Administration section).
 
Written informed consent must be obtained from the patient prior to the therapeutic use of sodium iodide I-131. This consent should advise the patient of the possibility of requiring more than one course of therapy, the risk of short-term and long-term side effects, the necessity of thyroid hormone withdrawal, the dietary restrictions prior to treatment, and the long-term follow up required after treatment.
 
Hypersensitivity to sodium iodide I-131 is possible. Use with caution in patients with iodine hypersensitivity. Sodium iodide I-131 diluted solution (not the concentrate) contains sodium bisulfite, which is a sulfite that may cause allergic-type reactions including anaphylactic symptoms and life-threatening or less severe asthmatic episodes in certain, susceptible people. The incidence of sulfite hypersensitivity is thought to be low in the general population, although it is higher in people with asthma.
 
The recent intake of stable iodine in any form, or the use of thyroid or anti -thyroid drugs will affect the uptake of radioiodide (see drug interactions). Accordingly, the patient should be questioned carefully regarding previous and current medications (e.g., anticholinergic medications, nutritional supplements) and procedures involving radiographic contrast administration. Instruct patients to follow a low-iodine diet for 1—2 weeks prior to therapy. In order to optimize iodide uptake into metastatic lesions, some practitioners advocate pretreatment measure of urinary iodine in all patients and the delay of sodium iodide I-131 therapy in patients with elevated serum iodine concentrations of greater than 200 mcg/L.
 
In an effort to facilitate removal of radioactive iodine that is not absorbed by thyroid tissue, patients should be adequately hydrated before and after sodium iodide I-131 therapy. It may be prudent to avoid sodium iodide I-131 therapy in patients with pre-existing dehydration. In addition, preexisting vomiting and diarrhea represent contraindications to the therapeutic use of radioiodide.
 
Renal impairment, including renal failure, may decrease the excretion of radioiodide and increase the amount of radiation exposure. The risk of adverse reactions to this drug may be greater in patients with impaired renal function, including elderly patients. Further, adequate and well controlled studies on the relationship of age to the effect of radioiodide have not been performed in the geriatric population. Care should be taken in dose selection and it may be useful to monitor the renal function in elderly patients and in those with preexisting renal disfunction.
 
Hyperthyroidism and thyrotoxic cardiac disease may be aggravated by radiation thyroiditis. Pre- and post-treatment with antithyroid agents and/or beta-blockers should be considered.
 
Hypochloremia may increase the thyroid uptake of sodium iodide I-131.
 
Radioiodide will NOT be taken up by specific neoplastic disease including giant cell or spindle cell carcinoma of the thyroid or by amyloid solid carcinomas.
 
Sodium iodide I-131 is not usually used for the treatment of hyperthyroidism in patients under 30 years of age unless circumstances preclude other methods of treatment. The safety and effectiveness in children have not been established.
 
Sodium iodide I-131 is classified in FDA pregnancy category X. Sodium iodide I-131 is contraindicated for use in women who are or may become pregnant. Sodium iodide I-131 may cause harm to the fetal thyroid gland when administered to pregnant women. Review of the literature has shown that transplacental passage of radioiodide may cause severe, and possibly irreversible, hypothyroidism in neonates. Use of sodium iodide I-131 in women of childbearing age should be deferred until appropriate contraceptive measures have been taken or when pregnancy testing is negative; the patient should be apprised of the potential hazard to a fetus. In order to minimize the risk of miscarriage after therapeutic dosed sodium iodide I-131 administration, conception should delayed for a period of 1 year.
 
Women should be advised to discontinue breast-feeding their infants before treatment with sodium iodide I-131. Radioiodine is excreted in human milk during lactation. The manufacturer recommends substituting formula feedings until radioiodide levels have substantially decreased; however, the Society of Nuclear Medicine recommends that in patients receiving therapeutic doses of sodium iodide I-131, breast-feeding should be discontinued and sodium iodide I-131 treatment delayed until lactation ceases. Furthermore, the patient should not resume breast-feeding that infant; however, patients may breast-feed after the birth of future infants.

    Headline
    dehydration, diarrhea, vomiting
    General Information
    Therapeutic use of sodium iodide I-131 is contraindicated in patients with vomiting and diarrhea. Radiation exposure can occur from handling contaminated waste products, including urine, feces, and vomit. Follow safe administration instructions and educate patients on how to reduce unnecessary radiation exposure to others, particularly family members. Additionally, in an effort to facilitate removal of radioactive iodine that is not absorbed by thyroid tissue, patients should be adequately hydrated before and after diagnostic use of sodium iodide I-131. It may be prudent to avoid sodium iodide I-131 therapy in patients with pre-existing dehydration.
    Headline
    geriatric, renal disease, renal failure, renal impairment
    General Information
    Renal disease (nephrosis) may increase the uptake of iodine by the thyroid. Renal impairment, including renal failure, may decrease the excretion of radioiodide and increase the amount of radiation exposure after sodium iodide I-131 administration. Clinical experience has not identified differences in the safety or efficacy of sodium iodide I-131 in geriatric patients compared to younger patients; however, because the elderly are more likely to have impaired renal function and co-morbid conditions, enhanced evaluation, careful dose selection, and appropriate follow-up may be necessary. Evaluate renal function in all patients prior to administration.
    Headline
    children, infants, neonates
    General Information
    Safety and efficacy of diagnostic and therapeutic sodium iodide I-131 have not been established in neonates, infants, children, and adolescents. Published reports suggest the thyroid gland of pediatric patients is more sensitive (e.g., increased rate of absorption) to the adverse effects of sodium iodide I-131. If off-label use is considered, carefully weigh the risks, particularly carcinogenic risks, and benefits of sodium iodide I-131 use against those of alternative treatments.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Sodium iodide I-131 is contraindicated in pregnancy because fetal exposure may lead to severe and irreversible neonatal hypothyroidism. Sodium iodide I-131 crosses the placenta and begins to concentrate in the fetal thyroid during the tenth to twelfth week of gestation. In literature describing maternal sodium iodide I-131 exposures at doses of 333 to 8,325 MBq (9 to 225 mCi) during gestational weeks 4 to 26, the most common adverse outcomes were hypothyroid infants and children. Monitor thyroid function in any infant with in utero exposure to sodium iodide I-131; a delay in the diagnosis of neonatal hypothyroidism can result in severe sequelae including cognitive impairment and delayed skeletal maturation.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Sodium iodide I-131 is contraindicated with breast-feeding. Women should be advised to discontinue breast-feeding immediately after administration of sodium iodide I-131 when it is used as a diagnostic agent. When used therapeutically, advise women to discontinue breast-feeding and breast-pumping at least 6 weeks prior to use of sodium iodide I-131 to minimize the absorbed radiation dose to the breast tissue. The Society of Nuclear Medicine recommends that breast-feeding be discontinued and sodium iodide I-131 treatment delayed until lactation ceases in patients receiving therapeutic doses of sodium iodide I-131. If sodium iodide I-131 is administered postpartum, breast-feeding should not be restarted for the remainder of the postpartum period; however, patients may breast-feed after the birth of future infants if the mother does not receive sodium iodide I-131 during that postpartum period. Radioiodine is excreted in human milk during lactation and may reach concentrations equal to or greater than concentrations in maternal plasma. Published studies show that sodium iodide I-131 in breast milk is taken up by the thyroid of a breast-fed infant. Infants exposed to sodium iodide I-131 through breast milk are at risk for developing hypothyroidism.
    Headline
    contraception requirements, infertility, pregnancy testing, reproductive risk
    General Information
    Sodium iodide I-131 is associated with reproductive risk. Infertility has been reported after sodium iodide I-131 therapy. Doses of 370 to 22,000 MBq (10 to 595 mCi) have resulted in transient impairment of testicular function (including spermatogenesis), with higher cumulative doses (i.e., 19,000 to 29,000 MBq [520 to 800 mCi]) causing permanent testicular impairment. Discuss sperm banking for individuals who anticipate receiving a high cumulative sodium iodide I-131 dose and prior to treatment of thyroid carcinoma. The risk of persistent testicular dysfunction increases after administration of repeated or high cumulative radioiodide exposure. Transient menstrual cycle irregularities (including amenorrhea and primary ovarian insufficiency) have been observed after cumulative doses of 1,000 to 59,000 MBq (27 to 1,594 mCi). The effects on fertility occurred in up to 30% of females treated with sodium iodide I-131, and may resolve 12 months after treatment. Use of sodium iodide I-131 in women of childbearing age should be deferred until appropriate contraceptive measures have been taken or when pregnancy testing is negative. Obtain a pregnancy test to verify the absence of pregnancy within 24 hours before initiating treatment with sodium iodide I-131 in a female of childbearing age. In order to minimize the risk of miscarriage after therapeutic use of sodium iodide I-131 administration, conception should be delayed for a period of 1 year. Discuss contraception requirements; use of 2 effective methods of contraception is recommended for at least 6 months after sodium iodide I-131 administration and advising individuals that this period may be extended longer (i.e., 1 year) if additional therapy or imaging is anticipated.

Adverse Reactions
  Headline
  Adverse Reactions
  General Information
  Adverse reactions following sodium iodide I-131 treatment of hyperthyroid states are usually mild. Following the larger doses used in treating thyroid carcinoma, adverse reactions may be much more severe and present special problems. Reactions associated with the administration of iodine containing radiopharmaceuticals for diagnostic use are rare.Immediate adverse reactions typically are related to hypersensitivity (e.g., anaphylactoid reactions, rash (unspecified), urticaria (hives), bronchospasm). Although such reactions are infrequent, advise patients to report any signs or symptoms of hypersensitivity immediately.Adverse reactions result from the selective concentration of sodium iodide I-131 by the salivary glands; further, the oral and GI mucosa are exposed to radioactivity both from the oral administration and from the subsequent concentration of the radioactive iodide in the saliva. Temporary loss of taste (dysgeusia) and radiation sickness, including nausea/vomiting and gastritis, are common in the first days following sodium iodide I-131 administration. Tenderness and swelling of the neck, pain on swallowing, throat irritation, and cough may occur around the third day after treatment and are usually amenable to analgesics. Chronic sialadenitis or xerostomia is uncommon but occurrence can contribute to significant patient distress. Prevention and treatments include adequate hydration, encouragement of salivation (e.g., using sugarless sour candy, using lemon juice, chewing gum), parotid gland massage, and good oral hygiene; cholinergic medications may be necessary in severe cases.Temporary alopecia may occur 2—3 months after treatment.Dose-dependent adverse events have been reported including bone marrow depression, leukopenia, thrombocytopenia, acute leukemia, anemia, blood dyscrasia, chromosomal abnormalities, radiation sickness, and death. Since a retrospective review of data in the 1960's revealed no significant bone marrow depression in patients with a single dose treatment of less than 2 Gy of sodium iodide I-131 , practitioners have maintained this dosing paradigm. More recent data suggest that the risk of leukemia is significantly increased in patients who receive cumulative doses of greater than 18.5 GBq (500 mCi).Metabolic adverse events include the increased risk of exaggerated hyperthyroidism due to radiation thyroiditis. Radiation thyroiditis is characterized by symptoms of inflammation and eventual necrosis of thyroid tissue; symptoms are usually mild and resolve within one month. In severe cases, a large spike of thyroid hormones released from the apoptosis of thyroid tissue may result in thyrotoxicosis requiring treatment with anti-inflammatory drugs and beta-blockers. Most patients will progress to a euthyroid state within 3—6 months of therapy; eventually, in most patients, hypothyroidism ensues requiring life-long thyroid replacement therapy.The risk of secondary malignancy after therapeutic use of sodium iodide I-131 has been debated. Experiments in animals administered sodium iodide I-131 have demonstrated that radioiodide administration can induce thyroid adenoma and carcinoma. However, studies in humans have shown conflicting results. Patients can lessen their risk by complying with written and verbal instructions regarding their care. Normal renal function and adequate hydration prior to and in the days following sodium iodide I-131 treatment is essential to assure rapid urinary elimination of the radioiodide that is not absorbed by thyroid tissue; rapid urinary elimination limits the exposure of other body tissues (e.g., the bladder, lower colon, kidneys, ovaries, testes, and stomach) to radioactivity. Because frequent urination may reduce the exposure to the bladder, advise patients to void often. Further, the patient should be encouraged to have at minimum, one bowel movement daily; laxatives may be useful.

Although such reactions are infrequent, advise patients to report any signs or symptoms of hypersensitivity immediately.

When treating hyperthyroidism, consider pretreatment with antithyroid medication; discontinue the antithyroid medication at least 3 days prior to initiation of sodium iodide I-131 therapy. Consider beta-blocker therapy before administration of therapeutic sodium iodide I-131 to decrease the risk of hyperthyroidism and thyroid storm. Most patients will progress to an euthyroid state within 3 to 6 months of therapy; eventually, in most patients, hypothyroidism ensues requiring life-long thyroid replacement therapy. In addition, hypoparathyroidism or sites of iodide avid tumor may develop in some patients. Local swelling of thyroid has also been reported, and may occur with diagnostic or therapeutic use.

Description
  Text
  Sodium iodide I-131 (Na131I), is an oral radioactive iodide isotope that is used both therapeutically and diagnostically. The radiopharmaceutical is selectively trapped by non-malignant and, to a lesser degree, malignant thyroid tissue. Sodium iodide I-131 was approved in 1957 for radioactive iodide ablation in the treatment of hyperthyroidism and select cases of thyroid carcinoma. Radioiodide is not taken up by the majority of medullary and anaplastic carcinomas including giant cell and spindle cell carcinoma of the thyroid or amyloid solid carcinomas. When used therapeutically, the preferred formulation and dosing regimen is highly physician specific. Sodium iodide I-131 was also approved as a diagnostic aid in December 2005; the gamma and beta particle emissions are used to evaluate thyroid function via the radioactive iodine (RAI) uptake test and for thyroid imaging.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Mechanism of Action: Sodium iodide I-131 is a radiopharmaceutical. The radioactive isotope decays by beta and gamma emission. Sodium iodide I-131 mimics nonradioactive iodine exactly, and is rapidly trapped by thyroid tissue after administration. The extent of iodide uptake into the thyroid is estimated to be 10—35% at 24 hours post oral administration ; however, the extent of uptake is altered by the function of the thyroid gland and the pretreatment iodine status of the patient.When used as a diagnostic agent, the administration of radioactive iodine can be used to determine iodide uptake as a measure of thyroid function. Sodium iodide I-131 emits gamma radiation that can be detected by a gamma probe several hours after oral administration. By comparing the amount of sodium iodide I-131 that is taken up by the thyroid to the standard uptake in euthyroid patients, the patient's thyroid function can be determined. Similarly, sodium iodide I-131 is taken up by metastatic thyroid carcinoma lesions that can be detected by whole body iodine scans. Beta-1, Beta-3, Beta-4, Gamma-7, Gamma-14, and Gamma-17 photons are useful for detection and imaging.When used as a therapeutic agent, at doses required for ablation of thyroid tissue, approximately 90 percent of the local irradiation is caused by beta emissions and 10 percent is caused by gamma emissions. The radiation disrupts chemical bonds within the surrounding cells resulting in DNA molecule damage, subsequent cellular damage, and cell death. Unintended adverse events result both from radionucleotide concentration by other tissues (i.e., the salivary glands, oral mucosa, gastrointestinal mucosa) and radionucleotide excretion by the kidneys.

    Renal Impairment
    Description
    Sodium iodide I-131 is substantially excreted by the kidney. The pharmacokinetics of sodium iodide I-131 in 2 patients with thyroid cancer and end stage renal disease undergoing continuous ambulatory peritoneal dialysis (CAPD) were compared to 8 euthyroid CAPD patients and 8 thyroid cancer patients with normal renal function. In patients undergoing CAPD, serum radioiodine half-times were 5 times longer than half-times in patients with normal renal function. Similarly, clearance rates in the CAPD patients were 20% of the clearance rates in patients with normal renal function. Subsequently, sodium iodide I-131 was administered to the 2 CAPD patients with thyroid cancer at 20 percent of standard dosage with resultant equivalent exposure to total body and red marrow. In addition, sodium iodide I-131 is readily dialyzable; in 2 chronic hemodialysis patients with thyroid carcinoma that received ablation thyroid therapy, sodium iodide I-131 was effectively removed 20 to 24 hours after I-131 administration during a standard 3 or 4 hour hemodialysis session. In both cases, after 1 session, radiation levels were less than 5 mR/hour allowing patients to be discharged to home.

  Text
  Sodium iodide I-131 is administered orally. After absorption, sodium iodide I-131 is distributed within the extracellular space, where it is then taken up by the thyroid or excreted renally. The sodium-iodide symporter (NIS) is responsible for the concentration of iodide in the thyroid. Under normal circumstances, this active transport process concentrates iodide 20 to 40 times the plasma concentration. Thyroid uptake is increased in hyperthyroidism (up to 10-fold) and decreased in hypothyroidism. In the thyroid, trapped iodide is oxidized to iodine and organified. NIS also mediates iodide transport to other tissues including the salivary glands, nasolacrimal duct, lacrimal sac, stomach, choroid plexus, breasts, liver, gall bladder, and kidneys. The normal range of urinary excretion is 37% to 75% of the administered dose, varying with the thyroid and renal function of the patient; fecal excretion is approximately 10% and sweat excretion is negligible. Sodium iodide I-131 decays by beta emission and associated gamma emission, with a physical half-life of 8.04 days.

Administration
  General Information
  For storage information, see the specific product information within the How Supplied section.
NOTE: Sodium iodide I-131 is a radioactive material. To assure minimum radiation exposure to occupational workers and to minimize radiation exposure to the patients, observe appropriate precautions that are consistent with proper patient management. Radiopharmaceuticals should only be used by nuclear physicians and/or radiopharmacists who are qualified by training and experience in the safe use and handling of radionuclides and whose experience and training have been approved by the appropriate governmental agency authorized to license the use of radionuclides.
NOTE: Storage and disposal of sodium iodide I-131 should be controlled in compliance with the appropriate regulations of the governmental agency authorized to license the use of this radionuclide.
 
Management of patients after therapeutic dose administration:
NOTE: Special precautions are not necessary in patients receiving sodium iodide I-131 for diagnostic purposes.
According to the Nuclear Regulatory Commission, patients should be isolated until radiation levels are less than 5 mR/hour.
Health care providers should follow all applicable guidelines while giving effective in-hospital patient care, these may include: using radiation monitors; observing universal precautions and isolation techniques; using shoe covers and gloves in the patient's room and disposing of such items in the room upon leaving; maintaining the most distance possible during patient care and minimizing the time spent in the patient's room; confining all trash, linens, and food or drink related items in the room; washing hands immediately after leaving the room.
Patients should be advised of and adhere to the following precautions for 48 to 96 hours following radioactive iodine administration because radioactivity will be present in the urine :
Patients should increase their fluid intake and void their bladder and bowels frequently to efficiently eliminate the radioactive iodine. In addition, encourage patients to use sugarless sour candy, lemon juice, or chewing gum to increase salivation and increase elimination of radioactive iodine.
Patients should use a toilet, when possible, rather than a urinal. The toilet should be flushed several times after each use. Spilled urine should be cleaned up immediately and completely. Patients should be instructed to wash their hands thoroughly after voiding and/or cleaning up urine spills. If blood or urine gets into clothing, wash the clothing separately, or store it for 1 to 2 weeks to allow for decay of the sodium iodide I-131.
Patients should wash the sink and tub after each use. Separate towels and washcloths should be used. All clothes and linens should be laundered separately.
Patients should avoid handling or using another person's eating or drinking utensils, food or drink, toothbrushes, or personal hygiene materials. Utensils and personal items should be thoroughly cleaned after use.
Patients should maintain a minimum distance of 3 feet from others for 2 to 3 days and avoid prolonged contact with others, especially children and breast-feeding or pregnant women. Patients should sleep alone with at least 7 feet of distance and should avoid intimate contact with others for 5 to 11 days, depending on dose of sodium iodide I-131 administered.

Dosage Adjustment Guidelines
  Hepatic Impairment
  Specific guidelines for dosage adjustments in hepatic impairment are not available; it appears that dosage adjustments are not needed.
  Renal Impairment
  Radiation exposure is greater in patients with impaired renal function. Dosage should be modified depending on clinical response and degree of renal impairment, but no standardized recommendations are available.
 
Intermittent hemodialysis
Definitive recommendations have not been made. However, case reports indicate that for therapeutic use, dosage modification is probably not necessary in this population. It appears that the patient's radioactivity concentrations can be reduced to less than 5 mR/hour with a 3 to 4 hour hemodialysis session administered at least 20 to 24 hours after dose administration.
 
Peritoneal dialysis
Definitive recommendations have not been made. However, 2 case reports in patients with thyroid carcinoma undergoing CAPD indicate that administering a 5-fold decrease in the standard dose for ablation therapy results in equivalent total body and red marrow exposure.
