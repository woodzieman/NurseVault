Drug Class: Chlordiazepoxide and clidinium are a long-acting benzodiazepine and an antimuscarinic, respectively, used together in an oral preparation to control emotional and somatic factors in gastrointestinal disorders.

Routes: oral; Common adult doses: 40 mg/day PO chlordiazepoxide and 20 mg/day PO clidinium.

Common Side Effects (excluding routine GI upset): blurred vision, ileus, mydriasis, urinary retention, ataxia, confusion, drowsiness, syncope, eeg changes, libido decrease, libido increase, menstrual irregularity, tremor, agitation, excitability, irritability, anaphylactoid reactions, edema, rash, urticaria, physiological dependence, psychological dependence, withdrawal

In an Overdose: Signs of overdose may be most apparent by the anticholinergic effects of clidinium, such as xerostomia, blurred vision, urinary retention and constipation. Thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura (TTP) has been reported in the literature with the use of chlordiazepoxide; clidinium; in this case an immune-mediated mechanism was suspected, but whether the product caused the event was not known....

  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  General Information
  NOTE: This monograph discusses the use of the chlordiazepoxide; clidinium combination product for the management of GI disorders. Clinicians may wish to consult the individual chlordiazepoxide drug monograph for more information.
 
Chlordiazepoxide; clidinium is contraindicated in any patient with a known or suspected hypersensitivity to chlordiazepoxide clidinium, or any component of the formulation. Patients with a benzodiazepine hypersensitivity to other benzodiazepines may experience cross-sensitivity with chlordiazepoxide. 
 
The anticholinergic effects of chlordiazepoxide; clidinium may be significant and are additive with other anticholinergic medications.
 
Chlordiazepoxide; clidinium is contraindicated in patients with prostatic hypertrophy or benign bladder neck obstruction (bladder obstruction). Chlordiazepoxide; clidinium can exacerbate urinary retention in patients with urinary tract obstruction . Chlordiazepoxide; clidinium should not be used in these patients. The anticholinergic component of the drug may worsen these conditions.
 
Clidinium decreases GI motility and should be avoided if possible in GI obstruction, toxic megacolon, severe ulcerative colitis, or adynamic ileus. Clidinium should be used cautiously in patients with gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD) or hiatal hernia associated with reflux esophagitis. Antimuscarinics decrease gastric motility and relax the lower esophageal sphincter. These effects promote gastric retention and aggravate reflux in these patients. In addition, in patients with gastric ulcers, clidinium may delay gastric emptying resulting in antral stasis. Anticholinergics should not be used to treat diarrhea, which may be an early sign of intestinal obstruction, especially in patients with ileostomy or colostomy. The use of alosetron in combination with chlordiazepoxide; clidinium should be avoided due to serious adverse reactions reported in patients who experienced severe constipation while taking alosetron. These 2 drugs would have additive pharmacodynamic constipating effects (see Drug Interactions).
 
Due to CNS depression, patients should be cautioned against driving or operating machinery until they know how chlordiazepoxide; clidinium may affect them. Avoid use of chlordiazepoxide; clidinium in patients with acute ethanol intoxication, coma, head trauma, or shock due to worsening CNS depression. Some patients may experience excessive sedation and impaired ability to perform tasks. Increased CNS effects may be seen with concurrent use of chlordiazepoxide; clidinium and other CNS depressant agents (see Drug Interactions). Anterograde amnesia has been reported with the benzodiazepines.
 
Chlordiazepoxide can cause physical and psychological dependence, and should be used with extreme caution in patients with known, suspected or a history of substance abuse. Generally, benzodiazepines should be prescribed for short periods (2—4 weeks) with continued re-evaluation of the need for treatment. Tolerance (or tachyphylaxis) may develop to the sedative effects of benzodiazepines. Patients should be questioned about the need for escalating doses, and the clinician may need to intervene to prevent further tolerance or increased risk for addiction. Abrupt discontinuation of chlordiazepoxide after prolonged use should be avoided. Abrupt discontinuation of benzodiazepine therapy has been reported to cause withdrawal symptoms, especially following high dose or prolonged therapy (see Adverse Effects). However, benzodiazepine dependence can occur following administration of therapeutic doses for as few as 1—2 weeks, and withdrawal symptoms may be seen following the discontinuation of therapy. Patients with a history of a seizure disorder or who are taking other drugs that lower the seizure threshold (i.e., tricyclic antidepressants, phenothiazines) should not be withdrawn abruptly from benzodiazepines due to the risk of precipitating seizures; status epilepticus has also been reported. Benzodiazepines should be withdrawn slowly, using a gradual dosage-tapering schedule. During withdrawal, the greatest risk of seizure appears to be during the first 24—72 hours. Clinicians should be aware that the use of flumazenil may increase the risk of seizures, particularly in long-term users of benzodiazepines and in cyclic antidepressant overdoses (see Flumazenil monograph).
 
Chlordiazepoxide; clidinium should be administered cautiously and prescribed in the smallest possible amount to patients with major depression, suicidal ideation or psychosis. Paradoxical reactions such as excitement, stimulation or rage have been reported with the use of chlordiazepoxide in psychiatric patients. Occasionally, pre-existing depression may emerge or worsen with the use of benzodiazepines. Chlordiazepoxide; clidinium should be used cautiously in patients with bipolar disorder because mania and hypomania have been reported in conjunction with the use of benzodiazepines in depressive disorders.
 
Chlordiazepoxide; clidinium should be administered extremely cautiously, if at all, to patients with Parkinson's disease. Patients with late stage Parkinson's disease may experience worsening of their psychotic episodes or impaired cognition with administration of benzodiazepines. Benzodiazepines may also cause incoordination or paradoxical reactions that may worsen symptoms of Parkinson's disease. Use cautiously in patients with organic brain syndromes (e.g., dementia) or other types of psychosis.
 
Chlordiazepoxide; clidinium should be used with extreme caution in patients with respiratory depression, pulmonary disease such as chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) (i.e., emphysema or chronic bronchitis) or sleep apnea because chlordiazepoxide can exacerbate ventilatory failure.
 
The safe and effective use of chlordiazepoxide; clidinium in children has not been established. Children are generally more sensitive to the CNS effects of the benzodiazepines and clidinium has not been shown to be safe in children. Paradoxical reactions, including acute rage, stimulation and excitement have been reported in hyperactive aggressive pediatric patients and should be assessed, if needed, during chlordiazepoxide therapy.
 
The clearance and/or elimination of many drugs are reduced in the elderly. Delayed elimination can either intensify or prolong the actions or adverse reactions of chlordiazepoxide. Benzodiazepines have been associated with an increased incidence of falls in the elderly. The impairment of cognitive and motor function may be more marked in this patient group and lower initial dosage is recommended together with close monitoring (see Dosage). The concomitant use of other psychotropic agents should be approached with caution in the elderly. Chlordiazepoxide; clidinium should be administered cautiously to patients with severe hepatic disease because the elimination half-life of the drug can be prolonged, possibly resulting in toxicity. The liver is utilized for metabolism of chlordiazepoxide. Patients with hepatic disease should receive reduced initial dosages and be closely monitored for tolerability. Additionally, chlordiazepoxide metabolites may accumulate leading to severe and prolonged stupor in patients with cirrhosis or hepatitis.
 
Patients with renal impairment or renal failure should be carefully monitored during prolonged treatment with chlordiazepoxide; clidinium to avoid toxicity due to drug accumulation. Adequate renal function is required for elimination of both chlordiazepoxide and clidinium. Dosages may need to be reduced by 50% or more in renal failure.
 
Chlordiazepoxide; clidinium has not been assigned to an FDA pregnancy risk category. In general, it is not safe during pregnancy. Chlordiazepoxide is classified as pregnancy risk category D because it could cause harm to the fetus when administered to pregnant women. Chlordiazepoxide should not be administered during the first trimester of pregnancy. Positive evidence of human fetal risk exists based on investigational, marketing, or human studies. Clidinium is classified as pregnancy risk category C. Quaternary anticholinergics have been associated with minor fetal malformations. Chlordiazepoxide is not recommended for use in obstetrical procedures, labor, or obstetric delivery, including cesarean section. Neonatal flaccidity has been reported in an infant whose mother was using benzodiazepines during pregnancy. Chlordiazepoxide; clidinium should be administered during pregnancy only if any possible benefit to the mother outweighs the risk to the fetus. Patients should communicate to their health care provider if they intend to become pregnant while receiving chlordiazepoxide; clidinium, and providers should actively question patients about their intent to become pregnant.
 
The use of chlordiazepoxide; clidinium during breast-feeding is generally not recommended. Chlordiazepoxide is distributed into breast milk and can cause sedation, feeding difficulties, and weight loss in the nursing infant. The American Academy of Pediatrics considers anti-anxiety medications, such as chlordiazepoxide, as a drug for which the effect on nursing infants is unknown but may be of concern. Long-term neurodevelopmental effects in the infant are not known. Clidinium, due to anticholinergic effects, may reduce the production of breast-milk.
 
Chlordiazepoxide; clidinium should be avoided or used with caution in patients with a neuromuscular disease, such as muscular dystrophy or myotonia as these conditions can be exacerbated. Although clidinium is considered an antimuscarinic anticholinergic, it also possesses significant action at nicotinic receptors. Chlordiazepoxide; clidinium should not be used in patients with myasthenia gravis, although clidinium's antagonism at the neuromuscular junction usually is seen only with toxic doses. Chlordiazepoxide may also aggravate myasthenia gravis. Clidinium should be used cautiously in patients with autonomic neuropathy. Use anticholinergics cautiously in patients with Down's syndrome, brain damage or spastic paralysis.
 
Clidinium can block the actions of acetylcholine on the heart, resulting in tachycardia. Chlordiazepoxide; clidinium should be used cautiously in patients with hyperthyroidism, coronary artery disease, congestive heart failure, hypertension, or cardiac arrhythmias because the drug could worsen these conditions.
 
Patients receiving chlordiazepoxide; clidinium should be cautioned about exposure to hot or humid environments because of the risk of ambient temperature increase and heat stroke.
 
The administration of chlordiazepoxide can exacerbate acute intermittent porphyria, so chlordiazepoxide; clidinium should be used with caution in patients with this condition.
 
Chlordiazepoxide; clidinium is contraindicated in patients with glaucoma.Mydriasis and increased intraocular pressure may occur due to the anticholinergic effects of clidinium. Clidinium is less likely than some other anticholinergics to affect the pupil or ciliary muscle of the eye.

    coadministration with certain medications, pulmonary disease, respiratory insufficiency
    General Information
    Chlordiazepoxide; clidinium coadministration with certain medications, such as opioids, may result in serious adverse effects, including profound sedation, CNS depression, respiratory depression, coma, and death. The concomitant use of these medications has demonstrated an increased risk of drug-related mortality compared to the use of opioids alone. The combined use of opioids and benzodiazepines should be reserved for patients in whom alternative treatment options are inadequate. If the decision is made to prescribe chlordiazepoxide; clidinium concomitantly with opioids, the lowest effective dose should be prescribed for the shortest duration of use, and patients should be followed closely for signs and symptoms of respiratory depression and sedation. Advise patients and caregivers about the risks of respiratory depression and sedation when chlordiazepoxide; clidinium is used with opioids. Deaths have been reported in people with severe pulmonary disease after initiation of chlordiazepoxide; closely monitor patients with impaired respiratory function (respiratory insufficiency). If signs and symptoms of respiratory depression, hypoventilation, or apnea occur, discontinue chlordiazepoxide; clidinium.
    Headline
    activities requiring coordination and concentration, driving or operating machinery
    General Information
    Chlordiazepoxide; clidinium has the potential to impair judgment, thinking, or motor skills. Individuals should use caution when driving or operating machinery or participating in other activities requiring coordination and concentration until they know how this combination may affect them. Some patients may experience excessive sedation and impaired ability to perform tasks. Given the primary CNS effects of chlordiazepoxide, use caution during coadministration with other CNS depressants. Alcohol may further impair cognitive and motor skills and can increase the risk of serious CNS or respiratory depression, and alcohol should be avoided during chlordiazepoxide; clidinium treatment.
    Headline
    abrupt discontinuation, increased risk for benzodiazepine withdrawal, substance abuse disorder
    General Information
    The use of benzodiazepines, including chlordiazepoxide; clidinium, exposes users to serious adverse events, including risks of abuse, misuse, and addiction, which can lead to overdose or death. Abuse and misuse of benzodiazepines commonly involve concomitant use of other medications, alcohol, and/or illicit substances, which can increase the frequency of serious adverse outcomes. Before prescribing and throughout treatment, assess each patient's risk for abuse, misuse, and addiction using a standardized screening tool. Counsel patients about the risks and proper use of chlordiazepoxide; clidinium and prescribe the lowest effective dose to reduce excess exposure and advise patients on proper disposal of unused medication. Minimize the concomitant use of CNS depressants or other substances associated with abuse, misuse, and addiction (e.g., opioid analgesics, stimulants). If a substance abuse disorder is suspected, evaluate the patient and institute or refer them for early treatment. Chronic, continued use of benzodiazepines may also lead to physical benzodiazepine dependence. The risk of dependence increases with longer treatment duration and higher daily doses and may lead to an increased risk for benzodiazepine withdrawal upon discontinuation. Abrupt discontinuation or rapid dose reduction can precipitate acute withdrawal reactions, which can be life-threatening. To reduce the risk of withdrawal reactions, use a gradual taper to reduce the dose or discontinue chlordiazepoxide; clidinium. In some cases, patients using benzodiazepines have developed a protracted withdrawal syndrome with symptoms lasting weeks to more than 12 months. Patients with a history of a seizure disorder should not be withdrawn abruptly from benzodiazepines due to the risk of precipitating seizures; status epilepticus has also been reported. Clinicians should be aware that the use of flumazenil may increase the risk of seizures, particularly in long-term users of benzodiazepines.

    Headline
    geriatric
    General Information
    Delayed elimination of these medications can either intensify or prolong the actions or adverse reactions of chlordiazepoxide; clidinium in the geriatric adult. In geriatric and debilitated individuals, use the smallest effective dosage; gradually increase as needed and tolerated only with close clinical monitoring. Because chlordiazepoxide can cause drowsiness and a decreased level of consciousness, there is a higher risk of falls, particularly in the elderly, with the potential for subsequent severe injuries. The Beers Criteria state that chlordiazepoxide; clidinium is a potentially inappropriate medication in geriatric adults and should be avoided due to the high anticholinergic properties of clidinium. Geriatric adults have an increased sensitivity to benzodiazepines and slower metabolism of long-acting agents such as chlordiazepoxide, which increases the risk of cognitive impairment, delirium, falls, fractures, and motor vehicle accidents. Avoid use in those with the following conditions due to the potential for symptom exacerbation or adverse effects: dementia/cognitive impairment (drug-induced adverse CNS effects), delirium/high risk of delirium (new-onset or worsening delirium), or lower urinary tract symptoms/benign prostatic hyperplasia in men (urinary retention or hesitancy with anticholinergic agents). Avoid use in geriatric adults with a history of falls or fractures, unless safer alternatives are not available since benzodiazepines can produce ataxia, impaired psychomotor function, syncope, and additional falls. If a benzodiazepine must be used, consider reducing the use of other CNS-active medications and implement other strategies to reduce fall risk.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Published data from observational studies of benzodiazepine use during pregnancy have not established a drug-associated risk of major birth defects. Early studies suggested an increased risk of congenital malformations with chlordiazepoxide and diazepam use. However, more recent case-control and cohort studies of benzodiazepine use during pregnancy, adjusted for confounding exposures to other substances, have not confirmed these findings. Benzodiazepines cross the placenta. Neonates exposed to benzodiazepines during late pregnancy, including during labor and delivery, may experience symptoms of sedation. Those with prolonged exposure during late pregnancy may experience withdrawal after delivery. The incidence, time to onset, and duration of sedation and withdrawal are multifactorial (e.g., duration of use, drug lipophilicity, placental disposition, degree of accumulation in neonatal tissues). Chlordiazepoxide is not a preferred benzodiazepine to use during pregnancy because of its long duration of action and active metabolites. Individuals who become pregnant or intend to become pregnant while taking chlordiazepoxide should be advised to discuss the possibility of discontinuing the drug with their physician. If a benzodiazepine is required during pregnancy, avoid first trimester administration if possible, consider short-acting agents, limit treatment to the shortest possible duration and lowest effective dose, and discontinue the drug well before delivery. No published data are available to evaluate the risk of major birth defects, miscarriage, or other adverse developmental outcomes associated with clidinium use during pregnancy.
    Headline
    neonates and infants exposed to this medication in utero
    General Information
    Monitor neonates and infants exposed to this medication in utero for symptoms of withdrawal, such as hyperreflexia, irritability, restlessness, tremors, inconsolable crying, and feeding difficulties. In neonates exposed during late pregnancy, including during labor and delivery, monitor for sedation, including respiratory depression, lethargy, and hypotonia. If symptoms are present, manage accordingly.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Avoid the use of chlordiazepoxide; clinidium during breast-feeding. Chlordiazepoxide and its metabolites are excreted into human milk in moderate concentrations. Based on its long half-life and active metabolites, other benzodiazepines are preferred in lactation. Monitor children exposed to benzodiazepines through breast milk for excessive sedation, slowed breathing rate, poor feeding, and inadequate weight gain. Withdrawal may occur in those with long-term exposure to the drug. This may occur with cessation of breast-feeding or cessation of the medication in the breast-feeding person. There are no data on the presence of clidinium in human or animal milk, its effects on the breastfed child, or its effects on milk production. Anticholinergic agents may reduce the production of human milk.

Description
  Text
  Chlordiazepoxide and clidinium are a long-acting benzodiazepine and an antimuscarinic, respectively, used together in an oral preparation to control emotional and somatic factors in gastrointestinal disorders. Chlordiazepoxide; clidinium may also be used as adjunctive therapy to treat GI-related problems such as irritable bowel syndrome and peptic ulcer disease. Chlordiazepoxide; clidinium does not aid in the healing of peptic ulcers and other, clinically proven therapies should be used for active ulcer disease. The use of this combination for the treatment of acute enterocolitis is not recommended as the drug may lead to a reduction in GI motility resulting in retention of the GI toxin. Doses of each ingredient should be titrated and individualized, especially in older adults. As with all benzodiazepines, product labels carry a boxed warning about a risk of fatal respiratory depression particularly when used with opioids, as well as risks for substance abuse, misuse and addiction, and withdrawal reactions with discontinuation. The combination product of chlordiazepoxide; clidinium was initially FDA approved in 1966.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Chlordiazepoxide and clidinium are combined in a single formulation for antianxiety effects (chlordiazepoxide) and anticholinergic/antispasmolytic effects (clidinium). The additive effects of these two drugs may help to control the emotional and somatic factors that appear to be important in functional gastrointestinal disorders.
•Chlordiazepoxide: Benzodiazepines act at the level of the limbic, thalamic, and hypothalamic regions of the CNS to produce CNS depression. Recent evidence indicates that benzodiazepines exert their effects through enhancement of the gamma-aminobutyric acid (GABA)-benzodiazepine receptor complex. GABA is an inhibitory neurotransmitter that exerts its effects at specific receptor subtypes designated GABA-A and GABA-B. GABA-A is the primary receptor subtype in the CNS and is thought to be involved in the actions of anxiolytics and sedatives.
•Clidinium: Clidinium is an anticholinergic and antisecretory agent used to decrease motility in the GI, urinary, and biliary tracts. Clidinium, a quaternary compound, is structurally related to belladonna alkaloids. Clidinium is a competitive inhibitor at autonomic postganglionic cholinergic receptors, located in many tissues including the GI tract. The principal clinical effects of antimuscarinics such as clidinium are a reduction in salivary, bronchial, and sweat gland secretions; mydriasis; cycloplegia; changes in heart rate; contraction of the bladder detrusor muscle and of the GI smooth muscle; decreased gastric secretion; and decreased GI motility. The early spike increase in rectosigmoid activity following a meal (also known as the gastrocolic response) is inhibited by clidinium; this reduction in rectosigmoid activity may be useful in irritable bowel syndrome. At lower doses, a paradoxical decrease in heart rate occurs. At higher doses, effects are seen at nicotinic receptors in autonomic ganglia, causing restlessness, hallucinations, disorientation, and/or delirium.

Chlordiazepoxide: Chlordiazepoxide distributes throughout the body and is about 96% protein-bound. It crosses the CSF, placenta and is distributed into breast milk. The elimination half-life is biphasic with an initial half-life of 2.4 hours and a terminal half-life of 20 hours. Dosage Adjustment Guidelines
  Hepatic Impairment
  Specific guidelines for dosage adjustments are not available; use lower initial dosages and monitor closely for oversedation or other adverse effects. Use chlordiazepoxide in patients with significant hepatic disease with extreme caution; there is a high risk of drug accumulation with repeated dosing.
  Renal Impairment
  eGFR 10 mL/minute or more: Use with caution. It is advisable to start with low initial dosage and adjust to response and tolerance in patients with renal impairment.
eGFR less than 10 mL/minute or end-stage renal disease (ESRD): Reduce chlordiazepoxide dosage by 50%. Monitor closely for excessive sedation and other adverse effects. Use the fixed dose combination of chlordiazepoxide; clidinium product with caution.
