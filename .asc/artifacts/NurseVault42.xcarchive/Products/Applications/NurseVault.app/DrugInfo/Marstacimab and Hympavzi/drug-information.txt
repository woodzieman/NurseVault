Indication Specific Dosing
  General Information
  General Dosing Information
When changing from prophylactic factor replacement therapy to marstacimab, discontinue treatment with clotting factor concentrates (factor VIII or factor IX concentrates) or bypassing agents [e.g., recombinant factor VIIa or activated prothrombin complex concentrate (aPCC)] prior to initiation of marstacimab; may initiate marstacimab at any time after discontinuation of the clotting factor concentrates or bypassing agents.
No data are available in individuals changing from non-factor-based hemophilia medications to marstacimab.
Factor VIII products, factor IX products, or bypassing agents [e.g., recombinant factor VIIa or activated prothrombin complex concentrate (aPCC)] can be administered for the treatment of breakthrough bleeding in individuals receiving marstacimab. Do not use additional doses of marstacimab to treat breakthrough bleeds.
Discuss the dose and schedule of clotting factor concentrates or bypassing agents to use, if required, while receiving marstacimab prophylaxis, including utilizing the lowest possible effective dose of clotting factor concentrate or bypassing agents, with all individuals and/or caregivers.

    Headline
    thrombophilia
    General Information
    Consider the benefit and risk of marstacimab in individuals with thrombophilia or known risk factors for thromboembolism. If factor VIII or factor IX products are indicated in an individual receiving marstacimab prophylaxis, the minimum effective dose of factor VIII or factor IX according to the product label is recommended. Individuals with independent risk factors for thromboembolic events may be at increased risk for thromboembolic events with marstacimab use. Marstacimab has not been studied in individuals with a history of previous thromboembolic events.
    Headline
    planned procedure
    General Information
    Marstacimab has not been evaluated in individuals undergoing major surgery or a planned procedure. In clinical trials, minor surgical procedures were performed without discontinuing marstacimab prophylaxis. For major surgery, discontinue marstacimab at least 7 days before the procedure and initiate management according to local standards of care using clotting factor concentrates or bypassing agents, as appropriate, along with measures to reduce the risk of perioperative venous thrombosis. Consult the prescribing information for the selected clotting factor concentrate or bypassing agent for dosing recommendations in individuals with hemophilia undergoing major surgery. Consider the individual's overall clinical status, including post-surgical thromboembolic risk factors and concomitant use of hemostatic products or other medications, when determining when to resume marstacimab therapy.
    Headline
    sepsis, trauma
    General Information
    Consider temporary dose interruption of marstacimab in individuals with critical illness (e.g., serious infection, sepsis, trauma) in which there may be increased activation of coagulation and which the health care provider considers could increase the risks associated with marstacimab administration. There is limited experience with the use of marstacimab in individuals with acute severe illness. Manage acute severe illness per local standard of care. Weigh continued treatment with marstacimab against the potential risks involved. Resume marstacimab therapy once the individual has clinically recovered.
    Headline
    immune tolerance induction associated with hemophilia treatment
    General Information
    The safety and efficacy of concomitant use of marstacimab in individuals receiving ongoing immune tolerance induction associated with hemophilia treatment, a desensitization strategy for the eradication of inhibitors, have not been established, and no data are available. Carefully assess the potential benefits and risks if continuation or initiation of marstacimab during immune tolerance induction is considered.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Based on its mechanism of action, marstacimab may cause fetal harm when administered during pregnancy. There are no available data on marstacimab use during pregnancy to evaluate for a drug-associated risk of major birth defects, miscarriage, or other adverse maternal or fetal outcomes. Female animal reproduction studies have not been conducted with marstacimab. Although there are no data on marstacimab, monoclonal antibodies can be actively transported across the placenta and marstacimab may cause fetal harm.

    antibody formation
    General Information
    During the 12-month active treatment phase of a clinical trial in individuals 12 years and older, anti-drug antibody (ADA) formation occurred in 33 of 167 (19.8%) ADA-evaluable marstacimab-treated subjects. Among the 33 subjects with antibody formation, 8 (24.2%) developed neutralizing antibodies (NAbs). Development of anti-marstacimab antibodies was associated with reduced marstacimab steady-state concentrations, with geometric mean decreases ranging from 19% to 47% compared with subjects who did not develop antibodies. In a clinical trial of pediatric patients 6 to 17 years of age, 14 of 91 (15.4%) ADA-evaluable subjects developed ADAs, including 4 of 24 (16.7%) adolescents 12 to 17 years of age and 10 of 67 (14.9%) children 6 to 11 years of age. Among ADA-positive pediatric subjects, 5 of 14 (35.7%) developed NAbs. In adolescents who developed ADAs, marstacimab steady-state concentrations decreased by a geometric mean of 28% to 64%; in children 6 to 11 years of age who developed ADAs, steady-state concentrations increased by a geometric mean of 45% to 113%, although exposure was comparable between ADA-positive and ADA-negative subjects after accounting for body weight. No clinically significant effects of ADAs, including NAbs, on the safety or efficacy of marstacimab were identified during 12 months of treatment, and the overall safety profile was similar in subjects with and without ADAs.

Description
  Text
  Marstacimab is an anti-tissue pathway factor inhibitor indicated for routine prophylaxis to prevent or reduce the frequency of bleeding episodes in individuals with hemophilia A with or without factor VIII inhibitors or hemophilia B with or without factor IX inhibitors. Factor VIII, factor IX products, or bypassing agents [e.g., recombinant factor VIIa or activated prothrombin complex concentrate (aPCC)] can be administered for the treatment of breakthrough bleeding in individuals receiving marstacimab. Marstacimab may increase the risk of thromboembolic events. Marstacimab was FDA-approved in October 2024.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Marstacimab is a human monoclonal immunoglobulin G1 (IgG1) antibody directed against the Kunitz domain 2 (K2) of tissue factor pathway inhibitor (TFPI) to neutralize TFPI activity and enhance coagulation. TFPI is the primary inhibitor of the extrinsic coagulation cascade and negatively regulates thrombin generation within the extrinsic pathway of coagulation by inactivating the protease functions of factor Xa/factor VIIa/tissue factor complex. TFPI binds to and inhibits the factor Xa active site via its second Kunitz inhibitor domain (K2).
Pharmacodynamics
Marstacimab causes an increase in total TFPI (comprised of free TFPI and TFPI bound to marstacimab) and downstream biomarkers of thrombin generation such as prothrombin fragments 1+2, peak thrombin, and D-dimer in individuals with hemophilia. These changes were observed and persisted over a 7-day time frame after a single subcutaneous dose and were reversible after treatment discontinuation. Sporadic or transient increases in D-dimer and prothrombin fragment 1+2 above physiologic values were observed without safety concerns. No clinically meaningful differences in pharmacodynamic effects were observed between individuals with and without inhibitors or across age groups following once-weekly subcutaneous administration.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Subcutaneous Route
    Description
    The bioavailability of marstacimab after subcutaneous administration is approximately 71%. The median Tmax ranges from 23 to 59 hours after multiple subcutaneous administrations in individuals with hemophilia. No clinically significant differences in marstacimab bioavailability were observed when administered subcutaneously in the arm, thigh, or abdomen. Estimated steady-state Cmin and Cmax after once-weekly administration of marstacimab 150 mg (with a 300 mg subcutaneous loading dose) were 13.2 and 17.7 mcg/mL, respectively, in adults without inhibitors and 16.1 and 21.9 mcg/mL, respectively, in adults with inhibitors. In adults and adolescents, marstacimab AUC and Cmax increase in a greater-than-dose-proportional manner over the dose range of 100 to 450 mg (0.67 to 3 times the approved recommended dose). The mean steady-state accumulation ratio is approximately 4 to 5, and steady-state concentrations are achieved by approximately 60 days (eighth or ninth weekly subcutaneous dose).

  Text
  Marstacimab is administered subcutaneously. The steady-state apparent Vd of marstacimab is 6 L in individuals with hemophilia. It is expected to be metabolized into small peptides and amino acids by catabolic pathways in the same manner as endogenous immunoglobulin G (IgG). Marstacimab is cleared by linear and non-linear mechanisms and exhibits non-linear pharmacokinetics due to target-mediated drug disposition, which occurs when it forms the marstacimab-tissue factor pathway inhibitor complex. Once the target becomes saturated, the linear pathway (i.e., catabolism) dominates. Based on population pharmacokinetic analysis in adults and adolescents, 90% of marstacimab is expected to be eliminated by the end of approximately 1 month after the last dose (median time for 50% of drug to be eliminated is approximately 7 to 11 days).
 
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes and drug transporters: none

  Route Specific Administrations
    Administration Details
      Name
      Subcutaneous Administration
      Description
      After proper training in subcutaneous injection technique, an individual 12 years and older may self-inject or the individual's caregiver may administer marstacimab if a health care provider determines that it is appropriate.
Prior to subcutaneous administration, remove marstacimab from the refrigerator and allow it to warm at room temperature up to 30 degrees C (86 degrees F) in the carton for 15 to 30 minutes, protected from direct sunlight. Do not warm by using a heat source, such as hot water or a microwave.
It is normal to see air bubbles in the syringe or pen.
Choose an injection site on the abdomen (at least 2 inches away from the belly button) or front of thigh. Other injection sites are acceptable if required.
Administration in the back of upper arm (prefilled syringe only) or buttocks (prefilled pen only) must be performed by a caregiver or health care provider.
Clean the injection site with soap and water, or an alcohol swab, and allow the site to dry. Do not touch, fan, or blow on the clean injection site.
Administer by subcutaneous injection, once weekly, at any time of the day.
Do not administer into bony areas or areas where the skin is bruised, red, tender or hard, or areas where there are scars or stretch marks. Do not inject into a vein or inject through clothes.
If more than 1 injection is required to deliver a complete dose, administer each injection at a different injection site. Rotate the injection site with each new injection.
During treatment with marstacimab, administer other medications for subcutaneous administration at different anatomical sites.
After administration, press lightly on the injection site for a few seconds with a clean cotton ball or gauze pad. Do not rub the injection site.
Dispose of the used prefilled syringe or pen in an appropriate sharps disposal container.
Storage: If needed, marstacimab may be stored at room temperature, up to 30 degrees C (86 degrees F) in the original carton for up to 7 days. Do not return marstacimab to the refrigerator after storing at room temperature. Discard marstacimab that has been left out of the refrigerator for more than 7 days. Do not freeze. Protect from light.
 
Single-Dose, Prefilled Syringe
Hold the syringe by the barrel and pull the needle cover straight off carefully. Dispose of the needle cover into a sharps disposal container right away. Do not touch the needle or let it touch any surfaces.
Pinch the clean skin between the thumb and fingers to create a firm surface. Fully insert the needle into the skin at a 45-degree angle. Do not hold or push on the plunger while inserting the needle. Keep the skin pinched throughout the injection.
Slowly inject the dose by gently pushing the plunger rod all the way down until the barrel is empty.
Count slowly to 5 after the plunger rod has been fully pushed down before removing the needle from the skin.
Pull the needle and syringe out of the skin at the same angle as inserted. Check the syringe to ensure the gray plunger is at the bottom of the syringe.
 
Single-Dose, Prefilled Pen
Twist and pull off the cap. Dispose of the cap into a sharps disposal container right away. The needle cover will stay inside the cap after cap removal.
Handle the pen with care as it contains a needle. Do not touch the needle guard; this may cause a needle injury.
Hold the pen straight at a 90-degree angle against the clean skin so that the window is visible.
Push the pen down firmly straight against the skin and keep pushing until the injection is complete. A first click will be heard when the injection starts.
If a click is not heard when pushing the pen against the skin, try pushing down harder. If the injection is still not able to be started, obtain a new marstacimab prefilled pen.
If a decision is made to change the injection site after inserting the needle into the skin, dispose of the pen and obtain a new marstacimab prefilled pen.
Keep pushing the pen firmly against the skin while the yellow bar moves across the window. A second click will be heard when the injection is almost complete.
Count slowly to 5 after hearing the second click to ensure the full dose is administered.
Remove the pen from the skin after slowly counting to 5 and hearing the second click, as well as ensuring the yellow marker completely fills the window.
After removing the pen from the skin, the needle guard will automatically cover the needle and lock in place.
Check the window to ensure all the medication has been injected. If the yellow bar is not at the bottom of the syringe, the full dose has not been administered.
The pen cannot be reused.

Missed dose:
Adults and Children/Adolescents 12 to 17 years
If a dose is missed, administer the dose as soon as possible and resume the usual once-weekly dosing schedule (either the original schedule or a new schedule based on the date the missed dose was administered).
For individuals receiving a maintenance dose of 150 mg, if more than 13 days have elapsed since the last dose was administered, administer a 300 mg subcutaneous loading dose, then resume 150 mg subcutaneously once weekly thereafter.
For individuals receiving a maintenance dose of 300 mg, no additional loading dose is required.
Children 6 to 11 years
If a dose is missed, administer the dose as soon as possible and resume the usual once-weekly dosing schedule (either the original schedule or a new schedule based on the date the missed dose was administered).
For individuals receiving a maintenance dose of 75 mg, if more than 13 days have elapsed since the last dose was administered, administer a 150 mg subcutaneous loading dose, then resume 75 mg subcutaneously once weekly thereafter.
For individuals receiving a maintenance dose of 150 mg, no additional loading dose is required.
