Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  General Information
  Glatiramer acetate is contraindicated in patients with known glatiramer hypersensitivity or mannitol hypersensitivity. The only recommended route of administration is subcutaneously; glatiramer should not be given by intravenous administration.
 
Use glatiramer acetate cautiously in patients with preexisting immunosuppression or those who are receiving vaccination. Glatiramer acetate can modify the human immune response. It should be kept in mind that glatiramer could interfere with useful immune function. The manufacturer theorizes that glatiramer might interfere with the body's recognition of foreign antigens in a way that would undermine the body's defenses against infection and tumor surveillance. Although glatiramer is intended to minimize the autoimmune response to myelin, there is the possibility that continued alteration of cellular immunity due to chronic treatment with glatiramer might lead to the induction of host responses that can cause adverse events. Results of limited tests reveal no risk of glatiramer-induced suppression of human immune function; however, there is no way to absolutely exclude this possibility.
 
Glatiramer has not been specifically studied in the elderly.
 
Glatiramer acetate is classified as FDA pregnancy risk category B. There are no adequate and well-controlled studies in pregnant women. No adverse effects were noted in animal reproduction studies. Because animal reproduction studies are not always predictive of human response, glatiramer acetate should be used during pregnancy only if clearly needed.
 
It is unknown whether glatiramer acetate is excreted in human milk. Because many drugs are excreted in breast milk, caution should be exercised when administering the drug to breast-feeding women.
 
The safety and effectiveness of glatiramer acetate in children < 18 years of age have not been established.

    Headline
    serious hypersensitivity reactions or anaphylaxis
    General Information
    Glatiramer is contraindicated in people with known glatiramer hypersensitivity, including serious hypersensitivity reactions or anaphylaxis. Life-threatening and fatal anaphylaxis (e.g., wheezing or difficulty breathing, swelling of the face, lips, throat, hives, and severe rash) have been reported with glatiramer. Anaphylaxis generally appears within 1 hour of injection, and in some cases, results in hospitalization and death. Initial symptoms of anaphylaxis can overlap with immediate post-injection reactions (e.g., flushing, chest pain, palpitations, anxiety, shortness of breath, rash or hives), typically occurring within minutes of administration. Immediate post-injection reactions are generally transient, self-limited, and usually resolve without specific treatment within 15 to 30 minutes. Anaphylaxis symptoms are more severe, worsen over time, and require urgent medical attention. Anaphylaxis and post-injection reactions can occur at any time during treatment, including after the first dose or after doses administered months or years after initiation. Educate people about the signs and symptoms of anaphylaxis and immediate post-injection reactions. People experiencing anaphylaxis after glatiramer administration should seek immediate medical attention by going to an emergency room or calling 911. Do not restart glatiramer in people who experience anaphylaxis unless a clear alternative etiology is identified.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Data from pharmacovigilance and published observational studies have not identified a drug-associated risk of major birth defects, miscarriage, or other adverse outcomes associated with glatiramer use during pregnancy. However, published comparative observational studies have methodological limitations (e.g., short exposure duration during pregnancy, confounding, selection bias, and exposure misclassification). In animals exposed to glatiramer during organogenesis, no adverse effects on embryo-fetal or offspring development were observed. Glatiramer should be used during pregnancy only if necessary. If an individual becomes pregnant while receiving glatiramer therapy, counsel them on the benefits and risks of continuing treatment for multiple sclerosis (MS). In many cases, disease-modifying drugs for MS are discontinued during pregnancy. Data from a large, multicenter trial of pregnant individuals with MS suggest that patients have a decrease in the number and severity of MS relapses during pregnancy, despite discontinuation or lack of receipt of disease-modifying medications. Experts generally recommend the discontinuation of glatiramer when trying to conceive and throughout gestation. However, in pregnant individuals with severe or highly active MS, the benefit of glatiramer treatment may outweigh the possible risk to the fetus.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Glatiramer is considered compatible with breast-feeding. There are no data on the presence of glatiramer in human milk, its effects on the breastfed child, or its effects on milk production. Based on the low systemic exposure, breast-feeding is not expected to result in clinically relevant exposure of the infant to the drug. Consider the benefits of breast-feeding, the individual's clinical need for treatment, and any potential adverse effects on the breastfed child from the medication or from the individual's underlying medical condition.

Interactions
Alemtuzumab: (Major) Concomitant use of glatiramer with alemtuzumab may increase the risk of immunosuppression. Avoid the use of these drugs together.
Cholera Vaccine: (Moderate) Patients receiving immunosuppressant medications may have a diminished response to the live cholera vaccine. When feasible, administer indicated vaccines prior to initiating immunosuppressant medications. Counsel patients receiving immunosuppressant medications about the possibility of a diminished vaccine response and to continue to follow precautions to avoid exposure to cholera bacteria after receiving the vaccine.
Dengue Tetravalent Vaccine, Live: (Contraindicated) Avoid administration of the live dengue virus vaccine with immunosuppressive drug therapy and prior to immune recovery following treatment with immunosuppressive drug therapy. When feasible, administer indicated live virus vaccines at least 4 weeks before planned immunosuppression or wait until at least 3 months after discontinuation. The time to restoration of immune competence may be longer in some patients. Patients with altered immunocompetence may be at increased risk for severe adverse reactions due to uninhibited growth of the attenuated live virus. Additionally, vaccines may be less effective if administered during a period of altered immunocompetence.
Ocrelizumab: (Moderate) Ocrelizumab has not been studied in combination with other immunosuppressive or immune modulating therapies used for the treatment of multiple sclerosis, such as glatiramer. Concomitant use of ocrelizumab with glatiramer may increase the risk of immunosuppression. Avoid the use of these drugs together.
Ocrelizumab; Hyaluronidase: (Moderate) Ocrelizumab has not been studied in combination with other immunosuppressive or immune modulating therapies used for the treatment of multiple sclerosis, such as glatiramer. Concomitant use of ocrelizumab with glatiramer may increase the risk of immunosuppression. Avoid the use of these drugs together.
Ofatumumab: (Moderate) Concomitant use of ofatumumab with glatiramer may increase the risk of immunosuppression. Ofatumumab has not been studied in combination with other immunosuppressive or immune modulating therapies used for the treatment of multiple sclerosis, such as glatiramer. Consider the duration and mechanism of action of drugs with immunosuppressive effects when switching therapies for multiple sclerosis patients.
Ozanimod: (Moderate) Concomitant use of ozanimod with glatiramer may increase the risk of immunosuppression. Ozanimod has not been studied in combination with other immunosuppressive or immune modulating therapies used for the treatment of multiple sclerosis. Ozanimod can generally be started immediately after discontinuation of glatiramer.

Adverse Reactions
  Headline
  Adverse Reactions
  General Information
  An immediate post-injection reaction occurred in approximately 10% of patients with multiple sclerosis who received glatiramer during premarketing studies. The reaction consisted of a constellation of symptoms which included flushing, chest pain (unspecified), palpitations, anxiety, dyspnea, constriction of the throat, and urticaria. The symptoms were usually transient and self-limiting and did not require specific treatment. In general, these symptoms have an onset several months after the start of glatiramer treatment; however, they may occur earlier in the course of treatment. Approximately 26% of patients treated with glatiramer experienced at least one episode of chest pain (unspecified). While some of the episodes occurred immediately post-injection, many did not. The pain was transient, usually only lasting a few minutes, often unassociated with other symptoms, and appeared to have no important clinical sequelea. Some patients experienced more than one episode, and episodes usually began at least 1 month after initiating therapy.The most commonly observed adverse reactions in controlled clinical trials of glatiramer were injection site reaction (pain, erythema, inflammation, pruritus, mass, induration, welt, hemorrhage, urticaria), transient flushing, vasodilatation, chest tightness, asthenia, infection, pain, nausea/vomiting, arthralgia, anxiety, and hypertonia.Other adverse reactions which occurred in at least 2% of patients treated with glatiramer include acne, agitation, anorexia, back pain, bronchitis, chills, confusion, depression, diaphoresis, diarrhea, dizziness, dysmenorrhea, ear pain, ecchymosis, eye disorder, facial edema, fever, flu-like syndrome, gastroenteritis, headache, laryngospasm, lymphadenopathy, neck pain, nervousness, nystagmus, peripheral edema, rash (unspecified), rhinitis, skin nodule, speech disorder, syncope, urinary urgency, vertigo, and weight gain. All these adverse reactions have been reported more frequently than placebo.Other adverse reactions reported in 1% of patients during all clinical trials include injection site edema, injection site atrophy, abscess, hypertension, bowel urgency, oral candidiasis, salivary gland enlargement, tooth caries, ulcerative stomatitis, abnormal dreams, emotional liability, stupor, hyperventilation, eczema, herpes zoster, pustular rash, skin atrophy, warts, amenorrhea, hematuria, impotence, menorrhagia, suspicious pap smear, and vaginal hemorrhage.

    General Information
    An immediate post-injection reaction occurred in approximately 2% to 16% of people during placebo-controlled trials who received glatiramer. This reaction occurs within seconds to minutes, with the majority of symptoms seen within 1 hour after injection. The reaction consisted of a constellation of symptoms that included at least 2 of the following: flushing, chest pain (unspecified), palpitations, tachycardia, anxiety, dyspnea, throat constriction, and urticaria. The symptoms were usually transient and self-limiting and did not require specific treatment. Symptoms of an immediate post-injection reaction may overlap with those of anaphylaxis. Prompt identification of anaphylaxis is important to avoid delay in treatment. It is unknown if any of these symptoms represent a specific syndrome. Also, it is unknown if an immunologic or nonimmunologic mechanism mediates these episodes or if several similar episodes seen in a given individual have identical mechanisms. Approximately 2% to 13% of people treated with glatiramer experienced at least 1 episode of transient chest pain (unspecified). Some of the episodes occurred immediately post-injection, but many did not. The pain was often not associated with other symptoms and appeared to have no significant clinical sequelae. Some people experienced more than 1 episode, and episodes usually began at least 1 month after initiating therapy. The pathogenesis of this symptom is unknown. People who received 40 mg three times per week (n = 943) during a controlled clinical trial also experienced dyspnea (3%).

    Glatiramer may interfere with the recognition of foreign antigens in a way that would undermine the body's defenses against infection. A systematic evaluation of this risk is unavailable. Description
  Text
  Glatiramer acetate (formerly known as copolymer-1) is a synthetic random peptide created by polymerizing L-alanine, L-glutamic acid, L-lysine, and L-tyrosine and is similar in structure to myelin basic protein. Glatiramer is given subcutaneously and is used to reduce the frequency of attacks in adults with relapsing forms of multiple sclerosis (MS), including clinically isolated syndrome, relapsing-remitting disease, and active secondary progressive disease. Glatiramer is often 1 of the medications used off-label in children and adolescents with MS. In a phase 3 study with a 2-year follow-up, glatiramer 20 mg daily produced a 29% reduction in relapse rate, prolonged the time to relapse, and increased the number of individuals who were relapse-free. In a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial, use of 40 mg 3 times weekly significantly reduced relapses compared to placebo, with a relative risk reduction of 34% over 1 year. Glatiramer has been associated with significant reductions in cumulative numbers of MS-related lesions on MRI scans. The use of glatiramer delays the time to clinically definite multiple sclerosis (CDMS) among individuals who have experienced a first clinical episode and have MRI results consistent with multiple sclerosis. A clinical trial (the PROMiSE trial) of glatiramer in adults with primary progressive multiple sclerosis was terminated after an interim evaluation suggested the drug was unlikely to demonstrate a significant benefit. Cases of anaphylaxis (e.g., wheezing or difficulty breathing, swelling of the face, lips, or throat, hives, and severe rash), some resulting in hospitalization and death, have been reported with glatiramer. Immediate post-injection reactions (e.g., flushing, chest pain, dyspnea, and urticaria) that were generally transient and resolved without specific treatment have also been reported. Initial symptoms associated with anaphylaxis and immediate post-injection reactions may overlap. Anaphylaxis and immediate post-injection reactions can occur at any time during treatment, including after the first dose or after doses administered months or years after initiation.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Glatiramer acetate modifies immune processes that are responsible for the pathogenesis of multiple sclerosis (MS). The exact mechanism of action in treating MS is unclear at this time. It is proposed that the drug serves as a decoy to locally-generated autoantibodies. These antibodies, along with certain T-cells, are thereby neutralized before they can cause tissue damage. Studies in animals and in vitro systems suggest that glatiramer-specific suppressor T-cells are induced and activated in the periphery. Because glatiramer modifies immune functions, concerns exist about its potential to alter naturally occurring immune responses. Results of limited tests reveal no risk of this concern; however, there is no way to absolutely exclude this possibility.

    Description
    It is assumed that a substantial fraction of glatiramer acetate is hydrolyzed locally after subcutaneous injection. Nevertheless, larger fragments of glatiramer acetate can be recognized by glatiramer-reactive antibodies. Some fraction of the injected material, either intact or partially hydrolyzed, is presumed to enter the lymphatic circulation thereby enabling it to reach regional lymph nodes, and some of the injected material may enter the systemic circulation intact.

Subcutaneous Injection:
If refrigerated, remove the prefilled syringe from the refrigerator and allow the solution to warm for 20 minutes at room temperature. Visually inspect the product before use.
There may be small air bubbles in the syringe. Do not try to push the air bubble from the syringe before administering the injection.
Sites for injection include upper arms, abdomen, hips, and thighs.
Pinch about a 2-inch fold of skin at the chosen site. Insert the needle at a 90-degree angle straight into the skin. Slowly inject the entire dose. Pull the needle straight out when finished.
Rotate injection sites with each injection. Do not use any injection site more than once a week.  This is important to reduce the risk for lipoatrophy and skin necrosis at the injection sites.
The prefilled syringe is for single use only and does not contain preservatives; use immediately and discard unused portions. Discard of used needles and syringes in an appropriate sharps container.
