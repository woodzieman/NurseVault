    Headline
    amyloid-related imaging abnormalities, cerebral amyloid angiopathy, cerebral edema, intracerebral hemorrhage, superficial siderosis
    General Information
    Clinicians should carefully weigh the potential benefit of donanemab treatment against the risk of amyloid-related imaging abnormalities (ARIA) and serious hemorrhages when considering use. Monoclonal antibodies targeting aggregated beta amyloid, like donanemab, can cause ARIA. These may be characterized as ARIA with edema (ARIA-E), which can be observed on brain magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) as cerebral edema or sulcal effusions, and ARIA with hemosiderin deposition (ARIA-H), which includes microhemorrhage (small areas of intracranial bleeding) and superficial siderosis. ARIA can occur spontaneously in individuals with Alzheimer disease, particularly in patients with MRI findings suggestive of cerebral amyloid angiopathy (CAA). ARIA often occurs early in treatment and is usually asymptomatic, although serious and life-threatening events (including seizures and status epilepticus) can occur. ARIA can be fatal. When present, ARIA symptoms may include headache, confusion, visual changes, dizziness, nausea, gait disturbance, or focal neurologic deficits. Symptoms typically resolve over time. Baseline MRI is recommended before treatment, with follow-up MRIs before the 2nd, 3rd, 4th, and 7th infusions. Increased clinical vigilance for ARIA is recommended during the first 24 weeks of treatment. If a patient experiences symptoms suggestive of ARIA, clinical evaluation (including an MRI, if indicated) should be performed. Suspension of treatment and additional monitoring may be needed for patients who develop ARIA symptoms or show radiographic findings of ARIA-H; severe cases may require permanent discontinuation. Additionally, risks for ARIA and related symptoms may be influenced by genetics. In donanemab clinical trials, ARIA incidence was higher in patients who were apolipoprotein Eepsilon4 (ApoEepsilon4) homozygotes (55%) than heterozygotes (36%) or noncarriers (25%), with symptomatic ARIA in 8%, 7%, and 4%, respectively. Serious ARIA events occurred in 3% of homozygotes, 2% of heterozygotes, and 1% of noncarriers. Testing for ApoEepsilon4 informs risk but is not mandatory; current FDA-approved tests may vary in accuracy and design. In addition to ARIA, intracerebral hemorrhages greater than 1 cm and fatal hemorrhages have been reported during donanemab use. Neuroimaging findings indicative of CAA may include evidence of prior intracerebral hemorrhage, cerebral microhemorrhage, and cortical superficial siderosis. The presence of ApoEepsilon4 is also associated with CAA. In clinical studies, the baseline presence of at least 2 microhemorrhages or the presence of at least 1 area of superficial siderosis on MRI, which may be suggestive of CAA, were identified as major risk factors for ARIA. Patients with evidence of prior intracerebral hemorrhage greater than 1 cm in diameter, more than 4 microhemorrhages, more than 1 area of superficial siderosis, severe white matter disease, or vasogenic cerebral edema were excluded from initial clinical studies.

    Use donanemab with caution during breast-feeding. There are no data on the presence of donanemab in human milk, the effects on the breastfed infant, or the effects of the drug on milk production. Published data from other monoclonal antibodies generally indicate low passage of monoclonal antibodies into human milk and limited systemic exposure in the breastfed infant. Adverse Reactions

  Adverse Reactions
    Headline
    chest pain (unspecified), chills, dyspnea, erythema, hypertension, hypotension, infusion-related reactions, nausea, vomiting
    General Information
    In clinical trials, infusion-related reactions were reported in 9% to 16% of patients treated with donanemab and 0.5% (4/874) of patients on placebo. Most (70% to 88%) of these cases occurred within the first 4 infusions, and typically occur during the infusion or within 30 minutes post-infusion; the majority of cases were mild (47% to 57%) to moderate (39% to 50%) in severity. However, 2.8% to 4% of patients treated with donanemab discontinued treatment due to infusion-related reactions. Symptoms of infusion-related reactions include chills, erythema, nausea and/or vomiting, difficulty breathing or dyspnea, sweating, elevated blood pressure (hypertension), headache, chest pain (unspecified), and low blood pressure (hypotension). Patients should be advised that these symptoms may occur after they leave the infusion center and encouraged to contact their care team if these reactions occur. For patients experiencing an infusion-related reaction, the infusion rate may be reduced, or the infusion may need to be discontinued; institute appropriate therapy as clinically indicated. Patients experiencing these effects may be considered for prophylactic treatment with antihistamines, acetaminophen, or corticosteroids prior to future infusions.

    amyloid-related imaging abnormalities, confusion, dizziness, gait disturbance, hemosiderosis, intracranial bleeding, seizures, visual impairment
    General Information
    In initial studies, symptomatic amyloid-related imaging abnormalities that were edema-related (ARIA-E) occurred in 6% of patients through 18 months of treatment with donanemab. Clinical symptoms associated with ARIA-E resolved in approximately 85% of those patients. Including asymptomatic radiographic events, ARIA, ARIA-E, and ARIA associated with localized hemosiderin deposition (ARIA-H), or localized hemosiderosis, were observed in 36%, 24%, and 31% of patients treated with donanemab, respectively, compared to 14%, 2%, and 13% of patients on placebo, respectively. There was no increase in isolated ARIA-H (i.e., ARIA-H in patients who did not also experience ARIA-E) for donanemab compared to placebo. In a second study with an adjusted dosing protocol, symptomatic ARIA-E occurred in 3% of patients and symptomatic ARIA-H occurred in less than 1% of patients through 12 months of treatment with donanemab. Clinical symptoms associated with ARIA-E resolved in approximately 67% of patients at 12 months. Including asymptomatic radiographic events, ARIA, ARIA-E, and ARIA-H were observed in 29%, 16%, and 25% of patients treated with donanemab. Intracerebral hemorrhage (intracranial bleeding) greater than 1 cm in diameter was reported in 0.5% of patients receiving donanemab compared to 0.2% of patients on placebo in an initial study, and in 1% of patients on donanemab in a second study. Fatal events of intracerebral hemorrhage have been observed. While ARIA is usually asymptomatic, serious and life-threatening events can occur, including seizures and status epilepticus. When present, symptoms associated with ARIA may include headache, confusion, visual impairment, dizziness, nausea, and gait disturbance. Focal neurologic deficits may also occur and mimic symptoms of an ischemic stroke. In patients receiving donanemab who develop stroke-like symptoms, the treating clinician should carefully consider whether symptoms could be ARIA or are stroke-related prior to giving thrombolytic therapy. In clinical studies, 2 fatal intracerebral hemorrhages occurred in patients with ARIA who received a thrombolytic agent. Symptoms associated with ARIA usually resolve over time. If a patient experiences symptoms suggestive of ARIA, perform clinical evaluation, including MRI scanning if indicated. Adjustments to treatment dosing depend on clinical symptoms and/or radiographic severity of ARIA.

Description
  Text
  Donanemab is an intravenous amyloid beta-directed antibody indicated for the treatment of Alzheimer's disease. Treatment should be initiated in patients with mild cognitive impairment or mild dementia stage of disease, the population in which treatment was initiated in the clinical trials. There are no safety or effectiveness data on initiating treatment at earlier or later stages of the disease. The efficacy of donanemab was established in a phase 3, double-blind, placebo-controlled, parallel-group study of 1,736 participants across 8 countries with early symptomatic Alzheimer's disease and presence of confirmed Alzheimer's disease neuropathology (TRAILBLAZER-ALZ 2). Patients with a Mini-Mental State Examination (MMSE) score of 20 to 28 and progressive change in memory function for at least 6 months were enrolled; included patients were also reviewed by a visual assessment of tau PET imaging with flortaucipir and standardized uptake value ratio. Study patients received an infusion of donanemab or placebo every 4 weeks for up to 72 weeks. At the end of the trial, patients receiving donanemab showed a statistically significant reduction in clinical decline on the integrated Alzheimer Disease Rating Scale (iADRS) compared to placebo at week 76 (p less than 0.0001). Donanemab recipients also demonstrated a statistically significant reduction in clinical decline compared to placebo (-0.70, p less than 0.0001) at week 76 on the Clinical Dementia Rating Scale-Sum of Boxes (CDR-SB). Statistically significant differences (p less than 0.001) between treatment groups were also noted for the Alzheimer's Disease Assessment Scale-Cognitive subscale (ADAS-Cog13) and the Alzheimer's Disease Cooperative Study- instrumental Activities of Daily Living (ADCS-iADL). Dosing was continued or stopped in response to observed effects on amyloid imaging. The percentages of patients eligible for switch to placebo based on amyloid PET levels at Week 24, Week 52, and Week 76 timepoints were 17%, 47%, and 69%, respectively. Safety issues include a risk for serious hypersensitivity or infusion-related reactions and a warning for amyloid-related imaging abnormalities (ARIA), which most commonly present as temporary swelling in areas of the brain that usually resolves over time and does not cause symptoms, though some people may have symptoms such as headache, confusion, dizziness, vision changes, or nausea. In a multicenter phase 3b study (TRAILBLAZER-ALZ 6), standard donanemab dosing was compared with alternative dosing options that varied in donanemab dosage per infusion and frequency to determine the effect of alternative dosing regimens on ARIA with edema/effusions (ARIA-E) frequency at 24 weeks. Total donanemab exposure by week 16 of the trial was the same across all study arms. At week 24, the percentage of participants who experienced ARIA-E was 23.7% in the standard dosing arm and 13.7% for the modified titration arm. The modified titration arm met the 24-week primary outcome with a 40.5% lower relative ARIA-E risk versus the standard titration arm. Modified titration also met the secondary outcome of improved ARIA-E severity at 24 weeks, with radiographic severity significantly reduced compared to the standard titration arm (p = 0.011). In the modified dosing arm, 86.3% of participants had no ARIA-E by MRI through week 24 (compared with 76.3% of patients in the standard dosing arm) and no radiographically severe events were observed. Cortical superficial siderosis was also significantly reduced in the modified titration arm compared to the standard arm, with a 45% relative risk reduction. The frequency of ARIAs with hemorrhages/hemosiderin deposition (ARIA-H) or microhemorrhages was not significantly different between the standard and modified dosing arm. Cumulative exposure and mean amyloid reduction were similar between groups, indicating that modified titration of donanemab may decrease the frequency of ARIA-E without impacting amyloid removal. Patients receiving donanemab are encouraged to participate in data collection registries to help further understand the impact of treatment on Alzheimer's disease. Providers and patients can obtain information about available registries at 1-800-545-5979. The FDA approved donanemab in July 2024.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  The accumulation of amyloid beta plaques in the brain is a defining pathophysiological feature of Alzheimer's disease. Donanemab is a humanized immunoglobulin gamma 1 (IgG1) monoclonal antibody directed against insoluble N-truncated pyroglutamate amyloid beta. Donanemab reduces amyloid beta plaques in the brain in a time-dependent manner.

      Intravenous Administration
      Description
      Preparation of IV infusion:
Allow the appropriate number of vials of donanemab to equilibrate to room temperature before preparation. Each donanemab vial contains 350 mg/20 mL.
Prior to administration, donanemab must be diluted with 0.9% sodium chloride injection. Use aseptic technique when preparing the solution for intravenous infusion.
Withdraw the required volume of donanemab from the vial(s) and mix with 0.9% sodium chloride injection, to the recommended total volume for a final concentration of 4 mg/mL to 10 mg/mL. Use only 0.9% Sodium Chloride injection for dilution.
For a 350 mg dose: Dilute 1 vial of donanemab injection (20 mL) with 15 mL to 67.5 mL of 0.9% Sodium Chloride injection to a final infusion volume of 35 mL to 87.5 mL. The final concentration of the diluted solution will be 350/87.5 mL (4 mg/mL) to 350 mg/35 mL (10 mg/mL).
For a 700 mg dose: Dilute 2 vials of donanemab injection (40 mL) with 30 mL to 135 mL of 0.9% Sodium Chloride injection to a final infusion volume of 70 mL to 175 mL. The final concentration of the diluted solution will be 700 mg/175 mL (4 mg/mL) to 700 mg/70 mL (10 mg/mL).
For a 1,050 mg dose: Dilute 3 vials of donanemab injection (60 mL) with 45 mL to 202.5 mL of 0.9% Sodium Chloride injection to a final infusion volume of 105 mL to 262.5 mL. The final concentration of the diluted solution will be 1,050 mg/262.5 mL (4 mg/mL) to 1,050 mg/105 mL (10 mg/mL).
For a 1,400 mg dose: Dilute 4 vials of donanemab injection (80 mL) with 60 mL to 270 mL of 0.9% Sodium Chloride injection to a final infusion volume of 140 mL to 350 mL. The final concentration of the diluted solution will be 1,400 mg/350 mL (4 mg/mL) to 1,400 mg/140 mL (10 mg/mL).
Each vial is for single-use only and is preservative-free. Discard any unused portion.
Gently invert the diluted donanemab solution to mix completely. Do not shake.
Storage: After dilution, immediate use is recommended. If the diluted solution is not administered immediately, store refrigerated at 2 to 8 degrees C (36 to 46 degrees F) for up to 72 hours or at room temperature (20 to 25 degrees C; 68 to 77 degrees F) for up to 12 hours. Storage times include the duration of infusion. Do not freeze the diluted solution.
 
Administration of IV infusion:
Visually inspect the diluted donanemab solution for particles or discoloration prior to administration. Do not use if it is discolored, opaque, or foreign particles are seen.
If the diluted solution has been stored under refrigeration, allow the solution to warm to room temperature prior to infusion.
Administer the entire diluted solution intravenously over approximately 30 minutes.
Flush the line only with 0.9% Sodium Chloride injection at the end of the infusion per access specific line maintenance protocol.
Observe the patient post-infusion for a minimum of 30 minutes, and consider longer periods of observation if clinically indicated, to evaluate for infusion reactions and hypersensitivity reactions. The infusion rate may be reduced, or the infusion may be discontinued, and appropriate therapy administered as clinically indicated. Consider pre-medication at subsequent dosing with antihistamines, non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs, or corticosteroids prior to future infusions.
