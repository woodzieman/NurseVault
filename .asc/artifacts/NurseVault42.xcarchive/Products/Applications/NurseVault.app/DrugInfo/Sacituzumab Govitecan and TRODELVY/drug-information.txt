Drug Class: Sacituzumab govitecan is an antibody-drug conjugate composed of a humanized monoclonal antibody (sacituzumab) which binds to the trophoblast cell-surface antigen-2 (Trop-2) covalently linked to SN-38, a topoisomerase I inhibitor.

Routes: injection (IV/IM/SC); Common adult doses: 10 mg/kg IV.

Common Side Effects (excluding routine GI upset): bone marrow suppression, eosinophilia, leukopenia, lymphopenia, neutropenia, gastroesophageal reflux, anaphylactoid reactions, angioedema, cardiac arrest, infusion-related reactions, wheezing, hyperkalemia, hyperphosphatemia, hypocalcemia, hypokalemia, hypomagnesemia, hyponatremia, hypophosphatemia, cough, dyspnea, nasal congestion, rhinorrhea, pruritus, rash, skin hyperpigmentation, xerosis

In an Overdose: No overdose/antidote information is listed in this file.

Key Nursing Considerations: Contraindications/precautions: Sacituzumab govitecan can cause severe or life-threatening neutropenia in the first cycle of treatment. Do not administer sacituzumab govitecan if the ANC on day 1 is less than 1,500 cells/mm3 or if the ANC on day 8 is less than 1,000 cells/mm3.   Contraindications And Precautions
    Headline
    Drug Interactions
    General Information
    The coadministration of certain medications may lead to harm and require avoidance or therapy modification; review all drug interactions prior to concomitant use of other medications.
    Headline
    geriatric, neutropenia
    General Information
    Sacituzumab govitecan can cause severe or life-threatening neutropenia in the first cycle of treatment. Do not administer sacituzumab govitecan if the ANC on day 1 is less than 1,500 cells/mm3 or if the ANC on day 8 is less than 1,000 cells/mm3. Administer primary prophylaxis with G-CSF for all patients at increased risk of neutropenic fever, including geriatric patients, patients with previous neutropenia, poor performance status, organ dysfunction, or multiple comorbidities. Monitor complete blood counts periodically during treatment. An interruption of therapy, dose reduction, or discontinuation of therapy may be necessary for neutropenia. If neutropenic fever occurs, hold treatment and initiate anti-infective treatment immediately.
    Headline
    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components.
    Headline
    UGT1A1 poor metabolizer
    General Information
    Closely monitor patients with a known UGT1A1 poor metabolizer status (homozygous for the UGT1A1*28 allele) for adverse reactions. These patients are at increased risk for neutropenia, febrile neutropenia, and anemia during treatment due to decreased metabolism of sacituzumab govitecan and may also be at increased risk for other adverse reactions. Hold or permanently discontinue therapy with sacituzumab govitecan in patients with evidence of early-onset or unusually severe adverse reactions, based on clinical assessment of the onset, duration and severity of the observed adverse reactions, as this may indicate reduced UGT1A1 enzyme activity.
    Headline
    diarrhea
    General Information
    Severe diarrhea can commonly occur with sacituzumab govitecan treatment. Monitor patients for diarrhea and give fluid and electrolytes as needed; hold treatment if grade 3 or 4 diarrhea occurs until resolution to grade 1 or less. Administer atropine, if not contraindicated, for early diarrhea of any severity. Patients who exhibit an excessive cholinergic response to treatment (e.g., abdominal cramping, diarrhea, salivation, etc.) can receive premedication with atropine for subsequent treatments. At the onset of late diarrhea, evaluate for infectious causes and, if negative, promptly initiate loperamide (4 mg PO once, followed by 2 mg with every episode of diarrhea for a maximum of 16 mg daily). A dose reduction or discontinuation of therapy may be necessary.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Based on its mechanism of action, sacituzumab govitecan can cause teratogenicity and/or embryo-fetal lethality when administered during pregnancy. No human studies are available to inform drug-associated risk. No animal reproductive or developmental toxicity studies have been conducted. Sacituzumab govitecan contains a genotoxic component, SN-38, that is toxic to rapidly dividing cells. Apprise pregnant individuals and those of reproductive potential of the potential fetal risks.
    Headline
    people who can cause pregnancy in others, people who may become pregnant, reproductive risk
    General Information
    Counsel people who may become pregnant and people who can cause pregnancy in others about the reproductive risk associated with sacituzumab govitecan. Sacituzumab govitecan may cause fetal harm. Verify pregnancy status prior to treatment initiation. Advise people who may become pregnant to use effective contraception while taking sacituzumab govitecan and for 6 months after the last dose. For those that can cause pregnancy in others, contraception should be used during sacituzumab govitecan treatment and for 3 months after the last dose. Based on animal data, sacituzumab govitecan may impair female fertility.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in the breast-fed child, counsel patients to avoid breast-feeding during sacituzumab govitecan treatment and for 1 month after the last dose. There are no data on the presence of sacituzumab govitecan or SN-38 in human milk, the effects on the breast-fed child, or the effect on milk production.

    Headline
    bone marrow suppression, eosinophilia, leukopenia, lymphopenia, neutropenia
    General Information
    Treatment with sacituzumab govitecan causes bone marrow suppression including severe or life-threatening neutropenia; leukopenia and lymphopenia have also been reported. Do not administer sacituzumab govitecan if the ANC on day 1 is less than 1,500 cells/mm3 or if the ANC on day 8 is less than 1,000 cells/mm3; consider granulocyte-colony stimulating factor (G-CSF) for primary prophylaxis in all patients at increased risk of febrile neutropenia, including older patients, patients with previous neutropenia, poor performance status, organ dysfunction, or multiple comorbidities. Neutropenia occurred in 64% (grade 3 or 4, 48%) of patients treated with sacituzumab govitecan monotherapy in a pooled safety population from 4 clinical trials (n = 1,354); febrile neutropenia occurred in 6% of patients. G-CSF was used in 44% to 54% of patients who received sacituzumab govitecan in these trials. The incidence of severe (grade 3 or 4) neutropenia was highest in patients homozygous for the UGT1A1*28 allele (57%; n = 138), then in patients heterozygous for the UGT1A1*28 allele (48%; n = 531), and then in patients homozygous for the wild-type allele (41%; n = 533); febrile neutropenia was also more common in patients homozygous for the UGT1A1*28 allele. The median time to first neutropenia was 9 days in patients homozygous for the UGT1A1*28 allele, 19 days in patients heterozygous for the UGT1A1*28 allele, and 21 days in those homozygous for the wild-type allele. Decreases from baseline in lymphocytes (47% to 88%; grade 3 or 4, 16% to 31%), leukocytes (80% to 91%; grade 3 or 4, 26% to 41%), and neutrophils (78% to 84%; grade 3 or 4, 32% to 53%) were additionally reported in clinical trials. Mild (grade 1 or 2) eosinophilia occurred in 13% of patients treated with sacituzumab govitecan in 1 clinical trial. If febrile neutropenia occurs, hold treatment and initiate anti-infective treatment immediately. An interruption of therapy, dose reduction, or discontinuation of therapy may be necessary if neutropenia occurs.

    Headline
    anemia, thrombocytopenia
    General Information
    Anemia and thrombocytopenia have been reported in patients treated with sacituzumab govitecan. The incidence of severe (grade 3 or 4) anemia was highest in a pooled safety population of patients homozygous for the UGT1A1*28 allele (17%; n = 138); the incidence in patients heterozygous for the UGT1A1*28 allele (n = 531) and homozygous for the wild-type allele (n = 533) were similar (9% vs. 8%). Decreases from baseline in hemoglobin (73% to 94%; grade 3 or 4, 6% to 9%) and platelets (16% to 30%; grade 3 or 4, 1% to 6%) were also reported in patients who received sacituzumab govitecan. The median time to first onset of anemia was 22 days in patients homozygous for the UGT1A1*28 allele, and 29 days in patients heterozygous for the UGT1A1*28 allele or homozygous for the wild-type allele.

    anaphylactoid reactions, angioedema, cardiac arrest, infusion-related reactions, wheezing
    General Information

Description
  Text
  Sacituzumab govitecan is an antibody-drug conjugate composed of a humanized monoclonal antibody (sacituzumab) which binds to the trophoblast cell-surface antigen-2 (Trop-2) covalently linked to SN-38, a topoisomerase I inhibitor. It is approved for the treatment of patients with certain types of locally advanced or metastatic breast cancer, and both as monotherapy and in combination with pembrolizumab or pembrolizumab; berahyaluronidase alfa. After initial accelerated approval, an indication for urothelial cancer was voluntarily withdrawn in November 2024 due to failure to meet the primary end point of overall survival in the confirmatory phase 3 TROPiCS-04 trial. Patients who are homozygous or heterozygous for the UGT1A1*28 allele are at increased risk for neutropenia and other adverse reactions; the appropriate dose for these patients is not known and should be based on individual tolerance. All patients require premedication for potentially severe hypersensitivity reactions as well as chemotherapy-induced nausea/vomiting. Sacituzumab govitecan was FDA-approved in 2020.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Sacituzumab govitecan-hziy is an antibody-drug conjugate composed of a humanized monoclonal antibody (sacituzumab) which binds to the trophoblast cell-surface antigen-2 (Trop-2) covalently linked to SN-38, a topoisomerase I inhibitor, by a hydrolyzable linker (CL2A). Pharmacology data suggest that sacituzumab govitecan binds to Trop-2-expressing cancer cells and is internalized with the subsequent release of SN-38 via hydrolysis of the linker. SN-38 prevents the re-ligation of topoisomerase I-induced single-strand DNA breaks, leading to apoptosis and cell death. Sacituzumab govitecan decreased tumor growth in mouse xenograft models of triple-negative breast cancer.

  Text
  Sacituzumab govitecan is administered intravenously. The steady-state volume of distribution is 4.6 L. The median elimination half-life of sacituzumab govitecan and free SN-38 was 6.5 days and 22 hours, respectively. The terminal half-life of sacituzumab govitecan is 6.7 days (CV, 23%) and the apparent half-life of free SN-38 is 24 hours (CV, 50%). The estimated mean clearance of sacituzumab govitecan was 0.13 L/hour (CV, 20%). The small molecule moiety of sacituzumab govitecan, SN-38, is metabolized via UGT1A1; renal elimination contributes minimally to SN-38 excretion. The glucuronide metabolite of SN-38 (SN-38G) was detectable in the serum of patients.
Pharmacodynamics
Increases in the QTc interval were concentration-dependent based on SN-38 concentrations. At the recommended dose, the maximum mean change in QTc from baseline was 9.7 msec, with an upper bound of 2-sided 90% confidence interval of 16.8 msec.
The effect of anti-drug antibodies (ADA) on the pharmacokinetics and/or pharmacodynamics sacituzumab govitecan is unknown.
Affected cytochrome P-450 (CYP450) isoenzymes and drug transporters: UGT1A1
Metabolism studies with sacituzumab govitecan have not been conducted. The small molecule moiety, SN-38, is metabolized via UGT1A1; inhibitors or inducers of UGT1A1 are expected to increase or decrease SN-38 exposure, respectively.

Reconstitution
Slowly inject 20 mL of 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection into each vial.
Each vial contains overfill to compensate for liquid loss during preparation and after reconstitution, with the total resulting volume after dilution delivering a final concentration of 10 mg/mL.
Gently swirl the vials and allow them to dissolve for up to 15 minutes. Do not shake. The solution should be free of visible particles, clear, and yellow. Do not use the reconstituted solution if it is cloudy or discolored.
Storage: Use reconstituted vials immediately to prepare a diluted infusion solution of sacituzumab govitecan.
 
Dilution
Sacituzumab govitecan may be diluted using a polyvinyl chloride, polypropylene/polyethylene, polyolefin, or ethylene vinyl acetate infusion bag.
Calculate the volume of 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection sufficient to obtain a final concentration of sacituzumab govitecan in the range of 1.1 to 3.4 mg/mL.
Slowly inject the calculated dose of reconstituted sacituzumab govitecan to the 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection to minimize foaming; do not shake.
Protect from light.
Storage: If not used immediately, diluted sacituzumab govitecan may be refrigerated at 2 to 8 degrees C (36 to 46 degrees F) for up to 24 hours. Diluted sacituzumab govitecan must be used within 8 hours after returning to room temperature, including infusion time. Do not freeze or shake.
 
Intermittent IV Infusion
After refrigeration, allow the diluted solution to come to room temperature; the infusion must be completed within 8 hours of returning to room temperature.
While the infusion bag should be protected from light, it is not necessary to cover the infusion tubing or to use light-protective tubing.
An infusion pump may be used for administration.
First dose: Infuse over 3 hours.
Subsequent doses: Infuse over 1 to 2 hours if prior infusions were tolerated.
Observe patients during the infusion and for at least 30 minutes after for signs or symptoms of infusion-related reactions.
Flush the IV line with 20 mL 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection.
