Drug Class: Selumetinib is a mitogen-activated protein kinase 1 and 2 (MEK1/2) inhibitor indicated for the treatment of adults and pediatric patients 1 year of age and older with neurofibromatosis type 1 who have symptomatic, inoperable plexiform neurofibromas.

Routes: oral; Common adult doses: 25 mg/m2 PO twice daily, not to exceed 50 mg PO twice daily.

  Contraindications And Precautions
    Headline
    Drug Interactions
    General Information
    The coadministration of certain medications may lead to harm and require avoidance or therapy modification; review all drug interactions prior to concomitant use of other medications.
    Headline
    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components.
    Headline
    cardiac disease
    General Information
    Use selumetinib with caution in patients with a history of cardiac disease; the safety of selumetinib in patients with a history of clinically significant cardiac disease or left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) less than 55% at baseline has not been studied. Assess LVEF by echocardiogram prior to initiating treatment, every 3 months during the first year of treatment, and every 6 months thereafter and as clinically indicated; an interruption of therapy or discontinuation of therapy may be necessary if decreased EF occurs. In patients who interrupt therapy for decreased LVEF, obtain an echocardiogram or cardiac MRI every 3 to 6 weeks; upon resolution of LVEF to greater than or equal to the institutional LLN, obtain an echocardiogram or cardiac MRI every 2 to 3 months or as directed by the cardiologist. Cardiomyopathy has occurred in patients with neurofibromatosis type 1 (NF1) who were treated with selumetinib; all patients with decreased LVEF were asymptomatic and were identified during routine echocardiography.
    Headline
    ocular disease
    General Information
    Use selumetinib with caution in patients with a history of ocular disease; blurred vision, photophobia, cataracts, ocular hypertension, retinal tear, and vitreous floaters have occurred in patients treated with selumetinib. Serious ocular toxicities including retinal vein occlusion (RVO) and retinal pigment epithelial detachment (retinal detachment) have also occurred. Conduct comprehensive ophthalmic assessments prior to initiating selumetinib therapy, at regular intervals during treatment, and for new or worsening visual changes. An interruption of therapy, dose reduction, or discontinuation of therapy may be necessary for ocular toxicities, depending on the severity.
    Headline
    Child-Pugh class B, Child-Pugh class C, hepatic failure
    General Information
    Use selumetinib with caution in patients with moderate (Child-Pugh class B) or severe (Child-Pugh class C) hepatic impairment, including hepatic failure. A reduced dose is recommended in patients with moderate hepatic impairment; a recommended dose has not been established in patients with severe impairment.
    Headline
    people who can cause pregnancy in others, people who may become pregnant, reproductive risk
    General Information
    Counsel people who may become pregnant and people who can cause pregnancy in others about the reproductive risk associated with selumetinib. Selumetinib can cause fetal harm. Verify pregnancy status prior to treatment initiation. Advise people who may become pregnant and those that can cause pregnancy in others to use effective contraception while taking selumetinib and for 1 week after the last dose.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Selumetinib can cause fetal harm if used during pregnancy, based on its mechanism of action and data from animal studies. There are no human data to evaluate a drug-associated risk. Selumetinib caused reduced fetal weight, adverse structural defects (prematurely open eye(s) and cleft palate), and increased post-implantation loss in the absence of significant maternal toxicity in mice given doses approximately 5 times the human exposure at the clinical dose based on AUC during organogenesis. The incidence of malformations was increased even at the lowest dose exposure, which resulted in maximum concentrations of approximately 0.6 times those achieved at the human clinical dose. One case of first trimester spontaneous abortion was reported in an adult patient treated with selumetinib in the KOMET trial.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Advise patients to avoid breast-feeding during selumetinib treatment and for 1 week after the last dose due to the potential for serious side effects in the breast-fed child. There are no data on the presence of selumetinib or its active metabolite in human milk, their effects on the breast-fed child, or their effects on milk production. Selumetinib and its active metabolite are present in animal milk.

Adagrasib: (Major) Avoid coadministration of selumetinib and adagrasib due to the risk of increased selumetinib exposure which may increase the risk of adverse reactions. If coadministration is unavoidable, reduce the dose of selumetinib to 20 mg/m2 PO twice daily if original dose was 25 mg/m2 twice daily and 15 mg/m2 PO twice daily if original dose was 20 mg/m2 twice daily. If adagrasib is discontinued, resume the original selumetinib dose after 3 elimination half-lives of adagrasib. Selumetinib is a CYP3A substrate and adagrasib is a strong CYP3A inhibitor.     Headline
    cardiomyopathy, left ventricular dysfunction
    General Information
    Selumetinib can cause cardiomyopathy including left ventricular dysfunction, defined as a decrease in left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) of at least 10% below baseline. Selumetinib has not been studied in patients with LVEF of less than 55% prior to treatment or a history of clinically significant cardiac disease. In a pooled safety population of pediatric patients (n=134), grade 2 LVEF decrease (40% to 50%; 10% to 19% decrease from baseline) occurred in 17% of patients; decreases in LVEF of 20% or more occurred in 0.7% of patients in the same population. Decreased LVEF resolved in 75% of these patients. The median time to first onset of LVEF decrease was approximately 12 months with a median duration of approximately 3 months. In the KOMET trial (adult patients), grade 2 LVEF decrease (40% to 50%; 10% to 19% decrease from baseline) occurred in 14% of patients. The median time to first onset and median duration of LVEF decrease were approximately 4 months each. If asymptomatic or symptomatic decreases in LVEF occur, an interruption of therapy, dose reduction, or permanent discontinuation of therapy may be necessary depending on severity.

    Headline
    blurred vision, cataracts, ocular hypertension, photophobia, retinal detachment, retinopathy, visual impairment
    General Information
    Selumetinib can cause ocular toxicity, including retinal pigment epithelial detachment (retinal detachment) (RPED), retinal vein occlusion (RVO), central serous retinopathy (CSR), and blurred vision. Visual impairment, including blurred vision, photophobia, cataracts, ocular hypertension, and retinal tear, occurred in 13% of pediatric patients in a pooled safety population treated with selumetinib; ocular toxicity resolved in 72% of patients. Asymptomatic transient subretinal fluid (6%) and retinopathy (0.7%) also occurred; dose modification was not required. RPED has been reported in pediatric patients treated with single-agent selumetinib and resulted in permanent discontinuation. In adult patients treated with selumetinib in the KOMET trial, blurred vision and vitreous floaters occurred in 6% of patients. Serious ocular toxicities, including RVO and RPED, occurred in an unapproved population of adult patients treated with selumetinib as monotherapy or in combination with other anticancer agents. Conduct a comprehensive ophthalmic assessment for new or worsening visual changes. Permanently discontinue selumetinib if RVO occurs; withhold selumetinib if RPED occurs until resolution, then resume at a reduced dose. An interruption of therapy, dose reduction, or discontinuation of therapy may be necessary for other ocular toxicities based on severity. In postmarketing experience, RPED/CSR included central serous chorioretinopathy, serous retinal detachment, and serous retinopathy.

Description
  Text
  Selumetinib is a mitogen-activated protein kinase 1 and 2 (MEK1/2) inhibitor indicated for the treatment of adults and pediatric patients 1 year of age and older with neurofibromatosis type 1 who have symptomatic, inoperable plexiform neurofibromas. Selumetinib capsules can cause increased levels of vitamin E and an increased risk of bleeding; selumetinib oral granules do not contain vitamin E. Selumetinib can cause ocular toxicity, gastrointestinal toxicity, skin toxicity, increased creatine phosphokinase (CPK) and concurrent myalgia, and left ventricular dysfunction. Monitor echocardiograms, serum CPK level, and conduct comprehensive ophthalmic assessments regularly during treatment. Selumetinib was FDA approved in April 2020.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Selumetinib is a kinase inhibitor targeting mitogen-activated protein kinases 1 and 2 (MEK1/2). MEK1/2 proteins are upstream regulators of the extracellular signal-related kinase (ERK) pathway. Both MEK and ERK are critical components of the RAS-regulated RAF-MEK-ERK pathway, which is often activated in different types of cancers. Oral administration of selumetinib inhibited ERK phosphorylation and reduced neurofibroma numbers, volume, and proliferation in genetically modified mouse models of neurofibromatosis type 1 (NF1) that generate neurofibromas that recapitulate the genotype and phenotype of human NF1.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Oral Route
    Description
    At the recommended dose of 25 mg/m2 of selumetinib twice daily, the mean steady state Cmax was 792 ng/mL (CV, 49%) and the AUC was 2,141 ng x hr/mL (CV, 55%). The median time to peak plasma concentrations (Tmax) at steady-state was 1.5 hours. The AUC and Cmax of selumetinib increase proportionally over a dose range from 20 mg/m2 to 30 mg/m2; the accumulation range was 1.2 to 1.5-fold after administration at 25 mg/m2. The mean oral bioavailability of selumetinib is 62%. At the recommended dose of 25 mg/m2 of selumetinib oral granules (sprinkled on smooth yogurt, smooth fruit sauce, smooth fruit puree, or smooth fruit jam) twice daily in pediatric patients (age 1 year to less than 7 years), the AUC0-24h following the first dose was within the range of that in patients administered selumetinib capsules. No clinically relevant differences in the pharmacokinetics of selumetinib were observed following administration of a single-dose of either the granule or capsule dosage forms at equivalent dosages, under fasted or fed conditions, in healthy adults.
Effects of food: Following administration of a low-fat (400 to 500 calories) or high-fat (800 to 1,000 calories) meal, no clinically significant differences in selumetinib pharmacokinetics were observed.

  Text
  Selumetinib is administered orally. It is 98.4% bound to plasma proteins in vitro and is primarily bound to albumin. The mean apparent volume of distribution at steady state of selumetinib across a dose range of 20 mg/m2 to 30 mg/m2 ranged from 40 L to 2,710 L (CV, 66%). The apparent oral clearance was 16 L/hr/m2 (CV, 44%) and the mean elimination half-life was 9 hours (CV, 28%). After a single radiolabeled dose of selumetinib to healthy adults, 59% of the dose was recovered in feces (19% unchanged) and 33% in urine (less than 1% unchanged).
 
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes and drug transporters: CYP3A4, CYP2C19, CYP1A2, CYP2C9, CYP2E1, CYP3A5, UGT1A1, UGT1A3
Selumetinib is primarily metabolized by CYP3A4 and to a lesser extent by CYP2C19, CYP1A2, CYP2C9, CYP2E1, and CYP3A5. It also undergoes glucuronidation by UGT1A1 and UGT1A3. Approximately 56% of the intrinsic clearance of selumetinib can be attributed to CYP metabolism and about 29% to direct glucuronidation by UGT enzymes. Metabolism by CYP2C19 and CYP1A2 (with additional contribution by CYP2C9 and CYP2A6) generate an active metabolite (N-desmethyl selumetinib) that represents less than 10% of selumetinib levels in human plasma, but is 3 to 5 times more potent than the parent compound and contributes to about 21% to 35% of the overall pharmacologic activity. N-desemethyl selumetinib is metabolized through the same routes as the parent drug.

Patient Counseling
Selumetinib capsules contain vitamin E, therefore the use of supplemental vitamin E is not recommended if daily vitamin E intake (including the amount of vitamin E in selumetinib and supplement) will exceed the recommended or safe limits. Selumetinib oral granules do not contain vitamin E.
