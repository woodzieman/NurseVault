    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    There is no available data on the use of doxecitine; doxribtimine during pregnancy to evaluate for a drug associated risk of major birth defects, miscarriage, or other adverse maternal or fetal outcomes. Endogenous pyrimidine nucleosides are transported across the placenta. In animal reproductive studies, oral administration of doxecitine and doxribtimine to pregnant rats and rabbits during organogenesis resulted in maternal and fetal toxicities (dilated aorta with an associated narrow pulmonary trunk) in the rabbit population at dose exposures 1,233 and 811 times the maximum recommended human dose (MRHD) of 400 mg/kg/day doxecitine and 400 mg/kg/day doxribtimine. Mitochondrial myopathies are associated with increased adverse perinatal outcomes, including preterm birth, pre-eclampsia and gestational diabetes.

Data from published literature reports the presence of nucleosides and nucleotides in human milk. The developmental and health benefits of breast-feeding should be considered along with the mother's clinical need for doxecitine; doxribtimine and any potential adverse effects on the breastfed infant from doxecitine; doxribtimine or from the underlying maternal condition.

  Adverse Reactions
    Headline
    abdominal pain, diarrhea, vomiting
    General Information
    Diarrhea (72%), abdominal pain (23%), and vomiting (21%) were reported in adult and pediatric patients receiving doxecitine; doxribtimine during clinical trials. Vomiting occurred more often in pediatric patients (28%) compared to adult patients (7%). Diarrhea resulted in hospitalization in 2 pediatric patients during clinical trials. Dose reductions due to diarrhea occurred in 21% of individuals and 3% of individuals discontinued doxecitine; doxribtimine due to diarrhea. Dose reductions due to abdominal pain occurred in 3% of individuals. Based on severity of diarrhea and/or vomiting, reduce the dose of doxecitine; doxribtimine or interrupt treatment until diarrhea and/or vomiting improves or returns to baseline. Consider restarting therapy at the last tolerated dose, and increase the dose as tolerated. For persistent or recurring diarrhea and/or vomiting, consider permanently discontinuing therapy and providing supportive care, including electrolyte repletion as clinically indicated.

    elevated hepatic enzymes
    General Information
    Elevated hepatic enzymes occurred during clinical trials with doxecitine; doxribtimine. An increase in alanine aminotransferase (ALT) and an increase in aspartate aminotransferase (AST) was reported in 21% and 17% of adult and pediatric patients, respectively. Elevated liver transaminases occurred more frequently in pediatric patients (25% increased ALT, 22% increased AST) compared to adult patients (13% increased ALT; 7% increased AST). Permanent discontinuation occurred in 3% of individuals due to elevated liver enzymes. In a clinical trial, among individuals who began therapy with elevated AST/ALT, 5% experienced a worsening in ALT severity at their final post-baseline assessment during treatment. Two individuals permanently discontinued treatment upon recurrence of elevated liver enzymes after a rechallenge at a reduced dose. Obtain baseline ALT/AST and total bilirubin levels in individuals prior to treatment initiation with doxecitine; doxribtimine. If signs or symptoms of liver injury are observed, interrupt treatment with doxecitine; doxribtimine until ALT/AST and total bilirubin levels have either returned to baseline or stabilized at a new baseline value. Consider permanently discontinuing therapy if signs or symptoms of liver injury persist or worsen. Monitor LFTs yearly and as clinically indicated.

Description
  Text
  Doxecitine; doxribtimine is a combination of pyrimidine nucleosides indicated for the treatment of thymidine kinase 2 deficiency (TK2d) in individuals with an age of symptom onset at or before 12 years of age. TK2d is a rare mitochondrial myopathy characterized by progressive muscle weakness. It is caused by pathogenic variants in the TK2 gene, which plays a key role in maintaining mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA). Mitochondria require adequate mtDNA to generate energy. In individuals with two altered TK2 genes, the amount of mtDNA in muscle cells decreases over time, resulting in impaired energy production and muscle dysfunction. The most common symptom is weakness in the legs and arms, which worsens over time. A survival analysis of 78 matched pairs of treated and untreated individuals showed there were three deaths (4%) in the individuals receiving doxecitine; doxribtimine compared with 28 deaths (36%) in the external untreated control group. The restricted mean survival time at 10 years post treatment start was 9.6 years for the group receiving doxecitine; doxribtimine compared with 5.7 years in the control group. The most common side effects of doxecitine; doxribtimine therapy are diarrhea and elevated liver enzymes. Obtain baseline liver transaminase and total bilirubin levels prior to initiation, yearly, and as clinically indicated. Doxecitine; doxribtimine was FDA-approved in November 2025.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Doxecitine; doxribtimine is intended to incorporate missing pyrimidine nucleosides, deoxycytidine and deoxythymidine, into skeletal muscle mitochondrial deoxyribonucleic acid (DNA). This action restores mitochondrial DNA copy number in mice with thymidine kinase 2 deficiency.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Oral Route
    Description
    Following oral administration of doxecitine and doxribtimine in healthy adults, baseline-adjusted maximum plasma concentration (Cmax) and AUC increased in a less than dose proportional manner for doxecitine and more than a dose proportional manner for doxribtimine at doses ranging from 43 mg/kg to 133 mg/kg. There is minimal or no accumulation of doxecitine and doxribtimine following multiple doses. Administration of 133 mg/kg of doxecitine and 133 mg/kg doxribtimine with a high-fat, high-calorie meal in healthy adults increased the Cmax and AUC by 79% and 137%, respectively, for doxecitine and increased the Cmax and AUC by 27% and 74%, respectively for doxribtimine, compared to fasted conditions. Following oral administration at the recommended dosage of 800 mg/kg/day under fed conditions in 18 pediatric and adult patients with thymidine kinase 2 deficiency (TK2d), the unadjusted geometric mean Cmax at steady state was 12 nanograms/mL and 19 nanograms/mL for doxecitine and doxribtimine, respectively. The geometric mean AUC was 108 nanograms x hour/mL and 191 nanograms x hour/mL for doxecitine and doxribtimine, respectively. Inter-subject variability (geometric CV%) in Cmax and AUC values of doxecitine and doxribtimine were greater than 70%. The Tmax was approximately 2 hours for doxecitine and 4 hours for doxribtimine. The absolute bioavailability of doxecitine and doxribtimine following oral administration has not been established.

  Text
  Doxecitine; doxribtimine is administered orally. The in vitro plasma protein binding of doxecitine and doxribtimine was less than 10% over the concentration range between 0.23 mcg/mL and 23 mcg/mL. Doxecitine and doxribtimine are primarily catabolized by cytidine deaminase and thymidine phosphorylase, respectively, to their nucleobases and the 2-deoxy-alpha-D-ribose 1-phosphate moiety. Intermediate products of doxecitine catabolism are deoxyuridine, uracil, and dihydrouracil with the end products of beta-alanine, ammonia, and carbon dioxide. Thymine, the pyrimidine nucleobase of doxribtimine, is catabolized to dihydrothymine and ultimately to gamma-amino-isobutyric acid and carbon dioxide. Urine excretion of intact doxecitine and doxribtimine was less than 1% of the dose in healthy subjects following oral administration. The mean half-life was approximately 1 hour for doxecitine and 5 hours for doxribtimine.
 
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes and drug transporters: none
Doxecitine and doxribtimine are not inducers, inhibitors, or substrates of CYP isozymes. Doxecitine and doxribtimine do not inhibit P-gp, BCRP, BSEP, OATP1B1, OATP1B3, OAT1, OAT3, OCT1, OCT2, MATE1, or MATE2-K. Doxribtimine may be a substrate of BCRP, but its clinical significance is unknown.

Preparation
Only use the dosing cup, mixing bottle, and oral syringes provided with the ZX2000 administration kit.
Obtain the required number of doxecitine; doxribtimine packets to prepare a 1-day supply of solution each morning.
Use 40 mL of water per packet. Pour the prescribed volume of room temperature water into the mixing bottle. Do not use any other liquid.
Add the powder from the required number of packets into the mixing bottle.
Screw the dosing cup tightly onto the mixing bottle and gently invert the mixing bottle back and forth at least 20 times. Continue mixing until all powder is dissolved.
The mixed solution may appear cloudy and have some residual powder remaining at the bottom or top.
Oral Administration
Take with food.
Before each administration, gently invert the tightly closed mixing bottle slowly back and forth at least 3 times.
Use a dosing cup or oral syringe to administer the solution. Choose the method based on the volume of solution to be administered per dose.
Take doxecitine; doxribtimine solution in 3 equally divided doses approximately 6 hours apart (plus or minus 2 hours).
Do not administer another dose if the dose is spit out or if a full dose is not taken. Take the next dose at the next scheduled time.
Storage: Store the reconstituted solution at room temperature between 20 and 25 degrees C (68 and 77 degrees F) or in the refrigerator. Discard any remaining solution 16 hours after reconstitution or after taking or giving the 3 doses, whichever comes first.
Feeding Tube Administration
Doxecitine; doxribtimine is compatible with polyvinylchloride (PVC) free from DEHP (phthalates), polyurethane, and silicone material.
Draw up the solution using a syringe compatible with the feeding tube.
Administer the solution immediately through the feeding tube.
Flush any residual solution in the syringe or feeding tube until no solution is left. To flush the tube, a single flushing step with a volume of water equivalent to the tube's priming volume is sufficient.
Storage: Store the reconstituted solution at room temperature between 20 and 25 degrees C (68 and 77 degrees F) or in the refrigerator. Discard any remaining medication 16 hours after reconstitution or after taking or giving the 3 doses, whichever comes first.

Dosage Adjustment Guidelines
  Hepatic Impairment
  Specific guidelines for dosage adjustments in hepatic impairment are not available; it appears that no dosage adjustments are needed.
  Renal Impairment
  Plasma concentrations of doxecitine; doxribtimine increased in individuals with moderate or severe renal impairment. An appropriate dosage adjustment in individuals with renal impairment can not be determined since renal impairment has distinct effects on the PK of doxecitine and PK of doxribtimine, and it is not feasible to separately adjust the dosage for these medications.
