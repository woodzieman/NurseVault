Routes: oral; Common adult doses: 20 mg/day PO in chronic kidney disease associated with type 1 or type 2 diabetes; 40 mg/day PO in heart failure.

Common Side Effects (excluding routine GI upset): hyperkalemia, hyponatremia, hypotension, angioedema, rash, urticaria

In an Overdose: No overdose/antidote information is listed in this file.

Contraindications And Precautions

  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  Contraindications And Precautions
    Headline
    Drug Interactions
    General Information
    The coadministration of certain medications may lead to harm and require avoidance or therapy modification; review all drug interactions prior to concomitant use of other medications.
    Headline
    adrenal insufficiency
    General Information
    Finerenone is contraindicated in individuals with adrenal insufficiency. Finerenone is potassium-sparing and individuals with adrenal insufficiency are predisposed to elevated potassium concentrations.
    Headline
    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components.
    Headline
    Child-Pugh class B, Child-Pugh class C, hepatic failure
    General Information
    Avoid finerenone use in individuals with severe hepatic impairment or hepatic failure (Child-Pugh class C). No dosage adjustment is recommended in individuals with mild or moderate hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh class A or B); however, consider more frequent serum potassium monitoring in individuals with moderate hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh class B).
    Headline
    hyperkalemia, renal failure, renal impairment
    General Information
    Initiation of finerenone is not recommended in individuals with severe renal impairment or renal failure (eGFR less than 25 mL/minute/1.73 m2). Finerenone can cause worsening of renal function in individuals with heart failure; severe events associated with worsening renal function, including events requiring hospitalization, have rarely occurred. Consider postponing dose increases or interrupting treatment with finerenone in those individuals with heart failure who develop clinically significant worsening of renal function. Measure serum potassium concentration and estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) before initiation, 4 weeks after therapy initiation or dose adjustment, and periodically during treatment and adjust dose as needed. Do not initiate finerenone treatment if serum potassium is more than 5 mEq/L. If serum potassium concentration is more than 4.8 to 5 mEq/L, initiation of finerenone treatment in individuals with chronic kidney disease associated with type 1 or type 2 diabetes mellitus may be considered with additional serum potassium monitoring within the first 4 weeks based on clinical judgment and serum potassium concentrations. Withhold finerenone if serum potassium increases to more than 5.5 mEq/L in individuals with chronic kidney disease associated with type 1 or type 2 diabetes mellitus or 6 mEq/mL or more in individuals with heart failure during treatment. More frequent monitoring may be necessary for individuals at risk for hyperkalemia, including those on concomitant medications that impair potassium excretion or increase serum potassium. The risk of developing hyperkalemia increases with decreasing kidney function and is greater in individuals with higher baseline potassium concentrations or other risk factors for hyperkalemia.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    There are no available data on finerenone use in human pregnancy to evaluate for a drug-associated risk of major birth defects, miscarriage, or other adverse outcomes. Animal studies have shown developmental toxicity at exposures about 2 times those expected in humans, but the clinical implications of those findings in humans are unclear.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Advise patients to avoid breast-feeding while taking finerenone and for 1 day after the last dose. Finerenone has the potential to cause harm in the breast-fed child. There are no data on the presence of finerenone or its metabolite in human milk, the effects on the breast-fed child, or the effects on milk production. Findings suggest that finerenone is present in rat milk.

Interactions
Acetaminophen; Caffeine; Dihydrocodeine: (Moderate) Consider a reduced dose of dihydrocodeine with frequent monitoring for respiratory depression and sedation if concurrent use of finerenone at a dose of at least 40 mg/day is necessary. If finerenone at a dose of at least 40 mg/day is discontinued, consider increasing the dihydrocodeine dose until stable drug effects are achieved and monitor for evidence of opioid withdrawal. Concomitant use of dihydrocodeine with finerenone at a dose of at least 40 mg/day may increase dihydrocodeine plasma concentrations, resulting in greater metabolism by CYP2D6, increased dihydromorphine concentrations, and prolonged opioid adverse reactions, including hypotension, respiratory depression, profound sedation, coma, and death. If finerenone at a dose of at least 40 mg/day is discontinued, monitor the patient carefully and consider increasing the opioid dosage if appropriate. Dihydrocodeine is partially metabolized via CYP3A and finerenone at a dose of at least 40 mg/day is a weak CYP3A inhibitor.
Acetaminophen; Codeine: (Moderate) Concomitant use of codeine with finerenone at a dose of at least 40 mg/day may increase codeine plasma concentrations, resulting in greater metabolism by CYP2D6, increased morphine concentrations, and prolonged opioid adverse reactions, including hypotension, respiratory depression, profound sedation, coma, and death. It is recommended to avoid this combination when codeine is being used for cough. If coadministration is necessary, monitor patients closely at frequent intervals and consider a dosage reduction of codeine until stable drug effects are achieved. Discontinuation of finerenone at a dose of at least 40 mg/day could decrease codeine plasma concentrations, decrease opioid efficacy, and potentially lead to a withdrawal syndrome in those with physical dependence to codeine. If finerenone at a dose of at least 40 mg/day is discontinued, monitor the patient carefully and consider increasing the opioid dosage if appropriate. Codeine is primarily metabolized by CYP2D6 to morphine and by CYP3A to norcodeine (norcodeine does not have analgesic properties); finerenone at a dose of at least 40 mg/day is a weak CYP3A inhibitor.
Acetaminophen; HYDROcodone: (Moderate) Consider a reduced dose of hydrocodone with frequent monitoring for respiratory depression and sedation if concurrent use of finerenone at a dose of at least 40 mg/day is necessary. It is recommended to avoid this combination when hydrocodone is being used for cough. Hydrocodone is a CYP3A substrate, and coadministration with CYP3A inhibitors like finerenone at a dose of at least 40 mg/day can increase hydrocodone exposure resulting in increased or prolonged opioid effects including fatal respiratory depression, particularly when an inhibitor is added to a stable dose of hydrocodone. These effects could be more pronounced in patients also receiving a CYP2D6 inhibitor. If finerenone at a dose of at least 40 mg/day is discontinued, hydrocodone plasma concentrations will decrease resulting in reduced efficacy of the opioid and potential withdrawal syndrome in a patient who has developed physical dependence to hydrocodone.

    hyperkalemia, hyponatremia, hypotension
    General Information
    In 2 randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled clinical studies, in which 6,510 subjects received finerenone 10 mg or 20 mg once daily, the most commonly reported adverse reactions reported with finerenone and occurring more frequently than placebo were hyperkalemia (14% vs. 6.9%), hypotension (4.6% vs. 3%), and hyponatremia (1.3% vs. 0.7%). An interruption of therapy or dose reduction may be required in patients who experience an increase in serum potassium.

Description
  Text
  Finerenone is an oral non-steroidal mineralocorticoid receptor (MR) antagonist indicated to reduce the risk of cardiovascular (CV) mortality, heart failure hospitalizations, sustained eGFR decline, end-stage kidney disease, non-fatal myocardial infarction, and/or urgent heart failure visits in adults with either chronic kidney disease (CKD) associated with type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) or heart failure with left ventricular ejection fraction of 40% or more, and to reduce urinary albumin-to-creatinine ratio (UACR), which is expected to reduce the risk of sustained eGFR decline and end-stage kidney disease, in adults with CKD associated with type 1 diabetes mellitus (T1DM). Finerenone blocks MR-mediated sodium reabsorption and MR overactivation in both epithelial (e.g., kidney) and nonepithelial (e.g., heart, and blood vessels) tissues; MR overactivation is thought to contribute to fibrosis and inflammation. Finerenone therapy has been shown in clinical trials involving adults with CKD associated with T2DM (FIDELIO-DKD, FIGARO-DKD) to significantly reduce the incidence of the composite endpoint of sustained decline eGFR of 40% or more, kidney failure, or kidney death and the composite endpoint of CV death, non-fatal myocardial infarction, non-fatal stroke or hospitalization for heart failure. Guidelines recommend the use of finerenone in individuals with T2DM and CKD who are at increased risk for CV events or CKD progression or who are unable to use a sodium-glucose co-transporter 2 (SGLT2) inhibitor. In the randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled FINE-ONE study in 242 adults with CKD associated with T1DM, finerenone significantly reduced UACR versus placebo over 6 months (p=0.0001), with adjusted relative reductions of 22% at Month 3 and 28% at Month 6 (LSGMR 0.78 [95% CI, 0.68, 0.90] and 0.72 [95% CI, 0.60, 0.86], respectively). In the FINEARTS-HF study, finerenone significantly reduced the risk of the primary composite endpoint of CV death and total heart failure events (i.e., heart failure hospitalizations and urgent heart failure visits) and the secondary composite endpoint of total heart events, which includes heart failure hospitalizations and urgent heart failure visits, compared to placebo. Finerenone is metabolized by CYP3A4 and is subject to many drug interactions that increase the likelihood of developing hyperkalemia, the principal risk associated with finerenone. Finerenone was FDA-approved for use in July 2021.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Finerenone is a nonsteroidal, selective antagonist of the mineralocorticoid receptor (MR), which is activated by aldosterone and cortisol and regulates gene transcription. Finerenone has a high potency and selectivity for the MR and has no relevant affinity for androgen, progesterone, estrogen, and glucocorticoid receptors. Finerenone blocks MR-mediated sodium reabsorption and MR overactivation in both epithelial (e.g., kidney) and nonepithelial (e.g., heart, and blood vessels) tissues; MR overactivation is thought to contribute to fibrosis and inflammation.
 
Pharmacodynamics:
In 2 randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, multicenter studies in adult individuals with chronic kidney disease (CKD) associated with type 2 diabetes (FIDELIO-DKD and FIGARO-DKD), the placebo-corrected relative reduction in urinary albumin-to-creatinine ratio (UACR) in individuals randomized to finerenone was 31% (95% CI, 29% to 34%) and 32% (95% CI, 30% to 35%), respectively at month 4 and remained stable for the duration of the trial. In another randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, multicenter dose finding study (ARTS DN) in adults with CKD and type 2 diabetes, the placebo-controlled relative reduction in UACR at day 90 was 25% and 38% in individuals treated with finerenone 10 mg and 20 mg once daily, respectively.
In individuals treated with finerenone, the mean systolic blood pressure decreased by 3 mmHg and the mean diastolic blood pressure decreased by 1 to 2 mmHg at month 1, remaining stable thereafter. At a dose 2 times the maximum approved recommended dose, finerenone did not prolong the QT interval to any clinically relevant extent.

  Text
  Finerenone is administered orally. Plasma protein binding of finerenone is 92%, primarily to serum albumin in vitro. The volume of distribution at steady-state (Vss) of finerenone is 52.6 L. Finerenone is primarily metabolized by CYP3A4 (90%) and to a lesser extent by CYP2C8 (10%) to inactive metabolites. About 80% of the administered dose is excreted in urine (less than 1% as unchanged) and approximately 20% in feces (less than 0.2% as unchanged). The terminal half-life of finerenone is approximately 2 to 3 hours, and the systemic blood clearance is approximately 25 L/hour.
 
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes and drug transporters: CYP3A4, CYP2C8
Finerenone is a substrate of CYP3A4 and, to a lesser extent, CYP2C8. It is also a weak CYP2C8 inhibitor at a dose of 40 mg/day. Coadministration of finerenone and itraconazole, a strong CYP3A4 inhibitor, is predicted to increase finerenone exposure by more than 400%. Coadministration of finerenone with strong CYP3A4 inhibitors is contraindicated and use is not recommended with strong or moderate CYP3A4 inducers.

Intermittent hemodialysis
Use not recommended. Finerenone is unlikely to be efficiently removed by hemodialysis given its high plasma protein binding of approximately 90%.
