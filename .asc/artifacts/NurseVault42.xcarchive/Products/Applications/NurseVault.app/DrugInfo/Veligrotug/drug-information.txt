    General Information
    Veligrotug may cause an exacerbation of inflammatory bowel disease (IBD). IBD has been reported in some individuals receiving insulin-like growth factor-1 receptor inhibitors without a prior diagnosis of IBD. Monitor individuals for signs and symptoms of IBD, including individuals with or without a history of IBD. Discontinue veligrotug if IBD is suspected.
    Headline
    diabetes mellitus
    General Information
    Hyperglycemia or an increase in blood glucose may occur in individuals treated with veligrotug. Ensure individuals with hyperglycemia or preexisting diabetes mellitus have appropriate glycemic control before and during treatment with veligrotug. Assess individuals for elevated blood glucose and symptoms of hyperglycemia prior to infusion and continue monitoring throughout the course of treatment. Hyperglycemic events should be managed with medication for glycemic control, if needed. Continue to monitor blood glucose after treatment with veligrotug in individuals who experience hyperglycemia during treatment.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Avoid the use of veligrotug during pregnancy. Veligrotug may cause fetal harm when administered during pregnancy. Inhibiting IGF-1R signaling impacts normal embryonic development and placental development and function. There are insufficient data on the use of veligrotug during pregnancy to evaluate for drug-associated risks and adverse developmental outcomes. Animal reproductive and developmental toxicity studies have not been conducted. If an individual becomes pregnant during treatment with veligrotug, veligrotug should be discontinued and the individual advised of the potential risk to the fetus.

    Headline
    infusion-related reactions
    General Information
    Veligrotug may cause infusion-related reactions. Infusion reactions have been reported in approximately 9% of individuals treated with veligrotug during clinical trials. Signs and symptoms of an infusion-related reaction include transient increases in blood pressure, fever, chills, headache, and fatigue. Infusion-related reactions may occur during or soon after an infusion. Reactions are typically mild or moderate in severity and can usually be managed with corticosteroids, antihistamines, and antipyretics. In those who experience an infusion reaction, consider standard premedication and/or administering infusions at a slower infusion rate.

Description
  Text
  Veligrotug is an insulin-like growth factor-1 receptor inhibitor indicated for the treatment of thyroid eye disease, regardless of thyroid eye disease activity or duration. During clinical trials, the drug improved proptosis, diplopia, and clinical activity scores in treated individuals. Adverse reactions include infusion reactions, hyperglycemia, and a potential for hearing loss, which may be permanent. Veligrotug was FDA approved in June 2026.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Veligrotug is a monoclonal antibody that binds IGF-1R and inhibits IGF-1R signaling by blocking ligand-induced receptor autophosphorylation; however, its mechanism of action in thyroid eye disease (TED) has not been fully characterized. A preclinical study describes veligrotug as a full antagonist anti-IGF-1R antibody that blocks IGF-1-mediated receptor activation, including inhibition of IGF-1R autophosphorylation and downstream AKT phosphorylation. These findings are consistent with IGF-1R antagonism as veligrotug's proposed mechanism in thyroid eye disease, a pathway described in literature as associated with reducing TED-related inflammation and proptosis.

      Preparation
Calculate the dose based on the individual's weight. Each vial contains 500 mg/10 mL (50 mg/mL) of veligrotug.
Veligrotug is compatible with the following:
IV bags: polyvinyl chloride (PVC), ethyl vinyl acetate (EVA), polypropylene-styrene-ethylene-butylene-styrene (PP-SEBS), and ethylene-propylene copolymer (polyolefin [PO]).
Inline 0.2- or 0.22-micron filters: polyethersulfone solution filter (PES) and polyvinylidene fluoride air filter (PVDF).
Infusion sets: polyvinyl chloride/bis (2-ethylhexyl) phthalate (PVC/DEHP), polyurethane (PUR), and polyethylene (without DEHP).
Remove a volume equivalent to the amount of veligrotug solution required for the dose from a 250 mL 0.9% Sodium Chloride infusion bag.
Withdraw the required volume of veligrotug from the vial based on the individual's weight and transfer it to the IV infusion bag. Mix the diluted solution by gentle inversion to avoid foaming. Do not shake.
Storage: May store the prepared IV infusion at room temperature 20 to 25 degrees C (68 to 77 degrees F) for up to 4 hours or in a refrigerator at 2 to 8 degrees C (36 to 46 degrees F) for up to 72 hours. Do not freeze. Protect from light.
 
Administration
Administer as an IV infusion. Do not administer as an IV push or bolus.
If refrigerated, allow the diluted solution to reach room temperature prior to infusion.
Administer the diluted solution IV over 45 minutes for the first solution. If well tolerated, subsequent infusions can be administered over a minimum of 30 minutes. If not tolerated, the minimum time should remain at 45 minutes.
Veligrotug should not be infused concomitantly with other agents.
Monitor for infusion reactions during administration. In individuals who experience an infusion reaction, consider giving standard premedication and/or administering infusions at a slower infusion rate.
