    pregnancy
    General Information
    Available data on the use of birch triterpenes during human pregnancy are insufficient to evaluate a drug-associated risk of major birth defects, miscarriage, or other adverse outcomes. In an animal reproduction study, oral administration of birch triterpenes to pregnant rats during the period of organogenesis had no effects on reproductive or fetal parameters. Systemic absorption of birch triterpenes in humans is low following topical administration; maternal use is not expected to result in fetal exposure to the drug.
    Headline

There are no data on the presence of birch triterpenes or its metabolites in human milk, the effects on the breast-fed child, or the effect on milk production. However, no effects on the breast-fed child are anticipated due to low systemic maternal exposure with topical use.

Description
  Text
  Birch triterpenes is a topical gel indicated for the treatment of wounds due to dystrophic and junctional epidermolysis bullosa in adult and pediatric patients 6 months and older. Birch triterpenes is a botanical drug substance composed of a mixture of pentacyclic triterpenes. The botanical drug substance is a dry extract, refined, from birch bark from Betula pendula Roth, Betula pubescens Ehrh., as well as hybrids of both species, quantified to betulin, lupeol, betulinic acid, erythrodiol, and oleanolic acid. In the phase 3 EASE trial (n = 223; 6 months to 81 years), there was an increase in healing and wound closure at 45 days in subjects who received birch triterpenes compared to patients who received an active control gel (41% vs. 29%, respectively).

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  The mechanism of action of birch triterpenes in the treatment of wounds associated with epidermolysis bullosa is unknown.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Topical Route
    Description
    The potential for systemic exposure to birch triterpenes is minimal. Following treatment with birch triterpenes once daily (n = 27), every 2 days (n = 33), every 3 days (n = 4), or once weekly (n = 2) for 90 days in 66 evaluable subjects (age range: 13 months to 52 years) with a mean treatment area of 12% body surface area or affected wound surface area of 0.11 m2 at baseline, betulin blood concentrations in 68% of subjects (n = 45) were below the lower limit of quantification of 10 ng/mL on day 90. The highest concentrations of betulin on day 90 in adult and pediatric subjects (median age: 10 years; range: 13 months to 17 years) were 33 ng/mL and 207 ng/mL, respectively.
  Text
  Birch triterpenes is administered topically. Plasma protein binding of betulin in vitro is more than 99.9%. In vitro, betulin was mainly metabolized by cytochrome CYP3A. The relative contribution of CYP3A and phase II metabolizing enzymes in the overall metabolism of betulin has not been fully characterized.
 
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes: none
Clinical findings from in vitro studies suggest that birch triterpenes has no clinically meaningful effect on the pharmacokinetics of drugs metabolized by CYP 1A2, 2B6, 2C8, 2C9, 2C19, 2D6, or 3A.

Wash hands before and after applying, or wear gloves for application.
Apply to cleansed wounds with wound dressing changes until the wound is healed; do not rub in gel.
Cover the wound with a sterile non-adhesive wound dressing or apply directly to the dressing so that the topical gel is in direct contact with the wound.
