    General Information

    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components.
    Headline
    Child-Pugh class B, Child-Pugh class C, hepatic failure
    General Information
    Monitor patients with moderate hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh Class B; total bilirubin greater than 1.5 to 3 times the upper limit of normal [ULN] and any AST) for increased adverse reactions; datopotamab deruxtecan is metabolized in the liver and limited data are available regarding the pharmacokinetics of datopotamab deruxtecan exposure in these patients. The recommended dosage of datopotamab deruxtecan has not been established in patients with severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh Class C; total bilirubin greater than 3 times ULN and any AST) or hepatic failure.
    Headline
    pulmonary disease
    General Information
    Use datopotamab deruxtecan with caution in patients with pulmonary disease as it can cause severe, life-threatening, or fatal interstitial lung disease (ILD) or pneumonitis. Patients with a history of ILD or pneumonitis were excluded from clinical trials. Monitor all patients for new or worsening symptoms indicative of ILD/pneumonitis (e.g., dyspnea, cough, fever) during treatment; an interruption of therapy, treatment with systemic corticosteroids, or discontinuation of therapy may be necessary.
    Headline
    geriatric
    General Information
    Monitor geriatric patients for increased adverse reactions. Of the 365 patients in the TROPION-Breast01 trial who received datopotamab deruxtecan at the recommended dose, grade 3 and higher adverse reactions occurred in 33% of patients aged 65 years and older compared to 15% of those younger than 65 years.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Pregnancy should be avoided by people who may become pregnant during datopotamab deruxtecan treatment and for at least 7 months after the last dose. Although there are no adequately controlled studies in people who may become pregnant, datopotamab deruxtecan can cause embryofetal harm if taken during pregnancy as the topoisomerase inhibitor component of the drug, DXd, is genotoxic and affects actively dividing cells. Individuals who are pregnant or who may become pregnant while receiving datopotamab deruxtecan should be apprised of the potential hazard to the fetus.
    Headline

Description
  Text
  Datopotamab deruxtecan is a Trop-2-directed antibody and topoisomerase inhibitor conjugate. It is approved for adult patients with unresectable or metastatic triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) who are not candidates for PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitor therapy, previously treated unresectable or metastatic, hormone receptor (HR)-positive, human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2)-negative breast cancer and for previously treated locally advanced or metastatic, EGFR-mutated non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC). Datopotamab deruxtecan can cause severe ocular adverse reactions and requires the use of prophylactic lubricating eye drops, avoidance of contact lenses, and monitoring by an eye care professional. It was approved by the FDA in January 2025.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Datopotamab deruxtecan is a Trop-2-directed antibody-drug conjugate. Datopotamab, a humanized anti-Trop2 immunoglobulin G1 monoclonal antibody, is covalently linked by a plasma-stable, tumor-selective, cleavable linker to deruxtecan (DXd), a potent topoisomerase I inhibitor. Upon binding to Trop2 on cells, including tumor cells, datopotamab deruxtecan undergoes receptor-mediated internalization and subsequent lysosomal degradation resulting in intracellular release of DXd. Once internalized, DXd causes DNA damage and apoptotic cell death. Datopotamab deruxtecan had antitumor activity in mouse models of lung cancer (including EGFR-mutated lung cancer) and breast cancer.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics

    Intravenous Route
    Description
    The Cmax of datopotamab deruxtecan and DXd after the first dose of the approved dosage in cycle 1 was 154 mcg/mL (CV, 20%) and 2.8 nanograms/mL (CV, 58%), respectively; the AUC was 671 mcg x day/mL (CV, 31%) and 18 nanograms x day/mL (CV, 43%), respectively. Datopotamab deruxtecan and released DXd Cmax and AUC increase proportionally over a dose range of 4 mg/kg to 10 mg/kg with no clinically significant datopotamab deruxtecan accumulation between cycles 1 and 3.

  Text
  Datopotamab deruxtecan is administered intravenously. Deruxtecan plasma protein binding is approximately 98%; the blood to plasma ratio is 0.6 in vitro. The mean steady state volume of distribution of datopotamab deruxtecan is 3.5 liters (CV, 23%). Datopotamab deruxtecan undergoes intracellular cleavage by lysosomal enzymes to release DXd. The humanized Trop-2 IgG1 monoclonal antibody portion is expected to be degraded into small peptides and amino acids via catabolic pathways in the same manner as endogenous IgG. DXd is primarily metabolized by CYP3A4. The datopotamab deruxtecan median elimination half-life is 4.8 days and the released DXd median apparent half-life is approximately 5.5 days. The estimated datopotamab deruxtecan clearance is 0.6 L/day (CV, 31.5%).
Pharmacodynamics
In a pooled population of non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) and breast cancer patients, higher systemic exposure to datopotamab deruxtecan was associated with an increased incidence of serious adverse reactions, dose interruptions, dose reductions, stomatitis/oral mucositis, ocular adverse reactions, and grade 3 or higher adverse reactions.
Trough concentrations of datopotamab deruxtecan were 45% to 61% lower in patients with anti-datopotamab deruxtecan antibodies compared to those without; there was no apparent effect of anti-drug antibodies on datopotamab deruxtecan safety or efficacy.
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes and drug transporters: CYP3A4, OATP1B1, OATP1B3, MATE2-K, P-gp, MRP1 and BCRP
In vitro, DXd is primarily metabolized by CYP3A4. However, no clinically significant differences in DXd pharmacokinetics were predicted when used concomitantly with itraconazole (strong CYP3A inhibitor) or ritonavir (dual OATP1B and CYP3A inhibitor). DXd is also a substrate of OATP1B1, OATP1B3, MATE2-K, P-gp, MRP1 and BCRP in vitro.

Slowly inject 5 mL of Sterile Water for Injection into each vial.

Dilution
Dilute the calculated volume of datopotamab deruxtecan in 100 mL of 5% Dextrose Injection using an infusion bag made of polyvinyl chloride or polyolefin (polypropylene or copolymer of ethylene and propylene); do not use 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection.
