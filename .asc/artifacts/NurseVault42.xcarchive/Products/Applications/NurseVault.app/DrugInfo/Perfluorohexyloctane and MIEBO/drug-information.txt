    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    There are no adequate and well controlled studies on perfluorohexyloctane use during human pregnancy. In animal reproduction studies, administration of oral doses 162-times the recommended human ophthalmic dose (RHOD) to pregnant rats during organogenesis did not result in any adverse developmental effects. In pregnant rabbits, oral doses of 41-times the RHOD and greater administered during organogenesis produced maternal toxicity (i.e., reduced body weight and food consumption), miscarriages, and reduced fetal weights; however, no fetal mortality nor malformations were observed.

  Adverse Reactions

    blurred vision, conjunctival hyperemia, ocular pruritus
    General Information
    The most common adverse reactions reported in patients receiving perfluorohexyloctane during clinical trials included blurred vision (1% to 3%) and conjunctival redness (conjunctival hyperemia, 1% to 3%). In 4 pre-marketing studies in which subjects were treated with at least 1 dose of perfluorohexyloctane, the most common adverse reaction was hypersensitivity. Signs of ocular hypersensitivity might include ocular pruritus, burning, stinging, irritation, redness, or swelling of the eyelids. Discontinue use if hypersensitivity is suspected.

Description
  Text
  Perfluorohexyloctane is an ophthalmic semifluorinated alkane indicated for treatment of the signs and symptoms of dry eye disease. It helps to reduce tear evaporation at the surface of the eye. The drug must be administered topically to each affected eye 4-times daily. Instruct patients to remove contact lenses prior to and for at least 30 minutes after drug administration. The most frequently reported adverse reactions with perfluorohexyloctane include blurry vision and conjunctival redness.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  The exact mechanism of action of perfluorohexyloctane in the treatment of dry eye disease is unknown; however, the drug does form a monolayer at the air-liquid interface of the tear film which is expected to reduce evaporation.

Pharmacokinetics
  Text
  Perfluorohexyloctane is administered topically to the eyes. Pharmacokinetic data are limited; however, a single pharmacokinetic study was conducted that showed low drug concentrations in the blood after topical ocular administration. Additionally, an in vitro analysis found the drug was not metabolized by human liver microsomes.
 
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes: None

  Route Specific Administrations
    Name
    Ophthalmic Administration
    Description
    Ophthalmic solution
Wash hands before and after use.
Remove contact lenses. They may be reinserted 30 minutes after drug administration.
Remove the cap from the eye drop bottle.
Holding the bottle upright, gently squeeze the bottle.
While squeezing, turn the bottle upside down and release the pressure to draw air into the bottle.
Keeping the bottle upside down, place the bottle above the eye and squeeze it again to release a drop into the eye.
Repeat the above steps for the second affected eye.
Care should be taken to avoid contamination. Do not touch the tip of the dropper to the eye, fingertips, or other surfaces. To avoid contamination or the spread of infection, do not use dropper for more than 1 person.
