    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Available data on the use of berdazimer during pregnancy are insufficient to evaluate a drug-associated risk of major birth defects, miscarriage, or other adverse outcomes. In animal studies, oral administration of berdazimer to rats and rabbits during organogenesis resulted in increased fetal malformations in the presence of severe maternal toxicity. The clinical relevance of these findings are unknown given that the bioavailability of berdazimer after oral administration is significantly higher than after topical administration. In rats, oral doses of 28, 95, and 189 mg/kg/day were administered during organogenesis. At the 95 and 189 mg/kg/day doses, maternal mortality and elevated methemoglobin concentrations were observed. Fetal skeletal malformations, fetal visceral malformations (cleft palate), and decreased fetal weights were observed at the 189 mg/kg/day exposure. In rabbits, oral doses of 47, 142, and 284 mg/kg/day were administered during organogenesis. Maternal mortality, aborted fetuses, adverse clinical observations, and elevated methemoglobin concentrations were observed at the 142 and 284 mg/kg/day doses. Decreased fetal weights were noted from pregnant rabbits receiving 284 mg/kg/day.

    General Information
    Application site reactions, including allergic contact dermatitis, have occurred in patients receiving berdazimer. Mild, moderate, and severe application site reactions reported during clinical trials include pain (12.3%, 6.1%, 0.2%, respectively), erythema (5.2%, 6%, 0.4%), pruritus (3.9%, 1.6%, 0.1%), exfoliative dermatitis (2%, 2.8%, 0.2%), contact dermatitis (1.7%, 2.8%, 0.3%), edema or swelling (1.9%, 1.5%, 0.1%), skin erosion (0.8%, 0.5%, 0.3%), skin discoloration (1.4%, 0.1%, 0%), vesicles (0.5%, 1%, 0%), skin irritation (0.8%, 0.4%, 0%), and infection (0.4%, 0.4%, 0.2%). If a patient experiences pain, pruritus, swelling, or erythema at the application site that lasts longer than 24 hours, suspect allergic contact dermatitis. If allergic contact dermatitis occurs, discontinue berdazimer and initiate appropriate treatment.

Description
  Text
  Berdazimer is a nitric oxide (NO) releasing agent with antiviral properties. It is indicated for the topical treatment of molluscum contagiosum (MC) in adults and pediatric patients 1 year of age and older. The drug is available as a kit containing 2 tubes of gel (Tube A and Tube B) and a Dosing Guide. Immediately prior to administration, the gels from Tube A and Tube B must be mixed using the Dosing Guide. Ensure the drug is applied only to the molluscum contagiosum lesions, as accidental exposure of the surrounding healthy skin may cause localized skin reactions (pain, vesiculation, erosion, exfoliation, pruritus, skin discoloration, erythema). Additionally, avoid administering the drug via the oral, mucosal, or ophthalmic routes.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Berdazimer is a nitric oxide (NO) releasing agent with antiviral properties. The product is available as a kit containing 2 tubes and a Dosing Guide; Tube A contains the berdazimer gel and Tube B contains hydrogel. When mixed, the hydrogel acts as a proton donor, which stimulates the release of nitric oxide from the berdazimer sodium macromolecule. Topical application of this mixture allows for timely and specific targeting of nitric oxide directly to the molluscum contagiosum lesions, where nitric oxide produces an antiviral effect through S-nitrosylation of proteins and nuclear factor-kappa B (NF-kB) modulation.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Topical Route
    Description
    Plasma hydrolyzed MAP3 (hMAP3), a structural marker for berdazimer, and nitrate concentrations were assessed in 34 patients (2 to 12 years of age) with molluscum contagiosum. The patients were administered once daily topical doses of berdazimer for 2 weeks to a total treatment area of 484 cm2 (mean lesion count = 34); the mean daily dose was approximately 3 mL. Quantifiable plasma hMAP3 concentrations were not observed in any patient on Day 1 and in only 2 patients on Day 15. Mean plasma nitrate concentrations were similar on Days 1 and 15, and remained relatively flat during the PK sampling period (baseline through 1, 3, and 6 hours post-application). No differences in methemoglobin concentrations were observed throughout the study.

  Route Specific Administrations
    Administration Details
      Name
      Other Topical Formulations
      Description
      Topical gel
Ensure you are ready to apply the treatment before preparing the product. DO NOT premix.
Wash and dry hands.
Dispense equal amounts (0.5 mL) of berdazimer gel from Tube A and hydrogel from Tube B onto the Dosing Guide. Immediately put the caps tightly back on Tubes A and B.
Using a fingertip, thoroughly mix the gels together on the Dosing Guide for at least 20 seconds. DO NOT mix the gels directly on the skin or palm of the hand.
Once mixed, immediately apply an even thin layer to each lesion. Ensure all lesions are treated.
Avoid applying to uninvolved skin. Do not apply to an open wound. If the gel gets into an open wound, rinse the wound with water. Do not treat lesions close to the eyes or get the gel in the eyes. If ocular exposure occurs, rinse the eyes thoroughly with water.
Wash hands after applying, unless the hands are being treated.
Allow the applied gel to dry for 10 minutes to avoid transferring the product to other areas, including the eyes.
Do not swim, bathe, or wash for 1 hour after the application.
Retain the Dosing Guide for future use, but throw away any unused mixture. The mixture cannot be stored.

    For topical use only. Do not administer via the ophthalmic, oral, or intravaginal routes. Seek medical attention if the gel is swallowed.
The product is supplied in a carton containing:

These gels are flammable (Tube A contains isopropyl alcohol, Tube B contains ethanol). Keep the product away from fire, flame, or smoking.
