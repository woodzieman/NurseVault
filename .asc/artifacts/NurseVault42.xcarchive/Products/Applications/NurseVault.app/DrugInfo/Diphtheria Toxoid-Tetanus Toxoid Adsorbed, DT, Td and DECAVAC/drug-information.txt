Drug Class: Diphtheria toxoid; tetanus toxoid adsorbed, Td is an intramuscular vaccine indicated for active immunization for the prevention of diphtheria and tetanus.

Routes: intramuscular; Common adult doses: 0.5 mL/dose IM.

Common Side Effects (excluding routine GI upset): anaphylactoid reactions, angioedema, arthralgia, bronchospasm, dizziness, fatigue, fever, headache, lymphadenopathy, maculopapular rash, malaise, myalgia, peripheral edema, pruritus, rash, urticaria, guillain-barre syndrome, neuritis, paresthesias, weakness

In an Overdose: No overdose/antidote information is listed in this file.

Key Nursing Considerations: Contraindications/precautions: Do not administer a DT or Td vaccine to a patient with thimerosal hypersensitivity. All DT and Td products may contain at least trace amounts of thimerosal.

Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  General Information
  Do not administer a DT or Td vaccine to a patient with thimerosal hypersensitivity. All DT and Td products may contain at least trace amounts of thimerosal. The single dose, preservative free products may contain trace thimerosal concentrations (e.g., Decavac 10 years previously should generally receive Td in preference to Tdap while they are pregnant, although Tdap is not contraindicated during pregnancy. If the pregnant woman has an indication for Td but is likely to have sufficient protection against tetanus and diphtheria and has not previously received a dose of Tdap, the CDC's ACIP recommends that Tdap be administered in the immediate postpartum period rather than Td being administered during pregnancy.
 
Whether or not the diphtheria and tetanus toxoids adsorbed vaccine is excreted in human milk is unknown. According to ACIP guidelines, vaccines administered to a lactating woman do not affect the safety of breast-feeding. In addition, breast-feeding does not adversely affect immunization and is not a contraindication for any vaccine.
 
Safety and efficacy in neonates or infants < 6 weeks of age have not been established. It is recommended to not administer DT to individuals in these patient populations.
 
Do not give DT or Td vaccines via intravenous administration or subcutaneous administration. DT or Td vaccines are for intramuscular (IM) use only. Take care to avoid injecting DT or Td into a blood vessel (avoid intraarterial administration) (see Administration section). Any condition that contraindicates the use of intramuscular (IM) injections contraindicates the use of DT or Td. The manufacturer recommends avoidance of DT or Td for persons with any bleeding disorder or coagulopathy, such as hemophilia or thrombocytopenia, or for persons with vitamin K deficiency or on anticoagulant therapy unless the potential benefit clearly outweighs the risk of administration. If the decision is made to administer the vaccine in such persons, it should be given with caution, with steps taken to avoid the risk of bleeding and hematoma formation following injection.
 
DT or Td immunization should be postponed in patients with moderate or severe febrile illness, a severe respiratory infection, shock, or acute infection. Minor illness, such as a mild upper respiratory infection with or without low grade fever, does not preclude vaccine administration. Patients receiving antibiotic therapy may receive this vaccine. If a contraindication to using DT or Td exists, passive immunization with TIG should be considered whenever an injury resulting in broken skin other than a clean or minor wound is sustained.
 
Patients suffering significant immunosuppression may not have an adequate antibody response to vaccination with DT or Td toxoids and vaccines. The vaccine should be administered before or 1 month after completing immunosuppressive therapy, if possible. According to the CDC, patients with HIV infection may receive DT or Td immunization. Vaccines given to immunocompromised patients cannot be assumed to be as effective as when given to normal individuals. Post administration antibody measurement can be done if available. In the absence of specific antibody information, appropriate immune globulins should be considered for exposures to vaccine-preventable diseases.
 
If Guillain-Barre Syndrome has occurred within 6 weeks of receipt of a prior vaccine containing tetanus toxoid, the decision to give subsequent doses of any vaccine containing tetanus toxoid should be based on careful consideration of the potential benefits and possible risks.

    Headline
    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components. The tip caps on the prefilled syringes of diphtheria toxoid; tetanus toxoid adsorbed (Tenivac) may contain natural rubber latex, which may cause an allergic reaction in latex sensitive individuals.
    Headline
    Treatment-Related Restrictions of Use
    General Information
    Deferral of diphtheria toxoid; tetanus toxoid adsorbed, Td vaccine may be appropriate for a patient with a moderate or severe acute illness such as infection with or without fever. The Advisory Committee on Immunization Practices recommends that vaccinations be delayed during the course of a moderate or severe acute febrile illness and administered after the acute phase of illness has resolved. All vaccines can be administered to persons with minor illnesses such as diarrhea, mild upper-respiratory infection with or without low-grade fever, or other low-grade febrile illness. Vaccinate persons with moderate or severe febrile illness as soon as they have recovered from the acute phase of the illness. If a contraindication to using Td exists, passive immunization with Tetanus Immune Globulin (TIG) should be considered whenever an injury resulting in broken skin other than a clean or minor wound is sustained.
    Headline
    immunosuppression
    General Information
    Patients with significant immunosuppression may not have an adequate antibody response to vaccination with diphtheria toxoid; tetanus toxoid adsorbed, Td vaccine. Immunosuppressed persons may include patients with human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) infection; severe combined immunodeficiency (SCID); hypogammaglobulinemia; agammaglobulinemia; altered immune states due to generalized neoplastic disease; or an immune system compromised by radiation therapy or drug therapy (e.g., chemotherapy or corticosteroid therapy with greater than physiologic doses). Short-term (less than 2 weeks) corticosteroid therapy or intra-articular, bursal, or tendon injections with corticosteroids should not be immunosuppressive.
    Headline
    premature neonates
    General Information
    In some premature neonates, apnea after intramuscular vaccination has been observed. Consider the infant’s medical status and the vaccine's potential benefits and possible risks when deciding when to administer an intramuscular vaccine such as diphtheria toxoid; tetanus toxoid adsorbed (DT) to infants born prematurely.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Available human data are insufficient to assess a vaccine-related risk associated with the administration of diphtheria toxoid; tetanus toxoid, adsorbed, Td vaccine during pregnancy. In an animal developmental-toxicity study, no evidence of fetal harm was observed. Toxoid-containing vaccines are not live vaccines. According to CDC/ACIP guidance, Tdap is recommended during each pregnancy, preferably at 27 through 36 weeks' gestation, regardless of when the last tetanus- or diphtheria-toxoid-containing vaccine was given. It may be administered earlier when indicated, but only 1 dose should be given per pregnancy. For individuals who are unvaccinated or incompletely vaccinated, Tdap should replace one dose of Td, with Td used for any remaining doses needed to complete the series.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    No human or animal data are available on the presence of diphtheria toxoid; tetanus toxoid, adsorbed in human milk, its effects on the breastfed child, or its effects on milk production. Non-live vaccines administered to lactating individuals do not affect the safety of breast-feeding for the lactating individual or their child.
    Headline
    history of significant adverse event(s) associated with previous use
    General Information
    The diphtheria toxoid; tetanus toxoid adsorbed, Td vaccine should be used with caution in individuals who have experienced a history of significant adverse event(s) associated with previous use of a vaccine containing diptheria toxoid or tetanus toxoid that is not attributable to another cause. If Guillain-Barre syndrome has occurred within 6 weeks of receipt of a prior vaccine containing tetanus toxoid, the decision to administer any vaccine containing tetanus toxoid should be based on careful consideration of the potential benefits and possible risks.

When feasible, administer indicated vaccines at least 4 weeks before planned high-dose corticosteroid therapy or wait at least 2 weeks after discontinuation. If vaccine administration is necessary, consider revaccination following restoration of immune competence. Counsel patients receiving high-dose corticosteroids about the possibility of a diminished vaccine response and to continue to follow precautions to avoid exposure after receiving the vaccine.

Although conflicting study results were documented, the larger trial found no causal relationship. Description
  Text
  Diphtheria toxoid; tetanus toxoid adsorbed, Td is an intramuscular vaccine indicated for active immunization for the prevention of diphtheria and tetanus. Diphtheria and tetanus toxoids are prepared from toxins produced during culture growth of Corynebacterium diphtheriae and Clostridium tetani, respectively, and detoxified with formaldehyde. The Td vaccine (Tenivac) is indicated for primary and booster immunization in individuals 7 years and older. Td may be administered off-label to children younger than 7 years who develop a contraindication to pertussis-containing vaccines; however, these children may have suboptimal protection against diphtheria. An Institute of Medicine review found evidence for a causal relationship between tetanus toxoid and both brachial neuritis and Guillain-Barre syndrome. If Guillain-Barre syndrome occurs within 6 weeks after receipt of a previous tetanus toxoid-containing vaccine, the decision to administer Tenivac or another tetanus toxoid-containing vaccine should be based on careful consideration of the potential risks and benefits. Tenivac was FDA-approved in 2003.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Diphtheria toxoid; tetanus toxoid adsorbed, Td induces active immunity for the prevention of diphtheria and tetanus by stimulating production of neutralizing antibodies against diphtheria toxin and tetanus toxin.
Diphtheria Toxoid: Diphtheria is an acute toxin-mediated disease caused by toxigenic strains of Corynebacterium diphtheriae. Diphtheria toxoid induces the production of neutralizing antibodies against diphtheria toxin. A serum diphtheria antitoxin concentration of at least 0.1 International Units/mL is generally regarded as protective. A concentration of 0.01 International Units/mL is the lowest level associated with some degree of protection, while a concentration of at least 1 International Unit/mL has been associated with long-term protection.
Tetanus Toxoid: Tetanus is caused by a potent neurotoxin produced by Clostridium tetani. Tetanus toxoid induces the production of neutralizing antibodies against tetanus toxin. A serum tetanus antitoxin concentration of at least 0.01 International Units/mL, as measured by a neutralization assay, is considered the minimum protective level. A concentration of at least 0.1 International Units/mL, as measured by the ELISA used in some clinical studies of TENIVAC, is considered protective.

During a booster immunization study, 470 adolescents and children 11 to 17 years of age received a booster dose of Td. Infants 2 to 6 months
The immunogenicity of a pediatric DT vaccine was evaluated in 137 infants who received DT at 2, 4, and 6 months of age. At 8 months of age, diphtheria and tetanus antitoxin concentrations of 0.01 International Units/mL or greater, a concentration associated with minimal protection, were detected in 99% and 100% of infants, respectively. Geometric mean titers ranged from 0.25 to 0.35 International Units/mL for diphtheria antibodies, and from 0.75 to 0.8 International Units/mL for tetanus antibodies.

Administration
  General Information
  For storage information, see the specific product information within the How Supplied section.
Verify immunization status in the state or local immunization information system in addition to the health care electronic record. If this vaccine has been previously given, question the patient, parent, or guardian about any symptoms or signs of an adverse reaction.

Record manufacturer, lot number, administration date, and the name and address of the person administering the vaccine.
