    infection
    General Information
    Treatment with brepocitinib increases the risk of serious infections that may lead to hospitalization or death. Avoid the use in individuals with active, serious infection, including localized infections. Use brepocitinib with caution in individuals at increased risk of serious infections, including those with chronic or recurrent infection, a history of serious or opportunistic infections, and underlying conditions that may predispose to infection. Individuals who have been exposed to tuberculosis (TB) or resided or traveled in areas of endemic TB or mycoses are at an increased risk of TB or fungal infections. Brepocitnib increases the risk of TB infection. Screen all patients for active or latent TB infection prior to initiating therapy and periodically during treatment. If positive, treat TB prior to brepocitinib treatment. Prior to initiation of brepocitinib, consider anti-tuberculosis therapy in individuals with a past history of latent or active TB in whom an adequate course of treatment cannot be confirmed. Consult a physician with expertise in treating TB about whether starting anti-tuberculosis therapy is appropriate for the individual patient. Viral reactivation, including herpes zoster reactivation, has occurred with brepocitinib treatment. Consider temporarily interrupting brepocitinib therapy in patients who develop herpes zoster until the episode resolves. Perform screening for viral hepatitis in accordance with clinical guidelines before starting brepocitinib. Treatment with brepocitinib is not recommended in individuals with active hepatitis B or hepatitis C infection. Closely monitor individuals for signs and symptoms of TB, other infections, and viral hepatitis reactivation during and after therapy, even in patients who tested negative for latent TB prior to initiation. If hepatitis B virus DNA is detected during treatment with brepocitinib, consult a liver specialist. For patients who develop an infection during brepocitinib treatment, perform a prompt and complete diagnostic workup appropriate for immunocompromised individuals and initiate appropriate antimicrobial therapy. If a serious infection, including serious opportunistic infection, occurs, interrupt treatment until the infection resolves or is adequately treated.

    cardiovascular disease, mortality, serious cardiovascular events, thromboembolism, thrombophilia, tobacco smoking
    General Information
    Serious cardiovascular events and thromboembolism have been reported with the Janus kinase (JAK) inhibitors class of medications, including brepocitinib. Avoid use of brepocitinib in individuals with thrombophilia or other risk factors for thrombosis. Higher rates of all-cause mortality (including sudden cardiovascular death), major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE; defined as cardiovascular death, non-fatal myocardial infarction (MI), and non-fatal stroke), and thrombosis (including deep venous thrombosis (DVT), pulmonary embolism (PE), and arterial thrombosis) were observed in individuals with rheumatoid arthritis taking another JAK inhibitor compared to individuals taking tumor necrosis factor (TNF) inhibitors. Brepocitinib is not approved for the treatment of rheumatoid arthritis. Individuals at an increased risk for these adverse events include those with a current or past history of tobacco smoking or risk factors for cardiovascular disease. Thrombosis, including DVT, PE, and arterial thrombosis, have occurred in individuals taking JAK inhibitors, such as brepocitinib, for the treatment of inflammatory conditions; many events were serious and some resulted in death. Advise patients to seek emergency medical attention if they experience signs and symptoms of serious cardiovascular events. Discontinue brepocitinib in an individual who has experienced a myocardial infarction or stroke. If symptoms of thrombosis develop, discontinue treatment, promptly evaluate the individual, and treat appropriately.
    Headline
    malignancy
    General Information
    Breprocitinib may increase the risk of malignancy. Malignancies have occurred in individuals treated with brepocitinib. In a large, postmarketing, randomized safety study, a higher rate of malignancies (excluding non-melanoma skin cancer), lymphomas, and lung cancer was observed in individuals with rheumatoid arthritis treated with another Janus kinase (JAK) inhibitor compared to those treated with tumor necrosis factor (TNF) inhibitors. Individuals with a current or past history of tobacco smoking were also at a higher risk of lung cancer and an increased risk of overall malignancies. Brepocitinib is not approved for use in individuals with rheumatoid arthritis. Consider individual benefits and risks prior to starting or continuing brepocitinib therapy, particularly in individuals with a known malignancy other than successfully treated non-melanoma skin cancer, risk factors such as smoking, and those who develop malignancy during brepocitinib treatment. Periodic skin examination is recommended for people at increased skin-cancer risk. Instruct individuals to limit ultraviolet exposure by wearing protective clothing and using broad-spectrum sunscreen.

    pregnancy
    General Information
    Brepocitinib may cause fetal harm when administered during pregnancy. Available human pregnancy data are insufficient to establish a drug-associated risk of major birth defects, miscarriage, or adverse maternal or fetal outcomes. In animal studies, fetal skeletal malformations, early or late resorption, lower mean number of viable fetuses, and increased post-implantation loss occurred in rats and rabbits at exposures of approximately 1.6 and 3 times the maximum recommended human dose exposure (MRHD), respectively. A rat pre- and postnatal study identified no adverse developmental, neurobehavioral, or reproductive effects in offspring at exposures up to approximately 5.5 times the human exposure. Greater dermatomyositis disease activity during pregnancy is associated with adverse pregnancy outcomes including preterm delivery, low birth weight, and small-for-gestational-age birth. Report any pregnancy to Priovant Therapeutics by calling 1-800-511-9141 or emailing contactcenter@priovant.com.

    infection, influenza, sepsis, viral reactivation
    General Information
    Infection, including serious bacterial infection, invasive fungal infection presenting as disseminated rather than localized disease, viral infection (e.g., herpes zoster), tuberculosis infection, or infections due to other opportunistic pathogens have been reported in individuals receiving brepocitinib. Infections occurred in 69% of individuals receiving brepocitinib and 57% of placebo-treated patients during a clinical trial. Upper respiratory tract infection (15%), urinary tract infection (11%), bronchitis (10%), influenza (6%) and COVID-19 were the most commonly reported infections. Serious infections occurred in 10% of brepocitinib-treated individuals compared with 1% of placebo-treated individuals. Pneumonia and sepsis were the most frequently reported serious infections, each occurring in 2 individuals receiving brepocitinib. Viral reactivation occurred in 5% of individuals receiving brepocitinib; all reported viral reactivations were herpes zoster. If an individual develops herpes zoster, consider interrupting brepocitinib until the episode resolves. Evaluate and test individuals for latent and active tuberculosis (TB) infections before and during treatment and treat TB when indicated. Consider anti-TB therapy prior to initiation of brepocitinib in individuals with a past history of latent or active TB in whom an adequate course of treatment cannot be confirmed. Closely monitor individuals for signs and symptoms of infection during and after treatment with brepocitinib. If a serious infection occurs, interrupt treatment until the infection resolves or is adequately treated. If a new infection occurs, promptly complete diagnostic testing, initiate appropriate antimicrobial therapy and closely monitor the individual.

Description
  Text
  Brepocitinib is a Janus kinase (JAK) and tyrosine kinase 2 (TYK2) inhibitor indicated for the treatment of dermatomyositis. Dermatomyositis is a rare, immune-mediated inflammatory disorder characterized by inflammatory and degenerative changes of the muscles and skin. Janus kinases are intracellular enzymes that transmit signals arising from cytokine interactions on the cellular membrane to influence cellular processes of immune function. JAK inhibitors have been shown useful for a variety of inflammatory conditions. Brepocitinib carries a boxed warning regarding the increased risk for serious infections, mortality, malignancy, major adverse cardiovascular events, and thrombosis. Do not use brepocitinib concomitantly with other JAK inhibitors, other TYK2 inhibitors, or biologic DMARDs. Verify the pregnancy status of people who may become pregnant prior to starting brepocitinib therapy and advise them to use effective contraception while taking the medication and for 3 days after their last dose. Brepocitinib was FDA-approved in August 2026.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Brepocitinib is a Janus kinase (JAK) and tyrosine kinase 2 (TYK2) inhibitor. Janus kinases, part of the tyrosine kinase enzyme family, are intracellular enzymes that transmit signals arising from cytokine receptor activation to influence cellular processes of immune cell function and hematopoeiesis. Upon ligand and receptor interaction, JAKs phosphorylate and activate signal transducers and activators of transcription proteins (STATs), which modulate intracellular activity including gene expression. JAK-mediated signaling is pivotal in immune activation, as cytokine receptors are expressed on most immune cells. JAKs are responsible for the intracellular signaling in type I and type II cytokine receptors located on over 50 cytokines, interleukins, interferons, colony-stimulating factors and hormones. Brepocitinib has greater inhibitor potency against TYK2 and JAK1 than against JAK2 (3.4-fold higher or more) and JAK3 (286-fold higher or more). JAKs mediate cytokine signaling through hetero- and homodimeric pairing. Brepocitinib inhibits TYK2- and JAK1-dependent cytokine signaling, thereby reducing downstream STAT phosphorylation and transcriptional responses. This may affect signaling pathways involving TYK2/JAK1, TYK2/JAK2, JAK1/JAK3, and TYK2/JAK1/JAK2. The relevance of inhibition of specific JAK combinations to therapeutic effectiveness is not known.
 
Pharmacodynamics
In peripheral blood mononuclear cells isolated from individuals with dermatomyositis, brepocitinib resulted in concentration-dependent inhibition of type I interferon (IFN) induced and interleukin-6 (IL-6) induced phosphorylation of STAT1 and STAT3. In whole blood from healthy donors, brepocitinib resulted in concentration-dependent inhibition of IFN-gamma, IL-10, IL-12, IL-13, and IL-23 induced STAT1, STAT3, STAT4, STAT6, and STAT3 phosphorylation, respectively.

  Text
  Brepocitinib is administered orally. Plasma protein binding is 39% and the blood-to-plasma partition ratio is 0.84. Brepocitinib primarily undergoes metabolism via CYP1A1 and CYP1A2, with a minor contribution from CYP3A4. Pharmacologic activity is attributable to the parent drug. Unchanged brepocitinib and the inactive M1 metabolite represented approximately 48% and 37% of circulating plasma radioactivity, respectively, in a radiolabeled study. Following radiolabeled brepocitinib administration, 88% of the total radioactivity was recovered in urine (7.7% as unchanged brepocitinib and 51% as M1 metabolite) and 8.7% was recovered in feces (0.8% as unchanged drug and 0.7% as M1). The mean terminal half-life ranges from 5.4 to 12 hours.
 
Pharmacodynamics
Following administration of brepocitinib 30 mg once daily to individuals with dermatomyositis, a reduction in the expression of genes associated with Type 1 signaling was observed by week 12 and was sustained through week 52 in whole blood; the clinical relevance of this observation is unclear.
 
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes and drug transporters: CYP1A1, CYP1A2, CYP3A4, P-gp, BCRP, OCT1, OCT2, MATE1, and MATE2-K.
Brepocitinib is primarily a substrate of CYP1A1 and CYP1A2, with minor contributions from CYP3A4. Smoking causes induction of CYP1A1 and CYP1A2. At steady-state, the brepocitinib AUC and Cmax are estimated to be 45% and 33% lower, respectively, in current smokers compared to current non-smokers. Brepocitinib inhibits P-gp, BCRP, OCT1, OCT2, MATE1, and MATE2-K in vitro. The effect on P-gp and BCRP has not been clinically studied.

Dosage Adjustment Guidelines
  Hepatic Impairment
  No dosage adjustment is recommended for mild (Child-Pugh A) or moderate (Child-Pugh B) hepatic impairment. Brepocitinib has not been evaluated and is not recommended in severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh C).
  Renal Impairment
  No dosage adjustment is recommended for mild renal impairment (eGFR 60 to 89 mL/minute) or moderate renal impairment (eGFR 30 to 59 mL/minute). Brepocitinib is not recommended in severe renal impairment (eGFR less than 30 mL/minute).
