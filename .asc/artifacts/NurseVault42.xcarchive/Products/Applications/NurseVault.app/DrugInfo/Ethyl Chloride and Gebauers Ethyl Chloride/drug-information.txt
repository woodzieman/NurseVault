      Route Age Groups
        Heading
        Adults, Adolescents, and Children >/= 3 years
        Dosage Description
        Hold bottle or aerosol can 12—18 inches away from the target area at an acute angle to the skin. If spray is near the face, cover the patient's eyes, nose, and mouth. Spray in parallel sweeps of 0.5—1 inch apart at a rate of 4 inches/second. Continue spraying until the entire muscle is covered. During the spraying process, stretch the muscle with gradually increasing force until the muscle relaxes to its full normal length. Apply moist heat for 10—15 minutes after treatment. Repeat treatment if needed.
    Heading
    For use as a counterirritant in the management of musculoskeletal pain (myofascial pain), restricted motion, and muscle tension

Clinicians should closely monitor patients for the development of methemoglobinemia when a combination local anesthetic is used during a procedure. Adverse Reactions
  Headline
  Adverse Reactions
  Adverse Reactions
    Headline
    impaired wound healing, infection, skin discoloration, skin irritation
    General Information
    Skin irritation may rarely occur following administration of ethyl chloride. If ethyl chloride is applied in excess, freezing of the skin may develop. Symptoms associated with ethyl chloride induced freezing includes pain, skin discoloration, impaired wound healing, and increased infection potential. During the application process, health care providers should take measures to avoid skin freezing.

Description
  Text
  Ethyl chloride is a topical local anesthetic agent. It is used for local anesthesia to control pain associated with injections, minor surgery, athletic injuries, and myofascial pain, and for restricted motion and muscle spasm. Ethyl chloride works by inhibiting the conduction of nerve impulses from sensory nerves. Ethyl chloride was initially marketed for use before 1962, which is when Congress passed the amended Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act requiring that the FDA establish both safety and efficacy for all subsequently approved drugs.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  When administered topically to the skin, ethyl chloride immediately evaporates resulting in a cooling effect at the application site. This coldness decreases conduction velocity of the C and A-delta nerve fibers; thereby interrupting nociceptive inputs to the spinal cord and ultimately resulting in transient numbness at the application site.

      Spray Formulation
For topical use only. Avoid exposure to eyes, nose, and mouth.
If using the glass bottle: invert the bottle over the treatment area while completely opening the dispenseal spring valve.
If using the stream spray: turn the blue locking ring counterclockwise in the direction of the open arrow, aim the spray at the intended area, and fully depress the handle.
If using the aerosol can (i.e., Mist Spray Can): hold the can upright over the treatment area and depress the valve completely.
Flammable, keep away from heat, sparks, open flames including smoking, hot surfaces, and cautery equipment.
 
Anesthesia prior to injection or minor surgery:
Clean injection or surgical site with a suitable antiseptic prior to administering ethyl chloride.
Hold the bottle or aerosol can 3 to 9 inches from the treatment area. Spray continuously for 3 to 7 seconds using the bottle, for 4 to 10 seconds using the aerosol can, or until the target area begins to turn white; do not frost the skin.
Inject or make incision immediately after ethyl chloride application as the anesthetic effect lasts approximately one minute.
 
Temporary relief of minor sports injuries:
Determine the extent of injury. The amount of cooling depends on the dosage, which varies with the duration of application; use the smallest dose necessary to achieve the desired effect.
Hold the bottle or aerosol can 3 to 9 inches from the treatment area. Spray continuously for 3 to 7 seconds using the bottle, for 4 to 10 seconds using the aerosol can, or until the target area begins to turn white; do not frost the skin.
 
Myofascial pain:
Evaluate the patient for pain caused by an active, irritated trigger point.
Hold the inverted bottle 12 to 18 inches from target area, aiming the stream to spray at an acute angle to the skin.
Cover the patients eyes, nose, and mouth if spraying near the face.
Spray in parallel sweeps of 0.5 to 1 inch apart at a rate of 4 inches per second. Continue until entire muscle has been covered.
Passively stretch the muscle during the application process. Increase the stretching force with each successive sweep to reach the full normal length of the muscle.
Rewarm the muscle. Repeat the process if needed.
Following treatment, apply moist heat for 10 to 15 minutes.
