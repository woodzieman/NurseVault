Interactions
Chlorhexidine: (Major) Avoid the use of chlorhexidine gluconate solution on the wound following application of keratinocytes and dermal fibroblast collagen. Administering these drugs together may reduce wound repair and regeneration. Chlorhexidine gluconate has been shown to be toxic to keratinocytes and human dermal fibroblasts.
Mafenide: (Major) Avoid the use of mafenide acetate in patients receiving treatment with keratinocytes and dermal fibroblast collagen. Administering these drugs together may reduce wound repair and regeneration. Mafenide acetate, a topical synthetic sulfonamide antibiotic, has been shown to reduce keratinocyte viability and disrupt the integrity of tissue-engineered human skin substitutes composed of NIKS keratinocytes or primary keratinocytes.
Silver Nitrate: (Major) Avoid the use of silver nitrate in patients receiving treatment with keratinocytes and dermal fibroblast collagen. Administering these drugs together may reduce wound repair and regeneration. In vitro data suggest silver may decrease the viability of keratinocytes and human dermal fibroblasts.
Silver Sulfadiazine: (Major) Avoid the use of silver-containing antimicrobials or dressing (such as silver sulfadiazine) in patients receiving treatment with keratinocytes and dermal fibroblast collagen. Administering these drugs together may reduce wound repair and regeneration. In vitro data suggest silver may decrease the viability of keratinocytes and human dermal fibroblasts.

Description
  Text
  NOTE: All products associated with this monograph are off-market. The content is retained for historical reference only and is no longer actively maintained.
 
Keratinocytes; fibroblasts; collagen are allogeneic cellularized scaffold (sheet) products containing human extracellular matrix proteins and metabolically active cells that produce and secrete a variety of growth factors and cytokines. When applied topically, these products aid in wound repair and regeneration. Two products are available. The first to be approved was Gintuit, which is applied topically (non-submerged) to a surgically created vascular wound bed to treat mucogingival conditions in adults. The second product is Stratagraft, which is topically applied to a prepared wound bed (excision/debridement) to treat adults with thermal burns containing intact dermal elements for which surgical intervention is clinically indicated (i.e., deep partial-thickness burns). Both products are derived from human and animal material; as such, the possibility exists for transmission of infectious diseases or infectious agents. Health care providers are advised to monitor for both early and late signs and symptoms of hypersensitivity reactions; if present, treat in accordance with standard practice.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  NOTE: All products associated with this monograph are off-market. The content is retained for historical reference only and is no longer actively maintained.
 
Keratinocytes; fibroblasts; collagen are allogeneic cellularized scaffold (sheet) products containing human extracellular matrix proteins and metabolically active cells that produce and secrete a variety of growth factors and cytokines. Introduction of these growth factors, cytokines, and extracellular matrix proteins to the treated site aids in wound repair and regeneration. These products do not remain permanently engraft, but rather, are replaced by the patient's own cells over time.

Preparation of keratinocytes; fibroblasts; collagen (Stratagraft)
The product is supplied as follows:
A carton with an inner foil pouch [shipped on dry ice and stored between -70 and -90 degrees C (-94 to -130 degrees F)].
The foil pouch contains a Product Dish which holds a single Stratagraft 100 cm2 rectangular sheet loosely adhered to a polycarbonate membrane that is constrained within a polystyrene insert tray.
Hold Solution in a plastic bottle and packaged separately within a foil pouch [shipped at ambient temperature and stored between 2 and 8 degrees C (36 to 46 degrees F)].
Hold Dish packaged separately in a clear pouch (shipped and stored at ambient temperature).
Additional supplied needed for the preparation:
Mesher: Stratagraft is compatible with autograft meshing devices (crushing or non-crushing) and can be meshed at ratios up to 1:1.
Warming oven/cabinet or water bath.
The product must be prepared in an appropriate surgical environment. Two operators (1 sterile operator and 1 non-sterile operator) are required to prepare Stratagraft.
Determine the number of Stratagraft constructs that need to be thawed based on the size of the wound. The following instructions are for preparing 1 construct. If 2 or more constructs are required, prepare them in 1 or more batches. Stratagraft preparation should take approximately 20 minutes for a single construct or group of constructs.
Remove the Hold Solution bottle from its pouch and inspect for discoloration or particulate matter. The Hold Solution is a clear liquid. DO NOT use if the solution appears cloudy or turbid. Use 1 bottle of Hold Solution for 1 construct being prepared for placement.
Warm the Hold Solution as follows:
Option 1: Place Hold Solution bottle in a warming oven/cabinet operating at 35 to 39 degrees C (95 to 102 degrees F) for AT LEAST 45 minutes prior to use.
Option 2: Place Hold Solution bottle in a water bath at 35 to 39 degrees C (95 to 102 degrees F) for AT LEAST 15 minutes prior to use. DO NOT submerge the cap or threads of the bottle.
The non-sterile operator peels open the seal of the Hold Dish clear pouch and aseptically presents the Hold Dish to the sterile operator. The sterile operator removes the Hold Dish and places it in the sterile field.
The Stratagraft carton is removed from the freezer or dry ice. The next 6 steps MUST be completed within 10 minutes of product removal from the freezer or dry ice.
Open the carton and remove the foil pouch.
Peel the foil pouch open and carefully remove the CLOSED Product Dish. The unopened Product Dish containing the construct may be placed on a flat surface outside the sterile field.
The non-sterile operator removes 1 Hold Solution bottle from the warming device and IMMEDIATELY pours the entire contents of that bottle into 1 sterile Hold Dish using aseptic technique.
The non-sterile operator then holds the Product Dish and removes the Product Dish lid without contacting the insert tray.
The sterile operator aseptically removes the insert tray from the Product Dish using either sterile forceps or sterile gloved fingers.
The sterile operator uses sterile gloved fingers to place the insert tray into the Hold Dish beginning with 1 edge and lowering it into the opposite edge to minimize bubbles from being trapped beneath the insert tray. If bubbles are present beneath the insert tray, gently lift the insert tray and place it slowly back down into the Hold Solution.
Maintain the construct in the Hold Solution for a minimum of 15 minutes and a maximum of 4 hours.
Remove Stratagraft from the polycarbonate membrane of the insert tray with sterile gloved fingers or a pair of atraumatic forceps and mesh the Stratagraft up to a 1:1 ratio. The Stratagraft construct may be meshed with either side up; however, prior to meshing, make note of which side is the dermis (shiny) side and which side is the epidermis (matte) side. Stratagraft is packaged with the dermal (shiny) side in contact with the polycarbonate membrane.
Do not allow Stratagraft to dry. Use Hold Solution, sterile 0.9% Normal Saline, or Lactated Ringer's Solution to moisten the mesher/tissue board as needed to prevent adhesion and to maintain Stratagraft moisture.
Application of Stratagraft
Stratagraft is applied under appropriate aseptic conditions by a trained health care provider.
If utilizing fibrin glue to anchor Stratagraft, apply fibrin glue on the prepared wound bed prior to placing the meshed Stratagraft. This step may be skipped if utilizing anchoring methods other than fibrin glue.
Place the meshed Stratagraft with the dermal (shiny) side down in contact with the patient's prepared wound bed. Ensure the epidermal (matte) side is facing up.
When placing multiple constructs, abut the edges. Overlapping the edges is NOT required.
If the area to be treated is smaller than 1 construct, trim excess Stratagraft before or after anchoring to the wound bed. Ensure Stratagraft has contact across the entire surface area of the wound bed. DO NOT stretch or expand Stratagraft, as doing so may degrade the structural integrity.
Anchor Stratagraft with staples, sutures, or tissue adhesive such as cyanoacrylate.
Place a porous, nonadherent contact dressing over Stratagraft and leave in place for 1 week before changing.
Place a second layer of dressing that does NOT contain silver.
As clinically appropriate and at the discretion of the physician, place an outer bolster or wrap to keep Stratagraft from moving.
Dispose of any unused product and any materials that have come in contact with Stratagraft as surgical biohazardous waste in accordance with local requirements.

Prepare application site
Following local anesthesia, surgically create a vascular wound bed with viable wound edges and establish adequate hemostasis.
Preparation of keratinocytes; fibroblasts; collagen (Gintuit)
The sterility of Gintuit is confirmed prior to shipping. However, test results are not available for up to 14 days after shipping. If microbial contamination is detected after the product has shipped, the manufacturer will notify health care providers and recommend appropriate actions.
Inspect sealed bag for evidence of damage. Do not use if the product appears contaminated, has visible particles, or has a pungent odor. Verify that the product has not expired.
Using the agarose gel medium pH Color Chart provided, verify that the pH of the product is within the acceptable range (6.8 to 7.7).
Once application site is prepared, open product and apply as directed; product must be applied within 15 minutes of opening.
Application of Gintuit
The application of Gintuit should be done under aseptic technique by a trained health care professional. For detailed application instructions, refer to the manufacturer product labeling. The following is a brief synopsis of application directions:
Lift off the tray lid and place it on a surgical sterile field with the outer lid surface facing down. Gintuit is packaged with the upper layer (containing keratinocytes that appear dull, with a matte finish) facing up and the lower layer (containing fibroblasts in extracellular matrix that appears glossy) facing down, resting on the polycarbonate base attached to the plastic insert.
The manufacturer describes in detail the technique used to remove Gintuit from the package, including directions for trimming the product to proper size. Proper product orientation must be maintained.
Place the lower layer (i.e., fibroblast/extracellular matrix side) of Gintuit in direct contact with the surgical site, so that there are no pockets or wrinkled edges. If needed, use saline to smooth wrinkles.
Immobilize and attach Gintuit to the surgical mucosal defect with sutures placed at the papillae and apically at each corner. If necessary, add a central suture at the apical margin or lateral margins. Use a crisscross (i.e., suspensory or sling) suture over Gintuit to enhance stability and help maintain direct contact with the wound bed. Gintuit must not move during mucosal or muscle traction. Tension may cause Gintuit to tear.
To help protect Gintuit, place an additional layer of Gintuit, extending laterally beyond the defect margins over the initial layer, and suture at the 4 corners.
Place a non-occlusive protective dressing over the treated site.
Dispose of any unused product and any materials that have come in contact with Gintuit as surgical biohazardous waste in accordance with local requirements.
