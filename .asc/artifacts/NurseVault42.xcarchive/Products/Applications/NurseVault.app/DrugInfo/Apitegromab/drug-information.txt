Drug Class: Apitegromab is a recombinant monoclonal antibody targeting proforms of myostatin indicated for the treatment of spinal muscular atrophy in individuals currently receiving a survival motor neuron 2-targeted treatment.

Routes: intravenous; Common adult doses: 10 mg/kg/dose IV once every 4 weeks.

Common Side Effects (excluding routine GI upset): arthralgia, hematoma, myalgia, atopic dermatitis, flushing, maculopapular rash, rash, urticaria, cough, infection, influenza, nasal congestion, pharyngitis, respiratory failure, rhinitis, rhinorrhea, sinusitis

In an Overdose: No overdose/antidote information is listed in this file.

Key Nursing Considerations: Contraindications/precautions: This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components. Use apitegromab with caution in individuals with a history of low bone density, including osteopenia, osteoporosis, or a history of multiple pathologic bone fracture.

    Headline
    osteopenia, osteoporosis, pathologic bone fracture
    General Information
    Use apitegromab with caution in individuals with a history of low bone density, including osteopenia, osteoporosis, or a history of multiple pathologic bone fracture. Apitegromab may increase the risk of fractures, including serious fractures. Most individuals with fractures had a history of low bone density, previous fractures, or contractures. Some fracture events did not have a clear precipitating event (such as a fall). In individuals who experience a bone fracture, consider the risks and benefits of continuing treatment.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    There are no adequate data on the use of apitegromab during pregnancy to evaluate for a drug-associated risk of major birth defects, miscarriage, or adverse outcomes. Monoclonal antibodies are known to cross the placental barrier with a higher likelihood of fetal exposure with administration during the third trimester. During animal studies, apitegromab administered by IV infusion once weekly in rats during pregnancy and lactation caused delayed sexual maturation and altered neurobehavioral function (increased auditory startle response) in offspring of both sexes at all doses and impaired reproductive function in male offspring at the highest dose. Apitegromab administered by IV injection once weekly throughout organogenesis, from gestation day 6 through gestation day 17, produced no adverse effects on embryofetal development.
    Headline
    people who can cause pregnancy in others, people who may become pregnant, reproductive risk
    General Information
    Counsel people who may become pregnant and people who can cause pregnancy in others about the reproductive risk associated with apitegromab; based on animal data, apitegromab may cause fetal harm and may affect reproductive function. Animal data suggest adverse effects of apitegromab on male and/or female reproductive function after exposure during postnatal development or in adulthood. In rats dosed IV once weekly prior to and during mating and continuing in females to gestation day 6, decreased sperm concentrations, estrous cycle disruption, decreased male and/or female mating and fertility indices, and increased postimplantation loss were observed at all doses. A no-effect dose for adverse effects on fertility and early embryonic development was not identified, and the lowest dose tested (30 mg/kg) was associated with exposures (AUC) lower than that in humans at the maximum recommended human dose (MRHD).
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Use apitegromab with caution while breast-feeding. There are no data on the presence of apitegromab in human milk, its effects on the breastfed child, or its effects on milk production. Maternal IgG is known to be present in human milk, and the potential for absorption of apitegromab to lead to inhibition of myostatin activation in the breastfed child is unknown. Consider the benefits of breast-feeding, the patient's clinical need for treatment, and any potential adverse effects on the breastfed child from the medication or from the patient's underlying medical condition.

    Across clinical trials, bone fractures occurred in 9% of individuals (n = 53; age 2 to 12 years) receiving 10 mg/kg and 6% to 9% of individuals receiving 20 mg/kg, twice the recommended dosage, compared with 2% of placebo-treated individuals. Fractures included foot fracture, clavicle fracture, femur fracture, tibia fracture, and torus fracture. In individuals who experience fractures, consider the risks and benefits of continuing treatment.

Description
  Text
  Apitegromab is a recombinant monoclonal antibody targeting proforms of myostatin indicated for the treatment of spinal muscular atrophy in individuals currently receiving a survival motor neuron 2-targeted treatment. Apitegromab acts on skeletal muscle rather than on motor neuron SMN protein production. In a clinical trial in nonambulatory individuals (age: 2 to 21 years) receiving nusinersen or risdiplam, individuals who received apitegromab had a least squares mean difference from placebo in the change from baseline in Hammersmith Functional Motor Scale Expanded (HFMSE) total score of 2.2 points (95% CI, 0.49 to 3.95) at 1 year (nominal p-value 0.0121). Apitegromab may increase the risk of fractures, including serious fractures. Based on animal data, apitegromab may cause fetal harm and may affect reproductive function. Apitegromab was FDA approved in September 2026.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Apitegromab is a monoclonal IgG4 antibody that binds to the inactive precursor forms of myostatin, including promyostatin and latent myostatin. By inhibiting myostatin activation, apitegromab reduces myostatin signaling that normally limits muscle growth, and may help preserve or improve muscle function in spinal muscular atrophy (SMA). Because apitegromab targets myostatin precursors rather than mature myostatin, it is designed to avoid interfering with related TGF-beta family growth factors. Apitegromab acts on myostatin in skeletal muscle, addressing a different component of SMA pathophysiology from the component targeted by SMN2-targeted treatments.
 
Pharmacodynamics
In a clinical trial, increases in serum total latent myostatin concentrations were comparable among individuals aged 2 to 12 years who received apitegromab 10 or 20 mg/kg and individuals aged 13 years and older who received 20 mg/kg.

  Apitegromab is administered intravenously. In individuals with spinal muscular atrophy (SMA) aged 2 to 21 years, the estimated mean steady-state Vd is 2.16 L. Apitegromab is expected to be degraded into small peptides and amino acids via catabolism. It is not expected to undergo renal elimination or metabolism by hepatic enzymes.     Administration Details
      Name
      Intravenous Administration
      Description
      Dilution
Calculate the required dose to be administered based on the individual's weight.
Remove vials from the refrigerator and allow the vials to warm to room temperature. Do not use external heat sources to warm the vials. Do not shake.
Withdraw the required volume of apitegromab with a sterile syringe and transfer into a di(2-ethylhexyl)phthalate (DEHP)-free infusion bag.
Dilute with 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection to make a final concentration of 5 mg/mL.
Gently invert to mix; do not shake.
Do not mix or dilute with other medications.
Discard unused drug remaining in the vial(s).
Storage: Administer immediately after preparation. If not used immediately, store the infusion bag protected from light at room temperature between 20 and 25 degrees C (68 and 77 degrees F) or refrigerate between 2 and 8 degrees C (36 and 46 degrees F) for up to 4 hours, including preparation and infusion time. Do not freeze.
 
Intermittent Intravenous Infusion
If refrigerated, allow the diluted solution to warm to room temperature without external heat sources.
Use a dedicated line with an infusion set containing a 0.2 or 0.22 micron low protein binding polyethersulfone (PES) in-line filter and use DEHP-free infusion sets and lines.
Infuse IV over approximately 60 to 120 minutes at a rate no greater than 150 mL/hour.
After completion of the infusion, flush the line with 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection to ensure all the apitegromab dose has been administered.
Missed dose: Administer as soon as possible. Restart the dosing schedule 4 weeks after the missed dose is administered. Alternatively, if a planned dose is missed, dosing may be resumed on the next scheduled dose to maintain the original dosing schedule.
