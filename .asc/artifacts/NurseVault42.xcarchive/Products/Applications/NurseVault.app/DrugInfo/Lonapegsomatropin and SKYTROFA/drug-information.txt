Contraindications And Precautions

  Contraindications And Precautions
    Headline
    Drug Interactions
    General Information
    The coadministration of certain medications may lead to harm and require avoidance or therapy modification; review all drug interactions prior to concomitant use of other medications.
    Headline
    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components.
    Headline
    critical illness, respiratory failure, trauma
    General Information
    Lonapegsomatropin is contraindicated in individuals with acute critical illness due to complications after open heart or abdominal surgery, multiple accidental trauma, and in those with acute respiratory failure due to the risk of increased mortality. The safety of continuing lonapegsomatropin treatment in individuals who subsequently develop these illnesses has not been established.
    Headline
    epiphyseal closure
    General Information
    Lonapegsomatropin is contraindicated in individuals with epiphyseal closure. Slipped capital femoral epiphysis may occur more frequently during periods of rapid growth and can lead to osteonecrosis. Cases of slipped capital femoral epiphysis, with or without osteonecrosis, have been reported in pediatric patients with short stature receiving lonapegsomatropin. Evaluate pediatric patients presenting with a new limp or complaints of hip or knee pain.
    Headline
    malignancy
    General Information
    Lonapegsomatropin is contraindicated in individuals with active malignancy due to the risk of progression. Complete treatment of preexisting neoplastic disease before instituting growth hormone therapy. Discontinue lonapegsomatropin if there is evidence of recurrent disease. There is an increased risk of secondary malignancy, most commonly intracranial tumors such as meningiomas, in childhood cancer survivors who were treated with radiation therapy to their brain/head and subsequently developed growth hormone deficiency that was treated with somatropin. Monitor these individuals for progression or recurrence of the tumor. Consider the risks and benefits of lonapegsomatropin therapy in pediatric patients with certain rare genetic causes of short stature, as these individuals are at increased risk of new primary malignancy. If therapy is initiated, monitor individuals for new malignancy. Monitor individuals for increased growth or potential malignant changes of preexisting nevi. Advise individuals and caregivers to report significant changes in behavior, onset of headaches, vision disturbances, and/or changes in skin pigmentation or the appearance of preexisting nevi.
    Headline
    diabetes mellitus, proliferative diabetic retinopathy, severe non-proliferative diabetic retinopathy
    General Information
    Lonapegsomatropin is contraindicated in individuals with active proliferative diabetic retinopathy or severe non-proliferative diabetic retinopathy because treatment with somatropin may worsen this condition. Monitor glucose concentrations in all individuals receiving lonapegsomatropin, especially in those with risk factors for type 2 diabetes mellitus (e.g., obesity, family history). Monitor individuals with type 1 or type 2 diabetes or impaired glucose tolerance during treatment initiation and adjust doses of antihyperglycemic drugs as needed. Treatment with somatropin may decrease insulin sensitivity, especially at higher doses. Undiagnosed impaired glucose tolerance and overt type 2 diabetes may be unmasked.
    Headline
    obesity, obstructive sleep apnea, respiratory insufficiency
    General Information
    Lonapegsomatropin is contraindicated in individuals with Prader-Willi syndrome who are severely obese or have severe respiratory impairment. Unless individuals with Prader-Willi syndrome also have a diagnosis of growth hormone deficiency, lonapegsomatropin is not indicated for long-term treatment of pediatric patients who have growth failure due to genetically confirmed Prader-Willi syndrome. There have been reports of fatalities with the use of growth hormone in pediatric patients with Prader-Willi syndrome who had one or more of the following risk factors: severe obesity, history of respiratory insufficiency or sleep apnea, or unidentified respiratory infection. Male individuals with one or more of these factors may be at increased risk. Individuals with Prader-Willi syndrome should be evaluated for upper airway obstruction before initiation of treatment with growth hormone. If during treatment with growth hormone, individuals show signs of upper airway obstruction (including onset of or increased snoring), treatment should be interrupted. All individuals with Prader-Willi syndrome should be evaluated for obstructive sleep apnea and monitored if sleep apnea is suspected.
    Headline
    adrenal insufficiency, hypothyroidism, pituitary insufficiency
    General Information
    Individuals who have or at risk for pituitary insufficiency, and are receiving lonapegsomatropin, may be at risk for reduced serum cortisol concentrations and/or unmasking of central (secondary) adrenal insufficiency. Individuals treated with glucocorticoid replacement for previously diagnosed adrenal insufficiency may require an increase in their maintenance or stress doses following initiation of lonapegsomatropin treatment. In addition, individuals with untreated hypothyroidism will have an inadequate response to lonapegsomatropin therapy. Periodic thyroid function tests should be performed and treatment with thyroid hormone initiated when indicated.
    Headline
    scoliosis
    General Information
    Individuals with a history of scoliosis should receive lonapegsomatropin with caution. Because growth hormone increases growth rate, individuals with scoliosis can experience progression of scoliosis. Individuals should be monitored for progression of scoliosis.
    Headline
    geriatric
    General Information
    Lonapegsomatropin may increase the risk of adverse reactions in geriatric individuals. According to the Beers Criteria, growth hormone is considered a potentially inappropriate medication (PIM) in geriatric individuals due to its small effect on body composition relative to a significant adverse effect profile (e.g., edema, arthralgia, carpal tunnel syndrome, gynecomastia, elevated fasting glucose). Use in individuals rigorously diagnosed by evidence-based criteria with growth hormone deficiency due to an established etiology is acceptable.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    There are no available data on the use of lonapegsomatropin during human pregnancy to evaluate a drug-associated risk of major birth defects, miscarriage, or other adverse outcomes. Available published data over several decades for somatropin, the active component of lonapegsomatropin, have not identified risk. In animal studies, no embryo-fetal or neonatal harm occurred in pregnant rats administered subcutaneous doses up to 13-fold the human clinical pediatric dose of 0.24 mg/kg/week and approximately 30-fold the maximum clinical therapeutic dose of 6.3 mg hGH/week for adults.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Use lonapegsomatropin with caution during breast-feeding. There are no data on the presence of lonapegsomatropin in human milk, its effect on the breast-fed child, or its effect on milk production. High molecular weight therapeutic proteins, such as lonapegsomatropin, are expected to have low excretion into human milk and limited systemic exposure to a breast-fed infant. Published data indicate exogenous somatropin does not increase normal human milk concentrations of growth hormone. No adverse effects on the breast-fed child have been reported with somatropin. Consider the benefits of breast-feeding, the risk of potential infant drug exposure, and the risk of an untreated or inadequately treated condition.

Description
  Text
  Lonapegsomatropin is a long-acting prodrug of somatropin (a human growth hormone). It is indicated for replacement of endogenous growth hormone in individuals with growth hormone deficiency (GHD) and for the treatment of growth failure due to inadequate secretion of endogenous growth hormone. In a multi-center, randomized, parallel-group, placebo-controlled (double-blind), phase 3 study, the change from baseline in trunk fat mass and total body lean mass at 38 weeks was -0.5 kg and +1.6 kg, respectively, in longapegsomatropin-treated adult subjects with GHD. After 38 weeks, treatment with lonapegsomatropin in adult subjects resulted in normalization of insulin-like growth factor standard deviation score (IGF-1 SDS) to 1.4 compared to -2.6 in placebo-treated subjects. In a clinical trial of 161 treatment-naive, pre-pubescent subjects (mean age 8.5 years) with GHD, lonapegsomatropin efficacy was determined by the increase in growth rate after 52 weeks. At the end of the 52-week treatment period, growth rate increased 11.2 cm per year in subjects receiving once-weekly lonapegsomatropin compared to 10.3 cm for daily somatropin. Prior to starting lonapegsomatropin therapy, perform a fundoscopic eye examination to exclude papilledema and reassess periodically. Papilledema may be a symptom of intracranial hypertension and growth hormones may induce or worsen intracranial hypertension. Lonapegsomatropin was FDA-approved in August 2021.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Lonapegsomatropin is a pegylated form of human growth hormone somatropin. Somatropin binds to growth hormone (GH) receptors in the cell membrane of target cells, which results in intracellular signal transduction and a number of other pharmacodynamic effects. It produces a variety of physiologic effects that include direct tissue and metabolic effects and indirect effects mediated by insulin-like growth factor-1 (IGF-1). IGF-1 is responsible for stimulation of chondrocyte differentiation and proliferation, stimulation of hepatic glucose output, protein synthesis, and lipolysis. Somatropin also stimulates skeletal growth in pediatric patients with growth hormone deficiency (GHD) as a result of effects on the growth plates of long bones.

Pharmacodynamics
Somatropin released from lonapegsomatropin produces a linear insulin-like growth factor 1 (IGF-1) dose response. In adult subjects, a dose change of 1 mg/week resulted in a mean change in weekly average IGF-1 standard deviation score (SDS) of 0.7 (range, 0.3 to 1.2). At steady state, maximum IGF-1 concentrations were observed approximately 2 days post-dose, with the average weekly IGF-1 occurring approximately 4.5 days post-dose. IGF-1 concentrations were in the normal range for growth hormone deficiency (GHD) subjects for most of the week, similar to daily somatropin. In pediatric patients, an average dose change of 0.02 mg/kg/week resulted in a mean change in weekly average IGF-1 SDS of 0.17.
 
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes and drug transporters: unknown
Limited published data indicate that growth hormone treatment increases CYP450-mediated antipyrine clearance. Thus, lonapegsomatropin may alter the clearance of compounds known to be metabolized by CYP450 enzymes. No specific data are available.
