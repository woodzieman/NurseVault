    Headline
    acute hepatic failure, hypervolemia, hypoxia, respiratory failure, respiratory insufficiency
    General Information
    Terlipressin is contraindicated in individuals with hypoxia or worsening respiratory symptoms (e.g., respiratory insufficiency, respiratory failure) due to risk for serious or fatal respiratory failure. Assess oxygen saturation (SpO2) prior to initiating terlipressin; do not initiate terlipressin in individuals experiencing hypoxia (SpO2 less than 90%) until improvement of oxygen saturation occurs. Monitor for changes in respiratory status, including hypoxia, during terlipressin therapy using continuous pulse oximetry and regular clinical assessments. Individuals with hypervolemia or acute hepatic failure, specifically acute-on-chronic hepatic failure Grade 3, are at increased risk for respiratory failure. Avoid use of terlipressin in acute-on-chronic hepatic failure Grade 3 due to significant risk of respiratory failure. Manage intravascular volume overload by reducing or discontinuing albumin and/or other fluids and judicious use of diuretics.
    Headline
    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components.
    Headline
    acute myocardial ischemia, cardiovascular disease, mesenteric ischemia, peripheral vasoconstriction or ischemia
    General Information
    Terlipressin is contraindicated in individuals with ongoing coronary ischemia (acute myocardial ischemia), mesenteric ischemia, or peripheral vasoconstriction or ischemia. Avoid terlipressin in individuals with a history of severe cardiovascular disease, cerebrovascular disease, or ischemic disease. Terlipressin may cause cardiac, cerebrovascular, peripheral, or mesenteric ischemia.
    Headline
    planned procedure
    General Information
    Terlipressin-associated adverse reactions of respiratory failure or ischemia may cause an individual to be ineligible for a planned procedure of a liver transplant. The benefits of terlipressin may not outweigh the risks for individuals with high priority for liver transplant (e.g., model for end-stage liver disease (MELD) score of 35 or more).
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Terlipressin may cause fetal harm when used during pregnancy based on the mechanism of action and data from published literature. In small studies, administration of a single terlipressin dose during the first trimester or pregnancy induced uterine contractions and endometrial ischemia. The limited published data are insufficient to determine a drug-associated risk for major birth defects or miscarriage. In reproductive toxicity animal studies, administration of terlipressin to pregnant guinea pigs at a doses lower than the recommended human dose caused a significant decrease in blood flow to the uterus and placenta. Terlipressin is embryotoxic and teratogenic (i.e., increased resorptions, increased implantation loss, fetal anomalies, fetal deformities) in rabbits.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Use terlipressin with caution during breast-feeding. There are no data on the presence of terlipressin in human or animal milk, its effects on the breast-fed child, or its effects on milk production. Consider the benefits of breast-feeding, the patient's clinical need for treatment, and any potential adverse effects on the breast-fed child from the medication or from the patient's underlying medical condition.

Description
  Text
  Terlipressin is an intravenous vasopressin receptor agonist indicated to improve kidney function in adults with hepatorenal syndrome (HRS) with rapid reduction in kidney function. Individuals with a SCr more than 5 mg/dL are not likely to benefit from the use of terlipressin. Terlipressin is associated with serious or fatal respiratory failure in certain at-risk populations. Terlipressin was FDA-approved in September 2022.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Terlipressin is a vasopressin agonist with twice the selectivity for vasopressin V1 receptors compared to V2 receptors. It is a prodrug for lysine-vasopressin in addition to having its own pharmacologic activity. Terlipressin is thought to increase renal blood flow in individuals with hepatorenal syndrome by decreasing portal hypertension and blood circulation in portal vessels and increasing arterial volume and mean arterial pressure (MAP).

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Intravenous Route
    Description
    After administration of a single 0.85 mg dose of terlipressin in persons with hepatorenal syndrome, an increase in diastolic, systolic, and mean arterial pressure (MAP) and a decrease in heart rate occurred within 5 minutes after the dose and were maintained for at least 6 hours post-dose. The maximum change in blood pressure and heart rate occurred at 1.2 to 2 hours post-dose. The estimated maximum effect for MAP was an increase of 16.2 mmHg, and the estimated maximum effect for heart rate was a decrease of 10.6 beats/minute. The median Cmax and AUC24h of terlipressin acetate after terlipressin 1 mg IV were 70.5 nanograms/mL and 123 nanograms x hour/mL, respectively. For lysine-vasopressin, the median Cmax and AUC24h were 1.2 nanograms/mL and 11.2 nanograms x hour/mL. Both terlipressin and lysine-vasopressin display linear pharmacokinetics in healthy subjects.

  Text
  Terlipressin is administered intravenously. The Vd of terlipressin and lysine-vasopressin are 6.3 L and 1,370 L, respectively. Terlipressin metabolism occurs by cleavage of N-terminal glycyl residues of terlipressin by tissue peptidases causing a release of the active metabolite, lysine-vasopressin. Lysine-vasopressin is metabolized by body tissue by various peptidase-mediated routes. Terlipressin is not metabolized in blood or plasma. Based on the presence of peptidases in tissue, the metabolism of terlipressin is not likely to be affected by disease state or other medications. Clearance of terlipressin and lysine-vasopressin are 27.4 L/hour and 318 L/hour, respectively. Less than 1% of terlipressin and less than 0.1% of lysine-vasopressin is excreted in the urine. The terminal half-life of terlipressin is 0.9 hours and 3 hours for lysine-vasopressin.
