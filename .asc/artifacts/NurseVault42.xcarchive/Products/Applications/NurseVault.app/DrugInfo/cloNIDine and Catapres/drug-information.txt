Drug Class: Clonidine is a centrally acting alpha-2 agonist used for the treatment of hypertension, attention-deficit hyperactivity disorder (ADHD), and as an agent for refractory cancer pain.

Routes: topical, oral, injection (IV/IM/SC); Common adult doses: 2.4 mg/day PO immediate-release tablets and oral solution; two Catapres-TTS-3 transdermal patches/week (delivers 0.6 mg/day for 7 days) applied topically to skin; 40 mcg/hour continuous epidural infusion; 0.52 mg/day PO extended-release tablets for HTN...

Common Side Effects (excluding routine GI upset): arrhythmia exacerbation, av block, bradycardia, chest pain (unspecified), heart failure, hypotension, orthostatic hypotension, palpitations, peripheral vasoconstriction, qt prolongation, raynaud phenomenon, sinus tachycardia, syncope, alopecia, angioedema, contact dermatitis, edema, erythema, maculopapular rash, pallor, pruritus, rash, skin hyperpigmentation, skin ulcer, urticaria, vesicular rash, impotence (erectile dysfunction), libido decrease, nocturia, urinary incontinence

In an Overdose: Of note, it is not known whether the medication is evenly dispersed through the patch and there is a potential risk of accidental misplacement, which could cause an overdose of medication.

Key Nursing Considerations: Contraindications/precautions: Abrupt discontinuation of clonidine, regardless of route of administration, can precipitate a withdrawal syndrome consisting of rebound increases in both serum and urine catecholamines (see Adverse Reactions). If it is necessary to discontinue clonidine, doses should be slowly tapered over 2—4 days...

Indication Specific Dosing
  General Information
  Clonidine discontinuation following use for any indication:
Avoid sudden cessation of clonidine treatment in order to avoid withdrawal symptoms, which may be serious and include a rapid rise in blood pressure or other serious events. The likelihood of serious withdrawal reactions appears to be greater after administration of higher doses or continuation of concomitant beta-blocker treatment; special caution is therefore advised in these situations. If therapy is to be discontinued in individuals receiving a beta-blocker and clonidine concurrently, the beta-blocker should be withdrawn several days before the gradual discontinuation of clonidine. Withdrawal of clonidine prior to surgery is not recommended.
Based on the clonidine formulation prescribed, the following discontinuation regimens are recommended:
Clonidine immediate-release tablets (e.g., Catapres): Slowly taper dose over 2 to 4 days.
Clonidine immediate-release solution (e.g., Javadin): Slowly taper dose over 2 to 4 days.
Clonidine extended-release tablets (e.g., Nexiclon XR): Slowly taper dose over 2 to 4 days.
Clonidine extended-release tablets (e.g., Kapvay): Slowly taper dose every 3 to 7 days.
Clonidine extended-release oral suspension (e.g., Onyda XR): Slowly taper dose by decrements of no more than 0.1 mg every 3 to 7 days.
Clonidine transdermal system (e.g., Catapres TTS): Reduce the dose gradually over 2 to 4 days.
 
Equivalent doses of immediate-release tablets (e.g., Catapres) to extended-release tablets (e.g., Nexiclon XR) tablets:
An initial dose of 0.1 mg PO twice daily immediate-release tablets is equivalent to 0.17 mg PO once daily extended-release tablets.
Maintenance dose titration increments of 0.05 mg PO twice daily immediate-release tablets is equivalent to 0.09 mg PO once daily extended-release tablets.
Common doses used for hypertension are:
0.1 mg PO twice daily of immediate-release tablets is equivalent to 0.17 mg PO once daily extended-release tablets.
0.2 mg PO twice daily of immediate-release tablets is equivalent to 0.34 mg PO once daily extended-release tablets.
0.3 mg PO twice daily of immediate-release tablets is equivalent to 0.52 mg PO once daily extended-release tablets.

Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  General Information
  Abrupt discontinuation of clonidine, regardless of route of administration, can precipitate a withdrawal syndrome consisting of rebound increases in both serum and urine catecholamines (see Adverse Reactions). If it is necessary to discontinue clonidine, doses should be slowly tapered over 2—4 days to avoid withdrawal symptoms. Patients who have received clonidine therapy for greater than 4 weeks may require slower dosage tapers (i.e., dosage reduction every 3 days).
 
Clonidine is classified as a pregnancy category C drug. Adequate and well-controlled studies have not been performed in humans. Risk-benefit should be considered before use in pregnant patients. There is one case report suggesting that fetuses who are exposed to long-term clonidine (>300 mcg/day) during pregnancy may be more likely to develop sleep disorders later in life.
 
The manufacturer does not recommend the use of epidural clonidine as an analgesic during labor and delivery, post-partum, or peri-operative analgesia due to the risks of hemodynamic instability, especially hypotension and bradycardia. However, potential benefits of clonidine may outweigh the possible risks in a rare obstetrical, post-partum, or perioperative patient. Several trials examining the efficacy, safety, or dosing of epidural clonidine in obstetrics have been reported. In a study comparing epidural clonidine combined with bupivacaine and bupivacaine alone for analgesia during labor, there was no difference in maternal blood pressure decreases and apgar scores at 1 and 5 minutes between the two groups. The duration of labor was prolonged in patients receiving clonidine. Females and lower weight patients may be more susceptible to the hypotensive effects of epidural clonidine.
 
According to the manufacturer, because clonidine is excreted in breast milk, it should be administered cautiously to breast-feeding women. The concentration of clonidine in breast milk is approximately twice that found in the maternal plasma. Consider the benefits of breast-feeding, the risk of potential infant drug exposure, and the risk of an untreated or inadequately treated condition. If a breast-feeding infant experiences an adverse effect related to a maternally ingested drug, healthcare providers are encouraged to report the adverse effect to the FDA.
 
Safe and effective use of clonidine in children has not been adequately established; there are no clear guidelines for clonidine use in pediatric patients. In the use of clonidine for attention deficit hyperactivity disorder (ADHD), sedation and hypotension have been reported. Reports of sudden death and hypotension in children receiving oral clonidine in combination with other therapies for ADHD have been published. Such cases have also been reported with the use of clonidine alone. This may indicate that children and infants are more sensitive to the effects of clonidine and should be treated with extreme caution. The American Heart Association (AHA) has recommended that children and adolescents receiving clonidine be monitored for changes in blood pressure at treatment initiation, periodically during treatment, and when tapering the drug, even when clonidine is used for psychotropic indications. In addition, the AHA recommends obtaining a detailed patient and family history and physical examination prior to the therapy initiation; obtaining a baseline electrocardiogram (ECG) is also a reasonable addition to the initial evaluation. The use of epidural clonidine should be restricted to those children with severe intractable pain from malignancy that is unresponsive to epidural or spinal opiates or other more conventional analgesic techniques.
 
Use clonidine cautiously in patients with a history of major depression because the drug can induce depressive episodes.
 
Warn patients of the potential sedative and hypotensive effects of clonidine and advised against driving or operating machinery, or performing other hazardous tasks, until they are aware of how the medication affects them.
 
Use clonidine cautiously in patients with cerebrovascular disease, myocardial infarction, or severe heart failure. The hypotensive effects of clonidine may decrease perfusion and worsen ischemia in these conditions. The vagal effect of clonidine may exacerbate atrioventricular node function impairment.
 
Use clonidine cautiously in patients with sinus node function impairment such as sick sinus syndrome, although its effects on cardiac conduction appear to be slight.
 
Use clonidine cautiously in patients with Raynaud's disease or thromboangiitis obliterans (Buerger's disease). Clonidine may exacerbate these conditions.
 
The drug should be used cautiously in patients with diabetes mellitus because transient elevations in blood glucose have been noted. Clonidine has, however, been used safely in many diabetic patients.
 
Clonidine has been used safely in patients with renal disease. Clonidine is 45% renally excreted and drug concentrations may accumulate in renal failure. Therefore, the manufacturer recommends careful monitoring and dosage adjustment (no specific guidelines) based on the degree of renal impairment in patients with renal disease. In clinical practice, dosage adjustments are usually not clinically needed in patients with renal failure or renal disease, and the dosage of clonidine is titrated to achieve clinical goals. Elderly patients should be treated with caution because they are more likely to have decreased renal function and are more susceptible to the hypotensive and sedative effects of clonidine; dosage reduction may be considered.
 
Clonidine epidural injection is contraindicated in patients receiving anticoagulant therapy or in patients with a coagulopathy. Epidural clonidine is also contraindicated in patients with an infection at the site of injection.
 
Administration of epidural clonidine above the C4 dermatome is contraindicated because there are no adequate safety data to support such use.
 
Epidural clonidine should be used cautiously in patients with severe cardiac disease or who are hemodynamically unstable due to the potential for severe hypotension. When clonidine is administered into the upper thoracic spinal segments more profound decreases in blood pressure may be seen.
 
The manufacturer does not recommend intrathecal administration of clonidine. Due to clonidine's lipophilicity, increased effects may be noticed following intrathecal administration, although, inadvertent intrathecal administration has not been associated with a significant increase in side effects. The use of intrathecal clonidine in intra- and postoperative analgesia and in obstetrics (labor and Cesarean section) have been reported.
 
NOTE: The following relative contraindications apply to the transdermal system preparations only (in addition to those listed above):
 
Clonidine transdermal systems should be removed prior to defibrillation (cardioversion) because the drug can alter electrical conductivity, increasing the likelihood that electrical arcing will occur. In addition, skin burns have been reported at the patch site in several patients wearing an aluminized transdermal system during a magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) scan. Because the Catapres-TTS® patch contains aluminum, it is recommended to remove the system before undergoing an MRI.
 
Absorption of the drug can be decreased in patients with polyarteritis nodosa, scleroderma, or systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE), and the patches should not be placed on affected areas.
 
Absorption of clonidine can be increased in areas of skin irritation or skin abrasion, so placement of the patches in these areas should be avoided.

    General Information
    Individuals, especially pediatric patients, receiving oral clonidine who experience gastrointestinal illness associated with vomiting may be at risk for withdrawal due to abrupt inability to take medication. Abrupt discontinuation of clonidine, regardless of the route of administration, can precipitate rebound hypertension and increases in plasma catecholamine concentrations, especially with higher doses or concomitant beta-blocker therapy. Symptoms may include tachycardia, rapid elevation in blood pressure, and in some cases, hypertensive encephalopathy, stroke and death. Careful monitoring of infusion pump function and catheter tubing for obstruction or dislodgement is recommended in individuals receiving clonidine injection in order to prevent inadvertent premature discontinuation. If it is necessary to discontinue clonidine, doses should be slowly tapered based on individual formulation to avoid withdrawal symptoms. When discontinuing both beta-blocker and clonidine therapies, withdraw beta-blocker therapy several days before initiating clonidine taper. Monitoring of blood pressure and heart rate during weaning is recommended, even when clonidine is used for psychotropic indications.
    Headline
    cardiovascular disease, dehydration, hypotension, syncope
    General Information
    Use caution and titrate clonidine slowly in individuals with a history of hypotension, in those with underlying conditions that may be worsened by hypotension and bradycardia (e.g., AV block, cardiovascular disease, cerebrovascular disease, coronary artery disease, acute myocardial infarction, chronic renal or hepatic failure), and in those with cardiac conduction abnormalities. Treatment with clonidine can result in dose-dependent decreases in blood pressure and heart rate, which are usually less pronounced as the time of therapy progresses. In addition, the sympatholytic action of clonidine may worsen sinus node dysfunction and AV block, especially in those taking sympatholytic drugs. Use epidural clonidine with caution in individuals with severe cardiac disease or who are hemodynamically unstable due to the potential for severe hypotension; profound reductions in blood pressure may occur with administration into the upper thoracic spinal segments. Measure heart rate and blood pressure at baseline, after dose adjustments, periodically throughout therapy, and upon discontinuation in all individuals; monitor vital signs frequently in those with cardiac conduction abnormalities or receiving concomitant therapy with another sympatholytic agent. In individuals with a history of or those who have a condition that predisposes them to syncope (e.g., hypotension, orthostatic hypotension, bradycardia, dehydration), advise individuals to avoid becoming dehydrated or overheated. Guidelines recommend that all children and adolescents receiving clonidine be monitored for changes in blood pressure at treatment initiation, periodically during treatment, and when tapering the drug, even when clonidine is used for psychotropic indications. In addition, it is recommended to obtain a detailed individual and family history and physical examination prior to the therapy initiation; obtaining a baseline electrocardiogram (ECG) is also a reasonable addition to the initial evaluation.
    Headline
    planned procedure, surgery
    General Information
    Due to the risk of hemodynamic instability, specifically hypotension and bradycardia, epidural administration of clonidine is not recommended for pain management during surgery. Epidural clonidine for intraoperative or postoperative analgesia may cause a marked hypotensive response, even if pre-treatment with intravenous fluids is given. Careful consideration should be given to the scheduling of clonidine doses near the time of surgery or planned procedure; specific recommendations are dependent on the dosage form. Immediate-release clonidine (e.g., tablets, solution) may be administered up to 4 hours prior to surgery and resumed as soon as possible post-operatively. Extended-release clonidine indicated for treatment of hypertension may be administered up to 28 hours prior to surgery and then resumed the following day. Transdermal clonidine therapy should not be interrupted during the surgical period. Note that if transdermal clonidine therapy is initiated during the perioperative period, it takes 2 to 3 days to reach therapeutic plasma clonidine concentrations after initial application of the transdermal patch. Although the manufacturers of clonidine injection and extended-release clonidine for ADHD do not provide specific recommendations regarding dosing near surgery, these formulations should not be abruptly discontinued due to the risk of withdrawal symptoms. Carefully monitor blood pressure during surgery and utilize additional measures to control blood pressure if necessary.

    Headline
    labor, obstetric delivery, postpartum, pregnancy
    General Information
    Prolonged experience with the use of clonidine during pregnancy has not identified a drug-associated risk of major birth defects, miscarriage, or adverse outcomes. Clonidine readily crosses the placenta. Its concentrations are equal in maternal and umbilical cord plasma; amniotic fluid concentrations can be up to 4 times that found in serum. In animal studies, no teratogenic or embryotoxic effects were seen in rabbits given doses up to 3 times the maximum recommended human dose (MRHD) on a mg/m2 basis during organogenesis. In pregnant rats, doses as low as one-third the MRHD were associated with increased resorption when dams were given clonidine from 2 months prior to mating and throughout gestation. Increased resorptions were not associated with treatment at the same or higher dose levels (up to 3 times the MRHD) when treatment was restricted to gestation days 6 through 15. Increased resorptions were observed in mice and rats given 4 and 8 times or more than the MRHD, respectively, during gestation days 1 through 14. There is a pregnancy registry that monitors pregnancy outcomes in patients exposed to ADHD medications, including clonidine, during pregnancy. Health care providers are encouraged to register patients by calling the National Pregnancy Registry for ADHD Medications at 1-866-961-2388 or by visiting www.womensmentalhealth.org/adhd-medications/. Epidural clonidine is not recommended as an analgesic during labor and obstetric delivery, or for postpartum or peri-operative analgesia due to the risks of hemodynamic instability. Perfusion of the placenta is critically dependent on blood pressure. Studies using epidural clonidine during labor have not shown an increased incidence of adverse effects on the newborn at the time of delivery; these infants were not monitored for hemodynamic effects in the days after delivery. In a study comparing a combination of epidural clonidine with bupivacaine and bupivacaine alone for analgesia during labor, there was no difference in maternal blood pressure reductions and infant Apgar scores at 1 and 5 minutes between the two groups. The duration of labor was prolonged in patients receiving clonidine.

  Headline
  Adverse Reactions
  General Information
  The adverse effects most commonly reported during oral clonidine therapy are lethargy, drowsiness (sedation), constipation, and xerostomia. Headache, dizziness, fatigue, and weakness also have occurred with clonidine therapy. These effects generally will subside as therapy progresses or as dosages are reduced. Many of these effects will diminish with continued therapy. Transdermal therapy typically results in less severe adverse systemic effects. The most common non-cardiovascular adverse reactions to epidural clonidine include anxiety, asthenia, chest pain, confusion, diaphoresis, dizziness, drowsiness, dyspnea, fever, nausea/vomiting and xerostomia.
 
Adverse cardiovascular effects of clonidine therapy include hypotension, orthostatic hypotension, palpitations, sinus tachycardia, and sinus bradycardia. Reports of sudden death and hypotension in children receiving oral clonidine in combination with other therapies for ADHD have been published. Such cases have also been reported with the use of clonidine alone. This may indicate that children and infants are more sensitive to the effects of clonidine and should be treated with extreme caution (see Contraindications). Severe rebound hypertension can occur during withdrawal from clonidine. This reaction is more likely to occur if clonidine is abruptly discontinued regardless of route of administration. Symptoms include hypersalivation, nervousness, headache, sinus tachycardia, palpitations, agitation, anxiety, diaphoresis, nausea, muscle pain, and abdominal pain. This effect is probably due to a drug-induced increase in the level of circulating catecholamines that follows abrupt cessation of therapy. Slowly tapering clonidine over several days will prevent this problem, and resumption of clonidine therapy will alleviate the symptoms. Clonidine therapy should not be interrupted for surgery; transdermal systems may be left in place during surgery.
 
The hypotension associated with epidural clonidine is usually responsive to fluids. Hypotension is observed more commonly in women and in lower weight patients, but no dose-response relationship has been established.
 
Sodium and fluid retention can cause weight gain during the first few days of oral clonidine therapy. These effects can be alleviated with diuretic therapy.
 
Transient blood glucose elevations have been reported, although clonidine has been administered safely to diabetic patients. In addition, clonidine has been used in the treatment of diabetic diarrhea.
 
Transient, localized, dermatological effects have occurred following the administration of clonidine patches and include erythema, pruritus, contact dermatitis, and skin hyperpigmentation.
 
Sexual dysfunction including impotence (erectile dysfunction), libido decrease, and ejaculation dysfunction (delayed or retrograde ejaculation) have been reported with clonidine therapy, as have urinary retention, nocturia, and dysuria.

Description
  Text
  Clonidine is a centrally acting alpha-2 agonist used for the treatment of hypertension, attention-deficit hyperactivity disorder (ADHD), and as an agent for refractory cancer pain. Clonidine is used off-label for opiate or benzodiazepine withdrawal, neonatal abstinence syndrome, tic disorders (e.g., Tourette's syndrome), autism spectrum disorder, and as a diagnostic agent for growth hormone deficiency. Clonidine works by stimulating alpha2-receptors in the brain stem, inhibiting sympathetic outflow and tone. Stimulating these receptors decreases vascular tone in the heart, kidneys, and peripheral vasculature; lowers peripheral resistance; and reduces blood pressure. When clonidine is used for the treatment of ADHD, the American Heart Association recommends careful cardiovascular screening and monitoring of individuals; weighing the benefits of treatment against the risks (e.g., sedation, hypotension). Clonidine is effective in the treatment of severe pain in cancer patients refractory to opiate agonists. The American Academy of Neurology practice guidelines state that clonidine is probably more likely than placebo to reduce tic severity in individuals with Tourette's syndrome or chronic tic disorders. For those with comorbid ADHD, clonidine may provide benefits for both conditions. Abrupt withdrawal from clonidine therapy, regardless of the route of administration, may cause rebound hypertension. Symptoms of rebound hypertension may include tachycardia, rapid elevation in blood pressure elevations, and, in some cases, hypertensive encephalopathy, stroke, and death. The risk of these reactions is greater after administration of higher clonidine doses or with concomitant beta-blocker therapy. Gradual tapering of clonidine prior to complete cessation of therapy may prevent adverse events. Clonidine was FDA-approved in 1974.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Clonidine is an agonist at presynaptic alpha2-receptors in the medulla, specifically, the nucleus tractus solitarius (i.e., the depressor area of the vasomotor center of the medulla oblongata). Stimulation of these receptors results in the inhibition of sympathetic outflow and tone. Suppression of efferent sympathetic pathways decreases vascular tone in the heart, kidneys, and peripheral vasculature; lowers peripheral resistance; and reduces blood pressure. Reflex tachycardia usually does not occur. Instead, stimulation of the central alpha-receptors by clonidine results in a reciprocal increase in vagal tone, causing an increase in baroreceptor activity and bradycardia. Clonidine is also a partial agonist at presynaptic alpha2-adrenergic receptors of peripheral nerves in vascular smooth muscle, however, this site of action contributes little, if anything, to its antihypertensive effects. Doses higher than those required to lower blood pressure are necessary to demonstrate agonism at this peripheral site. Further, it has been shown that alpha2-agonists that cannot penetrate the blood-brain barrier do not effectively lower blood pressure. Agonism at peripheral presynaptic alpha2-receptors may interfere with the peripheral regulation of norepinephrine.
 
Intravenous administration or large oral doses of clonidine also can stimulate alpha1-receptors in peripheral vascular smooth muscle, resulting in acute vasoconstriction and a transient increase in blood pressure. An accurate correlation between clonidine's plasma concentrations and its antihypertensive effects is evident only at lower plasma concentrations. Higher plasma concentrations of clonidine will result in reduced antihypertensive activity because of the increased contribution of the pressor effect.
 
Inhibition of sympathetic outflow by clonidine results in a variety of pharmacodynamic effects. In the supine position, decreased sympathetic tone reduces heart rate (HR), mean arterial pressure (MAP), cardiac output (CO), and stroke volume (SV), with essentially no change in the total peripheral resistance (TPR), renal blood flow (RBF), renal plasma flow (RPF), glomerular filtration rate (GFR), urinary potassium excretion, or renal vascular resistance (RVR). Urinary sodium and chloride excretion are increased, however. Thus, the principal antihypertensive effect in the supine position is related to the reduction in cardiac output secondary to the reduced stroke volume and heart rate.
 
In the erect position (45-degree tilt), clonidine reduces MAP, CO, HR, and TPR, with no significant change in stroke volume. Cardiac output due to clonidine decreases less in hypertensive patients than in normal patients, and the antihypertensive effects of the drug appear to be related to a reduction in both cardiac output and TPR, with the effects of the reduced TPR predominating in the erect position. Renin and aldosterone output are reduced. Clonidine appears to decrease catecholamine excretion during prolonged therapy, but it does not deplete catecholamine stores. As an antihypertensive, clonidine reduces LVH and does not cause detrimental effects on glucose tolerance, although sexual dysfunction is a significant problem.
 
Clonidine is used to treat hypertension and the subsequent decline of renal function in patients with scleroderma renal crisis (SRC). SRC is associated with elevated peripheral renin concentrations. Clonidine reduces plasma renin activity by reducing sympathetic activity while increasing parasympathetic activity.
 
Because of clonidine's ability to inhibit sympathetic outflow, it has been used successfully to manage withdrawal from opiate agonists, ethanol, or nicotine; and 'hot flashes' associated with menopause. By inhibiting intrarenal vasoconstriction, clonidine has been used to offset cyclosporine-induced nephrotoxicity. Since pheochromocytomas are not under neurologic control, clonidine has been used successfully to differentiate the presence of these tumors from other hypertension-associated disease states.
 
Clonidine administered epidurally produces a dose-dependent analgesia that is not antagonized by opiate antagonists. By preventing pain signal transmission to the brain, clonidine produces analgesia at presynaptic and postjunctional alpha2-adrenoceptors in the spinal cord. Yohimbine, an alpha2-adrenoreceptor antagonist, will partially reverse the analgesic and sedation effects of epidural clonidine with no effect on clonidine-induced changes in blood pressure or heart rate. Clonidine-induced analgesia occurs only in body regions innervated by spinal segments where analgesic concentrations of clonidine are present. Clonidine enhances epidural or peripheral nerve blocks by blocking the conduction of C and A delta fibers and increasing potassium conductance in neurons and causing local vasoconstriction decreasing the elimination of the local anesthetic.
 
The mechanism of action of clonidine in ADHD is not known.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Oral Route
    Description
    Immediate- and extended-release clonidine have different pharmacokinetics characteristics; dose substitution on a mg-for-mg basis will result in differences in exposure. Food does not influence the pharmacokinetics of oral clonidine. Elimination half-life of clonidine administered orally is approximately 12 to 16 hours.
 
Immediate-release (IR) tablets (e.g., Catapres)
After oral administration of clonidine IR tablets, peak plasma concentrations are reached in approximately 1 to 5 hours. The absolute bioavailability of orally administered clonidine is 70% to 80%. The pharmacokinetics of clonidine are dose-proportional in the range of 0.1 to 0.6 mg. Mean single-dose pharmacokinetic parameters in healthy adult individuals (fasted-state; n = 15) during clinical trials were as follows: Cmax = 443 pg/mL; AUC = 7,313 hour x pg/mL; Tmax = 2.07 hours; half-life = 12.57 hours.
 
Immediate-release oral solution
The absolute bioavailability of orally administered clonidine is 70% to 80%. The pharmacokinetics of clonidine are dose-proportional in the range of 0.1 to 0.6 mg.
Javadin
Following an oral dose of 0.3 mg clonidine solution in healthy adult subjects under fasting conditions, the median Tmax is 2.8 hours (range: 1 to 6 hours).
Qlonilik
Following oral administration of Qlonilik 50 mcg/mL solution under fasting conditions, the median Tmax is 2 hours (range: 0.75 to 8 hours).
 
Extended-release (ER) tablets (e.g., Kapvay)
After oral administration of clonidine ER tablets, peak clonidine concentrations were approximately 50% of those achieved with the IR formulation and occurred approximately 5 hours later. Similar half-lives were observed and total systemic bioavailability after the ER tablet was approximately 89% of that after the IR formulation. Mean single-dose pharmacokinetic parameters in healthy adult individuals (fasted-state; n = 14) during clinical trials were as follows: Cmax = 258 pg/mL; AUC = 6,729 hour x pg/mL; Tmax = 6.5 hours; half-life = 12.65 hours.
 
Extended-release tablets (e.g., Nexiclon XR)
After a single oral dose of 0.17 mg extended-release tablets, clonidine mean peak plasma concentrations (0.49 nanograms/mL) occurred at 7.8 hours (+/-1.7 hours) and the mean plasma half-life was 13.7 hours.
 
Extended-release (ER) suspension (e.g., Onyda XR)
Following a single dose of 0.2 mg clonidine extended-release suspension in healthy adult volunteers under fasting conditions, the median Tmax occurred at 7.5 hours (range: 4 to 17 hours). Peak concentration was 95.6% of the Cmax of clonidine extended-release tablets and the relative bioavailability was approximately 96.1% compared with an equal dose of clonidine extended-release tablet. Following oral administration of 0.2 mg clonidine extended-release suspension once daily over 5 days under fasted conditions in healthy adult subjects, the peak steady state plasma concentration was 107.9%, and steady state relative bioavailability was 97.7% compared with 0.1 mg of clonidine extended-release tablet administered twice daily under fasting conditions. The minimum concentration at steady state clonidine extended-release suspension was about 26% lower than that of the equal dose of clonidine extended-release tablet.

    Other Route(s)
    Description
    Transdermal Route
The clonidine transdermal system consists of a patch, or 0.2 mm thick film, that contains four layers of a microporous polypropylene membrane. This patch holds a reservoir of clonidine that is released and absorbed across the skin at a constant rate over a 7-day period. The absolute bioavailability of clonidine from the transdermal patch is approximately 60%. Steady-state clonidine plasma concentrations are reached within 3 days after transdermal application to the upper outer arm and increase linearly with increasing size of the transdermal patch. Mean steady-state plasma concentrations with the 3.5 cm2 (0.1 mg clonidine/day), 7 cm2 (0.2 mg clonidine/day) and 10.5 cm2 (0.3 mg clonidine/day) systems are approximately 0.4 ng/mL, 0.8 ng/mL, and 1.1 ng/mL, respectively. Similar clonidine steady-state concentrations are reached after application to the chest. Steady-state clonidine plasma levels remain constant after removal of one system and application of a new system of the same size. After removal of the transdermal system, clonidine plasma concentrations decline slowly with a half-life of approximately 20 hours.
 
Epidural Route
Following epidural administration, peak clonidine plasma and cerebrospinal fluid concentrations were achieved in 19 and 26 minutes, respectively. Epidurally administered clonidine distributes into plasma via the epidural veins. Clonidine is highly lipid soluble and distributes widely throughout the body tissues, including the central nervous system. The CSF elimination half-life of clonidine is 1.3 hours. After an epidural dose of clonidine, women had a lower mean plasma clearance, longer mean plasma half-life, and higher mean peak concentration of clonidine in the plasma and CSF compared to men. The pharmacokinetics of epidural clonidine in children 1 to 9 years of age is similar to adults.

  Text
  Clonidine is administered epidurally, orally, and transdermally. Fifty percent (50%) of a circulating dose is metabolized in the liver to inactive compounds. The apparent volume of distribution of oral and intravenous clonidine is 3 to 5.5 L/kg and 2.1 L/kg, respectively. Clonidine crosses the placental barrier in humans and has been shown to cross the blood brain barrier in rats. Following intravenous administration, clonidine displays biphasic disposition with a distribution half-life of about 20 minutes and an elimination half-life ranging from 12 to 16 hours. After oral administration, approximately 40% to 60% of the absorbed dose is recovered in the urine as unchanged drug in 24 hours.
 
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes and drug transporters: none

Dosage Adjustment Guidelines
  Hepatic Impairment
  Specific guidelines for dosage adjustments in hepatic impairment are not available; it appears no dosage adjustments are needed.
  Renal Impairment
  Initial dose should be based on the degree of renal impairment. A lower initial dose may be beneficial in individuals with renal impairment; monitor individuals for bradycardia, sedation, and hypotension.
 
Intermittent hemodialysis
Clonidine is minimally removed by hemodialysis. In general, no supplemental dosage is needed following hemodialysis. Adjust dosage based on clinical response. For individuals with end stage kidney disease on maintenance dialysis taking clonidine extended-release tablets for hypertension, initiate therapy at a dose of 0.09 mg/day and slowly up-titrate the dose to minimize adverse events.
