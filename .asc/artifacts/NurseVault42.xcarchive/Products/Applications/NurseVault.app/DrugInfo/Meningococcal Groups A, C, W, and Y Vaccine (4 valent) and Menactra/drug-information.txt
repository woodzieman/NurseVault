Drug Class: Meningococcal groups A, C, W, and Y vaccine is indicated for active immunization to prevent invasive disease caused by Neisseria meningitidis serogroups A, C, W, and Y.

Routes: intramuscular; Common adult doses: older than 56 years: 0.5 mL IM of MenQuadfi; safety and efficacy of Menactra or Menveo have not been established.

Common Side Effects (excluding routine GI upset): dehydration, bell's palsy, dizziness, guillain-barre syndrome, myelitis, paresis, paresthesias, anaphylactoid reactions, angioedema, dyspnea, hypotension, pruritus, rash, urticaria, wheezing, arthralgia, chills, drowsiness, fatigue, fever, headache, inconsolable crying, irritability, malaise, myalgia, seizures, lymphadenopathy, ptosis, vertigo

In an Overdose: No overdose/antidote information is listed in this file.

Key Nursing Considerations: Contraindications/precautions: Meningococcal polysaccharide diphtheria toxoid conjugate vaccine (MCV-4) should not be given via intravenous administration or subcutaneous administration; MCV-4 vaccine is only for intramuscular (IM) administration.

Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  General Information
  Meningococcal polysaccharide diphtheria toxoid conjugate vaccine (MCV-4) should not be given via intravenous administration or subcutaneous administration; MCV-4 vaccine is only for intramuscular (IM) administration. Any condition that contraindicates the use of IM injections contraindicates the use of MCV-4. These conditions may include thrombocytopenia or other bleeding disorders, or coagulopathy. Patients with thrombocytopenia, vitamin K deficiency, a coagulopathy (e.g., hemophilia) or receiving anticoagulant therapy could have bleeding occur at the IM injection site. If a decision is made to give MCV-4, the manufacturer recommends cautious use and appropriate clinical action to avoid bleeding risk or hematoma formation.
 
MCV-4 should not be used in patients with latex hypersensitivity as the stopper of the vial contains dry natural rubber latex, which may cause an allergic reaction in latex sensitive patients. MCV-4 is contraindicated in persons with known hypersensitivity to any component of the vaccine such as diphtheria toxoid. A life-threatening reaction after previous administration of a vaccine containing similar components is also a contraindication to MCV-4 administration. As with any biologic product, the prescriber or health care professional should take precautions to prevent allergic reactions. The health care professional should have immediate availability of epinephrine (1:1000) injection and other agents used in the treatment of severe anaphylaxis in the event of a serious allergic reaction to the vaccine.
 
MCV-4 immunization should be postponed in patients with moderate or severe febrile illness, a severe respiratory infection, or acute infection. Minor illness, such as a mild upper respiratory infection with or without low grade fever, does not preclude MCV-4 administration. Also, MCV-4 is not indicated for the treatment of meningococcal infections.
 
The immune response to MCV-4 in patients with immunosuppression has not been studied. Patients with immunosuppression may not have an adequate antibody response to vaccination with MCV-4. Providers may elect to vaccinate patients aged 2—10 years who are infected with human immunodeficiency virus (HIV), but the efficacy of MCV-4 among these patients is unknown. Patients with HIV infection likely are at increased risk for meningococcal disease, although not to the extent that they are at risk for invasive Streptococcus pneumoniae infection.
 
Although classified as FDA pregnancy risk category C, administration of MCV-4 vaccine during pregnancy is not recommended. There are no adequate and well controlled trials in pregnant women. Animal reproduction studies have shown no effects on fetal health. Because animal studies are not always predictive of human responses, MCV-4 should be used in pregnancy only if the risk outweighs the benefit. If a woman was pregnant at the time of immunization, contact Aventis Pasteur Inc. at 800—822—2463. Tell all women of childbearing potential who discover they were pregnant at the time of vaccination to report their pregnancy to Aventis Pasteur Inc. or to a healthcare professional. The manufacturer maintains a pregnancy register to monitor fetal outcomes.
 
There is no information on the excretion of MCV-4 antigens or antibodies in breast milk during breast-feeding. Because many drugs are excreted in breast milk, MCV-4 should be administered with caution to a nursing mother.
 
MCV-4 is currently not recommended for use in children less than 2 years of age or infants. Safety and efficacy have not been established in children less than 2 years of age.
 
Safety and efficacy have not been established in patients greater than 55 years of age, including the elderly. Vaccination with the meningococcal polysaccharide diphtheria toxoid conjugate vaccine is not recommended for patients greater than 55 years of age.
 
MCV-4 is contraindicated in patients with a known history of Guillain-Barre syndrome. The Centers for Disease Control and Prevention recommends that persons with a history of Guillain-Barre syndrome not receive MCV-4, although persons with a history of Guillain-Barre syndrome at especially high risk for meningococcal disease, such as microbiologists routinely exposed to isolates of Neisseria meningitidis, might consider vaccination. Guillain-Barre syndrome has occurred after meningococcal polysaccharide diphtheria toxoid conjugate vaccination, but a causal relationship has not been established (see Adverse Reactions).

    Headline
    Treatment-Related Restrictions of Use
    General Information
    Deferral of meningococcal groups A, C, W, and Y vaccine (4 valent) administration may be appropriate for a patient with a moderate or severe acute illness such as infection with or without fever. The Advisory Committee on Immunization Practices recommends that vaccinations be delayed during the course of a moderate or severe acute febrile illness and administered after the acute phase of illness has resolved. All vaccines can be administered to persons with minor illnesses such as diarrhea, mild upper-respiratory infection with or without low-grade fever, or other low-grade febrile illness. Vaccinate persons with moderate or severe febrile illness as soon as they have recovered from the acute phase of the illness.
    Headline
    immunosuppression
    General Information
    Individuals with significant immunosuppression may not have an adequate antibody response to meningococcal groups A, C, W, and Y vaccine. Immunosuppressed persons may include individuals with human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) infection; severe combined immunodeficiency (SCID); hypogammaglobulinemia; agammaglobulinemia; altered immune states due to generalized neoplastic disease; complement deficiencies; an immune system compromised by radiation therapy or drug therapy (e.g., chemotherapy or corticosteroid therapy with greater than physiologic doses); or individuals receiving treatment that inhibits terminal complement activation (e.g. eculizumab). Individuals with certain complement deficiencies and those receiving treatment that inhibits terminal complement activation are at an increased risk for disease caused by N. meningitidis, even if they develop antibodies after vaccination. Short-term (less than 2 weeks) corticosteroid therapy or intra-articular, bursal, or tendon injections with corticosteroids should not be immunosuppressive. Individuals vaccinated with meningococcal vaccine within 2 weeks before starting immunosuppressive therapy or while receiving immunosuppressive therapy should be considered unvaccinated and should be revaccinated at least 3 months after therapy is discontinued if immune competence has been restored. In addition, evidence suggests that persons with human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) infection do not respond optimally to a single dose. Individuals with HIV infection, functional/anatomical asplenia, or persistent complement deficiencies (i.e., C3, C5 to C9, properdin, factor D or factor H) require a 2 to 4 dose primary series, depending on age at first dose and product administered. Booster doses should be administered while individuals remain at increased risk of meningococcal disease. 34362
    Headline
    acute inflammatory demyelinating polyneuropathy
    General Information
    Individuals previously diagnosed with acute inflammatory demyelinating polyneuropathy or Guillain-Barre syndrome (GBS) may be at increased risk for recurrence of GBS after vaccination with meningococcal groups A, C, W, and Y vaccine. Conisder the potential benefits and risks of meningococcal conjugate vaccine administration in individuals with a history of GBS. Cases of GBS have been reported in temporal association with administration of a meningococcal quadrivalent polysaccharide conjugate vaccine; however, postmarketing data has not established a causal relationship or confirmed an increased risk of GBS.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Available data on the use of meningococcal groups A, C, W, and Y vaccine during human pregnancy do not suggest an increased risk of major birth defects and miscarriage in individuals who received the vaccine during pregnancy, or within 28 days prior to conception. Animal studies have revealed no evidence of fetal harm. According to the Advisory Committee on Immunization Practices (ACIP), administration of inactivated vaccines during pregnancy has not resulted in adverse fetal effects. The ACIP recommends vaccination during pregnancy when the likelihood of disease exposure is high, when potential infection would cause harm to the pregnant person or fetus, and when the vaccine is unlikely to cause harm. MenQuadfi has a pregnancy exposure registry. To enroll or obtain information, contact the manufacturer at 1-800-822-2463.
    Headline
    premature neonates
    General Information
    In some premature neonates, apnea has been observed after intramuscular vaccination. Consider the infant's medical status and the vaccine's potential benefits and possible risks when deciding when to administer an intramuscular vaccine, such as meningococcal conjugate vaccine, to infants born prematurely.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Use meningococcal groups A, C, W, and Y vaccine with caution during breast-feeding. There are no data on the presence of the vaccine in human milk, its effects on the breastfed child, or its effects on milk production. However, inactivated vaccines are not expected to pose a risk to breast-feeding individuals or to the breastfed child.

  General Information
  Over 7600 patients have received the meningococcal polysaccharide diphtheria toxoid conjugate vaccine (MCV-4) in clinical trials. The most commonly reported adverse reactions in 1384 adults 18—55 years of age include erythema (14%), edema (12%), induration (17%), pain (53%), headache (41%), malaise (23%), fatigue (34%), arthralgia (19%), diarrhea (16%), anorexia (11%), chills (9%), nausea/vomiting, rash (unspecified), and fever. Redness at the injection site that was greater than 2 inches in size occurred in 3.9% of children.Guillain-Barre syndrome has occurred after meningococcal polysaccharide diphtheria toxoid conjugate vaccination, but a causal relationship has not been established. Guillain-Barre syndrome, which is characterized by muscular weakness of the extremities and can lead to paralysis, can occur in a variety of circumstances. Other signs and symptoms of Guillain-Barre syndrome may include tingling, numbness, and a decrease in deep tendon reflexes in extremities; gait difficulties; slurred speech; and swallowing difficulties. Seventeen reports of Guillain-Barre syndrome after administration of the vaccine have been received through the Vaccine Adverse Event Reporting System (VAERS); 15 cases occurred within 6 weeks after vaccination of patients 11—19 years of age, and 2 cases have been confirmed in persons 20 years of age and older. All individuals are reported to be recovering or have recovered. The mean time from the date of vaccination to the date of Guillain-Barre syndrome symptom onset for the 17 cases was 15.7 days (range, 2—33 days). The Centers for Disease Control (CDC) estimates that the apparent rate of postvaccination Guillain-Barre syndrome is about 1.25 cases per 1 million adolescent and teen vaccinees whereas about 1.2 cases of meningitis are reported per 100,000 people in this age group. As data on background rates of Guillain-Barre syndrome are limited, as meningitis kills up to 14% of patients diagnosed with the condition, and as the link between Guillain-Barre syndrome and MCV-4 vaccination is under investigation, the CDC has not altered current MCV-4 vaccination recommendations (see Dosage). Report any suspected cases of Guillain-Barre syndrome that occur in vaccine recipients (see Administration).Allergic and anaphylactoid reactions with the use of MCV-4 have been reported in post-marketing safety data. Currently, there is insufficient data to establish a causal relationship between the development of anaphylactoid reactions and the administration of MCV-4. The vaccine recipient may experience difficulty breathing, wheezing, urticaria, erythema, pruritus, hypotension, or swelling of the face and throat. MCV-4 should not be used in patients with latex hypersensitivity, as the stopper of the vial contains dry natural rubber latex (see Contraindications). Health care professionals should take precautions to prevent allergic reactions and have immediate availability of agents used in the treatment of severe anaphylaxis (e.g., epinephrine (1:1000) injection).

Description
  Text
  Meningococcal groups A, C, W, and Y vaccine is indicated for active immunization to prevent invasive disease caused by Neisseria meningitidis serogroups A, C, W, and Y. Meningococcal disease is a result of an invasive infection by N. meningitidis; the 5 main N. meningitidis serogroups responsible for meningococcal disease are A, B, C, W, and Y. Although relatively uncommon in the U.S., meningococcal disease is fatal in approximately 15% of patients despite appropriate antibiotic therapy, with an additional 10% to 20% experiencing permanent complications (brain damage and limb loss). Individuals at increased risk for meningococcal disease include infants and children younger than 5 years, adolescents and young adults 16 to 21 years, and the elderly. Routine vaccination is recommended for adolescents 11 to 18 years, preferably with 1 dose of a meningococcal conjugate vaccine at age 11 or 12 years, and a booster dose at 16 years; booster doses may be indicated for individuals who remain at continued risk.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  The presence of bacterial anti-capsular antibodies has been associated with protection from invasive meningococcal disease. Serogroup A, C, W, and Y capsular polysaccharide (Menactra) or oligosaccharides (Menveo) antigens are individually conjugated to diphtheria toxoid protein. The polysaccharide conjugate vaccine (MenQuadfi) is conjugated to tetanus toxoid protein. Polysaccharide vaccines only elicit a B-cell response, so although antibodies are produced, there is no long-term memory. By coupling the polysaccharides to a carrier protein, a T-cell dependent immune response is generated. A polysaccharide conjugated to a protein carrier also stimulates both B-cell immune response and T-helper cell response to the protein carrier, resulting in antibodies to the protein carrier and immune memory. The diphtheria and/or tetanus toxoid protein does not induce immunity to the diphtheria and/or tetanus antigen. Immunization against diphtheria and/or tetanus does not occur after vaccination with the meningococcal diphtheria toxoid conjugate vaccine or the meningococcal tetanus toxoid conjugate vaccine.

    Description
    In separate active-controlled trials comparing Menveo with Menactra and MenQuadfi with Menactra in adults 18 or 19 through 55 years of age, seroresponse rates to each serogroup 28 to 30 days after vaccination were obtained. Reconstitution (Menveo 2 vial presentation only)
Use the MenCYW-135 liquid conjugate component (Vial 1, gray cap) to reconstitute the MenA lyophilized conjugate component (Vial 2, orange cap) to form Menveo.
Using a sterile needle and sterile graduated syringe, withdraw the entire contents of the vial of MenCYW-135 liquid conjugate component (Vial 1, gray cap) while slightly tilting the vial.
Slowly transfer the entire contents of the syringe into the MenA lyophilized conjugate component (Vial 2, orange cap). Invert and shake the reconstituted vial until lyophilized conjugate component is completely dissolved.
After reconstitution, withdraw 0.5 mL from the vial containing the reconstituted vaccine.
Administer vaccine immediately after reconstitution.
 
Intramuscular Injection (Menactra, Menveo, and MenQuadfi)
When Daptacel and Menactra are to be given together for children 4 through 6 years, administer the 2 vaccines concomitantly or administer Menactra before Daptacel. Clinical trials found a reduced meningococcal antibody response when Menactra was administered 1 month after Daptacel.
Do not mix with any other vaccine.
When administering more than 1 vaccine during the same visit, administer each vaccine at a different anatomic site and do not combine vaccines in the same syringe.
Carefully observe patients for approximately 15 minutes after administration; syncope may occur.

    Description
    Visually inspect parenteral products for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration whenever solution and container permit.
Menactra solution should be clear to slightly turbid.
Menveo and MenQuadfi are a clear, colorless solution that is free from visible foreign particles. If discoloration or visible particulate matter are present, discard the vial or syringe.
