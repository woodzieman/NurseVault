Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  General Information
  Fomepizole is classified pregnancy category C. Animal reproduction studies have not been conducted. It is also not known whether fomepizole can cause fetal harm when administered to pregnant women or can affect reproduction capacity. Fomepizole should be used during pregnancy only if clearly needed.
 
It is not known whether fomepizole is excreted in human breast milk. Because many drugs are excreted in human milk, fomepizole should be used cautiously in breast-feeding women.
 
The safe and effective use of fomepizole in children has not been established.
 
Fomepizole is substantially excreted by the kidney. The risk of toxic reactions to this drug may be increased in patients with renal impairment or renal disease. Because the elderly patients are more likely to have decreased renal function, care should be taken in dose selection in this patient population.
 
Fomepizole should not be administered to patients with a documented serious hypersensitivity reaction to fomepizole or other pyrazoles.
 
In addition to specific antidote treatment with fomepizole, patients intoxicated with ethylene glycol must be managed for metabolic acidosis, acute renal failure, adult respiratory distress syndrome, and hypocalcemia. Fluid therapy and sodium bicarbonate administration are potential supportive therapies. In addition, potassium and calcium supplementation and oxygen administration are usually necessary. Hemodialysis is necessary in the anuric patient, or in patients with severe metabolic acidosis or azotemia. Treatment success should be assessed by frequent measurements of blood gases, pH, electrolytes, BUN, creatinine, and urinalysis, in addition to other laboratory tests as indicated by individual patient conditions. Ethylene glycol plasma and urine concentrations and presence of urinary oxalate crystals should also be monitored frequently throughout treatment to assess the status of ethylene glycol and metabolite clearance. Since acidosis and electrolyte imbalances can affect the cardiovascular system, electrocardiography should be performed. In the comatose patient, electroencephalography may also be required. Hepatic enzymes and white blood cell counts should be monitored during treatment, as transient increases in serum transaminase levels and eosinophilia have been noted with repeated fomepizole dosing.

Description
  Text
  Fomepizole is a competitive inhibitor of alcohol dehydrogenase and is an antidote in ethylene glycol and methanol poisoning. It prevents the conversion of these substances into toxic metabolites. In ethylene glycol poisoning, fomepizole stops the formation of glycolate and oxalate, which cause metabolic acidosis and renal damage. In methanol poisoning, it blocks formaldehyde and formic acid formation, averting metabolic acidosis and visual disturbances. When preparing the drug, avoid contact with polycarbonate syringes and needles, as fomepizole can interact with polycarbonate. The drug is administered diluted via intravenous infusion over 30 minutes. In patients with renal failure, significant metabolic acidosis, or a measured ethylene glycol or methanol concentration 50 mg/dL or greater, consider concomitant hemodialysis. If given with hemodialysis, increase fomepizole dosing frequency as it is dialyzable. Monitoring recommendations include frequent checks of blood gases, pH, electrolytes, BUN, creatinine, and urinalysis. Ethylene glycol and methanol concentrations should be tracked, along with urinary oxalate crystals in ethylene glycol poisoning. Electrocardiography is recommended due to potential cardiovascular effects. Hepatic enzymes and white blood cell counts should also be monitored. Supportive therapies include fluids, sodium bicarbonate, electrolyte supplementation (calcium and potassium), and oxygen administration. Fomepizole was FDA-approved in 1997.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Fomepizole is used as an antidote for ethylene glycol and methanol poisoning. It acts as a competitive inhibitor of alcohol dehydrogenase, an enzyme that initiates the metabolism of ethylene glycol and methanol into their toxic metabolites. By inhibiting alcohol dehydrogenase, fomepizole prevents the conversion of ethylene glycol to glycolaldehyde and subsequently to glycolate, glyoxylate, and oxalate, which are responsible for metabolic acidosis and renal damage in ethylene glycol poisoning. Similarly, it prevents the metabolism of methanol to formaldehyde and then to formic acid, which causes metabolic acidosis and visual disturbances in methanol poisoning. Fomepizole was found to be effecive in the treatment of ethylene glycol and methanol intoxication in two prospective U.S. trials and a French trial.

  Text
  Fomepizole is administered by intravenous infusion. Once in systemic circulation, fomepizole rapidly distributes to total body water with a volume of distribution ranging from 0.6 to 1.02 L/kg. The primary mode of elimination is metabolism, which follows Michaelis-Menten kinetics after acute doses, leading to saturable elimination at therapeutic blood concentrations. After multiple doses, fomepizole induces its own metabolism via CYP450 enzyme induction, significantly increasing its elimination rate after approximately 30 to 40 hours. Post-enzyme induction, elimination adheres to first-order kinetics. The main metabolite, 4-carboxypyrazole, accounts for approximately 80% to 85% of the administered dose, while other metabolites include 4-hydroxymethylpyrazole and the N-glucuronide conjugates of both 4-carboxypyrazole and 4-hydroxymethylpyrazole. Fomepizole and its metabolites are excreted via the urine, with only 1% to 3.5% of the dose eliminated unchanged in healthy volunteers. Irrespective of renal function, the plasma half-life varies with dose and has not been calculated.
