Drug Class: Telisotuzumab vedotin is a c-Met-directed antibody and microtubule inhibitor conjugate.

Routes: intravenous; Common adult doses: 1.9 mg/kg (max dose 190 mg for weight 100 kg or greater) IV every 2 weeks.

Common Side Effects (excluding routine GI upset): hyperesthesia, hypoesthesia, neuropathic pain, paresthesias, peripheral neuropathy, cough, dyspnea, fever, interstitial lung disease, pneumonitis, blurred vision, keratitis, photophobia, visual impairment, xerophthalmia, chills, hypotension, infusion-related reactions, hypermagnesemia, hypocalcemia, hypokalemia, hypomagnesemia, hyponatremia, anemia, leukopenia, lymphopenia, neutropenia, thrombocytopenia

In an Overdose: No overdose/antidote information is listed in this file.

          Dosage Description
          1.9 mg/kg (maximum dose, 190 mg) IV every 2 weeks until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity. Coadministration of certain drugs may need to be avoided or dosage adjustments may be necessary; review drug interactions. Treatment with telisotuzumab vedotin resulted in an overall response rate of 35% (all partial responses) by blinded independent central review (BICR) in patients with locally advanced or metastatic NSCLC with c-Met protein overexpression and treatment with prior systemic therapy (including no more than one line of prior chemotherapy) in the locally advanced or metastatic setting. The median duration of response was 7.2 months, with 59% of patients maintaining a response of at least 6 months, and 21% of patients maintaining a response of at least 12 months.

    Headline
    Child-Pugh class B, Child-Pugh class C, hepatic failure
    General Information
    Avoid the use of telisotuzumab vedotin in patients with moderate or severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh Class B, Child-Pugh Class C, or total bilirubin greater than 1.5 times the upper limit of normal [ULN] with any AST) or hepatic failure. Telisotuzumab vedotin has not been studied in these populations, and patients are likely to have increased exposure to the microtubule-disrupting agent, monomethyl auristatin E (MMAE) which may increase the risk of adverse reactions.
    Headline
    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components.
    Headline
    pulmonary disease
    General Information
    Use telisotuzumab vedotin with caution in patients who have a history of pre-existing pulmonary disease other than lung cancer; severe pneumonitis/interstitial lung disease has been reported in patients treated with telisotuzumab vedotin. Monitor patients for new or worsening respiratory symptoms including fever, cough, or dyspnea. Immediately interrupt telisotuzumab vedotin therapy in patients with new or worsening respiratory symptoms and permanently discontinue therapy if no other potential causes are identified.
    Headline
    contact lenses, dry eye disease, keratitis
    General Information
    Use telisotuzumab vedotin with caution in patients with a history of keratitis or severe dry eye disease. Keratitis has occurred in patients treated with telisotuzumab vedotin; patients who wear contact lenses are also at increased risk. Patients who develop an onset of new eye symptoms or visual disturbance such as ocular pain, inflammation, lacrimation, light sensitivity, blurred vision, and/or red eye should be evaluated and managed appropriately; an interruption or discontinuation of therapy may be necessary.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Pregnancy should be avoided during telisotuzumab vedotin treatment and for at least 2 months after the last dose. Although there are no adequately controlled human studies in pregnancy, telisotuzumab vedotin can cause fetal harm or death when administered during pregnancy based on its mechanism of action and animal studies. Administration of monomethyl auristatin E (MMAE), the microtubule-disrupting agent in telisotuzumab vedotin, to pregnant rats during organogenesis resulted in embryofetal mortality and structural abnormalities (e.g., protruding tongue, agnathia, malrotated limbs, and gastroschisis) at exposures similar to the clinical exposure at the recommended dose. Individuals who are pregnant or who may become pregnant while receiving telisotuzumab vedotin should be apprised of the potential hazard to the fetus.
    Headline
    peripheral neuropathy
    General Information
    Monitor patients treated with telisotuzumab vedotin for signs and symptoms of new or worsening peripheral neuropathy, including hypoesthesia, hyperesthesia, paresthesia, a burning sensation, neuropathic pain, or muscle weakness. An interruption of therapy, dose reduction, or discontinuation of therapy may be necessary if neuropathy occurs.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in nursing infants from telisotuzumab vedotin, advise individuals to discontinue breast-feeding during treatment and for 1 month after the final dose. It is not known whether telisotuzumab vedotin is present in human milk, although many drugs are excreted in human milk.
    Headline
    people who can cause pregnancy in others, people who may become pregnant, reproductive risk
    General Information
    Counsel people about the reproductive risk associated with telisotuzumab vedotin. People who may become pregnant should avoid pregnancy and use effective contraception during and for 2 months after treatment with telisotuzumab vedotin. People who may become pregnant should undergo pregnancy testing prior to initiation of telisotuzumab vedotin. People who can cause pregnancy in others should use effective contraception during and for 4 months after treatment with telisotuzumab vedotin. Individuals who become pregnant while receiving telisotuzumab vedotin should be apprised of the potential hazard to the fetus. Although there are no data regarding the effect of telisotuzumab vedotin on human fertility, male and female infertility has been observed in animal studies.

    Headline
    hyperesthesia, hypoesthesia, neuropathic pain, paresthesias, peripheral neuropathy
    General Information
    Telisotuzumab vedotin can cause peripheral neuropathy, including peripheral sensory and peripheral motor neuropathy. Symptoms may include hypoesthesia, hyperesthesia, paresthesias, burning sensation, neuropathic pain, or muscle weakness. In a pooled safety population, peripheral neuropathy was reported in 51% (grade 3, 11%) of patients treated with telisotuzumab vedotin; peripheral sensory neuropathy occurred in 45% while peripheral motor neuropathy occurred in 9% of these patients. The median time to onset of peripheral neuropathy was 105 days. Of the 7 patients with ongoing motor neuropathy as of their last dose of telisotuzumab vedotin, 6 had persistent symptoms (grade 1 or 2) 30 days after their last dose. Higher telisotuzumab vedotin exposure is associated with increased rates of grade 3 peripheral neuropathy. If peripheral neuropathy occurs, an interruption of therapy, dose reduction, or discontinuation of therapy may be necessary depending on the severity.

    Headline
    cough, dyspnea, fever, interstitial lung disease, pneumonitis
    General Information
    Telisotuzumab vedotin can cause severe interstitial lung disease (ILD)/pneumonitis, including life-threatening and fatal cases. Symptoms may include cough, dyspnea, fever or any new or worsening respiratory symptoms. In a pooled safety population, ILD/pneumonitis occurred in 10% (grade 3 or 4, 3.6%) of telisotuzumab vedotin-treated patients, including 3 fatal cases. The median time to onset of ILD/pneumonitis was 48 days. If ILD/pneumonitis is confirmed, telisotuzumab therapy may need to be interrupted or discontinued depending on severity; treatment with corticosteroids may be necessary.

    Headline
    blurred vision, keratitis, photophobia, visual impairment, xerophthalmia
    General Information
    Telisotuzumab vedotin can cause ocular surface disorders including visual impairment, blurred vision, keratitis, and xerophthalmia.  In a pooled safety population, ocular surface disorders occurred in 25% of patients treated with telisotuzumab vedotin, including blurred vision (15%; grade 3, 1.2%), keratitis (11%; grade 3, 0.6%), and dry eye (5%). The median time to onset of ocular surface disorders was 47 days. Higher telisotuzumab vedotin exposure is associated with increased rates of grades 2 and 3 ocular surface disorders. Photophobia also occurred in less than 10% of patients treated with telisotuzumab vedotin in a single-arm, open-label trial. If grade 2 or higher ocular toxicity occurs, withhold telisotuzumab and refer to an eye care professional for ophthalmic exam and treatment; telisotuzumab therapy may need to be interrupted or discontinued depending on severity.

    Headline
    chills, hypotension, infusion-related reactions
    General Information
    Telisotuzumab vedotin can cause infusion-related reactions (IRR) with symptoms including dyspnea, flushing, chills, nausea, chest discomfort, and hypotension. In a pooled safety population, IRR occurred in 3% (grade 3 or 4, 1.8%) of patients treated with telisotuzumab vedotin. The median time to onset of IRR was 28 days. Interrupt the infusion, administer supportive treatment, and resume at a slower infusion rate for mild to moderate reactions (grade 1 to 3); permanently discontinue telisotuzumab vedotin for severe or life-threatening IRR (grade 4). Administer premedication, including an antipyretic (e.g. acetaminophen), a glucocorticoid (e.g. methylprednisolone), an H2-blocker (e.g. famotidine), and an H1-blocker (e.g. diphenhydramine), prior to all future doses for patients who experience an IRR.

Description
  Text
  Telisotuzumab vedotin is a c-Met-directed antibody and microtubule inhibitor conjugate. It is approved for treatment of adult patients with locally advanced or metastatic non-squamous non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) with high c-MET protein over expression (50% or more of tumors with strong [3+] staining), who have received a prior systemic therapy. Telisotuzumab vedotin can cause peripheral neuropathy, interstitial lung disease/pneumonitis, infusion-related reactions, and ocular surface disorders including visual impairment and keratitis. It was approved by the FDA in May 2025.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Telisotuzumab vedotin is a c-Met-directed antibody drug conjugate (ADC). Telisotuzumab is a humanized IgG1k antibody directed against c-Met, the cell surface receptor for hepatocyte growth factor. The microtubule-disrupting agent, MMAE, is the small molecule attached to the antibody by a protease cleavable linker. Upon binding to c-Met-expressing cells, telisotuzumab vedotin undergoes internalization and intracellular cleavage of MMAE. Once internalized, MMAE induces cell cycle arrest and apoptotic cell death by disrupting the microtubule network of actively dividing cells. In xenograft models of NSCLC, telisotuzumab vedotin exhibited antitumor activity.

  Text
  Telisotuzumab vedotin is administered intravenously. The estimated volume of distribution of telisotuzumab vedotin is 3.4 L (CV, 16.5%); MMAE plasma protein binding ranges from 68% to 82% in vitro. Telisotuzumab vedotin undergoes intracellular cleavage to release MMAE. While telisotuzumab vedotin catabolism has not been studied in humans, it is expected to be degraded into small peptides, amino acids, unconjugated MMAE, and unconjugated MMAE-related catabolites. MMAE is primarily metabolized by CYP3A4. The elimination half-life is approximately 3 days for telisotuzumab vedotin and approximately 4 days for MMAE. The estimated clearance is 1.3 L/day (CV, 31.5%) for telisotuzumab vedotin and 76 L/day (CV, 52.1%) for MMAE.
 
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes and drug transporters: CYP3A4, CYP3A5, P-gp
In vitro, MMAE is a substrate of CYP3A4, CYP3A5, and P-gp and is an inhibitor of CYP3A4 and CYP3A5. No clinical studies have evaluated the potential for drug-drug interactions with telisotuzumab vedotin. However, the AUC of MMAE is predicted to increase by 1.4-fold following concomitant administration with ketoconazole (a strong CYP3A4 inhibitor). MMAE AUC is predicted to decrease by 70% following concomitant administration with rifampin (a strong CYP3A4 inducer); no clinically significant difference in the pharmacokinetics of midazolam (a sensitive CYP3A substrate) is predicated when used concomitantly with telisotuzumab vedotin.

Withdraw the calculated volume of telisotuzumab vedotin and add to an IV bag containing 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection to achieve a final concentration between 1 to 10 mg/mL. Mix by gentle inversion. Do not shake.
Discard any unused drug remaining in the vials.
Storage: Diluted solution can be stored under refrigeration (2 to 8 degrees C or 36 to 46 degrees F) for up to 24 hours and an additional 4 hours at room temperature (9 to 30 degrees C or 48 to 86 degrees F) until the end of administration. Do not freeze.
