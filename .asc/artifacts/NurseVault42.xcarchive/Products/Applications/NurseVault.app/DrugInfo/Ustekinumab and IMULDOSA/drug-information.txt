NURSING QUICK-REFERENCE SUMMARY
Auto-generated on 2026-09-25 from the prescribing information in this file; full details follow below.

Drug Class: Ustekinumab is a monoclonal antibody that inhibits interleukin-12 and interleukin-23 by binding their shared p40 subunit, making it useful for several inflammatory conditions.

Routes: injection (IV/IM/SC); Common adult doses: weighing more than 85 kg: 520 mg single dose IV; 90 mg/dose subcutaneously.

Common Side Effects (excluding routine GI upset): appendicitis, candidiasis, cholecystitis, dental caries, infection, influenza, meningitis, ocular infection, pharyngitis, sinusitis, arthralgia, asthenia, back pain, depression, dizziness, fatigue, fever, headache, myalgia, ecchymosis, erythema, injection site reaction, skin irritation, anaphylactoid reactions, angioedema, pruritus, rash, urticaria, vasculitis, dyspnea

In an Overdose: No overdose/antidote information is listed in this file.

Key Nursing Considerations: Contraindications/precautions: This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components. The needle cover of the Stelara prefilled syringe contains natural rubber, a derivative of latex; latex sensitive patients should not handle the needle cover of this product.

Contraindications And Precautions

  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  Contraindications And Precautions
    Headline
    Drug Interactions
    General Information
    The coadministration of certain medications may lead to harm and require avoidance or therapy modification; review all drug interactions prior to concomitant use of other medications.
    Headline
    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components. The needle cover of the Stelara prefilled syringe contains natural rubber, a derivative of latex; latex sensitive patients should not handle the needle cover of this product.
    Headline
    General Information
    General Information
    Update immunizations in agreement with current immunization guidelines prior to initiating ustekinumab therapy. Avoid the use of live vaccines in patients receiving ustekinumab. Avoid administering Bacillus Calmette-Guerin (BCG) vaccines one year before, during, and for 1 year after ustekinumab therapy. Exercise caution when administering live vaccines to household contacts of patients receiving ustekinumab due to the potential risk of transmission. Non-live vaccinations received during a course of treatment may not elicit an immune response sufficient to prevent disease.
Before starting ustekinumab, test potential drug recipients for hepatitis B (surface antigen and core antibody), hepatitis C (IgG), and HIV (HIV-1 and HIV-2 antibodies and HIV-1 antigen). Consider ongoing screening (e.g., annually) in people who are at increased risk for HIV, hepatitis B, or hepatitis C infection. Retest for HIV in any person who displays symptoms or other conditions that may suggest HIV. In patients found to be HIV positive, consider involving relevant specialists to ensure the HIV viral load is suppressed through antiretroviral therapy. Similarly, retest for viral hepatitis in any person who develops unexplained elevations in hepatic enzymes. It is recommended to consult a hepatologist when using biological therapy in patients with hepatitis B or C, whether acute (newly diagnosed) or chronic.
    Headline
    infection, interleukin-12 deficiency, interleukin-23 deficiency
    General Information
    Treatment with ustekinumab increases the risk of serious infections that may lead to hospitalization or death. Avoid starting treatment in people with any clinically important active infection until the infection resolves or is adequately treated. Consider the risks and benefits of treatment prior to starting ustekinumab in patients with a chronic infection or history of recurrent infection. Instruct patients to seek medical advice if signs or symptoms suggestive of an infection occur during treatment, and discontinue ustekinumab for serious or clinically significant infections until resolved or adequately treated. Evaluate all patients for tuberculosis (TB) infection prior to initiating ustekinumab. Avoid use in patients with active TB. Initiate treatment of latent TB before starting ustekinumab. Consider anti-tuberculosis therapy prior to initiation in patients with a past history of latent or active TB in whom an adequate course of treatment cannot be confirmed. Closely monitor all patients for signs and symptoms of active TB during and after treatment. Individuals with interleukin-12 deficiency and interleukin-23 deficiency are particularly vulnerable to disseminated infections from mycobacteria (including nontuberculous, environmental mycobacteria), salmonella (including non-typhi strains), and Bacillus Calmette-Guerin (BCG) vaccinations. Although it is unknown if pharmacologic blockade of IL-12/IL-23 with ustekinumab confers similar susceptibility, consider appropriate diagnostic testing as dictated by clinical circumstances.
    Headline
    geriatric, malignancy
    General Information
    The safety of ustekinumab has not been evaluated in patients who have a history of malignancy or  who have a known malignancy; use with caution in these patients. Monitor all recipients of ustekinumab for the appearance of non-melanoma skin cancer (NMSC), particularly geriatric adults (60 years and older) and those with a history of prolonged immunosuppressant treatment or history of Psoralen plus Ultraviolet A (PUVA) phototherapy. Malignancies, including NMSC, were reported with the use of ustekinumab during clinical trials. Additionally, there have been postmarketing reports of the rapid appearance of multiple cutaneous squamous cell carcinomas in patients receiving ustekinumab who had preexisting risk factors for developing NMSC.
    Headline
    alpha-gal syndrome, desensitization procedures
    General Information
    Ustekinumab products have not been evaluated in patients who have undergone allergy immunotherapy or desensitization procedures. Ustekinumab may decrease the protective effect of allergen immunotherapy (decrease tolerance) which may increase the risk of an allergic reaction to a dose of allergen immunotherapy. Therefore, exercise caution in patients receiving or who have received allergen immunotherapy, particularly for anaphylaxis. In addition, use ustekinumab with caution in people with alpha-gal syndrome. Some cases of serious hypersensitivity reactions to ustekinumab have been reported in individuals with a history of alpha-gal syndrome, allergy to mammalian (red) meat or meat products, or anti-alpha-gal IgE.
    Headline
    planned procedure
    General Information
    Patients who undergo surgery while taking biologic therapy, such as ustekinumab, may be at greater risk for postoperative infections. For a planned procedure, balance the risk of postoperative infection against the risk of developing a severe or unstable disease by stopping the biologic therapy. Recommendations for the use of immunomodulatory agents in the perioperative setting vary. The American Academy of Dermatology and National Psoriasis Foundation recommend patients with psoriasis and psoriatic arthritis continue to receive ustekinumab through low-risk surgeries or procedures. They also suggest that ustekinumab may be safely continued through intermediate- and high-risk surgeries on a case-by-case basis; however, The American College of Rheumatology recommends holding ustekinumab for 13 weeks before undergoing total knee or total hip arthroplasties. The British Association of Dermatologists advise stopping biologic therapy 3- to 5-times the half-life or the length of the treatment cycle (whichever is longer) between the last dose and a planned surgery. Restart the biologic therapy postoperatively if there is no evidence of infection and wound healing is satisfactory.
    Headline
    neonates and infants exposed to this medication in utero
    General Information
    Ustekinumab may theoretically interfere with immune response to infections in neonates and infants exposed to this medication in utero. Consider the risks and benefits of administering live vaccines to infants exposed to ustekinumab in utero. There are insufficient data regarding exposed infant serum drug concentrations at birth and the duration of persistence of ustekinumab in infant serum after birth. Although a specific time-frame to delay administration of live attenuated vaccines in infants exposed in utero is unknown, consider the risks and benefits of delaying a minimum of 6 months after birth because of the clearance of the product. Some experts recommend avoidance of live vaccines in exposed infants for at least the first 9 months of life or until drug concentrations become undetectable.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Available data from an observational pregnancy registry (101 participants, 107 pregnancies), published literature, and pharmacovigilance reports in pregnant individuals have not identified any association between ustekinumab exposure and increased risk of major birth defects, minor or major anomalies, miscarriage, or other adverse pregnancy outcomes. However, active inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) during pregnancy is linked to adverse outcomes such as preterm birth, low birth weight, and small for gestational age infants. Since IgG antibodies cross the placenta, with transport increasing throughout pregnancy and peaking in the third trimester, ustekinumab may be transferred to the fetus. In animal studies, administering ustekinumab to cynomolgus monkeys during organogenesis at doses more than 100 times the maximum recommended human dos (MRHD) showed no teratogenic or developmental toxicity.   In patients with IBD who are pregnant or attempting conception, guidelines conditionally recommend continuing ustekinumab maintenance therapy throughout pregnancy. The American College of Rheumatology conditionally recommends continuing treatment with ustekinumab while a person is attempting to conceive, but discontinuing once pregnancy is confirmed. Other rheumatologic guidelines recommend replacement of ustekinumab with alternative therapy prior to conception. When used for psoriasis, some experts recommend replacing ustekinumab with another medication before conception and during pregnancy until more data are available; use during pregnancy should be reserved for cases in which other treatments are ineffective. If ustekinumab is used during pregnancy, consider administering the last dose 6 to 10 weeks before the estimated due date to minimize placental transfer, which peaks during the third trimester.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Use ustekinumab with caution during breast-feeding. Limited data suggest that ustekinumab is present in human milk; however, drug concentrations are likely to be very low. The impact of local gastrointestinal exposure and limited systemic absorption of ustekinumab on a breastfed child is unknown. It is unlikely that ustekinumab is absorbed systemically to a large degree after ingestion. No adverse effects causally related to ustekinumab have been reported in the literature or postmarketing experience. The Global Consensus Group for Pregnancy and Inflammatory Bowel Disease considers ustekinumab use to be acceptable during breast-feeding. Consider the development and health benefits of breast-feeding along with the clinical need for ustekinumab and any potential adverse effects on the breastfed child from ustekinumab or from the underlying maternal condition.

Interactions
Abatacept: (Major) Concomitant use of abatacept with biological DMARDs, such as ustekinumab, is not recommended because of the possibility of additive immunosuppression and increased risk of infection. There is insufficient experience to assess the safety and efficacy of abatacept administered concurrently with ustekinumab.
Bacillus Calmette-Guerin Vaccine, BCG: (Contraindicated) If possible, administer all recommended vaccines before ustekinumab initiation. Ustekinumab recipients may receive inactive vaccines, but the elicited immune response may be insufficient to prevent disease. Do not administer live vaccines to a ustekinumab recipient. Furthermore, do not administer BCG live vaccines for either 1 year before or 1 year after ustekinumab receipt, due to the infectious risk for Mycobacteria. No data are available on the response to live vaccination or on the risk of infection or infection transmission after the administration of other live vaccines to ustekinumab recipients. Cautious administration of ustekinumab to household contacts of ustekinumab recipients may be warranted due to the potential risk for shedding from the household contact and transmission to the patient. Practitioners should also refer to the most recent CDC guidelines regarding vaccination of patients who are receiving drugs that adversely affect the immune system.
Baricitinib: (Major) Concomitant use of baricitinib with biologic DMARDs, such as ustekinumab, is not recommended because of the possibility of additive immunosuppression and increased infection risk. Baricitinib may be used as monotherapy or concomitantly with methotrexate or other nonbiologic DMARDs.

Description
  Text
  Ustekinumab is a monoclonal antibody that inhibits interleukin-12 and interleukin-23 by binding their shared p40 subunit, making it useful for several inflammatory conditions. Ustekinumab is indicated for the treatment of moderate to severe plaque psoriasis in people who are candidates for phototherapy or systemic therapy and active psoriatic arthritis (PsA). Use of ustekinumab in plaque psoriasis results in significant improvement in psoriatic skin lesions and a reduction of itching and inflammation. In people 6 years and older with PsA, ustekinumab improves clinical signs and symptoms, inhibits the radiographic progression of structural joint damage, and improves physical function. The ideal combination of therapy for individuals with plaque psoriasis or PsA is determined by treat-to-target strategies and severity of disease. The IL-inhibiting biologics may be considered for PsA when the individual has severe disease and either has contraindications or adverse reactions to TNF-blocking biologic agents or inadequate response. Ustekinumab is also approved for the treatment of moderate to severely active Crohn disease (CD) or ulcerative colitis (UC). Adult CD guidelines recommend ustekinumab as an advanced therapy for induction and maintenance of remission in moderately to severely active CD. It may be used as an initial advanced therapy or after failure or intolerance of other therapies, with treatment selection individualized according to prior treatment, disease characteristics, safety, access, and patient preference. The AGA CD guideline includes ustekinumab among higher-efficacy options for adults who have not previously received advanced CD therapy. Adult UC guidelines recommend ustekinumab as an advanced therapy option to induce remission and, in patients who respond, to maintain remission in moderately to severely active UC. Because ustekinumab modifies immune function, it may increase the risk of infection. Ustekinumab was FDA-approved in 2009.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Ustekinumab is a human IgG monoclonal antibody that binds with high affinity and specificity to the p40 protein subunit, which is part of both interleukin (IL)-12 and IL-23. Both IL-12 and IL-23 are naturally occurring cytokines that are involved in inflammatory and immune responses, such as natural killer cell activation and CD4+ T-cell differentiation and activation. During in vitro models, ustekinumab was shown to disrupt IL-12 and IL-23-mediated signaling and cytokine cascades by disrupting the interaction of these cytokines with a shared cell-surface receptor chain, IL-12R,beta-1. The cytokines IL-12 and IL-23 have also been implicated as important contributors to the chronic inflammation that is a hallmark of Crohn's disease and ulcerative colitis. In animal models of colitis, genetic absence or antibody blockade of IL-12 p40 and IL-23 p40 was shown to be protective. In a small exploratory study, a decrease was observed in the expression of mRNA of its molecular targets IL-12 and IL-23 in lesional skin biopsies in patients with psoriasis.
 
Normally, CD4+ T cells develop into T helper-1 (Th1) cells under the influence of IL-12. Th1 cells produce interferon gamma and mediate cellular immunity. In addition, IL-12 induces cutaneous lymphocyte antigen (CLA), resulting in T cell homing to the skin. Under the influence of IL-23, CD4+ T cells develop into IL-17 producing T cells (Th17). Th17 cells produce IL-17, IL-17F, IL-6, and TNF-alpha to mediate cellular immunity. Ustekinumab, by disrupting IL-12 and IL-23 signal transduction, suppresses the formation of the proinflammatory Th1 and Th17 cells. In vitro, ustekinumab inhibited IL-12 and IL-23-induced interferon-gamma, IL-17A, TNF-alpha, IL-2, and IL-10 secretion.
 
In rodent models, inhibition of IL-12/IL-23p40 increased the risk of malignancy. In mice deficient in IL-12 or in IL-12 and IL-23, ultraviolet-induced skin cancers developed earlier and more frequently; the relevance of these findings to humans is not known.

  Text
  Ustekinumab is administered subcutaneously and intravenously. Ustekinumab metabolism has not been characterized, but the drug is expected to be degraded into small peptides and amino acids via catabolic pathways in the same manner as endogenous IgG. The Vd of ustekinumab in the central compartment was 2.7 to 3 L in patients with Crohn disease or ulcerative colitis. The total Vd at steady-state was 4.4 to 4.6 L. Mean half-life ranged from 14.9 +/- 4.6 days to 45.6 +/- 80.2 days across all psoriasis studies following subcutaneous administration. Clearance was 0.19 L/day with an estimated median terminal half-life of approximately 19 days in patients with Crohn disease or ulcerative colitis.
 
Affected cytochrome P450 enzymes and drug transporters: Unknown
The effects of interleukins IL-12 or IL-23 on the regulation of CYP450 enzymes were evaluated in an in vitro study using human hepatocytes, which showed that IL-12 and/or IL-23 (at concentrations of 10 ng/mL) did not alter human CYP450 enzyme activities (CYP1A2, 2B6, 2C9, 2C19, 2D6, or 3A4). In another study, no clinically significant changes in the exposure of a CYP1A2 substrate (caffeine), CYP2C9 substrate (warfarin), CYP2C19 substrate (omeprazole), CYP2D6 substrate (dextromethorphan), or CYP3A substrate (midazolam) were observed when ustekinumab was administered concurrently at the recommended dosage in patients with Crohn disease. However, the formation of CYP450 enzymes can be suppressed by increased concentrations of certain cytokines (e.g., IL-1, IL-6, TNF-alpha, IFN) during chronic inflammation. Thus, ustekinumab, an antagonist of IL-12 and IL-23, could normalize the formation of CYP450 enzymes. Monitor the therapeutic effect or drug concentration of any concomitant CYP450 substrates, particularly those with a narrow therapeutic index, when ustekinumab is initiated or discontinued.
