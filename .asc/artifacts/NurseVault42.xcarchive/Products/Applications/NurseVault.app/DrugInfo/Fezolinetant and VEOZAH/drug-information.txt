Drug Class: Fezolinetant is an oral neurokinin 3 (NK3) receptor antagonist indicated for the treatment of moderate to severe vasomotor symptoms (VMS) due to menopause.

Routes: oral; Common adult doses: 45 mg/day PO.

In an Overdose: No overdose/antidote information is listed in this file.

Key Nursing Considerations: Contraindications/precautions: This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components. Fezolinetant is contraindicated in individuals with severe renal impairment (eGFR 15 to 29 mL/minute/1.73 m2) or end-stage renal failure (eGFR less than 15 mL/minute/1.73 m2).

    renal failure, renal impairment
    General Information
    Fezolinetant is contraindicated in individuals with severe renal impairment (eGFR 15 to 29 mL/minute/1.73 m2) or end-stage renal failure (eGFR less than 15 mL/minute/1.73 m2). No dosage adjustment is needed in those with mild or moderate renal impairment (eGFR of 30 mL/minute/1.73 m2 or more).
    Headline
    hepatic cirrhosis, hepatotoxicity
    General Information
    Fezolinetant is contraindicated in people with hepatic cirrhosis. Fezolinetant has not been studied in individuals with severe Child-Pugh Class C hepatic cirrhosis, but exposures of the drug were elevated in patients with Child-Pugh class A and B cirrhosis relative to subjects with normal hepatic function. Hepatotoxicity has occurred with the use of fezolinetant in the postmarketing setting. During clinical trials, treated patients with ALT or AST elevations were generally asymptomatic. In the postmarketing setting, cases of drug-induced liver injury with elevations of ALT, AST, alkaline phosphatase (ALP), and total bilirubin occurred within 40 days of starting fezolinetant. The patients' signs and symptoms gradually resolved after drug discontinuation. Transaminase levels returned to pretreatment levels (or close to these) without sequelae with dose continuation, and upon dose interruption, or discontinuation. During clinical trials, individuals with cirrhosis were not studied. Perform baseline liver function tests (LFTs) to evaluate for hepatic function and injury [including serum ALT, serum AST, serum ALP, and serum bilirubin (total and direct)] prior to fezolinetant initiation. Do not start fezolinetant if ALT or AST is 2-times or more above the upper limit of normal (ULN) or if the total bilirubin is 2-times or more above the ULN. During treatment, perform follow-up LFTs monthly for the first 3 months, at 6 months, and 9 months after initiation of therapy. Advise individuals to discontinue fezolinetant and seek medical attention if they have symptoms that suggest liver injury (e.g., new onset fatigue, decreased appetite, nausea, vomiting, pruritus, jaundice, pale feces, dark urine, or abdominal pain). Discontinue fezolinetant if transaminase elevations are greater than 5 times the ULN, or greater than 3 times the ULN with total bilirubin elevations greater than 2 times the ULN. Perform more frequent follow-ups of LFTs if transaminase elevations greater than 3 times the ULN occur and exclude any other causes that could explain elevations.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    There are no available data on the use of fezolinetant in human pregnancy to evaluate drug-associated risk of major birth defects, miscarriage, or adverse maternal or fetal outcomes. In embryo-fetal toxicity animal studies, embryo-lethality was seen at 128- and 174-fold the human AUC24 at the human therapeutic dose for rats and rabbits, respectively, but no teratogenicity was observed. No observed adverse effects were seen in embryo-fetal development of rats and rabbits at 62- and 16-fold the human AUC24 at the human therapeutic dose, respectively. Fezolinetant showed no effects on fertility and early embryonic development in rats. In the pre- and post-natal development animal study, delayed parturition and embryo-lethality occurred at high doses (100 mg/kg/day) above the human therapeutic dose in rats. In addition, in the male offspring delayed male reproductive maturation was observed, characterized by incomplete preputial separation, which affected male fertility at doses above the human therapeutic dose in rats.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Use fezolinetant with caution during breast-feeding. There are no data on the presence of fezolinetant or its metabolites in human milk, the effects on the breastfed infant, or the effects on milk production. Consider the benefits of breast-feeding, the risk of potential infant drug exposure, and the risk of an untreated or inadequately treated maternal condition.

    Headline
    elevated hepatic enzymes, hepatotoxicity
    General Information
    Hepatotoxicity has been reported with fezolinetant. In clinical trials, elevated serum alanine aminotransferase (ALT) and/or aspartate aminotransferase (AST) concentrations at 3 times the upper limit of normal occurred in 2.3% of women treated with fezolinetant, compared to 0.9% of placebo treated patients. No serum elevations were seen in total bilirubin. Patients with the elevated hepatic enzymes were generally asymptomatic; levels returned to at or near pretreatment levels without sequelae with dose continuation, and upon interruption, or discontinuation. Cases of hepatotoxicity with elevations of ALT, AST, alkaline phosphatase (ALP), and total bilirubin have been reported during postmarketing experience with fezolinetant. Patients reported a general sense of feeling unwell and symptoms of fatigue, nausea, pruritus, jaundice, pale feces, and dark urine within 40 days of starting fezolinetant. The patients' signs and symptoms gradually resolved after discontinuation of fezolinetant. Evaluate liver function tests for hepatic function and injury (including ALT, AST, ALP, and total and direct serum bilirubin) prior to fezolinetant initiation. Do not start fezolinetant if the concentration of ALT or AST is equal to or exceeds 2 times the ULN or if the total bilirubin is equal to or exceeds 2 times the ULN for the evaluating laboratory. Perform follow-up evaluations of hepatic transaminase concentration monthly for the first 3 months, at 6 months, and 9 months after initiation of therapy. Advise patients to discontinue fezolinetant and seek medical attention when symptoms (such as new onset fatigue, decreased appetite, nausea, vomiting, pruritus, jaundice, pale feces, dark urine, or abdominal pain) suggest liver injury. Discontinue fezolinetant if transaminase concentrations are greater than 5 times the ULN, or greater than 3 times the ULN with total bilirubin concentrations greater than 2 times the ULN. Perform more frequent follow-ups of hepatic laboratory tests if transaminase elevations greater than 3 times the ULN occur and exclude any other causes that could explain elevations.

Description
  Text
  Fezolinetant is an oral neurokinin 3 (NK3) receptor antagonist indicated for the treatment of moderate to severe vasomotor symptoms (VMS) due to menopause. VMS, during menopause, are predicted to result from increased kisspeptin/neurokinin B/dynorphin (KNDy) neuron activity from estrogen withdrawal. During 2 randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled clinical trials, subjects receiving fezolinetant 45 mg had statistically significant and clinically meaningful (2 or more hot flashes over 24 hours) reduction from baseline in the frequency of moderate to severe VMS compared to placebo at weeks 4 and 12. Statistically significant reductions from baseline in the severity of moderate to severe VMS were also seen in subjects receiving fezolinetant 45 mg at weeks 4 and 12 compared to placebo. Abdominal pain, diarrhea, insomnia, back pain, and increased hepatic transaminase elevations were reported with fezolinetant use. Fezolinetant contains a black box warning for hepatotoxicity and is contraindicated in patients with cirrhosis, severe renal impairment, end stage renal disease, and in patients taking CYP1A2 inhibitors.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Fezolinetant is a neurokinin 3 (NK3) receptor antagonist. It works by blocking neurokinin B (NKB) binding on the kisspeptin/neurokinin B /dynorphin (KNDy) neuron to modulate neuronal activity in the thermoregulatory center of the body. Fezolinetant has high affinity for the NK3 receptor (Ki value of 19.9 to 22.1 nmol/L), which is more than 450-fold higher than binding affinity to NK1 or NK2 receptors. Estrogen withdrawal in postmenopausal women can cause increased KNDy neuron expression and lead to increased thermoregulatory activity by the hypothalamus. Thermoregulatory activity can result in vasomotor symptoms such as hot flashes and night sweats. Antagonistic action to the KNDy signal pathway can potentially reduce the occurrence of vasomotor symptoms in postmenopausal women.

  Fezolinetant is administered orally. Fezolinetant is 51% bound to plasma protein. The mean apparent volume of distribution of fezolinetant is 189 L. Fezolinetant is primarily metabolized by CYP1A2 and minor metabolism occurs by CYP2C9 and CYP2C19. A major metabolite of fezolinetant was found in plasma; it is 20 times less potent than the parent drug with a metabolite -to-parent ratio ranging from 0.7 to 1.8. After oral administration of fezolinetant, 76.9% of the dose was excreted in urine (1.1% unchanged) and 14.7% in feces (0.1% unchanged). The apparent clearance at steady-state of fezolinetant is 10.8 L/hour. The effective half-life of fezolinetant is 9.6 hours.
Affected cytochrome P450 (CYP450) isoenzymes and drug transporters: CYP1A2, CYP2C9, CYP2C19
Fezolinetant is primarily metabolized by CYP1A2, with minor metabolism by CYP2C9 and CYP2C19. Concomitant use of fezolinetant with drugs that are weak, moderate, or strong CYP1A2 inhibitors, increase the plasma Cmax and AUC of fezolinetant. Fezolinetant is contraindicated in individuals using CYP1A2 inhibitors. In vitro, fezolinetant and its major metabolite are not inhibitors of CYP1A2, CYP2B6, CYP2C8, CYP2C9, CYP2C19, CYP2D6, and CYP3A4. Fezolinetant and its major metabolite are not inducers of CYP1A2, CYP2B6, and CYP3A4. Fezolinetant is not a substrate nor an inhibitor of P-glycoprotein (P-gp). The major metabolite is a substrate of P-gp, but not an inhibitor of P-gp. Both fezolinetant and its major metabolite are not a substrate of BCRP, OATP1B1, and OATP1B3. In addition, the major metabolite is not a substrate of OAT1, OAT3, OCT2, MATE1, and MATE2-K.
