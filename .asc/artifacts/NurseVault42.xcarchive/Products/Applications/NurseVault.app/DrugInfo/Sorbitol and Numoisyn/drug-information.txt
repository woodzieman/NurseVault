Drug Class: Sorbitol (chemical designation D-glucitol) is a sugar alcohol found naturally in fruits.

Routes: topical, oral; Common adult doses: 16 lozenges/24 hours PO for xerostomia; 45 ml/day PO and 120 ml/day PR for constipation.

Common Side Effects (excluding routine GI upset): gi bleeding, pruritus ani, angina, edema, hypotension, pulmonary edema, sinus tachycardia, dehydration, diuresis, metabolic acidosis, urinary retention, back pain, chills, rhinitis, urticaria, vertigo

In an Overdose: It can be used with activated charcoal in some oral overdose or poison ingestions to facilitate the charcoal-toxin complex through the GI tract.

Key Nursing Considerations: Contraindications/precautions: This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components. Sorbitol urologic irrigation solution is contraindicated in individuals with anuria and should be used with caution in individuals with severe renal impairment or renal failure.

    Headline
    anuria, renal failure, renal impairment
    General Information
    Sorbitol urologic irrigation solution is contraindicated in individuals with anuria and should be used with caution in individuals with severe renal impairment or renal failure. Urologic irrigation fluids have been demonstrated to enter the systemic circulation in relatively large volumes; absorption of large volumes can result in osmotic diuresis that may significantly affect renal dynamics. Use with caution in elderly individuals as they are more susceptible to age-related renal disease. 44681
    Headline
    hereditary fructose intolerance
    General Information
    Do not use sorbitol in individuals with documented or suspected hereditary fructose intolerance; life-threatening reactions have been reported in individuals with fructose intolerance.
    Headline
    cardiac disease, pulmonary disease
    General Information
    Use sorbitol urologic irrigation solution cautiously in individuals with cardiopulmonary dysfunction (i.e., cardiac disease, pulmonary disease). Urologic irrigation fluids have been demonstrated to enter the systemic circulation in relatively large volumes; absorption of large volumes can alter cardiopulmonary function and significant expansion of intravascular fluid can lead to heart failure. Because fluid overload may occur, appropriate patient monitoring should be maintained. Additionally, electrolyte monitoring should be continued beyond the acute phase due to delayed fluid absorption.
    Headline
    diabetes mellitus
    General Information
    Use sorbitol urologic irrigation solution cautiously in individuals with diabetes mellitus. It is a sugar alcohol and once metabolized, hyperglycemia may occur in individuals with diabetes. Sorbitol should not be used in individuals unable to metabolize sorbitol rapidly enough to avoid the development of hyperosmolar states.
    Headline
    hyponatremia, hypovolemia
    General Information
    Use sorbitol urologic irrigation solution cautiously in individuals with dehydration, hyponatremia, or hypovolemia. These individuals should be closely monitored as these conditions could worsen with systemic absorption of the solution and potential subsequent osmotic diuresis. Monitor for fluid overload, which requires intensive fluid and electrolyte management; delayed fluid absorption may occur necessitating monitoring beyond the acute phase.
    Headline
    geriatric
    General Information
    Geriatric adults are more susceptible to age-related declines in cardiac or renal function, which may lead to an increased risk of adverse effects in the use of sorbitol urologic irrigations. Dose selection should be cautious in elderly individuals.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Sorbitol use has not been systematically studied in well-controlled trials during human pregnancy. Use therapeutic pharmaceutical oral products (e.g., sorbitol 70% w/w oral solutions marketed as laxatives) with caution, as safer alternatives may be available. Overuse or excessive dosage of sorbitol oral solution may cause dehydration or other metabolic disturbances in the pregnant individual, which may affect the fetus. Pregnant individuals should obtain expert advice and approval before use of these dosage forms. When systemically absorbed, sorbitol will be either metabolized to carbon dioxide and dextrose or excreted by a normally functioning kidney. For occasional constipation during pregnancy not responding to diet and fluid intake, bulk/fiber laxatives, or stool softeners, guidelines favor osmotic laxatives with more established safety data, such as polyethylene glycol or magnesium hydroxide, over sorbitol. It is not known if sorbitol bladder irrigation solution can cause fetal harm when administered during pregnancy, and it should be used during pregnancy only if clearly needed. Sorbitol is a commonly used sugar alcohol found as a sweetener in food and oral drug products such as throat lozenges and some saliva substitutes; use of these types of products containing sorbitol is generally recognized as safe and is thought to have low risk for fetal harm.
    Headline
    abdominal pain, nausea, vomiting
    General Information
    Do not use sorbitol solution rectally when nausea, vomiting, or abdominal pain are present.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Sorbitol oral solution is compatible with breast-feeding. Use sorbitol bladder irrigation with caution in breast-feeding patients. It is not known if sorbitol is excreted in human milk. If absorbed either intravascularly or extravascularly during irrigant use, sorbitol will be either metabolized to carbon dioxide and dextrose or excreted by a normally functioning kidney.

Description
  Text
  Sorbitol (chemical designation D-glucitol) is a sugar alcohol found naturally in fruits. It is used as a low-calorie sweetener in some sugarless candies and gum and has been studied in the prevention of dental caries. Similar to some other sugar alcohols, the FDA has approved the label 'does not promote tooth decay' for sugar-free foods that contain sorbitol. Sorbitol  is used as a humectant in some saliva substitutes, as a genitourinary irrigant during transurethral surgical procedures, and as a laxative. When used for constipation its hyperosmotic laxative effects are equivalent to lactulose, and it is a less expensive alternative; however, it is usually not first line treatment for constipation due to abdominal pain, cramping, and diarrhea. Use in combination with sodium polystyrene sulfonate for hyperkalemia is not recommended due to reports of bowel necrosis. It can be used with activated charcoal in some oral overdose or poison ingestions to facilitate the charcoal-toxin complex through the GI tract. Sorbitol has been recognized by the FDA as GRAS (Generally Recognized As Safe). It was approved by the FDA as a topical bladder irrigant in May 1980. Numoisyn products (lozenges and oral solution) were approved by the FDA in 2006.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Sorbitol, an isomer of mannitol, is a sugar alcohol found naturally in fruits and produced commercially from the reduction of glucose.
 
Laxative activity: osmotic laxative that works by drawing water into the large intestine which produces a bowel movement.
Saliva substitute: moisturizes the oral mucosa and pharynx and mimics natural saliva. Sorbitol can help relieve symptoms of dry mouth by enhancing swallowing, improving speech mechanics, and lubricating the oral cavity. It acts to replace moisture and lubricate the mouth, it does not stimulate salivary gland production.
Urologic irrigant activity: acts as a lavage for the urinary bladder and prostate bed removing blood and tissue fragments, including during transurethral surgical procedures. Lavage helps prevent infection and increase visibility for procedures. Maintains the patency of an indwelling catheter in the immediate postoperative period.
