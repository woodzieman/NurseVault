          Dosage Description
          7.1 mg/kg IV every 4 weeks; discontinue treatment if the patient does not experience a decrease in RBC transfusion burden after 6 doses (24 weeks) or if unacceptable toxicity occurs at any time. Premedicate at least 30 minutes prior to administration with diphenhydramine (25 mg to 50 mg IV or PO, or equivalent) and hydrocortisone (100 mg to 200 mg IV or PO, or equivalent); monitor patients for at least 1 hour after completion of the infusion. In a multicenter, randomized, double-blind clinical trial (IMerge), treatment with imetelstat significantly improved the rate of transfusion independence for 8 weeks or more (39.8% vs. 15%) and 24 weeks or more (28% vs. 3.3%) compared with placebo in patients with transfusion-dependent IPSS low- or intermediate-1 risk MDS who had failed to respond to, lost response to, or were ineligible for ESAs; patients were ineligible if they had del(5q) or if they had previously received treatment with lenalidomide or hypomethylating agents. Baseline serum erythropoietin levels were less than or equal to 500 microunits/mL in 73.7% of patients in the imetelstat arm and in 60% of those in the placebo arm.

    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Pregnancy should be avoided by females of reproductive potential during imetelstat treatment and for 1 week after the last dose. Although there are no adequately controlled studies in pregnant women, imetelstat can cause fetal harm or death when administered during pregnancy based on its mechanism of action and animal studies. Administration of imetelstat to pregnant mice and rabbits during organogenesis resulted in embryofetal mortality at maternal exposures of 2.5 times and 9.3 times the human exposure at the recommended dose, respectively. Women who are pregnant or who become pregnant while receiving imetelstat should be apprised of the potential hazard to the fetus.
    Headline
    contraception requirements, infertility, pregnancy testing, reproductive risk
    General Information
    Counsel patients about the reproductive risk and contraception requirements during imetelstat treatment. Imetelstat can be teratogenic if taken by the mother during pregnancy. Females of reproductive potential should avoid pregnancy and use effective contraception during and for 1 week after treatment with imetelstat. Females of reproductive potential should undergo pregnancy testing prior to initiation of imetelstat. Women who become pregnant while receiving imetelstat should be apprised of the potential hazard to the fetus. Although there are no data regarding the effect of imetelstat on human fertility, reversible female infertility has been observed in animal studies.

    General Information
    Due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in nursing infants from imetelstat, advise women to discontinue breast-feeding during treatment and for 1 week after the final dose. It is not known whether imetelstat is present in human milk, although many drugs are excreted in human milk.
    Headline

    General Information

Elevated liver function tests (LFTs) can occur with imetelstat therapy; an interruption, dose reduction, or discontinuation of therapy may be necessary based on incidence and severity. Monitor LFTs at baseline, weekly for the first cycle, prior to each cycle thereafter, and as clinically indicated.

Adverse Reactions

  Adverse Reactions
    Headline
    thrombocytopenia
    General Information
    Treatment with imetelstat can cause thrombocytopenia, with 97% of patients with myelodysplastic syndrome (MDS) treated with imetelstat in a randomized clinical trial reporting a decrease in platelet counts from baseline compared with 34% of those who received placebo (grade 3 or 4, 65% vs. 8%). The median time to onset of first occurrence of grade 3 or 4 decreased platelets was 6 weeks and the median time to recovery to grade 2 or lower for each occurrence was 1.3 weeks. Grade 3 or 4 decreased platelets occurred in 48% of patients during cycles 1 to 3, 31% during cycles 4 to 6, 33% during cycles 7 to 12, and 24% during cycles 13 and beyond. Higher imetelstat exposure is associated with a higher incidence of grade 3 and 4 thrombocytopenia in patients with MDS. Monitor complete blood counts at baseline, weekly for the first 2 cycles, prior to each cycle thereafter, and as clinically indicated. Administer platelet transfusions as appropriate. Monitor patients with thrombocytopenia for bleeding. An interruption of therapy, dose reduction, or discontinuation of therapy is necessary for a platelet count less than 50,000 cells/mm3.

Description
  Text
  Imetelstat is an oligonucleotide telomerase inhibitor indicated for the treatment of adult patients with low- to intermediate-1 risk myelodysplastic syndromes (MDS) with transfusion-dependent anemia requiring 4 or more red blood cell units over 8 weeks who have not responded to or have lost response to or are ineligible for erythropoiesis-stimulating agents (ESAs). Infusion-related reactions may occur; premedicate with the required medications at least 30 minutes prior to dosing and monitor patients for at least an hour after completion of the infusion.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Imetelstat is an oligonucleotide telomerase inhibitor. In normal cells, telomeres gradually get shorter with each DNA replication, eventually leading to cell senescence; a lack of cell senescence leads to genomic instability and tumor development. Telomerase is a reverse transcriptase that binds to telomeres and leads to telomere elongation, prolonging cell life. Telomerase is present in 85% to 95% of cancers but is not detected in normal tissues outside of embryonic cells and stem cells that quickly divide. There are 2 telomerase subunits: human telomerase RNA (TR) and human telomerase reverse transcriptase (TERT). Increased telomerase activity and TERT RNA expression have been reported in myelodysplastic syndrome and malignant stem cells and progenitor cells. Imetelstat binds to the template region of the RNA component of TR, preventing the telomere from binding to the telomerase subunit and inhibiting telomerase activity. It is active in the G2/M part of the cell cycle.
Nonclinical studies showed imetelstat treatment led to reduction of telomere length, reduction of malignant stem and progenitor cell proliferation, and induction of apoptotic cell death.

Pharmacodynamics
Higher imetelstat exposure is associated with a higher incidence of grade 3 and 4 thrombocytopenia in patients with myelodysplastic syndrome.
  Text
  Imetelstat is administered intravenously. In vitro, imetelstat is greater than 94% bound to human plasma proteins. The geometric mean volume of distribution is approximately 14.1 liters (CV, 27.2%) after a single 7.1-mg/kg IV dose administered over 2 hours in patients with myelodysplastic syndrome. The geometric mean apparent plasma half-life is approximately 4.9 hours (CV, 43.2%) at the recommended dosage.
 
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes and drug transporters: None
Imetelstat is expected to be metabolized by nucleases to nucleotides of various lengths. In vitro, imetelstat is not an inhibitor or inducer of CYP450 isoenzymes; it is an inhibitor of OATP1B1 and OATP1B3.

Dilution
Withdraw a volume of 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection equal to the required volume of imetelstat from a 500 mL bag of 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection.
Add the appropriate volume of imetelstat to the 500 mL bag of 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection. Discard any unused portion of the reconstituted vial.
Gently invert the infusion bag at least 5 times to mix; do not shake.
Storage after dilution: After dilution, imetelstat may be stored at room temperature (20 to 25 degrees C or 68 to 77 degrees F) for up to 18 hours or under refrigeration (2 to 8 degrees C or 36 to 46 degrees F) for up to 48 hours including the time of reconstitution and completion of the infusion.
