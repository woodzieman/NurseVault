    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    There are no available data on brensocatib use during pregnancy to assess the risk of major birth defects, miscarriage, or other adverse maternal or fetal outcomes. In animal studies, high doses of brensocatib at 128 times the maximum recommended human dose (MRHD) on AUC basis were associated with fetal malformation, including bent scapula, in pregnant rats. No fetal malformations were noted in pregnant rabbits or pregnant rats at 20 and 17 times the MRHD on an AUC basis, respectively. Maternal toxicity, as evidenced by reductions in body weight gain and food consumption, was noted at doses 5 times the MRHD on an AUC basis.

Description
  Text
  Brensocatib is a dipeptidyl peptidase 1 (DPP1) inhibitor indicated for the treatment of non-cystic fibrosis bronchiectasis. During clinical trials, both the 10 mg and 25 mg doses of brensocatib demonstrated a reduction in the annual rate of pulmonary exacerbations (PEx) compared with placebo. Both doses also showed an increase in time to first PEx (49 to 50.71 weeks) compared to placebo (36.71 weeks). Additionally, the proportion of patients who remained PEx free at week 52 was higher with brensocatib (48.5% for 10 mg and 25 mg) compared to placebo (40.3%) with an odds ratio of 1.4 favoring brensocatib. The most common side effects are upper respiratory treat infection, headache, dry skin, rash, and gingival and periodontal effects. Brensocatib was FDA-approved in August 2025.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Brensocatib is a competitive, reversible inhibitor of dipeptidyl peptidase 1 (DPP1). During neutrophil maturation in the bone marrow, DPP1 activates pro-inflammatory neutrophil serine proteases (NSPs), including neutrophil elastase, cathepsin G, and proteinase 3. These activated NSPs play a key role in the inflammation associated with neutrophil-mediated non-cystic fibrosis bronchiectasis (NCFB). In cell-based assays, DDP1 inhibition by brensocatib reduces the activity of NSPs, thereby potentially mitigating inflammation and tissue damage related to excessive neutrophil activity.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Oral Route
    Description
    Based on data from a mass balance study in healthy subjects, the oral absorption of brensocatib in humans is greater than 80%. Following a single dose of 10 mg or 25 mg brensocatib, the median Tmax (min, max) was 1 to 1.4 hours (0.5, 3 hours). Following administration of brensocatib 10 mg, the estimated geometric mean (CV%) Cmax was 85.4 nanograms/mL (33%) and AUC was 1,360 nanograms x hour/mL (41%). Following once daily administration of brensocatib 25 mg, the estimated geometric mean Cmax was 259 nanograms/mL (31%) and AUC was 4,060 nanograms x hour/mL (39%). Brensocatib Cmax and AUC increase in a greater than dose proportional manner between doses of 10 mg and 1.6 times the highest recommended dose. Between doses of 10 mg and 1.6 times the highest recommended dose, brensocatib once daily, the geometric mean steady state Cmax increased by 5.1-fold and the AUC increased by 5.3-fold. In healthy subjects, at steady state, Cmax increased by about 1.5-fold and AUC increased by about 2-fold when compared to those observed following a single dose. No clinically relevant differences in brensocatib pharmacokinetics were observed following administration of brensocatib with a high-fat meal (990 calories, 52% fat).

  Text
  Brensocatib is administered orally. The protein binding of brensocatib in human plasma is 87.2%. Following once daily doses of 10 mg or 25 mg bresocatib in individuals with non-cystic fibrosis bronchiectasis (NCFB), its Vd was 126 to 138 L. Metabolism occurs primarily via CYP3A enzymes, and to a lesser extent by CYP2C8 and CYP2D6. In plasma, brensocatib accounted for 16.2% of the total radioactivity after radiolabeled dosing. The major metabolite, thiocyanate, accounted for 51% of total plasma radioactivity AUC. After administration of a single oral dose of radiolabeled brensocatib (1.6 times the highest recommended dose) in healthy subjects, 54.2% of the dose was recovered in the urine (22.8% as unchanged brensocatib) and 28.3% in feces (2.4% as unchanged brensocatib). Following administration of 10 mg or 25 mg brensocatib in healthy individuals, the elimination half-life ranged from 25 to 39 hours and the apparent oral clearance was between 6.4 to 10.7 L/hour.
 
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes and drug transporters: CYP3A, CYP2C8, CYP2D6
In vitro studies show bensocatib is a substrate of CYP3A. Bensocatib does not inhibit CYP1A2, CYP2B6, CYP2C8, CYP2C9, CYP2C19, CYP2D6 or CYP3A. Brensocatib is a weak CYP3A inducer, but not an inducer of CYP1A2, CYP2B6, CYP2C8, CYP2C9, or CYP2C19.
In vitro studies show brensocatib is a substrate of P-gp and BCRP, but is not a substrate of MATE1, MATE2-K, OAT1, OAT3, OATP1B1, OATP1B3, OCT1, and OCT2. Brensocatib is an inhibitor of BCRP, OATP1B, MATE1, and MATE2-K, but is not an inhibitor of P-gp, OAT1, OCT2, OAT3, or OATP1B3.
