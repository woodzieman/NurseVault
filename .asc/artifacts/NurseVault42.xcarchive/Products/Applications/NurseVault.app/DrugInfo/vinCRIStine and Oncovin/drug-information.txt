Drug Class: Vincristine is a vinca alkaloid agent.

Routes: injection (IV/IM/SC); Common adult doses: 1.4 mg/m2 per week; due to the risk of vincristine-induced peripheral neuropathy, weekly doses were capped at 2 mg in some clinical studies.

Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  General Information
  Vincristine is an extremely toxic drug with a low therapeutic index, and therapeutic doses are likely to cause toxicity. Therefore, this drug should be used only by clinicians experienced in administering chemotherapy.
 
Vincristine can cause fetal harm and/or death and should not be administered during pregnancy. Women receiving vincristine should be advised to avoid becoming pregnant. Vincristine is classified as FDA pregnancy category D. It is unknown whether vincristine is excreted in breast milk. Breast-feeding should be discontinued during vincristine therapy because of the possibility of severe adverse reactions to the infant.
 
Vincristine itself can cause a variety of neurologic problems and can worsen any underlying neurological disease. A detailed description of antecedent neurologic problems can aid in monitoring toxicity from vincristine. Patients with hereditary motor and sensory neuropathy type I, childhood poliomyelitis, and Charcot-Marie-Tooth syndrome should not be given vincristine. Elderly patients are more susceptible to the neuropathic toxicities of vincristine.
 
Because vincristine is metabolized by the liver and excreted through the bile, dosages should be reduced in the presence of hepatic disease, obstructive jaundice or biliary tract disease. These patients may be at increased risk of vincristine-induced toxicity. Increases in alkaline phosphatase have been associated with increased neurotoxicity. Because vincristine can cause an ileus, a preexisting adynamic ileus is a direct contraindication. Patients receiving vincristine should receive a prophylactic bowel regimen.
 
Bone marrow suppression rarely occurs with vincristine. Leukopenia is minimal and the nadir usually occurs within 4 days. Vincristine should be not be given to patients receiving radiation therapy through ports that include the liver. Patients with an active infection should be treated prior to receiving vincristine. Patients with a history of varicella zoster, other herpes infection (e.g., herpes simplex), or other viral infection are at risk for reactivation of the infection when treated with chemotherapy.
 
Hyperkalemia, hyperphosphatemia, hyperuricemia, hypocalcemia, and decreased urine output may be indicative of busulfan-induced tumor lysis syndrome (TLS). Appropriate measures (e.g. aggressive hydration and allopurinol) must be taken to prevent severe electrolyte imbalances and renal toxicity during and following chemotherapy administration in patients with large chemosensitive tumors. Generally, this is not a problem in patients with breast cancer, but clinicians should closely monitor susceptible patients (i.e., those with leukemia, lymphoma, or extensive small cell lung cancer).
 
Use care to avoid accidental exposure to vincristine during preparation, handling and administration. Use of protective gowns, goggles, and gloves is recommended. Avoid ocular exposure of vincristine solutions. If exposure occurs, the eye should be rinsed immediately and thoroughly. Ocular exposure may result in irritation or corneal ulceration. Caution is recommended in the handling and administration of vincristine solution to avoid skin. If exposed to vincristine, skin should be thoroughly rinsed.
 
Vincristine is considered a vesicant. The intravenous needle or catheter must be properly placed prior to vincristine administration. Intramuscular administration and subcutaneous administration of vincristine are contraindicated due to severe skin and tissue necrosis that may occur following subcutaneous or intramuscular injections. Extravasation of vincristine infusions should be avoided. Patients should be closely monitored for signs and symptoms of extravasation including pain, swelling and poor blood return. In case of extravasation, local injection of hyaluronidase (see hyaluronidase monograph, Dosage section) and the application of moderate heat to the area of extravasation help disperse the drug and are thought to minimize discomfort and the possibility of cellulitis.
 
Intrathecal administration of vincristine is universally fatal, and this route must not be used. Syringes containing vincristine should be labeled using auxiliary labels: 'FATAL IF GIVEN INTRATHECALLY. FOR IV USE ONLY.' Extemporaneously prepared syringes must be packaged in an overwrap that is labeled 'DO NOT REMOVE COVERING UNTIL MOMENT OF INJECTION. FATAL IF GIVEN INTRATHECALLY. FOR IV USE ONLY.'
 
Vaccination during chemotherapy or radiation therapy should be avoided because the antibody response is suboptimal. When chemotherapy is being planned, vaccination should precede the initiation of chemotherapy by >= 2 weeks. The administration of live vaccines to immunocompromised patients should be avoided. Those undergoing chemotherapy should not be exposed to others who have recently received the oral poliovirus vaccine (OPV). Measles-mumps-rubella (MMR) vaccination is not contraindicated for the close contacts, including health care professionals, of immunocompromised patients. Passive immunoprophylaxis with immune globulins may be indicated for immunocompromised persons instead of, or in addition to, vaccination. When exposed to a vaccine-preventable disease such as measles, severely immunocompromised children should be considered susceptible regardless of their vaccination history.

    demyelinating Charcot-Marie-Tooth, neuromuscular disease
    General Information
    Vincristine is contraindicated in patients with demyelinating Charcot-Marie-Tooth syndrome. Neurotoxicity is a dose-limiting toxicity of vincristine therapy; carefully monitor patients for the development of neurologic toxicity. Use vincristine with caution in patients with pre-existing neuromuscular disease and when given with other neurotoxic drugs; these patients may be at increased risk for developing neurotoxicity.
    Headline
    hepatic failure
    General Information
    Use vincristine with caution in patients with hepatic impairment, including hepatic failure. A dose reduction is recommended in patients with a direct serum bilirubin value above 3 mg/100 mL.
    Headline
    serious adverse effects
    General Information
    Vincristine may cause serious adverse effects including extravasation during administration. Vincristine is for intravenous use only; fatal events have occurred if given by other routes of administration.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Vincristine use is contraindicated during pregnancy. There are no adequate and well-controlled studies on vincristine use during human pregnancy. Animal studies indicate that vincristine is embryotoxic and teratogenic at doses that are not toxic to the pregnant animal. In mice and hamsters, vincristine caused resorption in 23% to 85% of fetuses. Fetal malformations were seen in those that survived. In monkeys exposed to vincristine during early pregnancy, 2 of 5 viable fetuses had gross malformations at birth. The American Society of Clinical Oncology provides additional information on the use of certain medications for the management of cancer during pregnancy.
    Headline
    pulmonary disease
    General Information
    Use vincristine with caution in patients with pulmonary disease or dysfunction. Acute shortness of breath and severe bronchospasm have been reported following the administration of vinca alkaloids including vincristine. These reactions occurred more often when the vinca alkaloid was used in combination with mitomycin and particularly in patients with pre-existing pulmonary dysfunction. Discontinue vincristine in patients who develop progressive dyspnea requiring chronic therapy.

    Counsel people who may become pregnant and people who can cause pregnancy in others about the reproductive risk associated with vincristine. People of reproductive potential should avoid becoming pregnant. Additionally, vincristine may cause male and femal infertility.
    Headline

    Headline

    Advise patients to avoid breast-feeding while receiving vincristine. There are no data on the presence of vincristine in human milk, its effects on the breastfed child, or its effects on milk production. Consider the benefits of breast-feeding, the patient's clinical need for treatment, and any potential adverse effects on the breastfed child from the medication or from the patient's underlying medical condition.

Interactions
Abrocitinib: (Moderate) Monitor for vincristine-related adverse reactions if coadministration of abrocitinib is necessary as concurrent use may increase vincristine exposure. Vincristine is a P-gp substrate and abrocitinib is a P-gp inhibitor.
Adagrasib: (Major) Avoid coadministration of vincristine with adagrasib due to increased plasma concentrations of vincristine, resulting in an earlier onset and/or increased severity of neuromuscular side effects. Vincristine is a CYP3A and P-gp substrate and adagrasib is a strong CYP3A and P-gp inhibitor.
Amiodarone: (Moderate) Monitor for vincristine-related adverse reactions if coadministration of amiodarone is necessary as concurrent use may increase vincristine exposure. Vincristine is a P-gp substrate and amiodarone is a P-gp inhibitor.
Amobarbital: (Major) Vincristine is a substrate for cytochrome P450 (CYP) 3A4. Agents that induce CYP 3A4 may increase the metabolism of vincristine and decrease the efficacy of drug, including barbiturates. Patients receiving these drugs concurrently should be monitored for possible loss of vincristine efficacy.
Amoxicillin; Clarithromycin; Omeprazole: (Major) Increased concentrations of vincristine are likely. Consider if alternative antibiotic therapy is appropriate. Monitor for vincristine-related side effects, including neurotoxicity, if these drugs must be used together. Clarithromycin is a potent inhibitor of CYP3A4 and also inhibits P-gp. Vincristine is a CYP3A4 and P-gp substrate. Postmarketing reports of interactions, including serious toxicity, between clarithromycin or similar macrolides and vinca alkaloids have been noted.
Apalutamide: (Major) Avoid coadministration of vincristine with apalutamide due to decreased plasma concentrations of vincristine. Vincristine is a CYP3A4 substrate and apalutamide is a strong CYP3A4 inducer.
Aprepitant, Fosaprepitant: (Moderate) Aprepitant, fosaprepitant is indicated for the prophylaxis of chemotherapy-induced nausea/vomiting and may be used in combination with vincristine. However, use caution and monitor for a possible increase in non-emetogenic vincristine-related adverse effects for several days after administration of a multi-day aprepitant regimen. Vincristine is a CYP3A4 substrate. Aprepitant, when administered as a 3-day oral regimen (125 mg/80 mg/80 mg), is a moderate CYP3A4 inhibitor and inducer and theoretically could increase plasma concentrations of vincristine. For example, a 5-day oral aprepitant regimen increased the AUC of another CYP3A4 substrate, midazolam (single dose), by 2.3-fold on day 1 and by 3.3-fold on day 5. After a 3-day oral aprepitant regimen, the AUC of midazolam (given on days 1, 4, 8, and 15) increased by 25% on day 4, and then decreased by 19% and 4% on days 8 and 15, respectively. As a single 125 mg or 40 mg oral dose, the inhibitory effect of aprepitant on CYP3A4 is weak, with the AUC of midazolam increased by 1.5-fold and 1.2-fold, respectively. Adverse Reactions
  Headline
  Adverse Reactions
  General Information
  Neurotoxicity is the dose-limiting adverse effect of vincristine therapy. Initially, vincristine-induced neurotoxicity presents as sensory impairment and paresthesias and then progresses to neuropathic pain and decreased motor skills. Clinical manifestations include peripheral neuropathy, peripheral paresthesias, neuritis, numbness, hyporeflexia, neuropathic pain, and tingling. The peripheral neuropathy is usually a bilateral, sensory neuropathy that occurs in a "stocking-and-glove" pattern. Prolonged or high-dose therapy can cause cramps, loss of deep tendon reflexes, ataxia, cranial nerve palsy, atrophy, slapping gait, wrist drop, foot drop, limb, back or bone pain, difficulty in walking or paralysis. Clinical signs of cranial nerve palsies, which may affect any of the cranial nerves, include hoarseness, diplopia, facial palsy, trigeminal neuralgia, and jaw, pharyngeal and parotid pains. Cranial nerves responsible for extraocular and laryngeal muscles are most commonly affected. Life-threatening vocal cord paralysis has been reported. Extraocular neuropathy, optic atrophy with blindness, ptosis, diplopia, blurred vision, night blindness, and transient cortical blindness also have been reported. Vestibular and auditory damage to the eighth cranial nerve has been reported rarely with vincristine therapy. Symptoms include partial or total hearing loss, which may be permanent, dizziness, vertigo, and nystagmus. Central nervous system toxicity has been reported with vincristine despite the fact it does not extensively cross the blood brain barrier. CNS toxicity includes depression, confusion, agitation, hallucinations, seizures and coma. Seizures may be accompanied by hypertension. Seizures followed by coma have been reported in children. Neurotoxicity may occur following cumulative doses as low as 5—6 mg and is severe following cumulative doses >15 mg. Children are less susceptible to the neurotoxic effects of vincristine while elderly patients are at increased risk. Leucovorin has been used in animal models and in some cases of vincristine overdose in humans to protect against otherwise lethal doses of vincristine. Although in a clinical trial, leucovorin offered no additional protection against vincristine-induce neurotoxicity. Administration of glutamic acid 500 mg PO three times a day with vincristine has been associated a decreased incidence of vincristine-induced neurotoxicity. Glutamic acid did not affect the incidence of gastrointestinal or hematologic side effects. Most clinicians reduce the dosage or discontinue vincristine if paresthesias, depression of reflexes (hyporeflexia), and/or motor weakness develops. Vincristine-induced neurotoxicity may slowly reverse following discontinuance of the drug but can last up to 2 months after stopping vincristine. More severe cases of vincristine-induced neurotoxicity may not fully resolve.Gastrointestinal toxicity due to autonomic dysfunction can develop during vincristine therapy and is manifested as severe constipation (30%) and abdominal pain or cramps. Impaction of stool in the upper colon may occur due to constipation. Patients receiving vincristine should be maintained on a prophylactic bowel regimen including laxatives and stool softeners. Nausea/vomiting associated with vincristine administration is usually mild and may be treated as needed with antiemetics. Adynamic ileus (5%), which may mimic a "surgical abdomen", occurs frequently in children receiving vincristine. This usually resolves following discontinuation of vincristine. Metoclopramide (10—20 mg IV every 4—6 hours) has been reported to reverse the symptoms of adynamic ileus in these patients. Diarrhea, anorexia, abdominal distension, stomatitis, and oral ulceration can occur in patients receiving vincristine. Intestinal necrosis and/or perforation occur rarely and can be life-threatening. Patients with hepatic disease and those receiving high doses of vincristine are at increased risk for these adverse effects.Alopecia and rash (unspecified) occur in approximately 20% of patients during vincristine therapy and is reversible upon discontinuance of the drug. Vincristine can cause general symptomatic complaints such as fatigue, fever and anorexia in children. Fevers of 38—39 degrees C may occur 6—24 hours after administration of vincristine. These symptoms may continue for up to 2—3 days or longer in patients not receiving corticosteroids.Although causality has not been established, vincristine (in combination with other chemotherapeutic agents) has been reported to cause myocardial infarction and coronary artery disease. Orthostatic hypotension, arterial hypotension, or other cardiac abnormalities may occur as a result of an autonomic effects during vincristine therapy.Urinary retention, polyuria, dysuria, and urinary incontinence caused by an autonomic neuropathy resulting in bladder atony has occurred during therapy with vincristine. Elderly patients and patients receiving other medications which may cause urinary retention may be at increased risk.Reduced urine output also can occur secondary to the syndrome of inappropriate secretion of antidiuretic hormone (SIADH) sometimes seen with vincristine. Vincristine may cause SIADH by directly affecting the hypothalamus, neurohypophyseal tract or the posterior pituitary. Patients receiving aggressive hydration, receiving other agents associated with SIADH or have CNS involvement of their disease may be at increased risk.Intrathecal and cisternal administration of vincristine is uniformly fatal; immediate neurosurgical intervention is required in order to prevent ascending muscle paralysis leading to death. In patients receiving accidental intrathecal injections, the clinical course usually progresses rapidly from headache and backache (day 1), to generalized muscle weakness and loss of deep tendon reflexes (day 2), apnea (day 5), loss of brain wave activity (days 7—9) and death (day 12). Special labeling of syringes containing vincristine is required to help avoid this accident. Treatment of patients following intrathecal administration has consisted of immediate removal of spinal fluid and flushing with Lactated Ringer's, as well as other solutions, has not prevented the ascending paralysis or death. In one case, progressive paralysis in an adult was arrested using the following treatment initiated immediately after the intrathecal injection: 1) as much spinal fluid was removed as could be safely done through lumbar access; 2) the subarachnoid space was flushed with Lactated Ringer's solution infused continuously through a catheter in a cerebral lateral ventricle at the rate of 150 ml/hour. The fluid was removed through a lumbar access; 3) Fresh frozen plasma, 25 ml diluted in 1 L Lactated Ringer's solution was infused through the cerebral catheter at the rate of 75 ml/hour then adjusted to maintain the protein level in the spinal fluid of 150 mg/dl. The fluid was removed through the lumbar access; 4) Glutamic acid 10 g was given intravenously over 24 hours followed by 500 mg PO 3 times a day for 1 month or until neurologic dysfunction stabilized. The role of glutamic acid in this treatment is not certain and may not be essential.Extravasation of vincristine can lead to an injection site reaction including phlebitis, skin ulcer, and tissue necrosis. Patients should be observed for any injection site reaction especially severe pain, swelling, erythema and poor blood return. Extravasations of vinca alkaloids cause blisters after a few weeks and evolve slowly into necrotic ulcers. Some healing may occur; however, neurologic symptoms including paresthesias and sensory deficits at the site may be permanent. If extravasation occurs, treatment with warm compresses and hyaluronidase is effective in preventing ulcer formation. Cold compresses significantly increase vinca alkaloid toxicity.Vincristine-induced cellular destruction can result in tumor lysis syndrome (TLS) which includes hyperkalemia, hyperphosphatemia, hyperuricemia, and hypocalcemia. Uric acid nephropathy, acute renal failure due renal tubular obstruction, metabolic acidosis, and nephrolithiasis can also occur. These problems are more severe when a highly chemosensitive disease is treated (i.e., leukemia or lymphoma). Aggressive alkalinization of the urine and use of allopurinol can prevent urate nephropathy; close monitoring of serum electrolytes and renal function is recommended.Thrombocytopenia, mild leukopenia or mild anemia can occur with vincristine therapy, but rarely at usual doses.Following ocular exposure of vincristine, severe ocular irritation and corneal ulceration may occur.

Description
  Text
  Vincristine is a vinca alkaloid agent. It is indicated for the treatment of acute leukemias, including acute lymphocytic leukemia, in pediatric and adult patients. It is also used in combination with other anticancer agents in patients with Hodgkin lymphoma, non-Hodgkin lymphoma, rhabdomyosarcoma, neuroblastoma, and Wilms tumor. Vincristine is for intravenous use only; fatalities have been associated with inadvertent intrathecal administration of vinca alkaloids.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Mechanism of Action: Vincristine, as with all vinca alkaloids, primarily exerts its cytotoxic effects on the cell by interfering with microtubules, which compose mitotic spindle fibers leading to cell cycle arrest in the metaphase. Vinca alkaloids bind to a common pair of sites on each subunit of tubulin (alpha, beta) during the M-phase of the cell cycle. The binding sites for vinca alkaloids are different from the binding sites of the taxanes and colchicine. There are at least 2 types of tubulin binding sites for vinca alkaloids. The high-affinity sites, which are found in small numbers, are responsible for the disruption of microtubule assembly. Vinca alkaloid binding to the low-affinity binding sites results in the splitting of the microtubules into spiral aggregates or spiral protofilaments, which leads to the disintegration of the microtubule. Binding to the low-affinity sites occurs at high drug concentrations. The concentration of vinca alkaloids that induces metaphase arrest in 50% of cells results in inhibition of cell proliferation. At their lowest effective concentration, vinca alkaloids cause metaphase arrest by inhibition of mitotic spindle function. At higher concentrations, the organization of the microtubules and chromosomes deteriorates. After exposure to vinca alkaloids, some cells undergo a cell cycle block, or stathmokinesis, which is only temporary. If the drug concentration falls beneath a certain level, these cells may avoid the cytotoxic effects and divide normally. Vinca alkaloids also affect microtubules involved in chemotaxis, migration, intracellular transport, movement of organelles such as mitochondria, secretory processes, membrane trafficking, transmission of receptor signals and cell structural integrity. Vinca alkaloids have other effects that may or may not be related to their effects on tubulin. These activities include competition for transport of amino acids into cells, inhibition of purine synthesis, inhibition of RNA, DNA and protein synthesis, disruption of lipid metabolism, inhibition of glycolysis, changes in antidiuretic hormone release and disruption of cell membrane integrity and membrane function. Differences in the cytotoxic effects of different vinca alkaloids may be due to differences in cellular retention, intracellular concentrations of guanosine triphosphate (GTP), and pharmacokinetics.Resistance to vinca alkaloids is thought to occur through a couple of mechanisms. Multidrug resistance (MDR) results in decreased intracellular drug accumulation and retention. MDR is due to overexpression of the mdr-1 gene, which codes for a membrane P-glycoprotein (P-gp) that acts as a drug efflux pump. The degree of resistance is proportional to the amount of P-gp. P-gp is expressed in many malignancies including renal and large bowel cancers, and in post-treatment lymphomas, leukemias and multiple myeloma. The second mechanism of resistance to vinca alkaloids is due to alterations in the alpha- and beta-tubulin subunits. These changes result in either decreased drug binding or increased resistance to microtubule disassembly. This type of resistance to vinca alkaloids may confer sensitivity to taxanes, which inhibit microtubule disassembly.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Intravenous Route
    Description
    The cytotoxicity of vincristine is associated with intracellular concentration above certain critical threshold for a certain period of time. Most bolus schedules obtain high peak concentrations (200—400 nM) for a short period of time (< 2 hours). Exposure to 100nM of vincristine for 3 hours or exposure to 10 nM for 6—12 hours are required to kill 50% of leukemia or lymphoma cell lines in culture. Despite lower peak concentrations, continuous infusion dosage schedules are associated with longer tissue exposure to vincristine concentrations above the critical cytotoxic level. The long terminal half-life, AUC and cumulative dose of vincristine are thought to contribute to its increased incidence of neurotoxicity.
  Text
  Vincristine is administered parenterally and is not available as an oral formulation. It is widely distributed throughout many tissues in the body and binds readily to red blood cells and platelets. Vincristine has a high volume of distribution and rapid distribution half-life of <5 minutes due to extensive tissue binding. Vincristine does not cross the blood-brain barrier. It is not cleared readily from the CSF after accidental intrathecal administration, and this route of administration universally leads to death. Approximately 50% of a dose is hepatically metabolized, and most of a dose is excreted as metabolites or the parent drug in the bile and feces. Seventy-six percent of a dose is eliminated through the biliary tree over 72 hours. The beta-half-life of vincristine ranges between 50—155 minutes and the final elimination half-life is between 23—85 hours. The long terminal half-life, AUC and cumulative dose of vincristine are thought to contribute to its increased incidence of neurotoxicity. Because of the extensive biliary elimination, patients with significant biliary obstruction may need a dosage reduction. One commonly used method is to reduce the dose by 75% for a direct bilirubin of 3 mg/dl or greater.
 
Affected cytochrome P450 (CYP450) isoenzymes and drug transporters:
Vincristine is a substrate for the cytochrome P450 (CYP) 3A4 isoenzyme and P-glycoprotein (P-gp).

Dosage Adjustment Guidelines
  Hepatic Impairment
  There are differing recommendations for vincristine dosage adjustments.
 
Direct bilirubin concentration greater than 3 mg/dL: The manufacturer recommends reducing the dose by 50%. Other sources recommend avoiding administration.
Direct bilirubin concentrations 1.5 to 3 mg/dL: The manufacturer does not address dosage adjustments for these concentrations; other sources recommend reducing the dose by 50%.
 
Aminotransferase concentrations greater than 3 times the ULN: Consider reducing the dose by 50%.

  Specific guidelines for dosage adjustments in renal impairment are not available; it appears that no dosage adjustments are needed.
