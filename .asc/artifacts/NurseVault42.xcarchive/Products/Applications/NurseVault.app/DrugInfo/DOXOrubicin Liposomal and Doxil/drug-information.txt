Drug Class: Liposomal doxorubicin is a cytotoxic anthracycline antibiotic that inhibits nucleotide replication by intercalation into DNA and RNA; it is also a topoisomerase II inhibitor.

Routes: intravenous; Common adult doses: ovarian cancer, 50 mg/m2 IV every 4 weeks; AIDS-related Kaposi's sarcoma, 20 mg/m2 IV every 3 weeks; multiple myeloma, 30 mg/m2 IV every 21 days.

Common Side Effects (excluding routine GI upset): anemia, neutropenia, thrombocytopenia, bundle-branch block, cardiac arrest, chest pain (unspecified), hypotension, palpitations, peripheral vasodilation, sinus tachycardia, dysgeusia, gi bleeding, ileus, oral ulceration, cough, dyspnea, interstitial lung disease, pharyngitis, rhinitis, sinusitis, dizziness, drowsiness, dysesthesia, headache, paresthesias, peripheral neuropathy, elevated hepatic enzymes, hepatitis, hyperbilirubinemia, hypercalcemia

        Dosage Description
        20 mg/m2 IV over 60 minutes once every 21 days, until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity. Begin infusion at a rate of 1 mg/minute and increase the rate as tolerated. Guidelines recommend use with antiretroviral therapy in patients with ACTG Stage T0 (localized skin or lymph node involvement or minimal oral disease) or ACTG Stage T1 (extensive/symptomatic skin lesions, extensive oral disease, tumor-associated edema or ulceration, or any visceral involvement). In an open-label, single-arm, multicenter study, 27% of evaluable patients with disease progression on, or intolerance to, prior systemic combination chemotherapy who received liposomal doxorubicin (n = 34) had a partial response (PR) and 29% had stable disease (SD); the median time to PR was 43 days and duration of PR was 73 days. Of patients who had previously received conventional doxorubicin (n = 20), 30% experienced PR and 40% had SD; the median time to PR and duration of PR were 53 days and 89 days, respectively.

        Dosage Description
        50 mg/m2 IV over 60 minutes on day 1 every 28 days, until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity. Begin infusion at a rate of 1 mg/minute and increase the rate as tolerated. In a phase II trial, the overall response rate was 25.7% in patients with progressive disease following either cisplatin or carboplatin and paclitaxel or at least 1 platinum-based and 1 paclitaxel-based regimen. The majority of patients in this trial required dose reductions to at least 40 mg/m2 IV every 4 weeks due to severe mucositis and skin toxicity. In a randomized, multicenter, open-label, phase III trial of patients with recurrent ovarian cancer (n = 474), patients treated with liposomal doxorubicin did not have improved time to progression (primary outcome) after treatment with liposomal doxorubicin compared with topotecan (4.1 months vs. 4.2 months; p = 0.62). However, secondary endpoints of overall survival (14.4 months vs. 13.7 months; p = 0.05) and overall response rate (19.7% vs. 17%) were improved with liposomal doxorubicin. The median duration of response was 6.9 months vs. 5.9 months, respectively. For women treated with pegylated liposomal doxorubicin, there was an 18% reduction in risk of death for those with platinum-refractory disease while a 30% reduction in risk of death was noted for women with platinum-sensitive disease.

Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  General Information
  NOTE: The conventional formulation of doxorubicin is discussed in a separate monograph. Substitution of pegylated liposomal doxorubicin and conventional doxorubicin HCl formulations has resulted in severe side effects. Pegylated liposomal doxorubicin should not be substituted for doxorubicin HCl on a mg per mg basis.Pegylated liposomal doxorubicin is contraindicated in patients who have a history of doxorubicin hypersensitivity; it is prudent to use with caution in patients with documented allergic reaction to other anthracycline medications (i.e., anthracycline hypersensitivity). The Stealth® liposome formulation used in the pegylated liposomal doxorubicin formulations (Doxil®) contains phospholipid hydrogenated soy bean phosphatidylcholine (HSPC). Patients with soya lecithin hypersensitivity may be at higher risk for hypersensitivity reactions with this product.Acute infusion-related reactions characterized by flushing, acute bronchospasm, facial swelling, headache, chills, and/or hypotension have occurred in patients receiving Doxil® (see Adverse reactions). The majority of infusion-related events occurred during the first infusion and, in most patients, these reactions resolve over the course of several hours to a day once the infusion is terminated. Medications to treat such reactions, as well as emergency equipment, should be available for immediate use. In some patients, the reaction resolves when the rate of infusion is slowed. The initial rate of infusion should be 1 mg/min to help minimize the risk of infusion reactions.There is limited experience in evaluating the cardiac risk of Doxil® at high cumulative doses. Therefore, the manufacturer recommends warnings regarding the use of conventional doxorubicin and congestive heart failure be observed. Patients with preexisting heart failure (LVEF 60 years. Caution should be taken in patients who have received other anthracyclines or anthracenodiones. The total dose of doxorubicin HCl given should take into account any previous or concomitant therapy with other anthracyclines or related compounds. Females and pediatric patients may be more sensitive to the cardiotoxic effects of doxorubicin. Generally, patients with a left ventricular ejection fraction 20% from baseline if the resting left LVEF remained in the normal range, or a decrease of > 10% if the resting LVEF became abnormal (less than the institutional lower limit of normal). Clinical trial data indicate that cumulative doses of pegylated doxorubicin > 500—550 mg/m2 are associated with a significantly lower risk of cardiotoxicity than similar cumulative doses of conventional doxorubicin. Due to the bone marrow suppression secondary to their HIV disease or concomitant medicines, patients treated with Doxil® for AIDS-related Kaposi's sarcoma are at increased risk for severe myelosuppression, despite lower doses, than patients treated for ovarian cancer. Careful monitoring of these patients is necessary and dosage reductions may be indicated for severe neutropenia and thrombocytopenia (see Dosage). Pegylated liposomal doxorubicin should be used cautiously in patients who have had previous myelosuppressive therapy such as chemotherapy or radiotherapy. Monitor hematologic parameters at baseline and throughout liposomal doxorubicin therapy. Patients should be treated for any infection prior to receiving pegylated liposomal doxorubicin. Patients with a history of varicella zoster, other herpes infection (e.g., herpes simplex), or other viral infection are at risk for reactivation of the infection when treated with chemotherapy. Patients may require the use of colony-stimulating factors to support their hematologic status and to continue therapy.Patients who have had previous radiation therapy may experience radiation recall reactions during pegylated liposomal doxorubicin (Doxil®) therapy. Doxorubicin is a radiation sensitizer and should be used with caution in patients receiving concurrent radiation therapy. In addition, mediastinal radiation may increase the risk for doxorubicin-induced cardiotoxicity.Myelosuppressive effects of doxorubicin can increase the risk of infection or bleeding; therefore, dental work should be delayed until blood counts have returned to normal. Patients, especially those with dental disease, should be instructed in proper oral hygiene, including caution in use of regular toothbrushes, dental floss, and toothpicks. Pegylated liposomal doxorubicin (Doxil®) is more likely to cause mucositis than conventional doxorubicin, because it persists longer in the bloodstream. A reduction in dosage and/or dosage interval is indicated in patients with moderate to severe stomatitis (see Dosage).The safety and effectiveness of liposomal doxorubicin formulations in children has not been established.Liposomal doxorubicin formulations are considered irritants and extravasation of infusions should be avoided. Patients should be closely monitored during IV infusions for signs and symptoms of extravasation such as poor blood return, pain and swelling. If extravasation occurs, stop the infusion and remove the tubing. Attempt to aspirate the drug prior to removing the needle. Elevate the effect area and treat with ice packs for 30 minutes. Intramuscular administration and subcutaneous administration of liposomal doxorubicin formulations are not recommended due the potential for local reactions.Intramuscular injections should not be administered to patients with platelet counts = 2 weeks. The administration of live vaccines to immunocompromised patients should be avoided. Those undergoing chemotherapy should not be exposed to others who have recently received the oral poliovirus vaccine (OPV). Measles-mumps-rubella (MMR) vaccination is not contraindicated for the close contacts, including health care professionals, of immunocompromised patients. Passive immunoprophylaxis with immune globulins may be indicated for immunocompromised persons instead of, or in addition to, vaccination. When exposed to a vaccine-preventable disease such as measles, severely immunocompromised children should be considered susceptible regardless of their vaccination history.

    Headline
    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components. Doxorubicin liposomal is contraindicated in patients who have a history of severe hypersensitivity reactions, including anaphylaxis, to doxorubicin hydrochloride.
    Headline
    infusion-related reactions
    General Information
    Infusion-related reactions have been reported with liposomal doxorubicin; some cases were life-threatening or fatal. These reactions typically occur during the first infusion. Medications to treat infusion-related reactions and cardiopulmonary resuscitative equipment should be available for immediate use prior to starting the liposomal doxorubicin infusion. Interrupt or discontinue the infusion depending on the severity of the reaction.
    Headline
    cardiac disease, cardiomyopathy, thoracic radiation therapy
    General Information
    Use liposomal doxorubicin with caution in patients with a history of cardiac disease; perform a risk benefit analysis before initiating therapy. Cardiotoxicity (e.g., congestive heart failure, acute left ventricular failure) has been reported with liposomal doxorubicin therapy. Monitor cardiac function (e.g., echocardiograms, multigated radionuclide (MUGA) scans) before initiation of therapy, during treatment, and after completion of therapy. The risk of cardiomyopathy may be higher in patients with cumulative anthracycline exposure (450 to 550 mg/m2), prior thoracic radiation therapy, or treatment with concomitant use of other cardiotoxic agents. Other anthracycline and anthracenedione use should be included in the calculation of the cumulative anthracycline dose.
    Headline
    hepatic failure
    General Information
    Use liposomal doxorubicin with caution in patients with hepatic impairment, including hepatic failure. A reduced doxorubicin liposomal dose is recommended in patients with hepatic impairment (e.g., bilirubin 1.2 mg/dL or higher). Evaluate hepatic function (e.g., liver function tests) prior to doxorubicin liposomal therapy.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Liposomal doxorubicin may cause fetal harm when administered during pregnancy, based on its mechanism of action and data from animal studies. Avoid the use of liposomal doxorubicin during the first trimester. Available data is not conclusive regarding the risk of major birth defects and miscarriage when liposomal doxorubicin is used during the second or third trimesters. Advise pregnant people of the potential fetal risk. Liposomal doxorubicin was embryotoxic in rats and abortifacient in rabbits when administered during organogenesis at doses of approximately 0.12 times the recommended clinical dose.
    Headline
    people who can cause pregnancy in others, people who may become pregnant, reproductive risk
    General Information
    Counsel people who may become pregnant and people who can cause pregnancy in others about the reproductive risk associated with liposomal doxorubicin. Liposomal doxorubicin can cause fetal harm. Verify pregnancy status prior to initiation. Advise people who may become pregnant and people who can cause pregnancy in others to use effective contraception while taking liposomal doxorubicin and for 6 months after the last dose. Doxorubicin may cause infertility. Amenorrhea, premature menopause, oligospermia, azoospermia, and permanent loss of fertility have been reported. Recovery of menses and ovulation is related to age at treatment. Sperm counts have been reported to return to normal levels, sometimes several years after the end of treatment.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Advise patients to avoid breast-feeding while taking liposomal doxorubicin, due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in the breastfed child. There are no data on the presence of liposomal doxorubicin in human milk.

  NOTE: The conventional formulation of doxorubicin is discussed in a separate monograph.Myelosuppression is the dose-limiting toxicity of pegylated liposomal doxorubicin (Doxil®). Some patients receiving pegylated liposomal doxorubicin are more likely to present with myelosuppression due to underlying HIV disease or concomitant medication. Signs of myelosuppression include pancytopenia, leukopenia, neutropenia, anemia, and thrombocytopenia. For HIV-infected patients treated with pegylated liposomal doxorubicin, leukopenia occurs in about 50%, anemia in 20%, and thrombocytopenia in 10%. HIV-infected patients who experience persistent severe myelosuppression may develop superinfection or hemorrhage. Sepsis occurred in 5% of patients. Cardiac events included cardiomyopathy and/or congestive heart failure. Other severe events included arrhythmia, nonspecified, pericardial effusion and tachycardia. Clinical trials have examined the cardiac safety of pegylated liposomal doxorubicin (Doxil®) in patients who have received cumulative doses higher than 440 mg/m2. In one trial, myocardial tissue from 10 Kaposi's sarcoma patients who had received cumulative pegylated liposomal doxorubicin of 440—840 mg/m2 were evaluated and compared to historical controls treated with conventional doxorubicin. Patients treated with pegylated liposomal doxorubicin had significantly lower biopsy scores compared to controls despite higher cumulative doses of anthracyclines. Another study looked at 42 patients who had been treated with pegylated liposomal doxorubicin at cumulative doses > 500 mg/m2 (range 500—1500 mg/m2). None of these patients developed clinical congestive heart failure secondary to cardiomyopathy. Five patients had a decrease in LVEF of >=10%; three of these patients had received prior doxorubicin. One patient developed congestive heart failure who had received cumulative pegylated liposomal doxorubicin of 312 mg/m2 plus mitoxantrone 120 mg/m2 and chest radiation. Cardiac toxicity may also occur at lower cumulative doxorubicin HCl doses (400 mg/m2) in patients with prior mediastinal irradiation or who are receiving concurrent cyclophosphamide therapy.Nausea/vomiting occurs in 18.2—46% and 7.8—32.6% of patients treated with pegylated liposomal doxorubicin (Doxil®), respectively, and increases with increasing dose. It appears the pegylated liposomal formulation is not as emetogenic as conventional doxorubicin but premedication with antiemetics is still necessary. Stomatitis and mucositis may occur more frequently with the pegylated liposomal formulation due to the longer half-life than conventional doxorubicin. In patients treated with higher doses, the incidence of stomatitis is increased (37%) and 7% of patients develop severe reactions. Diarrhea has been reported in 20.9% and constipation in 30.1% of patients treated with Doxil®. Other adverse reactions reported with Doxil® include alopecia (9—15%), anorexia (20.1%), asthenia (9.9—40.2%%), abdominal pain (33.5%), peripheral edema (11.3%), pharyngitis (5.5%) and xerosis (5.5%). Local skin irritation, pain, stinging or burning at the injection site may indicate extravasation of the infusion. If extravasation occurs, stop the infusion immediately and aspirate any fluid. Elevate the affected area and treat with ice packs. Liposomal doxorubicin causes only mild edema and erythema at the site of extravasation and is not associated with tissue necrosis as is the conventional formulation.Similar to conventional doxorubicin, a radiation recall reaction can occur after administration of liposomal doxorubicin formulations at the sites of prior radiation. This reaction causes erythema, exfoliative dermatitis, pain, and burning, similar to that experienced with the original radiation therapy. Esophagitis may also be seen if it was included in the prior radiation fields as the esophagus can be especially sensitive.The possible adverse effects in infertility in males and females in humans or experimental animals have not been adequately evaluated. Pegylated liposomal doxorubicin resulted in mild to moderate ovarian and testicular atrophy in mice after a single dose of 36 mg/kg (approximately twice the 50 mg/m2 human dose on a mg/m2 basis). Decreased testicular weights and hypospermia were presented in rats after repeat doses >= 0.25 mg/kg/day (about one-thirtieth the 50 mg/m2 human dose on a mg/m2 basis), and diffuse degeneration of the seminiferous tubules and a marked decrease in spermatogenesis were observed in dogs after repeat doses of 1 mg/kg/day (about one-half the 50 mg/m2 human dose on a mg/m2 basis).

Monitor patients for symptoms of infusion-related reactions (e.g., flushing, shortness of breath, facial swelling, headache, chills, chest pain, back pain, tightness in the chest and throat, fever, tachycardia, pruritus, rash, cyanosis, syncope, bronchospasm, asthma, apnea, and hypotension). Hold the liposomal doxorubicin infusion in patients who develop grade 1, 2, or 3 infusion-related reactions and resume at a reduced rate; discontinue therapy in patients who have grade 4 or a life-threatening reaction. Allergic reaction occurred in 1% to 5% of patients with AIDS-related Kaposi sarcoma who received single-agent liposomal doxorubicin (n = 705) in a randomized trial.

Description
  Text
  Liposomal doxorubicin is a cytotoxic anthracycline antibiotic that inhibits nucleotide replication by intercalation into DNA and RNA; it is also a topoisomerase II inhibitor. Liposomal formulations incorporate the doxorubicin into a liposomal carrier. Liposomal doxorubicin is indicated for the treatment of ovarian cancer after failure of platinum-based chemotherapy, AIDS-related Kaposi's sarcoma after failure to or intolerance of prior systemic chemotherapy, and multiple myeloma in combination with bortezomib in patients who have not previously received bortezomib and have received at least 1 prior therapy. Cardiomyopathy and infusion-related reactions have been reported with liposomal doxorubicin.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Doxorubicin is the active cytotoxic agent in Doxorubicin Liposomal. Doxorubicin complexes with DNA by intercalating between DNA base pairs, causing the helix to change shape. This simple act of changing the conformation of DNA can interfere with strand elongation by inhibiting DNA polymerase and can inhibit protein synthesis due to affects on RNA polymerase. Doxorubicin may also form complexes with iron or copper. These complexes have high association constants and may contribute to doxorubicin-induced cardiotoxicity by enhancing redox cycling to produce membrane and mitochondria damage. Doxorubicin also affects topoisomerase II, an enzyme responsible for DNA strand breaks during transcription. Doxorubicin stabilizes the initial DNA-enzyme complex leading to double-strand DNA breaks. Doxorubicin also undergoes one-electron reduction to form oxygen free radical intermediates. In the presence of oxygen and metal catalysts such as Fe2+, doxorubicin undergoes reduction to the semiquinone radical. In the presence of oxygen, the semiquinone radical can form a superperoxide that in the presence of hydrogen peroxide forms hydroxyl radicals. Doxorubicin-derived free radicals can induce membrane lipid peroxidation, DNA strand scission, and direct oxidation of purine or pyrimidine bases, thiols and amines. Doxorubicin is considered cell cycle non-specific; although, it has maximum cytotoxic effects in the S-phase. Cells exposed to doxorubicin in G1 may proceed through the S-phase but then are blocked and die in the G2 phase.
 
Resistance to doxorubicin may occur through several mechanisms. One of the most important mechanisms of resistance is the multidrug resistance (MDR) mechanism that is mediated through an overexpression of a P170-glycoprotein. This membrane protein functions as an energy-dependent drug efflux pump in resistance cells. Several compounds including cyclosporine, cyclosporine analogs and verapamil may block this protein and reverse resistance. Other mechanisms of resistance include changes in topoisomerase II and glutathione activity. Liposomal doxorubicin formulations are less susceptible to tumor resistance via MDR.
 
Doxorubicin-induced free radical formation also contributes to its cardiotoxicity. Once doxorubicin enters cardiac cells, it is reduced to an anthracycline free radical that is rapidly oxidized with oxygen to form the original drug and superoxide anions. Normally, these superoxide radicals are converted back to oxygen via glutathione peroxidase (GP); however, the heart is essentially devoid of this enzyme. Administration of doxorubicin destroys any GP activity present in cardiac cells. Consequently, H2O2 is forced to react with ferrous ions (Fe2+) to form the highly toxic superhydroxide free radical that causes severe lipid peroxidation leading to extensive mitochondrial destruction. Both cardiac and malignant cells are rich in mitochondria. Additionally, these free radicals crosslink sulfhydryl groups of calcium-release channels and inhibit Ca-ATPase which leads to extensive depletion of sarcoplasmic reticulum (SR) calcium stores and prevention of restoration of calcium stores in the SR, respectively.

  Text
  Pegylated liposomal doxorubicin (Doxil) is administered intravenously. Pegylated liposomal doxorubicin distributes to the liver, spleen, and lymphatic system, but less extensively to the heart, kidneys, and lungs. In a pharmacokinetic study which administered a single dose of liposomal doxorubicin at 10 mg/m2 and 20 mg/m2 (n = 23), the volume of distribution (Vd) was 2.83 +/- 0.145 L/m2 and 2.72 +/- 0.120 L/m2, respectively; the small volume of distribution relative to conventional doxorubicin suggests that liposomal doxorubicin is mainly confined to vascular fluid. In the pharmacokinetic study, liposomal doxorubicin exhibited linear pharmacokinetics, with an alpha half life of 4.7 and 5.2 hours and a terminal half life of 52.3 and 55 hours for doses of 10 and 20 mg/m2, respectively. At doses > 50 mg/m2 the pharmacokinetics are non-linear and the elimination half-life is longer. Plasma clearance was 0.056 +/- 0.01 L/h/m2 and 0.041 +/- 0.004 L/h/m2, respectively; comparatively, plasma clearance after administration of conventional doxorubicin is 24 to 35 L/h/m2.
 
Liposomal doxorubicin formulations are made up of microscopic liposomes, which exhibit unique characteristics that influence the pharmacokinetics of the product. A liposome is a closed, spherical vehicle consisting of a single or multiple phospholipid bilayer. As high concentrations of phospholipids are mixed in an aqueous solution, the hydrophobic tails gather together to avoid water molecules, resulting in the binding of the hydrophilic heads thus forming a liposome. Liposomes may encapsulate water-soluble and fat-soluble drugs within different areas of the lipid bilayer. Liposomes are effective drug carriers due to increases blood circulation time and small size allow which allows penetration into areas of inflammation or malignant disease. These areas tend to have leaky capillaries or enlarged spaces within the lining of blood vessels which allows the liposome to pass. The pegylated liposomal doxorubicin products uses Stealth liposomes which are formulated with surface-bound methoxypolyethylene glycol (MGEG) to avoid detection by the reticuloendothelial system (RES) and increase blood circulation time.
 
Once inside the tumor, the liposomal covering allows release of the encapsulated doxorubicin. Although the exact mechanism of drug release from the liposome has not been determined, several processes which may be important to the process have been reported including endocytosis and lysosome degradation which releases their contents, lipid exchange between the liposome and the cell membrane resulting in delivery of the liposome contents to the cell and fusion of the liposome to the cellular membrane. Liposomes also attach to the cell surface allowing the contents of the liposome to diffuse across the cell membrane into the cytoplasm. The concentration of liposomal doxorubicin in Kaposi's sarcoma lesions is up to 22-times that in normal skin.
 
Doxorubicin is extensively metabolized in the liver and eliminated primarily as glucuronide or hydroxylated conjugates. Doxorubicinol is the primary metabolite and has 1/20th cytotoxic properties of doxorubicin; however, it may be a more potent cardiotoxin than doxorubicin. The formation of doxorubicinol following administration of pegylated liposomal doxorubicin is dramatically reduced resulting in very low plasma concentrations.
