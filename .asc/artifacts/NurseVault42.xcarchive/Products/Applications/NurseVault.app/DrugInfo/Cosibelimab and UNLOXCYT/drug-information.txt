Drug Class: Cosibelimab-ipdl is a programmed death ligand-1 (PD-L1) blocking antibody indicated for the treatment of metastatic cutaneous squamous cell carcinoma (mCSCC) or locally advanced CSCC (laCSCC) in adults who are not candidates for curative surgery or curative radiation.

          Dosage Description
          1,200 mg IV repeated every 3 weeks until disease progression. Therapy may need to be temporarily withheld or permanently discontinued in patients who develop immune-related reactions. Permanently discontinue therapy for severe or life-threatening infusion-related reactions. In a multicohort study, treatment with cosibelimab resulted in objective response rates of 50% (complete response [CR], 13%) and 55% (CR, 26%) in patients with metastatic CSCC or locally advanced CSCC, respectively. In patients with metastatic CSCC, the median duration of response (DOR) was not reached (range, more than 1.4 months to more than 45.3 months); the DOR was at least 12 months in 67% of patients. In patients with locally advanced CSCC, the median DOR was not reached (range, 8.3 months to more than 31.3 months); the DOR was at least 12 months in 88% of patients.

    Headline
    autoimmune disease, inflammatory bowel disease
    General Information
    Use cosibelimab with caution in patients with a history of autoimmune disease, including inflammatory bowel disease. Immune-mediated reactions, which may be severe or fatal, can occur in patients treated with drugs that bind to either the programmed death-receptor 1 (PD-1) or the PD-ligand 1 (PD-L1), thus removing inhibition of the immune response, such as cosibelimab. These immune-mediated reactions can occur in any organ system or tissue and can also affect more than one body system simultaneously. Although usually occurring during treatment with PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors, immune-mediated reactions can occur at any time after starting therapy including after discontinuation of therapy. Because early identification and management is critical to safe usage of PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors, closely monitor patients for signs and symptoms that may be clinical manifestations of underlying immune-mediated reactions. Initiate an appropriate workup to exclude alternative etiologies including infection in cases of suspected immune-mediated reaction. Begin medical management promptly, including specialty consultation as appropriate. An interruption or discontinuation of cosibelimab therapy may be necessary, depending on the severity of the reaction. In general, if an interruption or discontinuation of therapy is necessary, administer systemic corticosteroids (1 to 2 mg/kg per day of prednisone or equivalent); upon improvement to grade 1 or less, begin a steroid taper over at least 1 month. Consider additional systemic immunosuppression in patients whose symptoms are not controlled with corticosteroid therapy.

    Headline
    pulmonary disease, thoracic radiation therapy
    General Information
    Use cosibelimab with caution in patients who have a history of pre-existing pulmonary disease; immune-mediated pneumonitis/interstitial lung disease has been reported in patients treated with cosibelimab. The incidence is higher in patients who have received prior thoracic radiation therapy, except in patients with classical Hodgkin lymphoma, where pneumonitis rates were similar regardless of prior thoracic radiation. Monitor patients for signs and symptoms of pneumonitis (e.g., new or worsening cough, chest pain, shortness of breath). If confirmed, cosibelimab therapy may need to be interrupted or discontinued depending on severity; treatment with systemic corticosteroids may be necessary.
    Headline
    corneal transplant, organ transplant
    General Information
    Use cosibelimab with caution in patients who have previously had a solid organ transplant or other transplant (including corneal transplant). Immune-mediated organ transplant rejection has been reported with PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitor therapy.
    Headline
    neurological disease
    General Information
    Use cosibelimab with caution in patients who have a history of neurological disease such as myasthenia gravis or Guillain-Barre syndrome. Immune-mediated neurotoxicity has been reported with cosibelimab or other PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors. Cosibelimab may need to be interrupted or discontinued depending on the severity of the neurotoxicity; treatment with systemic corticosteroids may also be necessary.
    Headline
    allogeneic stem cell transplant
    General Information
    Perform a risk/benefit analysis prior to starting treatment with a PD-1/PD-L1 blocking antibody, such as cosibelimab, in patients who may receive or who have a history of undergoing an allogeneic stem cell transplant (SCT). Fatal and other serious complications including hyperacute, acute, and chronic graft-versus-host-disease, sinusoidal obstruction syndrome (SOS) previously termed veno-occlusive disease (VOD) after reduced intensity conditioning, and a steroid-requiring febrile syndrome (without an identified infectious cause) have been reported in patients who receive an allogeneic SCT before or after being treated with a PD-1/PD-L1 blocking antibody. Monitor patients closely for evidence of transplant-related complications and intervene promptly. Complications may occur despite intervening therapy between PD-1/PD-L1 blockade and allogeneic SCT.
    Headline
    obesity
    General Information
    Monitor patients with obesity as well as those who are considered overweight for an increased risk of infusion-related reactions when treated with cosibelimab. Patients with a high BMI have a greater risk even if they have a low metabolic risk (i.e., fewer than 2 of diabetes, dyslipidemia, hypertension).
    Headline
    cardiac disease
    General Information
    Use cosibelimab with caution in patients with cardiovascular risk factors such as hypertension, heart failure, cardiac disease, and myocardial infarction as they are at increased risk of cardiac immune-related adverse reactions such as myocarditis, vasculitis, and pericarditis.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Cosibelimab can cause fetal harm when used during pregnancy based on its mechanism of action. There are no available data on the use of cosibelimab during human pregnancy. A central function of the PD-1/PD-L1 pathway is to preserve pregnancy by maintaining immune tolerance to the fetus. Animal studies indicate that PD-1/PD-L1 inhibition may increase the risk of immune-mediated rejection of the developing fetus, resulting in fetal death. Human IgG4 antibodies are known to cross the placenta, so cosibelimab has the potential to be transmitted to the fetus. Fetal exposure to cosibelimab may increase the risk of developing immune-mediated disorders or altering the normal immune response. Patients should be counseled on the potential fetal risks.
    Headline
    people who may become pregnant, reproductive risk
    General Information
    Counsel people who may become pregnant about the reproductive risk associated with cosibelimab. Cosibelimab can cause fetal harm. Verify pregnancy status prior to treatment initiation. Advise people who may become pregnant to use effective contraception while taking cosibelimab and for 4 months after the last dose.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Advise patients to avoid breast-feeding while taking cosibelimab and for 4 months after the last dose due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in the breastfed child. There are no data on the presence of cosibelimab in human milk, its effects on the breast-fed child, or its effects on milk production.

    Headline
    immune-mediated reactions
    General Information
    Immune-mediated reactions have been reported with programmed death-receptor 1/PD-ligand 1 (PD-1/PD-L1) inhibitors, including cosibelimab; some cases were severe or fatal. Hold or permanently discontinue cosibelimab depending on toxicity severity. Immune reactions may occur in any organ system or tissue during therapy or after therapy is discontinued. Monitor patients for signs and symptoms of immune-mediated reactions. Evaluate liver enzymes, serum creatinine, and thyroid function tests at baseline and periodically during therapy. Initiate appropriate workup to exclude alternative etiologies, particularly infectious etiologies. If an immune-mediated reaction is confirmed, begin medical management promptly; obtain a specialty consultation as appropriate. If cosibelimab is held or discontinued, administer systemic corticosteroid therapy (i.e., 1 to 2 mg/kg per day of prednisone or equivalent) until the toxicity improves to grade 1 or less. When the toxicity improves to grade 1 or less, begin a steroid taper over at least 1 month. If the immune-mediated toxicity is not improved with systemic corticosteroids, consider administering other systemic immunosuppressants.

Description
  Text
  Cosibelimab-ipdl is a programmed death ligand-1 (PD-L1) blocking antibody indicated for the treatment of metastatic cutaneous squamous cell carcinoma (mCSCC) or locally advanced CSCC (laCSCC) in adults who are not candidates for curative surgery or curative radiation. Treatment with cosibelimab may result in severe immune-related adverse reactions requiring interruption or discontinuation of therapy, as well as treatment with high-dose corticosteroids. Infusion-related reactions may also occur. Cosibelimab was approved by the FDA in December 2024.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Cosibelimab is a human IgG1 lambda monoclonal antibody, produced in Chinese hamster ovary (CHO) cells, that inhibits programmed death ligand 1 (PD-L1) interactions with the PD-1 and B7.1 receptors. Binding of PD-L1 to the PD-1 and B7.1 receptors found on T cells and antigen presenting cells suppresses cytotoxic T-cell activity, T-cell proliferation, and cytokine production. Cosibelimab binds PD-L1 and blocks the interaction between PD-L1 and its receptors PD-1 and B7.1. This interaction releases the inhibitory effects of PD-L1 on the anti-tumor immune response. Cosibelimab has also been shown to induce antibody-dependent cell-mediated cytotoxicity (ADCC) in vitro. The PD-1 pathway regulates the balance between T-cell activation and protection of healthy tissues from immune-mediated damage. In cancer, the PD-1 pathway is thought to play an important role in the interaction of tumor cells with the host immune response.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics

    Intravenous Route
    Description
    The mean concentrations of cosibelimab 1,200 mg every 3 weeks ranged between 120 mcg/L (coefficient of variation [CV], 46.3%) and 453 mcg/L (CV, 22.2%) and steady state exposure was achieved after 84 days. The steady state exposure at 1,200 mg every 3 weeks (the recommended dosage) was comparable to the exposure at 800 mg every 2 weeks. The cosibelimab plasma Cmax was 492 mcg/L (CV, 24.3%) and AUC was 112,000 mcg x hour/mL (CV, 39.6%). The Cmax and AUC increased proportionally over the dose range of 800 mg to 1,200 mg following single dosing. Steady-state concentrations of cosibelimab are reached by 12 weeks.

      Intravenous Administration
      Description
      Dilution:
Withdraw the required volume of cosibelimab from the vial(s).
Add the cosibelimab to an infusion bag containing 250 mL 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection; compatible with infusion bags made of polyolefin or polyvinyl chloride.
Mix by gentle inversion; do not shake.
Storage after dilution: Store diluted solution at room temperature (up to 25 degrees C; 77 degrees F) or under refrigeration (2 to 8 degrees C; 36 to 46 degrees F) for no more than 24 hours from the time of preparation until the end of infusion.
Infusion:
Do not administer as an IV push or bolus injection.
Do not co-administer other drugs through the same infusion line.
If the IV bag was refrigerated, allow it to come to room temperature prior to administration.
Administer by IV infusion over 60 minutes through an IV line containing a sterile, in-line or add-on filter (0.2 to 0.22 micron).
