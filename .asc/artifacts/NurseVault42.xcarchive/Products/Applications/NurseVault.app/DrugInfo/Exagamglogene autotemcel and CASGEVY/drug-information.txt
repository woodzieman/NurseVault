    anemia, epistaxis, hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis, intracranial bleeding, leukopenia, lymphopenia, neutropenia, thrombocytopenia
    General Information
    Epistaxis (grade 3 or 4, 7% to 13%) and intracranial bleeding (i.e., cerebellar hemorrhage, 2%) were reported with exagamglogene autotemcel after busulfan myeloablative conditioning therapy in clinical trials. Hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis (2% to 7%) was also reported with exagamglogene autotemcel after busulfan myeloablative conditioning therapy in clinical trials. Grade 3 or 4 hematologic laboratory abnormalities reported between the start of busulfan myeloablative conditioning and 24 months after exagamglogene autotemcel treatment included anemia (84% to 100%), leukopenia (98% to 100%), lymphopenia (50% to 79%), CD4 lymphocytes decreased (0% to 23%), neutropenia (100%), prolonged activated partial thromboplastin time (9% to 27%), and thrombocytopenia (100%); laboratory abnormalities were generally consistent with those expected from busulfan myeloablative conditioning and stem cell transplant. Grade 3 or 4 febrile neutropenia was reported in 48% to 87% of individuals (age 5 years and older) treated with exagamglogene autotemcel after busulfan myeloablative conditioning therapy in clinical trials. Platelet engraftment is defined as 3 consecutive measurements of platelet counts of 50,000 cells/mm3 or more in persons with sickle cell disease and 20,000 cells/mm3 or more in persons with transfusion-dependent beta-thalassemia (TDT), obtained on 3 different days after exagamglogene autotemcel infusion, without administration of platelet transfusions for 7 days. The median (range) time to platelet engraftment was 47 (24, 78) days in individuals 5 to 11 years, 40 (23, 64) days in individuals 12 to 16 years, and 32.5 (23, 126) days in individuals 17 years and older with sickle cell disease. One patient with sickle cell disease received a thrombopoietin (TPO) mimetic, which was started on Day 52 and continued through Day 151. In individuals with TDT, the median time to platelet engraftment was 51 (22, 82) days for individuals 5 to 11 years, 46 (20, 199) days for individuals 12 to 16 years, and 40 (24, 200) days for individuals 17 years and older. There is an increased risk of bleeding until platelet engraftment is achieved; there was no association observed between bleeding events and time to platelet engraftment. In the TDT trial, subjects without a spleen had an earlier median time to platelet engraftment than subjects with an intact spleen. The median (range) time to platelet engraftment was 34.5 (20, 78) days in subjects without a spleen and 47.5 (27, 200) days in subjects with an intact spleen. While the use of TPO mimetics was not specified in the trial protocol, 5 subjects (10%) with TDT received a TPO mimetic at the time of platelet engraftment, and all 5 patients continued TPO mimetic use for thrombocytopenia beyond engraftment. The total duration of TPO mimetic use was 98 to 457 days. Monitor for bleeding according to standard guidelines and medical judgment. Conduct frequent platelet counts until platelet engraftment and platelet recovery are achieved. Perform blood cell count determination and other appropriate testing whenever clinical symptoms suggestive of bleeding arise. Neutrophil engraftment failure is a potential risk in stem cell transplant. Neutrophil engraftment is defined as 3 consecutive measurements of absolute neutrophil count (ANC) of 500 cells/mm3 or more on 3 different days after exagamglogene autotemcel infusion, without use of the unmodified rescue CD34+ cells. The median (range) time to neutrophil engraftment was 28 (20, 37) days in individuals 5 to 11 years, 29 (26, 40) days in individuals 12 to 16 years, and 26 (15, 38) days in individuals 17 years and older with sickle cell disease. In individuals with TDT, the median time to neutrophil engraftment was 30 (19, 38) days for individuals 5 to 11 years, 31 (19, 56) days for individuals 12 to 16 years, and 29 (12, 40) days for individuals 17 years and older. All treated subjects achieved neutrophil engraftment and none received rescue CD34+ cells. Monitor ANC and manage infections according to standard guidelines and medical judgment. Granulocyte colony-stimulating factor (G-CSF) is not recommended for 21 days after exagamglogene autotemcel infusion. In the event of neutrophil engraftment failure, infuse rescue CD34+ cells.

Description
  Text
  Exagamglogene autotemcel is an autologous genome edited hematopoietic stem cell-based gene therapy indicated for the treatment of sickle cell disease in persons with recurrent vaso-occlusive crises (VOCs) and for the treatment of transfusion-dependent beta thalassemia. Exagamglogene autotemcel is a cellular gene therapy consisting of autologous CD34+ hematopoietic stem cells edited by CRISPR/Cas9-technology at the erythroid specific enhancer region of the BCL11A gene to reduce BCL11A expression in erythroid lineage cells, leading to increased fetal hemoglobin (HbF) protein production. Efficacy and safety of a single dose of exagamglogene autotemcel was evaluated in a single-arm, multi-center trial in adults and adolescents with sickle cell disease. The primary efficacy outcome was the proportion of subjects free of protocol-defined severe VOCs for at least 12 consecutive months within the first 24 months after exagamglogene autotemcel administration (VF12 response). The VF12 response rate in an interim analysis was 93.5% (98% one-sided CI: 77.9% to 100%). Another open-label, single-arm, multi-center trial evaluated exagamglogene autotemcel in adults and adolescents with transfusion-dependent beta thalassemia. The primary outcome was the proportion of subjects achieving transfusion independence for 12 consecutive months (TI12) within the first 24 months after exagamglogene autotemcel administration. The TI12 responder rate in an interim analysis was 91.4% (98.3%, one-sided CI: 75.7% to 100%). Delayed platelet engraftment has been reported with exagamglogene autotemcel, and there is a potential risk for neutrophil engraftment failure. The risk of unintended, off-target editing in an individual's CD34+ cells cannot be ruled out due to genetic variants. Exagamglogene autotemcel was FDA-approved in 2023.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Exagamglogene autotemcel is a cellular gene therapy consisting of autologous CD34+ hematopoietic stem cells edited by CRISPR/Cas9-technology at the erythroid specific enhancer region of the BCL11A gene. After exagamglogene autotemcel infusion, the edited CD34+ cells engraft in the bone marrow and differentiate to erythroid lineage cells with reduced BCL11A expression. Reduced BCL11A expression results in an increase in gamma-globin expression and fetal hemoglobin (HbF) protein production in erythroid cells. In persons with severe sickle cell disease, HbF expression reduces intracellular hemoglobin S (HbS) concentration, preventing the red blood cells from sickling and addressing the underlying cause of disease, thereby eliminating vaso-occlusive crises. In persons with transfusion-dependent beta thalassemia, gamma-globin production improves the alpha-globin to non-alpha-globin imbalance thereby reducing ineffective erythropoiesis and hemolysis and increasing total hemoglobin concentrations, addressing the underlying cause of the disease, and eliminating the dependence on regular red blood cell transfusions.
 
To produce this autologous hematopoietic stem cell-based gene therapy, hematopoietic stem cells are collected from the blood via apheresis procedure(s); the autologous cells are enriched for CD34+ cells, and then genome edited ex vivo by introducing the CRISPR/Cas9 ribonucleoprotein (RNP) complex by electroporation. The guide RNA included in the RNP complex enables CRISPR/Cas9 to make a precise DNA double-strand break at a critical transcription factor binding site (GATA1) in the erythroid specific enhancer region of the BCL11A gene. As a result of the editing, GATA1 binding is disrupted and BCL11A expression is reduced. This reduction in BCL11A expression conversely results in an increase in gamma-globin expression and downstream HbF formation. The edited CD34+ cells are formulated into a suspension in a sterile cryopreservative medium and cryopreserved.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Intravenous Route
    Description
    In a study in individuals with sickle cell disease (age 12 years and older), the mean (SD) proportion of hemoglobin (Hb) comprised by fetal hemoglobin (HbF) was 43.9% (8.6%) at Month 6 after exagamglogene autotemcel and was maintained thereafter. Increases in mean (SD) total Hb concentrations were observed as early as Month 3 after exagamglogene autotemcel infusion, continued to increase to 12.5 (1.8) g/dL at Month 6, and was maintained thereafter. Consistent with the increase in HbF concentrations, the mean (SD) proportion of circulating erythrocytes expressing HbF (F-cells) at Month 3 after exagamglogene autotemcel was 70.1% (13.8%) and continued to increase over time to 94% (12.4%) at month 6, with concentrations remaining stable thereafter, indicating sustained pan-cellular expression of HbF. In a study of subjects with transfusion-dependent beta-thalassemia (TDT), increases in mean (SD) total Hb and HbF concentrations were observed as early as Month 3 after exagamglogene autotemcel infusion, continued to increase to 12.2 (2) g/dL and 10.9 g/dL (2.8), respectively, at Month 6, and were maintained thereafter, with HbF comprising 88% or more of total Hb. Consistent with the increase in HbF concentrations, the mean (SD) proportion of circulating erythrocytes expressing HbF (F-cells) at Month 3 after exagamglogene autotemcel was 73.8% (19.7%) and continued to increase over time to 95.9% (15.2%) at Month 6, with concentrations remaining stable thereafter, indicating pan-cellular expression of HbF.

Before thaw, confirm that exagamglogene autotemcel is printed on the vial label and that the recipient identity matches the unique personal identification information located on the exagamglogene autotemcel vial(s). Do not infuse if the information on the recipient-specific label on any of the vials does not match the intended recipient.
A dose of exagamglogene autotemcel may be contained in 1 or more cryopreserved recipient-specific vial(s). Ensure that the correct number of vials are present. Use the accompanying Lot Information Sheet to confirm the total number of vials to be administered and confirm that each vial is within the expiration date prior to preparation of exagamglogene autotemcel for infusion.
Thaw each exagamglogene autotemcel vial at 37 degrees C (98 degrees F) using a water bath. Thaw each vial holding the vial neck, gently agitating clockwise and counterclockwise. Thawing of each vial can take between 10 and 15 minutes. Thawing is complete when ice crystals are no longer visible in the vial. Ensure water bath temperature does not exceed 40 degrees C (104 degrees F) during thawing. Do not leave exagamglogene autotemcel unattended during thaw. Remove vial from water bath immediately once thawed. The thawed product should appear as a cellular suspension, which may contain proteinaceous particles or cellular aggregates. When the dose consists of multiple vials, thaw and administer 1 vial at a time. While thawing and administering a vial, remaining vials must remain in cryo-storage at -135 degrees C or less (-211 degrees F or less).
Remove the flip-away tab of the exagamglogene autotemcel vial cap; clean the septum with an alcohol swab. Remove the cap on the vial adapter spike. With the thumb and forefinger of both hands, push the adapter into the vial septum, applying equal pressure until there is a single pop. Pull up on the adapter until you feel it lock. Attach the filter to the vial adapter.
Attach an empty 30 mL syringe to the filter and withdraw the entire vial product volume. Remove the product-filled syringe from the filter and set aside.
Draw 5 to 10 mL of saline into the empty 10 mL syringe and attach the saline-filled syringe to the filter. Inject the saline into the exagamglogene autotemcel vial and remove the empty syringe from the filter. Discard the empty syringe.
Attach the product-filled syringe to the filter and withdraw the contents of the vial into the syringe, then remove the syringe from the filter. Peel the product/recipient identifier label from the Lot Information Sheet and affix to the product-filled syringe.
Do not wash, spin down, and/or resuspend exagamglogene autotemcel in new media prior to infusion.
Do not sample, alter, irradiate, or refreeze exagamglogene autotemcel.
Storage: Infuse exagamglogene autotemcel within 20 minutes of thaw.
 
Intravenous (IV) Push
Confirm the recipient's identity with the personal identifiers on the vial(s). Confirm the total number of vial(s) with the Lot Information Sheet.
If more than 1 vial is provided, thaw and administer each vial completely before proceeding to thaw the next vial.
Do not use an in-line blood filter or an infusion pump.
Administer as an IV bolus/push within 20 minutes of product thaw. The total volume of exagamglogene autotemcel administered within 1 hour must not exceed 2.6 mL/kg.
Flush the primary line with 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection, using enough volume to flush the tubing and the length of the IV catheter, after administration of each vial of exagamglogene autotemcel.
Repeat steps for each vial.
