        Children 4 years and older and Adolescents
        Dosage Description
        Various doses have been studied including levodopa-carbidopa 1.02/0.25 mg/kg PO 3 times daily, or 0.5 mg levodopa (with a 25% fixed-dose combination of carbidopa) PO 3 times daily; some studies compared the drug with patching or occlusion while others added the drug to patching/occlusion. Limited data indicate that there may be some benefit to combining levodopa/carbidopa with occlusion therapy. A single-dose study of 20 children (4 to 14 years) with amblyopia all undergoing occlusion therapy, compared levodopa-carbidopa 25/6.25 mg or 50/12.5 mg to placebo. Significant improvement of 1 line in visual acuity was observed at both levodopa/carbidopa dosages whereas no significant improvement in visual acuity was noted with use of placebo. In a 7-week study, 13 amblyopic children (7 to 12 years) who no longer responded to occlusion or penalization therapy were randomized to treatment with levodopa/carbidopa 1.02/0.25 mg/kg 3 times daily with or without occlusion (occlusion was used for 3 hours/day). A significantly greater improvement in visual acuity of the amblyopic eye was observed in the occlusion group compared to the non-occlusion group (2.1 lines vs. 0.8 lines, p = 0.002). In addition, the occlusion group maintained a greater improvement in the visual acuity of the amblyopic eye 4 weeks after study termination. No statistically significant difference was noted in the visual acuity of the dominant eye 4 weeks after study termination. In another 7-week study, amblyopic patients (4 to 22 years) were randomized to receive treatment with levodopa alone (0.5 mg/kg with a 25% fixed-dose combination of carbidopa 3 times daily), levodopa with part-time occlusion (3 hours/day), or levodopa with full-time occlusion (during all waking hours). None of the patients 15 years or older responded to therapy. An improvement in visual acuity occurred in 74% of the patients (n = 53), regardless of occlusion therapy. Forty-four of the responders were followed up 1 year after cessation of levodopa-carbidopa therapy. Improvement in visual acuity was maintained in approximately 50% of these patients. Although the addition of occlusion (part-time or full-time) did not facilitate recovery of vision, improvement in visual acuity was maintained for a longer duration in the patients who received full-time occlusion than in those receiving levodopa-carbidopa alone or with part-time occlusion.

Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  General Information
  Carbidopa; levodopa combinations are contraindicated in patients with a hypersensitivity to carbidopa, levodopa, or any other component of the drug. 
 
Avoid abrupt discontinuation of carbidopa; levodopa therapy. Sporadic cases of a symptom complex resembling neuroleptic malignant syndrome have been associated with dose reductions and withdrawal of carbidopa; levodopa therapy. Patients should be closely monitored if abrupt reduction or discontinuation of carbidopa-levodopa therapy is required, especially if they are also receiving neuroleptics. Patients should be observed closely for manifestations of this symptom complex including tachycardia, muscular rigidity, elevation of body temperature, mental status changes, diaphoresis, tachypnea, and increases in serum creatinine levels. Cases of neuroleptic malignant syndrome have been reported post-surgery so close monitoring is warranted; re-institute carbidopa-levodopa therapy as soon as possible post-surgery. In addition, some patients receiving levodopa have experienced post-operative bleeding episodes, so hematological studies are recommended for all patients who undergo surgery while receiving this drug.
 
Carbidopa; levodopa combinations should be administered with extreme caution to patients with cardiac disease. Carbidopa; levodopa should be used with caution in patients a history of myocardial infarction who have residual cardiac arrhythmias (i.e., atrial, nodal, or ventricular arrhythmias). Cardiac monitoring in a facility with provisions for intensive cardiac care is recommended during initial titration of carbidopa-levodopa in patients with significant cardiac disease.
 
Carbidopa; levodopa should be used with caution in patients with a history of bronchial asthma and emphysema on sympathomimetic therapies; levodopa has additional sympathomimetic effects. Because levodopa may alter breathing patterns or respiratory rates in some patients, it should be used under careful observation in patients with severe pulmonary disease.
 
Carbidopa; levodopa combinations should be administered with caution to patients with renal disease or hepatic disease.
 
Carbidopa; levodopa combinations may increase the risk of upper GI hemorrhage in patients with a history of active peptic ulcer disease, so it should be used with extreme caution in such patients.
 
Carbidopa; levodopa combinations can cause mental status or nervous system changes. The elderly may be more susceptible to the CNS effects of levodopa, especially when initiating therapy or increasing dosages. All patients receiving the drug should be monitored closely for changes in thought processes including depression and/or suicidal ideation. Carbidopa; levodopa should be used with caution in patients with a history of a seizure disorder or known psychosis or other psychiatric illness. 
 
Concomitant use of non-selective MAOI therapy with carbidopa; levodopa combinations can result in hypertensive crisis, and simultaneous use of these agents is contraindicated. Non-selective MAOIs should be discontinued 2—4 weeks before initiation of carbidopa;levodopa combination therapy (see Drug Interactions).
 
Patients with diabetes mellitus should be monitored during carbidopa; levodopa therapy. Alterations in blood glucose may occur in some individuals as a result of the sympathomimetic effects of levodopa. Carbidopa; levodopa may cause a false-positive reaction for urinary ketone bodies when a test tape is used for determination of ketonuria. False-negative tests may result with use of glucose-oxidase methods of testing for glucosuria.
 
Carbidopa; levodopa combinations are contraindicated for use in patients with closed-angle glaucoma. Levodopa may be used in patients with open-angle glaucoma if intraocular pressure is closely monitored and controlled.
 
Levodopa can activate melanoma, so the drug is contraindicated in patients with a history of melanoma, or in patients with suspicious, undiagnosed pigmented lesions.
 
Some patients receiving medications that increase dopaminergic tone have reported intense and uncontrollable urges to gamble, increased sexual urges, or other intense urges. Causality due to dopaminergic agents has not been established; however, in some cases the urges stopped after the dose was reduced or the drug was discontinued. Practitioners should inquire periodically about new or worsening urges in patients receiving carbidopa-levodopa. Likewise, patients should be instructed to report such changes while receiving carbidopa-levodopa. Dose reduction or discontinuation should be considered in those who experience these effects.
 
Carbidopa-levodopa is classified as FDA pregnancy risk category C. Although no adequate human studies have examined the effects of this drug on the fetus, animal reproduction studies have shown adverse fetal effects due to levodopa or carbidopa-levodopa administration. Levodopa does cross the placenta. Limited case reports are available describing the use of carbidopa-levodopa combinations in human pregnancy. Therefore, in making the decision to administer this drug during pregnancy, the potential risks to the fetus must be weighed against the potential benefits to the mother.
 
 Carbidopa; levodopa should be avoided in breast-feeding women. Carbidopa distributes into breast milk in small amounts. It is not known if levodopa is distributed into breast milk, however, levodopa inhibits lactation by inhibiting prolactin release. The suppression of milk production by levodopa may be partial or complete.
 
Carbidopa; levodopa combinations have not been adequately studied for use in children less than 18 years of age.
 
Rarely, falsely diagnosed pheochromocytoma has been reported in patients receiving carbidopa; levodopa. Caution should be exercised with interpreting the plasma and urine levels of catecholamines and catecholamine metabolites in patients on carbidopa; levodopa.

    Headline
    dyskinesia
    General Information
    Carbidopa; levodopa may cause or exacerbate pre-existing dyskinesia. If worsening of dyskinesias occurs during treatment, a reduction in the dose of dopaminergic medication or discontinuation of treatment may be necessary. Because carbidopa permits more levodopa to reach the brain and more dopamine to be formed, dyskinesias (involuntary movements), may occur at lower dosages and sooner with carbidopa; levodopa than with levodopa alone.
    Headline
    cardiovascular disease, hypotension
    General Information
    Caution should be exercised when prescribing carbidopa; levodopa to patients with severe cardiovascular disease, including patients with a history of myocardial infarction with residual atrial, nodal, or ventricular arrhythmias. These patients should receive monitoring of cardiac function with particular care during treatment initiation and dose adjustment in a facility with provisions for intensive cardiac care. Patients with Parkinson disease who are prone to hypotension or orthostatic hypotension should also receive carbidopa; levodopa with caution. Syncope and/or orthostatic hypotension have been reported with drugs that increase dopaminergic tone, including levodopa; carbidopa. Reports of syncope are generally more frequent in patients who have experienced prior episodes of documented hypotension. Monitor for orthostatic hypotension, especially after initiation of treatment or after increasing the carbidopa; levodopa dose.
    Headline
    activities requiring coordination and concentration, driving or operating machinery, sleep disorder
    General Information
    Carbidopa; levodopa has the potential to cause drowsiness or somnolence. In some cases, excessive drowsiness has resulted in automobile accidents or other harmful events in the course of daily living. Advise patients of this effect and be instruct them to use extreme caution when driving or operating machinery or performing other activities requiring coordination and concentration while receiving carbidopa; levodopa therapy. Reassessment for drowsiness or oversedation is necessary throughout therapy, as these symptoms may go unnoticed unless specifically asked about. Inform patients of the risk of sudden sleep before starting therapy and advise them to avoid hazardous activities, especially if they have a sleep disorder (e.g., narcolepsy, sleep apnea) or are taking alcohol or other CNS depressants, as these factors may increase the risk of sudden sleep onset. If a patient develops significant daytime sleepiness or episodes of falling asleep during activities that require active participation (e.g., conversations, eating, etc.), consider carbidopa; levodopa discontinuation. There is insufficient data to establish if dose reduction will eliminate sudden episodes of falling asleep.
    Headline
    depression, psychotic disorder, suicidal ideation
    General Information
    The use of carbidopa; levodopa can increase the risk of hallucinations and psychosis. Patients with a psychotic disorder should not be treated with carbidopa; levodopa due to the risk of exacerbating psychosis. In addition, medications that antagonize the effects of dopamine used to treat psychosis may exacerbate the symptoms of Parkinson disease and may decrease the effectiveness of levodopa. People receiving carbidopa; levodopa should be monitored closely for psychiatric effects, including depression and concomitant suicidal ideation; patients with a history of depression should be assessed closely for worsening of these symptoms. Dose adjustments or discontinuation of treatment may be necessary to mitigate psychiatric effects. An increased risk for hallucinations and psychosis has been observed in patients with advanced Parkinson disease taking some of the extended-release formulations of carbidopa; levodopa versus immediate-release formulas.
    Headline
    peptic ulcer disease
    General Information
    Carbidopa; levodopa treatment may increase the risk of upper GI bleeding in people with a history of peptic ulcer disease.   Because the enteral suspension of carbidopa; levodopa (i.e., Duopa) is administered into the jejunum using a PEG-J or naso-jejunal tube (NJ tube), intestinal complications due to the drug or due to the associated gastrointestinal procedures can occur. These complications include bezoar, ileus, implant site erosion/ulcer, intestinal hemorrhage, intestinal ischemia, intestinal GI obstruction, intestinal GI perforation, intussusception, pancreatitis, peritonitis, pneumoperitoneum, and post-operative wound infection. These complications may result in serious outcomes, such as death or the need for surgery. Instruct patients to notify their care team immediately if they experience abdominal pain, prolonged constipation, nausea, vomiting, fever, or melanotic stool while receiving this enteral suspension.
    Headline
    closed-angle glaucoma, open-angle glaucoma
    General Information
    Several formulations of carbidopa; levodopa are contraindicated for use in individuals with closed-angle glaucoma. Individuals with chronic wide-angle or open-angle glaucoma may be treated cautiously with these dosage forms provided the intraocular pressure is well-controlled, and the patient is monitored carefully for changes in intraocular pressure during therapy. Use the enteral suspension and extended-release capsule formulations of carbidopa; levodopa with caution in people with glaucoma. Monitor intraocular pressure in individuals with glaucoma after starting these dosage forms.
    Headline
    peripheral neuropathy
    General Information
    Patients should be evaluated for signs and symptoms of peripheral neuropathy before starting carbidopa; levodopa enteral suspension. Monitor patients periodically for signs of neuropathy after starting the enteral suspension, especially in people with pre-existing neuropathy and in patients taking medications or who have medical conditions that are also associated with neuropathy. In clinical studies, 5% of carbidopa; levodopa enteral suspension recipients developed generalized polyneuropathy. Most cases were classified as subacute or chronic in onset, and were most often characterized as sensory or sensorimotor. Electrodiagnostic testing indicated the neuropathy was most often consistent with an axonal polyneuropathy, and 1 patient was classified as having a demyelinating neuropathy. There was insufficient information to determine the potential role of vitamin deficiencies in the etiology of neuropathy associated with carbidopa; levodopa enteral suspension.
    Headline
    pulmonary disease
    General Information
    Carbidopa; levodopa immediate-release tablets, extended-release tablets, and orally disintegrating tablets should be administered cautiously to patients with severe pulmonary disease or bronchial asthma. The other dosage forms of carbidopa; levodopa do not have this precaution in their product labels.
    Headline
    endocrine disease, hepatic disease, renal disease
    General Information
    There are general, broad precautions that carbidopa; levodopa immediate-release tablets, extended-release tablets, and orally disintegrating tablets should be administered with caution to people with renal disease, hepatic disease, or endocrine disease. No dosage adjustments are recommended based on renal or hepatic impairment; titration occurs to clinical response.  Other carbidopa; levodopa dosage forms do not list endocrine, hepatic or renal disease as precautions in their product labels.
    Headline
    phenylketonuria
    General Information
    Patients with phenylketonuria should be informed that carbidopa; levodopa orally disintegrating tablets (ODT) contain aspartame, a source of phenylalanine.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    There are no adequate or well-controlled studies of carbidopa; levodopa during pregnancy. Limited data suggest that carbidopa crosses the placenta in humans in low concentrations.  Levodopa crosses the placenta and reaches levels in the fetus comparable to maternal blood, although pregnancy outcomes have been primarily unremarkable. Two miscarriages were reported in the first trimester during use of carbidopa; levodopa, and 1 infant exposed to levodopa in utero was reported to have osteomalacia. Maternal complications reported in 3 pregnancies during use of carbidopa; levodopa included first trimester vaginal bleeding, third trimester nausea and vomiting, depression, and preeclampsia. Animal studies have shown that carbidopa-levodopa can be developmentally toxic, including teratogenic, at doses relevant to clinical use. In pregnant rabbits, administering carbidopa; levodopa throughout organogenesis resulted in visceral and skeletal malformations in fetuses at all tested doses and ratios. Conversely, no teratogenic effects were observed in pregnant mice given the medication during organogenesis.     General Information
    Use carbidopa; levodopa with caution during breast-feeding. Levodopa is present in human milk, but there are no data on the presence of carbidopa in human milk. There are no data on the presence of carbidopa in human milk, the effects of levodopa; carbidopa on the breastfed child, or their effects on milk production. Carbidopa is present in rat milk. Levodopa decreases secretion of prolactin and may inhibit lactation. Partial to complete suppression of lactation has been observed in patients given levodopa for galactorrhea. If carbidopa; levodopa must be administered during breast-feeding, monitor the breast-fed child for commonly encountered adverse effects associated with dopaminergic therapy including dyskinesias, insomnia, sedation, nausea, and constipation. In a case report of a breast-feeding individual with Parkinson disease receiving sustained-release (SR) carbidopa/levodopa 50/200 mg 4 times daily, peak levodopa concentrations in milk occurred 3 hours after a dose and returned to baseline after 6 hours. After receiving the same dose of an immediate-release (IR) formulation, similar results were observed, except for a higher milk to plasma ratio with the IR product (0.32 vs. 0.28). The infant ingested 0.127 mg and 0.181 mg of levodopa after maternal intake of the SR and IR products, respectively. It was estimated that the infant received an average of 0.3% of the maternal weight-adjusted dosage of the SR product and 0.5% of the maternal weight-adjusted dose of the IR product. Levodopa did not inhibit lactation. No adverse effects were observed in the child, whose development was normal at 2 years of age. The authors suggest that breast-feeding or pumping milk at times of lower levodopa breast milk concentrations may help minimize the amount of levodopa received by a child.

Pregnancy And Lactation
  Pregnancy
    General Information
    There are no adequate or well-controlled studies of carbidopa; levodopa during pregnancy. Limited data suggest that carbidopa crosses the placenta in humans in low concentrations.  Levodopa crosses the placenta and reaches levels in the fetus comparable to maternal blood, although pregnancy outcomes have been primarily unremarkable. Two miscarriages were reported in the first trimester during use of carbidopa; levodopa, and 1 infant exposed to levodopa in utero was reported to have osteomalacia. Maternal complications reported in 3 pregnancies during use of carbidopa; levodopa included first trimester vaginal bleeding, third trimester nausea and vomiting, depression, and preeclampsia. Animal studies have shown that carbidopa-levodopa can be developmentally toxic, including teratogenic, at doses relevant to clinical use. In pregnant rabbits, administering carbidopa; levodopa throughout organogenesis resulted in visceral and skeletal malformations in fetuses at all tested doses and ratios. Conversely, no teratogenic effects were observed in pregnant mice given the medication during organogenesis. Additionally, in rats, exposure during organogenesis led to a decrease in the number of live pups delivered.

Food: (Major) Advise patients to avoid cannabis use while taking CNS depressants due to the risk for additive CNS depression and potential for other cognitive adverse reactions. (Major) Many levodopa products may be taken with or without food. Follow the directions of the product prescribed. However, foods with a high protein content may delay or impair the oral absorption of levodopa and may reduce efficacy. The Parkinson's Foundation states that most patients have no problem taking levodopa-containing products with meals, but some experience less benefit if they take such products with a stomach full of protein (including meats, cheeses and other dairy products). When this occurs, it is recommended to only take carbidopa/levodopa along with non-protein foods. For patients with more advanced PD, it is best to take levodopa-containing medications 30 to 60 minutes before eating a meal to limit food interference. If nausea occurs, the products may be taken with a small non-protein snack, such as fruit or a cracker, to help. (Major) Patients with Parkinson's disease should avoid foods high in fat around the time of taking carbidopa; levodopa. These foods may delay gastric emptying, which can decrease and delay the absorption of levodopa. Adverse Reactions
  Headline
  Adverse Reactions
  General Information
  Nearly all patients receiving carbidopa; levodopa experience some sort of adverse effect. These effects are usually dose-related and reversible. The carbidopa; levodopa combination preparation generally causes fewer GI effects than does levodopa alone. However, adverse CNS effects can occur more quickly and at lower doses with the combination preparations than with levodopa alone, due to the increased CNS bioavailability of levodopa.
 
Dyskinesia and sporadic movements are the most common of the serious effects of carbidopa; levodopa therapy and include choreiform reaction and dystonic reaction. These effects occur in up to 80% of patients receiving levodopa therapy for >= 3 years. Involuntary movements, including chewing, bruxism, gnawing, twisting, protrusion of the tongue, opening and closing the mouth, bobbing of the head, rhythmic movements of the feet or hands, quick movements of the shoulder, and ballismus, have been reported and may necessitate dosage reduction. Blepharospasm (which may indicate toxicity), muscle twitching, ataxia, myoclonia during sleep, and hand tremor also have been reported. Patients receiving the extended-release product may have an increased risk of dyskinesias compared to the regular-release formulation. The occurrence of dyskinesias may require dosage reduction. Peak-dose (occurring at times of peak levodopa plasma levels) and diphasic (occurring when levodopa levels are increasing or decreasing) dyskinesias may also occur.
 
'Bradykinetic episodes' or 'on-off' phenomena, including akinesia, sudden return of effectiveness, and akinesia paradoxica, can occur during levodopa therapy and is likely due to both disease progression and excessive levodopa dosage. The bradykinetic episode, a sudden lack of effectiveness with concomitant akinesia, can last from 1 minute to an hour. Bradykinesia may be followed by a sudden return of effectiveness, and the cycle may occur many times a day. Increasing the frequency of carbidopa; levodopa administration can minimize this effect in some patients by providing less dose-to-dose variation in CNS levodopa levels. Akinesia paradoxica or freezing is an abrupt hypotonic reaction resulting in a short-lived interruption of motor function. Akinesia paradoxic is most problematic if it occurs when the patient is walking, and may cause a fall. The etiology of the freezing episodes is not well understood. The response to levodopa decreases over time and many patients develop 'wearing-off' fluctuations of their symptoms. Wearing-off occurs during the day and consists of a gradual onset of parkinsonian symptoms before the next levodopa dose takes effect, usually 1—3 hours after the last dose.
 
Diaphoresis (excessive sweating) can be seen during carbidopa; levodopa therapy, although drenching sweats may also be a sign of Parkinson's disease. Lowering the levodopa dosage may be effective or, if medically appropriate, a beta-blocker may be used to decrease diaphoresis.
 
Psychiatric disturbances can occur with the administration of carbidopa; levodopa including agitation, anxiety, confusion, dizziness, headache, euphoria, fatigue, insomnia, malaise, memory loss, nervousness, nightmares, restlessness, and daytime somnolence. Mental depression also has been reported, as well as suicidal ideation, dementia, toxic delirium, hallucinations, paranoid delusion, psychosis, and hypomania. Nocturnal effects may be improved by reducing or stopping the last evening dose or giving the last dose earlier in the evening. Levodopa-induced psychiatric effects tend to be progressive and frequently a compromise must be reached between psychiatric effects and control of Parkinson symptoms.
 
Adverse GI effects are common in patients receiving carbidopa; levodopa and include nausea/vomiting, anorexia, and weight loss. Although levodopa-carbidopa can be administered with food to offset nausea, it should be noted that foods, especially high-protein or high-fat foods, could interfere with levodopa absorption. The incidence of nausea and vomiting is less with the combination of carbidopa; levodopa than with levodopa alone. Peripheral dopa decarboxylase is saturated by carbidopa at approximately 70—100 mg/day; patients receiving less than this amount are more likely to experience nausea and vomiting. Other reported GI adverse reactions include GI bleeding and GI/peptic ulcer.
 
Elevated hepatic enzymes including hyperbilirubinemia have been reported with carbidopa-levodopa. Abnormalities in blood urea nitrogen (BUN) and positive Coombs test have also been reported. Commonly, the levels of BUN, creatinine, and uric acid are lower during administration of carbidopa; levodopa combinations than with levodopa alone.
 
Orthostatic hypotension is a frequent adverse effect of levodopa therapy and occurs even with therapeutic dosages. Tolerance usually develops within a few months. Cardiac arrhythmias also can occur during carbidopa; levodopa therapy but are relatively infrequent. Flushing, hypertension, and palpitations have also been reported.
 
Saliva, sweat, or urine discoloration may be noted during carbidopa; levodopa therapy. The dark color (red, brown, or black) appears to be clinically insignificant, but may stain garments and other fabrics.
 
Neuroleptic malignant syndrome can develop following abrupt discontinuation or dose reduction of carbidopa-levodopa, especially in patients receiving antipsychotic agents concomitantly. Patients should be observed closely for manifestations of this symptom complex, which includes tachycardia, muscular rigidity, elevation of body temperature, mental changes, diaphoresis, tachypnea, and increases in serum creatinine levels. The early diagnosis of this condition is important for the appropriate management of these patients.
 
Other adverse effects associated with carbidopa; levodopa include bizarre breathing patterns, bullous lesions (including pemphigus-like reactions), hoarseness, episodic hyperventilation, increased libido, and increased nasal secretions. Urinary retention, polyuria, and urinary incontinence also have been reported. Agranulocytosis, leukopenia, non-hemolytic and hemolytic anemia, and thrombocytopenia although infrequent, can occur during carbidopa; levodopa therapy and warrant temporary discontinuance of the drug.
 
Some patients receiving medications that increase dopaminergic tone have reported intense and uncontrollable urges to gamble, increased sexual urges, or other intense urges. Causality due to dopaminergic agents has not been established; however, in some cases, the urges stopped after the dose was reduced or the drug was discontinued. Practitioners should inquire periodically about new or worsening urges in patients receiving carbidopa; levodopa. Likewise, patients should be instructed to report such changes while receiving carbidopa; levodopa. Dose reduction or discontinuation should be considered in those who experience these effects.

The onset of neuropathy could be determined in 13 of 19 patients. Most cases (12/19) were classified as subacute or chronic in onset. The peripheral neuropathy was most often characterized as sensory or sensorimotor. Electrodiagnostic results were most often consistent with an axonal polyneuropathy (15 of 16 patients), and one patient was classified as having a demyelinating neuropathy. There was insufficient information at the time of study to determine the potential role of vitamin deficiencies in the etiology of the peripheral neuropathy associated with the enteral suspension.

Description
  Text
  Levodopa is a dopamine precursor and carbidopa is a decarboxylase inhibitor. The combination of levodopa and carbidopa is used in the treatment of Parkinson disease and parkinsonism due to other causes to help allow for better motility. Carbidopa is added to levodopa to inhibit the peripheral destruction of levodopa, allowing more levodopa to be transported to the brain. Carbidopa-levodopa products are marketed in several strengths containing carbidopa and levodopa in ratios of either 1:10 or 1:4, respectively. The use of combination therapy leads to lower rates of side effects, such as nausea, while also decreasing the dose of levodopa required to reduce parkinsonian symptoms. The dosage of carbidopa-levodopa is highly individualized in attempt to balance the relief of symptoms and occurrence of side effects. Many times the dosage does not always correlate with the severity or duration of Parkinson disease. In clinical trials, patients with moderate to severe symptom fluctuations who received the extended-release preparation did not experience quantitatively significant reductions in 'off' time as compared to the immediate-release formulation; however, global symptom ratings improved. In patients without major fluctuations, the extended-release tablets provided the same therapeutic benefit as the immediate-release tablets but with less frequent dosing. The FDA first approved commercial products of levodopa-carbidopa prior to 1982; various dosage forms are now available to meet the needs of individual patients including extended-release tablets and capsules and orally disintegrating tablets (ODT). An enteral suspension is also available that is used for the treatment of motor fluctuations in patients with advanced Parkinson disease.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  The motor symptoms of Parkinson disease are related to destruction of dopaminergic neurons in the substantia nigra, leading to a depletion of dopamine in the corpus striatum. Administration of dopamine is ineffective in treatment of Parkinson disease, as it is unable to cross the blood-brain barrier. However, levodopa is the metabolic precursor of dopamine and crosses the blood-brain barrier and is converted into dopamine. When given orally, levodopa is rapidly decarboxylated to dopamine in the extracerebral tissues; only a small portion of levodopa is transported into the central nervous system and converted to dopamine. This leads to higher doses required for an adequate therapeutic effect; however, increased doses of levodopa have also been associated with adverse effects that may limit tolerability. To mitigate these effects, carbidopa is often given in combination with levodopa. Carbidopa is a noncompetitive decarboxylase inhibitor that, when administered with levodopa, inhibits the peripheral conversion of levodopa to dopamine, thereby increasing the CNS bioavailability of levodopa by approximately 75%. Carbidopa does not cross the blood-brain barrier. The addition of carbidopa allows administration of lower doses of levodopa and minimizes adverse reactions from levodopa such as nausea and vomiting. The carbidopa-levodopa combination also allows for a more rapid and even titration of levodopa dosage. With combination therapy, certain adverse effects (e.g., dyskinesias) will occur sooner and at lower levodopa dosages during therapy than with levodopa alone, as these adverse effects are centrally mediated.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Oral Route
    Description
    After oral administration, amino acid transport mechanisms carry levodopa across the membrane of the GI tract, with approximately 30% to 50% of the drug entering the circulation. As a result, it is thought that high concentrations of amino acids in the GI tract (i.e., a high-protein diet) can interfere with absorption of levodopa. There is evidence, however, to suggest that amino acid-levodopa transport competition is more likely to occur during levodopa active transport across the blood-brain barrier.
 
Immediate release tablets
At steady-state, the bioavailability of levodopa is 99%. The bioavailability of carbidopa at steady state is 99% from the regular-release tablet. The time to Cmax after a single dose is 0.5 hours. Following administration of the immediate-release tablets, the duration of action is 5 hours. However, the duration of effect is person- and disease-dependent; some people with advanced disease require every 2 hour dosing of this formulation.
 
Extended-release tablets
At steady-state, the bioavailability of levodopa in the extended-release (ER) tablet is 70% to 75% compared with regular-release tablets. The bioavailability of carbidopa at steady state from the ER tablet is 58%. The time to Cmax after a single dose is 2 hours for the ER tablet. The bioavailability and Cmax of levodopa after a single dose of a carbidopa 50 mg/levodopa 200 mg ER tablet increased by 50% and 25%, respectively, when administered with food. During clinical studies using similar daily doses, plasma levodopa concentrations with the ER tablet fluctuated in a narrower range than those with the immediate-release tablets. Plasma trough levels were higher with the ER tablet. The ER tablet releases levodopa and carbidopa over a 4 to 6-hour period; the apparent half-life of levodopa may be prolonged because of continuous absorption.
 
Extended-release capsules (Rytary)
At steady-state, the bioavailability of levodopa is 70% for the extended-release (ER) capsule relative to the immediate-release tablet. The bioavailability of carbidopa at steady state is 50% from the ER capsules. Maximum concentrations of the carbidopa component of the ER capsule occur at about 3 hours, while the levodopa component achieves an initial peak concentration in 1 hour. Plasma concentrations are maintained for about 4 to 5 hours before declining. In one study of healthy adults, a high-fat, high-calorie meal reduced the Cmax of the ER capsules by about 21% and increased the AUC about 13% for levodopa compared to administration in a fasted state. There may be a delay of 2 hours in the absorption of the ER capsules when taken with a high-fat, high-calorie meal, and absorption may be decreased by a high protein meal.
 
Extended-release capsules (Crexont)
The bioavailability of levodopa from this extended-release (ER) capsule formulation was approximately 88% to 99% relative to immediate-release tablets. The bioavailability of carbidopa was 103% to 123% relative to immediate-release tablets. For comparable doses, the ER capsules resulted in a levodopa Cmax that was 38% of the immediate-release formulation. Following an initial peak at 1 hour, plasma concentrations were maintained for 6 to 7 hours before declining. In people with Parkinson's disease, multiple-dose pharmacokinetics were comparable to single dose findings, indicating minimal accumulation of levodopa. Variation in levodopa peak to trough plasma concentrations at steady state was approximately 1.7 for the ER capsules compared to approximately 2.7 for the immediate-release product. In a clinical study of healthy adults, oral administration following a high-fat, high-calorie meal decreased both Cmax and AUC of carbidopa by approximately 64% and delayed Tmax by approximately 1 hour, while increasing levodopa Cmax and AUC by 19% and 18%, respectively, and delaying Tmax by approximately 2 hours, compared to administration in the fasted state. Oral administration by sprinkling the capsule contents on applesauce decreased carbidopa Cmax and AUC by approximately 13% and 9%, respectively, compared with the intact capsule administered in the fasted state, but did not significantly affect levodopa concentration time profiles.

    Description
    Intrajejunal Route (Duopa)
Following a 16-hour intrajejunal infusion of Duopa via a PEG-J tube, peak plasma levels of levodopa occur at approximately 2.5 hours. The gastric emptying rate does not influence the absorption since the enteral suspension is administered by continuous intestinal infusion. In a population pharmacokinetic analysis, the enteral suspension had comparable bioavailability to oral immediate-release carbidopa; levodopa (25/100 mg) tablets. The estimated bioavailability for levodopa from the enteral suspension relative to oral immediate-release carbidopa; levodopa tablets is 97%. The absorption of levodopa may be decreased in subjects on a high-protein diet due to competition between levodopa and certain amino acids for transportation across the gut wall.

  Text
  Carbidopa; levodopa is administered orally as immediate-release tablets, extended-release tablets, extended-release capsules, and as an enteral suspension delivered into the jejunum via a programmable enteral infusion pump. After administration, carbidopa; levodopa distributes throughout the body. Approximately 36% of carbidopa and 10% to 30% of levodopa bind to plasma proteins. Carbidopa does not penetrate the CNS. Instead, it inhibits the peripheral metabolism of levodopa, reducing the dosage of levodopa required to produce a given effect in the CNS by about 75%. When administered in combination, carbidopa extends the plasma half-life of levodopa and decreases levels of dopamine and homovanillic acid in the plasma and urine. Without carbidopa, less than 1% of levodopa would reach the CNS. The plasma half-life of levodopa is approximately 50 minutes. In the presence of carbidopa, it is increased to approximately 1.5 hours. The half-life of carbidopa is about 2 hours. Once levodopa enters the CNS, it is metabolized to dopamine by L-aromatic amino acid decarboxylase and 3-O-methyldopa. Peripheral dopa-decarboxylase may be saturated by carbidopa in other carbidopa/levodopa products at 70 to 100 mg/day, which produces equivalent exposure to 140 to 200 mg of carbidopa in the extended-release capsules. Carbidopa; levodopa is eliminated renally as dopamine metabolites, with levodopa metabolized to various metabolites via decarboxylation by dopa decarboxylase and O-methylation by catechol-O-methyltransferase. Carbidopa is metabolized to 2 main metabolites that are primarily eliminated in the urine as a glucuronide or as unchanged drug (30%).

    Oral Administration
    Description
    Many products may be taken with or without food. Follow the directions of the product prescribed.
A change in a patient's diet to foods that are high in protein may delay or impair the oral absorption of levodopa and may reduce efficacy. The Parkinson's Foundation states that most patients have no problem taking medications with meals, but some experience less benefit if they take carbidopa/levodopa with a stomach full of protein (including meats, cheeses and other dairy products). If nausea occurs, the products may be taken with a small non-protein snack, such as fruit or a cracker, to help.
Do not administer at the same time as a multivitamin containing iron or iron supplements as these may reduce levodopa absorption.
Advise patients that dark coloration (red, brown, or black) may appear in saliva, urine, or sweat. The color change is not harmful, but garments may become discolored.

Carbidopa and Levodopa enteral suspension (Duopa):
Administer into the jejunum via a percutaneous endoscopic gastrostomy with jejunal tube (PEG-J); the PEG-J tube is the procedure is performed by a gastroenterologist or other healthcare provider experienced in PEG-J tube placement.
The daily enteral suspension dose is delivered as a 16-hour infusion through a PEG-J for long-term administration. See below for recommended tubing sets for PEG-J administration.
The daily Duopa dose infusion has 3 parts:
a morning dose
On day 1, determine the total amount of levodopa in mg in the first dose of oral immediate-release carbidopa; levodopa that was taken on the previous day. Multiply the previous day morning oral dose by 0.8 and then divide by 20 mg/mL to convert the oral levodopa dose from mg to mL.
Add 3 mL to the morning dose to fill (prime) the intestinal tube to obtain the total morning dose.
Program the pump to deliver the total morning dose over 10 to 30 minutes.
a continuous rate dose
On day 1, determine the total amount of levodopa that was received from oral immediate-release carbidopa; levodopa doses throughout the previous day (16 waking hours), in mg, excluding the morning dose and doses taken at night. Divide the result by 20 mg/mL to convert mg to mL and then divide the volume by 16 hours to obtain the hourly infusion rate (mL/hour).
extra doses
At the end of the daily 16-hour administration period for the enteral suspension, the PEG-J should be disconnected from the pump and flushed with room temperature potable water with a syringe as directed. Do not use hot water as this may burn the patient's intestine.
After the daily 16-hour enteral infusion, the usual nighttime dose of oral carbidopa-levodopa tablets will be taken as prescribed.
For short-term, temporary administration prior to the PEG-J tube placement only, treatment may be initiated by a nasojejunal tube with observation of the patient's clinical response. See below for the recommended tubing sets for nasojejunal administration.
 
Duopa Cassettes:

Enteral infusion interruption or discontinuation:
Avoid sudden discontinuation or rapid dose reduction of carbidopa; levodopa. The enteral dose should be tapered or the patient switched to immediate-release dosage forms for oral use.
The patient should keep a supply of immediate-release tablets on hand in case the enteral infusion is interrupted or halted.
If the patient anticipates disconnecting the pump for a short period of time (less than 2 hours such as to swim, shower, or short medical procedure), no supplemental oral medication is needed, but the patient may be advised to take an extra-dose of Duopa before disconnecting. Instruct the patient to stop the continuous rate, turn off the pump, clamp the cassette tube, disconnect the tubing, and replace the red cap on the cassette tube. The Duopa cassette can remain attached to the pump until the tubing is reconnected.
If a prolonged (e.g., more than 2 hours) interruption of therapy occurs, advise the patient to contact their healthcare provider and to take oral carbidopa-levodopa until the patient is able to resume the Duopa enteral infusion.
 
Pharmacy Thawing and Storage instructions for Duopa:
The Duopa product will arrive to the pharmacy frozen and the cartons should be stored in the freezer, until ready to thaw, at -20 degrees C (-4 degrees F).
Assign a 12-week "Use By" date based on the time the cartons are put into the refrigerator to thaw.
Fully thaw the enteral suspension in the refrigerator prior to dispensing, between 36 degrees to 46 degrees F (2 to 8 degrees C). Do not re-freeze.
In order to ensure controlled thawing, take the cartons containing the seven individual cassettes out of the transport box and separate the cartons from each other.
Thawing may take up to 96 hours when the cartons are taken out of the transport box.
Once the product has thawed, the individual cartons may be packed in a closer configuration within the refrigerator.
Any unused medication will expire after the 12-week "Use By" date.
