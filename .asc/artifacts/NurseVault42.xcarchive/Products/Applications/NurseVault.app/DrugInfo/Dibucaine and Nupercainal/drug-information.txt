Drug Class: Dibucaine is a topical amide-type local anesthetic, similar to lidocaine.

Routes: topical; Common adult doses: 4 applications/day topically to the anorectal area.

Common Side Effects (excluding routine GI upset): anaphylactic shock, pruritus, rash, skin irritation, urticaria, confusion, drowsiness, hypotension

In an Overdose: No overdose/antidote information is listed in this file.

Key Nursing Considerations: Contraindications/precautions: This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components. Dibucaine is an amide local anesthetic and use is contraindicated in people with a known amide local anesthetic hypersensitivity.

    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    There is no available data on the use of dibucaine during pregnancy. In general, topical local anesthetics are not readily absorbed through intact skin.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    When applied rectally, dibucaine is compatible with breast-feeding. It is unknown whether dibucaine is excreted in human breast milk. Ensure the breastfed child will not come into direct contact with the area of application. Specifically, dibucaine should not be applied to the nipple or any part of the chest, as deaths due to dibucaine ingestion have been reported in the pediatric population.

Adverse Reactions
  Headline
  Adverse Reactions
  Adverse Reactions
    Headline
    anaphylactic shock, pruritus, rash, skin irritation, urticaria
    General Information
    Topical administration of dibucaine may cause skin irritation, such as transient stinging or burning. Allergic reactions may occur to local amide anesthetics as well. If rash, irritation, or bleeding occur at the site of application, instruct the patient to discontinue use and consult a physician for evaluation. More severe allergic reactions to local anesthetics may manifest as urticaria, bronchospasm or dyspnea, pruritus, anaphylactic shock or other reactions.

Description
  Text
  Dibucaine is a topical amide-type local anesthetic, similar to lidocaine. Available as a nonprescription (OTC) ointment, it is used for temporary relief of pain and itching associated with hemorrhoids and anorectal disorders.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Like other topical amide anesthetics, dibucaine interferes with the transmission of impulses along sensory nerve fibers to relieve pain, itching, and irritation. Local anesthetics block both the initiation and conduction of nerve impulses from sensory nerves by decreasing the permeability of the neuronal membrane to sodium ions. This reversibly stabilizes the membrane and inhibits depolarization, resulting in the failure of a propagated action potential and subsequent conduction blockade.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Topical Route
    Description
    Dibucaine is poorly absorbed from intact skin, but is readily absorbed from mucous membranes or abraded skin. The rate and extent of absorption depends upon the site of application, duration of exposure, concentration, and total dosage. The onset of action occurs within 15 minutes and lasts for a duration of 2—4 hours.
