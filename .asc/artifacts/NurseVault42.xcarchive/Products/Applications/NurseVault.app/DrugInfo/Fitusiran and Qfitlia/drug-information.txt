Drug Class: Fitusiran is an antithrombin-directed small interfering ribonucleic acid (siRNA) indicated for routine prophylaxis to prevent or reduce the frequency of bleeding episodes in individuals with hemophilia A or B, with or without factor VIII or IX inhibitors.

Routes: injection (IV/IM/SC); Common adult doses: 50 mg/month subcutaneously.

Common Side Effects (excluding routine GI upset): cholangitis, cholecystitis, cholelithiasis, gallbladder disease, pancreatitis, cough, headache, infection, pharyngitis, bleeding, erythema, hematoma, injection site reaction, pruritus, rash, skin atrophy, skin discoloration

In an Overdose: No overdose/antidote information is listed in this file.

Key Nursing Considerations: Contraindications/precautions: Treatment with fitusiran is associated with an increased occurrence of acute and recurrent gallbladder disease, including cholelithiasis and cholecystitis. This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components.

Indication Specific Dosing
  General Information
  General Dosing Information
Measure antithrombin activity before initiating fitusiran therapy. Do not initiate if antithrombin activity is less than 60%.
After fitusiran is initiated, prior clotting factor concentrates (CFC) or bypassing agent (BPA) prophylaxis may continue for the first 7 days of treatment. Discontinue CFC or BPA prophylaxis no later than 7 days after the initial fitusiran dose.

    Headline
    Child-Pugh class A, Child-Pugh class B, Child-Pugh class C, hepatic failure
    General Information
    Avoid fitusiran use in individuals with established Child-Pugh class A, Child-Pugh class B, and Child-Pugh class C hepatic impairment or hepatic failure.
    Headline
    thromboembolism, thrombophilia, venous catheter
    General Information
    Serious thrombotic events have been reported in fitusiran-treated individuals. The risk of thrombosis is greater in individuals with persistent antithrombin activity less than 15%, with comorbidities that predispose to thromboembolism, when bleed management guidelines are not followed in the postoperative setting, when there is an indwelling venous catheter, and with use of the 80 mg once monthly dose. Subjects with established thrombophilia or a history of thrombosis were generally excluded from studies with fitusiran. In clinical studies, subjects with hemophilia A or B with or without inhibitors have undergone both major and minor surgical procedures without discontinuing fitusiran prophylaxis. Utilize bleed management guidelines during the perioperative period for hemostatic management. The 80 mg once monthly dose is not approved or recommended for use. Treatment of breakthrough bleeding episodes with (CFC) or bypassing agent (BPA) at a dose greater or more frequent than recommended may also increase thrombotic risk. The decision to utilize higher dosing regimens of CFC or BPA in the setting of inadequate hemostasis requires an assessment of the benefits and risks and close clinical monitoring. Monitor antithrombin (AT) activity using an FDA-cleared test and target AT activity of 15% to 35% to reduce the risk of thrombosis.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Use fitusiran during pregnancy only if the potential benefit justifies the potential risks, including those to the fetus. There are no available data on fitusiran use in pregnancy to evaluate for a drug-associated risk of major birth defects, miscarriage, or other adverse maternal or fetal outcomes. It is not known whether fitusiran can cause fetal harm when administered during pregnancy or can affect reproduction capacity. Reproduction studies in pregnant animals have not been conducted with fitusiran.
    Headline

  Adverse Reactions
    Headline
    abdominal pain, cholangitis, cholecystitis, cholelithiasis, dyspepsia, gallbladder disease, pancreatitis
    General Information
    Fitusiran is associated with an increased occurrence of acute and recurrent gallbladder disease, including cholelithiasis and cholecystitis. In clinical trials of subjects receiving fitusiran prophylaxis using the antithrombin-based dose regimen that targeted antithrombin (AT) activity of 15% to 35% (n = 286), 3.8% experienced gallbladder events and 0.3% (1 subject) underwent cholecystectomy. All but the single subject who underwent cholecystectomy resumed fitusiran after surgery. Of subjects receiving fitusiran at a fixed-dose (non-AT-based dose) once monthly regimen (n = 270), 17% experienced gallbladder events and 4% underwent a cholecystectomy; fitusiran at a fixed-dose is not approved or recommended. A single subject who started on fitusiran fixed dosing experienced cholangitis and pancreatitis caused by gallstone disease more than a year after cholecystectomy while receiving fitusiran using the anti-thrombin based dose regimen. Subjects diagnosed with acute or recurrent gallbladder disease most commonly presented with epigastric pain, generalized abdominal pain, indigestion, nausea, and/or vomiting. If gallbladder disease is suspected, appropriate imaging and clinical follow-up are indicated. Consider interruption or discontinuation of fitusiran if gallbladder disease occurs. Abdominal pain and dyspepsia were also reported with fitusiran prophylaxis using the antithrombin-based dose regimen in less than 5% of subjects in clinical trials.

    elevated hepatic enzymes, hepatotoxicity
    General Information
    Elevated hepatic enzymes have been reported with fitusiran. In clinical trials of subjects receiving fitusiran prophylaxis using the antithrombin-based dose regimen, 3.4% of subjects had at least a single ALT value more than 3 times the upper limit of normal (ULN) with a median onset of 89 days after initial dosing (range 15 to 768 days). Hepatotoxicity, including increased ALT, increased AST, and liver injury, was reported in 8% of subjects receiving fitusiran prophylaxis using the antithrombin-based dose regimen. In 2 randomized clinical trials of subjects receiving a fixed-dose of fitusiran 80 mg once monthly, serum alanine aminotransferase (ALT) and aspartate aminotransferase (AST) elevations more than 3 times ULN occurred in 32% of subjects with hemophilia with inhibitors and 18% of subjects with hemophilia without inhibitors. A case of moderate hepatic injury, defined as ALT elevation more than 300 U/L and total serum bilirubin more than 3 mg/dL, was reported after a single dose and continued to rise with repeated dosing. Liver function tests returned to normal with drug discontinuation. The fitusiran 80 mg once monthly dose is not approved or recommended for use. Obtain baseline liver function tests, including AST, ALT, and total bilirubin, prior to initiating fitusiran therapy, monthly for at least the first 6 months of use, monthly for at least 6 months after a dose increase, and periodically as clinically indicated. If new or worsening liver function test abnormalities occur, perform appropriate diagnostic evaluations; initiate medical management as appropriate, and monitor laboratory parameters until they return to baseline. If ALT or AST elevations more than 5 times ULN occur, interrupt fitusiran therapy. Consider the risks and benefits of resuming fitusiran prophylaxis after resolution of transaminase elevations. If therapy is restarted and ALT or AST elevations more than 5 times the ULN reoccur or the individual experiences jaundice (total bilirubin 2.5 mg/dL or more) thought to be from hepatotoxicity with other causes of liver test elevation ruled out, permanently discontinue fitusiran.

    General Information
    Antibody formation has been reported with fitusiran. Description
  Text
  Fitusiran is an antithrombin-directed small interfering ribonucleic acid (siRNA) indicated for routine prophylaxis to prevent or reduce the frequency of bleeding episodes in individuals with hemophilia A or B, with or without factor VIII or IX inhibitors. Fitusiran is a double-stranded siRNA that causes degradation of antithrombin (AT) messenger RNA (mRNA) through RNA interference, causing a reduction in plasma AT concentrations. A decrease in plasma AT concentrations results in an increase in thrombin generation. In clinical studies, subjects with hemophilia A or B with or without inhibitors have undergone both major and minor surgical procedures without discontinuing fitusiran prophylaxis. Serious thrombotic events, acute and recurrent gallbladder disease, and hepatotoxicity have been reported with fitusiran. Fitusiran was FDA-approved in March 2025.

Pharmacodynamics
In clinical studies of subjects with hemophilia receiving fitusiran, the primary pharmacodynamic measure was plasma AT activity. Lower AT activity concentrations were associated with lower annualized bleeding rates; however, persistent AT activity of less than 15% is a risk factor for thrombotic events. The mean AT activity on the antithrombin-based dose regimen (AT-DR) was 24%. More than 99% of subjects reached steady-state AT concentrations (defined as less than 10% variability among consecutive measures) within 23 weeks after initiation of fitusiran dosing or dose escalation or de-escalation. The duration of persistent AT activity less than 60% (non-negligible impact of fitusiran on coagulation) after fitusiran treatment discontinuation was estimated to be approximately 6 months, after which standard doses of clotting factor concentrates/bypassing agent may be administered. In subjects with AT activity less than 15%, the time required prior to initiation of a lower dose was estimated to be 12 weeks to maximize the AT activity recovery to the target range of 15% to 35%. The extent of AT reduction with fitusiran did not differ between subjects with hemophilia A or B, with or without inhibitors.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Subcutaneous Route
    Description
    After administration of fitusiran 20 mg subcutaneously, the Cmax was 34.4 nanograms/mL and the AUC was 491 nanograms x hour/mL. The Cmax and AUC after administration of fitusiran 50 mg were 84.1 nanograms/mL and 1,290 nanograms x hour/mL, respectively. Fitusiran exhibited a dose proportional increase in plasma exposure with no accumulation observed after multiple once monthly doses. The median Tmax was 2.88 hours and 3.78 hours for fitusiran 20 and 50 mg, respectively.

  Text
  Fitusiran is administered subcutaneously. Fitusiran is 96.6% protein bound at clinically relevant exposures (0.5 mcg/mL). After administration of a fitusiran 20 and 50 mg dose, the mean Vss/F is 431 L/kg and 570 L/kg, respectively. Fitusiran distributes primarily to the liver and is metabolized by endo- and exonucleases to oligonucleotides of varying lengths, which are further metabolized to oligonucleotides of shorter lengths. Fitusiran pharmacodynamics is driven by liver pharmacokinetics rather than plasma pharmacokinetics. The primary metabolite is AS(N-1)3'. After administration of a fitusiran 50 mg dose, 14.6% of the dose is recovered unchanged in the urine over 24 hours. The mean apparent clearance and half-life of fitusiran after a 20 and 50 mg dose is 41.9 L/hour and 5.57 hours and 50.8 L/hour and 7.98 hours, respectively.

Administration
  General Information
  For storage information, see the specific product information within the How Supplied section.
  Route Specific Administrations
    Administration Details
      Name
      Subcutaneous Administration
      Description
      After proper training on the preparation and administration of fitusiran, an individual may self-inject fitusiran or the individual's caregiver may administer fitusiran.
In children aged 12 to 17 years, it is recommended that fitusiran be administered by or under the supervision of an adult.
If fitusiran is stored in the refrigerator, allow it to reach room temperature for at least 30 minutes. Do not warm by using a heat source or place in direct sunlight.
Do not shake the prefilled pen or single-dose vial at any time.
Choose an injection site on the abdomen (at least 2 inches away from the navel) or thigh. A caregiver can also inject fitusiran in the outer area of the individual's upper arm.
Clean the injection site with an alcohol swab and allow the site to dry.
Administer by subcutaneous injection.
Do not inject fitusiran into skin that is tender, damaged, bruised, or scarred. Do not inject into a vein or through clothes.
Do not rub the skin after injection.
Dispose of the used prefilled pen or vial, needle, and syringe in an appropriate sharps disposal container.
 
Prefilled Pen
Do not use the prefilled pen if the blue cap is missing or not securely attached. Do not use the prefilled pen if the window is yellow before use; a yellow window means the prefilled pen has been used.
The plunger stopper in the middle of the window is normal. It is normal to see air bubbles in the prefilled pen.
Remove the blue cap by pulling it straight off. Do not twist the blue cap off.
Do not touch the orange needle cover; the needle is active.
Place the orange needle cover on the skin at the clean injection site at a 90-degree angle.
Press down and hold the prefilled pen firmly in place until the orange needle cover is no longer visible. The pen will "click" when the injection starts and the window will start to turn yellow. Continue pressing the prefilled pen against the skin until the window turns completely yellow. A second "click" may be heard, indicating the injection is complete.
Pull straight up to remove the prefilled pen from the skin; the orange needle cover will cover the needle.
Storage: Do not freeze. The prefilled pen may be stored at room temperature, 59 to 86 degrees F (15 to 30 degrees C) for a single period of up to 3 months. Do not return the prefilled pen to the refrigerator after storing at room temperature. Discard any unused product remaining in the prefilled pen.
 
Single-Dose Vial
Remove the protective cap from the vial, but do not remove the stopper.
Wipe the rubber stopper of the vial with an alcohol wipe and allow it to dry.
It is recommended to use a sterile 1 mL Luer Lock syringe (polypropylene or polycarbonate) and a sterile 27 gauge, one-half inch (13 mm) Luer Lock needle to withdraw and inject fitusiran solution subcutaneously from the single-dose vial.
Attach the needle to the syringe and remove the needle cap. Do not touch the needle. Do not recap the needle.
Push the needle through the center of the rubber vial stopper. Leave the needle in the vial, and turn both the syringe and vial upside down so that the vial is on top.
Hold the syringe and vial firmly with 1 hand, ensuring the tip of the needle is in the solution.
With the other hand, pull back on the plunger rod to withdraw slightly more than the prescribed dose into the syringe.
For a 20 mg dose, withdraw slightly more than 0.2 mL.
For a 10 mg dose, withdraw slightly more than 0.1 mL.
If air bubbles are seen in the syringe, push the plunger rod up to push the bubbles out of the syringe while maintaining the vial in an inverted position.
Adjust the plunger to the prescribed dose.
For a 20 mg dose, push the plunger rod to 0.2 mL.
For a 10 mg dose, push the plunger rod to 0.1 mL.
Remove the needle from the vial.
Gently pinch a fold of skin at the clean injection site and insert the needle at a 45- to 90-degree angle.
Relax the skin pinch and slowly push the plunger rod all the way to the bottom of the syringe to inject all the solution.
Gently pull the needle and syringe from the injection site. Do not recap the needle.
Storage: Do not freeze. Do not return the vial to the refrigerator after storing at room temperature. Discard any unused product remaining in the vial.

    Injectable Administration
    Description
    Visually inspect parenteral products for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration whenever solution and container permit. Fitusiran is a clear, colorless to pale yellow solution. Do not use it if the solution is discolored or cloudy, or if it contains visible flakes or particles. Do not use fitusiran if it has been dropped or damaged.
Missed dose: Administer a missed dose as soon as possible and then resume the usual dosing schedule of once every month or once every 2 months, as applicable, from the last dose.
