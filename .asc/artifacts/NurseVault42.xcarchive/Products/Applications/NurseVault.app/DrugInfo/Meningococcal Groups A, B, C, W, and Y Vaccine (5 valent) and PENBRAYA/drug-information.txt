  General Dosing Information
The meningococcal groups A,B,C,W, and Y vaccine (5 valent) may be administered when both quadrivalent (serogroups A,C,W, and Y) and serogroup B meningococcal (MenB) vaccines are indicated at the same visit. MenB vaccines from different manufacturers are not interchangeable.
If Penbraya is used, complete the MenB series with Trumenba.
If Penmenvy is used, complete the MenB series with Bexsero.

    Headline
    immunosuppression
    General Information
    Patients with significant immunosuppression may not have an adequate antibody response to the meningococcal groups A, B, C, W, and Y vaccine. Immunosuppressed persons may include patients with severe combined immunodeficiency (SCID); hypogammaglobulinemia; agammaglobulinemia; altered immune states due to generalized neoplastic disease or human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) infection; complement deficiencies; an immune system compromised by radiation therapy or drug therapy (e.g., chemotherapy or corticosteroid therapy with greater than physiologic doses); or patients receiving treatment that inhibits terminal complement activation (e.g. eculizumab). Patients with certain complement deficiencies and those receiving treatment that inhibits terminal complement activation are at an increased risk for disease cased by N. meningitidis, even if they develop antibodies after vaccination. Short-term (less than 2 weeks) corticosteroid therapy or intra-articular, bursal, or tendon injections with corticosteroids should not be immunosuppressive.
    Headline
    acute inflammatory demyelinating polyneuropathy
    General Information
    Consider the risks and benefits of administering meningococcal groups A, B, C, W, and Y vaccine to individuals with a history of acute inflammatory demyelinating polyneuropathy. A temporal relationship between the administration of a meningococcal quadrivalent conjugate vaccine and acute inflammatory demyelinating polyneuropathy has been reported.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    There are no adequate and well-controlled studies on the use of meningococcal group A, B, C, W, and Y vaccine (5-valent) during human pregnancy. It is not known if the vaccine can cause fetal harm. In animal studies, female rabbits given a dose on 5 occasions (3 times prior to mating and 2 times during gestation), revealed no vaccine-related effects on fetal or postnatal development. Individuals who received this meningococcal group A, B, C, W, and Y vaccine (5-valent, Penbraya) during pregnancy are encouraged to enroll in a pregnancy registry by calling 1-877-390-2953.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information

Consider the benefits of breast-feeding, the patient's clinical need for vaccination, and any potential adverse effects on the breast-fed child from the vaccine or from the underlying maternal medical condition.

    General Information
    Injection site reaction, including pain at injection site (74% to 92%) was reported in patients receiving meningococcal group A, B, C, W, and Y vaccine during clinical trials. Injection site redness, erythema, or swelling was reported in 4% to 13% of patients receiving Penmenvy and 24% to 26% of patients receiving Penbraya. Hypersensitivity (0.1%) and lymphadenopathy (0.2%) were reported as unsolicited adverse events. An adolescent patient reported the appearance of petechiae on 2 different occasions after Penmenvy administration (7 days after dose 1 and 18 days after dose 2) and was subsequently diagnosed with a connective tissue disorder 44 days after the receipt of the second dose. Allergic reactions, anaphylactoid reactions, blisters at or around the injection site, ocular inflammation or eye swelling, injection site nodules, rash, skin exfoliation, swelling of the vaccinated limb, and cellulitis at the injection site have been reported with postmarketing use of components of the meningococcal group A, B, C, W, and Y vaccine.

Description
  Text
  The pentavalent meningococcal (meningococcal groups A, B, C, W, and Y) vaccine is indicated for active immunization to prevent invasive disease caused by Neisseria meningitidis serogroups A, B, C, W, and Y. Meningococcal disease is a result of an invasive infection by N. meningitidis; the 5 most common N. meningitidis serogroups responsible for meningococcal disease are A, B, C, W, and Y. Although relatively uncommon in the U.S., meningococcal disease is fatal in approximately 10% to 15% of patients despite appropriate antibiotic therapy, with an additional 10% to 20% experiencing permanent complications (brain damage and limb loss). Different formulations of the pentavalent meningococcal vaccine are commercially available. Each formulation includes two components: a lyophilized component containing polysaccharides of serotypes A, C, W and Y adsorbed to a conjugate protein and a liquid suspension containing unique sets of proteins derived from serotype B. Penbraya (MenACWY-TT/MenB-fHbp) contains N. meningiditis serogroup A, C, W, and Y components linked to a tetanus toxoid conjugate and 2 variants from the serogroup B. Penmenvy (MenACWY-CRM/MenB-4C) contains N. meningiditis serogroup A, C, W, and Y components linked to a diptheria protein. It also includes N. meningiditis proteins Neisseria adhesin A (NadA), Neisserial Heparin Binding Antigen (NHBA), factor H binding protein (fHbp), and outer membrane vesicles (OMV). Different manufacturers' serogroup B-containing vaccines are not interchangeable; subsequent doses in the series should be from the same manufacturer. Penbraya was FDA-approved in October 2023 and Penmenvy was FDA approved in February 2025.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Protection against invasive meningococcal disease is achieved by complement-mediated antibody-dependent killing of N. meningitidis. Vaccination does not ensure immunity. Vaccination with meningococcal groups A, B, C, W, and Y vaccine induces the production of bactericidal antibodies specific to the capsular polysaccharides of N. meningitidis serogroups A, C, W, and Y. The serogroup b component of Penbraya induces antibodies specific to factor H binding protein (fHbp) subfamily A and B variants of N. meningitidis group B. The serogroup b component of Penmenvy induces antibodies to Neisseria adhesin A (NadA), Neisserial Heparin Binding Antigen (NHBA), fHbp, and outer membrane vesicles (OMV).

Pharmacokinetics
  Text
  The meningococcal groups A, B, C, W, and Y vaccine is administered intramuscularly. The distribution, metabolism, and excretion of the meningococcal groups A, B, C, W, and Y vaccine have not been well defined.

Advise individuals on the benefits and risks of the vaccine. Provide a copy of the Vaccine Information Statement (VIS).
Record manufacturer, lot number, administration date, and the name and address of the person administering the vaccine.
Ensure supportive care, including epinephrine 1 mg/mL injection, is readily accessible in the event of a clinically significant hypersensitivity reaction.
Complete a Vaccine Adverse Event Reporting System (VAERS) report if adverse events have been identified. The VAERS toll-free information line is 1-800-822-7967 and email address is info@vaers.org.

      Penbraya
Meningococcal groups A, B, C, W, and Y vaccine is supplied in a kit that includes a vial of lyophilized MenACWY component, a prefilled syringe containing the MenB component, and a vial adapter.
Preparation of lyophilized MenACWY component
Remove the plastic flip-off cap from the vial of Lyophilized MenACWY Component.
Without removing the vial adapter from its packaging, peel off the top cover.
Keeping the vial adapter in the packaging, position the adapter vertically over the center of the vial so the adapter spike aligns with the center of the vial's rubber stopper.
Push the vial adapter straight down.
Remove the vial adapter packaging.
Resuspension of MenB component
Shake the syringe containing the MenB component vigorously to obtain a white homogeneous suspension. Do not use if the contents cannot be resuspended.
Hold the syringe only by the Luer lock adapter.
Twist to remove the syringe cap.
Connect the syringe to the vial adapter by turning the Luer lock.
Reconstitution of lyophilized MenACWY component with MenB component
Inject the entire contents of the syringe containing the MenB component into the vial.
Do not remove the empty syringe.
Holding the plunger rod down, gently swirl the vial in a circular motion until the powder is completely dissolved (less than 1 minute).
Invert the vial with the vial adapter and syringe still attached.
Draw out the entire contents of the vial slowly into the syringe to ensure 0.5 mL dose. Do not pull the plunger rod out.
Twist to disconnect the syringe from the vial adapter.
Attach a sterile needle suitable for IM injection to the syringe.
Inspect the syringe to verify the vaccine is a homogeneous white suspension. If the vaccine is not a homogeneous suspension, shake to resuspend prior to administration.
Storage: Store reconstituted vaccine between 2 and 30 degrees C (36 and 86 degrees F) if not administered right away. Discard reconstituted vaccine if not used within 4 hours. Do not freeze.
 
Penmenvy
Meningococcal groups A, B, C, W, and Y vaccine is supplied in cartons containing vials of lyophilized MenACWY component and prefilled TIP-LOK (Luer Lock) syringes containing the MenB component.
Resuspension of MenB Component
Invert the prefilled syringe of the MenB component multiple times to form a homogeneous suspension. Do not use if the contents cannot be resuspended.
Hold the syringe by the barrel and unscrew the cap by twisting counterclockwise.
Connect the hub of the sterile needle to the Luer Lock Adaptor of the prefilled syringe of the MenB component and rotate a quarter turn clockwise until it locks.
Reconstitution of lyophilized MenACWY component with MenB component
Cleanse the stopper of the vial containing lyophilized MenACWY Component.
Slowly transfer the entire contents of the syringe containing the MenB component into the vial containing the lyophilized MenACWY component.
Do not remove the empty syringe.
Gently swirl the vial in a circular motion until the powder is completely dissolved. Do not invert the vial or shake vigorously.
After reconstitution, invert the vial and slowly draw out the entire contents of the vial into the syringe to ensure 0.5 mL dose.
Inspect the syringe to verify the vaccine is a homogeneous white suspension.
Use immediately after reconstitution.
 
Intramuscular Injection
After preparation of the dose, administer immediately as an intramuscular injection.
