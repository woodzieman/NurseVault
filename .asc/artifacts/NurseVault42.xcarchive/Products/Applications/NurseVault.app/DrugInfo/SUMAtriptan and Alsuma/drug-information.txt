Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  General Information
  Sumatriptan is contraindicated in patients with hypersensitivity to sumatriptan or any components of the commercially available products.
 
Rare, but serious adverse cardiac effects, including heart attacks, life-threatening disturbances of cardiac rhythm, and death have been reported within a few hours of receiving 5-HT1 agonists. Many of these patients had concurrent cardiovascular risk factors; therefore, it is difficult to assess causality. Sumatriptan and other 5-HT agonists may cause coronary vasospasm, and therefore are contraindicated in patients with known or suspected coronary artery disease (CAD), angina pectoris, vasospastic angina such as Prinzmetal's variant angina, arteriosclerosis, silent myocardial ischemia, acute myocardial infarction, history of myocardial infarction, or other significant cardiac disease. Patients with CAD risk factors (e.g., high blood pressure, diabetes mellitus, hypercholesterolemia, obesity, tobacco smoking, family history of CAD, female with surgical or physiological menopause, or male > 40 years old) should not be given sumatriptan unless a cardiac evaluation determines they are reasonably free of CAD, myocardial ischemia or other significant cardiac disease. Patients who are long-term users of sumatriptan and who have or acquire risk factors predictive of CAD should undergo periodic cardiac evaluation. For patients with risk factors predictive of CAD who are determined to have a satisfactory cardiac evaluation, the first dose of sumatriptan should be given in a controlled setting such as a clinic or physician's office. ECG monitoring is strongly encouraged due to the possibility of asymptomatic cardiac ischemia during the time immediately following sumatriptan administration in patients with risk factors. In addition, patients with cardiac arrhythmias should not receive sumatriptan as rhythm disturbances have been reported with the use of 5-HT1 agonists. Patients with symptomatic Wolff-Parkinson-White syndrome or cardiac arrhythmias associated with other cardiac accessory conduction pathway disorders should not receive sumatriptan because serious cardiac events have been reported within a few hours of receiving 5-HT1 agonists. Additionally, inappropriate concomitant use of eletriptan and sumatriptan may have contributed to a fatal myocardial infarction in a patient with known cardiovascular risk factors; however, causality was not definitively determined.
 
Sumatriptan is contraindicated in patients with uncontrolled hypertension. Sumatriptan can produce a significant increase in blood pressure in patients with and without a history of hypertension. Patients with controlled hypertension may experience mild and transient elevations in blood pressure.
 
Sumatriptan is contraindicated in patients with cerebrovascular disease (i.e., stroke or transient ischemic attacks) and should be avoided in the presence of intracranial bleeding due to the vasospastic effects of 5-HT agonists. While stroke, cerebral hemorrhage, and related fatalities have been reported following administration of 5-HT1 agonists, these events may have been present prior to administration of the drug, and the drug was mistakenly given in response to the cerebrovascular symptoms. However, patients with migraines may be at an increased risk for cerebrovascular events (e.g., stroke, hemorrhage).
 
Sumatriptan is contraindicated in basilar/hemiplegic migraine because safety and efficacy have not been established. Safety and efficacy have not been established in cluster headaches. Sumatriptan should not be administered if the headache is atypical for the patient. Sumatriptan is not recommended for long-term prophylaxis of migraine headaches.
 
Intravenous administration of sumatriptan should be avoided because of the potential to cause vasospasm.
 
Sumatriptan is classified as FDA pregnancy risk category C. To date, no evidence of teratogenicity in humans has been found; however, the use of sumatriptan during pregnancy is not recommended unless the benefits outweigh the risks. A prospective report has suggested no increased risk of major birth defects with the use of sumatriptan during the first trimester. In this study, there was no major difference in the rates of live births, spontaneous abortions, therapeutic abortions or major birth defects between the group of women receiving sumatriptan and disease-matched controls and nonteratogen controls.
 
Sumatriptan is excreted into the breast milk of humans; however, the American Academy of Pediatrics classifies sumatriptan as compatible with breast-feeding.. Five women who had been lactating for an average of 22 weeks received a single 6 mg SQ dose of sumatriptan. Drug concentrations were measured in milk and plasma over an 8 hour period following drug administration. The mean milk:plasma ratio of sumatriptan was 4.9 (95% CI 4.1—5.7), indicating a significant transfer of sumatriptan into the milk compartment. However, only 0.24% (14 mcg; CI 6.1—22.7 mcg) of the 6 mg sumatriptan dose was recovered in the milk. Assuming oral bioavailability of sumatriptan in infants is similar to adults (mean 14%), the weight-adjusted infant dose is roughly 0.49%. Allowance for reduced clearance in the infant predicts an infant exposure varying from 4.9% in the very premature neonate to 0.7% in a 30 week old infant (mean 3.5%). Since sumatriptan is typically given as a single dose to the mother and has low oral bioavailability in the infant, the low level of excretion in breast milk suggests that breast feeding following its use will not pose a significant risk to the nursing infant. Pumping or expressing breast milk and discarding it up to 8 hours after a dose would help to avoid any potential infant exposure to sumatriptan.
 
All formulations of sumatriptan are contraindicated in patients with severe hepatic disease or impairment. Sumatriptan is metabolized significantly by the liver. The bioavailability and peak serum concentrations can increase markedly in patients with hepatic disease. The oral formulation should be used cautiously in those with mild to moderate hepatic impairment; dosage adjustments are recommended if treatment with the oral formulation is deemed necessary.
 
Sumatriptan is renally excreted; therefore, renal impairment may contribute to an increase in bioavailability. Sumatriptan should be used with caution in patients with renal impairment, including renal disease or renal failure.
 
Sumatriptan is not approved in children under 18 years for the treatment of migraine and the manufacturer does not recommend its use. However, there are studies of sumatriptan for the treatment of migraine in children (See Dosage). Serious post-marketing adverse events have been reported in the pediatric population (see Adverse Reactions) after use of subcutaneous, oral, and/or intranasal sumatriptan.
 
Use of sumatriptan in elderly patients is not recommended. The elderly are more likely to have decreased hepatic function, more pronounced blood pressure increases, and are at a higher risk for coronary artery disease (CAD).
 
Patients should be warned about the possibility of sedation while taking sumatriptan and to use caution when driving or operating machinery.
 
Sumatriptan is contraindicated in patients with peripheral vascular disease including ischemic bowel disease (ischemic colitis) and Raynaud's disease. Sumatriptan may cause vasospastic reactions leading to vascular and colonic ischemia with abdominal pain and bloody diarrhea.
 
There have been rare reports of seizure following administration of sumatriptan. Sumatriptan should be used with caution in patients with a history of seizures (seizure disorder) or structural brain lesions that lower their seizure threshold.
 
Rare reports of transient and permanent blindness, including significant partial vision loss have been reported with the use of sumatriptan. Visual disturbance may be a part of any type of migraine headache; therefore, causality cannot be determined.
 
Sumatriptan is contraindicated in patients currently receiving MAOI therapy (i.e., MAO-A inhibitors) or those who have received an MAO-A inhibitor within the previous 2 weeks. Sumatriptan is contraindicated in patients who have received ergotamine-containing or ergot-type medications within 24 hours of sumatriptan administration (see Drug Interactions).

    General Information
    Sumatriptan is contraindicated in basilar/hemiplegic migraine. Use sumatriptan with caution in individuals with a history of seizure disorder or conditions associated with a lowered seizure threshold.     General Information
    Sumatriptan is contraindicated in individuals with ischemic coronary artery disease (CAD) (i.e., angina pectoris, history of myocardial infarction, documented silent ischemia), coronary artery vasospasm (including Prinzmetal angina), arrhythmias associated with cardiac accessory conduction pathway syndrome (including Wolff-Parkinson-White syndrome), uncontrolled hypertension, peripheral vascular disease (including ischemic bowel disease), or a history of stroke (including transient ischemic attack). Sumatripan nasal powder is also contraindicated in individuals with other significant underlying cardiovascular disease. Perform a cardiovascular (CV) evaluation in triptan-naive individuals who have multiple CV risk factors (e.g., increased age, diabetes mellitus, hypertension, hypercholesterolemia, tobacco smoking, obesity, strong family history of ischemic heart disease, postmenopausal females, males older than 40 years) before administering sumatriptan. For individuals with risk factors who have a negative CV evaluation, consider administering the first dose of sumatriptan in a medically-supervised setting and obtaining an ECG immediately after administration. Consider periodic CV evaluation in intermittent long-term users of sumatriptan who have CV risk factors. Before treating headaches in individuals not previously diagnosed with migraine, and in individuals with migraine who present with atypical symptoms, exclude other potentially serious neurological conditions; individuals with migraine may be at increased risk of certain cerebrovascular events (e.g., stroke, hemorrhage, transient ischemic attack). Significant blood pressure elevation has been reported on rare occasions in individuals with or without a history of hypertension receiving serotonin receptor agonists (triptans).

    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Data from a prospective exposure registry and epidemiological studies have not detected an increased frequency of birth defects or a consistent pattern of birth defects in patients exposed to sumatriptan during pregnancy compared with the general population. Based on data from an international pregnancy registry (n = 626 exposed fetuses/infants), the occurrence of major birth defects was 4.2% (20/478; 95% CI 2.6% to 6.5%) with first-trimester exposure and 4.2% (24/576; 95% CI 2.7% to 6.2%) with exposure in any trimester. The sample size in this study had 80% power to detect at least a 1.73- to 1.91-fold increase in the rate of major malformations. The registry's sample size was insufficient to support definitive conclusions about overall malformation risk or to compare frequencies of specific birth defects. Of 20 birth defects reported after first trimester exposure, 4 infants had ventricular septal defects (one infant was exposed to both sumatriptan and naratriptan) and 3 had pyloric stenosis; no other birth defects were reported in more than 2 infants. Fetal deaths, spontaneous abortions, and elective abortions without reported defects were excluded from analysis. An analysis of data from the Swedish Medical Birth Register identified no increased risk of malformations (107/2,257 infants; RR 0.99, 95% CI 0.91 to 1.21) among live births with first-trimester exposure to sumatriptan compared to those without sumatriptan exposure during pregnancy. A Norwegian study using birth registry data linked to prescription database reported that, of the 415 patients who filled prescriptions for sumatriptan in the first trimester, 15 had infants with major congenital malformations (OR 1.16, 95% CI 0.69 to 1.94), while of the 364 patients who filled prescriptions for sumatriptan before, but not during, pregnancy, 20 had infants with major congenital malformations (OR 1.83, 95% CI 1.17 to 2.88), each compared to a population comparison group. In animal developmental toxicity studies, an increased incidence of cervicothoracic and umbilical abnormalities was associated with oral administration of sumatriptan to pregnant rats during organogenesis, with a highest no-effect dose of 60 mg/kg/day (approximately 3 times the maximum recommended human dose [MRHD] of 200 mg/day on a body surface area [BSA] basis). In rabbits, oral administration during organogenesis resulted in an increased incidence of embryo-lethality and fetal cervicothoracic vascular and skeletal abnormalities, with a highest no-effect dose of 15 mg/kg/day (approximately 2 times the MRHD based on BSA). Intravenous administration of sumatriptan to pregnant rabbits was also associated with an increased incidence of embryo-lethality; the highest no-effect dose was 0.75 mg/kg/day. Data suggests people with migraine may be at increased risk of preeclampsia and gestational hypertension during pregnancy. Advise pregnant individuals of the potential risks.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Use sumatriptan with caution during breast-feeding. Sumatriptan is present in human milk in small amounts, with a relative infant dose of 0.53% to 3.51%. Due to poor oral bioavailability and intermittent as-needed dosing, exposure to the breastfed child is likely low. Some experts suggest that withholding breast-feeding for 8 hours after a subcutaneous dose eliminates exposure risk. FDA-approved labeling recommends withholding breast-feeding for 12 hours after a dose. There is no information on the effects of sumatriptan on the breastfed child. A European pharmacovigilance study noted reports of painful, burning nipples and breast pain related to sumatriptan use, occasionally accompanied by a decrease in milk production. In one study, 5 people who had been lactating for an average of 22 weeks received a single 6 mg subcutaneous dose of sumatriptan.   General Information
  Sumatriptan can cause serious cardiovascular and cerebrovascular events, including fatalities, due to vasospasm. These events seem to be rare and occur more commonly in patients with risk factors for coronary artery disease or patients who have received ergotamine within the previous 24 hours, although some event have occurred in patients with no known cardiac risk factors. Serious cardiac events reported following sumatriptan include coronary vasospasm, rare cardiac arrhythmias (ventricular tachycardia, ventricular fibrillation, cardiac arrest, and death), angina, transient mycoardial ischemia, myocardial infarction and cardiac arrest. A myocardial infarction was reported in a 14 year old adolescent male following the use of oral sumatriptan; clinical signs occurred within 1 day of drug administration although causality cannot be definitively made. Frequent cardiovascular adverse effects associated with sumatriptan include either hypotension or hypertension, syncope, and palpitations. Infrequent cardiovascular events include hypertensive crisis, peripheral cyanosis, thromboembolism, heart block (AV block), sinus bradycardia, sinus tachycardia, atrial fibrillation, ventricular fibrillation, ventricular tachycardia, and transient ECG changes following parenteral use or nasal spray (e.g., ST-T wave changes, premature atrial contractions (PACs), premature ventricular contractions (PVCs), QT prolongation). Chest pain (unspecified) and a chest pressure syndrome, which included sensations of chest pain, tightness and or/heaviness and jaw pain and tightness and regional pain and pressure have been reported immediately following sumatriptan administration and have been associated with cardiac events. Sumatriptan tablets and injection have been associated with cerebral vasospasm resulting in intracranial bleeding, subarachnoid hemorrhage, stroke, seizures, and other cerebrovascular events, some of which have lead to fatalities. The relationship with sumatriptan is unclear. In a several cases, it appears that the cerebrovascular incident was the primary event and sumatriptan was given due to the incorrect assumption that these symptoms were due to a migraine when they were not. Sumatriptan should not be administered if the headaches are atypical for the patient. In post-marketing reports, stroke has been rarely noted as a serious adverse event in adolescents. Death associated with sumatriptan has also been reported in adolescents; causality is not known.Other vasospastic effects associated with sumatriptan include peripheral vascular ischemia and bowel ischemia. Abdominal pain, bloody diarrhea, and cramping have been associated with ichemic colitis following sumatriptan with a median time to onset of 48 hours following administration of sumatriptan.An injection site reaction has been reported in more than 50% of patients receiving sumatriptan (compared to roughly 25% of patients receiving placebo). Flushing, tingling and/or a sensation of warmth, burning, or heat generation have been reported following sumatriptan injection but appear to be transient. These effects usually disappear within 10—30 minutes after subcutaneous administration. Rare cases of angioedema have been reported during post-marketing experience with sumatriptan.Dizziness, lightheadedness, weakness, myalgia, or muscle cramps or stiffness have occurred in as many as 13% of patients receiving oral or subcutaneous sumatriptan. Headache is infrequently reported with the use of subcutaneous sumatriptan (2% vs. < 1% placebo). Anxiety and dysphagia have occurred frequently with subcutaneous use of sumatriptan, but these data are from open label trials and causality is unknown. Nausea/vomiting, fatigue, and drowsiness also can occur. Dizziness and nausea/vomiting can also occur with intranasal sumatriptan. Other adverse events reported for oral sumatriptan, occurring in 1% or more of patients include diarrhea, other gastric symptoms, throat symptoms, phonophobia, photophobia, dyspnea, diaphoresis, burning, numbness and paresthesias, and visual changes. Other adverse events occurring in 1% or more of patients receiving intranasal sumatriptan include atypical sensations (burning sensation); ear, nose, and throat disorder; nasal cavity, sinus, and throat discomfort; neurological symptoms; dysgeusia; and vertigo. These adverse events occurred more frequently in patients receiving the 20 mg dose of intranasal sumatriptan.Rare reports of transient and permanent blindness, including significant partial vision loss have been reported with the use of sumatriptan. Visual impairment, including blurred vision, may be a part of any type of migraine headache; therefore, causality cannot be determined. Incidence data for vision disturbances with sumatriptan are not available. Visual loss has also been reported in adolescents receiving sumatriptan.

Serotonin syndrome may occur with serotonin receptor agonists particularly during concurrent use of serotonergic agents such as SSRIs or SNRIs. Serotonin syndrome consists of symptoms such as mental status changes (e.g., agitation, confusion), diaphoresis, hyperreflexia, elevated blood pressure, hyperthermia, nauseousness, emesis, and diarrhea. Overuse of drugs for treating acute headaches, including triptans, may lead to medication overuse headache. Discontinuation of the overused drug and treatment of withdrawal symptoms (e.g., transient worsening of headache) may be necessary. Advise patients about the risks of medication overuse (e.g., use of sumatriptan or any combination of therapy for at least 10 days/month) and encourage them to keep a written record of headache frequency and drug use. Pediatric guidelines recommend no more than 9 days/month of triptans, 14 days/month of over-the-counter medication, and no more than 9 days/month of any combination of therapy to avoid medication overuse headache.

Following sensitization with the sumatriptan TDS, erythematous plaque and/or erythemato-vesicular or erythemato-bullous eruptions may develop. Clinical course is characterized by crescendo phenomenon of worsening pruritus and appearance over time with slower resolution to normal of affected skin areas. Patients sensitized from use of the sumatriptan TDS, as evidenced by development of ACD, may develop systemic sensitization or other systemic reactions if sumatriptan-containing products are taken via other routes, e.g., orally or subcutaneously. It is possible that some patients who developed ACD with sumatriptan by exposure to the sumatriptan TDS, and who have developed systemic sensitization, may not be able to take sumatriptan in any form. Patients who develop ACD with the sumatriptan TDS and require treatment with sumatriptan via other routes should receive their first subsequent dose under close medical supervision.

Description
  Text
  Sumatriptan is a serotonin-receptor 1B and 1D agonist (triptan) indicated for the acute treatment of migraine with or without aura. Some formulations of subcutaneous sumatriptan are also indicated for the treatment of cluster headaches. Sumatriptan is not indicated for long-term migraine prophylaxis or for the management of hemiplegic or basilar migraine. Serious cardiac adverse effects may occur during sumatriptan administration and are more likely to occur in patients with risk factors for coronary artery disease, although some events have occurred in patients without known cardiac risk factors.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Sumatriptan binds with high affinity to 5-HT1B/1D receptors. The therapeutic effect of sumatriptan in the treatment of migraine headache is presumably due to its agonist effects at the 5-HT1B/1D receptors on intracranial blood vessels and sensory nerves of the trigeminal system, resulting in cerebral blood vessel vasoconstriction and inhibition of the release of pro-inflammatory neuropeptides and nociceptive neurotransmission.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Oral Route
    Description
    Following oral administration of sumatriptan 25 mg and 100 mg, the Cmax was 18 ng/mL (range: 7 to 47 ng/mL) and 51 ng/mL (range: 28 to 100 ng/mL) respectively. When given as a single dose, sumatriptan displays dose proportionality in AUC over the dose range 25 mg to 200 mg, but the Cmax after 100 mg is approximately 25% less than expected based on 25 mg dose. The Cmax is similar during a migraine attack and during a migraine-free period, while the Tmax is slightly later during an attack, at approximately 2.5 hours compared to 2 hours. Bioavailability is about 15% with oral administration. Poor bioavailability after oral administration results from incomplete absorption and presystemic metabolism. A food effect trial involving administration of sumatriptan to subjects under fasting conditions and with a high-fat meal indicated that the Cmax and AUC were increased by 15% and 12%, respectively, when administered in the fed state.
    Name
    Subcutaneous Route
    Description
    Peak concentration is achieved within 5 to 20 minutes following injection via the subcutaneous route. Bioavailability after subcutaneous injection is about 97% +/- 16%. A single 3 mg subcutaneous dose of Zembrace SymTouch is bioequivalent to Imitrex subcutaneous injection. The Cmax of sumatriptan was 74 +/- 15 ng/mL after a single 6 mg subcutaneous dose injected into the deltoid area in 18 healthy volunteers (age 24 +/- 6 years). The Cmax was 61 +/- 15 ng/mL when the same dose was manually injected subcutaneously into the thigh, and 52 +/- 15 ng/mL when the same dose was injected into the thigh by autoinjector techniques.

  Text
  Sumatriptan is administered orally, subcutaneously, or intranasally. The apparent Vd is approximately 2.7 L/kg. Protein binding ranges from 14% to 21%. Sumatriptan is metabolized by monoamine oxidase-A (MAO-A) to an indole acetic acid (IAA) metabolite or the IAA glucuronide, both of which are inactive. Approximately 60% of sumatriptan is excreted renally. Approximately 3% of the dose can be recovered as unchanged sumatriptan. The elimination half-life of sumatriptan is approximately 2 to 3 hours. Total plasma clearance is 1,200 mL/minute.
 
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes and drug transporters: none

Dosage Adjustment Guidelines
  Hepatic Impairment
  Hepatic impairment may cause unpredictable increases in the bioavailability of orally administered sumatriptan. Do not exceed 50 mg/dose PO. Hepatic impairment does not significantly affect intranasal or subcutaneous sumatriptan. All formulations are contraindicated for use in patients with severe hepatic impairment.
  Renal Impairment
  Specific guidelines for dosage adjustments in renal impairment are not available; it appears that no dosage adjustments are needed.
 
Intermittent hemodialysis
It is not known whether hemodialysis removes sumatriptan from plasma.
 
Peritoneal dialysis
It is not known whether peritoneal dialysis removes sumatriptan from plasma.
