  Contraindications And Precautions
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    There are no available data on the use of garadacimab in pregnancy to evaluate for a drug-associated risk for major birth defects, miscarriage, or other adverse maternal or fetal outcomes. Monoclonal antibodies like garadacimab are transported across the placenta during the third trimester of pregnancy; therefore, potential effects are likely to be greater during the third trimester of pregnancy. Garadacimab produced no evidence of fetal harm at doses up to approximately 100 times the exposure achieved at the maximum recommended human dose (MRHD) of 200 mg once monthly in an embryo-fetal development study in pregnant rabbits. Additionally, garadacimab had no effects on survival, growth, or development of first generation offspring at doses resulting in approximately 76 times the exposure achieved at the MRHD in a pre- and post-natal development study in rabbits.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Consider the developmental and health benefits of breast-feeding along with the mother's clinical need for garadacimab and any potential adverse effects on the breastfed infant from garadacimab or the underlying maternal condition. There are no data on the presence of garadacimab or its metabolite in either human or animal milk, the effects on the breastfed infant, or the effects on milk production. Maternal immunoglobulin (IgG) is known to be present in human milk. The effects of local gastrointestinal exposure and limited systemic exposure in the breastfed infant to garadacimab are unknown.

    Headline
    laboratory test interference
    General Information
    Garadacimab may cause laboratory test interference and prolong activated partial thromboplastin time (aPTT) due to an interaction of garadacimab with the aPTT assay. The reagents used in the aPTT laboratory test initiate intrinsic coagulation through the activation of factor XII in the contact system. Therefore, inhibition of plasma activated factor XII by garadacimab can prolong aPTT in this assay. The incidence of prolonged activated partial thromboplastin time (aPTT), defined as more than 1.4 times the upper limit of normal (ULN), was 8% in garadacimab-treated subjects compared to 0% of subjects receiving placebo in clinical trials. Additionally, the incidence of prolonged prothrombin time (PT) or international normalized ratio (INR), defined as more than 1.3 times the ULN, was 15% in garadacimab-treated subjects compared to 4% of subjects receiving placebo. The increases in aPTT, PT, and INR were not associated with bleeding events.
    Headline
    antibody formation
    General Information
    Antibody formation may occur after garadacimab administration. Anti-garadacimab antibodies were reported in 2.6% of garadacimab-treated subjects during a 6-month treatment period in clinical trials. The development of anti-drug antibodies (ADA) against garadacimab did not affect pharmacokinetics, safety, or clinical response. The observed incidence of ADA is highly dependent on the sensitivity and specificity of the assay. Differences in assay methods preclude meaningful comparisons of the incidence of anti-drug antibodies in other studies.

Description
  Text
  Garadacimab is an activated factor XII (FXIIa) inhibitor (monoclonal antibody) indicated for prophylaxis to prevent attacks of hereditary angioedema (HAE). The inhibition of FXIIa decreases the activation of prekallikrein to kallikrein and the generation of bradykinin. A decrease in bradykinin reduces the cascade of events leading to an HAE attack as bradykinin is associated with inflammation and swelling in HAE. Garadacimab may cause laboratory test interference and prolong activated partial thromboplastin time (aPTT) due to an interaction of garadacimab with the aPTT assay. The development of anti-drug antibodies (ADA) may occur with garadacimab use. However, the development of ADA against garadacimab did not affect pharmacokinetics, safety, or clinical response in garadacimab clinical trials. Garadacimab was FDA-approved in June 2025.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Garadacimab is an inhibitor of activated factor II (FXII) that binds to the catalytic domain of activated factor XII (FXIIa and beta FXIIa) and inhibits its catalytic activity. FXII is the first factor activated in the contact activation pathway and initiates the inflammatory bradykinin-producing kallikrein-kinin system. The inhibition of FXIIa reduces a cascade of events leading to a hereditary angioedema (HAE) attack by decreasing the activation of prekallikrein to kallikrein and the generation of bradykinin. Bradykinin is associated with inflammation and swelling in HAE attacks.
Pharmacodynamics
Garadacimab concentration-dependent inhibition of FXIIa-mediated kallikrein activity was observed in subjects with HAE after the first dose.

Administration

  Route Specific Administrations
    Administration Details
      Name
      Subcutaneous Administration
      Description
      Garadacimab is intended for self-administration or administration by a caregiver after training on proper preparation and subcutaneous injection technique.
Prior to administration, remove garadacimab from the refrigerator and allow it to sit at room temperature for 30 minutes before use.
Do not try to speed up the warming process in any way. Do not warm it in a microwave, run hot water over it, or leave it in direct sunlight.
Injecting the medication cold could cause some discomfort.
Clean the injection site with an alcohol swab in a circular motion. Allow the injection site to air dry. Do not touch the cleaned injection site before administering the injection. Do not fan or blow on the cleaned skin area.
Inject garadacimab subcutaneously into the thigh or abdomen area 1 inch away from the navel. The upper arm may be used as an injection site if administered by a caregiver.
Rotate injection sites. Do not inject in the same injection site multiple times if the skin is damaged.
Do not inject into the navel, moles, scars, bruises, or into areas where the skin is tender, red, hard, or injured. Do not inject the prefilled syringe or autoinjector through clothing.
If there is bleeding at the injection site, press a cotton ball or gauze over the injection site. Do not rub the injection site. The injection site may be covered with a small adhesive bandage, if needed.
Do not reuse the prefilled syringe or autoinjector. Dispose of the prefilled syringe or autoinjector into an appropriate sharps container after injecting the dose.
Storage: Do not put the prefilled syringe or autoinjector back in the refrigerator after it has reached room temperature. Do not freeze. If the prefilled syringe or autoinjector has been frozen, do not use the prefilled syringe even if it has thawed.
Prefilled syringe
Do not hold onto the needle cap or plunger when removing the prefilled syringe from the carton box. Do not move or pull the plunger.
Do not remove the needle cap until ready for injection.
Hold the prefilled syringe by the body, with the needle facing away.
Pull the needle cap straight off with 1 hand while holding the prefilled syringe with the other hand.
Do not touch or hold the plunger during needle cap removal. Do not re-cap the prefilled syringe.
Dispose of the needle cap in an appropriate sharps disposal container.
It is normal to see a drop of liquid at the end of the needle.
Keep the needle sterile after removing the needle cap. Do not touch the needle or let it touch any surfaces after removing the needle cap.
Gently pinch the area of cleaned skin around the injection site and hold that area firmly until the injection is complete.
Fully insert the needle at a 45 to 90-degree angle on the cleaned injection site.
Do not hold or push on the plunger while inserting the needle into the skin.
Do not change the angle during the injection.
Hold the prefilled syringe in place and inject all the medication by firmly pushing the plunger all the way down.
Press the plunger all the way down until it stops to administer the full dose. Push firmly on the plunger until the end of the injection.
Slowly remove the thumb from the plunger before removing the syringe from the skin after the plunger has been fully pushed down, and the full dose has been injected. This will make the needle retract inside the syringe.
Do not remove the syringe from the skin before removing the thumb; this could result in a needle stick injury.
Release the pinch and remove the prefilled syringe from the skin.
Prefilled autoinjector
Do not remove the clear autoinjector cap until immediately before the injection.
Hold the autoinjector with 1 hand and pull the clear autoinjector cap straight off with the other hand.
Do not twist the clear cap.
Do not put the clear cap back on after it has been removed because this could start the injection and cause injury.
The clear cap has a metal part inside; this is normal.
Dispose of the clear cap in an appropriate sharps container.
Do not touch the gray needle shield of the autoinjector to avoid injury. Do not put the autoinjector down after removing the clear cap.
Gently pinch the area of cleaned skin around the injection site and hold the area firmly until the injection is complete.
Place the autoinjector at a 90-degree angle on the cleaned injection site. Confirm that the viewing window is visible.
Do not remove, tilt, or rotate the autoinjector during the injection.
The injection may take up to 15 seconds.
Press the gray needle shield down firmly against the pinched skin to start the injection and listen for a click.
The first click means that the injection has started. The yellow plunger will start moving in the viewing window.
Continue to press the autoinjector down and watch the viewing window. The window will turn yellow; listen for a 2nd click.
Continue to press the autoinjector down for 5 more seconds to ensure that the full dose is administered. Confirm that the yellow plunger completely fills the window and has stopped moving before the autoinjector is removed.
Release the pinch and remove the autoinjector at a 90-degree angle from the skin.
The gray needle shield returns to the original position and will lock into place, covering the needle, as the autoinjector is lifted from the skin.
