    Headline
    Child-Pugh class B, Child-Pugh class C, decompensated hepatic cirrhosis, hepatic failure
    General Information
    Use with caution in individuals with hepatic failure. Avoid use of resmetirom in individuals with decompensated hepatic cirrhosis (consistent with moderate to severe hepatic impairment or Child-Pugh class B and Child-Pugh class C). The safety and efficacy of resmetirom have not been established in individuals with metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis (MASH) cirrhosis. Moderate or severe hepatic impairment increases resmetirom exposure, which may increase the risk of adverse reactions. Hepatotoxicity has been observed with the use of resmetirom. In one individual, elevations in LFTs were accompanied by elevations in immunoglobulin G levels, suggesting drug-induced autoimmune-like hepatitis (DI-ALH). The liver tests returned to baseline in this individual following hospitalization and discontinuation of resmetirom without any therapeutic intervention. Monitor individuals during resmetirom treatment for elevations in LFTs and for signs and symptoms of hepatotoxicity (e.g., fatigue, nausea, vomiting, right upper quadrant pain or tenderness, jaundice, fever, rash, and/or eosinophilia). If hepatotoxicity is suspected, discontinue resmetirom and continue to monitor. If laboratory values return to baseline, weigh the potential risks against the benefits of restarting resmetirom. If laboratory values do not return to baseline, consider DI-ALH or autoimmune liver disease in the evaluation of LFT elevations.
    Headline
    gallbladder disease
    General Information
    Use with caution in individuals with gallbladder disease. Gallbladder disease-related adverse reactions such as cholelithiasis, acute cholecystitis, and obstructive pancreatitis (due to gallstone) were observed more often in individuals treated with resmetirom than those treated with placebo during clinical trials. Initiate gallbladder diagnostic studies and appropriate clinical follow-up if cholelithiasis is suspected. If an acute gallbladder event is suspected, interrupt resmetirom treatment until the event is resolved.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    There are no data on the use of resmetirom during human pregnancy to evaluate for a drug-associated risk of major birth defects, miscarriage, or adverse outcomes. There are risks to the pregnant individual and the fetus related to underlying maternal MASH with liver fibrosis, such as increased risks of gestational diabetes, hypertensive complications, preterm birth, and postpartum hemorrhage. In animal studies, adverse effects on embryo-fetal development occurred with resmetirom treatment in pregnant rabbits at 3.5 times the maximum recommended dose during organogenesis. The effects were associated with maternal toxicity, and embryo-fetal effects were not seen at lower dose levels with better tolerance in pregnant rabbits. Resmetirom or its metabolite MGL-3623 did not cause embryo-fetal developmental effects in pregnant rats. At 37 times the maximum recommended dose, decrease in birthweight, increased incidence of stillbirths, and increased mortality (postnatal days 1 to 4) were seen in rats with maternal dosing of resmetirom during organogenesis through lactation. The effects were associated with marked suppression of maternal T4, T3, and TSH levels. Report pregnancies in resmetirom- treated individuals to Madrigal Pharmaceuticals, Adverse Event reporting line at 1-800-905-0324 and https://pregnancyregistry.madrigalpharma.com.
    Headline
    geriatric
    General Information
    No overall differences in effectiveness were noted in geriatric versus younger adults during preapproval clinical trials, but a numerically higher incidence of adverse reactions have been observed in adults 65 years of age and older compared to younger adults.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Use resmetirom with caution during breast-feeding. There are no data on the presence of resmetirom in human or animal milk, its effects on the breast-fed child, or its effects on milk production. Because resmetirom is over 99% bound to plasma proteins, the amount in milk is likely to be low. If the individual requires resmetirom, it is not a reason to discontinue breast-feeding. Monitor the child for vomiting, jaundice, fever, and rash. Consider the benefits of breast-feeding, the individual's need for treatment, and any potential adverse effects on the breastfed child from the medication or from the individual's underlying medical condition.

    General Information
    Elevated hepatic enzymes (elevated LFTs) and hepatotoxicity have been observed with resmetirom treatment. Increases in mean ALT and AST levels were observed in the first 4 weeks after initiating treatment. Mean elevations of ALT and AST were less than 1.5 times above baseline at 4 weeks and values returned to baseline around 8 weeks after initiating treatment. During a clinical trial, ALT greater than 3 times the upper limit of normal (ULN) occurred in 11% and 13% of patients treated resmetirom 80 mg and resmetirom 100 mg, respectively, compared to 10% of placebo treated patients. ALT greater than 5 times ULN occurred in 2% of both treatment groups and the placebo group. AST greater than 3 times ULN occurred in 9% to 12% of patients treated with resmetirom 80 mg and 100 mg treated patients, respectively, compared to 10% of placebo treated patients. AST greater than 5 times ULN occurred in 1% and 4% of resmetirom 80 mg and 100 mg treated patients, respectively, compared to 2% of placebo treated patients. Increased total bilirubin (hyperbilirubinemia, including Gilbert syndrome) occurred in 1% and 3% of resmetirom 80 mg and resmetirom 100 mg treated patients, respectively, compared to 2% of placebo treated patients. One patient who was treated with resmetirom 80 mg developed substantial elevations of liver biochemistries (ALT/AST/total bilirubin) from baseline that resolved after treatment interruption. Upon reinitiating, ALT was 58 times ULN, AST was 66 times ULN, and total bilirubin was 15 times ULN. There was no elevation of alkaline phosphate (ALP). Elevated immunoglobulin G levels also occurred with elevated liver enzymes, suggesting drug-induced autoimmune-like hepatitis (DI-ALH). Baseline values in liver tests returned after hospitalization and discontinuation of resmetirom without therapeutic intervention. If hepatotoxicity is suspected, discontinue resmetirom therapy and continue to monitor the patient. If laboratory values return to baseline, weigh the potential risks vs. benefits of restarting treatment. If laboratory values do not return to baseline, consider evaluation for DI-ALH or autoimmune liver disease.

Description
  Text
  Resmetirom is an oral thyroid hormone receptor-beta (THR-beta) agonist indicated in conjunction with diet and exercise for the treatment of adults with noncirrhotic metabolic-dysfunction associated steatohepatitis (MASH; also known as nonalcoholic steatohepatitis or NASH) with moderate to advanced liver fibrosis (consistent with stages F2 to F3 fibrosis). The efficacy of resmetirom was based on a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial in which NASH patients with stage 2 or 3 fibrosis received resmetirom 80 mg (n=298), resmetirom 100 mg (n=296), or placebo (n=294). At month 12, patients treated with resmetirom 80 mg or 100 mg had demonstrated improvement in the primary endpoints of resolution of steatohepatitis and no worsening of liver fibrosis, and in improvement of liver fibrosis and no worsening of steatohepatitis compared to placebo. Both doses achieved statistical significance for their endpoints during the trial and were superior to placebo with respect to NASH resolution and improvement in liver fibrosis by at least 1 stage. Hepatotoxicity and gallbladder-related adverse reactions were reported during clinical trials, and gastrointestinal adverse reactions were common. The safety and effectiveness of resmetirom have not been established in patients with cirrhosis due to MASH and use should be avoided in patients with decompensated cirrhosis (consistent with Child-Pugh class B or C hepatic impairment). Resmetirom was FDA-approved in March 2024 under the accelerated approval; continued approval for this indication may be contingent upon verification and description of clinical benefit in confirmatory trials.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Resmetirom is a partial agonist of thyroid hormone receptor-beta (THR-B). Resmetirom works by stimulating the predominate (THR-B) in the liver, which in turn reduces intrahepatic triglycerides. In vitro, the maximum agonistic response to resmetirom was 83.3% when compared to triiodothyronine (T3) for THR-B activation (EC50 of 0.21 micromoles). Resmetirom also showed thyroid hormone receptor alpha (THR-A) stimulation with 48.6% agonistic response compared to T3 (EC50 of 3.74 micromoles). Resmetirom decreases liver fat content as measured by magnetic resonance imaging-protein density fat fraction (MRI-PDFF) or FibroScan controlled attenuation parameter (CAP). Reductions in liver fat content by MRI-PDFF were observed at 16 (the first assessment) and 52 weeks of treatment. Reductions in liver fat content by CAP were observed at 52 weeks of treatment.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Oral Route
    Description
    Following once daily dosing of resmetirom orally, steady-state is typically reached within 3 to 6 days of dosing. Median time to peak plasma concentrations are reached approximately 4 hours following multiple doses of resmetirom 80 mg or 100 mg. Steady-state exposure increases in a dose proportional manner between doses of 40 mg and 100 mg. At doses between 100 mg and 200 mg, resmetirom exposure is greater than dose proportional with increases by about 5.6-fold. Resmetirom exposure increased 1.5 to 3-fold following once-daily dosing with no accumulation of active metabolite MGL-3623. Clinically significant differences in pharmacokinetics were not seen following administration with a high-fat meal. Administration with food decreased resmetirom Cmax by 33%, decreased AUC by 11%, and delayed median Tmax by 2 hours when compared to fasted conditions. At steady-state of once daily resmetirom 80 mg, the mean Cmax was 778 ng/mL and AUC was 5850 ng x hour/mL. At steady state of once daily resmetirom 100 mg, the mean Cmax was 971 ng/mL and AUC was 7780 ng x hour/mL. Resmetirom exposure is similar between MASH subjects with F2 stage fibrosis and F3 stage fibrosis.

  Text
  Resmetirom is administered orally and has an apparent Vd of 68 L at steady-state. It is more than 99% bound to plasma proteins. Resmetirom is metabolized by CYP2C8 into the major metabolite MGL-3623. The activity of MGL-3623 is 28 times lower for thyroid hormone receptor-beta (THR-B) than resmetirom. MGL-3623 represents 33% to 51% of resmetirom AUC at steady-state following administration of 100 mg once daily. Following oral administration of a radioactive dose, approximately 67% of the total dose was recovered in the feces primarily as metabolites; 24% of the total dose was recovered in the urine. The major metabolite, MGL-3623, accounted for 3.3% and 16% of the dose recovered in feces and urine, respectively. Unchanged labeled resmetirom was not detected in feces and accounted for 1% of the dose recovered in urine. The oxalic acid metabolite was observed in plasma but not in the urine. Resmetirom has a median terminal plasma half-life of 4.5 hours and an apparent clearance of 17.5 L/hour at steady-state. Following once daily doses, steady-state is typically reached within 3 to 6 days of dosing.
 
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes and drug transporters: CYP2C8, BCRP, BSEP, OAT3, OATP1B1, OATP1B3, UGT1A4, and UGT1A9
Resmetirom is a substrate for CYP2C8, OATP1B1, OATP1B3, and BCRP. Resmetirom inhibits CYP2C8, BCRP, BSEP, OAT3, OATP1B1, OATP1B3, UGT1A4, and UGT1A9 (the clinical relevance of UGT1A4 and UGT1A9 inhibition is unknown). The clinical significance of OAT3 and BSEP inhibition is unknown.
