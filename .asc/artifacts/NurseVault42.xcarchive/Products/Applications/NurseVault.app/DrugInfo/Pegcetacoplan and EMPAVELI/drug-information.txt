Indication Specific Dosing
  General Information
  General Dosing Information
Vaccinate persons against encapsulated bacteria, including S. pneumoniae and N. meningitidis (serogroups A, C, W, Y, and B), at least 2 weeks prior to initiation of pegcetacoplan and according to the most current Advisory Committee on Immunization Practices (ACIP) recommendations.
If urgent pegcetacoplan treatment is indicated in persons not up to date with vaccines for S. pneumoniae and N. meningitidis according to ACIP recommendations, administer these vaccines as soon as possible and provide antibacterial medication prophylaxis.
Conversion from eculizumab: Administer pegcetacoplan loading dose while continuing eculizumab at its current dose. After 4 weeks, discontinue eculizumab and continue pegcetacoplan monotherapy.
Conversion from ravulizumab: Start pegcetacoplan no more than 4 weeks after the last dose of ravulizumab.

    Headline
    encapsulated bacteria infection
    General Information
    Systemic pegcetacoplan is contraindicated in individuals with unresolved serious encapsulated bacteria infection, including infections caused by S. pneumoniae, N. meningitidis (any serogroup, including non-groupable strains), and H. influenzae type B. Pegcetacoplan increases the risk of serious, life-threatening, or fatal infections caused by encapsulated bacteria. Complete or update vaccinations against encapsulated bacteria at least 2 weeks prior to administration of the first dose of pegcetacoplan, according to current Advisory Committee on Immunization Practices (ACIP) recommendations for individuals receiving a complement inhibitor. The ACIP-recommended vaccination schedule differs from that in the vaccine prescribing information and should be followed accordingly. Consider revaccination based on the duration of pegcetacoplan therapy. If urgent initiation of pegcetacoplan is necessary in patients who are not up to date with vaccines against encapsulated bacteria, administer the recommended vaccines as soon as possible and initiate antibacterial prophylaxis. However, the optimal duration, regimens, and efficacy of antibacterial prophylaxis in patients receiving complement inhibitors, including pegcetacoplan, have not been established. Consider the benefits and risks of pegcetacoplan treatment and antibacterial prophylaxis in both vaccinated and unvaccinated patients, given the increased risk of serious infections caused by encapsulated bacteria. Monitor all patients closely for early signs or symptoms of infection, and instruct them to seek immediate medical attention if symptoms occur. Prompt evaluation and treatment of infections are critical, as serious infections may become rapidly life-threatening or fatal if not recognized and treated early. In patients undergoing active treatment for serious infections, consider interrupting pegcetacoplan therapy. It is important to note that vaccination does not eliminate the risk of infections due to encapsulated bacteria, even in individuals who develop detectable antibody responses.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    There are insufficient human data to determine whether pegcetacoplan use during pregnancy is associated with a risk of major birth defects, miscarriage, or other adverse outcomes. Systemic exposure after ocular administration is low. In patients with paroxysmal nocturnal hemoglobinuria (PNH), untreated disease is associated with significant risks for the pregnant person and fetus, including worsening cytopenias, thrombosis, infection, bleeding, miscarriage, increased maternal mortality, fetal death, and preterm delivery. Use during pregnancy should be considered only after careful assessment of benefits and potential fetal risk. In animal studies, administration of doses 2.9 times the human exposure (based on AUC) from gestation through parturition was associated with a statistically significant increase in abortions or stillbirths; no such increase occurred at 1.3 times the human exposure. No maternal toxicity or teratogenic effects were observed in offspring delivered at term. No adverse developmental effects were noted in offspring up to 6 months postpartum. Fetal systemic exposure was less than 1% of the maternal concentration when pregnant monkeys were given doses 2.9 times the human exposure from organogenesis through the second trimester.

    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Advise patients to avoid breast-feeding during treatment with systemic pegcetacoplan and for 40 days after the last dose. Use intravitreal pegcetacoplan with caution during lactation. There are no data on the presence of pegcetacoplan in human milk, its effects on the breastfed child, or its impact on milk production. Pegcetacoplan has the potential to cause serious adverse reactions in a breastfed child. It is present in the milk of lactating monkeys after subcutaneous administration. Animal data suggest that exposure through breast milk after intravitreal administration is likely minimal; however, systemic absorption may occur, and a potential risk to growth and development cannot be excluded.

When feasible, administer indicated vaccines prior to initiating immunosuppressant medications. Counsel patients receiving immunosuppressant medications about the possibility of a diminished vaccine response and to continue to follow precautions to avoid exposure to cholera bacteria after receiving the vaccine.
Dengue Tetravalent Vaccine, Live: (Contraindicated) Avoid administration of the live dengue virus vaccine with immunosuppressive drug therapy and prior to immune recovery following treatment with immunosuppressive drug therapy. When feasible, administer indicated live virus vaccines at least 4 weeks before planned immunosuppression or wait until at least 3 months after discontinuation. The time to restoration of immune competence may be longer in some patients. Patients with altered immunocompetence may be at increased risk for severe adverse reactions due to uninhibited growth of the attenuated live virus. Additionally, vaccines may be less effective if administered during a period of altered immunocompetence.

Description
  Text
  Pegcetacoplan is a complement (C3) inhibitor indicated for the treatment of paroxysmal nocturnal hemoglobinuria (PNH); complement 3 glomerulopathy (C3G) or primary immune-complex membranoproliferative glomerulonephritis (IC-MPGN), to reduce proteinuria and the loss of kidney function; and geographic atrophy (GA) secondary to age-related macular degeneration (AMD). The use of pegcetacoplan for PNH, C3G, or IC-MPGN may predispose patients to serious, life-threatening, or fatal infections caused by encapsulated bacteria; vaccinate for these bacteria prior to starting pegcetacoplan. Patients with PNH switching from eculizumab or ravulizumab to pegcetacoplan require a cross-over treatment period to avoid the risk of hemolysis due to therapy disruption. Pegcetacoplan was FDA-approved in May 2021.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Pegcetacoplan is a complement (C3) inhibitor that binds to complement protein (C3) and its activation fragment C3b, thereby regulating the cleavage of C3 and the generation of downstream complement activation. In paroxysmal nocturnal hemoglobinuria (PNH), extravascular hemolysis (EVH) is facilitated by C3b opsonization while intravascular hemolysis (IVH) is mediated by the downstream membrane attach complex (MAC). Pegcetacoplan acts proximally in the complement cascade controlling both C3b-mediated EVH and terminal complement-mediated IVH. In complement 3 glomerulopathy (C3G) and primary immune-complex membranoproliferative glomerulonephritis (IC-MPGN), complement dysregulation and overactivation causes deposition of C3 fragments in glomeruli. This process contributes to the pathogenesis of C3G and is thought to contribute to the pathogenesis of primary IC-MPGN. By binding to C3 and its activation fragment C3b, pegcetacoplan inhibits C3 activation, decreasing C3 glomerular fragment deposition, resulting in a decrease in C5 convertase activity and subsequent assembly of C5b-9.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Subcutaneous Route
    Description
    The median Tmax of pegcetacoplan is between 108 and 144 hours (4.5 to 6 days) after a single dose. Steady-state serum pegcetacoplan concentrations were achieved approximately 4 to 6 weeks after the first dose and the mean (CV%) trough serum concentration was 706 (15.1%) mcg/mL in individuals with paroxysmal nocturnal hemoglobinuria (PNH) at Week 16 and sustained through Week 48. Exposure increased proportionally over a dosage range from 45 mg to 1,440 mg (0.04 to 1.33 times the approved recommended dose). In another pegcetacoplan clinical trial, the mean (CV%) trough serum concentration was 744 (25.5%) mcg/mL at Week 26. In individuals with complement 3 glomerulopathy (C3G) or primary immune-complex membranoproliferative glomerulonephritis (IC-MPGN), serum pegcetacoplan concentrations reached steady-state approximately 4 to 8 weeks after twice weekly subcutaneous infusion. The mean (CV%) trough serum concentrations ranged between 716 (31%) and 766 (23%) mcg/mL from Week 4 to Week 26 and were maintained through Week 52.

  Text
  Pegcetacoplan is administered subcutaneously and intravitreally. After subcutaneous infusion, the mean (CV%) Vd of pegcetacoplan ranges from 3.98 to 4.79 L (29% to 38%), and the estimated mean (CV%) clearance ranges from 0.3 to 0.36 L/day (30% to 54%). The median elimination half-life ranges from 8.6 to 10.8 days. Pegcetacoplan is expected to be metabolized into small peptides and amino acids by catabolic pathways. After intravitreal administration of pegcetacoplan, the geometric mean Vd is approximately 1.85 L (95% CI: 1.62 to 2.12). The estimated geometric mean clearance is 0.284 L/day (21.1%), and the geometric mean elimination half-life is approximately 4.5 days.
 
Pharmacodynamics
The mean C3 concentration increased from 94 mg/dL at baseline to 380 mg/dL at Week 16 in individuals with paroxysmal nocturnal hemoglobinuria (PNH) administered multiple doses of pegcetacoplan. The baseline percentage of PNH Type II + III red blood cells (RBCs) was 66.2%, which increased to 93.9% at Week 16. The mean percentage of PNH Type II + III RBCs with C3 deposition was 17.8% at baseline and decreased to 0.2% at Week 16 and was sustained through Week 48. In another pegcetacoplan clinical trial, the mean C3 concentration increased from 95 mg/dL at baseline to 356 mg/dL and the mean percentage of PNH Type II + III RBCs increased from 42.4% at baseline to 90% at Week 26. The mean percentage of PNH Type II + III RBCs with C3 deposition decreased from 2.85% at baseline to 0.09% at Week 26. In individuals with complement 3 glomerulopathy (C3G) or primary immune-complex membranoproliferative glomerulonephritis (IC-MPGN) administered pegcetacoplan subcutaneous infusion twice weekly, mean serum complement protein C3 concentrations increased from 62 mg/dL at baseline to 371 mg/dL at Week 26 compared to no change with placebo. These increases were sustained through Week 52. Mean plasma soluble C5b-9 concentrations decreased from 903 ng/mL at baseline to 290 ng/mL at Week 26 with pegcetacoplan compared to no change with placebo. These decreases were sustained through Week 52. In individuals with evaluable kidney biopsies (n = 69), 74% of pegcetacoplan-treated subjects displayed a decrease in C3 complement staining by at least 2 orders of magnitude from baseline to Week 26 compared to 12% with placebo.
 
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes and drug transporters: none

Intravitreal Administration
Only 0.1 mL (15 mg) should be administered to deliver a single dose. Any excess volume should be disposed. Ensure that the injection is given immediately after the preparation of the dose.
Prior to the intravitreal injection, monitor for elevated intraocular pressure (IOP) using tonometry. If necessary, ocular hypotensive medication can be given to lower the IOP.
The intravitreal injection procedure should be carried out under aseptic conditions, which includes the use of surgical hand disinfection, sterile gloves, a sterile drape, and a sterile eyelid speculum (or equivalent). Adequate anesthesia and a broad-spectrum topical microbicide to disinfect the periocular skin, eyelid, and ocular surface should be administered prior to the injection.
Inject intravitreally slowly until the rubber stopper reaches the end of the syringe to deliver the volume of 0.1 mL. Confirm delivery of the full dose by checking that the rubber stopper has reached the end of the syringe barrel.
Immediately after the intravitreal injection, monitor for elevations in IOP. Additional evaluation may include checking for perfusion of the optic nerve head and tonometry.
Instruct patients to report without delay any symptoms suggestive of endophthalmitis (e.g., eye pain, redness of the eye, photophobia, blurring of vision).
Each vial should only be used for the treatment of a single eye. If the fellow eye requires treatment, a new sterile, vial should be used and the sterile field, syringe, gloves, drapes, eyelid speculum, filter, and injection needles should be changed before pegcetacoplan administration. Repeat same procedure steps as above.
Any unused medicinal product or waste material should be disposed of in accordance with local regulations.
