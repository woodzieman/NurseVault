    There are no human data on the use of rezafungin during pregnancy to evaluate for a drug-associated risk of major birth defects, miscarriage, or other adverse outcomes. In animal studies, no adverse embryo-fetal outcomes were observed when rezafungin was administered to rabbits or rats during organogenesis at doses up to approximately 3 to 5 times the clinical exposure based on AUC comparison, respectively. Description
  Text
  Rezafungin is an intravenous echinocandin antifungal indicated for the treatment of candidemia and invasive candidiasis in individuals who have limited or no alternative options. Approval is based on limited clinical safety and efficacy data. Rezafungin has not been studied in individuals with endocarditis, osteomyelitis, or meningitis due to Candida. Rezafungin is associated with infusion-related reactions and abnormal liver function tests and may cause photosensitivity. Rezafungin was FDA-approved in 2023.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Rezafungin is an echinocandin antifungal that inhibits the 1,3-beta-D-glucan synthase enzyme complex present in fungal cell walls. This transmembrane protein complex is responsible for the synthesis of 1,3-beta-D-glucan, an important structural component of fungal cell walls. Inhibition of 1,3-beta-D-glucan synthase results in morphological changes in the cell walls leading to osmotic instability and fungal cell death. While this enzyme complex is essential for viability of the fungus, it is not present in mammalian cells.
 
Treatment with rezafungin results in concentration-dependent in vitro fungicidal activity against Candida sp. Rezafungin minimum inhibitory concentrations (MICs) are similar to the values obtained for other echinocandins. Also, as with other echinocandins, C. parapsilosis sensu lato, C. guilliermondii, and C. lusitaniae show intrinsic reduced echinocandin susceptibility (IRES) phenotype with 10- to 50-fold higher MIC50 than those of C. albicans. The Clinical and Laboratory Standards Institute (CLSI) defines the MICs as susceptible at 0.12 mcg/mL or less for C. dubliniensis, 0.25 mcg/mL or less for C. albicans, C. krusei, and C. tropicalis, 0.5 mcg/mL or less for C. auris and C. glabrata, and 2 mcg/mL or less for C. parapsilosis. The CLSI and the FDA differ on MIC interpretation for some Candida sp. The FDA MIC susceptibility breakpoints for C. albicans, C. tropicalis and C. glabrata are set at 0.12 mcg/mL or less. The FDA recognizes the CLSI MIC susceptibility breakpoint for C. parapsilosis, but not for C. auris, C. dubliniensis, or C. krusei.
 
Reduced echinocandin susceptibility is predominately caused by mutations in the glucan synthase catalytic subunit-encoding FKS genes (FKS1 and FKS2) that affect specific regions on the Fks protein (Fksp) called hot spot regions. Some Candida sp. (e.g., C. parapsilosis sensu stricto and C. guilliermondii) exhibit naturally occurring polymorphisms at Fksp hot spot regions and display IRES phenotype with more than 10-fold higher MIC values. For C. glabrata, the presence of a mutation at 1 or both of the Fksp hot spot regions is a risk factor for treatment failure. Rezafungin exhibits some cross-resistance to all fks mutations that confer reduced susceptibility to echinocandins; however, the relevance of the reduced susceptibility to clinical outcomes has not been fully established for rezafungin.

Pharmacokinetics

    Description
    Rezafungin displays a dose-proportional increase in maximum plasma concentrations (Cmax) and exposure (AUC) over a dosing range of 50 to 400 mg. When compared to healthy subjects, the mean Cmax and AUC of rezafungin are approximately 19% and 30% lower, respectively, in subjects with candidemia and invasive candidiasis. The Cmax, AUC, and minimum plasma concentrations (Cmin) in subjects with candidemia and invasive candidiasis who received a 400 mg loading dose, followed by a 200 mg dose once weekly were 19.2 +/- 5.9 mcg/mL, 827 +/- 252 mcg x hour/mL, and 2.4 +/- 0.9 mcg/mL, respectively, on treatment day 1 and 11.8 +/- 3.5 mcg/mL, 667 +/- 224 mcg x hour/mL, and 2.2 +/- 0.9 mcg/mL, respectively, on treatment day 15.

  Text
  Rezafungin is administered via intravenous infusion. It is approximately 87.5% to 93.6% bound to human plasma proteins (mean estimates in patients with candidemia or invasive candidiasis) with a Vd of 67 +/- 28 L. Rezafungin is primarily metabolized by hydroxylation of the terphenyl, pentyl ether side chain to form 3 hydroxylated metabolites (i.e., 2-, 3-, and 4-hydroxylpentyl rezafungin). A minor metabolic pathway involves loss of the pentyl group via O-dealkylation to form despentyl-rezafungin. Hepatic metabolism has not been observed, and it is unlikely that rezafungin is a substrate for CYP450 enzymes. Approximately 74.3% of the administered dose is excreted in the feces, primarily as unchanged rezafungin and 25.7% is excreted in the urine, primarily as inactive metabolites. The terminal half-life is 152 +/- 29 hours.
 
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes and drug transporters: none

      Description
      Reconstitution
Reconstitute each vial with 9.5 mL of Sterile Water for Injection to yield a concentration of 20 mg/mL.
Swirl the vial(s) gently. Avoid shaking to minimize foaming.
Storage: The reconstituted solution may be stored at 5 to 25 degrees C (41 to 77 degrees F) for 24 hours.
 
Dilution
The reconstituted solution must be diluted using either 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection, 0.45% Sodium Chloride Injection, or 5% Dextrose Injection.
Withdraw and discard the appropriate volume of diluent from a 250 mL infusion bag.
For the 400 mg loading dose: Remove 20 mL to obtain a total diluent volume of 230 mL.
For the 200 mg maintenance dose: Remove 10 mL to obtain a total diluent volume of 240 mL.
Transfer the indicated volume of the rezafungin reconstituted solution into the infusion bag.
For the 400 mg loading dose: Add 20 mL of the reconstituted solution to an infusion bag containing 230 mL of diluent to yield a final concentration of 1.6 mg/mL.
For the 200 mg maintenance dose: Add 10 mL of the reconstituted solution to an infusion bag containing 240 mL of diluent to yield a final concentration of 0.8 mg/mL.
Storage: The diluted infusion solution may be stored at 5 to 25 degrees C (41 to 77 degrees F) for 48 hours. Do not freeze.
 
Intermittent IV Infusion
Administer by IV infusion over approximately 1 hour.
If infusion-related reactions occur, slow or pause the infusion and restart at a lower rate.
Missed dose: If a scheduled dose is missed, administer the missed dose as soon as possible.
If the missed dose is administered within 3 days of the assigned day, the next weekly dose may be given on schedule.
If the missed dose is administered more than 3 days after the assigned day, revise the dosing schedule to ensure at least 4 days before the next dose.
If restarting after at least 2 weeks of missed dosing, start the dosing again at the 400 mg loading dose.
