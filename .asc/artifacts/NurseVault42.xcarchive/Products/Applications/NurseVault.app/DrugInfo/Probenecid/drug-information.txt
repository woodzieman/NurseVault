Drug Class: Probenecid is an oral uricosuric agent used to treat hyperuricemia associated with chronic gout or secondary to other causes (e.g., drug-induced hyperuricemia).

Routes: oral; Common adult doses: 2 grams/day PO.

Common Side Effects (excluding routine GI upset): hepatic necrosis, alopecia, anaphylactoid reactions, fever, flushing, pruritus, urticaria, costovertebral pain, gout, hematuria, increased urinary frequency, nephrotic syndrome, uric acid nephrolithiasis, uric acid nephropathy, anemia, aplastic anemia, hemolytic anemia, leukopenia

In an Overdose: Gastric intolerance may be indicative of overdosage in patients with hyperuricemia due to gout, and may be corrected by decreasing the probenecid dosage.

Key Nursing Considerations: Contraindications/precautions: Probenecid is contraindicated in patients with known probenecid hypersensitivity. It may be prudent to monitor patients with a known history of sulfonamide hypersensitivity for allergic-type reactions when initiating probenecid.

Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  General Information
  Probenecid is contraindicated in patients with known probenecid hypersensitivity. It may be prudent to monitor patients with a known history of sulfonamide hypersensitivity for allergic-type reactions when initiating probenecid. Although it contains a sulfonamide side chain, probenecid and other nonantibiotic sulfonamides do not contain the N4 aromatic amine or the N1-substituent that are present in sulfonamide antibiotics and thought to be responsible for hypersensitivity-type adverse reactions. The risk of cross-sensitivity in patients taking a nonantibiotic sulfonamide that have a history of sulfonamide hypersensitivity is low and has been confirmed by recent observational studies. In general, patients with a history of hypersensitivity to any drug are predisposed for subsequent hypersensitivity reactions to other drugs. Because of this, patients with a history of sulfonamide hypersensitivity should be monitored for hypersensitivity reactions to other drugs, including probenecid; however, treatment with a nonantibiotic sulfonamide may not need to be withheld in patients with a sulfonamide allergy as long as patients are monitored appropriately, especially if alternative therapies are not available.
 
Probenecid is contraindicated in patients with blood dyscrasias (hematological disease). Use with caution in patients with bone marrow suppression because the drug can exacerbate this conditions.
 
The use of probenecid to increase serum penicillin concentrations is not recommended for patients with renal impairment. Since renal function contributes to probenecid's mechanism, the drug should not be administered to patients with renal failure or renal disease associated with moderate to severe renal impairment (glomerular filtration rate < 50 ml/min). Probenecid loses some efficacy at CrCl < 80 ml/min and is completely ineffective when the CrCl is < 30 ml/min. The elderly are more likely to have age-related renal dysfunction.
 
Probenecid is contraindicated in patients with uric acid kidney stones (nephrolithiasis) because it increases the excretion of uric acid. Similarly, do not initiate probenecid therapy during an acute gouty attack. Probenecid should not be administered in medical conditions in which uric acid production can increase acutely (e.g., cancer chemotherapy or radiation therapy for malignancies).
 
Probenecid should be used with caution in patients with peptic ulcer disease, because of a possible increase in gastrointestinal adverse reactions.
 
Probenecid is contraindicated in infants and children < 2 years of age. Use is indicated in children age 2 years and older.
 
Probenecid is classified as a pregnancy risk category B. Probenecid crosses the placenta and appears in cord blood; however, has been administered during pregnancy without adverse effects observed in the mother or child. It is not known whether probenecid is excreted into human milk; however no problems have been reported during breast-feeding.

    Headline
    acute gout, uric acid nephrolithiasis
    General Information
    Probenecid is contraindicated in individuals with an acute gout attack or uric acid nephrolithiasis because it increases the excretion of uric acid, which can exacerbate stone formation. Therapy with probenecid should not be started until an acute gouty attack has subsided due to the increased risk for uric acid nephropathy. If an acute gout attack or exacerbation of gout is precipitated during probenecid therapy, probenecid may be continued without changing the dosage, and a full therapeutic dosage of colchicine or other appropriate therapy should be given to control the acute gout attack. Uric acid nephropathy or related symptoms associated with the use of probenecid in gouty individuals may be prevented by alkalization of the urine and liberal fluid intake. If urinary alkalinization is used, the acid-base status of the individual should be closely monitored to avoid acid/base imbalance. Advise individuals with gout to drink plenty of fluids each day and follow all instructions for dietary restrictions.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Use of probenecid during pregnancy is recommended only if the potential benefits to the mother outweigh the potential risks to the fetus. Probenecid crosses the placental barrier and appears in cord blood. There are no adequate and well controlled studies in pregnant women. Case reports of use for the treatment of hyperuricemia associated with gout or as single-doses with ampicillin for the treatment of urinary tract infection during pregnancy have not noted adverse fetal effects.
    Headline
    children, infants, neonates
    General Information
    Probenecid is contraindicated in neonates, infants, and children less than 2 years of age.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    The label for probenecid does not note any particular cautions for the use of probenecid during lactation. Data are limited regarding use of probenecid during breast-feeding; however, one case report has documented probenecid excretion into human milk. In this report, a 30-year-old lactating mother was administered probenecid (500 mg PO four times daily) in combination with cephalexin for a breast infection. After 16 days of treatment, 12 milk samples were collected. The average probenecid milk concentration was 964 mcg/L, corresponding to a relative infant dose 0.7% of the maternal weight-adjusted dose. Symptoms of severe diarrhea, discomfort, and crying were observed in the infant, and these were determined by the authors to be possibly related to probenecid, but more probably associated with cephalexin.
    Headline
    hematological disease
    General Information
    Probenecid therapy is contraindicated in individuals with known blood dyscrasia (hematological disease). Rarely, aplastic anemia, leukopenia, and anemia have been reported. Hemolytic anemia, which in some individuals could be related to genetic deficiency of G6PD in red blood cells, has also been reported with probenecid use in the adverse reactions section of the label. However, quality evidence that individuals with G6PD deficiency require special precaution with probenecid use are lacking, and experts state there is no evidence to avoid probenecid use in these patients despite some textbooks listing a caution.
    Headline
    geriatric, renal failure, renal impairment
    General Information
    The use of probenecid to increase serum penicillin concentrations is not recommended for people with renal impairment. Since renal function contributes to the mechanism of probenecid, avoid use in patients with gout who have renal failure or a glomerular filtration rate (GFR) of less than 30 mL/minute. Some experts recommend avoiding probenecid if the CrCl is less than 50 mL/minute. Probenecid loses some efficacy at creatinine clearance (CrCl) less than 80 mL/minute and is completely ineffective when the glomerular filtration rate is less than 30 mL/minute. According to the Beers Criteria, probenecid should be avoided in geriatric adults with a CrCl less than 30 mL/minute due to loss of effectiveness.
    Headline
    peptic ulcer disease
    General Information
    Probenecid should be used with caution in people with a history of peptic ulcer disease.
    Headline
    increased risk for tumor lysis syndrome
    General Information
    Use probenecid with caution in individuals with an increased risk for tumor lysis syndrome (TLS), and take appropriate preventive measures to reduce the risk. Probenecid may increase the risk of uric acid nephropathy in individuals at risk for tumor lysis syndrome.

Description
  Text
  Probenecid is an oral uricosuric agent used to treat hyperuricemia associated with chronic gout or secondary to other causes (e.g., drug-induced hyperuricemia). Probenecid is not effective for acute attacks of gout and actually can aggravate inflammation if administered during the initial stages. The drug may be coadminstered with colchicine or selected antibiotics to delay the renal clearance of these drugs, thereby elevating and prolonging serum concentrations when necessary for certain patients. Probenecid was approved by the FDA in 1951.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Probenecid is a uricosuric and renal tubular blocking agent. It inhibits the tubular reabsorption of urate, thus increasing the urinary excretion of uric acid and decreasing serum urate levels. Effective uricosuria reduces the miscible urate pool, retards urate deposition, and promotes resorption of urate deposits. Probenecid also competitively inhibits the active reabsorption of uric acid at the proximal convoluted tubule, facilitating urinary excretion of uric acid and decreasing plasma urate concentrations. The drug does not affect the glomerular filtration rate or reabsorption of glucose, arginine, urea, sodium, potassium, and chloride. The tubular reabsorption of phosphorus is inhibited in hypoparathyroid but not in euparathyroid individuals.
 
At the level of the proximal and distal tubules, probenecid inhibits the secretion of weak acids, thereby increasing plasma concentrations. Some of the medications affected by this action of probenecid include most cephalosporins and penicillins, and other beta-lactam-related antibiotics such as aztreonam and imipenem; indomethacin; and methotrexate. In some cases, probenecid administration can more than double the serum concentrations of the affected drug. Elimination half-life is prolonged correspondingly.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics

    Oral Route

    Probenecid is completely absorbed following an oral dose.

    Renal Impairment
    Description
    Probenecid has been used in patients with some renal impairment, but dosage requirements may be increased. Probenecid may not be effective in chronic renal insufficiency particularly when the glomerular filtration rate is 30 mL/minute or less.
  Text
  Probenecid is administered orally. The drug distributes throughout the body tissues and is 75% to 95% plasma protein-bound, predominantly to albumin. Probenecid undergoes hepatic metabolism resulting in active metabolites. Both parent drug and active metabolites are eliminated renally, mainly by tubular secretion. Small amounts of unchanged probenecid are filtered at the glomeruli. The parent drug is nearly completely reclaimed via tubular reabsorption; minor amounts of metabolites undergo tubular reabsorption. The plasma half-life of probenecid is dose-dependent. Half-life ranges from 3 to 8 hours for a 500 mg dose and 6 to 12 hours for larger doses. Both unchanged drug and its metabolites are excreted in the urine; the unchanged drug is reabsorbed.
 
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes and drug transporters: OAT1 and OAT3
Probenecid inhibits the drug transporters OAT1 and OAT3. Probenecid inhibits the tubular secretion of penicillin and usually increases penicillin plasma levels by any route the antibiotic is given. A 2-fold to 4-fold elevation has been demonstrated for various penicillins. Probenecid also has been reported to inhibit the renal transport of many other compounds including, but not limited to, indomethacin, methotrexate, sulfonamides, and sulfonylureas. Probenecid does not influence plasma concentrations of salicylates, nor the excretion of streptomycin, chloramphenicol, chlortetracycline, oxytetracycline, or neomycin.

Dosage Adjustment Guidelines
  Hepatic Impairment
  Specific guidelines for dosage adjustments in hepatic impairment are not available; it appears that no dosage adjustments are needed.
  Renal Impairment
  CrCl more than 50 mL/minute: Use with caution in patients with gout with renal impairment; adjust dosage to needed therapeutic response. Probenecid has been used in patients with some renal impairment, as some degree of renal impairment may be present in patients with gout. Use a reduced initial dosage in patients with renal impairment and titrate to response. A total daily dosage of 1,000 mg/day may be adequate. However, if necessary, the daily dosage may be increased by 500 mg increments every 4 weeks within tolerance (and usually not above 2,000 mg/day) if symptoms of gouty arthritis are not controlled or the 24 hour uric acid excretion is not above 700 mg. Because of its mechanism of action, probenecid is not recommended in conjunction with a penicillin medication in the presence of known renal impairment.
CrCl 50 mL/minute or less: Avoid use of drug. Probenecid may not be effective in chronic renal insufficiency particularly when the glomerular filtration rate is 30 mL/minute or less.
