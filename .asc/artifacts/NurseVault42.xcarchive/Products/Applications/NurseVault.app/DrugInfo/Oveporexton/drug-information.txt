  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  Contraindications And Precautions
    Headline
    Drug Interactions
    General Information
    The coadministration of certain medications may lead to harm and require avoidance or therapy modification; review all drug interactions prior to concomitant use of other medications.
    Headline
    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components.
    Headline
    overactive bladder
    General Information
    Oveporexton may cause or worsen urinary frequency and urgency. Prior to initiating treatment, inform patients about the risks of urinary frequency and urgency and screen them for lower urinary tract symptoms. People with a history of overactive bladder should be monitored for worsening urinary tract symptoms while receiving oveporexton.
    Headline
    Child-Pugh class C, hepatic failure
    General Information
    Avoid use of oveporexton in patients with severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh class C) or hepatic failure as it has not been studied in this population. No dosage adjustments are required when used in people with mild or moderate hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh class A or B).
    Headline
    renal failure
    General Information
    Use of oveporexton in people with renal failure (i.e., severe renal impairment on dialysis; eGFR less than 15 mL/minute) should be avoided, as it has not been studied in this population. No dosing adjustments are necessary for people with mild, moderate, or severe renal impairment not requiring dialysis (eGFR 15 mL/minute or greater).
    Headline
    substance abuse disorder
    General Information
    Oveporexton has the potential for abuse. Health care providers should carefully evaluate individuals for a recent history of substance abuse disorder, especially those with a history of CNS stimulant (e.g., methylphenidate, amphetamine, or cocaine) abuse and follow such individuals closely, observing them for signs of misuse or abuse of oveporexton (e.g., incrementation of doses, drug-seeking behavior).
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Available data from clinical trials with oveporexton use during pregnancy are insufficient to identify a drug-associated risk of major birth defects, miscarriage, or other adverse outcomes. Animal reproduction studies did not show evidence of embryofetal toxicity or effects on pre- and post-natal development. There is a pregnancy exposure registry that monitors pregnancy outcomes in people who are exposed to oveporexton during pregnancy. Patients should be encouraged to enroll in the oveporexton pregnancy registry if they become pregnant. To enroll or obtain information from the registry, call 1-877-371-0309.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Use oveporexton with caution during breast-feeding. There are no data available on the presence of oveporexton in human milk, the effects on the breastfed infant, or the effects on milk production. Animal studies indicate that oveporexton was present in the milk of lactating rats. When a drug is present in animal milk, it is likely that the drug will be present in human milk. The developmental and health benefits of breast-feeding should be considered along with the clinical need for oveporexton and any potential adverse effects on the breastfed infant from oveporexton or from the underlying maternal condition.

Description
  Text
  Oveporexton is an orexin receptor 2 (OX2R) agonist. It is indicated for the treatment of narcolepsy type 1 (narcolepsy with cataplexy) in adults. Narcolepsy type 1 (NT1) is characterized by a loss of orexin neuropeptide signaling; oveporexton works to maintain wakefulness and reduce excessive daytime sleepiness. The efficacy of oveporexton for the treatment of NT1 in adults was established in 2 randomized, multicenter, double-blind, placebo-controlled 12-week parallel group studies. In both studies, the primary endpoint was the change from baseline to week 12 in mean sleep latency from the Maintenance of Wakefulness test (MWT), with secondary endpoints including changes in the Epworth Sleepiness Scale total score (ESS), Weekly Cataplexy Rate (WCR), Narcolepsy Severity Scale for Clinical Trials (NSS-CT), and the Patient Global Impression of Change (PGI-C) score. Adults with NT1 receiving oveporexton demonstrated statistically significant improvements in the MWT, ESS, NSS-CT, WCR, and the PGI-C score at week 12. Common side effects include insomnia, urinary frequency and urinary urgency. Oveporexton was FDA approved in August 2026.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  The mechanism of action of oveporexton in the treatment of narcolepsy type 1 (NT1) is through agonism of the orexin receptor 2 (OX2R). OX2R activation promotes wakefulness and suppresses rapid eye movement (REM) sleep. Because NT1 is characterized by deficient orexin neuropeptide signaling, pharmacologic activation of OX2R may compensate for this loss by restoring activity within the endogenous orexin pathway. This mechanism may support normalization of sleep-wake regulation and REM sleep architecture, rather than providing symptomatic benefit alone.

CYP3A4 is the predominant isoenzyme involved in oveporexton metabolism. No major metabolites have been identified in the circulation. After a single 25 mg radiolabeled oral dose of oveporexton, 80.5% of the total dose was recovered in feces and 11% was recovered in urine. Renal elimination of unchanged drug accounts for less than 1% of the total clearance of oveporexton. The mean terminal elimination half-life of oveporexton is approximately 23.2 hours.
 
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes and drug transporters: CYP3A4
Oveporexton is predominantly metabolized by CYP3A4. Concomitant administration of oveporexton with a strong CYP3A4 inhibitor increased oveporexton AUC by 7.4-fold and Cmax by 1.7-fold compared with monotherapy. The half-life of oveporexton was approximately 5-fold longer in the presence of the strong CYP3A4 inhibitor. Concomitant use of oveporexton with a moderate CYP3A inhibitor increased oveporexton AUC by 2.9-fold and Cmax by 1.5-fold increase compared to the use of oveporexton alone. Concomitant administration of oveporexton with a strong CYP3A4 inducer decreased oveporexton AUC by 81% and Cmax by 53% compared with monotherapy. Concomitant use of oveporexton with a moderate CYP3A inducer is predicted to decrease AUC and Cmax by 73% and 55%, respectively, compared to use of oveporexton alone.

Dosage Adjustment Guidelines
  Hepatic Impairment
  Mild to moderate hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh class A or B): No dosage adjustment is necessary.
Severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh class C): Avoid use; no studies have been conducted in this population.
  Renal Impairment
  eGFR 15 to less than 90 mL/minute: No dosage adjustment necessary.
eGFR less than 15 mL/minute: Avoid use in people with severe renal impairment on dialysis; no studies have been conducted in this population.
