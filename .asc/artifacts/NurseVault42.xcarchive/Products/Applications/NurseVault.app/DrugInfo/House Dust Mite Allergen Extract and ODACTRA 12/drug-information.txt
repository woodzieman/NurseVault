Drug Class: House dust mite (HDM) allergen extract is a sublingual immunotherapy (SLIT) approved for the treatment of allergic rhinitis with or without conjunctivitis in patients 5 through 65 years of age.

Routes: sublingual, subcutaneous; Common adult doses: 1 tablet (12 SQ-HDM)/day sublingually. Maximum dosage not available for subcutaneous use.

Indication Specific Dosing
  General Information
  General Dosing Information
Prior to use of house dust mite (HDM) allergen, confirm hypersensitivity to HDM allergen by in vitro testing for IgE antibodies to Dermatophagoides farinae or Dermatophagoides pteronyssinus house dust mites, or skin testing to licensed house dust mite allergen extracts.
Standardized allergenic extracts are not directly interchangeable with allergenic extracts of the same labeled potency from different manufacturers. Patients being switched from other types of extracts to standardized allergenic extracts should be started as though they were coming under treatment for the first time. Re-evaluate the patient with the newly selected extract.
Consider mixtures of standardized mite (D. farinae and D. pteronyssinus) for treatment of patients who are sensitive to both species. Prior to the initiation of therapy, establish clinical sensitivity by careful evaluation of the patient's history confirmed by diagnostic skin testing. Hyposensitization should not be prescribed for sensitivities to allergens which can be easily avoided.

        Dosage Description
        The initial dose for immunotherapy is based on the individual's percutaneous skin test reactivity and history. Usual dose: 0.05 mL of a 0.01 to 0.03 Allergenic Units/mL dilution given 1 to 2 times per week until the maintenance dose is reached.     Headline
    cardiovascular disease, mast cell disorder, serious hypersensitivity reactions or anaphylaxis
    General Information
    House dust mite (HDM) allergen extract and standardized mite extract can cause life-threatening allergic reactions, including a risk of serious hypersensitivity reactions or anaphylaxis. HDM allergen extract is contraindicated in individuals with a history of any severe systemic allergic reaction or a history of any severe local reaction after taking any sublingual allergen immunotherapy. The use of more than 1 type of immunotherapy (i.e. allergy shots, sublingual immunotherapy) may increase the likelihood of a severe allergic reaction. Concomitant use of HDM allergen extract with other allergen immunotherapy has not been studied; concurrent use may increase the risk of local or systemic reactions to either subcutaneous or sublingual immunotherapy. Systemic allergic reactions including life-threatening anaphylaxis and severe local reactions (e.g., laryngopharyngeal swelling) that may compromise breathing may occur; treatment with epinephrine may be required. Discontinue the extract immediately in individuals who experience a systemic reaction. Re-evaluate individuals who have persistent or escalating local reactions in the mouth or throat, and consider discontinuation. Administration of the first dose of HDM allergen extract and standardized mite extract requires an experienced clinician and requires a specialized care setting due to the risk of severe allergic reactions; give the first dose in a healthcare setting where acute allergic reactions can be recognized and treated by an experienced clinician. Observe individuals for at least 30 minutes post-dose for signs or symptoms of a severe systemic or local reaction. If the individual tolerates the first HDM allergen extract dose, subsequent doses may be administered outside the health care setting when auto-injectable epinephrine is available. Instruct individuals to recognize symptoms of a severe allergic reaction, about the proper use of auto-injectable epinephrine, and to seek immediate medical care upon use. The extracts may not be suitable for individuals with medical conditions that may decrease the individual's ability to survive a serious allergic reaction or increase the risk of adverse reactions after epinephrine administration. These conditions may include, but are not limited to, mast cell disorder and cardiovascular disease, including unstable angina, recent myocardial infarction, significant cardiac arrhythmias, and uncontrolled hypertension. Individuals receiving treatment with beta-blockers may be refractory to the usual dose of epinephrine. The extracts may not be suitable for individuals taking medications that can potentiate or inhibit the effects of epinephrine or decrease the effectiveness of inhaled bronchodilators.
    Headline
    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components.
    Headline
    pulmonary disease, severe asthma, uncontrolled asthma
    General Information
    House dust mite (HDM) allergen extract is contraindicated in individuals with severe asthma, unstable asthma, or uncontrolled asthma. Withhold immunotherapy with HDM allergen extract if the individual is experiencing an acute asthma exacerbation. The extracts may not be suitable for individuals who have pulmonary disease with markedly compromised lung function (either chronic or acute) since they may reduce the individual's ability to survive a serious allergic reaction or increase the risk of adverse reactions after epinephrine administration.
    Headline
    eosinophilic esophagitis
    General Information
    House dust mite (HDM) allergen extract is contraindicated in patients with a history of eosinophilic esophagitis. Discontinue the extract in patients who experience severe or persistent gastroesophageal symptoms (e.g., dysphagia, chest pain) and consider a diagnosis of eosinophilic esophagitis.
    Headline
    oral wound, stomatitis
    General Information
    Withhold therapy with house dust mite (HDM) allergen extract in individuals with any type of oral inflammation (e.g., oral lichen planus, mouth ulcers, stomatitis, or thrush) or an oral wound, such as those after dental work including dental extraction or oral surgery, until complete healing of the oral cavity occurs.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Available data are insufficient to determine the pregnancy-related risks of house dust mite (HDM) allergen extract or standardized mite extract. In animal studies, HDM administration to pregnant mice on gestation days 6 through 17 did not produce adverse fetal developmental outcomes.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Use house dust mite (HDM) allergen extract and standardized mite extract with caution during breast-feeding. There are no data on the presence of these allergen extracts in human milk, their effects on the breastfed child, or their effects on milk production. Consider the developmental and health benefits of breastfeeding, the patient's clinical need for treatment, and any potential adverse effects on the breastfed child from the extracts or the underlying condition.

Description
  Text
  House dust mite (HDM) allergen extract is a sublingual immunotherapy (SLIT) approved for the treatment of allergic rhinitis with or without conjunctivitis in patients 5 through 65 years of age. HDM allergy is confirmed by in vitro testing for IgE antibodies to Dermatophagoides farinae or Dermatophagoides pteronyssinus house dust mites, or skin testing to licensed house dust mite allergen extracts. Standardized mite extract is indicated as subcutaneous therapy for the diagnosis and treatment of hypersensitivity in patients with symptoms compatible with dust mite allergy. It is a treatment for patients exhibiting allergic reactions to seasonal pollens, dust mites, molds, animal danders, and various other inhalants, in situations where the offending allergen cannot be avoided. Mixtures of standardized mite (Dermatophagoides farinae or Dermatophagoides pteronyssinus) should be considered for treatment of patients who are sensitive to both species. Prior to the initiation of therapy, clinical sensitivity should be established by careful evaluation of the patient's history confirmed by diagnostic skin testing; hyposensitization should not be prescribed for sensitivities to allergens which can be easily avoided. Allergen immunotherapy modifies the disease process and is used in patients who are not adequately controlled with environmental or medication changes. In general, SLIT is associated with fewer serious adverse reactions than subcutaneous immunotherapy; however, experience with SLIT in patients with severe asthma, a population that may be more likely to experience serious reactions, is limited. Most adverse reactions to SLIT are local and transient and do not require treatment discontinuation. However, as with any immunotherapy, severe systemic and local allergic reactions (e.g., anaphylaxis, laryngopharyngeal swelling) are possible. After administration of the first dose at the health care provider's office, where the patient can be observed for potential adverse reactions, HDM allergen extract sublingual tablets can be taken at home. Standardized mite extract immunotherapy is given under the direction of a health care provider. Standardized mite extract was FDA-approved in 1988. HDM allergen extract was approved by the FDA in March 2017.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  The precise mechanisms of action of house dust mite (HDM) allergen immunotherapy or standardized mite extract immunotherapy have not been fully established. When HDM allergen extract is allowed to dissolve sublingually, allergens bind to epithelial cells and cross the oral mucosa, where they are taken up by tolerogenic antigen-presenting cells (i.e., Langerhans cells and myeloid dendritic cells). The allergens are then processed into small immunogenic peptides, and the antigen-presenting cells migrate into local regional lymph nodes (submaxillary, cervical, internal jugular). There, allergen peptide fragments are presented to naive CD4+ T cells. This interaction stimulates suppressive T helper (Th) 1 and regulatory T cells and inhibits the activation and proliferation of Th2 cells. Subsequently, T cells encourage B cells to produce protective antibody responses, including secretion of allergen-specific IgG4 and IgA and, later, inhibition of IgE. Regulatory T cells may also suppress other inflammatory cells (e.g., eosinophils, mast cells, basophils) either by cytokine secretion or direct cell-to-cell contact. CD4+ T cells eventually migrate into the blood and tissues, resulting in allergen tolerance.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Oral Route
    Description
    Pharmacokinetic studies of subjects treated with radiolabeled allergens have demonstrated that the pharmacokinetic values of the sublingual route of administration are independent of the allergen involved. Parietaria judaica is a perennial plant with highly allergenic pollen. Human pharmacodynamic studies of radiolabeled Parietaria judaica allergen have shown little systemic absorption into the bloodstream through the sublingual mucosa, despite its highly vascular nature. In 1 study, radioactivity was not detectable in the plasma until swallowing occurred, at which point the plasma radioactivity slowly rose and peaked at approximately 2 hours. In another study using Parietaria judaica, a small amount of the allergen (about 2% of the administered dose) was detected within the oral mucosa 20 hours after dosing. It has been suggested allergens bind to epithelial cells within a few minutes. In a biodistribution study of sublingual radiolabeled ovalbumin in mice, allergen crossed the oral mucosa within 15 to 30 minutes and was captured by antigen-presenting cells within 30 to 60 minutes. At 60 minutes, allergen began to disappear from the submucosa, perhaps coinciding with uptake and processing by the antigen-presenting cells. Within 12 to 24 hours after administration, the antigen-presenting cells migrate to the lymph nodes where they interact with CD4+ cells and further promote the desensitization process. Trials have showed a relevant dose-dependent increase of allergen-specific IgE levels in all subjects receiving HDM sublingual immunotherapy (SLIT) tablets when compared to subjects receiving placebo. These trials have also found increased levels of IgE-blocking factor (IgE-BF), which has inhibitory activity against circulating IgEs, after 28 days of treatment with more than 4 HDM SLIT tablets.

      Diagnostic Testing
Use standardized mite extract (Dermatophagoides farinae) or standardized mite extract (Dermatophagoides pteronyssinus) to identify individuals who are allergic to either species. Do not use standardized mite mixture extract (D. farinae and D. pteronyssinus) for diagnostic testing. A positive result would not permit identification of the specific mite allergen.
Administer percutaneous test prior to the administration of the intradermal test.
In diagnosing the sensitive individual, the symptom history must be associated with exposure to the allergen. Skin testing is used in conjunction with a definitive history for diagnosing individual sensitivities.
Percutaneous Skin Testing
Place 1 drop of concentrated standardized mite extract on the skin and using a skin test device (sterile needle, lancet, or bifurcated needle), pierce through the skin with a slight lifting motion.
For individuals at higher risk for anaphylaxis, initiate skin testing with 1 drop of a diluted standardized mite extract. If negative, retest with the next higher concentration. Administer doses 15 to 20 minutes apart.
Include a positive histamine skin control test to identify any false negative skin tests in individuals that may have had recent antihistamine drug use.
Include a negative control (50% glycerin solution) to detect false positive responses, which can occur when the patient has a non-specific reaction to a diluent.
Intradermal Skin Testing
Dilute stock concentrations by a minimum of 100-fold for intradermal testing. Dilutions of 1,000-fold or greater are appropriate for individuals with a history of an adverse reaction.
Inject the dose intradermally using a 1 mL syringe. If a second intradermal test is needed for negative results, administer doses 15 to 20 minutes apart.
Include a positive histamine skin control test to identify any false negative skin tests in individuals that may have had recent antihistamine drug use.
Include a negative control (sterile albumin with phenol or sterile buffered saline with phenol) to detect false positive responses, which can occur when the patient has a non-specific reaction to a diluent.

Dilution
Dilute extract dilutions with sterile albumin saline with phenol or sterile normal saline with phenol.
Start from a stock concentrate and prepare a 10-fold dilution by adding 0.5 mL of concentrate to 4.5 mL of sterile aqueous diluent.
Prepare subsequent dilutions similarly. Prepare as many additional serial dilutions as necessary to reach the appropriate concentration.
Storage: Store extract dilutions between 2 and 8 degrees C (36 and 46 degrees F).
