Drug Class: Bictegravir; lenacapavir is a fixed-dose, 2-drug combination of bictegravir, an HIV-1 integrase strand-transfer inhibitor (INSTI) that blocks viral DNA integration, and lenacapavir, a multistage HIV-1 capsid inhibitor that disrupts capsid function during viral replication.

Routes: oral; Common adult doses: bictegravir 75 mg/day PO; lenacapavir 50 mg/day PO.

Common Side Effects (excluding routine GI upset): angioedema, stevens-johnson syndrome, toxic epidermal necrolysis, urticaria

In an Overdose: No overdose/antidote information is listed in this file.

Key Nursing Considerations: Contraindications/precautions: This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components. Bictegravir; lenacapavir has no activity against hepatitis B virus (HBV).

    Headline
    Treatment-Related Restrictions of Use
    General Information
    Bictegravir; lenacapavir has no activity against hepatitis B virus (HBV). Individuals with HBV coinfection who switch to bictegravir; lenacapavir from an antiretroviral regimen with activity against HBV, or who develop HBV coinfection while receiving bictegravir; lenacapavir, should be closely monitored, and specific anti-HBV therapy should be considered when clinically appropriate.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Available data from observational studies and the Antiretroviral Pregnancy Registry on bictegravir use during pregnancy have not established a drug-associated risk of major birth defects, miscarriage, or adverse maternal or fetal outcomes. Available data for lenacapavir from a randomized, controlled trial in individuals without HIV-1 have not identified a drug-associated risk of miscarriage or adverse pregnancy outcomes compared with an active control. Two major birth defects, both ventricular septal defects, occurred among 208 lenacapavir-exposed pregnancies; the observed rate did not exceed the background prevalence. However, risk estimates remain imprecise because of the limited number of exposed pregnancies. No adverse developmental effects were observed in animal studies at lenacapavir exposures at least 3 times the human exposure at the recommended human dose of bictegravir; lenacapavir. No clinically relevant changes in lenacapavir exposure occurred during pregnancy or postpartum; however, bictegravir exposure was lower during pregnancy than postpartum. Therefore, viral load should be monitored closely in pregnant individuals receiving bictegravir; lenacapavir. A pregnancy exposure registry monitors outcomes in individuals exposed to bictegravir; lenacapavir during pregnancy; health care providers are encouraged to register individuals with the Antiretroviral Pregnancy Registry at 1-800-258-4263.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Use bictegravir; lenacapavir with caution during breast-feeding. Bictegravir is present in human milk. There are no data on the effects of bictegravir on the breastfed child or milk production. Lenacapavir is present in human milk; however, infant exposure appears to be low, and no adverse effects have been observed. In a small study of 8 lactating individuals receiving injectable lenacapavir, the median milk-to-plasma ratio was 0.63 (range, 0.29 to 1.9). Among 10 breastfed infants exposed to lenacapavir from birth to less than 13 weeks postpartum, the median infant-to-lactating person plasma ratio was 0.06 (range, 0.01 to 0.2). It is not known whether lenacapavir affects milk production.

Interactions
Abacavir; Dolutegravir; lamiVUDine: (Moderate) Monitor for increased toxicity of dolutegravir if coadministered with lenacapavir. Concurrent use may increase the plasma concentrations of dolutegravir. Dolutegravir is a CYP3A, P-gp, and BCRP substrate and lenacapavir is a moderate CYP3A, P-gp, and BCRP inhibitor.
Abemaciclib: (Moderate) Monitor for an increase in abemaciclib-related adverse reactions if coadministration with lenacapavir is necessary; consider reducing the dose of abemaciclib in 50-mg decrements if toxicities occur. Discontinue abemaciclib for patients unable to tolerate 50 mg twice daily. Abemaciclib is a CYP3A substrate and lenacapavir is a moderate CYP3A inhibitor. Coadministration with other moderate CYP3A inhibitors is predicted to increase the relative potency adjusted unbound AUC of abemaciclib plus its active metabolites (M2, M18, and M20) by approximately 1.6- to 2.4-fold.
Acalabrutinib: (Major) Decrease the acalabrutinib dose to 100 mg PO once daily if coadministered with lenacapavir. Coadministration may result in increased acalabrutinib exposure and toxicity (e.g., infection, bleeding, and atrial arrhythmias). Acalabrutinib is a CYP3A substrate; lenacapavir is a moderate CYP3A inhibitor. In physiologically based pharmacokinetic (PBPK) simulations, the AUC of acalabrutinib was increased by approximately 2- to 3-fold when acalabrutinib was coadministered with moderate CYP3A inhibitors.
Acetaminophen; Caffeine; Dihydrocodeine: (Moderate) Consider a reduced dose of dihydrocodeine with frequent monitoring for respiratory depression and sedation if concurrent use of lenacapavir is necessary. If lenacapavir is discontinued, consider increasing the dihydrocodeine dose until stable drug effects are achieved and monitor for evidence of opioid withdrawal. Concomitant use of dihydrocodeine with lenacapavir may increase dihydrocodeine plasma concentrations, resulting in greater metabolism by CYP2D6, increased dihydromorphine concentrations, and prolonged opioid adverse reactions, including hypotension, respiratory depression, profound sedation, coma, and death. If lenacapavir is discontinued, monitor the patient carefully and consider increasing the opioid dosage if appropriate. Lenacapavir is a moderate inhibitor of CYP3A, an isoenzyme partially responsible for the metabolism of dihydrocodeine.
Acetaminophen; Codeine: (Moderate) Concomitant use of codeine with lenacapavir may increase codeine plasma concentrations, resulting in greater metabolism by CYP2D6, increased morphine concentrations, and prolonged opioid adverse reactions, including hypotension, respiratory depression, profound sedation, coma, and death. It is recommended to avoid this combination when codeine is being used for cough. If coadministration is necessary, monitor patients closely at frequent intervals and consider a dosage reduction of codeine until stable drug effects are achieved. Discontinuation of lenacapavir could decrease codeine plasma concentrations, decrease opioid efficacy, and potentially lead to a withdrawal syndrome in those with physical dependence to codeine. If lenacapavir is discontinued, monitor the patient carefully and consider increasing the opioid dosage if appropriate. Codeine is primarily metabolized by CYP2D6 to morphine, and by CYP3A to norcodeine; norcodeine does not have analgesic properties. Lenacapavir is a moderate inhibitor of CYP3A.
Acetaminophen; HYDROcodone: (Moderate) Consider a reduced dose of hydrocodone with frequent monitoring for respiratory depression and sedation if concurrent use of lenacapavir is necessary. It is recommended to avoid this combination when hydrocodone is being used for cough. Hydrocodone is a CYP3A substrate, and coadministration with CYP3A inhibitors like lenacapavir can increase hydrocodone exposure resulting in increased or prolonged opioid effects including fatal respiratory depression, particularly when an inhibitor is added to a stable dose of hydrocodone. These effects could be more pronounced in patients also receiving a CYP2D6 inhibitor. If lenacapavir is discontinued, hydrocodone plasma concentrations will decrease resulting in reduced efficacy of the opioid and potential withdrawal syndrome in a patient who has developed physical dependence to hydrocodone.

Amobarbital: (Major) Concurrent use of lenacapavir for HIV treatment and barbiturates is contraindicated due to the risk for reduced lenacapavir exposure, which may lead to loss of therapeutic efficacy and development of resistance. If lenacapavir is used for HIV PrEP and use with barbiturates is necessary, supplemental lenacapavir doses are required to maintain effective drug concentrations. On the day barbiturates are initiated, which should be at least 2 days after lenacapavir initiation, a supplemental dose of 927 mg subcutaneously and 600 mg orally should be given (Step 1). The following day, an additional oral dose of 600 mg should be administered (Step 2). If the barbiturate is continued for longer than 6 months, this 2-step supplemental dosing regimen must be repeated every 6 months. After discontinuation of barbiturates, the standard 6-month injection schedule for lenacapavir should be resumed. Recommendations are not available for those already on barbiturates at the time of lenacapavir initiation or those receiving weekly oral lenacapavir. Lenacapavir is a CYP3A substrate and barbiturates are strong CYP3A inducers. Concomitant use with another strong CYP3A inducer reduced lenacapavir overall exposure by 84%.

Avapritinib: (Major) Avoid coadministration of avapritinib with lenacapavir due to the risk of increased avapritinib-related adverse reactions. Barbiturates: (Major) Concurrent use of lenacapavir for HIV treatment and barbiturates is contraindicated due to the risk for reduced lenacapavir exposure, which may lead to loss of therapeutic efficacy and development of resistance. If lenacapavir is used for HIV PrEP and use with barbiturates is necessary, supplemental lenacapavir doses are required to maintain effective drug concentrations. On the day barbiturates are initiated, which should be at least 2 days after lenacapavir initiation, a supplemental dose of 927 mg subcutaneously and 600 mg orally should be given (Step 1). The following day, an additional oral dose of 600 mg should be administered (Step 2). If the barbiturate is continued for longer than 6 months, this 2-step supplemental dosing regimen must be repeated every 6 months. After discontinuation of barbiturates, the standard 6-month injection schedule for lenacapavir should be resumed. Recommendations are not available for those already on barbiturates at the time of lenacapavir initiation or those receiving weekly oral lenacapavir. Lenacapavir is a CYP3A substrate and barbiturates are strong CYP3A inducers. Concomitant use with another strong CYP3A inducer reduced lenacapavir overall exposure by 84%.
Benzhydrocodone; Acetaminophen: (Moderate) Consider a reduced dose of benzhydrocodone with frequent monitoring for respiratory depression and sedation if concurrent use of lenacapavir is necessary. Benzhydrocodone is a prodrug for hydrocodone. Hydrocodone is a CYP3A substrate, and coadministration with moderate CYP3A inhibitors like lenacapavir can increase hydrocodone exposure resulting in increased or prolonged opioid effects including fatal respiratory depression, particularly when an inhibitor is added to a stable dose of benzhydrocodone. These effects could be more pronounced in patients also receiving a CYP2D6 inhibitor. If lenacapavir is discontinued, hydrocodone plasma concentrations will decrease resulting in reduced efficacy of the opioid and potential withdrawal syndrome in a patient who has developed physical dependence to benzhydrocodone.

Description
  Text
  Bictegravir; lenacapavir is a fixed-dose, 2-drug combination of bictegravir, an HIV-1 integrase strand-transfer inhibitor (INSTI) that blocks viral DNA integration, and lenacapavir, a multistage HIV-1 capsid inhibitor that disrupts capsid function during viral replication. It is indicated as a complete regimen for virologically suppressed individuals with HIV-1 infection who are receiving a stable antiretroviral regimen and have no known or suspected resistance to either component. The dosing regimen requires a 2-day initiation period with oral lenacapavir. In the phase 3 ARTISTRY-1 and ARTISTRY-2 trials, virologically suppressed adults who switched to bictegravir; lenacapavir maintained virologic suppression through 48 weeks. Bictegravir; lenacapavir was FDA-approved in August 2026.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Bictegravir: Bictegravir inhibits the integrase strand transfer activity of HIV-1 integrase (integrase strand transfer inhibitor [INSTI]), an HIV-1-encoded enzyme that is required for viral replication. The inhibition of integrase prevents the integration of linear HIV-1 DNA into host genomic DNA, blocking the formation of the HIV-1 provirus and propagation of the virus.
Lenacapavir: Lenacapavir is a multistage, selective inhibitor of HIV-1 capsid function that directly binds to the interface between capsid protein (p24) subunits in hexamers. Surface plasmon resonance sensorgrams showed dose-dependent and saturable binding of lenacapavir to cross-linked wild-type capsid hexamer with an equilibrium binding constant (KD) of 1.4 nM. Lenacapavir inhibits HIV-1 replication by interfering with multiple essential steps of the viral lifecycle, including capsid-mediated nuclear uptake of HIV-1 proviral DNA (by blocking the binding of nuclear import proteins to capsid), virus assembly and release (by interfering with Gag/Gag-Pol functioning, reducing production of capsid protein subunits), and capsid core formation (by disrupting the rate of capsid subunit association, leading to malformed capsids). Lenacapavir has antiviral activity against HIV-1 and HIV-2; however, the drug is 15- to 25-fold less active against HIV-2 isolates relative to HIV-1.
 
Antiviral Activity
Antiviral activity of bictegravir and lenacapavir was not antagonistic in cell culture.
Bictegravir: Bictegravir demonstrated antiviral activity against HIV-1 clinical isolates representing groups M, N, and O, including subtypes A, B, C, D, E, F, and G. The median 50% effective concentration (EC50) was 0.55 nanomolar (range, less than 0.05 to 1.71 nanomolar). The EC50 against one HIV-2 isolate was 1.1 nanomolar.
Lenacapavir: Lenacapavir demonstrated antiviral activity against HIV-1 and HIV-2. The EC50 against laboratory and clinical HIV-1 isolates ranged from 30 to 190 picomolar. Against tested HIV-1 groups and subtypes, EC50 values ranged from 20 to 160 picomolar; the median EC50 against subtype B isolates was 40 picomolar. Lenacapavir was 15- to 25-fold less active against HIV-2 isolates than against HIV-1 isolates.
 
Resistance (Cell Culture)
Bictegravir: HIV-1 isolates with reduced susceptibility to bictegravir were selected in cell culture. When introduced into wild-type virus, the M50I, R263K, and M50I plus R263K substitutions conferred 1.3-, 2.2-, and 2.9-fold reductions in susceptibility, respectively. In a second selection, the T66I, S153F, and T66I plus S153F substitutions were associated with 0.4-, 1.9-, and 0.5-fold changes in susceptibility, respectively. The S24G and E157K substitutions also emerged during selection.
Lenacapavir: HIV-1 variants with reduced susceptibility to lenacapavir were selected in cell culture. The capsid substitutions L56I, M66I, Q67H, K70N, N74D or N74S, and T107N, alone or in dual combinations, conferred 4- to more than 3,226-fold reductions in phenotypic susceptibility compared with wild-type virus. The M66I substitution conferred a more than 3,226-fold reduction; Q67H and T107N conferred 4- to 6.3-fold reductions; K70N, N74D, and Q67H/N74S conferred 22- to 32-fold reductions; and L56I conferred a 239-fold reduction.
 
Resistance (Clinical Studies)
In ARTISTRY-1, 2 of 371 participants receiving bictegravir; lenacapavir met criteria for resistance testing through Week 48. One participant had no detectable resistance in capsid or integrase. The other had transient emergence of the Q148R integrase substitution at Week 24, which was associated with a 1.2-fold reduction in bictegravir susceptibility; Q148R was not detected at Week 48. In ARTISTRY-2, 2 of 383 participants receiving bictegravir; lenacapavir met criteria for resistance testing through Week 48. One participant had an isolated treatment-emergent R263K integrase substitution without detectable lenacapavir resistance substitutions in capsid. The R263K isolate remained phenotypically susceptible to bictegravir and was associated with a 2-fold reduction in bictegravir susceptibility. The other participant had no detectable emergent resistance substitutions in capsid or integrase.
 
Cross-resistance
Bictegravir: Cross-resistance has been observed among integrase strand-transfer inhibitors. Bictegravir showed no cross-resistance in mutants with resistance to lenacapavir or other antiretroviral classes.
Lenacapavir: In cell culture, lenacapavir remained active against HIV-1 variants resistant to integrase strand-transfer inhibitors, nonnucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitors, nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitors, and protease inhibitors, as well as fostemsavir, ibalizumab, maraviroc, and enfuvirtide. These findings support a nonoverlapping resistance profile.

Bictegravir: Bictegravir is more than 99% bound to human plasma proteins, with a blood-to-plasma ratio of 0.64. The majority of bictegravir elimination occurs through hepatic metabolism, which is mediated by CYP3A and UGT1A1 enzymes. Bictegravir is eliminated via the feces (60.3%) and urine (35%), with a median terminal plasma half-life of 19.3 to 21 hours.
Lenacapavir: Lenacapavir is more than 98.5% bound to human plasma proteins, with a blood-to-plasma ratio of 0.5 to 0.7. Lenacapavir is metabolized by CYP3A and UGT1A1 enzymes. The major route of elimination is excretion of unchanged drug into feces (76% of the dose); less than 1% of the dose is excreted in the urine. The median terminal plasma half-life is 10 to 12 days.
 
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes and drug transporters: CYP3A, UGT1A1, OCT2, MATE1, P-gp, BCRP
Bictegravir is a substrate of CYP3A and UGT1A1 and an inhibitor of OCT2 and MATE1. Lenacapavir is a substrate and moderate inhibitor of CYP3A, a substrate of UGT1A1, a substrate and inhibitor of P-gp, and an inhibitor of BCRP.
