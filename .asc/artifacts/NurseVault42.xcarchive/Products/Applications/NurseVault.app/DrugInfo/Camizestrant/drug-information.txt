    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components.
    Headline
    bradycardia, cardiomyopathy, coadministration with certain medications, congenital long QT syndrome, coronary artery disease, females, geriatric, hypocalcemia, hypokalemia, hypomagnesemia, QT prolongation
    General Information
    Avoid use of camizestrant in people with baseline QT prolongation or who have conditions that may increase the risk of QT prolongation or torsade de pointes, including bradycardia, congenital long QT syndrome, hypocalcemia, hypokalemia, hypomagnesemia, geriatric adults, females, structural abnormalities that interfere with electrical conduction (e.g., cardiomyopathy, coronary artery disease, ischemic heart disease), or in those who have other additional risk factors for QT prolongation or torsade de pointes. The use of other medications that have been associated with QT prolongation or torsade de pointes may further increase risk. The safety of camizestrant has not been established in patients with a baseline resting heart rate less than 55 bpm. Avoid coadministration with certain medications that can cause QTc interval prolongation and/or have a known risk of Torsades de Pointes, are strong CYP3A inhibitors, and/or drugs known to cause bradycardia. Perform an ECG at baseline, every week for the first 2 weeks of treatment, and periodically during treatment as clinically indicated; obtain serum electrolytes at baseline and during treatment as clinically indicated, and correct electrolyte abnormalities. Monitor heart rate more frequently during the first 30 days of treatment in patients with bradycardia (heart rate less than 60 bpm) at baseline, and in those on concomitant medications known to lower heart rate (e.g., beta-blockers). An interruption or discontinuation of therapy may be necessary if QT prolongation or bradycardia occur.
    Headline
    Child-Pugh class B, Child-Pugh class C, hepatic failure
    General Information
    Monitor patients with moderate or severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh class B or Child-Pugh class C) receiving camizestrant in combination with a CDK4/6 inhibitor for increased adverse reactions. A reduced dosing frequency of camizestrant is required for patients with severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh class C) who are receiving camizestrant in combination with ribociclib; no dosage modification is recommended for patients receiving camizestrant in combination with abemaciclib or palbociclib. There is no information on the use of camizestrant in patients with hepatic failure.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Avoid the use of camizestrant during pregnancy. Camizestrant can cause fetal harm, based on its mechanism of action and data from animal studies. There are no adequately controlled studies during human pregnancy. In animal reproduction studies, oral administration of camizestrant to rats during pregnancy at maternal exposures below the human AUC at the recommended dose resulted in no pregnancies, increased length of gestation, dystocia, and decreased pup survival and pup weight. Apprise individuals receiving camizestrant of the potential hazard to the fetus.
    Headline
    people who can cause pregnancy in others, people who may become pregnant, reproductive risk
    General Information
    Counsel people who may become pregnant and people who can cause pregnancy in others about the reproductive risk associated with camizestrant. Camizestrant may cause fetal harm. Verify pregnancy status prior to treatment initiation. Advise people who may become pregnant to use effective nonhormonal contraception while taking camizestrant and for 4 weeks after the last dose. For those that can cause pregnancy in others, contraception should be used during camizestrant treatment and for 1 week after the last dose. Based on findings from animal studies, camizestrant male and female infertility may occur; female infertility were reversible, but the reversibility of male fertility is unknown.
    Headline

    Advise patients to avoid breast-feeding while taking camizestrant and for 1 week after the last dose. There are no data on the presence of camizestrant or its metabolites in human milk, the effects on the breastfed child, or the effects on milk production.

Adverse Reactions

  Adverse Reactions
    Headline
    QT prolongation
    General Information
    Camizestrant is associated with QT prolongation when used in combination with a CDK4/6 inhibitor; there is insufficient information to fully characterize the effect size of camizestrant on the QTc interval. When used with ribociclib, a CDK4/6 inhibitor that causes QTc interval prolongation and is a strong CYP3A inhibitor (camizestrant is a CYP3A substrate), there is potential for an increased risk of Torsades de Pointes, other ventricular arrhythmias, and sudden death; one case of Torsades de Pointes was observed in a dose-finding trial when camizestrant was used with ribociclib. In a randomized clinical trial where patients received camizestrant in combination with palbociclib (76%), ribociclib (15%), or abemaciclib (10%), QTc interval prolongation occurred in 2.6% of patients. Avoid coadministration with certain medications that can cause QTc interval prolongation and/or have a known risk of Torsades de Pointes, are strong CYP3A inhibitors, and/or drugs known to cause bradycardia. An interruption or discontinuation of therapy may be necessary if QT prolongation occurs.

Description
  Text
  Camizestrant is an oral estrogen receptor antagonist. It is indicated, in combination with a CDK4/6 inhibitor, for the treatment of hormone receptor (HR)-positive, HER2-negative locally advanced or metastatic breast cancer upon detection of estrogen receptor-1 (ESR1) mutation during aromatase inhibitor and CDK4/6 inhibitor therapy. It carries a black box warning for the risk of arrhythmia when used concomitantly with other medications that prolong the QTc interval; avoid these medications (other than ribociclib) if possible. Monitor ECGs, heart rate, and electrolytes at baseline and during therapy. Camizestrant was approved by the FDA in September 2026.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Camizestrant is an estrogen receptor (ER) antagonist. In vitro, it binds to the ligand-binding domain of ER-alpha, antagonizing ER-alpha activity encoded by both wild-type and mutated ESR1. This induces proteasome-dependent degradation of ER-alpha, without agonizing it. Camizestrant demonstrated anti-tumor activity in ER-positive cell lines in vitro, and patient-derived xenograft (PDX) models in vivo, including those with wild-type or mutated ESR1 genes. Treatment with camizestrant and CDK4/6 inhibitors (abemaciclib, palbociclib, or ribociclib) demonstrated greater anti-tumor activity than single agent treatment in ER-positive cell lines and PDX models.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Oral Route
    Description
    Camizestrant has an absolute oral bioavailability of 43%. The Cmax of camizestrant is 73.6 ng/mL (CV, 40.2%) and the total systemic exposure (AUC) is 1,115 ng x hour/mL (CV, 40.8%); the median time to maximum concentration (Tmax) is 3 to 4 hours. The Cmax and AUC of camizestrant increase in greater than a dose-proportional manner over a range of 0.3 to 6 times the approved recommended dosage. Steady-state is reached by day 5; the accumulation ratio after multiple doses was 1.4 for Cmax and 1.8 for AUC.
Effects of Food
Administration of a high-fat meal (951 calories, 58% fat) did not have a clinically significant effect on the pharmacokinetics of camizestrant in healthy postmenopausal women.

  Text
  Camizestrant is administered orally. It is 75% bound to plasma proteins in a nonconcentration-dependent manner in vitro; the blood-to-plasma concentration ratio is 0.99. Camizestrant apparent volume of distribution is 1,888 L (CV, 29.3%). It has a terminal half-life of approximately 23 hours with an apparent clearance of 69 liters/hour (CV, 40.6%). Camizestrant is primarily metabolized by CYP3A and UGT1A4. Following a single oral radiolabeled dose to healthy subjects, 75% of total radioactivity was recovered in the feces (14.5% as unchanged drug) and 17% in the urine (4.8% as unchanged drug).
Pharmacodynamics
The exposure-response relationships for efficacy of camizestrant in combination with CDK4/6 inhibitors (abemaciclib, palbociclib, and ribociclib) have not been fully characterized. Increased camizestrant exposure was associated with an increased incidence of bradycardia and visual disturbances.
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes and drug transporters: CYP2B6, CYP2C9, CYP2C19, CYP2D6, CYP3A, P-gp, OATP1B1, UGT1A1, and UGT2B7
Camizestrant is primarily metabolized by CYP3A and UGT1A4. The Cmax and AUC of camizestrant increased by 1.4-fold and 1.9-fold, respectively, following 5 days of concomitant administration with itraconazole (a strong CYP3A inhibitor). Camizestrant is indicated in combination with ribociclib, a strong CYP3A inhibitor; the Cmax and AUC of camizestrant increased approximately 2-fold when coadministered with ribociclib. Concomitant use with a strong CYP3A inducer (carbamazepine or rifampin) decreased the camizestrant Cmax by 39% to 62% and the AUC by 70%. Moderate CYP3A inducers are predicted to decrease the camizestrant Cmax by 42% and 57%, respectively; however, when camizestrant is given in combination with ribociclib, moderate CYP3A inducers are predicted to have minimal effects on camizestrant exposure. Camizestrant is a strong CYP2C9 inhibitor, a strong CYP2C19 inhibitor, and a moderate CYP3A inhibitor. In vitro, camizestrant is a reversible inhibitor of CYP2B6 and CYP2D6; it also inhibits UGT1A1, UGT2B7, and can inhibit P-gp, BCRP, and OATP1B1 in vitro. In vitro it can induce CYP1A2 and CYP2B6, and is a substrate of P-gp.
