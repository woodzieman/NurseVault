Indication Specific Dosing
  General Information
  Complete or update meningococcal vaccination for serogroups A, C, W, and Y [MenACWY] and serogroup B [MenB] at least 2 weeks prior to administering the first dose of pozelimab, according to the most current Advisory Committee on Immunization Practices (ACIP) recommendations for persons receiving a complement inhibitor.
If urgent pozelimab therapy is indicated in persons who are not up to date with both MenACWY and MenB vaccines according to ACIP recommendations, administer meningococcal vaccine(s) as soon as possible and provide antibacterial medication prophylaxis. The efficacy, duration, and medication regimens for antibacterial medication prophylaxis have not been studied in persons receiving complement inhibitors.
In addition to meningococcal vaccination, also administer vaccinations for the prevention of S. pneumoniae and H. influenzae type b infections according to ACIP guidelines.

    meningococcal infection
    General Information
    Pozelimab is contraindicated in individuals with unresolved meningococcal infection. Pozelimab increases the risk of serious and life-threatening meningococcal infections caused by any serogroup. Individuals receiving pozelimab may have increased susceptibility to encapsulated bacteria infection, especially those caused by N. meningitidis but also S. pneumoniae, H. influenzae type B, and N. gonorrhoeae to a lesser extent. Complete or update meningococcal vaccination for serogroups A, C, W, and Y [MenACWY] and serogroup B [MenB] at least 2 weeks prior to administering the first dose of pozelimab, according to the most current Advisory Committee on Immunization Practices (ACIP) recommendations for individuals receiving a complement inhibitor. Revaccinate individuals based on ACIP recommendations considering the duration of pozelimab therapy. If urgent pozelimab therapy is indicated in individuals who are not up to date with both MenACWY and MenB vaccines according to ACIP recommendations, administer meningococcal vaccine(s) as soon as possible and provide antibacterial medication prophylaxis. The efficacy, duration, and medication regimens for antibacterial medication prophylaxis have not been studied in individuals receiving complement inhibitors. In addition to meningococcal vaccination, also administer vaccinations for the prevention of S. pneumoniae and H. influenzae type B infections according to ACIP guidelines. Vaccination does not eliminate the risk of meningococcal or encapsulated bacterial infections, despite development of antibodies after vaccination due to inhibition of complement activity by pozelimab as well as the risk of infection caused by nongroupable strains of N. meningitidis. Closely monitor for early signs and symptoms of meningococcal or encapsulated bacterial infection and evaluate individuals immediately if infection is suspected. Inform individuals receiving pozelimab and caregivers of these signs and symptoms and provide instruction to seek immediate medical care if these signs and symptoms occur. Meningococcal infection may become rapidly life-threatening or fatal if not recognized and treated early. Interrupt pozelimab treatment in individuals who are undergoing treatment for serious meningococcal or encapsulated bacterial infection until the infection is resolved. Counsel individuals about gonorrhea prevention and advise regular testing for at risk individuals.

    There are no available data on the use of pozelimab during pregnancy to inform a drug-associated risk of major birth defects, miscarriage, or adverse maternal or fetal outcomes. Description
  Text
  Pozelimab is an intravenously or subcutaneously administered complement inhibitor indicated for the treatment of CD55-deficient protein-losing enteropathy, also known as CHAPLE disease (complement hyperactivation, angiopathic thrombosis, protein losing enteropathy). CHAPLE disease is a rare (less than 100 persons diagnosed worldwide), genetic immune disease caused by mutations of the complement regulator CD55 gene that can lead to the complement system attacking the body's own cells. Symptoms of CHAPLE disease can include abdominal pain, nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, loss of appetite, weight loss, impaired growth, and edema. Severe and life-threatening thrombotic vascular occlusions can also occur in CHAPLE disease. The efficacy and safety of pozelimab were evaluated in a single-arm clinical trial (n = 10; age range 3 to 19 years; median 8.5 years) with outcomes compared to pre-treatment data in persons with active CHAPLE disease and hypoalbuminemia. Active CHAPLE disease was defined as hypoalbuminemia (serum albumin concentration of 3.2 g/dL or less) with 1 or more of the following signs or symptoms within the last 6 months: abdominal pain, diarrhea, peripheral edema, or facial edema. The median time for serum albumin to reach at least 3.5 g/dL was 15.5 days. All subjects achieved normalization of albumin concentrations by week 12 and maintained serum albumin concentrations within the normal range through at least 72 weeks of treatment. Pozelimab is contraindicated in persons with unresolved meningococcal infections. Life-threatening and fatal meningococcal infections have occurred in persons treated with complement inhibitors; meningococcal vaccination is required at least 2 weeks prior to treatment with pozelimab.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Pozelimab is a human, monoclonal immunoglobulin G4P (IgG4P) antibody directed against the terminal complement protein C5. Pozelimab inhibits terminal complement activation by blocking cleavage of C5 into C5a (anaphylatoxin) and C5b, thereby blocking the formation of the membrane-attack complex (C5b-C9), a structure mediating cell lysis.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Intravenous Route
    Description
    A single IV infusion of pozelimab results in dose proportional increases in mean Cmax and more than proportional increases in mean AUC (more than 16-fold) for total pozelimab concentrations in serum between 3 and 30 mg/kg in healthy subjects; the mean AUC increases by 3.5-fold between 10 and 30 mg/kg. A single dose of pozelimab 30 mg/kg IV resulted in a median total pozelimab trough concentration of 180 mg/L (range 52.8 to 268 mg/L) at week 1 in persons with CHAPLE disease. The effect of pozelimab on complement activity was measured by total complement hemolytic activity test (CH50). The magnitude and duration of reduction from baseline to CH50 by pozelimab was dose-dependent. After a dose of pozelimab 30 mg/kg IV in healthy subjects, the complete inhibition of CH50 was achieved at the end of the infusion in all subjects, maintained for 28 days, and returned to baseline 84 days post-dose. In persons with CHAPLE disease, CH50 was completely inhibited by week 1 for most subjects and by week 12 in all subjects after administration of a single dose of pozelimab 30 mg/kg IV followed by a weight-tiered subcutaneous injection once weekly starting at week 1. Additionally, serum albumin concentrations increased as early as week 1 and reached normal range (3.5 g/dL or more) by week 4 for most subjects and by week 12 for all subjects. Serum albumin concentrations were maintained within the normal range for the duration of treatment. Endogenous serum immunoglobulin G (IgG) concentrations were also increased from baseline at week 1 in all persons with CHAPLE disease and reached a stable concentration at approximately week 16.
    Name
    Subcutaneous Route
    Description
    After a single dose of pozelimab 600 mg subcutaneously in healthy subjects, the bioavailability of pozelimab is estimated as 51%. Mean Cmax increases 1.5-fold and mean AUC increases 2.2-fold after single pozelimab injections between 300 and 600 mg subcutaneously. The median time to reach peak concentrations is 7 days (range 3 to 7 days) after a single dose of 300 or 600 mg subcutaneously in healthy subjects. In persons with CHAPLE disease, steady-state total pozelimab concentrations were reached at approximately 20 weeks after subcutaneous administration once weekly. The predicted mean trough concentrations of total pozelimab at steady-state are 330 and 385 mg/L for pozelimab 10 and 12 mg/kg (up to a maximum of 800 mg) subcutaneously once weekly, respectively, after the IV loading dose. The effect of pozelimab on complement activity was measured by total complement hemolytic activity test (CH50). The magnitude and duration of reduction from baseline to CH50 by pozelimab was dose-dependent. After a single dose of pozelimab 600 mg subcutaneously, the maximum reduction from baseline in CH50 was achieved 7 days (range 3 to 14 days) post-dose in most subjects, corresponding to Tmax, and returned to baseline 56 days post-dose.

  Text
  Pozelimab is administered intravenously and subcutaneously. The mean Vd after a single dose of pozelimab 30 mg/kg IV is 3.3 L in healthy adults with a mean body weight of 70 kg; the mean Vd after a single dose of pozelimab 300 and 600 mg subcutaneously is 6 L and 8.6 L, respectively. Pozelimab is expected to undergo degradation into small peptides and amino acids by catabolic pathways similar to endogenous immunoglobulin G (IgG). Elimination of pozelimab occurs by linear and non-linear pathways; at higher concentrations, pozelimab elimination is primarily by a linear non-saturable proteolytic pathway, and at lower concentrations, non-linear, saturable C5 target-mediated elimination is the primary pathway. The median terminal half-life of total pozelimab in serum is 13.5 days (range 10 to 17.2 days) after a single dose of pozelimab 30 mg/kg IV and 14.1 days (range 8.6 to 17.3 days) after a single dose of pozelimab 600 mg subcutaneously.

      Intravenous Administration
      Description
      Dilution of Intravenous Loading Dose
Dilute pozelimab in an intravenous infusion bag of 25 to 250 mL of 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection or 5% Dextrose Injection to yield a final concentration of 6.7 to 20 mg/mL.
Mix the diluted solution by gentle inversion. Do not shake.
Storage: If not used immediately, store at room temperature up to 25 degrees C (77 degrees F) for no more than 8 hours from the time of preparation to the end of the infusion or refrigerate at 2 to 8 degrees C (36 to 46 degrees F) for no more than 24 hours from the time of preparation to the end of infusion. If stored in the refrigerator, allow the diluted solution to come to room temperature prior to administration. Discard the unused diluted pozelimab solution after 8 hours if stored at room temperature or after 24 hours if refrigerated. Do not freeze the diluted solution.
Intravenous (IV) Infusion Administration
Do not coadminister other medications through the same intravenous line.
Infuse through an intravenous line containing a sterile, in-line or add on 0.2- to 5-micron filter.
Infuse over a minimum of 1 hour; do not exceed maximum rate of 1,000 mg/hour.
Observe patient for 30 minutes after completion of the infusion.

      Subcutaneous Administration
      Description
      Preparation of Subcutaneous Maintenance Dose
For doses more than 400 mg, preparation of 2 separate injections is required for subcutaneous administration of the total dose volume.
Change the needle on the syringe to an injection needle fulfilling the following criteria: 25 to 27 gauge and one-half to five-eighths inch stainless steel needle with Luer-Lok.
Storage: Administer the subcutaneous injection within 4 hours of preparation.
Subcutaneous Administration
Inject pozelimab into the abdomen, thigh, or upper arm. Do not inject pozelimab into moles, scars, or areas where the skin is tender, bruised, red, hard, or not intact.
Administer consecutive doses at different injection sites if administering more than 1 injection.
Rotate injection sites.
Observe patient for 30 minutes after completion of the first subcutaneous injection.
Missed dose: Administer dose as soon as possible within 3 days of a missed dose. Do not administer 2 doses on the same day to make up for a missed dose. If more than 3 days have elapsed, skip the missed dose and administer the next dose on the regularly scheduled day. In each scenario, can then resume the regular once weekly dosing schedule. The day of weekly administration can be changed, if necessary, as long as the time between the 2 doses is at least 4 days (96 hours).
