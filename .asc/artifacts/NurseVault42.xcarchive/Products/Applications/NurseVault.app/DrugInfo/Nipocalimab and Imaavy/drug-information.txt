    infection
    General Information
    Delay nipocalimab treatment in people with an active infection until the infection resolves. Nipocalimab may increase the risk of infection. Monitor for signs and symptoms of infection during treatment. If a serious infection occurs, institute appropriate therapy and consider withholding treatment until the infection resolves.
    Headline
    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    There are limited data on the use of nipocalimab in pregnant individuals regarding the risk of major birth defects, miscarriage, or adverse maternal or fetal outcomes. In studies with pregnant monkeys, no direct adverse reactions on fetal development were observed at nipocalimab doses higher than the recommended human maintenance dose; however, adverse effects on the placenta were associated with placental ischemia, fetal loss, and decreased IgG concentrations in the offspring. IgG concentrations returned to normal by 6 months after birth, and no adverse reactions on immune function were noted. There is a pregnancy safety study for nipocalimab. If nipocalimab is administered during pregnancy, or if an individual becomes pregnant while receiving nipocalimab, report the exposure to the manufacturer at 1-800-526-7736 or www.imaavy.com.
    Headline
    Treatment-Related Restrictions of Use
    General Information
    The safety of immunization with live vaccines and the immune response to vaccination during treatment with nipocalimab are unknown. Live vaccines are not recommended during nipocalimab treatment because nipocalimab causes a transient reduction in IgG concentrations. Evaluate the need to administer age-appropriate vaccines according to immunization guidelines before initiating nipocalimab treatment.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Use nipocalimab with caution during breast-feeding. Nipocalimab is excreted in human colostrum and breast milk, based on limited data from a study of pregnant women (n = 13) receiving nipocalimab during pregnancy, where colostrum and breast milk were assessed in the first 8 days after birth. There is insufficient data on the effect of nipocalimab in the breastfed infant or the effect on milk production. The development and health benefits of breast-feeding should be considered along with the clinical need for nipocalimab, any adverse effects on the breastfed infant from nipocalimab or from the underlying maternal condition.
    Headline
    neonates and infants exposed to this medication in utero
    General Information
    Monoclonal antibodies are increasingly transferred across the placenta as pregnancy progresses, particularly in the third trimester. Nipocalimab reduces maternal serum IgG concentrations and impedes placental IgG transfer to the fetus. Passive immunity in neonates and infants exposed to this medication in utero may be reduced for 6 months or more. Monitor for the development of infection in infants exposed to nipocalimab. The effectiveness of vaccines may be reduced. Consider the risks and benefits prior to administering live vaccines to infants exposed to nipocalimab in utero.

    anaphylactoid reactions, angioedema, rash, urticaria
    General Information
    Hypersensitivity or anaphylactoid reactions (8% to 16%), including angioedema, rash, urticaria, eczema, atopic dermatitis, and gingival swelling, were reported in clinical trials in individuals receiving nipocalimab. The hypersensitivity reactions were mild or moderate. In the generalized myasthenia gravis (gMG) study, all hypersensitivity reactions occurred within 1 hour to 2 weeks of administration. In the warm autoimmune hemolytic anemia study, 76% of hypersensitivity reactions occurred within 2 weeks of administration. Urticaria in 1 individual led to the discontinuation of treatment. Monitor individuals during treatment and for 30 minutes after the administration of nipocalimab. If a hypersensitivity reaction occurs during administration, discontinue the infusion and treat if needed.

    General Information

    candidiasis, infection
    General Information
    Nipocalimab treatment may increase the risk of infection. Upper respiratory tract infection (34% to 46%), respiratory tract infection (18% to 33%; including pneumonia, bronchitis, COVID-19), urinary tract infection (6% to 15%), herpes (6% to 8%; including herpes zoster, herpes simplex, herpes zoster oticus), influenza (8%), oral infection (5% to 8%; including candidiasis and dental infection), skin infection (7%; including cellulitis), and gastroenteritis (5%) were reported in individuals receiving nipocalimab during clinical trials. A total of 71% of individuals (n = 113 to 186) reported 184 to 360 events of infections in clinical trials. Serious infections were reported in 7% to 12% of individuals in those studies. One case each of cellulitis, urinary tract infection, pneumonia, salmonella sepsis, and spontaneous bacterial peritonitis led to the discontinuation of nipocalimab. Individuals may be at increased risk of activation of latent viral infections, such as herpes zoster. Two individuals had serious adverse reactions related to Epstein-Barr virus (EBV) infection. One individual had fatal complications. Individuals who screened positive for hepatitis were excluded from the generalized myasthenia gravis study. Delay administration of nipocalimab in individuals with an active infection until the infection is resolved. Monitor individuals during treatment for signs and symptoms of infection. If a serious infection occurs, administer appropriate treatment and consider withholding nipocalimab therapy until the infection has resolved.

Description
  Text
  Nipocalimab is a neonatal Fc receptor (FcRn) blocker indicated for the treatment of anti-acetylcholine receptor (AChR) or anti-muscle-specific tyrosine kinase (MuSK) antibody positive generalized myasthenia gravis (gMG) and warm autoimmune hemolytic anemia (wAIHA) currently or previously treated with corticosteroids. Blockade of FcRn reduces circulating IgG antibody concentrations, including the abnormal anti-AChR and anti-MuSK autoantibodies in individuals with gMG and pathogenic anti-RBC IgG autoantibody concentrations in individuals with wAIHA. In a clinical trial, nipocalimab showed statistically significant changes in the Myasthenia Gravis Activities of Daily Living Scale and Quantitative Myasthenia Gravis total score, indicating greater functional and muscle strength improvements in individuals treated with nipocalimab. In a 24-week randomized, double-blind wAIHA study, a durable hemoglobin response occurred in 23.7% of individuals receiving nipocalimab versus 7.7% receiving placebo, an absolute difference of 16 percentage points (p = 0.015). Infections, infusion-related reactions, and hypersensitivity reactions were the most commonly reported adverse reactions. Monitor individuals during treatment and for 30 minutes after each infusion for infusion-related reactions. Treatment may also increase the risk of infection. Nipocalimab was FDA-approved in April 2025.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Nipocalimab is a human IgG1 monoclonal antibody that binds to the neonatal Fc receptor (FcRn) with high affinity and selectivity. This prevents FcRn-mediated recycling of IgG and results in the lowering of circulating IgG, including the pathogenic IgG. Nipocalimab selectively reduces IgG concentrations without detectable effects on other adaptive and innate immune functions.
 
Pharmacodynamics
Generalized Myasthenia Gravis
The pharmacological effect of nipocalimab was assessed by measuring the decrease in serum IgG concentrations, anti-acetylcholine receptor (AChR), and anti-muscle-specific tyrosine kinase (MuSK) autoantibody concentrations. AChR and MuSK autoantibody concentrations were reduced relative to baseline in individuals who were positive for AChR and MuSK autoantibodies and received nipocalimab. Total IgG concentrations decreased in a similar pattern. In pediatric patients with generalized myasthenia gravis, a similar reduction in AChR autoantibodies was observed compared to adults.
Warm Autoimmune Hemolytic Anemia
The pharmacologic effect of nipocalimab was assessed by measuring the decrease in serum IgG concentrations and pathogenic anti-RBC IgG autoantibody concentrations. Individuals treated with nipocalimab had a reduction in total IgG and anti-RBC IgG autoantibody relative to baseline.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Intravenous Route
    Description
    Nipocalimab exhibits nonlinear pharmacokinetics. After administration of a single infusion of nipocalimab at doses ranging from 0.3 to 60 mg/kg (4 times the recommended dose) in healthy individuals, the Cmax increased in a dose-proportional manner while the AUC increased in a greater than dose-proportional manner.

    Description

Gently invert the infusion container at least 10 times to mix. Do not shake.
Verify that a uniform solution has been achieved. Do not use if particulate matter or discoloration is present.
Storage: May store the diluted solution between 2 and 8 degrees C (36 and 46 degrees F) for no more than 24 hours. Do not freeze. Protect from light. After preparation or removal from the refrigerator, use or discard the prepared infusion within 12 hours, including infusion time. During these 12 hours, store under ambient light between 15 and 30 degrees C (59 and 86 degrees F).
 
IV infusion
If refrigerated prior to administration, allow the prepared infusion to warm to room temperature. Do not use an external heat source to warm.
Administer via intravenous infusion only.
Administer using an infusion set with an in-line or add-on, sterile, non-pyrogenic, low protein-binding filter made of polyethersulfone or polysulfone (pore size 0.2 micrometer or less). Administration sets must be made of polybutadiene, polyethylene, polyurethane, polypropylene, or polyvinylchloride.
Do not administer with other agents.
Administer the initial nipocalimab dose over at least 30 minutes. Administer the maintenance doses over at least 15 minutes.
Slow or stop the nipocalimab infusion if a hypersensitivity or infusion-related adverse reaction occurs during administration. Monitor the individual for 30 minutes after each infusion
If a hypersensitivity reaction occurs: discontinue the infusion and institute appropriate supportive measures if needed. A serious hypersensitivity reaction contraindicates readministration of nipocalimab.
If infusion-related reactions occur, the course of action may depend on the reaction severity:
If a severe infusion-related reaction occurs, discontinue the infusion and initiate appropriate therapy; Consider the risks and benefits of readministering the drug.
If a mild to moderate infusion-related reaction occurs, may rechallenge with close clinical observation, slower infusion rates, and premedication with the next infusion.
Missed dose: If a scheduled maintenance infusion appointment is missed, administer the dose as soon as possible. Resume nipocalimab dosing, maintaining the treatment interval.
