    Headline
    alcohol use disorder, hepatitis B infection, hepatitis C infection
    General Information
    Prior to administration of etranacogene dezaparvovec, perform liver health assessments, including enzyme testing [i.e., alanine aminotransferase (ALT), aspartate aminotransferase (AST), alkaline phosphatase, and total bilirubin] and hepatic ultrasound and elastography. In case of radiological liver abnormalities and/or sustained liver enzyme elevations, consider a consultation with a hepatologist to assess eligibility for etranacogene dezaparvovec. Perform laboratory tests to evaluate for active hepatitis B and C. Delay etranacogene dezaparvovec treatment until the individual does not have active hepatitis B infection or hepatitis C infection as active infection may reduce the efficacy of etranacogene dezaparvovec and/or increase the risk of adverse reactions. Integration of liver-targeting adeno-associated viral (AAV) vector DNA into the genome may lead to the development of hepatocellular carcinoma. Individuals with preexisting risk factors for hepatocellular carcinoma (i.e., history of hepatitis B and C infections, alcohol use disorder) should receive abdominal ultrasound screenings and be monitored annually for alpha-fetoprotein (AFP) elevations in the 5 years after etranacogene dezaparvovec administration.
    Headline
    factor IX inhibitors
    General Information
    Perform factor IX inhibitor titer testing prior to administration of etranacogene dezaparvovec. Do not administer etranacogene dezaparvovec to individuals with factor IX inhibitors or a prior history of factor IX inhibitors. Also monitor for development of factor IX inhibitors after administration of etranacogene dezaparvovec. Perform an assay that detects factor IX inhibitors if bleeding is not controlled, or plasma factor IX activity concentrations decrease. Monitor plasma factor IX activity (e.g., weekly for 3 months) by performing either activated partial thromboplastin time (aPTT)-based one-stage clotting assay (OSA) or chromogenic substrate assay (CSA). Use the same reagents and reference standards for OSA and CSA to minimize inconsistencies in factor IX activity. Factor IX activity results may be lower with CSA compared to OSA. Monitor factor IX activity using the same assay. Use of exogenous factor IX concentrates before and after etranacogene dezaparvovec may result in laboratory test interference and impede assessment of endogenous etranacogene dezaparvovec-derived factor IX activity.

    Headline
    anaphylactoid reactions, headache, infusion-related reactions
    General Information
    Moderate to severe hypersensitivity or anaphylactoid reactions (4%) and infusion-related reactions (33%) have been reported with etranacogene dezaparvovec. Anaphylaxis may also occur with etranacogene dezaparvovec treatment. Symptoms may include chest tightness, headache, abdominal pain, lightheadedness, flu-like symptoms, shivering, flushing, rash, and hypertension. Flu-like symptoms were reported with etranacogene dezaparvovec in 14% of subjects in clinical trials, and headache was reported in 18% of subjects. Monitor for signs and symptoms of hypersensitivity or infusion-related reaction throughout the infusion period and for at least 3 hours after the end of the infusion. Do not infuse etranacogene dezaparvovec faster than 500 mL/hour. If a hypersensitivity or infusion-related reaction develops during administration, the infusion may be slowed or stopped. Restart infusion at a lower rate when symptoms have resolved. Consider treatment with a corticosteroid or antihistamine for management of the reaction. Infusion-related reaction symptoms occurred during infusion in 7 subjects and after infusion in 12 subjects. Symptoms resolved on the day of or day after infusion in 11 subjects and within 8 days after infusion in 8 subjects. Infusions were temporarily discontinued in 3 subjects and restarted at a slower infusion rate after treatment with antihistamines and/or corticosteroids. Hypersensitivity reactions occurred within 10 to 12 minutes after initiation of etranacogene dezaparvovec infusion. A single subject required supportive therapy and received only 10% of the intended etranacogene dezaparvovec dose. Another subject did not require supportive therapy and received the full etranacogene dezaparvovec dose. Symptoms resolved in both subjects on the same day.

    Headline
    elevated hepatic enzymes, hepatotoxicity
    General Information
    Hepatotoxicity with elevated hepatic enzymes has occurred after etranacogene dezaparvovec treatment due to intravenous administration of a liver directed adeno-associated virus (AAV) vector. Transaminitis may be immune-mediated and reduce the therapeutic efficacy of the AAV-vector based gene therapy. Monitor alanine aminotransferase (ALT) concentrations weekly for 3 months and then monthly for up to 1 year after administration of etranacogene dezaparvovec to mitigate the risk of immune-mediated hepatotoxicity and potential decrease in factor IX activity. Investigate alternative cases of ALT and other transaminase elevations. If ALT increases to above the upper limit of normal or to twice the baseline, consider initiating a course of corticosteroids, along with factor IX activity monitoring. Prednisolone was used in clinical trials as prednisolone 60 mg/day PO for 1 week, 40 mg/day PO for 1 week, 30 mg/day PO for 2 weeks, 20 mg/day PO until ALT returns to baseline, and then taper dose by 5 mg/week to discontinue; medications equivalent to prednisolone may also be used. In clinical trials, the median time to corticosteroid initiation was 41 days (range: 22 to 61 days), and the median duration of corticosteroid use for elevated transaminases was 73 days (range: 51 to 130 days). A combined immunosuppressant regimen or the use of other products can be considered in case of prednisolone treatment failure or contraindication. Continue to monitor ALT until it returns to baseline or until after completion of corticosteroid treatment, or as clinically indicated. Elevated ALT and AST were reported in 40% and 42% of subjects, respectively, in clinical trials. During the first 2 years after etranacogene dezaparvovec administration, 23 subjects had asymptomatic elevated ALT concentrations more than the upper limit of normal (ULN) (median ALT = 65 International Units/L; range: 41 to 275 International Units/L); 17 of 23 subjects had elevated ALT concentrations in the first 4 months after etranacogene dezaparvovec administration, and the remaining 6 subjects had elevated ALT concentrations between months 4 and 24. ALT concentrations were elevated in 9 subjects at the end of the 2-year follow-up period. ALT elevations of more than 2 to 3 times the ULN (range: 89 to 130 International Units/L) occurred in 4 subjects, and a single subject had an ALT elevation of more than 3 to 5 times the ULN (range: 157 to 214 International Units/L). Additionally, a single subject had an ALT elevation of more than 5 times the ULN (275 International Units/L) that developed 3 weeks after etranacogene dezaparvovec administration. A tapered course of corticosteroids was given to 9 subjects with ALT elevations. Between 2 and 5 years after treatment, 14 subjects had asymptomatic ALT elevations above the ULN (median ALT = 72 International Units/L; range: 42 to 165 International Units/L) and were not treated with corticosteroids during that period. Of the 14 subjects, 6 subjects had elevated ALT concentrations more than 2 times the ULN before year 2. At the end of the 5-year follow-up period, 6 subjects still had ALT concentrations above the ULN (median ALT = 63 International Units/L; range: 49 to 119 International Units/L). Alternative causes explaining their ALT elevation were noted for 2 subjects. Two patients treated with corticosteroids in the first year of follow-up already had ALT values more than the ULN prior to etranacogene dezaparvovec treatment and continued to have ALT elevations through the 5-year follow-up.

Description
  Text
  Etranacogene dezaparvovec is an intravenous adeno-associated viral (AAV) vector-based gene therapy indicated for the treatment of hemophilia B in adults who currently use factor IX prophylaxis therapy, have current or historical life-threatening hemorrhage, or have repeated, serious spontaneous bleeding episodes. Efficacy of etranacogene dezaparvovec in persons with hemophilia B was evaluated in a prospective, open-label, single-dose, single-arm, multi-national clinical trial (n = 54). The main efficacy outcome was annualized bleeding rate (ABR) during months 7 to 18 after etranacogene dezaparvovec treatment compared to ABR during the lead-in period of at least 6 months. The estimated mean ABR during months 7 to 18 after treatment with etranacogene dezaparvovec was 1.9 bleeds/year compared to an estimated mean ABR of 4.1 bleeds/year during the lead-in period. Elevated hepatic enzymes have been reported with etranacogene dezaparvovec, and integration of liver-targeting AAV vector DNA into the genome may carry the theoretical risk of hepatocellular carcinoma development.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Etranacogene dezaparvovec is an adeno-associated virus serotype 5 (AAV5) based gene therapy designed to deliver a copy of a gene encoding the Padua variant of human coagulation Factor IX (hFIX-Padua). A single infusion of etranacogene dezaparvovec results in cell transduction and an increase in circulating factor IX activity in individuals with hemophilia B.

General Precautions
Prepare etranacogene dezaparvovec using sterile technique under aseptic conditions, proper engineering controls (e.g., biological safety cabinet or isolator) and according to institutional policies.
Do not expose etranacogene dezaparvovec to the light of an ultraviolet radiation disinfection lamp.
Confirm that the patient's identity matches the patient-specific identifier number on the outer carton.
Verify the required dose of etranacogene dezaparvovec based on the patient's body weight.
Confirm that the carton contains enough vials to prepare the diluted etranacogene dezaparvovec infusion.
Preparation of 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection infusion bags
Spike the 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection infusion bag prior to dilution with applicable connector.
Connect a luer-lock syringe at the mixing adapter site of the applicable connector.
Withdraw the volume equal to the calculated etranacogene dezaparvovec dose (in mL) from the 500 mL infusion bag(s) of 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection. The volume to withdraw and number of infusion bags will vary based on the patient body weight.
Less than 120 kg: one 500 mL 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection infusion bag; withdraw 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection equal to the total etranacogene dezaparvovec dose (in mL) from 1 infusion bag.
120 kg or more: two 500 mL 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection infusion bags; withdraw 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection equal to the total etranacogene dezaparvovec dose (in mL) as one-half of the dose equivalent volume from each of the 2 infusion bags.
Injection of etranacogene dezaparvovec into the 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection infusion bags
Dilute etranacogene dezaparvovec with 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection only prior to administration.
Gently swirl the vials 3 times for approximately 10 seconds to homogenize the etranacogene dezaparvovec suspension. Do not shake the vials to avoid foaming.
Withdraw etranacogene dezaparvovec from each vial using a 20 G needle/vial adapter and syringe. Use a 20 mL luer-lock or larger syringe that is suitable for volume measuring and a needle; do not use filter needles during preparation of etranacogene dezaparvovec. Use a new needle/vial adapter and syringe for each etranacogene dezaparvovec vial.
Slowly add the required etranacogene dezaparvovec dose from the syringe(s) directly to the 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection infusion bag(s) to bring the total volume in each infusion bag back to 500 mL. Do not add etranacogene dezaparvovec into the airspace of the bag to avoid foaming throughout this process.
Repeat the above steps with additional needles/vial adaptors and syringes to inject the total calculated etranacogene dezaparvovec infusion bag(s) required for the patient dose.
Gently invert the infusion bag(s) at least 3 times (about 10 seconds) to mix the solution and ensure even distribution of the diluted product. Do not shake the diluted etranacogene dezaparvovec infusion bags to avoid foaming.
Connect the infusion bag(s) to an infusion tube pre-filled with sterile 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection to decrease the risk of spill and/or aerosol formation.
Transport the diluted etranacogene dezaparvovec infusion bag(s) in the transport container/bag protected from light to the administration site, avoiding any shaking or excessive agitation.
Storage: Infuse the diluted etranacogene dezaparvovec within 24 hours after dose preparation.
Intravenous (IV) Infusion
Administer etranacogene dezaparvovec as a single-dose IV infusion through a peripheral venous catheter. Do not infuse through a central line or port.
Use an integrated (in-line) 0.2 micron filter made out of polyether sulfone (PES).
Connect the pre-filled IV infusion line to the main IV line that has been primed with sterile 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection prior to use.
Infuse diluted etranacogene dezaparvovec at a constant IV infusion rate of 500 mL/hour (8 mL/minute)
Do not infuse etranacogene dezaparvovec faster than 500 mL/hour.
Do not administer etranacogene dezaparvovec as an IV push or bolus dose.
Do not infuse the diluted etranacogene dezaparvovec in the same IV line with any other products.
If an infusion reaction occurs during administration, the rate of the infusion may be reduced or stopped to manage the infusion reaction.
If the infusion is stopped, restart at a slower rate when the infusion reaction is resolved.
If the infusion rate needs to be decreased, or stopped and restarted, etranacogene dezaparvovec should be infused within 24 hours after the dose preparation.
After the entire content of the bag(s) is infused, flush the IV infusion line at the same infusion rate with 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection to ensure all etranacogene dezaparvovec is delivered.
