          Dosage Description
          240 mg/m2 IV on each day chemotherapy is administered; complete the infusion no more than 4 hours prior to the start of chemotherapy. The interval between trilaciclib doses on sequential days should not exceed 28 hours. Therapy interruption or discontinuation may be necessary in patients who develop toxicity. Trilaciclib was evaluated in adult patients with extensive stage-small cell lung cancer (ES-SCLC) in 3 randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, phase 2 trials. Primary prophylaxis with granulocyte-colony stimulating factor (G-CSF) and erythropoiesis-stimulating agent (ESA) use were not permitted in cycle 1; however, both prophylactic G-CSF and ESAs were allowed in subsequent cycles as clinically indicated. Therapeutic G-CSF use and red blood cell (RBC) or platelet transfusions were allowed at any time if needed. In the G1T28-05 trial, the mean duration of severe neutropenia in cycle 1 (0 days vs. 4 days; p less than 0.0001) and the incidence of severe neutropenia during the treatment period (1.9% vs. 49.1%; p less than 0.0001) were significantly improved in patients with newly diagnosed ES-SCLC who received trilaciclib (IV once daily on days 1, 2, and 3 of each cycle) prior to carboplatin, etoposide, and atezolizumab (n = 54) compared with placebo prior to carboplatin, etoposide, and atezolizumab (n = 53). In the G1T28-02 trial, patients with newly diagnosed ES-SCLC who received trilaciclib (IV once daily on days 1, 2, and 3 of each cycle) prior to etoposide and carboplatin (n = 39) had significantly improved mean duration of severe neutropenia in cycle 1 (0 days vs. 3 days; p less than 0.001), incidence of severe neutropenia during treatment (5% vs. 43%; p less than 0.0001), and incidence of RBC transfusion on or after 5 weeks (5% vs. 24%; p less than 0.05) compared with patients who received placebo prior to etoposide and carboplatin (n =38). In the G1T28-03 trial, the duration of severe neutropenia in cycle 1 (2 days vs. 7 days; p less than 0.0001) and the incidence of severe neutropenia during the treatment period (40.6% vs.75.9%; p = 0.016) were significantly improved in patients with previously treated ES-SCLC who received trilaciclib (IV once daily on days 1, 2, 3, 4, and 5 of each cycle) prior to topotecan (n = 32) compared with placebo prior to topotecan (n = 29).

    pulmonary disease
    General Information
    Use trilaciclib with caution in patients who have a history of pre-existing pulmonary disease; severe pneumonitis/interstitial lung disease has been reported in patients treated with trilaciclib. Monitor patients for new or worsening respiratory symptoms including fever, cough, or dyspnea. Immediately interrupt trilaciclib therapy in patients with new or worsening respiratory symptoms. A dose reduction or permanent discontinuation of therapy may be necessary for recurrent or severe pneumonitis.
    Headline

    General Information
    Reduce the trilaciclib dosage in patients with moderate or severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh class B; Child-Pugh class C) due to increased exposure which may increase the incidence of treatment-related adverse reactions; data are not available for patients with hepatic failure.

    hemoptysis, interstitial lung disease, pneumonitis
    General Information
    Interstitial lung disease (ILD) or pneumonitis occurred in 1 patient (0.4%) who received trilaciclib (n = 272) in clinical trials. The grade 3 ILD/pneumonitis (which did not resolve) was reported 2 months after trilaciclib was discontinued; the patients was also receiving other medicines that may cause ILD/pneumonitis. Therapy interruption or discontinuation may be necessary in patients who develop grade 2 or higher ILD/pneumonitis.

Description
  Text
  Trilaciclib is a cyclin-dependent kinase (CDK) 4 and 6 inhibitor. It is indicated to decrease the incidence of chemotherapy-induced bone marrow suppression in adult patients with extensive-stage small cell lung cancer who are receiving a platinum/etoposide-containing regimen or topotecan-containing regimen. Hypersensitivity reactions and interstitial lung disease/pneumonitis have been reported with trilaciclib therapy.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Trilaciclib is a transient inhibitor of cyclin-dependent kinase (CDK) 4 and 6. Bone marrow hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells (HSPCs) depend on CDK 4 and 6 to proliferate and produce circulating neutrophils, red blood cells, and platelets. When administered prior to chemotherapy, trilaciclib causes proliferating cells to arrest in the G1 phase of the cell cycle, preventing chemotherapy-induced myelosuppression. In murine models, the transient arrest of HSPCs allowed for the protection of cell counts and increased survival when given with chemotherapy or radiation.

  Text
  Trilaciclib is administered intravenously (IV). In vitro, it has a blood/plasma ratio range of 1.21 to 1.53 at concentrations of 0.5 to 50 mcg/mL and is 69% bound to human plasma proteins independent of concentrations from 0.75 to 3 mcg/mL. Trilaciclib has a steady-state volume of distribution of 1,130 L, a mean terminal half-life of about 14 hours, and an estimated clearance of 158 L/hr. Trilaciclib is primarily metabolized in the liver by aldehyde oxidase and cytochrome CYP3A4 and CYP2C8. It is the predominant circulating compound in plasma after IV administration with about 50% of plasma total radioactivity. Trilaciclib is primarily eliminated in the feces. Following a single radiolabeled dose of trilaciclib 192 mg/m2 IV, approximately 79.1% of the dose was recovered in feces (7% unchanged) and 14% was recovered in urine (2% unchanged).
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes and transporters: OCT2, MATE1, and MATE-2K
Based on drug interaction studies, trilaciclib is a OCT2, MATE1, and MATE-2K transporter inhibitor; it is not a significant substrate, inhibitor, or inducer of CYP3A. Trilaciclib is a CYP1A2 inducer and a substrate of P-glycoprotein and breast cancer resistance protein based on in vitro studies.

Reconstitution
Calculate the appropriate dose of trilaciclib and reconstitute the correct number of vials for the dose.
Add 19.5 mL of 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection or 5% Dextrose Injection to each 300-mg lyophilized powder vial for a final vial concentration of 15 mg/mL.
Gently swirl the vial for up to 3 minutes until the sterile lyophilized cake is completely dissolved; do not shake.
Storage of reconstituted vial: Store at room temperature (20 to 25 degrees C; 68 to 77 degrees F) for up to 4 hours prior to dilution; do not refrigerate or freeze. Discard any unused portion in the vial.
Dilution
Withdraw the calculated dose/volume from the reconstituted trilaciclib vial(s) and dilute in a 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection or 5% Dextrose Injection infusion bag to a final diluted solution concentration between 0.5 mg/mL and 3 mg/mL.
Mix by gentle inversion; do not shake.
Storage of diluted admixture: Storage time is based on the diluent used and the IV infusion bag material. Store at room temperature (20 to 25 degrees C; 68 to 77 degrees F); do not refrigerate or freeze.
5% Dextrose Injection diluent
Polyvinyl chloride (PVC), Ethylene vinyl acetate (EVA), Polyolefin (PO), or Polyolefin/polyamide (PO/PA) IV bag: Store up to 12 hours.
0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection diluent
PCV, EVA, or PO: Store up to 8 hours.PO/PA: Store up to 4 hours.
Intravenous Infusion
Administer the diluted admixture IV over 30 minutes; complete the infusion no more than 4 hours prior to the start of chemotherapy.
Use an infusion set that includes a polyethylene sulfone (PES), polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF), or cellulose acetate (CA) in-line 0.2- or 0.22-micron filter.
Do not use a polytetrafluorethylene (PTFE) in-line filter; however, a PTFE air vent filter may be used.
Flush the infusion line/cannula with at least 20 mL sterile 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection or 5% Dextrose Injection at the completion of the infusion.
Do not administer with other drugs through the same infusion line.
Do not administer with other drugs through a central access device unless the device supports coadministration of incompatible drugs.
Monitor patients for signs and symptoms of injection-site reactions, phlebitis, and thrombophlebitis.

Dosage Adjustment Guidelines
  Hepatic Impairment
  Mild hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh class A): No trilaciclib dosage adjustment is necessary.Moderate or severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh class B or C): Reduce the dose to 170 mg/m2 IV.
  Renal Impairment
  Specific guidelines for dosage adjustments in renal impairment are not available; it appears that no dosage adjustments are needed.
