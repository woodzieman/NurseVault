Description
  Text
  Prademagene zamikeracel is an autologous cell sheet-based gene therapy indicated for the treatment of wounds in individuals with recessive dystrophic epidermolysis bullosa (RDEB). RDEB is characterized by widespread blistering and scarring of the skin and mucosal membranes, which can lead to significant deformities and major extracutaneous involvement. Prademagene zamikeracel is composed of autologous cells from skin punch biopsies of individuals with mutations in the collagen type VII alpha 1 chain (COL7A1) gene. These cells have been modified ex vivo using a replication-incompetent retroviral vector (RVV) that carries the complete COL7A1 gene. The resulting gene-modified cell sheets express functional collagen VII (C7) protein. During a clinical trial, 81% of wounds treated with prademagene zamikeracel healed by at least 50% from baseline at Month 6, compared to 16% of those in the control group (p less than 0.0001). Individuals in the prademagene zamikeracel group reported a pain reduction of -3.07 compared to the control group pain reduction of -0.9 (p = 0.0002). Complete wound healing by Month 3 and 6 in the prademagene zamikeracel group were 14% and 16%, respectively, with no complete wound healing at either time point for the control group. Post-procedure pain and itching were the most commonly reported adverse reactions. Prademagene zamikeracel was FDA-approved in April 2025.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  In individuals with recessive dystrophic epidermolysis bullosa (RDEB), mutations in both copies of the COL7A1 gene lead to a deficiency or absence of collagen type VII (C7) protein. The C7 protein is crucial for forming anchoring fibrils (AFs) that connect the epidermis to the dermis. This disruption results in fragile skin, blistering, and severe wounds. Prademagene zamikeracel is a treatment that involves gene-modifying an individual's own cells using a retroviral vector (RVV) transduction to express the COL7A1 gene, enabling the production of the C7 protein. These modified cells are then formed into cellular sheets for topical application onto wounds.
 
Pharmacodynamics
The C7 protein was assessed by both immunofluorescence and the presence of AFs at Months 3, 6, and 12 in a clinical study (n = 7). The NC2 domain of C7 and AFs were detected in 6 individuals at 3 months and in 5 individuals at 6 months. After 1 year of treatment, 3 individuals were positive for NC2 or AFs. After 2 years, 2 out of 3 individuals with biopsies were positive for NC2 or AFs. One individual was positive for both AFs and NC2 and the second individual was positive only for NC2, as an additional biopsy for AF assessment was not obtained.

Preparation
Transport prademagene zamikeracel to the operating room in the original transport thermoform protective case (clamshell) containing sterile transport media.
Verify patient's identity prior to application. Do not apply prademagene zamikeracel if the patient specific label does not match the intended patient.
Storage: Store in original packaging at 15 to 25 degrees C (59 to 77 degrees F) until application. Prademagene zamikeracel is stable for 84 hours at room temperature. Dispose of sheet(s) after 84 hours.
 
Administration
Prepare wound by debridement.
Pick up prademagene zamikeracel sheet with forceps by grasping the titanium ligating clips.
Do not trim the sheet.
Apply unaltered prademagene zamikeracel sheet onto the wound bed only (nylon suture on the prademagene zamikeracel sheet facing away from the wound) by affixing via resorbable sutures. Do not suture onto healthy intact skin.
Do not overlap sheets on wounds.
Apply all selected sheet(s) in a single surgical session.
Cover the prademagene zamikeracel sheet(s) with non-adhesive dressings and topical antibiotic ointment.
Leave the treated area undisturbed for 5 to 10 days.
Keep the dressing dry. Do not submerge the treated area in water until the gauze of the prademagene zamikeracel product falls off the treatment site. This typically falls off within 2 to 3 weeks.
