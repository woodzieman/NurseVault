    General Information
    There are no adequate or well-controlled studies of finafloxacin during pregnancy. However, because of the minimal systemic absorption after topical otic administration, there is expected to be minimal risk of fetal toxicity when administered during pregnancy. Description
  Text
  Finafloxacin is a fluoroquinolone antimicrobial. It is used for the treatment of acute otitis externa (AOE), with or without an otowick, caused by susceptible strains of Pseudomonas aeruginosa and Staphylococcus aureus. Prolonged use may lead to overgrowth of nonsusceptible organisms. There are limited adverse reactions associated with topical use. Finafloxacin was FDA-approved in 2014.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Finafloxacin, a fluoroquinolone antibacterial, is bactericidal via inhibition of DNA gyrase (topoisomerase II), an enzyme responsible for counteracting the excessive supercoiling of DNA during replication or transcription and topoisomerase IV, an enzyme that helps separate the daughter DNA molecules. In gram-negative bacteria, the primary target is the DNA gyrase A subunit, while the primary target in gram-positive bacteria is generally topoisomerase IV. There is an increased bactericidal effect with an acidic pH with finafloxacin. 58659
 
Pharmacodynamics
Fluoroquinolone antibacterials exhibit concentration-dependent pharmacodynamics where the ratio of area under the concentration curve of free drug to minimal inhibitory concentration (free AUC to MIC ratio) appears to best correlate with antibacterial activity. Additionally, fluoroquinolones exhibit a prolonged post-antibiotic effect (PAE) for gram-negative organisms.
 
Resistance
Resistance to quinolones, including ciprofloxacin, can occur due to multiple-step mutations in defined regions of the target bacterial enzymes topoisomerase IV and DNA gyrase, referred to as Quinolone-Resistance Determining Regions (QRDRs). Resistance also occurs with decreased outer membrane permeability or through altered efflux. Cross-resistance has been observed with finafloxacin and other fluoroquinolones.

In healthy subjects administered 4 drops in each ear twice daily for 7 days, quantifiable finafloxacin concentrations were observed in 2 of 14 individuals; and these concentrations were just above the quantitation limit (0.05 ng/mL). In individuals with acute otitis externa administered a single dose of 4 or 8 drops in each ear, quantifiable finafloxacin concentrations of up to 0.234 ng/mL were observed in plasma samples from 2 of 36 subjects.

  Route Specific Administrations
    Name
    Otic Administration
    Description
    Finafloxacin otic suspension is only for use in the ear. Do not use in the eyes or mouth. The suspension may be used with or without an otowick.
Warm the suspension by holding the bottle in the hand for 1 to 2 minutes prior to administering to avoid dizziness which may result from instillation of a cold suspension.
Shake well before each use.
Lie with the affected ear upward, instill the drops, and maintain the position for 1 to 2 minutes to facilitate penetration of the drops into the ear canal.
Repeat if necessary for the opposite ear.
