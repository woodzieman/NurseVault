    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Use gentian violet with caution during breast-feeding. Guidelines state that an aqueous solution of gentian violet (less than 0.5%) can be used for no more than 7 days when treating Candida infection of the nipples; however, safer alternatives may be available. Oral ulcerations have occurred in breastfed infants after application of gentian violet 1% or 2% solution to the mouth or tongue for thrush. In a case report, use of gentian violet 1% solution twice daily was interrupted after 1 day in a 2-week-old exclusively breast-fed infant due to perceived airway discomfort. When therapy was resumed after 2 days with gentian violet 1% four times daily, cough and difficulty feeding developed after 1 day, and the infant was hospitalized and required intubation.

Description
  Text
  Gentian violet is a topical violet triphenylmethane dye indicated for skin antisepsis to prevent skin infection in minor cuts, scrapes, or burns. Gentian violet has antifungal and antibacterial properties. It is fungicidal against Candida species and is highly effective against gram-positive bacteria; it is less effective against gram-negative bacteria. Gentian violet will stain clothing and skin. Application of gentian violet to ulcerative lesions can result in tattooing of the skin and should be avoided.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Gentian violet is a triphenylmethane dye with antibacterial and antifungal properties. It is fungicidal against Candida species and is highly effective against gram-positive bacteria; it is less effective against gram-negative bacteria and Mycobacterium likely due to its inability to penetrate the lipid surrounding the cell wall. The exact mechanism of action of gentian violet is unknown. Hypothesized mechanisms include altered redox potential, reduced nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide phosphate (NADPH) oxidase inhibition, free radical formation, unionized complex formation of bacteria with the dye, protein synthesis inhibition, glutamine synthesis inhibition, uncoupling of oxidative phosphorylation, bacterial cell wall formation inhibition, and biofilm disruption.
