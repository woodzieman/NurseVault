  Contraindications And Precautions

    General Information
    The coadministration of certain medications may lead to harm and require avoidance or therapy modification; review all drug interactions prior to concomitant use of other medications.
    Headline
    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components.
    Headline
    pulmonary hypertension due to idiopathic interstitial pneumonia
    General Information
    Riociguat is contraindicated in individuals with pulmonary hypertension due to idiopathic interstitial pneumonia.
    Headline
    autonomic neuropathy, hypotension, hypovolemia, left ventricular outflow tract obstruction
    General Information
    Riociguat reduces blood pressure. The potential for symptomatic hypotension or ischemia may be increased in individuals with hypovolemia, severe left ventricular outflow tract obstruction, resting hypotension, autonomic neuropathy (autonomic dysfunction) or concomitant administration with antihypertensive medications or strong P-gp/BCRP inhibitors. Consider a dose reduction if signs or symptoms of hypotension occur.
    Headline
    pulmonary veno-occlusive disease
    General Information
    Riociguat is not recommended in individuals with pulmonary veno-occlusive disease (PVOD). Pulmonary vasodilator therapies, such as riociguat, may significantly worsen the cardiovascular status of individuals with PVOD and may cause life-threatening pulmonary edema. Consider the possibility of PVOD in individuals who develop acute pulmonary edema. Discontinue use of riociguat if PVOD is confirmed.
    Headline
    tobacco smoking
    General Information
    Tobacco smoking significantly reduces the plasma concentrations of riociguat. Although the safety and efficacy of riociguat in doses more than 2.5 mg PO 3 times daily have not been established, higher doses may be considered in individuals who smoke in order to match exposure seen in nonsmoking individuals. Consider a dose reduction of riociguat in individuals who stop smoking.
    Headline
    activities requiring coordination and concentration, driving or operating machinery
    General Information
    Advise individuals ti avoid driving or operating machinery or participating in activities requiring coordination and concentration until they know how they react to riociguat therapy.
    Headline
    renal failure
    General Information
    Riociguat is not recommended for use in individuals with renal failure (CrCl less than 15 mL/minute) or on dialysis.
    Headline
    Child-Pugh class C, hepatic failure
    General Information
    Riociguat is not recommended for use in individuals with hepatic failure (Child-Pugh class C).
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Riociguat is contraindicated in pregnancy. Discontinue riociguat as soon as possible when pregnancy is detected. Based on data from animal reproductive studies, riociguat may cause fetal harm. Available data in humans is limited and do not establish the presence or absence of a drug-associated risk of major birth defects or miscarriage. Riociguat was teratogenic and embryotoxic at exposures 8 and 2 times, respectively, the human exposure when given to rats during organogenesis. In rabbits, abortions and fetal toxicity were observed at doses 4 and 13 times the human exposure, respectively. If pregnancy is detected, discontinue riociguat as soon as possible.
    Headline
    embryo-fetal toxicity, people who may become pregnant, reproductive risk
    General Information
    Counsel people who may become pregnant about the reproductive risk and embryo-fetal toxicity associated with riociguat. Verify pregnancy status prior to treatment initiation. Advise people who may become pregnant to use effective contraception prior to initiation of treatment, while taking riociguat, and for 1 month after the last dose. Advise individuals to contact their health care team if they become pregnant or suspect they may be pregnant. Counsel those who may become pregnant on pregnancy planning and prevention, including emergency contraception, or designate counseling by another member of the health care team trained in contraceptive counseling.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Advise patients to avoid breast-feeding while taking riociguat due to the potential for serious adverse effects, including hypotension, in the breastfed child. There are no data on the presence of riociguat in human milk, its effects on the breastfed child, or its effects on milk production. Riociguat is present in the milk of lactating rats.

Aprepitant, Fosaprepitant: (Moderate) Use caution if riociguat and aprepitant, fosaprepitant are used concurrently and monitor for an increase in riociguat-related adverse effects for several days after administration of a multi-day aprepitant regimen. Riociguat is a CYP3A4 substrate. Aprepitant, when administered as a 3-day oral regimen (125 mg/80 mg/80 mg), is a moderate CYP3A4 inhibitor and inducer and may increase plasma concentrations of riociguat. For example, a 5-day oral aprepitant regimen increased the AUC of another CYP3A4 substrate, midazolam (single dose), by 2.3-fold on day 1 and by 3.3-fold on day 5. After a 3-day oral aprepitant regimen, the AUC of midazolam (given on days 1, 4, 8, and 15) increased by 25% on day 4, and then decreased by 19% and 4% on days 8 and 15, respectively. As a single 125 mg or 40 mg oral dose, the inhibitory effect of aprepitant on CYP3A4 is weak, with the AUC of midazolam increased by 1.5-fold and 1.2-fold, respectively. After administration, fosaprepitant is rapidly converted to aprepitant and shares many of the same drug interactions. However, as a single 150 mg intravenous dose, fosaprepitant only weakly inhibits CYP3A4 for a duration of 2 days; there is no evidence of CYP3A4 induction. Fosaprepitant 150 mg IV as a single dose increased the AUC of midazolam (given on days 1 and 4) by approximately 1.8-fold on day 1; there was no effect on day 4. Less than a 2-fold increase in the midazolam AUC is not considered clinically important.

Description
  Text
  Riociguat is an oral soluble guanylate cyclase stimulator and is indicated for the treatment of adults with pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH) (WHO group I) and patients with persistent/recurrent chronic thromboembolic pulmonary hypertension (CTEPH) (WHO Group 4) after surgical treatment or inoperable CTEPH. Riociguat is the first FDA-approved soluble guanylate cyclase stimulator to treat PAH and the first drug of any class to show efficacy for patients with CTEPH. Soluble guanylate cyclase stimulators relax arteries to increase blood flow thereby decreasing blood pressure. In phase 3 studies (CHEST-1 and PATENT-1), treatment with riociguat resulted in a statistically significant improvement in 6-minute walking distance (6MWD), N-terminal pro-brain natriuretic peptide (NT-proBNP) levels and WHO functional class and a significant decrease in pulmonary vascular resistance in both patients with CTEPH and PAH. In patients with PAH, riociguat delayed the time to clinical worsening; however, in patients with CTEPH, there was no significant difference from placebo. Riociguat may cause serious birth defects and is only available to female patients through a special restricted distribution program called the Adempas REMS program. For women who are able to get pregnant, pregnancy testing is required before starting treatment and routinely during treatment. Male patients are not required to participate in the Adempas REMS program. More information about this program is available at www.AdempasREMS.com.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Riociguat is a soluble guanylate cyclase stimulator. Soluble guanylate cyclase (sGC) is an enzyme in the cardiopulmonary system and the receptor for nitric oxide (NO). When NO binds to sGC, cyclic guanosine monophosphate (cGMP) is synthesized. Pulmonary hypertension is associated with endothelial dysfunction, impaired synthesis of nitric oxide and insufficient stimulation of the NO-sGC-cGMP pathway. Riociguat sensitizes sGC to endogenous NO by stabilizing NO-sGC binding and directly stimulates sGC via a different binding site, independently of NO. When riociguat stimulates the NO-sGC-cGMP pathway, generation of cGMP with subsequent vasodilation is increased.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Oral Route
    Description
    Following oral administration, riociguat is rapidly absorbed with maximum plasma concentrations occurring within 1.5 hours. The absolute bioavailability is about 94%; food has no effect on bioavailability. Average systemic clearance of riociguat was about 1.8 L/h in patients with PAH and about 3.4 L/h in healthy subjects.
  Text
  Riociguat is administered orally. Pharmacokinetic parameters are dose proportional. Riociguat plasma concentrations directly correlate to hemodynamic parameters such as systemic vascular resistance, systolic blood pressure, pulmonary vascular resistance, and cardiac output. Riociguat is highly bound to plasma proteins (95%) and is metabolized by CYP1A1, CYP3A, CYP2C8 and CYP2J2. The major active metabolite, M1, is catalyzed via CYP1A1, and is 1/3 to 1/30 as potent as riociguat. M1 is further metabolized to the inactive N-glucuronide. Plasma concentrations of M1 in patients with pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH) are about half those for riociguat. Excretion occurs via both renal and fecal/biliary routes. The terminal elimination half-life is prolonged in patients with PAH (12 hours) compared with healthy subjects (7 hours).

Riociguat is a substrate of P-glycoprotein (P-gp) and breast cancer resistance protein (BCRP). In addition, riociguat is metabolized by CYP1A1, CYP3A, CYP2C8 and CYP2J2 and the major active metabolite is formed via CYP1A1. Therefore, pharmacokinetic interactions are possible when riociguat is coadministered with inhibitors or inducers or these enzymes and/or transporter proteins.

Dosage Adjustment Guidelines
  Hepatic Impairment
  Safety and efficacy have not been established in patients with severe hepatic impairment (Child Pugh C), therefore use is not recommended. No dosage adjustments are recommended in patients with mild to moderate hepatic impairment.
  Renal Impairment
  CrCl 15 mL/minute or more: No dosage adjustments are needed.
CrCl less than 15 mL/minute: Safety and efficacy have not been established, therefore use is not recommended.
 
Intermittent hemodialysis
Safety and efficacy have not been established; therefore, use is not recommended.
