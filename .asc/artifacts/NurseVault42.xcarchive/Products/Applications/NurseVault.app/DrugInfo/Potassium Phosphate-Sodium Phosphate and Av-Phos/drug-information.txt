Drug Class: Potassium phosphate; sodium phosphate is a phosphorus supplement that increases urinary phosphate and pyrophosphate, and can be used in the dietary management of hypophosphatemia.

Routes: oral; Common adult doses: 8 tablets (2,000 mg phosphorus) or 4 packets (1,000 mg phosphorus) PO per day.

Common Side Effects (excluding routine GI upset): weight gain, arthralgia, bone pain, muscle cramps, neuropathic pain, osteomalacia, paresthesias, weakness, confusion, dizziness, headache, seizures, dyspnea, nephrolithiasis, oliguria, peripheral edema, polydipsia

In an Overdose: No overdose/antidote information is listed in this file.

Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  Contraindications And Precautions
    Headline
    Drug Interactions
    General Information
    The coadministration of certain medications may lead to harm and require avoidance or therapy modification; review all drug interactions prior to concomitant use of other medications.
    Headline
    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components.
    Headline
    Treatment-Related Restrictions of Use
    General Information
    Phosphate therapy may be beneficial in the management of rickets; however, caution is warranted, as elevated serum phosphate concentrations increase the risk of extraskeletal calcification.
    Headline
    renal failure, renal impairment
    General Information
    Potassium phosphate; sodium phosphate is contraindicated in individuals with severe renal impairment (less than 30% of normal) or renal failure. Careful monitoring of renal function may be required at periodic intervals during phosphate therapy.
    Headline
    nephrolithiasis, struvite calculus
    General Information
    Potassium phosphate; sodium phosphate is contraindicated in patients who have infected phosphate stones (i.e., struvite calculus). Patients receiving phosphate therapy may experience passage of pre-existing kidney stones (nephrolithiasis) and should be advised of this possibility.
    Headline
    dehydration, hyperkalemia, hypernatremia, hyperphosphatemia, sodium restriction
    General Information
    Potassium phosphate; sodium phosphate is contraindicated in individuals with hyperphosphatemia. High serum phosphate concentrations may increase the incidence of extraskeletal calcification. Caution should be exercised when considering use in patients with acute dehydration, hypernatremia, or hyperkalemia. This product contains potassium and sodium and should be used with caution if potassium or sodium restriction is desired. Careful monitoring of serum calcium, phosphorus, potassium, and sodium may be required at periodic intervals during phosphate therapy.
    Headline
    cardiac disease, hypertension, peripheral edema, pulmonary edema
    General Information
    Exercise caution when considering the use of potassium phosphate; sodium phosphate in patients who have cardiac disease (particularly in digitalized patients), heart failure, hypertension, pulmonary edema, or peripheral edema.
    Headline
    hepatic disease
    General Information
    Use potassium phosphate; sodium phosphate with caution in patients who have cirrhosis of the liver or severe hepatic disease.
    Headline
    major burn
    General Information
    Use potassium-containing phosphorus salts with caution in patients with a major burn because these patients are prone to hyperkalemia secondary to extensive tissue breakdown.
    Headline
    acute pancreatitis, hypoparathyroidism, primary adrenal insufficiency
    General Information
    Use potassium phosphate; sodium phosphate with caution in patients with primary adrenal insufficiency (untreated Addison's disease), hypoparathyroidism, or acute pancreatitis.
    Headline
    preeclampsia, pregnancy
    General Information
    There are no adequate, well-controlled studies of potassium phosphate; sodium phosphate use during human pregnancy, and animal reproduction studies have not been conducted. However, administration of recommended doses is not expected to cause major birth defects, miscarriage, or adverse outcomes. Use with caution in patients with preeclampsia, as sodium-containing phosphorus salts may exacerbate toxemia of pregnancy.
    Headline
    potassium-aggravated skeletal muscle channelopathy
    General Information
    Use potassium supplements with caution in patients with potassium-aggravated skeletal muscle channelopathy, such as hyperkalemic periodic paralysis, paramyotonia congenita, and potassium-aggravated myotonia/paramyotonia.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Potassium phosphate; sodium phosphate is compatible with breast-feeding. Administration of the recommended doses of potassium phosphate; sodium phosphate is not expected to cause harm to the breastfed child. Phosphorus intake during lactation appears to have no significant effect on phosphorus concentrations normally found in human milk.

There is, however, no significant interference with phosphorus absorption by oral dietary calcium at intakes within the typical adult range. Description
  Text
  Potassium phosphate; sodium phosphate is a phosphorus supplement that increases urinary phosphate and pyrophosphate, and can be used in the dietary management of hypophosphatemia. It is also used for urinary acidification and to augment the efficacy of methenamine therapy in treating urinary tract infections. Patients requiring potassium or sodium restriction may not be good candidates for this formulation due to the potassium and sodium content (specific content varies depending on formulation). When treatment is initiated, some patients experience a mild laxative effect. This usually resolves after the first few days but could persist to an unpleasant degree, requiring a dose reduction until the effect subsides or potentially discontinuation. Additionally, patients who have kidney stones may pass old stones upon initiation of phosphate therapy. Such patients should be warned of this possibility.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Phosphorus homeostasis
Phosphorus has a number of important functions in the biochemistry of the body. The bulk of phosphorus is located in the bones, where it plays a key role in osteoblastic and osteoclastic activities. Enzymatically catalyzed phosphate-transfer reactions are numerous and vital in the metabolism of carbohydrate, lipid, and protein, and a proper concentration of the anion is of primary importance in assuring an orderly biochemical sequence. In addition, phosphorus plays an important role in modifying steady-state tissue concentrations of calcium. Phosphate ions are important buffers of the intracellular fluid, and also play a primary role in the renal excretion of hydrogen ion. In idiopathic hypercalciuria, phosphates lower urinary calcium concentrations.
 
Phosphorus is present in high-energy adenosine triphosphate (ATP) bonds, which fuel a variety of physiological processes, including muscle contractions, neurologic function, and electrolyte transport, as well as other important biochemical reactions. Intracellular inorganic phosphate serves as the phosphorus source from which ATP is resynthesized. The prime determinant of intracellular inorganic phosphate is extracellular inorganic phosphate.
 
The relationship between phosphorus and calcium is a reciprocal one and is regulated partially by parathyroid hormone. Parathyroid hormone decreases the reabsorption of phosphate by the kidney, thereby lowering phosphate levels. The hormone stimulates an increase in calcium levels by increasing bone resorption, gut calcium absorption, and reabsorption of calcium in renal tubules. When serum phosphorus levels are high, serum calcium levels are generally low, and vice versa.
 
Urinary acidification
Phosphates are excreted at the distal renal tubule where hydrogen ion is exchanged for sodium ion causing a decrease in urine pH.
