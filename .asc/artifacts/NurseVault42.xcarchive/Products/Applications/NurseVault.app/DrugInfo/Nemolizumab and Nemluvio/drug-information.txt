    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Available data on nemolizumab use during pregnancy are insufficient to evaluate for a drug-associated risk of major birth defects, miscarriage, or other adverse outcomes. Transport of human IgG antibodies across the placenta increases as pregnancy progresses and peaks during the third trimester; therefore, nemolizumab may be transferred to the developing fetus. In an enhanced pre- and postnatal development study in cynomolgus monkeys, an increase in early postnatal death was observed with subcutaneous doses 36- and 50-times the maximum recommended human dose administered during organogenesis to parturition. The relevance of this nonclinical finding to clinical outcomes is not known.
    Headline
    neonates and infants exposed to this medication in utero
    General Information
    It is unclear how the immune system of neonates and infants exposed to this medication in utero will respond to an infection. Nemolizumab is an immunomodulatory agent. Transport of endogenous IgG antibodies across the placenta increases as the pregnancy progresses and peaks in the third trimester. Monitor for the development of serious infection during the first 3 months of life in infants exposed to nemolizumab in utero.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Use nemolizumab with caution during breast-feeding. There are no data on the presence of nemolizumab in human milk, its effects on the breast-fed child, or its effects on milk production. Nemolizumab was detected in the milk of monkeys. Endogenous maternal IgG and monoclonal antibodies are transferred in human milk. The effects of local gastrointestinal exposure and limited systemic exposure to nemolizumab on a breastfed child are unknown. Consider the benefits of breast-feeding, the patient's clinical need for treatment, and any potential adverse effects on the breast-fed child from the medication or from the patient's underlying medical condition.

Cholera Vaccine: (Moderate) Patients receiving immunosuppressant medications may have a diminished response to the live cholera vaccine. When feasible, administer indicated vaccines prior to initiating immunosuppressant medications. Counsel patients receiving immunosuppressant medications about the possibility of a diminished vaccine response and to continue to follow precautions to avoid exposure to cholera bacteria after receiving the vaccine.
Dengue Tetravalent Vaccine, Live: (Contraindicated) Avoid administration of the live dengue virus vaccine with immunosuppressive drug therapy and prior to immune recovery following treatment with immunosuppressive drug therapy. When feasible, administer indicated live virus vaccines at least 4 weeks before planned immunosuppression or wait until at least 3 months after discontinuation. The time to restoration of immune competence may be longer in some patients. Patients with altered immunocompetence may be at increased risk for severe adverse reactions due to uninhibited growth of the attenuated live virus. Additionally, vaccines may be less effective if administered during a period of altered immunocompetence.

Description
  Text
  Nemolizumab, an interleukin-31 receptor alpha (IL-31RA) antagonist, is a humanized monoclonal modified immunoglobulin G (IgG) antibody indicated for the treatment of prurigo nodularis and moderate-to-severe atopic dermatitis (eczema) when the disease is not adequately controlled with topical prescription therapies. Nemolizumab is the first approved monoclonal antibody that specifically targets IL-31. IL-31 is a naturally occurring cytokine involved in pruritis, inflammation, epidural dysregulation, and fibrosis. In clinical trials for prurigo nodularis, nemolizumab produced rapid reductions in itch by as early as 4 weeks and significant clinical improvements in itch and skin nodules by 16 weeks. Similarly, in clinical trials for atopic dermatitis, individuals treated with nemolizumab every 4 weeks in combination with topical corticosteroids, with or without topical calcineurin inhibitors, showed statistically significant improvements in skin clearance in both co-primary endpoints at 16 weeks. When treating atopic dermatitis, use nemolizumab in combination with topical corticosteroids and/or calcineurin inhibitors; however, discontinuation of these therapies is recommended when the disease has sufficiently improved. Nemolizumab may interfere with immune response to infections; therefore, the risks and benefits should be considered prior to administering live vaccines. Nemolizumab was FDA-approved in August 2024.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Nemolizumab is a humanized IgG2 monoclonal antibody that inhibits IL-31 signaling by binding selectively to IL-31 receptor alpha (IL-31 RA). IL-31 is a naturally occurring cytokine that is involved in pruritus, inflammation, epidermal dysregulation, and fibrosis. Nemolizumab inhibits IL-31-induced responses including the release of proinflammatory cytokines and chemokines.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Subcutaneous Route
    Description
    After an initial subcutaneous dose of 60 mg, nemolizumab reached a mean (SD) Cmax of 7.5 (2.31) mcg/mL by approximately 6 days post dose. Nemolizumab systemic exposure increased in an approximately dose-proportional manner across the subcutaneous dose range up to 30 mg. There was a decrease in bioavailability by 9% with the 60 mg subcutaneous dose and by 15% with the 90 mg subcutaneous dose.
Atopic dermatitis
In clinical trials, the estimated mean (SD) steady state trough concentrations of nemolizumab were 2.63 (1.27) mcg/mL for 30 mg administered every 4 weeks and 0.74 (0.44) mcg/mL for 30 mg administered every 8 weeks. Steady state nemolizumab concentrations were achieved by week 4 after the initial 60 mg loading dose.
Prurigo nodularis
In clinical trials, the estimated mean (SD) steady state trough concentrations of nemolizumab were 3.04 (1.23) mcg/mL in subjects weighing less than 90 kg and 3.66 (1.63) mcg/mL in subjects weighing of 90 kg or more. Steady state nemolizumab concentrations were achieved by week 4 in subjects weighing less than 90 kg and by week 12 in subjects weighing 90 kg or more.

    Obesity
    Description
    Body weight affects exposure to nemolizumab. Exposure decreases with increasing body weight. After a 30 mg dose every 4 weeks, steady state mean exposure parameters of those weighing more than 87 kg are expected to be 1.7-fold lower than those weighing less than 62 kg. The difference in systemic exposure due to body weight had no clinically meaningful impact on efficacy in subjects with atopic dermatitis; however, in those with prurigo nodularis, the variability in systemic exposure had a clinically meaningful impact on skin lesion efficacy (IGA response) but not on pruritus improvement.
  Text
  Nemolizumab is administered subcutaneously. The Vd of nemolizumab is estimated to be 7.67 L. The metabolic pathway of nemolizumab is unknown; however, as a human monoclonal IgG antibody, nemolizumab is expected to be degraded into small peptides by catabolic pathways in the same manner as endogenous IgG. The terminal elimination half-life (SD) of nemolizumab is estimated to be 18.9 (4.96) days and systemic clearance is estimated to be 0.263 L/day.

    Administration Details
      Name
      Subcutaneous Administration
      Description
      Preparation
Remove the product from the refrigerator and allow it to reach room temperature before injecting (30 to 45 minutes).
Check that the pen has not been dropped, damaged, cracked, the expiration date has not passed, and the powder is white and not dissolved.
Hold the pen upright (gray cap pointing upwards) and turn the activation knob to the right until it stops.
Wait until the gray rod stops moving. Do not shake before the gray rod has completely stopped.
When the gray rod has completely stopped, shake the pen up and down for 30 seconds to dissolve the medicine.
Wait 5 minutes for the bubbles to decrease and the powder to completely dissolve. If not completely dissolved, shake again for 30 seconds and wait 5 more minutes. A small foam layer or a few small air bubbles may remain in the dissolved medicine.
Check that the solution is clear and colorless to slightly yellow. Do not use if the solution contains visible particulate matter or is discolored or cloudy.
Storage: Discard reconstituted pens if not used within 4 hours.
 
Subcutaneous Injection
For initial dose, administer each of the 2 injections at different injection sites.
Administer subcutaneous injection into the front upper thighs or abdomen, except for the 2 inches (5 cm) around the navel. Injection into the upper arm should only be performed by a caregiver or health care professional.
Alternate the injection site with each injection. Do not inject into skin that is tender, inflamed, swollen, damaged or has bruises or scars or open wounds.
Hold the pen upright, twist the gray cap until the orange needle guard pops up. Gently pull the cap off the orange needle guard and dispose of it.
Place the pen on the injection site vertically so that the orange guard is flat against the skin.
Gently push the pen down until the orange needle guard is completely pushed in. Do not lift the pen and keep pushing it down.
Inject for 15 seconds until the orange rod and gray rod have stopped moving.
Discard any unused portions after administration.

Administration is intended for use under the guidance of a health care professional. Prior to the first injection, provide patients and/or caregivers with proper training on preparation and administration. Missed dose: If a dose is missed, administer the dose as soon as possible, and thereafter, resume dosing at the regular scheduled time.
