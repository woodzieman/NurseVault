Drug Class: Trabectedin is an alkylating agent indicated for the treatment of unresectable or metastatic liposarcoma and leiomyosarcoma in patients that had previously received anthracycline-containing chemotherapy.

Routes: injection (IV/IM/SC); Common adult doses: 1.5 mg/m2 IV every 3 weeks.

Common Side Effects (excluding routine GI upset): anemia, neutropenia, thrombocytopenia, arthralgia, elevated creatine kinase, myalgia, renal failure (unspecified), rhabdomyolysis, elevated hepatic enzymes, hepatic failure, hyperbilirubinemia, hypoalbuminemia, hypoesthesia, paresthesias, peripheral neuropathy, asthenia, fatigue, malaise

In an Overdose: No overdose/antidote information is listed in this file.

          Dosage Description
          1.5 mg/m2 IV continuous infusion on day 1 and repeated every 21 days until disease progression. Administer with recommended premedication. Coadministration of certain drugs may need to be avoided or dosage adjustments may be necessary; review drug interactions. Therapy interruption, a dosage reduction, or discontinuation may be necessary in patients who develop severe toxicity. The primary endpoint of median overall survival time was not significantly improved with trabectedin compared with dacarbazine (13.7 months vs. 13.1 months) in patients with previously treated, unresectable locally advanced or metastatic liposarcoma or leiomyosarcoma in a multinational, randomized (2:1), open-label, phase 3 trial. The median progression-free survival time was significantly improved with trabectedin compared with dacarbazine (4.2 months vs. 1.5 months). The median duration of response was 6.9 months versus 4.2 months, respectively.

  Contraindications And Precautions
    Headline
    Drug Interactions
    General Information
    The coadministration of certain medications may lead to harm and require avoidance or therapy modification; review all drug interactions prior to concomitant use of other medications.
    Headline
    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components.
    Headline
    Child-Pugh class B, Child-Pugh class C, hepatic failure
    General Information
    Do not administer trabectedin in patients with severe (Child-Pugh class C) hepatic impairment (bilirubin level greater than 3 times ULN and any AST/ALT) including hepatic failure. Use trabectedin with caution in patients with moderate (Child-Pugh class B) hepatic impairment (bilirubin level greater than 1.5 to 3 times ULN and any AST/ALT less than 8 times ULN); an initial dose reduction is necessary in these patients. Hepatotoxicity, including hepatic failure, has been reported with trabectedin therapy. Obtain liver function tests before each dose of trabectedin and as clinically indicated based on the severity of pre-existing hepatic impairment. An interruption of therapy, dose reduction, or permanent discontinuation of therapy may be necessary in patients who develop hepatic impairment.
    Headline
    cardiac disease, geriatric
    General Information
    Use trabectedin with caution in patients with a history of cardiac disease, left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) below the lower limit of normal, prior cumulative anthracycline dose of 300 mg/m2 or higher, or geriatric patients 65 years or older; these patients may be at increased for developing cardiac dysfunction. Cardiomyopathy, including cardiac failure, congestive heart failure, decreased ejection fraction, diastolic dysfunction, or right ventricular dysfunction, leading to death may occur with trabectedin therapy. Monitor the left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) by echocardiogram or multigated acquisition (MUGA) scan before starting therapy with trabectedin and every 2 to 3 months thereafter. Discontinuation of therapy may be necessary based on severity of cardiotoxicity.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Trabectedin may cause fetal harm when administered during pregnancy based on its mechanism of action. There are no adequate and well-controlled studies of trabectedin use during pregnancy. Animal reproductive and developmental studies at relevant doses have not been conducted; however, placental transfer of trabectedin was demonstrated in pregnant rats.
    Headline
    people who can cause pregnancy in others, people who may become pregnant, reproductive risk
    General Information
    Counsel people who may become pregnant and people who can cause pregnancy in others about the reproductive risk associated with trabectedin. Trabectedin may cause fetal harm. Verify pregnancy status prior to treatment initiation. Advise people who may become pregnant to use effective contraception while taking trabectedin and for 8 months after the last dose. Trabectedin may cause male-mediated teratogenicity. For those that can cause pregnancy in others, contraception should be used during trabectedin treatment and for 5 months after the last dose. Trabectedin may also result in impaired male or female fertility.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Avoid breast-feeding during the use of trabectedin and for 3 months after the last dose. There are no data on the presence of trabectedin in human milk, its effects on the breast-fed child, or its effects on milk production. However, trabectedin has the potential to cause serious side effects in the breast-fed child.

Interactions
Adagrasib: (Major) Avoid the concomitant use of trabectedin with adagrasib due to the risk of increased trabectedin exposure. If short-term adagrasib (less than 14 days) cannot be avoided, begin administration 1 week after the trabectedin infusion and discontinue it the day prior to the next trabectedin infusion. Trabectedin is a CYP3A substrate and adagrasib is a strong CYP3A inhibitor. Description
  Text
  Trabectedin is an alkylating agent indicated for the treatment of unresectable or metastatic liposarcoma and leiomyosarcoma in patients that had previously received anthracycline-containing chemotherapy. Neutropenic sepsis, hepatotoxicity, rhabdomyolysis, and cardiomyopathy have been reported with trabectedin use. Trabectedin was approved by the FDA in October 2015.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Trabectedin causes alkylation by binding guanine residues in the minor groove of DNA, forming adducts and resulting in a bending of the DNA helix towards the major groove. This triggers a cascade of events that can affect the activity of DNA binding proteins, including some transcription factors, and DNA repair pathways, resulting in disruption of the cell cycle and eventual cell death.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Intravenous Route
    Description
    The pharmacokinetics of trabectedin are characterized by a rapid decline phase at the end of the infusion, and slower exponential phases. Based on population pharmacokinetic analyses, the pharmacokinetics are dose-proportional over a range of 0.024 to 1.8 mg/m2; exposure is time-dependent. Plasma accumulation of trabectedin is not observed when administered every 3 weeks.

  Text
  Trabectedin is administered by intravenous infusion; it is highly bound to plasma proteins (97%), independent of concentrations ranging from 10 to 100 ng/mL. The steady-state volume of distribution (Vd) is greater than 5,000 liters. The mean clearance is 31.5 L/hour (CV%, 50%) and the terminal elimination half-life is approximately 175 hours. Trabectedin is extensively metabolized, with negligible unchanged drug in urine or feces. Trabectedin is not significantly renally excreted; in patients with solid tumors, 64% of a radiolabeled 3-hour or 24-hour dose was recovered in 24 days, with 6% in urine and 58% in feces.
 
Affected cytochrome P450 (CYP) isoenzymes and drug transporters: CYP3A
CYP3A is the predominant enzyme responsible for the hepatic metabolism of trabectedin. In vitro, trabectedin has limited inhibition or induction potential of CYP1A2, CYP2A6, CYP2B6, CYP2C9, CYP2C19, CYP2D6, CYP2E1, and CYP3A4.

Dosage Adjustment Guidelines
  Hepatic Impairment
  Baseline Hepatic Impairment
Mild hepatic impairment (bilirubin level greater than 1- to 1.5-times the upper limit of normal [ULN] and any AST or ALT level): No initial dose reduction is necessary.
Moderate hepatic impairment (bilirubin level greater than 1.5- to 3-times the ULN and AST and ALT level less than 8-times the ULN): Start at a reduced dose of 0.9 mg/m2 IV.
Severe hepatic impairment (bilirubin level greater than 3-times the ULN and any AST and ALT level): Use not recommended.
 
Recommended Dose Reductions
NOTE: Dose reductions are based on baseline hepatic function. After a dose reduction, do not increase the dose in subsequent treatment cycles.
Baseline normal hepatic function or mild hepatic impairment (bilirubin level greater than 1- to 1.5-times the ULN and any AST or ALT level)
First dose reduction: 1.2 mg/m2 IV
Second dose reduction: 1 mg/m2 IV
Baseline moderate hepatic impairment (bilirubin level of greater than 1.5- to 3-times the ULN and AST and ALT level less than 8-times the ULN)
First dose reduction: 0.6 mg/m2 IV
Second dose reduction: 0.3 mg/m2 IV
 
Management of Treatment-Related Hepatotoxicity
Permanently discontinue trabectedin in patients:
with normal hepatic function at baseline who develop severe liver dysfunction (defined as a bilirubin level of 2-times the ULN and an AST or ALT level 3-times the ULN with an alkaline phosphatase (AP) level less than 2-times the ULN in the prior treatment cycle
with moderate hepatic impairment at baseline who have an exacerbation of liver dysfunction
who require a third dose reduction due to toxicity
who have persistent toxicity resulting in a dose delay of more than 3 weeks
Hyperbilirubinemia
Total bilirubin level greater than the ULN: Delay the next dose for up to 3 weeks; resume therapy at a reduced dose.
Elevated hepatic enzymes
AST or ALT level of 2.5- to 5-times the ULN: Delay the next dose for up to 3 weeks; resume therapy at the previous dose.
AST or ALT level greater than 5-times the ULN: Delay the next dose for up to 3 weeks; resume therapy at a reduced dose.
AP level greater than 2.5-times the ULN: Delay the next dose for up to 3 weeks; resume therapy at a reduced dose.
  Renal Impairment
  No dose adjustment necessary in patients with baseline mild (CrCl 60 to 89 mL/min) or moderate (CrCl 30 to 59 mL/min) renal impairment. The pharmacokinetics of trabectedin have not been evaluated in patients with severe renal impairment (CrCl less than 30 mL/min) or end stage renal disease. Hemodialysis is not expected to enhance the elimination of trabectedin.
