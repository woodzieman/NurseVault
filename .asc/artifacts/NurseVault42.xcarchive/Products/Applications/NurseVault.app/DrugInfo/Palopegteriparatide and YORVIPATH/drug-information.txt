Indication Specific Dosing
  General Information
  The goal of treatment is to maintain a serum calcium within the normal range without the need for active vitamin D (e.g., calcitriol) or therapeutic calcium doses (elemental calcium more than 600 mg/day). Calcium supplementation sufficient to meet daily dietary requirements may be continued.
Within 2 weeks before the first dose of palopegteriparatide, confirm serum 25 (OH) vitamin D is within the normal range and albumin-corrected serum calcium is 7.8 mg/dL or more.
Use only 1 injection to achieve the once daily recommended dosage. Using 2 injections to achieve the recommended once daily dosage increases the risk of unintended changes in serum calcium levels, including hypocalcemia and hypercalcemia.
The maintenance dosage is individualized and should be the palopegteriparatide dose that achieves serum calcium within the normal range, without the need for active vitamin D or therapeutic doses of calcium. Calcium supplementation sufficient to meet daily dietary requirements may be continued. Once the maintenance dosage is achieved, monitor for clinical signs and symptoms of hypocalcemia or hypercalcemia and measure serum calcium levels as indicated, and at a minimum every 4 to 6 weeks, as some patients may require further dose titration. If calcium levels remain low with the maximum recommended dosage of 30 mcg once daily, consider adding or restarting calcium and/or active vitamin D therapy and/or seek other treatment.
Adjust palopegteriparatide, active vitamin D, and/or calcium supplements per the titration schedule below. Some patients may require an increase in the palopegteriparatide dose over time to maintain the same therapeutic effect.

        Dosage Description
        Individualize dosing. Initiate with 18 mcg subcutaneously once daily. Titrate in 3 mcg increments or decrements to the recommended dosage range of 6 to 30 mcg once daily. Do not increase the dosage more often than every 7 days. Do not decrease the dosage more often than every 3 days. The maximum dose is 30 mcg once daily. Adjust palopegteriparatide, active vitamin D, and/or calcium supplements per the titration schedule below. LIMITATIONS OF USE: Palopegteriparatide was not studied in patients with acute post-surgical hypoparathyroidism. Palopegteriparatide's titration scheme was only evaluated in adults who first achieved an albumin-corrected serum calcium of at least 7.8 mg/dL using calcium and active vitamin D treatment. MISSED DOSE: Take palopegteriparatide as soon as possible if a dose is missed by less than 12 hours. Skip the missed dose if the dose has been missed by more than 12 hours. Take the next dose as scheduled. DOSE DELAY/INTERRUPTION: If palopegteriparatide treatment is delayed or interrupted for 3 days or more, evaluate patients for signs and symptoms of hypocalcemia and consider measuring serum calcium. If indicated, resume treatment with, or increase the dose of calcium supplements and active vitamin D. Resume palopegteriparatide at the previously prescribed dose as soon as possible after an interruption, then measure serum calcium within 7 to 10 days and adjust doses of palopegteriparatide, active vitamin D, and/or calcium supplements as per titration schedule below.

    Hypersensitivity

    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components.
    Headline
    increased risk for osteosarcoma
    General Information
    Avoid prescribing palopegteriparatide to individuals who are at an increased risk for osteosarcoma (i.e., those with Paget disease of bone or unexplained elevations of alkaline phosphatase, individuals with open epiphyses, individuals with prior external beam or implant radiation therapy involving the skeleton, or hereditary disorders predisposing to osteosarcoma). Individuals with bone metastases or a history of skeletal malignancies and those with metabolic bone disorders should also not receive treatment with palopegteriparatide. Rare cases of osteogenic sarcoma (osteosarcoma) have rarely been reported during postmarketing experience in individuals treated with PTH analogs, including teriparatide therapy; however, an increased risk of osteosarcoma was not detected in observational studies in humans. An increase in the incidence of osteosarcoma was observed during animal studies in male and female rats treated with teriparatide. Osteosarcoma occurrence in rats is dependent on teriparatide or PTH dose and treatment duration. Consider the potential risk of this new primary malignancy before treating individuals with palopegteriparatide. There is limited data assessing the risk of osteosarcoma beyond 2 years of teriparatide use.  Instruct individuals to promptly report symptoms such as persistent localized pain or signs of soft tissue mass that is tender to palpation.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    There are no adequate and well-controlled studies of palopegteriparatide during human pregnancy to determine a drug-associated risk of adverse developmental outcomes. However, disease associated risks to the mother and fetus related to hypocalcemia in pregnancy are present. Maternal hypocalcemia can result in an increased rate of spontaneous abortion, premature and dysfunctional labor, and possible preeclampsia. Infants born to a mother with hypocalcemia can have associated fetal and neonatal hyperparathyroidism. This can lead to fetal and neonatal demineralization, subperiosteal bone resorption, osteitis fibrosa cystica, and neonatal seizures. In animal studies, doses approximately 13 and 16 times the maximum reccommended human dose caused no significant adverse effects. There is a pregnancy exposure registry that monitors outcomes in pregnant patients exposed to palopegteriparatide; information about the registry can be obtained by calling 1-844-442-7236.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Use palopegteriparatide with caution during breast-feeding. It is not known if palopegteriparatide is excreted in human breast milk, affects milk production, or has an adverse effect on breastfed infants. Infants breastfed by females treated with palopegteriparatide should be monitored for signs and symptoms of hypercalcemia or hypocalcemia. Monitoring of serum calcium in the infant should be considered. Consider the benefits of breast-feeding, the risk of potential infant drug exposure, and the risk of an untreated or inadequately treated maternal condition.

  Adverse Reactions
    Headline
    hypercalcemia, hypocalcemia
    General Information
    Treatment with palopegteriparatide can result in hypercalcemia or hypocalcemia. Serious hypercalcemia requiring hospitalization has occurred in patients with palopegteriparatide. Symptomatic hypercalcemia was reported in 8% of patients during clinical trials compared to none of the patients who took placebo. These cases all occurred within the first 3 months after treatment initiation. The risk of hypercalcemia is highest when initiating or increasing palopegteriparatide, but may occur at any time. The incidence of elevated albumin-corrected serum calcium greater than 10.6 mg/dL post-baseline was 54.1% in palopegteriparatide treated patients, compared to 9.5% in placebo treated patients. Elevated albumin corrected serum calcium greater than 12 mg/dL occurred in 13.1% of palopegteriparatide patients, compared to none of placebo-treated patients. Serious events of hypocalcemia have also been observed with PTH products, including palopegteriparatide. The risk is highest when palopegteriparatide is abruptly discontinued, but may occur at any time, even in those with stable dosing. Measure serum calcium 7 to 10 days after treatment initiation or after any dose change, or if any signs and symptoms of hypocalcemia or hypercalcemia are present. At minimum, serum calcium should be checked every 4 to 6 weeks once maintenance dose is achieved. Treat hypercalcemia or hypocalcemia if needed. If albumin-corrected serum calcium is greater than 12 mg/dL, withhold palopegteriparatide for at least 2 to 3 days. Less serious hypercalcemia should be treated by adjusting the dose of palopegteriparatide, active vitamin D, and/or calcium supplements.

Description
  Text
  Palopegteriparatide is a subcutaneously administered parathyroid hormone analog that is a prodrug of teriparatide. Teriparatide is identical to the 34 N-terminal amino acids of human parathyroid hormone (PTH). Palopegteriparatide is indicated for the treatment of hypoparathyroidism in adults as a once daily treatment. It works by exerting effects similar to endogenous PTH on osteoblasts, osteocytes, renal tubular cells, and other cells to stimulate bone turnover, promote renal calcium reabsorption and phosphate excretion, and facilitate active vitamin D synthesis. These effects lead to increased intestinal absorption of calcium and phosphate. Effectiveness and safety of palopegteriparatide was demonstrated in a 26 week, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled phase 3 study in 82 patients with hypoparathyroidism. Efficacy was assessed by the overall response to treatment at week 26, which included the proportion of subjects who had albumin-corrected serum calcium within the normal range, independence from active vitamin D and independence from therapeutic doses of calcium, no increase in study drug at week 22, no missing active vitamin D and calcium data at week 22, and study drug dose of 30 mcg or less once daily during 26 weeks of treatment. Of patients treated with palopegteriparatide, 68.9% met the efficacy endpoint compared to 4.8% of placebo treated patients with a treatment difference of 64.2% (95% CI, 49.5 to 78.8). Palopegteriparatide was FDA approved for use in August 2024.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Palopegteriparatide is parathyroid hormone analog and a prodrug of teriparatide (PTH(1-34)), which binds to parathyroid hormone 1 receptor (PTH1R). PTH1R is highly expressed on osteoblasts, osteocytes, renal tubular cells, and in several other tissues. Similar to endogenous PTH, PTH(1-34) released from palopegteriparatide maintains extracellular calcium and phosphate homeostasis by increasing serum calcium and decreasing serum phosphate. These effects are mediated by stimulating bone turnover to mobilize calcium and phosphate from bone, promoting renal calcium reabsorption and phosphate excretion, and facilitating active vitamin D synthesis, in turn increasing intestinal absorption of calcium and phosphate.

  Palopegteriparatide is administered as a subcutaneous injection. Palopegteriparatide is a prodrug of PTH (1-34) and the active metabolite PTH (1-33). PTH (1-33) and PTH (1-34) have comparable affinity to and activation of PTH1R. Subcutaneous Administration
Patients and caregivers should receive proper training and instruction on the proper use of the palopegteriparatide pen.
