Routes: intravenous; Common adult doses: Volume administered should be less than 20% of the individual's estimated plasma volume.

Common Side Effects (excluding routine GI upset): anemia, bleeding, eosinophilia, epistaxis, hematochezia, hematoma, hyperkalemia, hypokalemia, hypomagnesemia, hyponatremia, neutropenia, thrombocytopenia, candidiasis, conjunctivitis, fever, infection, cough, rhinitis, wheezing, erythema, petechiae, rash, urticaria, xerosis

In an Overdose: No overdose/antidote information is listed in this file.

Key Nursing Considerations: Contraindications/precautions: This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components. Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  Contraindications And Precautions
    Headline
    Drug Interactions
    General Information
    The coadministration of certain medications may lead to harm and require avoidance or therapy modification; review all drug interactions prior to concomitant use of other medications.
    Headline
    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components. Hypersensitivity reactions, including anaphylaxis, may occur with etuvetidigene autotemcel therapy; reactions may be due to dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) in the product.
    Headline
    Treatment-Related Restrictions of Use
    General Information
    Etuvetidigene autotemcel is contraindicated in individuals with previous treatment with hematopoietic stem cell gene therapy. The safety and efficacy of vaccination during or following treatment with etuvetidigene autotemcel has not be studied. Vaccination with live virus vaccines is not recommended until immune reconstitution is completed following treatment with etuvetidigene autotemcel.
    Headline
    allogeneic stem cell transplant, autologous stem cell transplant
    General Information
    Etuvetidigene autotemcel is contraindicated in individuals with previous treatment with an allogeneic stem cell transplant or autologous stem cell transplant within 6 months prior to screening or hematopoietic stem cell transplant (HSCT) with evidence of residual donor cells.
    Headline
    blood, organ, tissue, or cell donor
    General Information
    Advise individuals to avoid becoming a blood, organ, tissue, or cell donor after receiving treatment with etuvetidigene autotemcel.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Do not administer etuvetidigene autotemcel during pregnancy because of the risk of fetal harm associated with conditioning. There are no data on the use of etuvetidigene autotemcel during pregnancy to evaluate for a drug-associated risk of major birth defects, miscarriage, or adverse outcomes. Animal reproductive and developmental studies have not been conducted. Pregnancy after etuvetidigene autotemcel infusion should be discussed with the treating care team.
    Headline
    people who can cause pregnancy in others, people who may become pregnant, reproductive risk
    General Information
    Counsel people who may become pregnant and people who can cause pregnancy in others about the reproductive risk associated with etuvetidigene autotemcel. Verify pregnancy status prior to the start of mobilization and re-confirm prior to conditioning procedures and before administration of etuvetidigene autotemcel. Advise people who may become pregnant and people who can cause pregnancy in others to use effective contraception from the start of mobilization through at least 6 months after the administration of etuvetidigene autotemcel. There are no data on the effects of etuvetidigene autotemcel on fertility; however, myeloablative conditioning may impair fertility in people of reproductive potential. Inform patients of the option to cryopreserve spermatogonial stem cells or ovarian tissue before treatment, if appropriate.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    There are no data on the presence of etuvetidigene autotemcel in human milk, its effects on the breastfed child, or its effect on milk production. Due to potential risks associated with myeloablative conditioning, counsel lactating patients to avoid breast-feeding during conditioning. Consider the benefits of breast-feeding, the patient's clinical need for treatment, and any potential adverse effects on the breastfed child from the medication or from the patient's underlying medical condition.

Description
  Text
  Etuvetidigene autotemcel is an autologous hematopoietic stem cell-based gene therapy indicated for individuals with Wiskott-Aldrich Syndrome (WAS) who have a mutation in the WAS gene for whom hematopoietic stem cell transplantation is appropriate and no suitable human leukocyte antigen-matched related stem cell donor is available. WAS is a rare disease affecting the immune system caused by mutations in the WAS gene leading to deficiency of the WASP protein. The WASP protein is important in the structure and function of most blood cells. Symptoms of WAS usually begin in infancy with bloody diarrhea, abnormal bleeding, recurrent infections, eczema, or petechiae. Potential complications of WAS include hemolytic anemia, arthritis, vasculitis, and kidney or liver damage. Across clinical studies of etuvetidigene autotemcel, treated individuals showed a reduction in severe infections from 2 to 0.2 infections per patient-year and a reduction in bleeding events from 2 to 0.8 events per patient-year, compared with their pre-treatment baseline rates. The most common adverse reactions reported during clinical studies of etuvetidigene autotemcel were catheter-related infections, bacterial and viral infections, diarrhea, vomiting, and liver injury. Etuvetidigene autotemcel was FDA-approved in December 2025.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Through transduction with Wiskott-Aldrich Syndrome (WAS) lentiviral vector (LVV), etuvetidigene autotemcel adds full-length copies of the human WAS complementary deoxyribonucleic acid (cDNA) into the individual's hematopoietic stem cells (HSCs). After the infusion, the engineered cells engraft in the bone marrow, repopulate the hematopoietic compartment, and generate lymphoid and myeloid progenitors whose progeny express WAS protein (WASP). WASP regulates the structural protein actin in blood cells.
 
Pharmacodynamics
Engraftment of gene corrected cells was observed 1 month after etuvetidigene autotemcel administration and throughout post-treatment follow-up, up to 9 years after gene therapy, as indicated by VCN values in bone marrow and peripheral blood cell lineages above the protocol-defined target of 4% in BM-derived CD34+ cells and 10% in PB derived CD3+ cells. The presence of the WAS transgene resulted in the restoration of WASP expression in the hematopoietic compartment of all individuals tested after treatment with etuvetidigene autotemcel, with more than 65% at 1 year of follow-up in lymphocytes and more than 74% at 30 days follow-up in platelets stably expressing WASP. This restoration led to higher platelet counts, improved platelet structure and activation, and improved immune cell function in treated individuals.

Pharmacokinetics
  Text
  Etuvetidigene autotemcel is administered intravenously. Etuvetidigene autotemcel is an autologous gene therapy which includes hematopoietic stem cells (HSCs) that have been genetically modified ex vivo. Due to the nature of etuvetidigene autotemcel therapy, conventional studies on pharmacokinetics, absorption, distribution, metabolism, and elimination are not applicable.

Preparation
Remove 1 infusion bag from the metal cassette. Thaw the infusion bag in its sealed overwrap bag at 37 degrees C (98.6 degrees F) in a controlled thawing device until there is no visible ice in the infusion bag. When more than 1 infusion bag is provided, thaw and administer each infusion bag completely before proceeding to thaw the next infusion bag.
Once thawed completely, remove the infusion bag from the thawing device. Remove the infusion bag from the overwrap bag.
Gently massage the infusion bag to resuspend the cells. Small clumps of cellular material should disperse with gentle manual mixing. Do not shake the infusion bag.
Administer as soon as thawing is complete. Do not sample, alter, irradiate, or refreeze etuvetidigene autotemcel.
Storage: Keep thawed infusion bag at room temperature (20 to 25 degrees C) until infusion. Complete infusion, including any interruptions, within 2 hours of thawing.
 
Intravenous (IV) infusion
Confirm the patient's identity with the patient identifiers on the infusion bag.
Administer etuvetidigene autotemcel intravenously using a central venous catheter.
Ensure the administration set consists of a blood transfusion set equipped with a 200 micrometer filter.
Infuse the contents of each bag within 30 minutes at a maximum infusion rate of 5 mL/kg/hour.
After administration of infusion, flush all etuvetidigene autotemcel remaining in the infusion bag and associated tubing with sodium chloride 0.9% to ensure as many cells as possible are infused into the individual.
Monitor vital signs, including blood pressure, heart rate, and oxygen saturation, every 10 minutes during the infusion and then every hour for 3 hours post-infusion.
