Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  General Information
  Some studies have noted an apparent induction of mania or hypomania with SAM-e treatment; SAM-e should be generally avoided or used with caution in patients with bipolar disorder. Patients with known psychosis or affective illness should consult with a health care professional prior to use. Caution should be used when patients are taking prescription medication for such problems. Patients should not alter their prescribed regimens without the advice of a qualified health care professional. SAM-e could interfere with prescribed therapies. The decision to self-treat depressed moods with SAM-e should be weighed against the possibility that the problem could coexist with another medical condition (e.g., thyroid disease, and neurologic disorders). If depression persists, patients should seek proper medical advice. Patients with suicidal ideation should not self-treat their depression with SAM-e.The exact role of SAM-e on the dopaminergic system in the CNS is unknown; in vivo animal and human data suggest that exogenous SAM-e may influence the symptoms of Parkinson's disease or may require special monitoring. Consult a health care professional prior to use. The use of SAM-e in other neurological disease, like Alzheimer's, migraine, and seizure disorder, has not been studied in large or appropriately controlled clinical trials.Endogenous SAM-e appears to be important in the endogenous metabolism of estrogens, and has been used for the palliation of intrahepatic cholestasis in late pregnancy (see Dosage). Currently it is unknown if there is any effect of exogenous administration of SAM-e estrogen-dependent functions in healthy females. Until more is known, women should not use SAM-e during pregnancy or breast-feeding except on the advice of a qualified health care professional. It is unknown if SAM-e is excreted in breast milk.Since no data are currently available, SAM-e should be used cautiously in children under the age of 18 years old.Those patients with acquired immunodeficiency syndrome (AIDS) who experience symptoms of neuromuscular disease (i.e., spastic paraparesis, sensory disturbances in the lower extremities, impotence, or urinary changes) are encouraged to pursue a complete medical evaluation prior to the use of SAM-e. Some myelopathies respond to certain medical treatments and may not respond to SAM-e. A proper diagnosis is essential in providing proper treatments and attenuating these symptoms. AIDS patients with vacuolar myelopathy are encouraged to use SAM-e through enrollment in clinical trials.Patients with hepatic disease should be medically evaluated before the use of SAM-e; this will avoid the delay in detection of important pathologic etiologies like hepatitis C. Those patients with hepatic disease who are treated with SAM-e are encouraged to avoid ethanol intoxication. Ethanol intoxication depletes S-adenosyl-L-methionine and glutathione stores and thus may counteract any therapeutic benefit of SAM-e supplementation.

Adverse Reactions
  Headline
  Adverse Reactions
  General Information
  Adverse reactions with S-adenosyl-L-methionine (SAM-e) are unclear at this time; nausea/vomiting and dyspepsia have been reported. One review reported that roughly 5% of patients discontinue use of SAM-e secondary to gastrointestinal side effects; most of these withdrawals occur during the initiation of therapy and with doses >= 800 mg/day. Nausea or vomiting might be limited if the dosage is initiated at a lower dosage and titrated as tolerated to the therapeutic dosage.Like other antidepressant therapies, SAM-e may induce mania or emotional lability in those with bipolar-type illness. This effect may be the result of the underlying illness in response to SAM-e treatment.Intracerebral injections of SAM-e have been reported to induce parkinsonism in animals. It is unclear if pseudoparkinsonism would be induced in humans as an adverse effect. However, at least one clinical report of the use of SAM-e for the treatment of depression in Parkinson's patients has been published. None of the patients required alterations in their levodopa dosage and the motor component of the disease was not affected.

Description
  Text
  S-adenosyl-L-methionine (SAM-e) is a naturally occurring molecule that is endogenous to humans; it is present in almost every tissue and fluid in the body. Synonyms for SAM-e include ademetionine, AdoMet, and S-adenosylmethionine. SAM-e is important in a variety of biochemical reactions involving enzymatic transmethylation. The endogenous donation of methyl groups from SAM-e is important in the synthesis, activation and/or metabolism of a variety of other endogenous molecules such as hormones, nucleic acids, proteins, phospholipids, neurotransmitters, and some drugs. The human body manufactures SAM-e from methionine, an amino acid found in protein-rich foods. Clinical trials with SAM-e, mostly of open, uncontrolled, or inadequate scientific design, have been conducted for over 25 years. SAM-e is available by prescription in Europe, where it is known by proprietary names. SAM-e products are promoted in the U.S. for the adjunctive treatment of depression, osteoarthritis, or early-stage hepatic cirrhosis; however, the U.S. FDA has not endorsed SAM-e for the diagnosis, prevention, or treatment of any disease or condition. SAM-e was first discovered and isolated in Italy in 1952. However, it was not until 1975 that the synthesis of a chemically stable formulation was available for clinical use. SAM-e is designated as an orphan drug by the FDA for the treatment of AIDS-associated myelopathy.
 
NOTE: In the U.S., herbal products and dietary supplements are marketed under the Dietary Supplement and Health Education Act of 1994 (DSHEA). Consequently, scientific data supporting claimed benefit(s) are not always available for dietary supplements as they are for traditional pharmaceuticals since supplements are not regulated as drugs. Consumers should also note that rigid quality control standards are not required for these products and substantial variability can occur in both the potency and the purity of these products.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Mechanism of Action: Exogenous administration of S-adenosyl-L-methionine (SAM-e) results in a variety of actions similar to endogenous SAM, dependent on the tissue or organ involved. SAM-e is known to be active in the maintenance of several cell membrane types including neuronal and glial cells, hepatocytes, chondrocytes, erythrocytes, enterocytes and adipocytes. This section discusses the pharmacologic activities of SAM-e most relevant to current clinical applications.•Central nervous system activity: SAM-e appears to affect adrenergic transmission; tyrosine hydroxylase activity can be activated in vitro by SAM-e. Increases in the turnover of norepinephrine, dopamine, and serotonin are noted in various brain regions in rats. SAM-e appears to have inconsistent and weak inhibitory actions on monoamine oxidase (MAO) type B activity in animals. The antidepressant activity of SAM-e appears to be distinct from the tricyclic antidepressants, but no definitive result of SAM-e administration on neurotransmitter activity in humans has been assessed.In patients with neurologic disorders like myelopathy or with inborn errors of folate and one-carbon (C-1) metabolism, the use of methionine, SAM-e, or betaine is associated with the restoration of CNS methylation processes and subsequent evidence of remyelination on magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) or clinical improvements. SAM-e appears to be important in maintaining the membrane fluidity of nerve cells by increasing the methylation of phosphatidylethanolamine (PE) to phosphatidylcholine within the CNS and in decreasing CNS ischemia. Other mechanisms in the CNS may be present. The implications of the effects of SAM-e for the treatment of diseases like Alzheimer's or migraine headache are poorly understood and have not been evaluated.•Hepatic tissue activity: S-adenosyl-L-methionine is essential to the proper maintenance and function of hepatocytes. Endogenous SAM-e transmethylates phosphatidylethanolamine (PE) to form phosphatidylcholine (PC) in hepatic microsomes, and also regulates the function of Na-K-ATPase in the hepatocytes. PC is rich in polyunsaturated fatty acids and is incorporated into hepatocellular membranes. SAM-e has been shown to restore glutathione stores in rat models of cirrhosis, resulting in less lipid peroxidation and slowing the advance of hepatic steatosis and fibrosis. Cirrhosis is known to produce aberrations in endogenous conversion of methionine to SAM-e. In baboons that were chronically fed a diet containing 50% of calories from ethanol, SAM-e decreased hepatic mitochondrial damage and helped to maintain glutathione stores. Human data show that SAM-e decreases acetaldehyde levels and reverses glutathione depletion induced by acute ethanol ingestion; chemicals that deplete glutathione appear to counteract the effects of SAM-e. In patients with intrahepatic cholestasis, pruritus and clinical laboratory parameters have been noted to improve with SAM-e use; however, the clinical course of the disease may not be significantly altered. Positive changes in the liver function tests of patients with alcoholic liver disease have also been noted with SAM-e administration. •Joint and musculoskeletal activity: Intraarticular and intramuscular administration of SAM-e resulted in reductions in degenerative disease as assessed by radiographic joint-space measurements and histology evaluation of joint cartilage, surrounding tissues, and bone in animals. In humans, anti-inflammatory and analgesic effects of SAM-e, comparable to those of the NSAIDs, have been noted. SAM-e appears to enhance proteoglycan synthesis and secretion in cultures of human chondrocytes. Proteoglycans are important molecules in cartilage repair; and increased endogenous SAM-e concentrations can be demonstrated in human cartilage when proteoglycans are activated. Consistent with its conversion to glutathione and in contrast to NSAIDs, SAM-e does not appear to inhibit prostaglandin synthesis and has no damaging effects on the GI mucosa.

  Text
  S-adenosyl-L-methionine (SAM-e) has been administered by the intravenous and intramuscular routes in Europe, where it is available in a parenteral formulation. In the US, SAM-e is administered orally. Bioavailability and purity issues related to oral SAM-e nutritional supplements exist. Raw materials of SAM-e are reported to biodegrade very quickly upon exposure to heat and moisture; inexperienced manufacturers may not properly handle the raw product. Among 13 brands of SAM-e dietary supplements tested in one study, only seven were found to contain labeled amounts of SAM-e according to ConsumerLab, an independent analysis group for dietary supplements marketed in the US. The use of pharmaceutical-grade dosage forms from reliable pharmaceutical manufacturers has been recommended. 
 
Plasma-protein binding is negligible. Some distribution to the CNS occurs. Animal data suggest that exogenously administered SAM-e is metabolized in the same manner as the endogenous compound. After transmethylation (i.e., transfer of a methyl-group from SAM-e to other acceptor compounds, like neurotransmitters and phospholipids), S-adenosylhomocysteine is formed. S-adenosylhomocysteine undergoes hydrolysis to homocysteine and ultimately to cysteine, which is then transulphurated to sulphates, taurine, and reduced-glutathione. Glutathione is the major sulfhydryl group donator for binding and detoxifying various compounds. In the presence of appropriate amounts of B-vitamins, like folate, the homocysteine produced is converted to methionine. Adenosine and ATP are released in the metabolic process. SAM-e also undergoes some minor decarboxylation to various polyamines.
 
The half-life of SAM-e ranges from 1.5—2 hours in healthy adults. Less than 16% of the drug is recovered in the urine within 48 hours; roughly 23% is excreted in the feces within 72 hours. Most of a SAM-e dose is expected to be metabolized and incorporated into cellular pools.
