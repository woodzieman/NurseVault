          Heading
          Adults weighing 50 kg or more
          Dosage Description
          160 mg PO twice daily until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity. Treatment with selpercatinib significantly improved progression-free survival (PFS) compared with pemetrexed plus platinum-based chemotherapy and pembrolizumab (24.8 months vs. 11.2 months) in patients with previously untreated unresectable, locally advanced or metastatic RET fusion-positive NSCLC in a multicenter, randomized, open-label, phase 3 trial (LIBRETTO-431). The overall response rate (ORR) was 84% versus 65% (complete response [CR], 7% vs. 6%), respectively, for a median duration of response (DOR) of 24.2 months in the selpercatinib arm compared with 11.5 months in patients who received chemotherapy plus pembrolizumab; a duration of at least 12 months was experienced by 60% and 30% of patients, respectively. Overall survival was immature, with crossover from the control arm to selpercatinib permitted following disease progression. Of patients with CNS metastases at baseline (n = 29), intracranial responses were observed in 82% of patients who received selpercatinib and in 58% of those treated with chemotherapy/pembrolizumab. The ORR of selpercatinib was 61% (CR, 7.3%) for a median duration of 28.6 months in one cohort of patients with advanced RET fusion-positive NSCLC who had progressed on platinum-based chemotherapy in a multicenter, multicohort trial (LIBRETTO-001); 63% of patients had a DOR of at least 12 months. In the same trial, the ORR was 84% (CR, 5.8%) in patients with treatment-naive locally advanced or metastatic RET fusion-positive NSCLC; the median DOR in this population was 20.1 months, with 50% having a duration of at least 12 months. Intracranial responses were observed in 86% of patients with measurable CNS metastases at baseline in these 2 populations (n = 21).

Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  Contraindications And Precautions
    Headline
    Drug Interactions
    General Information
    The coadministration of certain medications may lead to harm and require avoidance or therapy modification; review all drug interactions prior to concomitant use of other medications.
    Headline
    Child-Pugh class A, Child-Pugh class B, Child-Pugh class C, hepatic failure
    General Information
    Reduce the dose of selpercatinib in patients with severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh class C; or total bilirubin greater than 3 to 10 times the upper limit of normal [ULN] and any AST); there are no data on the use of selpercatinib in patients with hepatic failure. While a dose modification is not recommended for patients with mild or moderate hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh class A, Child-Pugh class B, total bilirubin less than or equal to ULN with AST greater than ULN, or total bilirubin greater than 1 to 3 times ULN with any AST), monitor these patients for selpercatinib-related adverse reactions. Additionally, selpercatinib can cause hepatotoxicity; monitor ALT and AST at baseline, every 2 weeks for the first 3 months of therapy, and then monthly thereafter and as clinically indicated. An interruption of therapy, dose reduction, or discontinuation of therapy may be necessary if hepatotoxicity occurs.
    Headline
    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    Hypersensitivity may occur with use of selpercatinib. Signs and symptoms of hypersensitivity include fever, rash and arthralgias or myalgias with concurrent decreased platelets or transaminitis. The median time to onset was 1.9 weeks (range: 5 days to 2 years). If hypersensitivity occurs, withhold selpercatinib and begin corticosteroids at a dose of 1 mg/kg prednisone (or equivalent). Upon resolution of the event, resume selpercatinib at a reduced dose and increase the dose by one dose level each week as tolerated, until reaching the dose taken prior to onset of hypersensitivity. Continue steroids until the target dose is attained and then taper the corticosteroid. Permanently discontinue the drug for recurrent hypersensitivity.
    Headline
    pulmonary disease
    General Information
    Use selpercatinib with caution in patients who have a history of pre-existing pulmonary disease; severe pneumonitis/interstitial lung disease has been reported in patients treated with selpercatinib. Monitor patients for new or worsening respiratory symptoms including fever, cough, or dyspnea. Immediately interrupt selpercatinib therapy in patients with new or worsening respiratory symptoms and permanently discontinue therapy if no other potential causes are identified.
    Headline
    hypertension
    General Information
    Do not begin selpercatinib therapy in patients with uncontrolled hypertension; optimize blood pressure prior to initiating treatment. Selpercatinib has commonly been associated with hypertension. Monitor blood pressure after 1 week of treatment, at least monthly thereafter, and as clinically indicated. Initiate or adjust antihypertensive therapy as appropriate. An interruption of therapy, dose reduction, or discontinuation of therapy may be necessary for patients if hypertension occurs.
    Headline
    bradycardia, cardiomyopathy, congenital long QT syndrome, coronary artery disease, females, geriatric, heart failure, hypocalcemia, hypokalemia, hypomagnesemia, QT prolongation
    General Information
    Avoid use of selpercatinib in people with baseline QT prolongation or who have conditions that may increase the risk of QT prolongation or torsade de pointes, including bradycardia, congenital long QT syndrome, hypocalcemia, hypokalemia, hypomagnesemia, geriatric adults, females, structural abnormalities that interfere with electrical conduction (e.g., cardiomyopathy, coronary artery disease, ischemic heart disease), or in those who have other additional risk factors for QT prolongation or torsade de pointes. The use of other medications that have been associated with QT prolongation or torsade de pointes may further increase risk. Additionally, monitor patients with severe or uncontrolled heart failure for QT prolongation. If selpercatinib is used in patients at increased risk for QT prolongation, assess the QT interval, electrolytes and TSH at baseline and periodically during treatment, adjusting the frequency based upon risk factors including diarrhea; monitor more frequently when selpercatinib is concomitantly administered with strong and moderate CYP3A inhibitors or drugs known to prolong QT interval. Correct any hypokalemia, hypomagnesemia, and hypocalcemia prior to initiating selpercatinib and during treatment. An interruption of therapy, dose reduction, or discontinuation of therapy may be necessary if QT prolongation occurs.
    Headline
    major surgery, planned procedure
    General Information
    Do not administer selpercatinib for at least 7 days prior to a planned procedure or elective surgery. After major surgery, wait at least 2 weeks and until adequate wound healing prior to restarting selpercatinib therapy. Impaired wound healing can occur in patients who receive drugs that inhibit the vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) signaling pathway such as selpercatinib. The safety of resuming treatment after resolution of wound healing complications has not been established.
    Headline
    children
    General Information
    Monitor growth plates in adolescents and children with open growth plates who are treated with selpercatinib; additionally, monitor pediatric patients for symptoms indicative of slipped capital femoral epiphysis/slipped upper femoral epiphysis (SCFE/SUFE). Based on animal data, there may be a risk for growth inhibition in pediatric patients. Consider interrupting or discontinuing therapy based on the severity of any growth plate abnormalities and based on an individual risk-benefit assessment. In a juvenile rat study, daily administration of selpercatinib during what would be the human equivalent of childhood to late adolescence increased the physeal thickness of multiple bones and was associated with irreversible decreases of trabecular bone. Growth plate changes were associated with impaired bone modeling, resulting in decreased femur length and reduction in bone mineral density. SCFE/SUFE occurred in 1 adolescent treated with selpercatinib (n = 27) in a multicohort trial; if SCFE/SUFE occurs, treat as medically and surgically appropriate.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Selpercatinib can cause fetal harm if used during pregnancy, based on its mechanism of action and data from animal studies. There are no available data on the use of selpercatinib during human pregnancy. Administration of selpercatinib to pregnant rats during organogenesis resulted in embryo-lethality and fetal malformations at exposures approximately equal to the human exposure at the clinical dose based on AUC. Description
  Text
  Selpercatinib is a kinase inhibitor that targets wild-type RET and multiple mutated RET isoforms. It is indicated for the treatment of adult patients with metastatic RET fusion-positive non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), and adult and pediatric patients 2 years of age and older with advanced or metastatic RET-mutant medullary thyroid cancer who require systemic therapy, advanced or metastatic RET fusion-positive thyroid cancer who require systemic therapy and who are radioactive iodine-refractory (if appropriate), and locally advanced or metastatic solid tumors with a RET gene fusion that have progressed on or following prior systemic therapy or who have no satisfactory alternative treatment options. Selpercatinib can cause hepatotoxicity, hypertension, hypothyroidism, and QT prolongation; monitoring is recommended. Selpercatinib can adversely affect wound healing and should be avoided for at least 7 days prior to elective surgery, for 2 weeks after major surgery, and until wounds have adequately healed after any surgery; the safety of resuming selpercatinib after the resolution of wound healing complications has not been established.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Selpercatinib is a kinase inhibitor that targets wild-type RET and multiple mutated RET isoforms, as well as VEGFR1 and VEGFR3. Certain point mutations in RET or chromosomal rearrangements involving in-frame fusions of RET with various partners can result in constitutively activated chimeric RET fusion proteins that can act as oncogenic drivers by promoting cell proliferation of tumor cell lines. In in vitro and in vivo tumor models, selpercatinib demonstrated anti-tumor activity in cells harboring constitutive activation of RET protein resulting from gene fusions and mutations, including CCDC6-RET, KIF5B-RET, RET V804M, and RET M918T. Additionally, selpercatinib showed anti-tumor activity in mice intracranially implanted with a patient-derived RET fusion-positive tumor. Selpercatinib has also inhibited FGFR1, FGFR2, and FGFR3 at concentrations approximately 60-fold higher than RET, and inhibited VEGF3 at concentrations approximately 8-fold higher than RET.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Oral Route
    Description
    The capsule and tablet dosage forms of selpercatinib are bioequivalent. The mean steady-state Cmax of selpercatinib was 2,980 ng/mL (CV, 53%) and the mean AUC was 51,600 ng x hour/mL (CV, 58%). The steady-state AUC and Cmax of selpercatinib increased in a slightly greater than dose-proportional manner over a range of 20 mg once daily to 240 mg twice daily. Steady-state was reached by approximately day 7, and the mean accumulation ratio after administration of 160 mg twice daily was 3.4-fold. The median time to maximal concentration (Tmax) is 2 hours. The mean bioavailability of selpercatinib in healthy subjects is 73% (range, 60% to 82%).
 
The administration of a high-fat meal (900 calories with 58 g carbohydrate, 56 g fat, and 43 g protein) did not have a clinically significant effect on the AUC or Cmax of selpercatinib in healthy subjects. Administration with a proton pump inhibitor decreased the AUC and Cmax of selpercatinib by 69% and 88%, respectively. However, the AUC of selpercatinib increased by 2% and the Cmax decreased by 49% when administered with a proton pump inhibitor and a high-fat meal; the AUC of selpercatinib was unchanged and the Cmax decreased by 22% when administered with a proton pump inhibitor and a low-fat meal. Patients should take selpercatinib with food if administration with a proton pump inhibitor is unavoidable. Administration of an H2-receptor antagonist 10 hours before and 2 hours after a selpercatinib dose did not result in a clinically significant difference in selpercatinib pharmacokinetics.

  Text
  Selpercatinib is administered orally. It is 97% protein-bound, independent of concentration. The volume of distribution of selpercatinib is 191 L, and the blood-to-plasma concentration ratio is 0.7. The apparent clearance of selpercatinib is 6 L/hour in treated patients, and the half-life is 32 hours in healthy subjects. Selpercatinib is metabolized in the liver, predominantly by CYP3A4. Following oral administration of a single radiolabeled 160 mg dose of selpercatinib to healthy subjects, unchanged selpercatinib constituted 86% of the radioactive drug components in plasma. After oral administration to healthy subjects, 69% of a single radiolabeled dose was recovered in feces (14% unchanged) and 24% in the urine (12% unchanged).
 
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes and transporters: CYP2C8, CYP3A4, P-gp, BCRP, MATE1
Selpercatinib is primarily metabolized by CYP3A4. Strong or moderate CYP3A4 inhibitors may increase selpercatinib plasma concentrations; strong or moderate CYP3A4 inducers decrease selpercatinib plasma concentrations. It is a moderate inhibitor of CYP2C8, a weak inhibitor of CYP3A4, and a P-gp and BCRP inhibitor. In vitro, selpercatinib inhibits MATE1 and may increase serum creatinine by decreasing renal tubular secretion of creatinine via inhibition of MATE1. Selpercatinib is also an in vitro substrate for P-gp and BCRP.

    Oral Administration
    Description
    Selpercatinib may be taken with or without food unless coadministered with a proton pump inhibitor. If the patient is also taking a proton pump inhibitor, selpercatinib must be taken with food.

Oral Dispersion
The 40 mg selpercatinib tablet may be dispersed and administered orally or via nasogastric or gastronomy tube; use only the selpercatinib 40 mg tablet to prepare the dispersion.
Refer caregivers to the Instructions for Use provided with the medication on how to correctly prepare and administer selpercatinib oral dispersion.
Preparation for Oral Administration
Add the correct number of selpercatinib 40 mg tablets required for the prescribed dose to a glass or medicine cup containing approximately 1 tablespoon (15 mL) of room temperature or chilled water or 100% carrot puree.
Stir intermittently until the tablet(s) have dispersed, approximately 7 to 10 minutes; some settling may occur and dispersion may appear cloudy if water is used.
Administer the entire dispersion immediately.
Swirl or stir an additional 1 tablespoon (15 mL) of water into the glass or medicine cup to collect any remaining medicine and administer immediately.
Discard selpercatinib dispersion if not taken within 2 hours.
Preparation for Administration via Gastronomy or Nasogastric Tube
Use only polyurethane (French size 8 or larger) or vinyl chloride (French size 10 or larger) nasogastric tube, or silicone (French size 14 or larger) gastronomy tube.
Add the correct number of selpercatinib 40 mg tablets required for the prescribed dose to a glass or medicine cup containing a minimum of 10 mL of room temperature water.
Stir intermittently until the tablet(s) have dispersed, approximately 7 to 10 minutes; some settling may occur and dispersion may appear cloudy.
Flush feeding tube according to manufacturer's instructions.
Draw the dispersion into an enteral syringe and immediately administer via the feeding tube.
Add 5 mL of water to the glass or medicine cup, swirl or stir to collect the remaining medicine, and administer the rinse via the feeding tube using the same syringe; repeat rinse as necessary to ensure the full dose is delivered.
Flush feeding tube according to manufacturer's instructions.
Discard selpercatinib dispersion if not taken within 2 hours.

Dosage Adjustment Guidelines
  Hepatic Impairment
  Baseline Hepatic Impairment
Mild hepatic impairment (total bilirubin less than or equal to the upper limit of normal (ULN) with AST greater than ULN; or total bilirubin 1.1 to 1.5 times ULN with any AST): No dosage adjustment is recommended.
Moderate hepatic impairment (total bilirubin 1.6 to 3 times ULN and any AST): No dosage adjustment is recommended.
Severe hepatic impairment (total bilirubin 3.1 to 10 times ULN and any AST): If the starting dose was 120 mg or 160 mg twice daily, reduce the dose of selpercatinib to 80 mg PO twice daily. If the starting dose was 80mg twice daily or 40 mg three times daily, reduce the dose of selpercatinib to 40 mg twice daily.
 
Treatment-Related Hepatotoxicity
Grade 3 or 4: Hold selpercatinib therapy and monitor AST/ALT once weekly. Upon resolution to grade 1 or baseline, reduce the dose of selpercatinib by 2 dose levels and resume treatment; monitor AST and ALT once weekly. After a minimum of 2 weeks without recurrence, increase the dose of selpercatinib by 1 dose level; continue to monitor AST and ALT once weekly. After a minimum of 4 weeks without recurrence, increase the dose of selpercatinib to the dose taken prior to the onset of grade 3 or 4 increased AST/ALT; continue to monitor AST and ALT once weekly for an additional 4 weeks, then resume regular monitoring.
  Renal Impairment
  Baseline Renal Impairment
Mild to severe renal impairment (eGFR 15 to 89 mL/minute/1.73 m2): No dosage adjustment is recommended.
End-stage renal impairment (eGFR less than 15 mL/minute/1.73 m2) or end-stage renal disease (ESRD): The recommended dose for selpercatinib has not been established.
