Drug Class: Lisocabtagene maraleucel is a chimeric antigen receptor (CAR) T-cell gene therapy.

Routes: injection (IV/IM/SC); Common adult doses: 110 X 106 CAR-positive viable T-cells as a single IV dose.

Common Side Effects (excluding routine GI upset): hepatitis b exacerbation, hypogammaglobulinemia, infection, anemia, lymphopenia, neutropenia, thrombocytopenia, cardiomyopathy, hypertension, hypotension, edema, fluid retention, peripheral edema, pulmonary edema, asterixis, muscle cramps, musculoskeletal pain, myopathy, ptosis, weakness, cough, dyspnea, hypoxia, pleural effusion, pulmonary embolism, stroke, thrombosis, acneiform rash, contact dermatitis, ecchymosis

In an Overdose: No overdose/antidote information is listed in this file.

Key Nursing Considerations: Contraindications/precautions: This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components. Serious hypersensitivity reactions, including anaphylaxis, may occur due to dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) in lisocabtagene maraleucel.

  Contraindications And Precautions
    Headline
    Drug Interactions
    General Information
    The coadministration of certain medications may lead to harm and require avoidance or therapy modification; review all drug interactions prior to concomitant use of other medications.
    Headline
    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components. Serious hypersensitivity reactions, including anaphylaxis, may occur due to dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) in lisocabtagene maraleucel.
    Headline
    General Information
    General Information
    Vaccination with live viral vaccines during or following treatment with lisocabtagene maraleucel has not been studied. Live virus vaccination is not recommended for at least 6 weeks prior to the start of lymphodepleting chemotherapy, during lisocabtagene maraleucel therapy, and until immune recovery after lisocabtagene maraleucel therapy.
    Headline
    cytokine release syndrome
    General Information
    Cytokine release syndrome (CRS) is a potentially fatal reaction that has been reported with lisocabtagene maraleucel therapy. Do not administer lisocabtagene maraleucel in patients with active infection or inflammatory disorder. Promptly initiate treatment of CRS with supportive care, tocilizumab and/or corticosteroids as indicated. Intensive care supportive therapy may be required in patients who have severe or life-threatening CRS.
    Headline
    activities requiring coordination and concentration, driving or operating machinery, neurological disease, neurotoxicity
    General Information
    Use lisocabtagene maraleucel with caution in patients with a history of neurological disease (e.g., seizures, stroke, or memory loss). Severe and fatal neurotoxicity (e.g., immune effector cell-associated neurotoxicity syndrome [ICANS], encephalopathy, seizures, cerebral edema) has been reported with lisocabtagene maraleucel therapy. Neurologic toxicity may occur concurrently with cytokine release syndrome (CRS) or after CRS resolves. Advise patients to avoid driving or operating machinery or performing activities requiring coordination and concentration for at least 2 weeks after the lisocabtagene maraleucel infusion due to the risk of neurologic events (e.g., mental status changes, seizures) and altered or decreased consciousness or coordination. Administer supportive care and/or corticosteroids as indicated. Begin nonsedating antiseizure medicines (e.g., levetiracetam) for seizure prophylaxis in patients who develop any grade neurotoxicity. Provide intensive care supportive therapy in patients who develop severe or life-threatening neurologic toxicities.
    Headline
    new primary hematologic malignancy
    General Information
    New primary hematologic malignancy has occurred following treatment with BCMA- and CD19-directed genetically modified autologous T cell immunotherapies, including lisocabtagene maraleucel. Mature T cell malignancies, including CAR-positive tumors, may present as soon as weeks following infusion, and may include fatal outcomes; life-long monitoring is recommended following lisocabtagene maraleucel therapy.
    Headline
    inflammatory disorder
    General Information
    Do not administer lisocabtagene maraleucel in patients with active infection or inflammatory disorder. Use lisocabtagene maraleucel with caution in patients with recent infection. Serious and fatal infections including bacterial infection, fungal infection, viral infection, and opportunistic infections have been reported with axicabtagene ciloleucel therapy. Administer prophylactic antimicrobials according to local guidelines. Hepatitis B virus (HBV) reactivation resulting in fulminant hepatitis, hepatic failure, and death may occur with drugs directed against B cells, such as lisocabtagene maraleucel. Screen for viral infection prior to initiating treatment; consider prophylactic anti-infective management in accordance with clinical guidelines before collection of cells for manufacturing (leukapheresis); consider antiviral prophylaxis per standard institutional guidelines in patients with a prior history of HBV.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Lisocabtagene maraleucel is not recommended for individuals who are pregnant, and pregnancy after administration should be discussed with the treating physician. There are no available human or animal data with the use of lisocabtagene maraleucel during pregnancy. It is not known if lisocabtagene maraleucel has the potential to be transferred to the fetus. However, based on its mechanism of action, fetal toxicity including B-cell lymphocytopenia and hypogammaglobulinemia may occur if the transduced cells cross the placenta.
    Headline
    people who can cause pregnancy in others, people who may become pregnant, reproductive risk
    General Information
    Counsel people who may become pregnant and people who can cause pregnancy in others about the reproductive risk associated with lisocabtagene maraleucel. Based on its mechanism of action, lisocabtagene maraleucel has the potential to cause fetal harm. Verify pregnancy status prior to treatment. Follow contraceptive guidance specific to the agents used in patients who receive lymphodepleting chemotherapy. There is insufficient exposure data to provide a recommendation concerning the duration of contraception required after treatment with lisocabtagene maraleucel. There are no data on the effects of lisocabtagene maraleucel on fertility.
    Headline
    blood, organ, tissue, or cell donor
    General Information
    Patients must avoid being a blood, organ, tissue, or cell donor during treatment with lisocabtagene maraleucel.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Use lisocabtagene maraleucel with caution during breast-feeding. There is no information on the presence of lisocabtagene maraleucel in human milk, its effects on the breastfed child, or its effects on milk production. A risk to the breastfed child cannot be excluded. Consider the developmental and health benefits of breast-feeding along with the clinical need for lisocabtagene maraleucel and any potential adverse effects on the breast-fed child from lisocabtagene maraleucel or from the patient's underlying maternal condition.

Adverse Reactions
  Headline
  Adverse Reactions
  Adverse Reactions
    Headline
    hepatitis B exacerbation, hypogammaglobulinemia, infection
    General Information
    Infection (33%; grade 3 or higher, 12%) and hypogammaglobulinemia (9%; or 29% when defined as an adverse reaction or immunoglobulin G level less than 500 mg/dL after therapy) were reported in patients with non-Hodgkin lymphoma (NHL) in clinical trials (n = 769). In clinical trials, infectious events included infection (pathogen unspecified) (12% to 29%; grade 3 or higher, 4.7% to 16%), viral infection (1.9% to 10%; grade 3 or higher, 2% or less), bacterial infection (3.5% to 13%; grade 3 or higher, 6% or less), upper respiratory tract infection (8% to 19%; grade 3 or higher, 2.3% or less), fungal infection (3% to 9%; grade 3 or higher, 0.7% or less), pneumonia (1.9% to 8%), sepsis including septic shock (0.9% to 10%; grade 3 or higher, 7% or less), and urinary tract infection (7% or less). Four months after treatment, 1 patient with follicular lymphoma who received 4 prior lines of therapy developed a fatal case of John Cunningham (JC) virus progressive multifocal leukoencephalopathy and 1 patient with mantle cell lymphoma who received 3 prior lines of therapy developed a fatal case of cryptococcal meningoencephalitis 35 days after treatment. Hepatitis B exacerbation/reactivation (HBV), in some cases resulting in hepatitis, hepatic failure, and death, can occur in patients treated with drugs directed against B cells; 35 of the 38 patients with a prior history of HBV were treated with concurrent antiviral suppressive therapy in clinical trials. Manage immunoglobulin deficiency with infection precautions, prophylactic antibiotic therapy, and immunoglobulin replacement per standard guidelines.

    Headline
    laboratory test interference
    General Information
    False-positive HIV tests have been reported in patients who received treatment with chimeric antigen receptor (CAR) T-cell immunotherapy, such as lisocabtagene maraleucel. The use of gammaretroviral or lentiviral vectors to reprogram T-cells as part of CAR T-cell therapy has resulted in laboratory test interference with HIV-1 nucleic acid amplification testing (NAAT).   Text
  Lisocabtagene maraleucel is a chimeric antigen receptor (CAR) T-cell gene therapy. It is CD19-directed immunotherapy that works by using a patient's own genetically altered immune cells to kill B-cell cancer cells in the blood. Lisocabtagene maraleucel is indicated for use in adult patients with relapsed or refractory large B-cell lymphoma, follicular lymphoma, mantle cell lymphoma, marginal zone lymphoma, and chronic lymphocytic leukemia or small lymphocytic lymphoma. It is not indicated for the treatment of patients with primary central nervous system lymphoma. Lisocabtagene maraleucel has black box warning for cytokine release syndrome, severe neurotoxicity, and new T-cell malignancies.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Lisocabtagene maraleucel is a chimeric antigen receptor (CAR) T-cell therapy that works by redirecting T-cells to target the CD19 antigen on B-cells in patients with hematologic malignancies. The intracellular domain, CD3-zeta, initiates T-cell activation and mediates antitumor activity; the 4-1BB (CD137) costimulatory domain promotes antitumor activity and enhances proliferation of CAR T-cells. The binding of CAR to CD19 activates lisocabtagene maraleucel and promotes cell expansion and differentiation and triggers the lysis of CD19-positive cells.
This immunotherapy involves removing, genetically modifying, and then re-infusing a patient's own T-cells. During the manufacturing process, a lentiviral vector encodes the CAR molecule via transduction; the vector enters the cell and becomes integrated into the chromosomes of T cells and directs transcription of the lisocabtagene maraleucel CAR. Lisocabtagene maraleucel manufacturing transduces and expands CD4+ and CD8+ T-cells and these are administered in a fixed 1:1 ratio.
To produce CAR T-cell therapy, T-cells are collected from the blood by leukapheresis, enriched, activated, incubated with a viral vector encoding the CD19 CAR, expanded, and then washed, concentrated, and cryopreserved.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Intravenous Route
    Description
    The Cmax and AUC(0-28) of lisocabtagene maraleucel were 2- to 2.3-fold and 1.8- to 1.9-fold higher, respectively, in responding patients with relapsed or refractory large B-cell lymphoma (LBCL) or chronic lymphocytic leukemia (CLL)/small lymphocytic lymphoma (SLL) compared to nonresponders in 2 clinical trials. In these trials, the Cmax of responders versus nonresponders with LBCL was 35,335 copies/mcg versus 15,527 copies/mcg, respectively, and the AUC was 273,552 day x copies/mcg versus 155,240 day x copies/mcg, respectively. The Cmax of responders versus nonresponders with CLL/SLL was 99,559 copies/mcg versus 48,948 copies/mcg, respectively, and the AUC was 793,893 day x copies/mcg versus 408,307 day x copies/mcg, respectively. The median Cmax and AUC were 30,968 copies/mcg and 375,006 day x copies/mcg, respectively, in patients with relapsed or refractory mantle cell lymphoma (MCL). The median time to maximal expansion in peripheral blood of lisocabtagene maraleucel occurred at 10 to 14 days following the lisocabtagene maraleucel infusion in patients with LBCL, CLL/SLL, MZL, and MCL. Additionally, it was present in peripheral blood for a median of approximately 12 to 18 months.

Pharmacodynamics: Peak levels of soluble biomarkers were observed within 14 days of the lisocabtagene maraleucel infusion; these levels typically returned to baseline within 28 days.

Administration
  General Information
  For storage information, see the specific product information within the How Supplied section.
NOTE: Lisocabtagene maraleucel may carry the risk of transmitting infectious diseases to health care professionals handling the product. Employ universal precautions in handling lisocabtagene maraleucel; follow local biosafety guidelines applicable for disposal.
Premedication
Premedicate patients with acetaminophen 650 mg PO and diphenhydramine 25 to 50 mg IV or PO (or another H1-antihistamine) 30 to 60 minutes prior to the infusion.
Avoid prophylactic use of systemic corticosteroids use except in the case of a life-threatening emergency.
  Route Specific Administrations
    Administration Details
      Name
      Intravenous Administration
      Description
      Lisocabtagene maraleucel is for autologous and intravenous use.
Each dose of lisocabtagene maraleucel contains a maximum of 110 x 10^6 CAR-positive viable T-cells consisting of 1:1 CD8 and CD4 components; each component is supplied separately in 1 to 4 single-dose 5 mL vials containing a maximum of 70 x 10^6 CAR-positive viable T cells.
Ensure that a minimum of 2 doses of tocilizumab and emergency equipment are available prior to the infusion and during the recovery period.
Coordinate the timing of the lisocabtagene maraleucel thaw and infusion; confirm the infusion time in advance and adjust the start time for thaw so that the recipient will be ready.
Preparation
Match the patient's identity with the patient identifiers on the cartons, vials, and syringe labels.
Remove the CD8 and CD4 CAR-positive viable T-cells vials from frozen storage; place vials on a protective barrier pad and thaw at room temperature until there is no visible ice in the vials.
Thaw all the vials at the same time; keep the CD8 and CD4 components separate.
Refer to the manufacturer's prescribing information regarding dose preparation; prepare the CD8 component syringe(s) first.
Storage: CD8 and CD4 CAR-positive viable T-cells must be administered within 2 hours of being completely thawed; place labeled CD8 and CD4 syringes on a protective barrier pad inside an insulated room temperature container and transport to the patient bedside.
Intravenous Infusion
Confirm the patient's identity with the patient identifiers on the syringe label.
Administer CD8 component syringe(s) doses first and the CD4 component syringe(s) doses second; do not use a leukocyte-depleting filter.
Infuse the entire volume of each component at approximately 0.5 mL/minute using the closest port or Y-arm; typically, each component will be infused in less than 15 minutes.
Administer syringes consecutively without any time in between infusions.
Flush the infusion tubing prior to and after each CD8 or CD4 component syringe is administered.
