Drug Class: Palovarotene is an oral retinoic acid receptor agonist indicated for reduction in the volume of new heterotopic ossification (HO) in adult and pediatric patients 8 years and older for females and 10 years and older for males with fibrodysplasia ossificans progressiva (FOP).

Routes: oral; Common adult doses: 20 mg/day PO.

Common Side Effects (excluding routine GI upset): alopecia, cheilitis, erythema, pruritus, rash, skin irritation, xerophthalmia, xerosis, arthralgia, back pain, fatigue, headache, musculoskeletal pain, myalgia, peripheral edema, elevated hepatic enzymes, hepatitis, hepatotoxicity, anxiety, depression, suicidal ideation

Indication Specific Dosing
  General Information
  General Dosing Information
Initiate flare-up treatment dosing at the onset of the first symptoms of a flare-up or high-risk traumatic event likely to lead to a flare-up, such as surgery, intramuscular immunization, mandibular blocks for dental procedures, muscle fatigue, blunt muscle trauma from bumps, bruises, falls, or influenza-like viral illnesses. Symptoms of a flare-up can include localized pain, soft tissue swelling or inflammation, redness, warmth, decreased joint range of motion, and stiffness.

Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  Contraindications And Precautions
    Headline
    Drug Interactions
    General Information
    The coadministration of certain medications may lead to harm and require avoidance or therapy modification; review all drug interactions prior to concomitant use of other medications.
    Headline
    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components. Palovarotene is contraindicated in individuals with retinoid hypersensitivity, including hypersensitivity to vitamin A, acitretin, tretinoin, and isotretinoin, due to the possibility of cross-sensitivity.
    Headline
    General Information
    General Information
    Retinoids may cause photosensitivity. Individuals should avoid prolonged sunlight (UV) exposure while receiving palovarotene. Sunscreen and protective clothing should be used when sun exposure cannot be avoided. Skin emollients, sunscreen, lip moisturizers, and artificial tears may be used to minimize the risk of mucocutaneous adverse reactions.
    Headline
    epiphyseal closure
    General Information
    Palovarotene can cause irreversible premature epiphyseal closure and affect growth in pediatric individuals. Premature epiphyseal closure has been observed as early as 6 months after starting therapy with the majority occurring at or after 12 months. Monitor growth closely. Obtain a baseline assessment of skeletal maturity via hand/wrist and knee x-rays, standard growth curves, and pubertal staging prior to starting palovarotene therapy in all growing pediatric individuals. Continue monitoring every 6 to 12 months until pediatric individuals reach their final adult height or skeletal maturity. If there is evidence of premature epiphyseal closure or adverse effects on growth based on clinical or radiologic evaluations, consider the benefits and risks of continuing therapy. Temporarily or permanently discontinue palovarotene until the individual achieves epiphyseal closure and skeletal maturity.
    Headline
    increased risk for osteoporosis
    General Information
    Use caution when treating individuals at increased risk for osteoporosis. Retinoids are associated with bone toxicity, including reductions in bone mass, osteoporosis, and bone fractures. Periodic radiological assessment of the spine is recommended. Palovarotene use in clinical trials resulted in decreased vertebral bone mineral content, decreased vertebral bone density, and an increased risk of radiologically observed vertebral (T4 to L4) fractures in treated adult and pediatric patients compared to untreated patients. Retinoids have also been associated with hyperostosis, bone spurs, and calcification of tendons or ligaments and may occur with palovarotene. These effects generally occur with long-term use, especially at high doses.
    Headline
    psychiatric disorder
    General Information
    Use palovarotene cautiously in individuals with a psychiatric disorder. New or worsening psychiatric events have been reported with palovarotene. These events include depression, anxiety, mood alterations, and suicidal ideation. Individuals with a history of a psychiatric disorder may be more susceptible to these adverse effects. Monitor for symptoms of a psychiatric event during treatment and advise individuals to be alert for signs of depression, worsening of depression, suicidal thoughts, or other mood changes.
    Headline
    activities requiring coordination and concentration, driving or operating machinery
    General Information
    Advise palovarotene recipients against driving or operating machinery or activities requiring coordination and concentration until the effects of palovarotene are known. Decreased night vision and other visual disturbances have been reported during palovarotene therapy; onset of visual changes may be sudden. The reaction may be dose dependent. Night blindness is generally reversible if the treatment is stopped but can also persist in some cases. Advise individuals to seek medical attention if visual impairment occurs.
    Headline
    children, infants
    General Information
    Safety and efficacy of palovarotene in infants and children younger than 8 years in females and younger than 10 years in males with fibrodysplasia ossificans progressiva have not been established and use is not recommended because of the potential for premature epiphyseal closure. Clinical studies show that growing individuals with open epiphyses are at risk for developing premature epiphyseal closure when treated with palovarotene.
    Headline
    renal failure, renal impairment
    General Information
    Use of palovarotene in individuals with severe renal impairment (CrCl 15 to 29 mL/minute) and renal failure is not recommended.
    Headline
    Child-Pugh class B, Child-Pugh class C, hepatic failure
    General Information
    Use of palovarotene in individuals with moderate (Child-Pugh class B) or severe (Child-Pugh class C) hepatic impairment or hepatic failure is not recommended. Palovarotene undergoes extensive hepatic metabolism.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Palovarotene is contraindicated during pregnancy due to the risk of fetal harm. There are no data on the use of palovarotene during human pregnancy. In animal studies, multiple fetal malformations consistent with retinoid-mediated embryopathy (e.g., cleft palate, malformed skull bone, shortening of long bones) were observed after pregnant rats were given oral palovarotene at doses well below the range of clinically relevant exposures during organogenesis. If pregnancy occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and refer the individual to an obstetrician/gynecologist or other specialist experienced in reproductive toxicity for further evaluation and counseling.
    Headline
    blood donor
    General Information
    A blood donor must be informed not to donate blood while receiving palovarotene and for 1 week following completion of therapy, because the donated blood might be given to a pregnant woman whose fetus must not be exposed to palovarotene.
    Headline
    embryo-fetal toxicity, people who may become pregnant, reproductive risk
    General Information
    Counsel people who may become pregnant about the reproductive risk and embryo-fetal toxicity associated with palovarotene. Palovarotene may cause fetal harm. Verify pregnancy status within 1 week prior to treatment initiation, periodically during treatment, and 1 month after the last dose. Advise people who may become pregnant to use effective contraception at least 1 month prior to treatment, during treatment, and for 1 month after the last dose, unless continuous abstinence is chosen. Palovarotene is present in semen (0.7 nanograms/mL) in amounts 100-fold lower than the maternal plasma exposure at the no observed adverse effect level (NOAEL) for fetal toxicity observed in animal studies. Administration of palovarotene to people who can cause pregnancy in others is unlikely to affect fetal development in a pregnant individual exposed to the patient's semen.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Advise patients to avoid breast-feeding while taking palovarotene and for at least 1 month after the final dose. Palovarotene has the potential to cause serious side effects in the breastfed child. There are no data on the presence of palovarotene or its metabolites in human or animal milk, their effects on the breastfed child, or their effects on milk production.

Acitretin: (Major) Avoid concomitant use of palovarotene and other retinoids, such as acitretin, due to the risk for hypervitaminosis A.
Adagrasib: (Major) Avoid concomitant use of palovarotene and adagrasib due to the risk for increased palovarotene exposure which may increase the risk for adverse effects. Palovarotene is a CYP3A substrate and adagrasib is a strong CYP3A inhibitor. Concomitant use with another strong CYP3A inhibitor increased palovarotene overall exposure by 3-fold.

    Mucocutaneous adverse reactions were common during clinical trials of palovarotene. Description
  Text
  Palovarotene is an oral retinoic acid receptor agonist indicated for reduction in the volume of new heterotopic ossification (HO) in adult and pediatric patients 8 years and older for females and 10 years and older for males with fibrodysplasia ossificans progressiva (FOP). In a single arm study, patients receiving palovarotene (n = 97) had less new HO when compared to untreated subjects (n = 101) utilizing the Natural History Study (NHS) as the external control. In patients receiving palovarotene, the mean annualized new HO was 9.4 cm3/year compared to 20.3 cm3/year in untreated subjects in the NHS based on a linear mixed effect model. The treatment effect was about 10.9 cm3/year (95% CI: -21.2 cm3/year, -0.6 cm3/year). Palovarotene is a member of the retinoid class of medications and is therefore contraindicated during pregnancy. Palovarotene can also cause irreversible premature epiphyseal closure and effect growth in pediatric patients; close monitoring of growth is recommended.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Abnormal bone formation in patients with fibrodysplasia ossificans progressiva (FOP) is due to a gain-of-function mutation in the bone morphogenetic protein (BMP) type I receptor ALK2 (ACVR1). BMP signaling plays a key role in skeletogenesis and promotes chondrogenesis. Palovarotene is an oral retinoic acid receptor agonist that is selective at the gamma subtype of the retinoic acid receptor (RAR). By binding to the gamma portion of the RAR, palovarotene decreases the BMP/ALK2 downstream signaling pathway by inhibiting the phosphorylation of SMAD1/5/8. This reduces ALK2/SMAD-dependent chondrogenesis and osteocyte differentiation which results in reduced endochondral bone formation.

  Text
  Palovarotene is administered orally. In vitro, palovarotene is 97.9% to 99.6% protein bound. The mean Vd is 237 L after a single 20 mg dose with food. The mean blood-to-plasma ratio in humans is 0.62. Palovarotene is extensively metabolized by CYP3A4 and to a minor extent by CYP2C8 and CYP2C19 leading to the formation of 4 major metabolites (M2, M3, M4a, and M4b). The metabolites M3 and M4b have 1.7% and 4.2% of the activity of the parent drug, respectively. After administration of radiolabeled palovarotene, 40% of plasma exposure was due to palovarotene and its metabolites. After elimination of the radiolabeled palovarotene in healthy subjects, 97.1% of the dose was recovered in the feces and 3.2% in the urine. The apparent total body clearance of palovarotene is estimated at 19.9 L/hour. The half-life is 8.7 hours after administration of a 20 mg once daily dosage for 14 days with a standard breakfast.
 
Affected cytochrome P450 (CYP450) isoenzymes and drug transporters: CYP3A4, CYP2C8, CYP2C19
Palovarotene is extensively metabolized by CYP3A4 and to a minor extent by CYP2C8 and CYP2C19. When palovarotene is administered with ketoconazole (strong CYP3A4 inhibitor), the Cmax and AUC increases 2- and 3-fold, respectively. When administered with erythromycin (moderate CYP3A4 inhibitor), palovarotene Cmax and AUC increases 1.6- and 2.5-fold, respectively. Coadministration of palovarotene and rifampin (strong CYP3A4 inducer) decreased the Cmax and AUC by 19% and 11%, respectively. Palovarotene does not affect the metabolism of other CYP3A4 substrates.

Dosage Adjustment Guidelines
  Hepatic Impairment
  No dose adjustment is recommended in patients with mild (Child-Pugh A) hepatic impairment. Avoid palovarotene in patients with moderate (Child-Pugh B) or severe (Child-Pugh C) hepatic impairment.
  Renal Impairment
  No dosage adjustment is recommended in patients with mild or moderate renal impairment (CrCl greater than 30 mL/minute). Palovarotene is not recommended in patients with severe renal impairment (CrCl 15 to 29 mL/minute).
