    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components.
    Headline
    neonates, premature neonates
    General Information
    Benzyl alcohol has been associated with "gasping syndrome", a potentially fatal condition characterized by metabolic acidosis and central nervous system, respiratory, circulatory, and renal dysfunction in premature neonates and low-birth weight infants. Infants under 6 months of age exhibit the potential for increased systemic absorption of topical medications due to a high ratio of skin surface area to body mass and an immature skin barrier. Benzyl alcohol lotion should not be used in infants less than 6 months, including neonates. Benzyl alcohol gingival gel should not be used in children and infants less than 2 years of age, including neonates.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Data for topical benzyl alcohol use during human pregnancy are insufficient to establish a drug-associated risk of major birth defects, miscarriage, or adverse outcomes. Topical benzyl alcohol is unlikely to be absorbed through the skin in clinically relevant amounts; therefore, use is not expected to result in significant fetal drug exposure. In animal studies, subcutaneous administration of benzyl alcohol to pregnant rats and rabbits during organogenesis did not result in any fetal malformations but was associated with decreased fetal weight. The consumer label for benzyl alcohol gingival gel does not include specific pregnancy cautions; however, adequate data are not available.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Use topical benzyl alcohol with caution during breast-feeding. There are no data on the presence of benzyl alcohol in human or animal milk, its effects on the breastfed child, or its effects on milk production. When used as prescribed, topical benzyl alcohol is unlikely to be absorbed through the skin in clinically relevant amounts and exposure through breast-feeding is not expected. The consumer label for benzyl alcohol gingival gel does not include specific breastfeeding cautions.

    General Information
    Benzyl alcohol gingival gel may cause oral skin irritation or rash at the application site; discontinue use if these occur. Benzyl alcohol lotion may result in application site skin irritation (2%), including anesthesia (2%), hypoesthesia (2%), and local pain (hyperesthesia) (1%). Description
  Text
  Benzyl alcohol is used topically and is a synthetic alcohol used in a variety of pharmaceutical applications. Benzyl alcohol lotion is a topical pediculicide that was used for the treatment of pediculosis capitis (i.e., head lice). Benzyl alcohol does not have ovicidal activity; therefore, the treatment must be repeated after 7 days. In clinical trials, approximately 75% of subjects were free of live lice 14 days after the second treatment. Benzyl alcohol lotion was FDA approved as a pediculicide in 2009, but marketing of the product was discontinued by the manufacturer in the U.S. A benzyl alcohol gingival gel is marketed without a prescription in the U.S. for the temporary relief of pain due to cold sores, canker sores, and mild gum irritations.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  The mechanism of benzyl alcohol varies with the indication for topical use.
Cold sores, canker sores, and mild gum irritation: Benzyl alcohol has mild and temporary antiseptic and external analgesic/anesthetic effects, providing symptomatic relief. Benzyl alcohol also has a drying effect on the sore; as the gel evaporates, it can act alongside other ingredients in the gel to form a protective, antiseptic film over the lesion. Benzyl alcohol does not kill the herpes simplex virus (HSV) or shorten the duration of the cold sore itself.
Pediculicidal activity: Benzyl alcohol inhibits lice from closing their respiratory spiracles, which results in obstruction of the spiracles by the vehicle and subsequent asphyxiation of the lice. Benzyl alcohol does not have ovicidal activity.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Topical Route
    Description
    Benzyl alcohol lotion: The topical absorption of benzyl alcohol was studied in nineteen pediatric subjects with head lice infestation. The subjects were divided into 2 age groups: 6 months to 3 years, and 4 to 11 years. Quantifiable amounts of benzyl alcohol were found in the plasma of 4 patients after topical application of benzyl alcohol lotion for an exposure period 3 times greater than the normal exposure period. Of 102 analyzed samples, three subjects in the younger age group had plasma concentrations ranging from 1.97 to 2.99 mcg/mL 30 minutes after treatment and one subject in the older age group had a plasma concentration of 1.63 mcg/mL 1 hour after treatment.
 
Benzyl alcohol gingival gel: Pharmacokinetic data are not available.
  Special Populations
    Name
    Pediatrics
    Description
    See topical route pharmacokinetics; use of benzyl alcohol lotion has been primarily studied in the pediatric population aged 6 months and older. Use of benzyl alcohol lotion is not recommended in individuals under 6 months of age because of the potential for increased systemic absorption due to a high ratio of skin surface area to body mass and the potential for an immature skin barrier, which may increase risk for toxicity. Use of benzyl alcohol gingival gel is not recommended for use in pediatric individuals less than 2 years of age for similar reasons.

    Administration Details
      Name
      Cream/Ointment/Lotion Formulations
      Description
      Benzyl alcohol lotion (e.g., Ulesfia)
NOTE: This product is discontinued in the U.S.
Used for the treatment of pediculosis capitis (head lice infestation).
Benzyl alcohol lotion is administered topically to the scalp. It is not for oral, ophthalmic, or intravaginal use.
Cover the face and eyes of the child or patient with a towel and keep their eyes closed tightly.
Apply lotion directly to dry scalp and dry hair. Lotion must cover the entire scalp and all scalp hair. An adult should apply the lotion to a child or other patient needing assistance.
Massage the lotion into the hair and scalp.
When the head is completely covered with enough lotion, dripping will happen. Protect the eyes and skin from these drips with a towel.
Be sure to apply lotion to the area behind the ears.
Be sure to apply lotion to the back of the neck.
If not enough lotion is used, some lice may escape treatment. It is important to use the full amount of lotion prescribed.
Allow lotion to stay on the hair for 10 minutes. Use a timer or clock and start timing after completely covering the hair and scalp with lotion.
After 10 minutes, completely rinse lotion from hair and scalp with water.
Wash your hands after application.
Hair may be shampooed right after the treatment.
One week (7 days) after the first application, repeat the steps above to help get rid of lice that hatched from eggs.
A fine-tooth comb or special nit comb may be used to remove dead lice and nits after both treatments.
Use lotion in the context of an overall lice management program. Wash (in hot water) or dry-clean all recently worn clothing, hats, used bedding, and towels. Additionally, wash personal care items such as combs, brushes and hair clips in hot water.
      Name
      Other Topical Formulations
      Description
      Benzyl Alcohol 10% Gingival Gel (e.g., Zilactin Early Relief Cold Sore Gel)
Dry the affected area before application.
Apply topically to the affected area (cold sore, ulceration or gum irritation) using cotton swab or clean finger. If the treated individual is a child, an adult should supervise the application and use.
Allow to dry for 30 to 60 seconds. This will create a protective film.
Do not swallow the gel. Avoid contact with the eyes.
The gel is flammable: Do not use near fire or flame.
Once applied, do not peel off protective film. Attempting to peel off film may result in skin irritation or tenderness. To remove film, first apply another coat of Zilactin to film, and immediately wipe the area with a moist gauze pad.
Stop use of benzyl alcohol gingival gel and consult a health care provider if sore mouth symptoms do not improve in 7 days, or if the condition worsens or if symptoms clear up but occur again within a few days. Stop use if swelling, rash or fever develops, or if irritation, pain or redness persists or worsens.
