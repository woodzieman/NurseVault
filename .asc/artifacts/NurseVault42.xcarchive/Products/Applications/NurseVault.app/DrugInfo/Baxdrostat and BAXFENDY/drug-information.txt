Drug Class: Baxdrostat is an aldosterone synthase inhibitor indicated for the treatment of hypertension in combination with other antihypertensive medications, to lower blood pressure in adults who are not adequately controlled on other medications.

Routes: oral; Common adult doses: 2 mg/day PO.

In an Overdose: No overdose/antidote information is listed in this file.

Key Nursing Considerations: Contraindications/precautions: This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components. Assess serum potassium concentration and correct serum potassium abnormalities (e.g., hyperkalemia) prior to initiation of therapy, as baxdrostat can cause hyperkalemia.

    Hypersensitivity
    General Information

    Headline
    geriatric, hyperkalemia, increased risk for hyperkalemia
    General Information
    Assess serum potassium concentration and correct serum potassium abnormalities (e.g., hyperkalemia) prior to initiation of therapy, as baxdrostat can cause hyperkalemia. Individuals at increased risk for hyperkalemia include those with chronic kidney disease or diabetes mellitus; geriatric adults (65 years and older); or individuals taking concomitant medications that increase potassium concentrations, including potassium supplements or potassium-containing salt substitutes. Periodically monitor serum potassium concentrations during treatment. More frequent monitoring of potassium is recommended in individuals at increased risk for hyperkalemia and those restarting baxdrostat after experiencing hyperkalemia.
    Headline
    hyponatremia
    General Information
    Assess serum sodium concentration and correct serum sodium abnormalities (e.g., hyponatremia) prior to initiation of therapy, as baxdrostat can cause hyponatremia. Individuals at risk for hyponatremia include those with low serum sodium concentrations prior to baxdrostat treatment and those receiving concomitant medications that may also cause hyponatremia. Periodically monitor serum sodium concentrations during treatment. More frequent monitoring of sodium is recommended in individuals at increased risk for hyponatremia and those restarting baxdrostat after experiencing hyponatremia.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    There are no available data on the use of baxdrostat during pregnancy to evaluate for a drug-associated risk of major birth defects, miscarriage or adverse maternal or fetal outcome. Hypertension during pregnancy poses a risk to both the pregnant individual and the fetus. Baxdrostat had no effects on embryo-fetal development in rats or rabbits dosed through organogenesis at exposures up to 54-fold (rat) and 29-fold (rabbit) the human dose of 2 mg. However, baxdrostat demonstrated greatly reduced potency in rats compared to humans, which limits the interpretability of the rodent data. Higher baxdrostat doses were associated with increased incidence of adverse skeletal malformations in rats and decreased fetal weights in rabbits. No adverse effects were noted in a pre- and postnatal study in rats dosed from gestation day 6 through lactation day 20 at exposures up to 65-fold the human exposure at 2 mg. Report any pregnancies while treated with baxdrostat to the manufacturer at 1-800-236-9933.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Use baxdrostat with caution during breast-feeding. There are no data on the presence of baxdrostat in human milk, the effects on breast-feeding or the effects on milk production. Baxdrostat transfers to milk in lactating rats. Consider the developmental and health benefits of breast-feeding along with the mother's clinical need for baxdrostat and any potential adverse effects on the breastfed infant from baxdrostat or from the underlying medical condition.

    General Information
    A decrease in mean estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) was reported in baxdrostat-treated subjects in clinical trials. At week 12, the mean reduction in eGFR appeared to plateau and the mean placebo-corrected decrease in eGFR was 8 mL/minute/1.73 m2 and 7.1 mL/minute/1.73 m2 in the baxdrostat 2 mg and baxdrostat 1 mg groups, respectively.     Headline
    hyperkalemia, hyponatremia
    General Information
    Hyperkalemia and hyponatremia were reported during baxdrostat clinical trials. Hyperkalemia was reported in 10.2% of subjects receiving baxdrostat 2 mg and 6.6% receiving baxdrostat 1 mg, compared to 2.5% receiving placebo. Serum potassium increases to more than 5.5 mEq/L were reported in 12.2% and 6.3% of subjects receiving baxdrostat 2 mg and 1 mg, respectively, versus 0.9% of placebo-treated subjects. Hyperkalemia led to permanent baxdrostat discontinuation in 1.8% of subjects receiving baxdrostat 2 mg, 0.6% of subjects receiving baxdrostat 1 mg, and no subjects receiving placebo. Among individuals aged 65 to 74 years, hyperkalemia was reported in 14% and 10% of subjects receiving baxdrostat 2 mg and 1 mg doses, respectively, compared with 3% of subjects receiving placebo. In individuals 75 years and older, hyperkalemia occurred in 21% of subjects receiving baxdrostat 2 mg and in no subjects receiving baxdrostat 1 mg or placebo. Hyponatremia was reported in 3.2% of subjects receiving baxdrostat 2 mg and 2.1% receiving baxdrostat 1 mg, compared to 0.9% receiving placebo. Serum sodium decreases to less than 130 mEq/L were reported in 3.7% and 3.3% of subjects receiving baxdrostat 2 mg and 1 mg, respectively, versus 0.9% of subjects receiving placebo. Assess serum potassium and sodium prior to initiation of baxdrostat and monitor periodically during treatment. If hyperkalemia or clinically significant hyponatremia occurs, treat the hyperkalemia or hyponatremia and consider interrupting or discontinuing baxdrostat. Consider more frequent monitoring of serum potassium and sodium in individuals who restart baxdrostat after experiencing hyperkalemia or hyponatremia. Permanently discontinue baxdrostat if clinically significant hyperkalemia or hyponatremia occurs.

Description
  Text
  Baxdrostat is an aldosterone synthase inhibitor indicated for the treatment of hypertension in combination with other antihypertensive medications, to lower blood pressure in adults who are not adequately controlled on other medications. By inhibiting aldosterone synthase, baxdrostat lowers plasma aldosterone concentrations, which reduces sodium and water retention and, thereby, lowers blood pressure. In a 12-week, phase 3 clinical trial of baxdrostat add-on therapy in individuals with uncontrolled and resistant hypertension, the placebo-corrected difference in systolic blood pressure reduction from baseline was 8.7 mmHg with baxdrostat 1 mg and 9.8 mmHg with baxdrostat 2 mg (p less than 0.0001). The placebo-corrected difference in diastolic blood pressure reduction was 3.3 mmHg with baxdrostat 1 mg (p = 0.0008) and 3.9 mmHg with baxdrostat 2 mg (p less than 0.0001). Baxdrostat may cause hyperkalemia and hyponatremia. Assess serum potassium and sodium prior to initiation of baxdrostat and periodically monitor during treatment. Baxdrostat was FDA-approved in May 2026.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Baxdrostat is an aldosterone synthase inhibitor. Aldosterone promotes vasoconstriction and the retention of sodium and water by the kidneys, which increases blood volume and raises blood pressure. Aldosterone can also cause vascular dysfunction, inflammation, fibrosis, and hypertension-related organ damage. Baxdrostat exerts selective and potent inhibition of aldosterone synthase (CYP11B2), a cytochrome P450 enzyme that catalyzes the final 3 steps of aldosterone synthesis, which decreases serum aldosterone concentrations and, ultimately, reduces blood pressure. Due to its higher potency and selectivity for aldosterone synthase compared to the closely related enzyme 11 beta-hydroxylase (CYP11B1; the final enzyme in cortisol synthesis), baxdrostat significantly decreases aldosterone concentrations without affecting cortisol concentrations. Baxdrostat does not inhibit synthesis of sex hormones such as testosterone and estrone.
 
Pharmacodynamics
After the initial dose in healthy subjects, a decrease in plasma aldosterone concentrations of approximately 70% and 83% occurred when baxdrostat 1.5 mg to 2.5 mg was given along with a normal salt diet and baxdrostat 2.5 mg to 5 mg was given with a low salt diet (0.75 to 2.5 times the maximum recommended dose), respectively. Compared to baseline, reductions in aldosterone concentrations on day 10 remained up to approximately 68% lower in the normal salt group and 73% lower in the low salt group. In individuals with hypertension who received repeated doses of baxdrostat 0.5 mg (0.5 times the lowest recommended dose), 1 mg, and 2 mg, there was a sustained dose-dependent decrease in 24-hour urinary excretion of aldosterone, decrease in plasma aldosterone concentrations, and an increase in plasma renin activity. Decreased plasma aldosterone concentrations were observed up to the last time point of assessment at 32 weeks. Baxdrostat did not have an observed effect on adrenocorticotropic hormone (ACTH)-stimulated cortisol secretion in individuals with hypertension who received baxdrostat 2 mg for 8 weeks or in healthy subjects treated with baxdrostat doses up to 360 mg (180 times the maximum recommended dose) administered as a single dose or with multiple ascending doses up to 10 mg (5 times the maximum recommended dose) at steady state.

  Text
  Baxdrostat is administered orally. The volume of distribution at steady state is 205 L. The plasma protein binding of baxdrostat was approximately 74% and the blood to plasma ratio was 0.95 in vitro. Baxdrostat is primarily metabolized by CYP3A4 to an intermediate ketone metabolite, which is further metabolized by carbonyl reductase 1 (CBR1) to an alcohol metabolite (M2). Following a single, radiolabeled dose of baxdrostat, the parent compound accounted for 71% of total radioactivity exposure. Approximately 69% of a radiolabeled dose of baxdrostat was excreted in the urine (17% unchanged) and 15% in feces (6% unchanged). Baxdrostat has a mean effective half-life of approximately 26 hours (range: 23 to 32 hours), a mean total clearance of 2.8 L/hour, and renal clearance of 0.46 L/hour.
 
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes and drug transporters: CYP3A4, P-gp, BCRP.
Baxdrostat is CYP3A substrate. Strong or moderate CY3A inducers may decrease baxdrostat plasma concentrations. Additionally, baxdrostat is a P-gp and BCRP substrate; however, this is not expected to result in clinically significant drug interactions.
