Drug Class: Sodium glycerophosphate is an organic phosphate salt that is hydrolyzed by plasma alkaline phosphatase to phosphate and glycerol, providing a source of phosphorus.

Routes: injection (IV/IM/SC); Common adult doses: 40 mmol/day IV.

Common Side Effects (excluding routine GI upset): hypernatremia, hyperphosphatemia, hypocalcemia, arrhythmia exacerbation, av block, bradycardia, cardiac arrest, chest pain (unspecified), hypotension, confusion, lethargy, muscle paralysis, paresthesias, weakness

In an Overdose: No overdose/antidote information is listed in this file.

Key Nursing Considerations: Contraindications/precautions: This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components. Sodium glycerophosphate is contraindicated in individuals with hyperphosphatemia, hypercalcemia, or hypocalcemia.

    Headline
    diabetic ketoacidosis, hypercalcemia, hyperphosphatemia, hypocalcemia
    General Information
    Sodium glycerophosphate is contraindicated in individuals with hyperphosphatemia, hypercalcemia, or hypocalcemia. Check serum phosphorus and calcium concentrations prior to administration and normalize calcium concentration before administration. Sodium glycerophosphate may cause hypomagnesemia when administered to patients with hypercalcemia or diabetic ketoacidosis.
    Headline
    edema, heart failure, hepatic cirrhosis, hypernatremia, renal failure, renal impairment, sodium retention
    General Information
    Sodium glycerophosphate is contraindicated in individuals with severe renal impairment or renal failure (eGFR less than 30 mL/minute/1.73 m2) and in individuals receiving dialysis. Sodium glycerophosphate is also contraindicated in individuals with hypernatremia. Closely monitor individuals with congestive heart failure, renal impairment, hepatic cirrhosis, and other conditions associated with edema or sodium retention. The sodium content of sodium glycerophosphate is 2 mEq/mL (46 mg/mL) and should be factored into the total sodium dose calculation for the prepared PN admixture. The risk of adverse reactions may be greater in individuals with renal impairment. In individuals with an eGFR of 30 to 59 mL/minute/1.73 m2, start at the low end of the dosage range.
    Headline
    neonates
    General Information
    Injectable sodium glycerophosphate contains aluminum. Thus, aluminum may reach toxic concentrations with prolonged administration in patients with renal impairment. Premature neonates are at particular risk for aluminum toxicity because of immature renal function, and they require large amounts of calcium and phosphate solutions, which contain aluminum. Research indicates that patients with renal impairment, including neonates, who receive parenteral aluminum at rates greater than 4 to 5 mcg/kg/day may accumulate aluminum at concentrations associated with central nervous system and bone toxicity. Tissue loading may occur at lower administration rates.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Sodium glycerophosphate is compatible with breast-feeding. Administration of the recommended dose of sodium glycerophosphate is not expected to cause harm to a breastfed child. There are no data available on the effects of sodium glycerophosphate on milk production.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Administration of the recommended dose of sodium glycerophosphate during pregnancy is not expected to cause major birth defects, miscarriage, or other adverse outcomes. Phosphorus is an essential mineral element, and parenteral supplementation with sodium glycerophosphate as part of parenteral nutrition should be considered during pregnancy if requirements cannot be met by oral or enteral intake. Animal reproduction studies have not been conducted with intravenous sodium glycerophosphate.

    hypernatremia, hyperphosphatemia, hypocalcemia
    General Information
    Hyperphosphatemia can occur with intravenous administration of phosphate-containing solutions, especially in individuals with renal impairment. Hyperphosphatemia can cause formation of insoluble calcium-phosphorus products, with consequent hypocalcemia, neurological irritability with tetany, nephrocalcinosis with acute kidney injury, and, more rarely, cardiac irritability with arrhythmias. Hypernatremia has been reported with intravenous sodium glycerophosphate.

Description
  Text
  Sodium glycerophosphate is an organic phosphate salt that is hydrolyzed by plasma alkaline phosphatase to phosphate and glycerol, providing a source of phosphorus. It is an intravenous phosphorus replacement product indicated as a source of phosphorus as part of parenteral nutrition (PN) when oral or enteral nutrition is not possible, insufficient, or contraindicated. Sodium glycerophosphate must be diluted and administered as part of a PN admixture; it is not intended for direct intravenous infusion. Sodium glycerophosphate was FDA-approved in 2026.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Sodium glycerophosphate is an organic phosphate salt that functions as a source of phosphorus. Phosphorus, in the form of organic and inorganic phosphate, has a variety of biochemical functions, including critical roles in nucleic acid structure, energy storage and transfer, cell signaling, cell membrane composition and structure, acid-base balance, mineral homeostasis, and bone mineralization.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Intravenous Route
    Description
    Following a single intravenous infusion of sodium glycerophosphate or inorganic phosphate at the same dose (80 mmol phosphorus) in healthy adults, serum phosphate concentrations were slightly lower with sodium glycerophosphate; the difference was not clinically meaningful for parenteral nutrition use.
  Text
  Sodium glycerophosphate is administered intravenously. Approximately 85% of serum phosphate is free and ultra-filterable, and 15% is protein-bound. Following intravenous administration, glycerophosphate is hydrolyzed in plasma to phosphate and glycerol by serum alkaline phosphatase. Phosphate not taken up by tissues is excreted almost entirely in the urine. More than 80% of filtered phosphorus is actively reabsorbed by the renal tubules.
 
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes and drug transporters: none

  Route Specific Administrations
    Administration Details
      Name
      Intravenous Administration
      Description
      Preparation
Must be diluted and used as an admixture in parenteral nutrition solutions; do not administer by direct IV infusion.
Follow proper admixing sequence to minimize compatibility issues. Add sodium glycerophosphate after amino acids and dextrose and before other electrolytes, including calcium; add lipids last, if included.
Consider calcium and phosphate ratios when preparing PN admixtures. Excessive amounts of calcium and phosphate, particularly mineral salts, may result in calcium-phosphate precipitates.
Inspect the final PN admixture for precipitates after mixing or addition of additives and again before administration; discard if precipitates are present. If lipids are included, inspect for emulsion separation, which may appear as yellowish streaking or accumulation of yellowish droplets; discard if the emulsion has separated.
Account for the sodium content (2 mEq/mL [46 mg/mL]) when calculating the total sodium content of the PN admixture.
Storage:
Single dose vial: For single use only; discard any remaining contents.
Pharmacy Bulk Package vial: Penetrate the vial closure only once with a suitable sterile transfer device or dispensing set that allows for measured dispensing of the contents. Use within 4 hours at room temperature after the container has been penetrated; discard any remaining contents.
Final PN Admixture: Use PN solution promptly. If not used promptly, store for up to 4 hours at room temperature or up to 24 hours under refrigeration. Protect from light. After removal from refrigeration, complete the infusion within 24 hours; discard any remaining admixture.
 
Intravenous Infusion
Administer the PN admixture via a central or peripheral vein; select the route based on the osmolarity of the final infusate. Infuse PN admixtures with an osmolarity of 900 mOsmol/L or greater through a central catheter.
