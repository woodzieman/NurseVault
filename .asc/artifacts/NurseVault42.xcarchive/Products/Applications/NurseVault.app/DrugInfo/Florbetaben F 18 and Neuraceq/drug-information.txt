Description
  Text
  Florbetaben F 18 is an intravenous, radioactive diagnostic agent indicated for positron emission tomography (PET) imaging of the brain to estimate amyloid beta neuritic plaque density in adults with cognitive impairment for evaluation of Alzheimer disease and other causes of cognitive decline or for selection of individuals who are indicated for amyloid beta-directed therapy. A positive PET scan establishes the presence of moderate to frequent amyloid beta neuritic plaques. Neuropathological examination has shown that moderate to frequent amyloid beta neuritic plaques are present in individuals with Alzheimer disease but may also be present in individuals with other types of neurologic conditions as well as older people with normal cognition. A negative PET scan indicates sparse to no amyloid beta neuritic plaques. In individuals being evaluated for Alzheimer disease and other causes of cognitive decline who have not been treated with amyloid beta-directed therapy, a negative scan is inconsistent with a neuropathological diagnosis of Alzheimer disease at the time of image acquisition and reduces the likelihood that an individual's cognitive impairment is due to Alzheimer disease. A negative scan result does not preclude the accumulation of amyloid beta in the brain in the future. Quantification of amyloid beta neuritic plaque concentrations can be used in conjunction with visual assessment and performed with FDA-authorized software indicated for quantification of brain amyloid beta PET scans. Florbetaben F 18 contributes to an individual's overall long-term cumulative radiation exposure; long-term cumulative radiation exposure is associated with an increased risk of cancer. Florbetaben F 18 was FDA-approved in March 2014.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Florbetaben F 18 is a radiopharmaceutical used to estimate amyloid beta plaque density in the gray matter of the brain. After intravenous administration, florbetaben F 18 crosses the blood-brain barrier and displays differential retention in brain regions that contain amyloid beta deposits. Differences in signal intensity between brain regions showing specific and non-specific florbetaben F 18 uptake form the basis for the image interpretation method. Once bound, the F 18 isotope produces a positron signal that can be detected by a positron emission tomography (PET) scan. A positive scan indicates moderate (6 to 19) to frequent (20 or more) neuritic plaque counts and a negative scan indicates none to sparse (1 to 5) neuritic plaque counts. These scans, in conjunction with other diagnostic tests, are used to evaluate for Alzheimer disease and other causes of cognitive decline or for selection of individuals who are indicated for amyloid beta-directed therapy.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics

    Description
    The amount of radiation absorbed following exposure to the recommended florbetaben F 18 dose is 5.8 mSv; use of a computerized tomography (CT) scan increases exposure to 8 mSv. The products radioactive half-live is 109.8 minutes.
  Text
  Florbetaben F 18 is administered intravenously. It is 98.5% bound to plasma protein and distributes rapidly after administration. Within 10 minutes of injection, approximately 6% of the radioactivity is distributed to the brain and, unless bound to beta-amyloid plaques, is subsequently removed by cerebral perfusion. Florbetaben F 18 is quickly eliminated from plasma via the hepatobiliary route, with only 25% of the drug remaining after 20 minutes and 10% remaining by 50 minutes; mean biological half-life is approximately 1 hour. The majority of the radioactivity that remains in circulation during the 45—130 minute imaging window are polar F 18 metabolites. These metabolites are formed following metabolism of florbetaben by CYP2J2 and CYP4F2. At 12 hour post-dose, 30% of the injected radioactivity is excreted in the urine, primarily as the polar metabolite; only trace amounts of the parent drug can be detected in urine.
 
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes and drug transporters: CYP2J2, CYP4F2
In vitro studies have found florbetaben to be a substrate for CYP2J2 and CYP4F2.
