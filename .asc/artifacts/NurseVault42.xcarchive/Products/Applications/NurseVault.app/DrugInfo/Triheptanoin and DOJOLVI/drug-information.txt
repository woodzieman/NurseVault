    Hypersensitivity

    Headline
    pancreatic insufficiency
    General Information
    Avoid administration of triheptanoin in individuals with pancreatic insufficiency. Pancreatic enzymes hydrolyze triheptanoin and release heptanoate as medium-chain fatty acids in the small intestine. Low or absent pancreatic enzymes may result in reduced absorption of heptanoate, subsequently leading to insufficient supplementation of medium-chain fatty acids.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    There are no data available on triheptanoin use during human pregnancy to evaluate for a drug-associated risk of major birth defects, miscarriage, or other adverse outcomes. In animal reproduction studies conducted in pregnant rats and rabbits during the period of organogenesis, the primary toxicological effect (reduced body weight gain) was considered to be specific to decreased food consumption related to taste aversion in animals and, therefore, is not relevant to clinical use in the intended populations. There is a pregnancy safety study for triheptanoin. Report triheptanoin exposure during pregnancy to Ultragenyx Pharmaceutical Inc. at 1-888-756-8657.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Use triheptanoin with caution during breast-feeding. There are no data on the presence of triheptanoin in human milk, its effects on the breast-fed child, or its effects on milk production. Medium-chain triglycerides and other fatty acids are normal components of human milk. The composition of human milk varies within feedings, over stages of lactation, and between individuals and populations due to factors including genetics, environment, and diet. Consider the benefits of breast-feeding, the patient's clinical need for treatment, and any potential adverse effects on the breast-fed child from the medication or from the patient's underlying medical condition.

Interactions
Orlistat: (Major) Avoid coadministration of triheptanoin with orlistat. Coadministration with a pancreatic lipase inhibitor, such as orlistat, may reduce exposure to the triheptanoin metabolite, heptanoate, and reduce the efficacy of triheptanoin.

  Adverse Reactions
    Headline
    abdominal pain, diarrhea, nausea, vomiting
    General Information
    The most common adverse reactions reported with triheptanoin in pooled safety data from 2 clinical studies (n = 79; age 4 months to 63 years) were gastrointestinal (GI)-related, and included abdominal pain (abdominal discomfort, abdominal pain, abdominal distension, abdominal pain upper, GI pain; 60%), diarrhea (44%), vomiting (44%), and nausea (14%). The median time to onset of the first occurrence of a GI adverse reaction was 7.3 weeks. GI adverse reactions led to dose reductions in 35% and 12% of patients in Study 1 and Study 2, respectively. GI effects from high MCT formulas may be minimized by a gradual dose titration and slow administration.

Description
  Text
  Triheptanoin is a medium-chain triglyceride (MCT) approved as a source of calories and fatty acids for the treatment of molecularly confirmed long-chain fatty acid oxidation disorders (LC-FAODs) in adult and pediatric patients. Triheptanoin is the first FDA-approved therapy for LC-FAOD, which are a group of autosomal recessive conditions that prevent the body from breaking down long-chain fatty acids into energy. This leads to the accumulation of high concentrations of potentially toxic fatty acid intermediates. Patients with LC-FAOD present with acute crises of energy metabolism and severe energy deficiency, which can lead to potentially severe complications, such as rhabdomyolysis, hypoglycemia, hypotonia, muscle weakness, and cardiomyopathy. Triheptanoin is intended to provide people with LC-FAOD with medium-length, odd-chain fatty acids that are metabolized to bypass faulty pathways and provide an alternative energy source. In clinical studies, treatment with triheptanoin improved exercise tolerance and health-related quality of life and reduced both the frequency and duration of major clinical events (i.e., hospitalizations, hypoglycemia, cardiomyopathy, rhabdomyolysis). Gastrointestinal reactions are commonly reported with MCT formulas; GI effects may be minimized by a gradual dose titration and slow administration.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Triheptanoin is a medium-chain triglyceride consisting of 3 odd-chain 7-carbon length fatty acids (heptanoate) that provide a source of calories and fatty acids to bypass the long-chain fatty acid oxidation disorder (LC-FAOD) enzyme deficiencies for energy production and replacement. Unlike MCT oil, triheptanoin is an anaplerotic compound that restores TCA cycle substrate balance in patients with LC-FAOD due to its medium chain length, allowing it to by-pass defects in the long-chain fatty acid oxidation pathway, and generation of propionyl-CoA. Propionyl-CoA is an anaplerotic molecule that is metabolized to succinate, providing both even and odd carbon substrates for the TCA cycle. Triheptanoin can also be gluconeogenic via the TCA cycle, which can also contribute to energy metabolism. Thus, triheptanoin is hypothesized to improve energy generation in LC-FAOD patients who manifest symptoms of energy deficit, such as chronic muscle weakness, cardiomyopathy, and rhabdomyolysis.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics

    Oral Route
    Description
    After single-dose administration of 0.3 g/kg or 0.4 g/kg of triheptanoin with food, the mean Cmax and AUC were 178.9 micromol/L and 336.5 micromol x hour/L, respectively, for the 0.3 g/kg dose and 259.1 micromol/L and 569.1 micromol x hour/L, respectively, for the 0.4 g/kg dose. The median time to peak concentration was 0.5 hours (range: 0.4 to 1 hour) and 0.8 hours (range: 0.4 to 6.4 hours) for the 0.3 g/kg and 0.4 g/kg doses, respectively. After multiple-dose administration of 0.3 g/kg administered 4 times daily for 2 days, the mean Cmax and AUC were 319.9 micromol/L and 789.8 micromol x hour/L, respectively. The median time to peak concentration was 1.2 hours (range: 0 to 2.4 hours). Of note, after oral administration of tripheptanoin, more than 1 peak concentration is observed.
  Text
  Triheptanoin is administered orally. After oral administration, triheptanoin is extensively hydrolyzed to heptanoate and glycerol by pancreatic lipases in the intestines. The exposure of tripheptanoin in human plasma is minimal. Pharmacokinetics of heptanoate exhibit high inter-patient variability. Heptanoate exposure increases greater than dose-proportional in the dose range between tripheptanoin 0.3 g/kg and 0.4 g/kg. The plasma protein binding of heptanoate is approximately 80% and is independent of total concentration. Heptanoate, formed by hydrolysis of triheptanoin, can be metabolized to beta-hydroxypentanoate (BHP) and beta-hydroxybutyrate (BHB) in the liver. After administration of a single dose of either 0.3 g/kg or 0.4 g/kg of triheptanoin to healthy subjects, the mean apparent clearance of heptanoate was 6.05 and 4.31 L/hour/kg, respectively. After administration of single or multiple repeat doses of triheptanoin to healthy subjects, triheptanoin and its metabolites were minimally excreted in the urine. The elimination half-life of heptanoate could not be determined due to multiple peak concentrations of heptanoate observed.

Feeding Tube Preparation and Administration
Administer triheptanoin as an oral or enteral bolus medication. Do not add triheptanoin to the feeding bag, as the feeding bag may degrade over time.
Triheptanoin can be administered via oral or enteral feeding tubes manufactured of silicone or polyurethane. Do not use feeding tubes manufactured of polyvinyl chloride (PVC). Feeding device performance and functionality can degrade over time, depending on usage and environmental conditions. Regularly monitor the feeding tube to ensure proper functioning and integrity.
Use an oral syringe or measuring cup made of compatible materials, as listed above, to withdraw the prescribed volume of triheptanoin from the bottle.
Add the prescribed amount of triheptanoin to a clean bowl, cup, or container, made of compatible materials as listed above, which contains an amount of medical food or formula that takes into consideration the age, size, and fluid needs of the patient in order to ensure administration of the full dose.
Mix triheptanoin thoroughly into the medical food or formula prior to administering via feeding tube, y-connector, or feeding tube extension set made of silicone or polyurethane.
Draw up the entire amount of the triheptanoin mixture into a slip tip syringe. Remove the residual air from the syringe and connect the syringe directly into the feeding tube feeding port. Push the syringe contents into the feeding tube feeding port using steady pressure until empty.
Flush the feeding tube with 5 to 30 mL of water. Modify the flush volume based on specific patient needs and in cases of fluid restriction.
Discard any unused triheptanoin mixture in the trash. Do not pour down the sink. Do not save for later use.
