Drug Class: IncobotulinumtoxinA is a parenteral acetylcholine release inhibitor and neuromuscular blocking agent indicated for the treatment or improvement of individuals with blepharospasm, cervical dystonia, upper limb spasticity, moderate to severe facial wrinkles, or chronic sialorrhea.

Routes: injection (IV/IM/SC); Common adult doses: 50 units/eye IM for blepharospasm; 120 units/treatment session IM for cervical dystonia; 20 units/treatment session IM for glabellar lines, 20 units/treatment session for horizontal forehead lines, 24 units/treatment session for lateral canthal lines; 400...

Common Side Effects (excluding routine GI upset): anaphylactoid reactions, edema, erythema, pruritus, rash, serum sickness, urticaria, blepharedema, blepharospasm, blurred vision, corneal erosion, diplopia, ectropion, paresis, photophobia, ptosis, visual impairment, xerophthalmia, distant spread of toxin effects, dysarthria, dysphonia, dyspnea, ecchymosis, hematoma, injection site reaction, infection, influenza, pharyngitis, arthralgia, asthenia

  Contraindications And Precautions
    Headline
    Drug Interactions
    General Information
    The coadministration of certain medications may lead to harm and require avoidance or therapy modification; review all drug interactions prior to concomitant use of other medications.
    Headline
    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components. Do not use this medication in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to any botulinum neurotoxin, sucrose, or human albumin. Human albumin and sucrose are excipients in this product.
    Headline
    Treatment-Related Restrictions of Use
    General Information
    IncobotulinumtoxinA is contraindicated in the presence of infection at the proposed injection site(s). Use in individuals with an infection at the injection site could lead to severe local or disseminated infection.
    Headline
    General Information
    General Information
    Exercise caution when using incobotulinumtoxinA in individuals with excessive weakness or atrophy of the targeted muscles.
In the treatment of glabellar lines, use caution when incobotulinumtoxinA is used in patients who have marked facial asymmetry, with surgical alterations to the facial anatomy, pre-existing eyelid or eyebrow ptosis, when excessive weakness or atrophy is present in the target muscles, excessive dermatochalasis, deep dermal scarring, thick sebaceous skin (e.g., the inability to substantially lessen glabellar lines even by physically spreading them apart).
    Headline
    distant spread of toxin effects
    General Information
    Postmarketing safety data suggest injection of botulinum toxins may, in some cases, result in the distant spread of toxin effects resulting in systemic symptoms (e.g., asthenia, generalized muscle weakness, diplopia, ptosis, trouble swallowing, dysphonia, dysarthria, urinary incontinence, breathing difficulties). Advise individuals or caregivers to seek immediate medical care if swallowing, speech, or respiratory disorders occur. These symptoms have been reported hours to weeks after injection. There have been reports of death related to the spread of toxin effects. Risk is probably greatest in children treated for spasticity, but symptoms can also occur in adults treated for other indications, especially in those who have underlying conditions predisposing them to these effects. In unapproved uses and in approved indications, cases of spread of effect have been reported at doses comparable to those used to treat cervical dystonia and spasticity and at lower doses.
    Headline
    asthma, dysphagia, emphysema, respiratory insufficiency
    General Information
    Treatment with incobotulinumtoxinA and other botulinum toxins can result in swallowing or breathing difficulties. Individuals with pre-existing dysphagia or respiratory insufficiency, such as asthma or emphysema, may be more susceptible to these complications. In most cases, this is a consequence of weakening of muscles in the area of injection. When distant effects occur, additional respiratory muscles may be involved. Treatment of cervical dystonia with botulinum toxins may weaken neck muscles that serve as accessory muscles of ventilation. This may result in critical loss of breathing capacity in individuals with respiratory disorders who have become dependent upon these accessory muscles. Serious breathing difficulties, including respiratory failure, have been reported in individuals with cervical dystonia treated with botulinum toxins. Individuals with smaller neck muscle mass and patients who require bilateral injections into the sternocleidomastoid muscles have been reported to be at greater risk of dysphagia. Deaths as a complication of severe dysphagia have been reported. Aspiration may result from severe dysphagia and is a particular risk when treating individuals in whom swallowing or respiratory function is already compromised. Dysphagia may persist for several months. Limit the dose injected into the sternocleidomastoid muscle to decrease the occurrence of dysphagia.
    Headline
    neuromuscular disease
    General Information
    Use incobotulinumtoxinA with caution in individuals with peripheral motor neuropathic diseases, amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS), or neuromuscular junction disorders (myasthenia gravis or Lambert-Eaton syndrome). Individuals with known or unrecognized neuromuscular disease or neuromuscular junction disorders may be at increased risk of clinically significant systemic effects including generalized weakness, diplopia, ptosis, dysphonia, dysarthria, severe dysphagia, and respiratory compromise from typical doses of incobotulinumtoxinA. Exercise caution when using incobotulinumtoxinA in individuals with excessive weakness or atrophy of the targeted muscles.
    Headline
    closed-angle glaucoma
    General Information
    Use incobotulinumtoxinA with caution, in patients with or at risk for closed-angle glaucoma, as the toxin has anticholinergic effects.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    There are no data on the presence of incobotulinumtoxinA in human milk, its effects on the breast-fed child, or its effects on milk production. Consider the developmental and health benefits of breast-feeding along with the person's clinical need for incobotulinumtoxinA and any potential adverse effects on the breast-fed child from incobotulinumtoxinA or the underlying maternal condition. With appropriate intramuscular administration, systemic maternal botulinum toxin exposure is expected to be low, and subsequent excretion in human milk is unlikely. One infant was safely breastfed during maternal botulism; no botulinum toxin was detectable in human milk or in the infant. A breast milk sample obtained 4 hours after administration of 2 vials of trivalent botulism antitoxin to the lactating person did not contain any detectable botulinum toxin or botulism organisms. The infant continued to breast-feed throughout the maternal illness without any adverse effects.
    Headline
    activities requiring coordination and concentration, driving or operating machinery
    General Information
    Caution all individuals to avoid driving or operating machinery or performing activities requiring coordination and concentration if they experience a loss of strength, muscle weakness, blurred vision, or drooping eyelids as a result of incobotulinumtoxinA therapy.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Use incobotulinumtoxinA during pregnancy only if the potential benefit justifies the potential risk to the fetus. There are no adequate data on the developmental risk associated with the use of incobotulinumtoxinA in pregnant individuals. IncobotulinumtoxinA was embryotoxic in rats and increased abortions in rabbits when given intramuscularly at doses higher than the maximum recommended human dose (MRHD) for cervical dystonia (120 units), on a body weight basis. The no-effect doses for embryotoxicity in rats and increased abortion in rabbits were 6 units/kg (3 times the MRHD for cervical dystonia on a body weight basis) and 2.5 units/kg (similar to the MRHD for cervical dystonia on a body weight basis), respectively.

Description
  Text
  IncobotulinumtoxinA is a parenteral acetylcholine release inhibitor and neuromuscular blocking agent indicated for the treatment or improvement of individuals with blepharospasm, cervical dystonia, upper limb spasticity, moderate to severe facial wrinkles, or chronic sialorrhea. Unlike other marketed botulinum toxins, the final incobotulinumtoxinA formulation is a purified product that does not contain accessory protein complexes, which is expected to decrease the immunogenic potential. In noninferiority trials, no statistically significant differences in time to onset, paralytic effect, or response rates were noted between incobotulinumtoxinA and onabotulinumtoxinA treated muscles. Like other botulinum toxins, botulinum toxin effects may be observed beyond the site of local injection, in some cases. During 2 randomized, double-blind, multi-center, placebo-controlled clinical trials, treatment success at Day 30 for incobotulinumtoxinA was 49% to 53% for glabellar lines, 58% to 67% for horizontal forehead lines, and 33% to 53% for lateral canthal lines (LCL) (or 24% with LCL-only dosing) compared to placebo (0%). Swallowing and breathing difficulties can be life-threatening; deaths related to the spread of toxins have been reported. IncobotulinumtoxinA was FDA approved in July 2010.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  IncobotulinumtoxinA blocks cholinergic transmission at the neuromuscular and salivary neuroglandular junctions by inhibiting the release of acetylcholine from peripheral cholinergic nerve endings. The neurotoxin first binds to the cholinergic nerve terminals, enters the nerve terminals, then the light-chain portion of the neurotoxin molecule translocates into the cytosol of the nerve terminal where it cleaves the SNAP25 protein, which is integral to the successful docking and release of acetylcholine from vesicles within nerve endings. In both muscles and glands, impulse transmission is re-established when new nerve endings are formed.

    Description
    The median first onset of effect occurs within 7 days after injection. Typical duration of each treatment is up to 12 to 16 weeks; however, this is highly individualized. In a comparator study of incobotulinumtoxinA and onabotulinumtoxinA, the paralytic effect was measured by changes in compound muscle action potential (CMAP) after the IM injection of 4 units of either drug into the extensor digitorum brevis muscle of the foot in 14 healthy male adults.
