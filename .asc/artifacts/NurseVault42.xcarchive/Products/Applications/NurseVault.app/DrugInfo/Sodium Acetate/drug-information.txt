    Headline
    edema, fluid retention, heart failure, hypernatremia
    General Information
    Sodium acetate is contraindicated in individuals with hypernatremia or fluid retention. Use sodium acetate with caution in those with heart failure and other clinical states in which there exists edema with sodium retention. Administer parenteral fluids, especially those containing sodium ions, with caution to patients receiving corticosteroid therapy or corticotropin due to the risk of sodium and fluid retention. To avoid sodium overload and water retention, infuse sodium acetate slowly.
    Headline
    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components.
    Headline
    geriatric, neonates, oliguria, premature neonates, renal failure, renal impairment
    General Information
    Use sodium acetate with caution in individuals with severe renal impairment or renal failure, including those with anuria or oliguria. In individuals with diminished renal function, sodium acetate administration may cause fluid and/or solute overload and sodium retention. To avoid sodium overload and water retention, infuse sodium acetate slowly. Sodium acetate products may contain aluminum, and aluminum may reach toxic concentrations with prolonged sodium acetate administration in those with renal impairment. Individuals with renal impairment, including neonates, who receive parenteral aluminum at rates more than 4 to 5 mcg/kg/day may accumulate aluminum at concentrations associated with central nervous system and bone toxicity. Tissue loading may occur at lower administration rates. Premature neonates are at particular risk for aluminum toxicity because of immature renal function, and they require large amounts of calcium and phosphate solutions, which contain aluminum. Because geriatric individuals are more likely to have decreased renal function, take care in sodium acetate dose selection; it may be useful to monitor renal function.
    Headline
    Child-Pugh class C, hepatic failure, metabolic alkalosis, respiratory alkalosis
    General Information
    Use sodium acetate with caution in individuals with metabolic alkalosis or respiratory alkalosis, and conditions in which there is increased acetate concentrations or impaired acetate utilization, such as those with severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh class C) or hepatic failure. Additionally, use sodium acetate with caution in individuals with conditions which there exists edema with sodium retention, such as cirrhosis. To avoid sodium overload and water retention, infuse sodium acetate slowly.

Description
  Text
  Sodium acetate is a parenteral electrolyte indicated for the treatment or prevention of hyponatremia in persons with restricted or no oral intake. It is indicated as a source of sodium for addition to large volume intravenous fluids. It is also useful as an additive for preparing specific intravenous fluid formulas when needs cannot be met by standard electrolyte or nutrient solutions. Acetate is an alternate source of bicarbonate by metabolic conversion in the liver. Sodium acetate can cause fluid retention and/or solute overload, resulting in the dilution of other serum electrolyte concentrations, overhydration, and congested states or pulmonary edema.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Sodium is the principal cation of extracellular fluid. It comprises more than 90% of total cations at its normal plasma concentration of approximately 140 mEq/L. The sodium ion exerts a primary role in controlling total body water and its distribution. Acetate, a source of hydrogen ion acceptors, is an alternate source of bicarbonate by metabolic conversion in the liver.

Pharmacokinetics
  Text
  Sodium acetate is administered intravenously. Sodium is the principal cation of extracellular fluid. Acetate is an alternate source of bicarbonate by metabolic conversion in the liver, which has been shown to proceed readily, even in the presence of severe liver disease.

Intravenous infusion
Central line administration is preferred for hypertonic solutions (more than 154 mEq/L); however peripheral administration is acceptable in critically ill patients who require immediate therapy. Central access should be obtained for continued use. Monitor peripheral administration of hypertonic solutions carefully for potential extravasation and local tissue damage.
To avoid sodium and water retention, infuse sodium acetate solution slowly. The dose and rate of administration are dependent on individual needs.
Storage: Store admixtures under refrigeration and use within 24 hours after compounding.
