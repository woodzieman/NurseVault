Routes: topical; Common adult doses: 4 applications/day topically.

In an Overdose: No overdose/antidote information is listed in this file.

    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Pramoxine; zinc acetate is compatible with breast-feeding. There are no data on the presence of pramoxine; zinc acetate in human milk, the effects on the breastfed child, or the effects on milk production. No adverse reactions have been reported in breast-feeding individuals using pramoxine. Direct ingestion of pramoxine has caused nausea and vomiting, but no serious toxicity. If pramoxine; zinc acetate is applied to the chest area, advise lactating individuals to wipe it off thoroughly prior to breast-feeding.

Clinicians should closely monitor patients for the development of methemoglobinemia when a combination local anesthetic is used during a procedure. Description
  Text
  Pramoxine; zinc acetate is a topical combination of an analgesic and skin protectant indicated for the temporary relief of pain or itching associated with rashes due to poison ivy, oak, or sumac, insect stings, or minor skin irritation or cuts. It also dries the oozing and weeping of poison ivy, oak, or sumac. Pramoxine is not structurally related to ester or amide local anesthetics; its unique chemical structure is likely to minimize the risk of cross-sensitivity reactions in patients allergic to other anesthetics.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Pramoxine is a topical anesthetic and acts by stabilizing the neuronal membrane of nerve endings with which it comes into contact. Pramoxine is not structurally related to ester or amide local anesthetics; its unique chemical structure is likely to minimize the risk of cross-sensitivity reactions in patients allergic to other anesthetics. Topically administered zinc acts as an astringent and weak antiseptic. These actions are thought to be mediated by precipitation of proteins by zinc ions.
