Indication Specific Dosing
  General Information
  Avoid abrupt discontinuation or sudden large reductions in dosage of oral, intravenous, or subcutaneous treprostinil, as this may result in worsening pulmonary arterial hypertension.
Avoid treatment interruptions. All individuals receiving treprostinil should have immediate access to a back-up infusion pump and infusion set or inhalation device.
For individuals taking oral treprostinil who must undergo a planned, short-term interruption of therapy, consider a temporary subcutaneous or intravenous infusion of treprostinil.

          Children† and Adolescents†
          Dosage Description
          NOTE: Both transition methods described here are significantly more rapid than FDA-approved for adult patients. Based on the half-life of treprostinil, rapid titrations may not allow time for steady state concentrations to be reached. Use caution and carefully monitor patient's clinical status during entire transition period. Very limited data are available. One retrospective analysis describes 2 methods of transitioning pediatric patients receiving stable doses of IV epoprostenol to IV treprostinil. During the slow transition method (n = 7), IV treprostinil was initiated at the original epoprostenol dose, and the epoprostenol dose was decreased by half. Every 1 to 2 hours, the epoprostenol dose was decreased by 10 ng/kg/minute and the treprostinil dose was increased by 5 to 10 ng/kg/minute. The target treprostinil dose was 1.25 to 1.75 times the epoprostenol dose that the patient was receiving prior to the transition. During the rapid transition method (n = 6), the epoprostenol infusion was discontinued and the treprostinil infusion was initiated at the previous epoprostenol dose. The treprostinil dose was increased by 5 to 10 ng/kg/minute every 20 to 60 minutes, depending on clinical symptoms and adverse effects. The target treprostinil dose was 1.25 to 1.75 times the epoprostenol dose that the patient was receiving prior to the transition. At the time of transition, the mean epoprostenol dose was 36 +/- 13 ng/kg/minute IV, and 12 to 24 hours after transition, the mean treprostinil dose was 51.4 +/- 20 ng/kg/minute. Although both methods were reported to be well tolerated, the authors warn that the slower transition may be safer for patients with severe disease. A stable dose is usually between 50 to 80 ng/kg/minute; maximum dose is unknown.

Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  General Information
  Treprostinil is contraindicated in any patient with a known hypersensitivity to the drug or to structurally related compounds.
 
Treprostinil injection is indicated for continuous subcutaneous or continuous IV administration only. Because of the risks associated with chronic, indwelling central venous catheters that are required for continuous IV infusion, intravenous administration should be reserved for patients who are intolerant of the SC route (e.g., severe pain, severe adverse reactions). The use of chronic indwelling central venous catheters is associated with blood stream infections and sepsis, which may be fatal (see Adverse reactions). The patient's ability to accept and care for a self-inserted SC catheter or a surgically placed central venous catheter and to use an infusion pump should be carefully assessed prior to initiation of treprostinil. Additionally, aseptic technique must be used in the preparation and administration of treprostinil in an effort to reduce the risk of infection. Treprostinil therapy may be required for months or years in patients with PAH. Patients should be informed that subsequent disease management may require the initiation of an alternative intravenous therapy, such as epoprostenol.
 
Treprostinil is a potent pulmonary and systemic vasodilator. Initiation of treprostinil injection should be performed in an adequate setting for monitoring physiologic response and emergency care. Only clinicians experienced in the diagnosis, treatment and follow-up of PAH should use treprostinil. Abrupt discontinuation of treprostinil therapy, including sudden reductions in dosage and interruptions in drug delivery, may result in worsening symptoms of primary arterial hypertension, and should be avoided. Symptoms can include dyspnea, dizziness and asthenia. If temporary cessation of the infusion is necessary for less than a few hours, the infusion can be restarted at the previously used dose and rate; however, if the infusion is stopped for longer than a few hours, re-titration of the dose may be necessary. NOTE: Treprostinil should be used by clinicians experienced in the diagnosis and treatment of pulmonary arterial hypertension. Initiation of treprostinil must be performed in a setting with adequate personnel and equipment for physiological monitoring and emergency care.
 
The safe and effective use of treprostinil oral inhalation have not been established in patients with significant underlying pulmonary disease (e.g., asthma or chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD)). Monitor patients with acute pulmonary infections to detect any worsening of pulmonary disease and loss of drug effect.
 
Since treprostinil can cause hypotension, it should be used cautiously with other hypotensive drugs to avoid additive effects.
 
Unless contraindicated, anticoagulant therapy should be administered to patients receiving treprostinil to reduce the risk of thromboembolic disease, including pulmonary thromboembolism or systemic embolism.
 
Dosage adjustments of treprostinil are required in patients with mild to moderate hepatic insufficiency, which may include hepatic disease or hepatitis. Studies have not been performed in patients with severe hepatic impairment.
 
No studies of treprostinil have been performed in patients with renal disease, renal impairment, or renal failure. Considering that roughly 80% of the dose is excreted in the urine, with most as metabolites of unknown biological activity, caution and close monitoring for treprostinil toxicity is warranted.
 
Elderly patients often have decreased renal, hepatic or cardiac function. In general, dose selection of treprostinil for elderly patients should be cautious, given the greater frequency of decreased organ function or concomitant disease and drug therapy in geriatric patients versus younger adults. Cautious titration and observation for toxicity is recommended as specific dosage adjustment recommendations based on age alone are not available. Sufficient numbers of patients >= 65 years of age were not included in clinical trials to determine if they respond differently than younger patients.
 
Treprostinil is classified in FDA pregnancy risk category B. Animal data in rats have not revealed evidence of harm to the fetus when doses of treprostinil up to 16 times the average rate in humans were given in human during organogenesis and late gestational development. In pregnant rabbits, skeletal rib malformations were noted in conjunction with maternal toxicity at rates 5 times the average human dose in humans. In rats, continuous SC infusion of treprostinil from implantation through the end of lactation, at rates up to 450 ng/kg/min, did not affect the growth or development of offspring. Treprostinil should only be used in pregnant women if clearly needed. Animal reproduction studies are not always predictive of human outcome.
 
According to the manufacturer, it is not known if treprostinil is excreted in human breast milk. Consider the benefits of breast-feeding, the risk of potential infant drug exposure, and the risk of an untreated or inadequately treated condition. If a breast-feeding infant experiences an adverse effect related to a maternally ingested drug, healthcare providers are encouraged to report the adverse effect to the FDA.
 
Safety and efficacy of treprostinil injection in adolescents and children <= 16 years of age have not been established. Clinical trials did not contain adequate numbers of patients <= 16 years of age to determine if there is a difference in response to treprostinil injection. Safety and efficacy of treprostinil oral inhalation in adolescents and children < 18 years of age have not been established. Clinical trials did not contain adequate numbers of patients < 18 years of age to determine if there is a difference in response to treprostinil oral inhalation. In general, dose selection should be cautious.

    General Information

Consider a temporary infusion of subcutaneous or intravenous treprostinil for individuals on oral therapy who must undergo a planned short-term interruption of therapy.
Chronic intravenous administration of treprostinil delivered via external infusion pump with an indwelling central venous catheter is associated with a risk of blood stream infections (BSIs) and sepsis, which may be fatal. Administration of IV treprostinil with a high pH glycine diluent such as Sterile Diluent for Flolan or Sterile Diluent for Epoprostenol Sodium has been associated with a lower incidence of BSI compared to neutral diluents (Sterile Water for Injection, 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection) when used along with catheter care guidelines.
    Headline
    hypotension
    General Information
    Use of treprostinil, a pulmonary and systemic vasodilator, in individuals with low systemic arterial pressure (hypotension) may result in symptomatic hypotension.
    Headline
    diverticulitis
    General Information
    Use oral treprostinil tablets with caution in patients with diverticulitis. The tablet shell does not dissolve and can lodge in the diverticulum.
    Headline
    Child-Pugh class A, Child-Pugh class B, Child-Pugh class C, hepatic failure
    General Information
    Due to an increase in systemic exposure of treprostinil, individuals with hepatic impairment may require dosage adjustments or alternate therapy. Oral treprostinil is contraindicated in individuals with severe hepatic impairment or hepatic failure (Child-Pugh Class C). Avoid use of oral treprostinil in individuals with moderate hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh Class B) and adjust dose in individuals with mild hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh Class A). Upward titration of treprostinil injection and treprostinil inhalation should be slow and cautious. Studies with treprostinil for injection or inhalation have not been performed in individuals with severe hepatic impairment.
    Headline
    asthma, bronchospasm, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease
    General Information
    Administration of treprostinil via inhalation may cause acute bronchospasm, especially in individuals with reactive airway disease. Individuals with asthma, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), or other bronchospasm/bronchial hyperreactivity related conditions are at an increased risk of bronchospasm during treprostinil administration via inhalation. Prior to the start of and during treprostinil inhalation treatment, individuals should be optimally treated for their reactive airway disease.
    Headline
    geriatric
    General Information
    The incidence of absolute and relative adverse reactions with oral treprostinil was slightly higher with geriatric adults compared to younger adults. In general, dose selection and titration in geriatric adults should be cautious, given the greater frequency of decreased organ function, such as hepatic, renal, or cardiac dysfunction, or concomitant disease and drug therapy in geriatric adults versus younger adults.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Limited case reports of treprostinil use during human pregnancy are insufficient to inform a drug-associated risk of adverse developmental outcomes. Animal studies show no adverse reproductive or developmental effects in pregnant rats given high subcutaneous infusion doses (about 117 times the starting human infusion rate and about 16 times the average rate achieved in clinical trials). In rabbits, external fetal and soft tissue malformations and skeletal malformations were observed with treprostinil given subcutaneously at 7 and 5 times the average rate used in clinical trials. Nebulized and inhaled forms of treprostinil at doses much higher than human exposure showed no adverse effects on reproduction or development in animals after a single dose.   Adverse Reactions
  General Information
  The most common adverse reactions seen with subcutaneous (SC) treprostinil include infusion-related or injection site reactions such as erythema, hematoma, induration, pruritus, rash (unspecified), and injection site pain. The associated injection site reaction and pain may be severe and require discontinuation of treatment. In clinical trials, 38% of treprostinil recipients reported a severe infusion site reaction vs. 1% in the placebo group; these reactions lead to drug discontinuation in 3% of treprostinil-treated patients. Severe pain was reported in 39% in the treprostinil group vs. 2% in the placebo group. Up to 32% of patients experiencing severe pain in the active group required narcotics, and 7% of the initial 39% of patients with severe pain discontinued treatment. In total, roughly 85% of patients reported some type of infusion site pain or reaction, whether it was severe or not (vs. 27% of placebo). Local pain improved after several weeks in some patients. In clinical studies, pain has been treated with topical cooling, topical corticosteroids, acetaminophen, NSAIDs, short-term narcotics or short courses of oral prednisolone. Prednisolone (2 mg/kg/d) has been noted as being very effective for treprostinil injection site pain. Pruritus occurred at an incidence of 8% vs. 6% and rash occurred in 14% vs. 11% of patients receiving treprostinil vs. placebo, respectively. In addition, maculopapular rash and cellulitis have been infrequently reported in post-marketing experience.
 
Central nervous system adverse reactions reported with treprostinil injection (n = 236) during chronic subcutaneous administration at an incidence of at least 3% and higher than placebo (n = 233) included dizziness (9 vs. 8%) and headache (27 vs. 23%). Headache was also reported more frequently than placebo during the clinical trial of treprostinil oral inhalation solution (41% vs. 23%). Syncope was reported in 6% of treprostinil oral inhalation-treated patients compared to = 4% of treprostinil oral inhalation-treated patients and more frequently than placebo and included: cough (54% vs. 29%) and throat irritation or pharyngeal pain (29% vs. 17%). Other adverse reactions involving the respiratory tract included: epistaxis and wheezing. Pneumonia was reported in 8 subjects during the open-label phase. There were also 3 serious, including 1 fatal, episodes of hemoptysis during the open-label phase.

Description
  Text
  Treprostinil is a tricyclic benzidine analog of epoprostenol (prostacyclin) approved for patients with pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH; WHO Group I). Studies that established the effectiveness of treprostinil in PAH treatment included predominately individuals with New York Heart Association (NYHA) Class II to IV symptoms, Class III symptoms, and Class II to III symptoms for treprostinil injection, nebulizer solution and inhalation powder, and oral tablets, respectively. Treprostinil oral inhalation is also approved for use in patients with pulmonary hypertension associated interstitial lung disease (WHO Group 3) to improve exercise ability. Treprostinil causes direct and potent vasodilation of pulmonary and systemic arterial beds, inhibition of platelet aggregation, and inhibition of smooth muscle cell proliferation resulting in reduced right and left ventricular afterload, increased cardiac output, and increased stroke volume. Clinical improvements are seen in dyspnea, fatigue, and exercise capacity. Individuals with asthma or chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) may be at an increased risk of bronchospasm with treprostinil therapy. Intravenous administration through a central catheter may expose patients to complications such as catheter-related embolism or thrombosis, sepsis, and delivery system malfunction. Treprostinil was FDA-approved in 2002.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  The major actions of treprostinil, a stable prostacyclin analog, are direct and potent vasodilation of pulmonary and systemic arterial beds, inhibition of platelet aggregation, and inhibition of smooth muscle cell proliferation. The vasodilatory effect results in reduced right and left ventricular afterload, increased cardiac output, and increased stroke volume. Clinically, dyspnea, fatigue and exercise capacities are improved.
Data also indicate that inflammatory mechanisms may be important in the pathogenesis of primary pulmonary hypertension. Treprostinil has been shown to dose-dependently inhibit inflammatory cytokine (TNF-alpha, IL-1, IL-6, and GM-CSF) secretion and gene expression by human alveolar macrophages. Cytokine antagonism by blocking NF-kB nuclear translocation may also contribute to the efficacy of treprostinil therapy in pulmonary hypertension. Prostaglandins have been shown to inhibit this proliferation, theoretically by inducing elevations in cAMP. Treprostinil produced a significantly larger and more sustained increase in cAMP compared with beraprost, cicaprost or iloprost.
 
Pharmacodynamics:
Among healthy volunteers, clinical and supratherapeutic doses of inhaled treprostinil prolonged the QTc interval by approximately 10 msec. The QTc effect rapidly dissipated as the concentration of treprostinil decreased. This effect on the QT interval may be an artifact of rapidly changing heart rate. The injectable and oral formulations of treprostinil have not been evaluated in a QT study.

    Description
    The absolute bioavailability of treprostinil tablets is approximately 17%, with maximum plasma concentrations occurring between 4 and 6 hours following oral administration and time to steady-state concentration is 1 to 2 days.   Special Populations
    Name
    Hepatic Impairment
    Description
    In a small population of patients with portopulmonary hypertension and mild or moderate hepatic insufficiency who were treated with subcutaneous treprostinil, both Cmax and AUC were significantly increased compared to healthy subjects; clearance was reduced by up to 80%. Among a small population of subjects with mild, moderate, or severe hepatic impairment, administration of treprostinil oral tablets also resulted in significant elevations in Cmax (1.6-fold, 4-fold, and 4.8-fold) and AUC (2.1-fold, 4.8-fold, and 7.6-fold) compared to healthy subjects. Dose reductions are required in patients with hepatic insufficiency treated with oral tablets or injectable treprostinil.
    Name
    Renal Impairment
    Description
    No studies of injectable or inhalation treprostinil have been performed in patients with renal insufficiency; no specific advice regarding dosage in these patients is available. Although only 4% of the dose is excreted unchanged in the urine, all five of the identified metabolites are excreted substantially in the urine. Oral treprostinil has been evaluated in patients requiring dialysis. Administration of a single 1 mg dose before and after dialysis did not result in significant changes in treprostinil exposure compared to healthy subjects.

Storage: Store the inhaler in a clean, dry place with the mouthpiece cover in place and at a room temperature of 20 to 25 degrees C (68 to 77 degrees F). Unused capsules should remain in the blister card to protect the capsules from moisture and light. Only remove capsules from the blister card when ready to deliver a dose. Capsules must be inhaled within 5 minutes of opening the blister card.
Dry Powder Capsule Inhaler setup:
Wash and dry hands.
Do not use the inhaler or capsules if they are damaged. Check the expiration date on the strip. Do not use if past the expiration date.
Gather the dry powder capsule inhaler and blister cards.
Open the inhaler by pulling the cover straight off the inhaler.
Rotate the mouthpiece in the direction of the arrow (counter-clockwise) to open the inhaler and expose the capsule chamber.
If the mouthpiece separates from the base of the inhaler, gently reattach the 2 pieces.
Separate 1 blister strip from the card by tearing along the pre-cut lines. Do not remove the capsule from the blister strip until ready to deliver a dose.
Remove the capsule from the blister strip by peeling the foil away. Do not push the capsule through the foil. Do not remove the desiccant tab.
The capsule must be used within 5 minutes of opening the blister card.
Hold the inhaler in an upright position and place 1 capsule in the capsule chamber at the base of the inhaler. Do not place the capsule in the mouthpiece.
Put one finger on top of the capsule to hold it down while firmly pressing both puncture buttons with the other hand, then release. Only press puncture buttons one time.
Hold the base of the inhaler and rotate the mouthpiece to close.
Administration:
Hold the inhaler upright and away from the mouth. Do not hold the inhaler by the puncture buttons.
Breathe out fully and away from the inhaler. Do not exhale into the mouthpiece.
Close lips around the mouthpiece, slightly tilt the head back, and take a deep, comfortable breath until the lungs feel full.
Inhaling properly will cause a whirring noise as the capsule spins and releases medicine.
Take the inhaler out of the mouth and hold breath for 5 seconds or as long as comfortably able. Then breathe out normally.
To ensure the capsule is completely emptied of medicine, repeat the exhalation and inhalation process. Always inhale each capsule 2 times to get the full dose.
If coughing occurs when inhaling, repeat the process.
To remove the empty capsule, rotate the mouthpiece in the direction of the arrow (counter-clockwise) to expose the capsule chamber. Remove the empty capsule and dispose of it in household trash.
If the prescribed dose requires more than 1 capsule, repeat the above steps with each capsule. The capsules may be used in any order, regardless of capsule strength. For doses of 132.5 mcg or more, consult the dosing chart in the 'Instructions for Use' to determine the capsule combinations needed.

Dosage Adjustment Guidelines
  Hepatic Impairment
  Oral treprostinil:
Mild hepatic insufficiency (Child-Pugh Class A): Reduce the initial dose to 0.125 mg PO twice daily; increase by 0.125 mg increments twice daily, but not more frequently than every 3 to 4 days.
Moderate hepatic insufficiency (Child-Pugh Class B): Avoid use.
Severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh Class C): Do not use; contraindicated.
 
Injectable treprostinil:
Mlld to moderate hepatic insufficiency: Reduce the initial dose of treprostinil injection to 0.625 ng/kg/minute (using ideal body weight) subcutaneously or IV, and cautiously titrate upward. Follow the usual titration guidelines for indication.
Severe hepatic impairment: Treprostinil for injection or inhalation has not been studied in severe hepatic impairment.
 
Inhalational treprostinil:
Specific guidelines for dosage adjustments of treprostinil for oral inhalation in hepatic impairment are not available; the plasma clearance of treprostinil is reduced in patients with hepatic insufficiency and these patients may therefore be at increased risk of dose-dependent adverse reactions. Cautiously titrate up the oral inhalation dose.
  Renal Impairment
  No dosage adjustments are required.
 
Hemodialysis
Treprostinil is not removed by dialysis.
