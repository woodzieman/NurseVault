Drug Class: Tislelizumab is a programmed death receptor-1 (PD-1) blocking monoclonal antibody that works by preventing the interaction between PD-1 and the PD-L1 and PD-L2 ligands.

Routes: injection (IV/IM/SC); Common adult doses: 150 mg IV every 2 weeks.

Common Side Effects (excluding routine GI upset): cough, dyspnea, interstitial lung disease, pneumonitis, colitis, elevated hepatic enzymes, hepatitis, hyperbilirubinemia, adrenocortical insufficiency, hypophysitis, hypopituitarism, acneiform rash, erythema, maculopapular rash, pruritus, rash, stevens-johnson syndrome, toxic epidermal necrolysis, asthenia, fatigue, malaise, arthralgia, back pain, bone pain, musculoskeletal pain, myalgia, rhabdomyolysis, bleeding, epistaxis, gi bleeding

In an Overdose: No overdose/antidote information is listed in this file.

Median OS was numerically improved in the HER2 IHC2+/ISH+ population (21.5 months vs. 18.7 months); median PFS was 8.6 months versus 10 months, respectively.

    Headline
    Drug Interactions
    General Information
    The coadministration of certain medications may lead to harm and require avoidance or therapy modification; review all drug interactions prior to concomitant use of other medications.
    Headline
    autoimmune disease, inflammatory bowel disease
    General Information
    Use tislelizumab with caution in patients with a history of autoimmune disease, including inflammatory bowel disease. Immune-mediated reactions, which may be severe or fatal, can occur in patients treated with drugs that bind to either the programmed death-receptor 1 (PD-1) or the PD-ligand 1 (PD-L1), thus removing inhibition of the immune response, such as tislelizumab. These immune-mediated reactions can occur in any organ system or tissue and can also affect more than one body system simultaneously. Although usually occurring during treatment with PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors, immune-mediated reactions can occur at any time after starting therapy including after discontinuation of therapy. Because early identification and management is critical to safe usage of PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors, closely monitor patients for signs and symptoms that may be clinical manifestations of underlying immune-mediated reactions. Initiate an appropriate workup to exclude alternative etiologies including infection in cases of suspected immune-mediated reaction. Begin medical management promptly, including specialty consultation as appropriate. An interruption or discontinuation of tislelizumab therapy may be necessary, depending on the severity of the reaction. In general, if an interruption or discontinuation of therapy is necessary, administer systemic corticosteroids (1 to 2 mg/kg per day of prednisone or equivalent); upon improvement to grade 1 or less, begin a steroid taper over at least 1 month. Consider additional systemic immunosuppression in patients whose symptoms are not controlled with corticosteroid therapy.
    Headline
    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components.
    Headline
    pulmonary disease, thoracic radiation therapy
    General Information
    Use tislelizumab with caution in patients who have a history of pre-existing pulmonary disease; immune-mediated pneumonitis/interstitial lung disease has been reported in patients treated with tislelizumab. The incidence is higher in patients who have received prior thoracic radiation therapy, except in patients with classical Hodgkin lymphoma, where pneumonitis rates were similar regardless of prior thoracic radiation. Monitor patients for signs and symptoms of pneumonitis (e.g., new or worsening cough, chest pain, shortness of breath). If confirmed, tislelizumab therapy may need to be interrupted or discontinued depending on severity; treatment with systemic corticosteroids may be necessary.
    Headline
    allogeneic stem cell transplant
    General Information
    Perform a risk/benefit analysis prior to starting treatment with a PD-1/PD-L1 blocking antibody, such as tislelizumab, in patients who may receive or who have a history of undergoing an allogeneic stem cell transplant (SCT). Fatal and other serious complications including hyperacute, acute, and chronic graft-versus-host-disease, sinusoidal obstruction syndrome (SOS) previously termed veno-occlusive disease (VOD) after reduced intensity conditioning, and a steroid-requiring febrile syndrome (without an identified infectious cause) have been reported in patients who receive an allogeneic SCT before or after being treated with a PD-1/PD-L1 blocking antibody. Monitor patients closely for evidence of transplant-related complications and intervene promptly. Complications may occur despite intervening therapy between PD-1/PD-L1 blockade and allogeneic SCT.
    Headline
    corneal transplant, organ transplant
    General Information
    Use tislelizumab with caution in patients who have previously had a solid organ transplant or other transplant (including corneal transplant). Immune-mediated organ transplant rejection has been reported with PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitor therapy.
    Headline
    neurological disease
    General Information
    Use tislelizumab with caution in patients who have a history of neurological disease such as myasthenia gravis or Guillain-Barre syndrome. Immune-mediated neurotoxicity has been reported with tislelizumab or other PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors. Tislelizumab may need to be interrupted or discontinued depending on the severity of the neurotoxicity; treatment with systemic corticosteroids may also be necessary.
    Headline
    obesity
    General Information
    Monitor patients with obesity as well as those who are considered overweight for an increased risk of infusion-related reactions when treated with tislelizumab. Patients with a high BMI have a greater risk even if they have a low metabolic risk (i.e., fewer than 2 of diabetes, dyslipidemia, hypertension).
    Headline
    cardiac disease
    General Information
    Use tislelizumab with caution in patients with cardiovascular risk factors such as hypertension, heart failure, cardiac disease, and myocardial infarction as they are at increased risk of cardiac immune-related adverse reactions such as myocarditis, vasculitis, and pericarditis.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Tislelizumab may cause fetal harm if used during pregnancy, based on its mechanism of action. There is no available data on tislelizumab use during human pregnancy. Tislelizumab is a humanized IgG4 monoclonal antibody; human IgG4 immunoglobulins are known to cross the placenta. Fetal exposure to tislelizumab may increase the risk of developing immune-mediated disorders or altering the normal immune response. The PD-1 pathway is associated with decidual CD8-positive T-cell function and the PD1/PD-L1 interaction is important in maintaining fetal tolerance during pregnancy. Inhibiting PD-1/PDL-1 binding and signaling led to an increase in fetal loss in murine models. Potential risks for blocking the PD-1/PDL-1 pathway include an increased incidence of abortion or stillbirths.
    Headline
    people who may become pregnant, reproductive risk
    General Information
    Counsel people who may become pregnant about the reproductive risk associated with tislelizumab. Tislelizumab may cause fetal harm. Verify pregnancy status prior to treatment initiation. Advise people who may become pregnant to use effective contraception while taking tislelizumab and for 4 months after the last dose.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Advise patients to avoid breast-feeding while taking tislelizumab and for 4 months after the last dose due to the potential to cause serious adverse reactions in breast-fed children. There are no data on the presence of tislelizumab in human milk, its effects on the breast-fed child, or its effects on milk production. Consider the benefits of breast-feeding, the patient's clinical need for treatment, and any potential adverse effects on the breast-fed child from the medication or from the patient's underlying medical condition.

Interactions
Cholera Vaccine: (Moderate) Patients receiving immunosuppressant medications may have a diminished response to the live cholera vaccine. When feasible, administer indicated vaccines prior to initiating immunosuppressant medications. Counsel patients receiving immunosuppressant medications about the possibility of a diminished vaccine response and to continue to follow precautions to avoid exposure to cholera bacteria after receiving the vaccine.
Dengue Tetravalent Vaccine, Live: (Contraindicated) Avoid administration of the live dengue virus vaccine with immunosuppressive drug therapy and prior to immune recovery following treatment with immunosuppressive drug therapy. When feasible, administer indicated live virus vaccines at least 4 weeks before planned immunosuppression or wait until at least 3 months after discontinuation. The time to restoration of immune competence may be longer in some patients. Patients with altered immunocompetence may be at increased risk for severe adverse reactions due to uninhibited growth of the attenuated live virus. Additionally, vaccines may be less effective if administered during a period of altered immunocompetence.
SARS-CoV-2 (COVID-19) vaccines: (Moderate) Patients receiving immunosuppressant medications may have a diminished response to the SARS-CoV-2 virus vaccine. When feasible, administer indicated vaccines prior to initiating immunosuppressant medications. Counsel patients receiving immunosuppressant medications about the possibility of a diminished vaccine response and to continue to follow precautions to avoid exposure to SARS-CoV-2 virus after receiving the vaccine.

When feasible, administer indicated vaccines prior to initiating immunosuppressant medications.   Adverse Reactions
    Headline
    cough, dyspnea, interstitial lung disease, pneumonitis
    General Information
    Immune-mediated interstitial lung disease (ILD)/pneumonitis occurred in 4.7% (grade 3 or 4, 1.7%) of patients in a pooled safety population who were treated with tislelizumab across clinical trials; 0.1% of cases were fatal. In patients treated with other PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors, the incidence of pneumonitis is higher in patients who have received prior thoracic radiation therapy. Treatment with systemic corticosteroids was required in 71.7% of patients with pneumonitis; resolution occurred in 48.7% of these patients. Upon resolution of pneumonitis, 26 patients reinitiated tislelizumab therapy; 5 of these patients (19%) experienced a recurrence of pneumonitis. Cough occurred in 22% (grade 3 or 4, 0.4%) of patients with advanced esophageal cancer treated with tislelizumab. Dyspnea occurred in less than 10% of patients with advanced gastric or gastroesophageal adenocarcinoma treated with tislelizumab in combination with chemotherapy. If pneumonitis is confirmed, tislelizumab therapy may need to be interrupted or discontinued depending on severity; treatment with systemic corticosteroids may be necessary.

    colitis, diarrhea, gastritis
    General Information
    Immune-mediated colitis occurred in 0.8% (grade 3, 0.3%) of patients from a pooled safety population who were treated with tislelizumab across clinical trials. Treatment with systemic corticosteroids was required in 89.5% of patients with colitis; resolution occurred in 89.5% of these patients. Upon resolution of colitis, 9 patients reinitiated tislelizumab therapy; 2 patients (22%) experienced a recurrence of colitis. Cytomegalovirus (CMV) viral infection/reactivation has also been reported in patients with steroid-refractory immune-mediated colitis treated with PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors. Diarrhea including colitis occurred in 13% (grade 3 or 4, 1%) of patients with advanced esophageal cancer treated with tislelizumab. It occurred in at least 20% of patients with advanced gastric or gastroesophageal adenocarcinoma treated with tislelizumab plus platinum- and fluoropyrimidine-based chemotherapy in 1 clinical trial, and in 83% (grade 3 or 4, 26%) of patients in a similar population treated with tislelizumab plus platinum and fluoropyrimidine-based chemotherapy and zanidatamab in another clinical trial. Colitis occurred in less than 10% of patients who received tislelizumab and chemotherapy in 1 of these trials (grade 3 or 4, 2.2%). Tislelizumab therapy may need to be interrupted or discontinued depending on severity; treatment with systemic corticosteroids may be necessary. In patients with steroid-refractory colitis, consider repeating an infectious workup to exclude alternative etiologies. Immune-mediated gastritis and duodenitis have been reported in less than 1% of patients treated with tislelizumab or other PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors.

    elevated hepatic enzymes, hepatitis, hyperbilirubinemia
    General Information
    Immune-mediated hepatitis, which can be fatal, occurred in 1.3% (grade 3 or 4, 0.9%) of patients from a pooled safety population who were treated with tislelizumab across clinical trials. Treatment with systemic corticosteroids was required in 83.3% of patients with immune-mediated hepatitis; resolution occurred in 66.7% of these patients. Upon resolution of hepatitis, 7 patients reinitiated tislelizumab therapy; 1 patient experienced a recurrence of hepatitis. Elevated hepatic enzymes including increased AST (27%; grade 3 or 4, 0.8%) and increased ALT (23%; grade 3 or 4, 0.8%), as well as increased alkaline phosphatase (32%; grade 3 or 4, 3%) were reported in patients with advanced esophageal cancer treated with tislelizumab; hyperbilirubinemia occurred in 11% of these patients (grade 3 or 4, 2%). Elevated hepatic enzymes including increased AST (36% to 58%; grade 3 or 4, 3.4% to 6%) and increased AST (28% to 41%; grade 3 or 4, 3.1% to 4.8%) were reported in patients treated with tislelizumab in combination with chemotherapy in 2 additional clinical trials. Tislelizumab therapy may need to be interrupted or discontinued depending on the severity of hepatitis and if there is tumor involvement of the liver; treatment with systemic corticosteroids may also be necessary.

Description
  Text
  Tislelizumab is a programmed death receptor-1 (PD-1) blocking monoclonal antibody that works by preventing the interaction between PD-1 and the PD-L1 and PD-L2 ligands. It is indicated as first-line therapy for advanced PD-L1 positive esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) in combination with platinum-containing chemotherapy, for advanced HER2-negative, PD-L1 positive gastric or gastroesophageal junction cancer in combination with platinum- and fluoropyrimidine-based chemotherapy, and for advanced HER2-positive gastroesophageal cancer in combination with platinum- and fluoropyrimidine-based chemotherapy and zanidatamab. It is approved as monotherapy for unresectable or metastatic ESCC after prior systemic chemotherapy that did not include a PD-(L)1 inhibitor. Immune-mediated adverse reactions including colitis, pneumonitis, hepatitis, hypophysitis, nephritis, hypothyroidism/hyperthyroidism, and encephalitis have been reported with tislelizumab use; treatment with high-dose corticosteroids may be necessary for patients who develop immune-mediated toxicity. Tislelizumab was FDA-approved in March 2024.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Tislelizumab is a humanized monoclonal IgG4 kappa antibody that binds to the programmed death receptor-1 (PD-1) found on T-cells and blocks the interaction of PD-1 with its ligands, PD-L1 and PD-L2, on the tumor cell. In some tumors, upregulation of PD-1 ligands and PD-1/PD-L1 pathway signaling can contribute to inhibition of active T-cell immune surveillance of tumors. Blocking the PD-1/PD-L1 pathway inhibits T-cell proliferation and cytokine production, releasing PD-1 pathway-mediated inhibition of the immune response (including the anti-tumor immune response). Tislelizumab-jsgr decreased tumor growth in xenograft models and a human PD-1 transgenic mouse model.

Dilution
Withdraw the appropriate volume from the tislelizumab vials and add to an IV bag containing 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection to achieve a final concentration between 2 mg/mL and 5 mg/mL.
Mix by gentle inversion to avoid foaming or excess shearing of the solution. Do not shake.
Discard any unused tislelizumab remaining in the vials.
Storage after dilution: Store at room temperature (20 to 25 degrees C or 68 to 77 degrees F) for up to 4 hours including preparation and infusion time, or refrigerated at 2 to 8 degrees C (36 to 46 degrees F) for up to 10 days (240 hours) including preparation and infusion time. If refrigerated, allow the diluted solution to come to room temperature before infusion. Do not freeze.
 
Intravenous Infusion
Do not administer as an IV push or single bolus injection.
Infuse through an IV line with a sterile, nonpyrogenic, low-protein binding 0.2 micron or 0.22 micron in-line or add-on filter.
For 150 mg and 200 mg doses: Administer the first dose over 60 minutes. If tolerated, all subsequent doses may be administered over 30 minutes.
For 300 mg doses: Administer the first dose over 90 minutes. If tolerated, administer the second dose over 60 minutes. If the second infusion is tolerated, all subsequent doses may be administered over 30 minutes.
For 400 mg doses: Administer the first dose over 120 minutes. If tolerated, administer the second dose over 60 minutes. If the second infusion is tolerated, all subsequent doses may be administered over 30 minutes.

Tumor involvement of the liver
NOTE: For patients with baseline AST/ALT at the ULN or less, follow the recommendations for hepatitis with no tumor involvement of the liver.
Baseline AST/ALT more than 1 to 3 times ULN and increasing to more than 5 to 10 times ULN: Hold tislelizumab and administer corticosteroids (i.e., prednisone 1 to 2 mg/kg per day or equivalent, followed by a 1-month taper). Resume therapy when the adverse event recovers to grade 1 or less after the corticosteroid taper. Permanently discontinue tislelizumab in patients who do not have a complete or partial resolution of toxicity or who cannot reduce their daily prednisone dose to less than 10 mg (or equivalent) within 12 weeks of initiating steroids.
Baseline AST/ALT more than 3 to 5 times ULN and increasing to more than 8 to 10 times ULN: Hold tislelizumab and administer corticosteroids (i.e., prednisone 1 to 2 mg/kg per day or equivalent, followed by a 1-month taper). Resume therapy when the adverse event recovers to grade 1 or less after the corticosteroid taper. Permanently discontinue tislelizumab in patients who do not have a complete or partial resolution of toxicity or who cannot reduce their daily prednisone dose to less than 10 mg (or equivalent) within 12 weeks of initiating steroids.
Any baseline AST/ALT increasing to more than 10 times ULN or total bilirubin to more than 3 times ULN: Permanently discontinue tislelizumab and administer corticosteroids (i.e., prednisone 1 to 2 mg/kg per day or equivalent, followed by a 1-month taper starting when the toxicity resolves to grade 1 or less).
