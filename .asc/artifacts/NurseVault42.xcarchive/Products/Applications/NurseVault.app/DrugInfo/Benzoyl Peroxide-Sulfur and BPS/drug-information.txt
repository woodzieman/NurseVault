    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Per the manufacturer, benzoyl peroxide; sulfur topical products should be used during pregnancy only if clearly needed. However, use as intended for acne treatment appears to be acceptable during pregnancy. Benzoyl peroxide is considered a safe topical acne treatment during all phases of pregnancy by dermatology experts, as there are no reported teratogenic effects and minimal systemic drug absorption. Sulfur topical application in acne products is also considered safe for pregnant individuals; sulfur is an element that exists in nature that has keratolytic and mild antimicrobial effects, but should not be confused with sulfonamide antibiotics, which have a different chemical structure and activity.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Benzoyl peroxide; sulfur is compatible with breast-feeding. The manufacturer advises caution because it is not known whether the components are excreted in human milk. However, very little drug is absorbed systemically with topical use; therefore, minimal risk to the breast-fed child would be expected. Only water-miscible cream products should be applied to the breast because ointments may expose the infant to high levels of mineral paraffins. Consider the benefits of breast-feeding, the risk of potential drug exposure, and the risk of an untreated or inadequately treated condition. If a breast-fed child experiences an adverse effect related to a maternally administered drug, healthcare providers are encouraged to report the adverse effect to the FDA.

  Adverse Reactions
    Headline

    General Information
    The most frequent adverse reactions associated with benzoyl peroxide; sulfur gel include: excessive erythema and skin peeling in 5% of patients. Excessive erythema and peeling most frequently appear during the initial phase of drug use and skin irritation may normally be controlled by reducing the frequency of use. Contact dermatitis represents a sensitivity reaction and may occur in 10 to 25 of every 1000 patients (i.e., 1—2.5% of patients). If contact dermatitis or severe skin irritation occurs, discontinue use and institute appropriate therapy.

Description
  Text
  Benzoyl peroxide; sulfur is a topical treatment for acne. Benzoyl peroxide exhibits antibacterial activity and has drying actions, sebostatic effects, and causes mild skin desquamation. Benzoyl peroxide improves both inflammatory and non-inflammatory acne lesions. Sulfur has keratolytic, antifungal, and antiparasitic activity. Benzoyl peroxide and sulfur were originally marketed for use before 1962, which is when Congress passed the amended Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act requiring that the FDA establish both safety and efficacy for all subsequently approved drugs. Benzoyl peroxide; sulfur has not been formally evaluated by the FDA.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Benzoyl Peroxide: Benzoyl peroxide has antimicrobial effects against Propionibacterium acnes, which is the predominant organism in sebaceous follicles and comedones. The antibacterial effects of benzoyl peroxide are due to the release of free-radical oxygen species capable of oxidizing bacterial proteins. Benzoyl peroxide has a desquamative effect which may contribute to its efficacy.
Sulfur: Sulfur is believed to exert a beneficial effect in the treatment of acne because of its antimicrobial mild keratolytic action. The exact mechanism of action is unknown.
