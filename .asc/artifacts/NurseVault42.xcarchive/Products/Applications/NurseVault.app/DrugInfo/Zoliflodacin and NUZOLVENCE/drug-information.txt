Drug Class: Zoliflodacin is a spiropyrimidinetrione bacterial type II topoisomerase inhibitor indicated for the treatment of uncomplicated urogenital gonorrhea due to N. gonorrhoeae.

Routes: oral; Common adult doses: 3 g PO as a single dose.

Common Side Effects (excluding routine GI upset): dizziness, drowsiness, headache, hypoesthesia, insomnia, leukopenia, neutropenia, thrombocytopenia, alopecia, blepharedema, pruritus, rash, c. difficile-associated diarrhea, candidiasis, pseudomembranous colitis, superinfection, asthenia, chills, fatigue, fever, hot flashes, malaise, musculoskeletal pain, night sweats

In an Overdose: No overdose/antidote information is listed in this file.

Key Nursing Considerations: Contraindications/precautions: This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components. pregnancy Avoid the use of zoliflodacin during pregnancy. Zoliflodacin may cause fetal harm, based on data from animal studies.

    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Avoid the use of zoliflodacin during pregnancy. Zoliflodacin may cause fetal harm, based on data from animal studies. There are no human data to evaluate the drug-associated risk of major birth defects, miscarriage, or adverse outcomes. In pregnant mice, fetal malformations (exencephaly) and increased embryo-fetal loss were observed with repeat oral administration of zoliflodacin during organogenesis, resulting in AUC exposures 1.6 times the maximum recommended human dose (MRHD). Decreased fetal weights occurred at exposures 2.9 times the MRHD. In pregnant rats, zoliflodacin increased embryo-fetal loss at exposures 8.5 times the MRHD and decreased fetal weights at 3.2 times the MRHD. No effect on embryo-fetal survival was observed at exposures 5.5 times the MRHD. No effects on pup survival, birth weight, or growth were observed when zoliflodacin was administered to rats throughout pregnancy, parturition, and lactation at exposures up to 2.4 times the MRHD. Advise pregnant individuals of the potential fetal risk. If pregnancy occurs during treatment, report the exposure to the manufacturer's pregnancy safety program at 1-800-651-3861.
    Headline
    people who can cause pregnancy in others, people who may become pregnant, reproductive risk
    General Information
    Counsel people who may become pregnant and people who can cause pregnancy in others about the reproductive risk associated with zoliflodacin. Zoliflodacin may cause fetal harm when given to pregnant individuals. Additionally, there may be an increased risk of pregnancy loss in partners of people treated with zoliflodacin, based on animal data. Verify pregnancy status prior to treatment initiation. Advise those who can cause pregnancy in others to use effective contraception for at least 3 months after their single dose treatment. Zoliflodacin may cause testicular toxicity and impair male fertility based on findings from animal studies.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Use zoliflodacin with caution during breast-feeding. There are no data on the presence of zoliflodacin in human or animal milk, its effects on the breastfed child, or its effects on milk production. If zoliflodacin is present in breast milk, intestinal flora alteration may occur in the exposed child. Consider the developmental and health benefits of breast-feeding along with the patient's clinical need for zoliflodacin and any potential adverse effects on the breastfed child from zoliflodacin or from the patient's underlying condition.

    Headline
    leukopenia, neutropenia, thrombocytopenia
    General Information
    Neutropenia and leukopenia were observed in individuals receiving zoliflodacin in clinical trials. Neutropenia occurred in approximately 12% of individuals with normal baseline neutrophil counts, with most cases classified as mild to moderate; severe neutropenia (neutrophil counts less than 500 cells/mm3) occurred in 0.2% of individuals. Neutropenia was typically observed 4 to 8 days or 27 to 33 days following administration, and no individuals required treatment. Of the 71 individuals who developed neutropenia, 25% were living with HIV and 8% had unknown status. Leukopenia occurred in approximately 9% of individuals with normal baseline white blood cell counts and was generally mild; no individuals required intervention. Of those who developed leukopenia, 16% were living with HIV and 11% had unknown HIV status. Other hematologic adverse reactions occurring in less than 2% of individuals include thrombocytopenia, decreased monocyte count, and decreased hemoglobin.

    Headline
    C. difficile-associated diarrhea, candidiasis, pseudomembranous colitis, superinfection
    General Information
    Microbial overgrowth and superinfection can occur with antibiotic use. C. difficile-associated diarrhea (CDAD) or pseudomembranous colitis has been reported for nearly all systemic antibacterial drugs, including zoliflodacin, which may range in severity from mild to life-threatening. Treatment with antibacterial agents alters the normal flora of the colon leading to overgrowth of C. difficile. If pseudomembranous colitis is suspected or confirmed, ongoing antibacterial therapy not directed against C. difficile may need to be discontinued. Institute appropriate fluid and electrolyte management, protein supplementation, C. difficile-directed antibacterial therapy, and surgical evaluation as clinically appropriate. Careful medical history is necessary as pseudomembranous colitis has been reported to occur over 2 months after the administration of antibacterial agents. Tinea infections or candidal infections (candidiasis) were reported in less than 2% of individuals receiving zoliflodacin in clinical trials.

Description
  Text
  Zoliflodacin is a spiropyrimidinetrione bacterial type II topoisomerase inhibitor indicated for the treatment of uncomplicated urogenital gonorrhea due to N. gonorrhoeae. It is a first-in-class, single-dose, oral antibiotic with activity against N. gonorrhoeae, including strains resistant to existing therapies, and provides a convenient oral alternative to injectable regimens. In a pivotal Phase 3 trial (n = 930), zoliflodacin demonstrated non-inferiority compared with ceftriaxone plus azithromycin for treating uncomplicated urogenital gonorrhea. Zoliflodacin is associated with embryo-fetal toxicity based on animal studies and should be avoided during pregnancy. Zoliflodacin was FDA-approved in December 2025.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Zoliflodacin is a spiropyrimidinetrione inhibitor of the bacterial type II topoisomerases (DNA gyrase and topoisomerase IV), which are required for DNA synthesis. Zoliflodacin binds within the cleaved DNA-gyrase complex, blocking re-ligation, and interacts with conserved amino acids in the gyrase B subunit.
 
Pharmacodynamics
Zoliflodacin exhibits concentration-dependent pharmacodynamics where the ratio of area under the concentration curve of free drug to minimal inhibitory concentration (free AUC to MIC ratio) appears to best correlate with antibacterial activity.
 
Susceptibility
For N. gonorrhoeae, isolates are considered susceptible to zoliflodacin at MICs of 0.25 mcg/mL or less.
 
Resistance
Resistance to spiropyrimidinetriones is associated with mutations in bacterial type II topoisomerases (DNA gyrase and DNA topoisomerase IV) and with increased activity of efflux pumps. Zoliflodacin primarily targets DNA gyrase subunit B in N. gonorrhoeae, and reduced in vitro activity has been observed with up-regulation of efflux pumps, including MtrCDE, MacAB, and NorM. Spontaneous resistance to zoliflodacin occurs at low frequencies at 8 times the minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC). Cross-resistance with other antimicrobial classes, including penicillins, cephalosporins, aminoglycosides, macrolides, fluoroquinolones, and tetracyclines, has not been identified.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Oral Route
    Description
    Zoliflodacin is orally absorbed, with peak plasma concentrations occurring approximately 2.5 hours (range: 1 to 4 hours) under fasted conditions. The predicted mean Cmax and AUC for zoliflodacin in adults are 28.5 mcg/mL and 353 mcg x hr/mL, respectively. Following single-dose administration, zoliflodacin generally exhibited dose-proportional increases in exposure up to 800 mg (0.27 times the recommended dosage); doses above 800 mg resulted in slightly less than dose-proportional increases in exposure up to 4 g (1.3 times the recommended dosage).
Effects of Food
Administration with food delays absorption and increases systemic exposure of zoliflodacin; at the recommended 3 g dose, median Tmax occurs at approximately 4 hours (range: 3 to 5.5 hours) under fed conditions, while Cmax is increased by approximately 1.5-fold and AUC is increased by approximately 1.5- to 2-fold. An inverse relationship between body weight and zoliflodacin exposure has been observed, informing weight-based food administration recommendations.

  Text
  Zoliflodacin is administered orally. Zoliflodacin is approximately 83% bound to human plasma proteins and has a blood-to-plasma ratio of 0.69. The apparent volume of distribution is 177 L and is lower (98.7 L) when zoliflodacin is administered with food compared with fasted conditions. Zoliflodacin is primarily cleared through metabolism via both cytochrome P450-mediated and non-cytochrome P450 pathways. Cytochrome P450 metabolism is predominantly mediated by CYP3A4, with lesser contributions from CYP1A2, CYP2C9, CYP2C8, and CYP2C19. Non-cytochrome P450 metabolic pathways have not been fully characterized. The primary route of elimination is fecal excretion (79.6%). The terminal elimination half-life is approximately 6 hours under fasted conditions and approximately 5.5 hours when administered with food. Apparent oral clearance is reduced in the fed state compared with fasted conditions. A small proportion of the administered dose is excreted unchanged in urine.
 
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes and drug transporters: CYP3A, P-gp, BCRP, OAT1/3, OATP1B1/3
Zoliflodacin is primarily metabolized through CYP3A4, with lesser contributions from CYP1A2, CYP2C9, CYP2C8, and CYP2C19. It is a substrate for P-gp and BCRP and possibly for OATP1B1/3. Zoliflodacin also inhibits P-gp, BCRP, OAT1/3, and OATP1B1/3. Zoliflodacin did not inhibit CYP2C8, CYP2C9, or CYP2C19 at clinically relevant concentrations, as inhibitory effects were observed only at exposures substantially higher than anticipated therapeutic concentrations. Zoliflodacin was not an inducer of CYP1A2, CYP2B6, or CYP3A4 in inducible human hepatocyte-like HepaRG cells at clinically relevant concentrations.

Preparation and Administration
Must mix with water prior to administration.
Do not mix zoliflodacin with liquids other than water and do not sprinkle on food.
Measure 60 mL of water into the provided mixing container.
Add the entire contents of 1 unit-dose packet of zoliflodacin to the mixing container.
Secure the lid and shake vigorously for at least 60 seconds, continuing until all granules are suspended and a uniform suspension is achieved.
Administer the entire contents immediately after mixing.
To ensure the full dose is consumed, add an additional 60 mL of water to the same mixing container, shake, and administer the entire volume.
Administer the full dose within 15 minutes of mixing. If not administered within 15 minutes, discard and prepare a new dose.
Administer with or without food based on body weight:
weight 50 kg or more: Take on an empty stomach, 1 hour before or 2 hours after food.
weight 35 to 49 kg: Take with food.
