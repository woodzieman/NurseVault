Drug Class: Fondaparinux is a factor Xa inhibitor used for the treatment of venous thromboembolism (VTE) and prevention of DVT and PE in patients undergoing surgery.

Routes: injection (IV/IM/SC); Common adult doses: weighing more than 100 kg: 2.5 mg/day subcutaneously for prophylaxis; 10 mg/day subcutaneously for treatment.

Common Side Effects (excluding routine GI upset): bleeding, ecchymosis, epistaxis, gi bleeding, hematemesis, hematoma, hematuria, intracranial bleeding, ocular hemorrhage, petechiae, purpura, retroperitoneal bleeding, spinal hematoma, vaginal bleeding, injection site reaction, pruritus, rash, anemia, hypokalemia, hypotension, confusion, dizziness, insomnia

In an Overdose: Protamine sulfate is not an effective antidote to counteract the effects of fondaparinux; an antidote for fondaparinux is not available. Fondaparinux should be discontinued in overdose situations associated with bleeding complications.

Key Nursing Considerations: Contraindications/precautions: NOTE: Fondaparinux cannot be used interchangeably with heparin, low molecular weight heparins (LMWH) or heparinoids.

Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  General Information
  NOTE: Fondaparinux cannot be used interchangeably with heparin, low molecular weight heparins (LMWH) or heparinoids. All products differ in their manufacturing process, activity, and dosage. Routine laboratory measures of coagulation, such as Prothrombin Time (PT) and Activated Partial Thromboplastin (aPTT) are relatively insensitive measures of fondaparinux activity. International standards of heparin or LMWH are not calibrators to measure anti-factor Xa activity of fondaparinux. The activity of fondaparinux is expressed as mg of the fondaparinux calibrator. If unexpected changes in coagulation parameters occur, fondaparinux should be discontinued.
 
The presence of active major bleeding, including GI bleeding or hemorrhagic stroke in the acute phase, bacterial endocarditis, or a known hypersensitivity to fondaparinux are contraindications to the use of fondaparinux. Fondaparinux prophylactic therapy is also contraindicated in patients with body weight = 50 kg (5.4% vs. 2.1%, respectively). Similarly, in patients undergoing abdominal surgery, the incidence of major bleeding was higher in patients weighing = 50 kg (5.3% vs. 3.3%, respectively). To minimize bleeding risk, fondaparinux injection should be administered according to the recommended dosage regimen for peri-operative prophylaxis (see Dosage); administration before 6 hours after surgery has been associated an increased risk of major bleeding. In addition, the bleeding risk for spinal or epidural hematomas (leading to paralysis) may be greater with postoperative use of indwelling epidural catheters or concomitant use of other drugs affecting hemostasis (e.g., NSAIDs). Risk appears to be increased by traumatic or repeated epidural or spinal puncture. If epidural anesthesia, lumbar puncture or spinal anesthesia is employed, patients should be frequently monitored for symptoms of neurological impairment; fondaparinux should be discontinued and appropriate therapy initiated if needed. Clinicians should consider the risk versus benefit of neuraxial intervention in patients anticoagulated or to be anticoagulated for thromboprophylaxis.
 
Fondaparinux sodium should be injected subcutaneously; it is not suitable for intramuscular administration. Other intramuscular injections should be administered cautiously to patients receiving fondaparinux. IM injections may cause bleeding, bruising, or hematoma formation in patients who are anticoagulated.
 
Fondaparinux should be used cautiously in the elderly, particularly in patients >=75 years of age. Renal clearance in this population is reduced by roughly 25% and accumulation of the unbound, active drug may lead to serious adverse events, including major bleeding. The risk of major bleeding increases with age. During clinical prophylaxis trials in patients with hip fracture, or hip or knee replacement surgery, major bleeding occurred in 1.8% of patients = 75 years. Similarly, during clinical prophylaxis trials with fondaparinux after abdominal surgery, major bleeding occurred in 3% (19/644) of patients = 75 years of age. In the DVT and PE treatment clinical trials, the risk of fondaparinux-associated major bleeding increased with age: 0.6% in patients = 75 years. Renal parameters should be assessed prior to use of fondaparinux in the elderly; in addition, careful attention to dosing directions and concomitant medications that affect hemostasis is advised.
 
The presence of thrombocytopenia associated with a positive in-vitro test for anti-platelet antibody in the presence of fondaparinux is a contraindication to the use of fondaparinux. Thrombocytopenia has occurred with fondaparinux. The manufacturer suggests that if platelet counts fall below 100,000 mm3, fondaparinux should be discontinued. In clinical trials, patients with platelet counts = 2. Occurrence of major bleeds in clinical trials for fondaparinux prophylaxis in hip fracture, hip replacement, or knee replacement surgery was 1.6% (25/1565), 2.4% (31/1288), 3.8% (19/504), and 4.8% (4/83) in patients with normal, mild, moderate, or severe renal impairment, respectively. In patients treated with fondaparinux for DVT and PE prophylaxis after abdominal surgery, major bleeding occurred in 2.1% (13/606), 3.6% (22/613), 6.7% (12/179), and 7.1% (1/14) of patients with normal, mild, moderate, and severe renal impairment, respectively. Occurrences of major bleeding in patients fondaparinux for treatment of DVT and PE with normal renal function, mild renal impairment, moderate renal impairment, and severe renal impairment have been found to be 0.4% (4/1132), 1.6% (12/733), 2.2% (7/318), and 7.3% (4/55), respectively. Therefore, fondaparinux is contraindicated in patients with CrCl 2 mg/dl were excluded from study randomization. Fondaparinux should be used cautiously in patients with moderate renal function (CrCl 30 to 50 ml/min). All patients receiving fondaparinux should be regularly evaluated for adequate renal function and the drug should be discontinued if severe renal impairment or labile renal function occurs.
 
As with other antithrombotic agents, fondaparinux should be used with extreme caution in patients with an increased risk of hemorrhage. Such conditions include: congenital or acquired bleeding disorders (e.g., hemophilia and idiopathic thrombocytopenic purpura (ITP), history of or active peptic ulcer disease, diverticulitis, inflammatory bowel disease, decreased platelets, recent GI bleeding, non-hemorrhagic stroke, menstruation, angiodysplastic GI disease, dissecting aneurysm, threatened abortion or other abnormal vaginal bleeding, recent brain, spinal or ophthalmological surgery, severe renal or hepatic disease, uncontrolled hypertension, hypertensive or diabetic retinopathy, or concomitant treatment with platelet inhibitors. Patients should be observed closely for bleeding if fondaparinux is administered during or immediately following diagnostic lumbar puncture, epidural anesthesia, spinal anesthesia, or tube drainage of the stomach or small intestine. Patients should be monitored closely for a fall in hematocrit and/or a fall in blood pressure, hematuria, hematemesis, and other signs or symptoms of bleeding. Periodic complete blood counts, serum creatinine levels and stool occult blood tests are recommended. Unlike coumarins or heparinoids, an antidote to reverse bleeding with fondaparinux is not available.
 
Fondaparinux is classified as FDA pregnancy risk category B. Pregnant rat and rabbits studies at 32 and 65 times the recommended human dose, respectively, have not revealed evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. No adequate and well controlled studies exist in pregnant women. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed.
 
Fondaparinux was excreted in the milk of lactating rats; it is unknown if fondaparinux is excreted into human breast milk. Use caution when administering fondaparinux to women who are breast-feeding their infants.
 
Safe and effective use of fondaparinux in children has not been established.
 
Fondaparinux should be used with caution in patients receiving other anticoagulant therapy (e.g., warfarin), thrombolytic therapy (e.g., alteplase, reteplase, streptokinase), and/or platelet inhibitors (e.g., aspirin, ticlopidine) due to the potential increased risk of bleeding.
 
Patients who are anticoagulated are at increased risk of bleeding during dental procedures. Patient should inform health care providers of anticoagulant therapy prior to having dental work performed. Patients, especially those with dental disease, should be instructed in proper oral hygiene, including caution in use of regular toothbrushes, dental floss, and toothpicks.

    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components. The needle guard for the prefilled syringe contains dry natural latex rubber and may cause reactions in individuals with latex hypersensitivity.
    Headline
    Treatment-Related Restrictions of Use
    General Information
    Fondaparinux prophylactic therapy is contraindicated in adults with body weight less than 50 kg who are undergoing hip fracture, hip replacement, knee replacement, or abdominal surgery. An increased risk of bleeding has been observed in adults weighing less than 50 kg compared with those weighing 50 kg or more.
    Headline
    neurosurgical procedure, spinal deformity, spinal hematoma
    General Information
    Carefully consider the potential benefits and risks before performing a neurosurgical procedure in individuals who are receiving or are expected to receive fondaparinux. Epidural or spinal hematoma, which may result in long-term or permanent paralysis, can occur in anticoagulated individuals undergoing neuraxial anesthesia or spinal puncture. Risk factors for epidural or spinal hematoma include use of an indwelling epidural catheter, concomitant use of other agents that affect hemostasis (e.g., NSAIDs, platelet inhibitors, other anticoagulants), a history of spinal deformity or spinal surgery, and traumatic or repeated puncture. The optimal timing between the administration of fondaparinux and neuraxial procedures is not known. Monitor individuals undergoing these procedures frequently for neurological impairment and instruct them to report symptoms immediately. If an epidural or spinal hematoma is suspected, urgently diagnose and treat, including consideration of spinal cord decompression.
    Headline
    bleeding disorder, bone marrow suppression, diabetic retinopathy, GI angiodysplasia, hypertension, infective endocarditis, intestinal lymphangiectasia, major bleeding, malignancy, peptic ulcer disease, surgery, systemic infection, systemic lupus erythematosus, thoracostomy tube, thoracotomy
    General Information
    Fondaparinux is contraindicated in individuals with active major bleeding, infective endocarditis, or thrombocytopenia associated with a positive in vitro test for anti-platelet antibody in the presence of fondaparinux. Use fondaparinux with caution in individuals who have an increased risk of hemorrhage, including those with severe uncontrolled hypertension, congenital or acquired bleeding disorder, active peptic ulcer disease, GI angiodysplasia, hemorrhagic stroke, or those who have recently undergone brain, spinal, or ocular surgery. Fondaparinux may further increase bleeding risk in individuals with thrombocytopenia or platelet function defects, hypertensive or diabetic retinopathy, or recent GI bleeding. Pediatric conditions associated with an increased risk of bleeding include systemic lupus erythematosus, malignancy (including Wilms tumor), thrombophilia (e.g., antiphospholipid antibody syndrome, antithrombin III deficiency, Factor V Leiden), bone marrow suppression (pancytopenia), indwelling thoracostomy tube, thoracotomy, invasive systemic infection, hypertensive encephalopathy, intestinal lymphangiectasia, and von Willebrand disease. To minimize postoperative bleeding risk, administer fondaparinux no earlier than 6 to 8 hours after surgery, as administration before 6 hours postoperatively has been associated with an increased incidence of major bleeding.
    Headline
    renal failure, renal impairment
    General Information
    Fondaparinux is contraindicated in individuals with severe renal impairment or renal failure (CrCl less than 30 mL/minute). Fondaparinux increases the risk of bleeding in adults with renal impairment due to reduced clearance. Assess renal function periodically in individuals receiving fondaparinux and discontinue therapy immediately if severe renal impairment develops. The anticoagulant effects may persist for 2 to 4 days after stopping treatment in those with normal renal function and for a longer duration in those with renal impairment. There are no data to support the safe and effective use of fondaparinux in pediatric patients with any degree of renal impairment.
    Headline
    labor, obstetric delivery, pregnancy
    General Information
    A clear association with fondaparinux and fetal adverse developmental outcomes has not been demonstrated with available data from published literature and postmarketing reports. Fondaparinux plasma concentrations obtained from 4 pregnant women treated with fondaparinux and their newborn infants demonstrated low placental transfer of fondaparinux. Use of fondaparinux may increase the risk of bleeding in the fetus or neonate; monitor neonates for bleeding. In a study of 5 pregnant women treated with fondaparinux 2.5 mg/day during the third trimester, 4 women had increased anti-factor Xa activity in the cord blood; anti-factor Xa clotting times were 37.5 to 50.9 seconds. Umbilical cord plasma fondaparinux concentrations were one-tenth the concentration in maternal plasma. No infants experienced adverse effects. Fondaparinux use during labor or obstetric delivery in women who are receiving neuraxial anesthesia may result in epidural or spinal hematomas. Monitor pregnant women receiving fondaparinux carefully for evidence of bleeding or unexpected changes in coagulation parameters. Consider use of a shorter acting anticoagulant as delivery approaches. Clinical practice guidelines recommend low molecular weight heparin for the prevention and treatment of venous thromboembolism during pregnancy.
    Headline
    Child-Pugh class A, Child-Pugh class B, Child-Pugh class C, hepatic failure
    General Information
    Although no dosage adjustment is necessary in individuals with mild (Child-Pugh class A) or moderate hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh class B), closely monitor these individuals, as they have an increased risk of bleeding. In clinical trials, individuals with moderate hepatic impairment experienced a higher incidence of bleeding, particularly mild hematomas at the blood sampling or injection site, compared with subjects with normal hepatic function. No data are available for individuals with severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh class C) or hepatic failure, or for pediatric patients with any degree of hepatic impairment.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Fondaparinux is compatible with breast-feeding. Although there are no data on the presence of fondaparinux in human milk, due to its large molecular weight, it is not likely to be excreted in human breast milk or absorbed by a nursing infant. There are no data on the effects of fondaparinux on milk production. Clinical practice guidelines also note that although data for fondaparinux in breast-feeding patients are limited, significant oral absorption by the infant is unlikely.
    Headline
    geriatric
    General Information
    In geriatric adults, the risk of fondaparinux-associated bleeding and serious adverse events increases with age; careful attention to dosing recommendations and concomitant medications, especially antiplatelet agents, is advised. In clinical studies, the incidence of major bleeding was 1.6% to 2.2% in adults 65 to 74 years of age and 2.1% to 5% for adults 75 years and older, compared with 0.6% to 3% for adults less than 65 years. Because fondaparinux is substantially excreted by the kidneys and renal function tends to decline with age, assess renal function before and during therapy to reduce the risk of bleeding.

Description
  Text
  Fondaparinux is a factor Xa inhibitor used for the treatment of venous thromboembolism (VTE) and prevention of DVT and PE in patients undergoing surgery. Fondaparinux selectively inhibits factor Xa via antithrombin-dependent actions. Unlike unfractionated heparin or low molecular weight heparins (LMWHs), fondaparinux does not inhibit thrombin (factor IIa). Therefore, fondaparinux inhibits a single, targeted step in the coagulation cascade (factor Xa), resulting in antithrombotic action. Fondaparinux was FDA-approved in December 2001.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Fondaparinux exerts antithrombotic activity as a result of antithrombin III (ATIII)-mediated selective inhibition of factor Xa. Fondaparinux is a copy of the antithrombin III binding area of heparin. When fondaparinux binds to ATIII, a permanent conformation change in the ATIII molecule allows an increased affinity for factor Xa. The increased affinity potentiates ATIII's ability to inhibit factor Xa by roughly 300-fold. Neutralization of factor Xa interrupts the blood coagulation cascade that leads to thrombin formation and thrombus development. A single fondaparinux molecule is recycled and re-binds to several consecutive ATIII molecules. The structurally changed ATIII-Xa complex is then cleared from circulation. The anti-Xa activity of fondaparinux reaches a maximum value in roughly 3 hours. Fondaparinux does not inactivate thrombin (factor IIa) and has no known effect on platelet function. At recommended doses, fibrinolytic activity or bleeding time is not altered. An antidote to reverse bleeding effects of fondaparinux is not available; however, one is under investigation.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Subcutaneous Route
    Description
    Fondaparinux exhibits dose-dependent, linear pharmacokinetics. After subcutaneous administration, fondaparinux is rapidly absorbed with 100% bioavailability. Following a single dose of 2.5 mg, a maximum concentration of 0.34 mg/L is reached in approximately 2 hours. A peak steady-state concentration of 0.39 to 0.5 mg/L is reached within 3 hours post-dose. In patients with symptomatic deep vein thrombosis and pulmonary embolism, a weight-based dose (5 mg for weight less than 50 kg, 7.5 mg for weight 50 to 100 kg, 10 mg for weight greater than 100 kg) provided similar mean steady-state peaks and minimum plasma concentrations across all body weights. The mean steady-state plasma concentration was 1.2 to 1.26 mg/L. The mean minimum steady-state plasma concentration was in the range of 0.46 to 0.62 mg/L.

    Description
    Fondaparinux elimination is prolonged in elderly patients older than 75 years. In studies evaluating fondaparinux sodium 2.5 mg prophylaxis in hip fracture surgery or elective hip surgery, the total clearance of fondaparinux was approximately 25% lower in patients older than 75 years as compared to patients younger than 65 years. A similar relationship between fondaparinux clearance and age was observed in DVT treatment patients.
    Name
    Gender Differences
    Description
    The pharmacokinetic properties of fondaparinux are not significantly affected by gender.

    Ethnic Differences
    Description
    Pharmacokinetic differences in race have not been studied; however, in studies where healthy Asian (Japanese) subjects received fondaparinux, the studies did not reveal a different pharmacokinetic profile compared to healthy Caucasian subjects. No plasma clearance differences were observed between Black and Caucasian subjects undergoing orthopedic surgery.

    Other
    Description
    Low body weight
Total clearance of fondaparinux is decreased by approximately 30% in patients weighing less than 50 kg.
  Text
  Fondaparinux is administered subcutaneously. In vitro, fondaparinux is 94% protein bound, specifically to antithrombin III (ATIII). Significant binding to other proteins, including platelet factor 4 or red blood cells does not occur. The Vd is 7 to 11 L. Distribution is primarily in the blood and only to a minor extent in the extravascular fluid. Metabolism of fondaparinux appears to be negligible as the majority of the unbound drug is excreted unchanged in the urine. In healthy individuals 75 years of age or older, 77% of a parenteral dose is excreted unchanged in the urine in 72 hours. The elimination half-life is 17 to 21 hours.
 
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes and drug transporters: none

Preparation of pediatric-specific doses
For use in patients weighing 10 to 20 kg or patients weighing more than 20 kg when dose adjustments are needed and therapeutic concentrations are not achievable using the prefilled syringe strengths.
Determine the total dose and volume per the duration and the appropriate number of fondaparinux syringes needed. Use 1 fondaparinux syringe for volumes less than 0.5 mL. When the total dose required is greater than 0.5 mL, 2 fondaparinux syringes may be pooled together. The maximum number of syringes that may be pooled together is 2.
Do not combine prefilled syringes of different concentrations.
Using aseptic technique, dispense the full contents of the fondaparinux syringe(s) into an empty glass vial (recommended 5 mL) by fully depressing the plunger.
Using an empty graduated tuberculin sterile syringe and needle, withdraw the required dose from the vial into the tuberculin syringe. Repeat as needed for the appropriate number of tuberculin syringes necessary for the patient. Discard unused portion.
Storage: Store in refrigerator between 2 degrees and 8 degrees C (36 degrees and 46 degrees F). Do not freeze. Use prepared tuberculin syringes within 30 days after preparation.
 
Subcutaneous injection
If using a prefilled syringe affixed with an automatic needle protection system, do not expel the bubble of air from the syringe before injection.
Wipe the surface of the injection site with an alcohol swab. Follow the manufacturer's instructions for use of the syringe.
Inject into the left or right anterolateral or posterolateral abdominal wall.
Rotate injection site with each injection.

Dosage Adjustment Guidelines
  Hepatic Impairment
  Adult patients
Mild to moderate hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh Category A or B): No dosage adjustment is needed.

There are no adequate data to support safe and effective use of fondaparinux in pediatric patients with hepatic impairment.

  Adult patients
