Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  General Information
  Some Lactobacillus products should not be used in patients with milk allergy (milk protein hypersensitivity) because they are dairy based. These patients should choose Lactobacillus products that are dairy-free. Additionally, as Lactobacillus products that are dairy based will contain lactose, patients with lactase deficiency should take appropriate precautions with use.Unless directed by a health care professional, Lactobacillus products should not be used for long periods or in the presence of a high fever.Lactobacillus products should be used cautiously in patients with immunosuppression, prosthetic heart valves, or other valvular heart disease, due to a possible risk of bacteremia. Lactobacillemia has been reported in patients with severe underlying diseases (e.g., acquired immunodeficiency syndrome (AIDS) and others). While caution is recommended in all patients with immunosuppression, Lactobacillus reuteri has been used safely as a supplement in HIV-infected patients.Lactobacillus products are not recommended for children under 3 years unless prescribed by a health care prescriber.Lactobacillus rhamnosus GG has been administered to women during pregnancy and breast-feeding to protect against atopic disease in infants. In one study , it was reported that maternal administration and direct administration of Lactobacillus GG to the breast-fed infant resulted in similar amounts of Lactobacillus GG in infant feces. Lactobacillus reuteri has also been reported to be excreted in breast milk and can be passed on to the nursing infant. It is not known if the other Lactobacillus species are found in breast milk.

    Headline
    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components. Some Lactobacillus products are derived from dairy and should not be used in patients with milk allergy (milk protein hypersensitivity). These patients should choose Lactobacillus products that are dairy-free. Additionally, dairy-based Lactobacillus products may contain lactose and patients with lactase deficiency should also take appropriate precautions with use.
    Headline
    immunosuppression
    General Information
    Use Lactobacillus products with caution in patients with immunosuppression, as there is a potential risk of bacteremia, including Lactobacillemia. Case reports have documented such events in individuals with severe underlying conditions, including acquired immunodeficiency syndrome (AIDS) and other forms of significant immunocompromise. While caution is recommended in all patients with immunosuppression, Lactobacillus reuteri has been used safely as a supplement in people living with HIV.
    Headline
    neonates, premature neonates
    General Information
    Premature neonates who receive probiotics, such as Lactobacillus, are at risk of invasive, potentially fatal disease caused by the bacteria or fungi contained in probiotics. Microorganisms contained in probiotics have been reported in the medical literature as causing bacteremia or fungemia, sometimes with a severe clinical course, in very preterm or very low birth weight (VLBW) neonates. A preterm neonate, birth weight less than 1,000 grams, who was administered a probiotic as part of in-hospital care developed sepsis caused by the bacterium Bifidobacterium longum and subsequently died. Genomic sequencing data demonstrated the bacterium that caused sepsis in this neonate was a genetic match to the bacteria contained in this probiotic.

The health and medical conditions of the lactating individual may affect the composition of the milk microflora.

Description
  Text
  NOTE: In the US, nutraceuticals are marketed under the Dietary Supplement and Health Education Act of 1994 (DSHEA). Consequently, scientific data supporting claimed benefit(s) are not always available for nutraceuticals as they are for traditional pharmaceuticals since nutraceuticals are not regulated as drugs. Consumers should also note that rigid quality control standards are not required for nutraceuticals and substantial variability can occur in both the potency and the purity of these products. Monographs on nutraceuticals are included in CP when reliable clinical data are available. The information presented below is condensed from the best clinical data we could find.
 
Lactobacillus is an acid-producing probiotic bacterium. Four species are used clinically, Lactobacillus acidophilus, L. bulgaricus, L. reuteri, and L. rhamnosus GG. Lactobacillus acidophilus is a normal bowel inhabitant and is the most commonly used Lactobacillus species in the United States. Lactobacillus bulgaricus is added to some commercially available products but is not a normal inhabitant of the bowel. Combination products containing L. acidophilus and L. bulgaricus are available. Lactobacillus rhamnosus GG (Culturelle) is probably the most studied of the Lactobacillus species and is also not a normal inhabitant of the bowel. It is resistant to glycopeptide antibiotics such as vancomycin and teicoplanin. Lactobacillus rhamnosus GG has been shown to survive in gastric and bile secretions, effectively colonize the GI tract, and bind to epithelial cells. Generally, Lactobacillus preparations are used to reestablish the normal physiologic and bacterial flora of the intestinal tract. They are administered orally in the treatment of uncomplicated diarrhea, particularly that caused by modification of the intestinal flora by antibiotics. Lactobacillus preparations have shown efficacy in treating and preventing antibiotic-associated diarrhea, however, they appear to be ineffective in preventing traveler's diarrhea. Literature reviews report that Lactobacillus rhamnosus GG preparations provide significant benefits in treating children with acute rotavirus gastroenteritis. Premature neonates who receive probiotics are at risk of invasive, potentially fatal disease caused by the bacteria or fungi contained in probiotics. Microorganisms contained in probiotics have been reported in the medical literature as causing bacteremia or fungemia, sometimes with a severe clinical course, in very preterm or very low birthweight (VLBW) neonates.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Lactobacillus bacteria help to re-establish normal intestinal flora. The organisms create an environment unfavorable to potentially pathogenic fungi or bacteria through the production of lactic acid. This environment favors establishment of an acidic flora, thereby suppressing the growth of pathogenic microorganisms. Lactobacilli also produce several lactases, lipases, and proteases that may aid in digestion. Lactobacillus acidophilus has also been reported to lower serum cholesterol, however, these claims are inconclusive. Other mechanisms such as production of pathogen-inhibitory substances, inhibition of pathogen attachment, and stimulation of immunoglobulin A have also been evaluated in vitro.

Pharmacokinetics
  Text
  Lactobacillus products are administered orally in the form of capsules, tablets, granules, as well as through various food products (e.g., milk, yogurt, etc.). Pharmacokinetic data on Lactobacillus species are limited. Lactobacillus reuteri and Lactobacillus GG are associated with excretion in breast milk and can be passed on to the nursing infant. It is not known if the other Lactobacillus species are found in breast milk. Lactobacillus products have also been reported to cause Lactobacillemia in patients with severe underlying diseases, indicating systemic exposure can be possible after oral ingestion in selected patients.
