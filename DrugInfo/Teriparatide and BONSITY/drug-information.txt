Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  General Information
  Rare cases of osteosarcoma, a secondary malignancy, have been reported in patients on teriparatide therapy. In addition, teriparatide has been associated with an increased incidence of osteosarcoma in rat studies. This increased incidence was dependent on dose and duration of treatment; systemic exposure to teriparatide in the rats ranged from 3—60 times the exposure given to humans in a 20-mcg daily dose. The relevance of these rat osteosarcoma findings is uncertain relative to humans, however, the potential risk should be considered before treating patients with teriparatide. Teriparatide use has not been evaluated beyond 2 years; use of the drug beyond 2 years is not recommended. Patients who are at an increased risk for osteosarcoma (i.e., those with Paget's disease of bone or unexplained elevations of alkaline phosphatase, open epiphyses or lack of epiphyseal closure, or prior external beam or implant radiation therapy involving the skeleton) should not receive teriparatide. NOTE: Prior radiation therapy is not inclusive of: radioactive iodine therapy; low doses of non-penetrative radiation (i.e., Grenz radiation) in the treatment of skin conditions; diagnostic radiation (i.e., radiographs, CT scans, radionuclide scans); or non-radioactive forms of radiation (i.e., ultraviolet radiation, phototherapy). Patients with bone metastases or a history of skeletal malignancies, those with bone disorders other than osteoporosis, and patients with pre-existing hypercalcemia should also not receive treatment with teriparatide. Encourage all patients to enroll in the voluntary Forteo Patient Registry to assist in tracking the incidence of osteosarcoma in patients and the potential for human risk.
 
Teriparatide is classified as FDA pregnancy risk category C. The effect of teriparatide treatment on human fetal development has not been studied. Rat and mouse studies show an increased incidence of skeletal deviations and variations, as well as mild growth retardation in offspring, at doses >= 60 times the normal human dose. The use of teriparatide during pregnancy is not indicated and use during pregnancy should be limited based on the clinical situation (i.e., mother's disease state) where the potential benefit justifies the potential risk to the fetus.
 
No information is available regarding teriparatide's ability to be excreted in breast milk. The use of teriparatide in breast-feeding women should be limited to situations where the potential benefit justifies the potential risk to the infant.
 
Teriparatide is the amino-terminal fragment of parathyroid hormone and has actions similar to parathyroid hormone. Use in patients with hyperparathyroidism should be avoided.
 
Safety and efficacy of teriparatide has not been established in children < 18 years of age. Children and young adults are more likely to have open epiphyses, and therefore are at increased risk for osteosarcoma; do not use teriparatide in pediatric patients.

    hypercalcemia, primary hyperparathyroidism
    General Information
    Teriparatide may cause hypercalcemia and is not recommended in patients with pre-existing hypercalcemia due to the possibility of exacerbating the condition. Avoid the use of teriparatide in people who have an underlying hypercalcemic disorder (e.g., primary hyperparathyroidism) due to the risk of hypercalemia.     Use teriparatide cautiously in people with active or recent urolithiasis (nephrolithiasis). Teriparatide may cause hypercalciuria. It is unknown if teriparatide may exacerbate urolithiasis in patients with active urolithiasis.     pregnancy
    General Information
    Consider discontinuing teriparatide when pregnancy is recognized. There are no adequate or well-controlled studies on the use of teriparatide during human pregnancy. In reproductive animal studies, mouse offspring exhibited skeletal deviations or variations at subcutaneous doses equivalent to more than 60-fold the recommended human daily dose of 20 mcg (based on BSA), while rat offspring showed mild growth restriction and reduced motor activity at subcutaneous doses equivalent to more than 120-fold the human dose.

Adverse Reactions
  Headline
  Adverse Reactions
  General Information
  Hypercalcemia and hypercalciuria have been reported in patients treated with teriparatide. The serum and urine levels of calcium are transiently increased in patients treated with teriparatide, but typically remain within the normal range (or return to the normal range following a decrease in either teriparatide dose or daily calcium supplement dosage) and cause no associated adverse effects. Increases in serum uric acid levels have been reported, but were typically asymptomatic and rarely resulted in hyperuricemia or gout, the incidence of which was not different than placebo. Serum calcitriol levels significantly increase compared with baseline while receiving teriparatide. Hypomagnesemia requiring daily supplementation has been reported in patients receiving teriparatide for hypoparathyroidism. In single-dose studies, teriparatide produced transient phosphaturia and mild transient reductions in serum phosphorus concentrations, although, hypophosphatemia was not observed in clinical trials.
 
Patients reported an injection site reaction associated with teriparatide subcutaneous injections, consisting mainly of tenderness at the site.
 
Transient episodes of symptomatic orthostatic hypotension were observed, although infrequently, in clinical trials of teriparatide. An event typically began within 4 hours of dosing and spontaneously resolved within a few minutes to a few hours. When transient orthostatic hypotension occurred, it happened within the first several doses and was relieved by placing the patient in a reclining position.
 
In clinical trials, antibodies that cross-reacted with teriparatide were detected in 2.8% of females receiving the drug. Antibody formation appears to occur in the first 12 months of treatment, with diminished presence of antibodies following withdrawal of therapy. Among these patients, there was no evidence of hypersensitivity reactions, allergic reactions, effects on serum calcium, or effects on bone mineral density response reported.
 
In post-marketing experience, rare cases of anaphylactoid reactions including anaphylaxis, angioedema, and urticaria have been reported in patients taking teriparatide.
 
During clinical trials, leg muscle cramps were reported in 2.6% of patients and appeared to be related to teriparatide. Additionally, during post-marketing surveillance, muscle spasms of both the leg and back have been reported relatively commonly (between 1 to 10 patients for every 100 patients treated); sometimes the reports of the muscle spasms occur shortly after the first dose. Severe back muscle spasms are less common and reported in = 2% in teriparatide and placebo treated patients, at similar rates, included: pain, headache, asthenia, neck pain, hypertension, angina, syncope, nausea/vomiting, constipation, diarrhea, dyspepsia, arthralgia, dizziness, depression, insomnia, vertigo, rhinitis, cough, pharyngitis, dyspnea, pneumonia, rash (unspecified), and sweating.
 
Teriparatide may increase the risk of osteosarcoma, a secondary malignancy. Rarely cases of osteosarcoma have been reported in post-marketing experience. It is unclear if these cases are drug-related; however, use in rats has been associated with an increased incidence of osteosarcoma. This increased incidence was dependent on dose and duration of treatment; systemic exposure to teriparatide in the rats ranged from 3—60 times the exposure given to humans on a 20-mcg daily dose. The relevance of these rat osteosarcoma findings is uncertain relative to humans, however, the potential risk should be considered before treating patients with teriparatide. Teriparatide use has not been evaluated beyond 2 years; use of the drug beyond 2 years over a lifetime is not recommended. Patients at increased risk of osteosarcoma or those with bone metastases, a history of skeletal malignancies, bone disorders other than osteoporosis, or pre-existing hypercalcemia should not receive treatment with teriparatide (see Precautions/Contraindications). Encourage all patients to enroll in the voluntary Forteo Patient Registry to assist in tracking the incidence of osteosarcoma in teriparatide treated patients and the potential for human risk.

Description
  Text
  Teriparatide is a recombinant parathyroid hormone that is identical to the 34 N-terminal amino acids of human parathyroid hormone (PTH). It is an anabolic agent that promotes new bone growth. Teriparatide is indicated for the treatment of postmenopausal osteoporosis, primary or hypogonadal osteoporosis in men, and corticosteroid-induced osteoporosis in adults. Teriparatide is recommended only in people at a high risk of fracture (e.g., history of osteoporotic fracture or with multiple risk factors for fracture), or who have failed or are intolerant of other osteoporosis therapy. Teriparatide significantly improves bone mineral density in the hip, femoral neck, and lumbar spine and reduces the risk of vertebral and nonvertebral fractures in the indicated populations. Teriparatide and other anabolic agents are generally considered after first-line antiresorptive agents (e.g., bisphosphonates, denosumab) in most osteoporotic individuals. Teriparatide may be considered as initial treatment in people at very high risk of fracture or as an option for those individuals that have sustained a fracture despite antiresorptive treatment. Consider the need for daily injections, the cost of treatment, and adverse events in making individual treatment decisions. After discontinuation of teriparatide, the use of antiresorptive therapy (e.g., bisphosphonates or denosumab) is necessary to prevent rebound bone loss and continued reduction of fracture risk. Avoid teriparatide use in people with increased risk of osteosarcoma or hereditary risk factors for osteosarcoma. The cumulative use of teriparatide for more than 2 years during a person's lifetime should only be considered if the person remains at, or has returned to having, a high risk for fracture. Teriparatide has been investigated off-label for the treatment of hypoparathyroidism, but the role of the drug for treating this condition is not clear. Teriparatide was FDA-approved in 1987.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Teriparatide is a recombinant amino terminal fragment of parathyroid hormone (PTH), comprised of the first 34 amino acids of PTH which produce most of its chief biologic effects. Endogenous 84-amino acid parathyroid hormone (PTH) is the primary regulator of calcium and phosphate metabolism in bone and kidney. Physiological actions of PTH include regulation of bone metabolism, renal tubular reabsorption of calcium and phosphate, and intestinal calcium absorption. The biological actions of PTH and teriparatide are mediated through binding to specific high-affinity cell-surface receptors. Teriparatide and the 34 N-terminal amino acids of PTH bind to these receptors with the same affinity and have the same physiological actions on bone and kidney. Teriparatide is not expected to accumulate in bone or other tissues. The skeletal effects of teriparatide depend upon the pattern of systemic exposure. Once-daily administration of teriparatide stimulates new bone formation on trabecular and cortical (periosteal and/or endosteal) bone surfaces by preferential stimulation of osteoblastic activity over osteoclastic activity. In monkey studies, teriparatide improved trabecular micro-architecture and increased bone mass and strength by stimulating new bone formation in both cancellous and cortical bone. In humans, the anabolic effects of teriparatide manifest as an increase in skeletal mass, an increase in markers of bone formation and resorption, and an increase in bone strength. By contrast, continuous excess of endogenous PTH, as occurs in hyperparathyroidism, may be detrimental to the skeleton because bone resorption may be stimulated more than bone formation.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Subcutaneous Route
    Description
    Teriparatide is absorbed after subcutaneous injection; the absolute bioavailability is approximately 95% based on pooled data from 20-, 40-, and 80-mcg doses (1, 2, and 4-times the recommended dosage, respectively). The rates of absorption and elimination are rapid. The peptide reaches peak serum concentrations about 30 minutes after subcutaneous injection of a 20-mcg dose and declines to non-quantifiable concentrations within 3 hours. Pharmacokinetic data from another brand of injection (20-mcg dose) results in a Cmax of 109.5 +/- 62.8 pg/mL (mean +/- SD) at a median Tmax of 0.25 hours (0.12 minimum, 1.08 maximum). Concentrations decline to non-quantifiable within 3 hours.
  Special Populations
    Name
    Hepatic Impairment
    Description
    Teriparatide has not been studied in patients with hepatic impairment.
    Name
    Renal Impairment
    Description
    Teriparatide administered as a single dose has not been noted to have any pharmacokinetic differences in a limited number of patients with mild to moderate renal insufficiency (i.e., CrCl 30 to 72 mL/minute). In patients with severe renal insufficiency (i.e., CrCl less than 30 mL/minute), the AUC and half-life were increased by 73% and 77% respectively; however, maximum serum concentrations of teriparatide were not increased. No information is available for patients undergoing dialysis for chronic renal failure.

  Text
  Teriparatide is administered as a subcutaneous injection. Systemic clearance (approximately 62 L/hour in women and 94 L/hour in men) of teriparatide exceeds the rate of normal liver plasma flow, consistent with both hepatic and extra-hepatic clearance. Volume of distribution following intravenous injection is approximately 0.12 L/kg. Intersubject variability in systemic clearance and volume of distribution is 25% to 50%. The half-life of teriparatide in serum is 5 minutes when administered by intravenous injection and approximately 1 hour when administered by subcutaneous injection. The longer half-life following subcutaneous administration reflects absorption from the injection site. Peripheral metabolism of intact PTH, the 34 N-terminal amino acids, and, presumably, teriparatide, is believed to occur by non-specific enzymatic mechanisms in the liver followed by excretion via the kidneys.
 
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes and drug transporters: None

  Route Specific Administrations
    Administration Details
      Name
      Subcutaneous Administration
      Description
      Administer subcutaneously only. Do not inject intramuscularly or intravenously.
Administer the first dose under circumstances where the individual can sit or lie down if symptoms of orthostatic hypotension occur.
Individuals and/or caregivers who administer teriparatide should receive appropriate training and instruction on the proper use of the prefilled delivery device (pen) from their care team.
 
Subcutaneous Administration (Forteo or Bonsity Pen or generic equivalents)
A single patient use pen contains 28 daily doses of 20 mcg.
Do not inject more than 1 dose of teriparatide in the same day. 
Prepare for injection: Review the device user manual or 'Instructions for Use'.
Remove the cap on the device. Check the device to ensure the medication has not expired. Do not use an injection device that appears damaged.
Push a new needle straight onto the medication cartridge. Rotate the needle clockwise until it is firmly attached. Remove the large needle cover and save it.
Pull the black injection button until it stops. Ensure the red stripe on the device is visible. Remove and discard the small needle protector.
Inject subcutaneously into the thigh or abdomen. Rotate administration sites with each injection to prevent lipodystrophy.
Lightly pinch a fold of skin; insert the needle at a 90-degree angle. Push in the pen injection button until it stops. Hold it in and count to 5 slowly. Wait until the count of 5 to make sure the correct dose is administered. Then pull the needle from the skin.
Put the large needle cover on the pen without using your hands. Remove the covered needle.
Properly dispose of used pen needles in a puncture-resistant container. Replace the outer cap on the device.
Do not share pens; reserve the use of any pen for 1 individual only. Even if the disposable needle is changed, sharing may result in transmission of hepatitis viruses, HIV, or other blood-borne pathogens.
Storage: Store the pen under refrigeration at 2 to 8 degrees C (36 to 46 degrees F) at all times except when administering the product. Do not freeze. Do not use if the device has been frozen. Do not store the pen with the needle on, as the medication may leak out or air bubbles may form in the cartridge. Recap the delivery device when not in use to protect the cartridge from physical damage and light. During each use, time out of the refrigerator should be minimized; the dose may be delivered immediately following removal from the refrigerator. Return device to the refrigerator following each use. Each pen can be used for up to 28 days after the first injection. After 28 days, discard the pen, even if it still contains unused solution.
