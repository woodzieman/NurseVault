    bleeding, thrombocytopenia
    General Information
    Monitor platelet count prior to starting sotatercept, before each dose for the first 5 doses or longer if values are unstable, then periodically thereafter to determine if dosage adjustments are necessary. Do not initiate sotatercept in the setting of severe thrombocytopenia (platelet count less than 50,000/m3). Sotatercept may decrease platelet count. Thrombocytopenia occurred more often in individuals also receiving prostacyclin infusions. Severe thrombocytopenia may increase the risk of bleeding. Do not administer sotatercept in individuals experiencing serious bleeding.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Sotatercept may cause fetal harm if used during pregnancy, based on data from animal reproduction studies. There are no available data on the use of sotatercept during human pregnancy to inform a drug-associated risk of major birth defects, miscarriage, or adverse outcomes. Administration of sotatercept at exposures of 4-fold and 0.6-fold (based on AUC) of the maximum recommended human dose to pregnant rats and rabbits during organogenesis resulted in adverse developmental outcomes, including embryo-fetal mortality, alterations to growth, and structural variations. Individuals with pulmonary arterial hypertension are at an increased risk of adverse outcomes during pregnancy, including spontaneous abortion, premature labor, and intrauterine growth restriction. Report any sotatercept exposure during pregnancy to the manufacturer at 1-877-888-4231.

    erythrocytosis
    General Information
    Sotatercept may increase hemoglobin (Hgb) (i.e., erythrocytosis). Shifts in Hgb from normal to above normal concentrations occurred in 15.1% to 53% of subjects receiving sotatercept in clinical trials. Moderate elevations in Hgb more than 2 g/dL above the upper limit of normal (ULN) occurred in 15% of subjects receiving sotatercept; no elevations in Hgb of 4 g/dL or more above the ULN were observed. Monitor Hgb at the start of therapy, before each dose for at least the first 5 doses or longer if values are unstable, and periodically throughout treatment to determine if dose adjustments are needed. Delay sotatercept treatment by at least 3 weeks if Hgb increases to more than 2 g/dL from the previous value and is above ULN, Hgb increases to more than 4 g/dL from baseline, or Hgb increases to more than 2 g/dL above ULN. Recheck Hgb prior to restarting sotatercept. Treatment delays lasting more than 9 weeks require restarting at the initial dose and escalation after verifying Hgb and platelet count are acceptable. Severe erythrocytosis may increase the risk of thromboembolic events or hyperviscosity syndrome. Increases in Hgb were managed by dose reductions (6%), dose delays (10%), or both (5%).

    Headline
    bleeding, epistaxis, GI bleeding, hematemesis, intracranial bleeding, melena, thrombocytopenia
    General Information
    Sotatercept may decrease platelet count, and severe thrombocytopenia may increase the risk of bleeding. In clinical trials, shifts in platelet count from normal to below normal occurred in 25% of subjects receiving sotatercept compared with 16% of subjects receiving placebo. Severe thrombocytopenia (platelet count less than 50,000/mm3) occurred in 3% to 6% of sotatercept-treated subjects and was more common in individuals receiving concomitant prostacyclin infusion. Decreases in platelet count were managed by dose reduction (2%), dose delay (2%), or both (2%). Monitor platelet count prior to starting therapy, before each dose for at least the first 5 doses (or longer if values are unstable), and periodically thereafter to determine if dose adjustments are needed. Delay sotatercept treatment for at least 3 weeks if platelet count is below 50,000/mm3 and recheck platelet count before restarting therapy. If treatment is delayed for more than 9 weeks, restart sotatercept at the initial dose after confirming the hemoglobin and platelet counts are acceptable. Serious bleeding events, including GI bleeding and intracranial bleeding, occurred in 4% to 7% of sotatercept-treated subjects during clinical studies. Gingival bleeding occurred in 10.5% of subjects, and epistaxis occurred in 22.1% to 45.3%. Subjects who experienced serious bleeding were more likely to be on prostacyclin therapy and/or antithrombotic agents or have low platelet counts. Colonic angioectasia and GI bleeding - including GI hemorrhage, upper and lower GI hemorrhage, hematemesis, hematochezia, rectal hemorrhage, melena, and hemorrhagic gastritis - were reported in 1% to less than 10% of sotatercept-treated subjects.

Description
  Text
  Sotatercept is a subcutaneously administered activin signaling inhibitor indicated for the treatment of adults with pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH, WHO Group 1) to increase exercise capacity, improve WHO functional class and reduce the risk of clinical worsening events including hospitalization for PAH, lung transplantation, and death. Sotatercept may increase hemoglobin and decrease platelet count. Severe erythrocytosis may increase the risk of thromboembolic events or hyperviscosity syndrome, and severe thrombocytopenia may increase the risk of bleeding; monitoring of hemoglobin and platelet count before and during sotatercept therapy is required.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Sotatercept is a recombinant activin receptor type IIA-Fc fusion protein and activin signaling inhibitor that binds to activin A and TGF-beta superfamily ligands. As a result, sotatercept modulates vascular proliferation by improving the balance between the pro-proliferative and anti-proliferative signaling. In rat models of pulmonary arterial hypertension, a sotatercept analog reduced inflammation and inhibited proliferation of endothelial and smooth muscle cells in diseased vasculature. These cellular changes were associated with thinner vessel walls, partial reversal of right ventricular remodeling, and improved hemodynamics. In clinical trials, sotatercept treatment resulted in a statistically significant decrease from baseline in N-terminal pro b-type natriuretic peptide, pulmonary vascular resistance, and mean pulmonary artery pressure (mPAP) compared to placebo.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Subcutaneous Route
    Description
    After subcutaneous administration, the absolute bioavailability of sotatercept is approximately 66%. Steady-state geometric mean AUC and Cmax are 172 mcg x day/mL and 9.7 mcg/mL, respectively, after administration of 0.7 mg/kg/dose subcutaneously every 3 weeks in persons with pulmonary hypertension. AUC and Cmax increase proportionally with the dose. Steady-state is reached about 15 weeks after starting multiple dosing. The median time to Tmax is 7 days (range, 2 to 8 days) after multiple subcutaneous administration of sotatercept every 4 weeks.

Sotatercept is expected to be metabolized into small peptides by catabolic pathways. The effective half-life of sotatercept is approximately 24 days and its clearance is approximately 0.18 L/day.

      Name
      Subcutaneous Administration
      Description
      Select the appropriate sotatercept injection kit based on dose. If an individual's dose requires the use of two 45 mg vials or two 60 mg vials of lyophilized product, use a 2-vial kit instead of 2 individual 1-vial kits. A 2-vial kit includes instructions to combine the contents of 2 vials, which aids in measuring the proper dosage and eliminates the need for multiple injections.
Missed dose: Administer a missed dose as soon as possible. If the missed dose is not administered within 3 days of the scheduled dose, adjust the schedule to maintain 3-week dosing intervals.
 
Reconstitution
Remove the sotatercept injection kit from the refrigerator and wait 15 minutes to allow the prefilled syringe(s) and drug product to come to room temperature prior to preparation.
Hold the blue neck of the vial adapter and align the vial adapter on top of the vial; do not touch inside the vial adapter. Attach the vial adapter to the sotatercept vial.
Snap off the cap of the prefilled syringe of Sterile Water for Injection and attach to the vial adapter.
Inject all the Sterile Water for Injection from the attached syringe into the vial containing the lyophilized powder.
1 mL Sterile Water for Injection for sotatercept 45 mg vials
1.3 mL Sterile Water for Injection for sotatercept 60 mg vials
Gently swirl the vial to reconstitute the drug product to yield a final concentration of 50 mg/mL. Do not shake or vigorously agitate.
Allow the vial to stand for up to 3 minutes to allow bubbles to disappear.
If using a 2-vial kit, repeat the above steps for the second vial.
Withdraw the appropriate volume for injection, based on the individual's weight, from the vial using a syringe.
If the dose amount requires the use of 2 vials, withdraw the entire contents of the first vial and slowly transfer full contents into the second vial, and then withdraw the required amount of drug product. If necessary, remove excess drug product.
If necessary, remove excess air from the dosing syringe.
Storage: Use the reconstituted solution as soon as possible, but not later than 4 hours after reconstitution. Discard unused reconstituted solution.
 
Administration
Attach the injection needle to the dosing syringe.
Select the injection site on the abdomen (at least 2 inches away from navel), upper thigh, or upper arm that is not scarred, tender, or bruised, and swab with an alcohol wipe.
For self-administration or administration by a caregiver, use only the abdomen or upper thigh.
Pull the cap straight off the needle.
Gently pinch skin and hold a fold of skin, and insert the needle subcutaneously at a 45 to 90-degree angle. Push the plunger with slow, steady pressure all the way down until the dosing syringe is empty, then release the skin fold.
Activate the safety shield to cover the needle and discard the dosing syringe in a sharps disposal container.
Select a new site for each injection.
