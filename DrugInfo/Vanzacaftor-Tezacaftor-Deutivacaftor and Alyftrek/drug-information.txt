    Headline
    hepatic disease, hepatic failure, hepatotoxicity
    General Information
    Cases of serious and potentially fatal drug-induced liver injury/hepatotoxicity and hepatic failure have been reported in individuals who were taking a fixed-dose combination drug containing elexacaftor, tezacaftor, and ivacaftor, which contains the same or similar active ingredients as vanzacaftor; tezacaftor; deutivacaftor. After initiation of elexacaftor; tezacaftor; ivacaftor, liver injury was seen within the first month of therapy and up to 15 months later. Elevated liver enzymes have been observed in individuals taking vanzacaftor; tezacaftor; deutivacaftor. Do not use vanzacaftor; tezacaftor; deutivacaftor in individuals with severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh Class C). Vanzacaftor; tezacaftor; deutivacaftor is not recommended in individuals with moderate hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh Class B) and should only be considered if there is a clear medical need. Only use if the benefit outweighs the risk and monitor the individual closely. Assess liver function tests (AST, ALT, alkaline phosphatase, and bilirubin) prior to therapy, every month during the first 6 months of treatment, then every 3 months for the next 12 months, then at least annually thereafter. Monitor liver function more frequently in individuals with a history of hepatic disease or elevated liver function tests at baseline. If signs or symptoms of liver injury occur (ALT or AST more than 5 times the upper limit of normal (ULN), ALT or AST more than 3 times the ULN with bilirubin more than 2 times the ULN, jaundice, right upper quadrant pain, nausea, vomiting, altered mental status, ascites), interrupt vanzacaftor; tezacaftor; deutivacaftor therapy and consider a referral to a hepatologist. Follow individuals closely with clinical and laboratory monitoring until abnormalities resolve. Resume treatment if abnormalities resolve only if the benefit is expected to outweigh the risk. Monitor the individual closely if vanzacaftor; tezacaftor; deutivacaftor is reinitiated.

    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    There are no data available on the use of vanzacaftor; tezacaftor; deutivacaftor during pregnancy to evaluate for drug-associated risk of major birth defects, miscarriage, or other adverse outcomes. There are no animal data evaluating concomitant administration of vanzacaftor, tezacaftor, and deutivacaftor. In animal embryo-fetal development studies, oral administration of vanzacaftor during organogenesis demonstrated no adverse developmental effects at doses that produced exposures up to approximately 30 times (rats) and 22 times (rabbits) the exposure at the maximum recommended human dose (MRHD). Oral administration of tezacaftor to animals during organogenesis demonstrated no adverse developmental effects at doses that produced exposures up to approximately 3 times (rats) and 0.2 times (rabbits) the exposure at the MRHD based on summed AUCs of tezacaftor and the metabolite M1-TEZ. Deutivacaftor is a deuterated isotopologue of ivacaftor with a toxicity profile similar to ivacaftor. Oral administration of ivacaftor to animals during organogenesis demonstrated no adverse developmental effects at doses that produced exposures up to approximately 8 times (rats) and 9 times (rabbits) the exposure at the MRHD based on AUC. No adverse developmental effects were observed with oral administration of vanzacaftor, tezacaftor, or ivacaftor to rats during organogenesis through lactation at doses that produced exposures approximately 18 times, 1 time, and 8 times the exposure at the MRHD of each drug component, respectively. Placental transfer of vanzacaftor, tezacaftor, and ivacaftor was observed in animals.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Use vanzacaftor; tezacaftor; deutivacaftor with caution during breast-feeding. There are no data regarding the presence of vanzacaftor, tezacaftor, deutivacaftor, or their metabolites in human milk, their effects on the breastfed child, or their effects on milk production. Vanzacaftor, tezacaftor, and ivacaftor (an isotopologue of deutivacaftor) are present in the milk of lactating rats. Deutivacaftor has not been evaluated. Consider the benefits of breast-feeding, the patient's clinical need for treatment, and any potential adverse effects on the breast-fed child from the medication or from the patient's underlying medical condition.

Interactions
Abacavir; Dolutegravir; lamiVUDine: (Moderate) Monitor for increased toxicity of dolutegravir if coadministered with vanzacaftor; tezacaftor; deutivacaftor. Concurrent use may increase the plasma concentrations of dolutegravir. Dolutegravir is a BCRP and P-gp substrate and vanzacaftor; tezacaftor; deutivacaftor is a BCRP and P-gp inhibitor.
Adagrasib: (Major) Decrease the vanzacaftor; tezacaftor; deutivacaftor dosage during concomitant use of adagrasib; concomitant use may increase vanzacaftor; tezacaftor; deutivacaftor exposure and the risk for vanzacaftor; tezacaftor; deutivacaftor-related adverse effects. Specific dosing recommendations vary by age and weight. For all patients 12 years and older, and patients younger than 12 years weighing at least 40 kg, reduce the dose to 1 tablet of vanzacaftor 10 mg; tezacaftor 50 mg; deutivacaftor 125 mg once per week. For patients younger than 12 years weighing less than 40 kg, reduce the dose to 2 tablets of vanzacaftor 4 mg; tezacaftor 20 mg; deutivacaftor 50 mg once per week. Vanzacaftor; tezacaftor; deutivacaftor is a CYP3A substrate and adagrasib is a strong CYP3A inhibitor. Concomitant use with another strong CYP3A inhibitor increased vanzacaftor; tezacaftor; deutivacaftor overall exposure by 6.4-, 4.5-, and 11.1-fold, respectively. (Major) Reduce the dosing frequency of tezacaftor; ivacaftor when coadministered with adagrasib; coadministration may increase tezacaftor; ivacaftor exposure and adverse reactions. When combined, give one tezacaftor/ivacaftor combination tablet twice a week, approximately 3 to 4 days apart (i.e., Day 1 and Day 4). The evening dose of ivacaftor should not be taken. Both tezacaftor and ivacaftor are CYP3A substrates (ivacaftor is a sensitive substrate); adagrasib is a strong CYP3A inhibitor. Coadministration with another strong CYP3A inhibitor increased tezacaftor and ivacaftor exposure 4- and 15.6-fold, respectively.
Afatinib: (Moderate) If the concomitant use of vanzacaftor; tezacaftor; deutivacaftor and afatinib is necessary, monitor for afatinib-related adverse reactions. If the original dose of afatinib is not tolerated, consider reducing the daily dose of afatinib by 10 mg; resume the previous dose of afatinib as tolerated after discontinuation of vanzacaftor; tezacaftor; deutivacaftor. The manufacturer of afatinib recommends permanent discontinuation of therapy for severe or intolerant adverse drug reactions at a dose of 20 mg per day, but does not address a minimum dose otherwise. Afatinib is a P-gp substrate and vanzacaftor; tezacaftor; deutivacaftor is a P-gp inhibitor. Administration with another P-gp inhibitor, given 1 hour before a single dose of afatinib, increased afatinib exposure by 48%; there was no change in afatinib exposure when the P-gp inhibitor was administered at the same time as afatinib or 6 hours later. In healthy subjects, the relative bioavailability for AUC of afatinib was 119% when coadministered with the same P-gp inhibitor, and 111% when the inhibitor was administered 6 hours after afatinib.

    elevated hepatic enzymes, hepatic failure
    General Information
    Increased ALT (8%) and increased AST (7%) were reported in individuals 12 years and older receiving vanzacaftor; tezacaftor; deutivacaftor during clinical trials. When compared to individuals receiving elexacaftor; ivacaftor; tezacaftor (the same or similar active ingredients as vanzacaftor; tezacaftor; deutivacaftor) the incidence of elevated hepatic enzymes was 9% in individuals receiving vanzacaftor; tezacaftor; deutivacaftor compared to 7.1% in the elexacaftor; ivacaftor; tezacaftor group. Maximum ALT or AST elevation of more than 3 times the upper limit of normal (ULN), more than 5 times the ULN, and more than 8 times the ULN was 6%, 2.5%, and 1.3%, respectively, in the vanzacaftor; tezacaftor; deutivacaftor group vs. 3.1%, 1.2%, and 0.2%, respectively, in the elexacaftor; ivacaftor; tezacaftor group. In pediatric patients 6 to 11 years, the maximum ALT or AST elevation of more than 3 times the ULN, more than 5 times the ULN, and more than 8 times the ULN were 3.8%, 1.3%, and 0%, respectively. Cases of serious and potentially fatal drug-induced liver injury and hepatic failure were reported in individuals who were taking elexacaftor; tezacaftor, ivacaftor (which contains the same or similar active ingredients as vanzacaftor; tezacaftor; deutivacaftor). Liver injury has been reported within the first month of therapy and up to 15 months after initiation of elexacaftor; tezacaftor; ivacaftor. Assess liver function tests (ALT, AST, alkaline phosphatase, and bilirubin) in all individuals prior to initiating therapy with vanzacaftor; tezacaftor; deutivacaftor. Monitor LFTs every month during the first 6 months of treatment, then every 3 months for the next 12 months, then at least annually thereafter. Interrupt therapy for significant elevations in liver function tests or in the event of signs of symptoms of liver injury. Consider a referral to a hepatologist. Follow individuals closely with clinical laboratory monitoring until abnormalities resolve. If abnormalities resolve, resume treatment only if the benefits outweigh the risks and monitor closely.

Description
  Text
  Vanzacaftor; tezacaftor; deutivacaftor is a cystic fibrosis transmembrane conductance regulator (CFTR) modulator. It is indicated for the treatment of cystic fibrosis in individuals who have at least 1 variant in the CFTR gene that is either responsive to vanzacaftor; tezacaftor; deutivacaftor based on clinical and/or in vitro data or results in production of CFTR protein. It works by enhancing the function of the CFTR protein at the cell surface. This improves the flow of chloride and other ions in and out of the cells, which is needed for hydration and mucus consistency in organs like the lungs. In clinical trials, vanzacaftor; tezacaftor; deutivacaftor was non-inferior to elexacaftor; tezacaftor; ivacaftor in absolute change from baseline in percent predicted forced expiratory volume in 1 second. Vanzacaftor; tezacaftor; deutivacaftor also showed greater reduction in sweat chloride concentrations compared to elexacaftor; tezacaftor; ivacaftor. Elevated liver transaminases have been observed in individuals taking vanzacaftor; tezacaftor; deutivacaftor. Monitor liver function in all individuals prior to initiating, every month during the first 6 months, then every 3 months for the next 12 months, and then at least annually thereafter. Vanzacaftor; tezacaftor; deutivacaftor was FDA-approved in December 2024.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  The combination of vanzacaftor; tezacaftor; deutivacaftor increases the function of cystic fibrosis transmembrane conductance regulator (CFTR) at the cell surface. This results in increased CFTR activity as measured by CFTR mediated chloride transport in vitro and by sweat chloride in individuals with cystic fibrosis.
Vanzacaftor: Binds to a site on the CFTR protein (different from where tezacaftor binds) and has an additive effect in facilitating the cellular process and trafficking of select mutant forms of CFTR (including F508del-CFTR) to increase the amount of CFTR protein delivered to the cell surface compared to either molecule alone.
Tezacaftor: Binds to a site on the CFTR protein (different from where vanzacaftor binds) and has an additive effect in facilitating the cellular process and trafficking of select mutant forms of CFTR (including F508del-CFTR) to increase the amount of CFTR protein delivered to the cell surface compared to either molecule alone.
Deutivacaftor: Potentiates the channel open probability of the CFTR protein at the cell surface.
 
Pharmacodynamics
In clinical trials, vanzacaftor; tezacaftor, deutivacaftor demonstrated significant efficacy in reducing sweat chloride concentrations in cystic fibrosis (CF) individuals. In Trial 1, which included individuals who were heterozygous for the F508del and a minimum function variant, vanzacaftor; tezacaftor; deutivacaftor achieved a mean absolute change of -8.4 mmol/L in sweat chloride from baseline through Week 24, compared to elexacaftor; tezacaftor; ivacaftor. In Trial 2 involving individuals homozygous for the F508del variant, heterozygous for the F508del variant with gating or residual function variants, or at least 1 variant responsive to elexacaftor; tezacaftor; ivacaftor, the treatment difference of vanzacaftor; tezacaftor; deutivacaftor compared to elexacaftor; tezacaftor; ivacaftor for mean absolute change in sweat chloride from baseline to Week 24 was -2.8 mmol/L. In an open-label trial in pediatric patients (6 to 11 years) who had at least 1 responsive variant to elexacaftor; tezacaftor; ivacaftor, the mean absolute change in sweat chloride from baseline through Week 24 was -8.6 mmol/L.
