Drug Class: Sunitinib is an oral, multitargeted tyrosine kinase inhibitor with anti-angiogenic and anti-tumor activities due to selective inhibition of several receptor tyrosine kinases.

Routes: oral; Common adult doses: 87.5 mg/day PO for gastrointestinal stromal tumors and renal cell cancer; 62.5 mg/day PO for pancreatic neuroendocrine tumor.

Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  General Information
  Sunitinib may not be appropriate for patients with mannitol hypersensitivity, as the capsules contain mannitol.
 
Females of childbearing age should avoid becoming pregnant while taking sunitinib (FDA pregnancy risk category D). If the drug is used during pregnancy, or the patient becomes pregnant while receiving sunitinib, the woman should be apprised of the potential hazard to the fetus. There are no adequate and well-controlled studies of sunitinib in pregnant women. Following administration of sunitinib, inhibition of angiogenesis, which is critical component embryonic and fetal development, should be expected and may result in adverse effects on pregnancy. In animal studies, significant increases in incidence of embryolethality and structural abnormalities were observed in rats and rabbits at a doses that were between 0.3- to 5.5-times the systemic exposure of humans administered the daily recommended dose of sunitinib.
 
According to the manufacturer, sunitinib or breast-feeding should be discontinued because of the potential for serious adverse reactions in nursing infants from sunitinib. Excretion of sunitinib or of its primary active metabolite into human milk is unknown. Consider the benefits of breast-feeding, the risk of infant drug exposure, and the risk of an untreated or inadequately treated condition. If a breast-feeding infant experiences an adverse effect related to a maternally ingested drug, healthcare providers are encouraged to report the adverse effect to the FDA.
 
Left ventricular dysfunction and torsade de pointes, a life-threatening arrhythmia, have been reported in patients treated with sunitinib during clinical trials (see Adverse Reactions); sunitinib prolongs the QT interval in a dose dependent manner. Patients with cardiac events (e.g., myocardial infarction, severe/unstable angina, coronary/peripheral artery bypass graft, symptomatic congestive heart failure (CHF), cerebrovascular disease (e.g., stroke or transient ischemic attack), or pulmonary embolism) within 12 months prior to sunitinib administration were excluded from sunitinib clinical trials. The risk versus benefit of sunitinib therapy in patients with cardiac disease must be carefully considered. Patients with cardiac risk factors should be carefully monitored for clinical signs and symptoms of CHF including baseline and periodic evaluations of LVEF while receiving sunitinib. In patients without cardiac risk factors, a baseline ejection fraction should be considered. In the presence of clinical manifestations of CHF, discontinuation of sunitinib is recommended. The dose of sunitinib should be interrupted and/or reduced in patients without clinical evidence of CHF but an ejection fraction that is between 20—50% of baseline. Further, sunitinib may not be appropriate for patients at risk for QT prolongation such as patients with a history of QT prolongation; patients taking antiarrhythmics or strong CYP 3A4 inhibitors (see Drug Interactions); patients with cardiac disease; bradycardia; or electrolyte disturbances, especially hyperkalemia, hypokalemia, and hypomagnesemia.
 
Patients should be monitored for hypertension and treated as needed with standard anti-hypertensive therapy. In cases of severe hypertension, temporary suspension of sunitinib is recommended until hypertension is controlled.
 
Bone marrow suppression often occurs during sunitinib therapy. Neutropenia and thrombocytopenia have been reported in patients treated with sunitinib; regular monitoring of blood counts is recommended during therapy. Patients should be closely monitored for any signs or symptoms of bleeding, especially GI bleeding. Tumor-related hemorrhage has been observed in patients treated with sunitinib (see Adverse Reactions). These events may occur suddenly and, in the case of pulmonary tumors, may present as severe and life threatening hemoptysis or pulmonary hemorrhage. Fatal pulmonary hemorrhage occurred in 2 patients receiving sunitinib during a metastatic non-small cell lung cancer clinical trial. Both patients had squamous cell histology. Sunitinib is not FDA-approved for use in patients with non-small cell lung cancer. Patients with an active infection should be treated prior to receiving sunitinib when possible. Opportunistic infection, including viral infection and fungal infection, may occur in some patients due to severe immunosuppression. Patients with a history of varicella zoster, other herpes infection (e.g., herpes simplex), or other viral infection are at risk for reactivation of the infection when treated with chemotherapy. Patients should immediately report any symptoms of severe myelosuppression such as fever, sore throat, or abnormal bleeding.
 
Intramuscular injections should not be given to patients with a platelet count 18 mcg/dl) following stimulation. None of these patients were reported to have clinical evidence of adrenal insufficiency.
 
Vaccination during chemotherapy or radiation therapy should be avoided because the antibody response is suboptimal. When chemotherapy is being planned, vaccination should precede the initiation of chemotherapy by >= 2 weeks. Those undergoing chemotherapy should not be exposed to others who have recently received the oral poliovirus vaccine (OPV). Measles-mumps-rubella (MMR) vaccination is not contraindicated for the close contacts, including health care professionals, of immunocompromised patients. Passive immunoprophylaxis with immune globulins may be indicated for immunocompromised persons instead of, or in addition to, vaccination. When exposed to a vaccine-preventable disease such as measles, severely immunocompromised children should be considered susceptible regardless of their vaccination history.
 
Sunitinib may cause hypothyroidism (see Adverse Reactions). Assessment of thyroid function is recommended in all patients before sunitinib initiation. Patients with hypothyroidism and hyperthyroidism should be treated as per standard medical practice before sunitinib is started.
 
Although no sunitinib dose adjustment is needed for patients with mild or moderate hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh Class A or B), no data are available for patients with severe hepatic disease (Child-Pugh Class C). Patients with either an ALT or AST > 2.5 times the upper limit of normal (ULN) or > 5 times the ULN if due to liver metastases were excluded from cancer trials. Patients with severe hepatic disease or hepatic encephalopathy may not be appropriate candidates for sunitinib receipt; sunitinib and its primary active metabolite are primarily metabolized by the liver, and sunitinib causes dose-dependent QT prolongation.

    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components.
    Headline
    hepatotoxicity
    General Information
    Hepatotoxicity has occurred with sunitinib use in clinical trials which may result in liver failure or death. The safety of sunitinib in patients with ALT or AST greater than 2.5 times the upper limit of normal (ULN), or greater than 5 times ULN if due to liver metastases, has not been established. Monitor liver function tests (i.e., ALT, AST, bilirubin) at baseline, during each treatment cycle, and as clinically indicated; an interruption of therapy, dose reduction, or discontinuation of therapy may be necessary if hepatotoxicity occurs.
    Headline
    diabetes mellitus
    General Information
    Sunitinib can cause symptomatic hypoglycemia which may result in loss of consciousness or hospitalization; reductions in blood glucose may be worse in patients with diabetes mellitus. Monitor blood glucose levels in all patients at baseline, regularly during treatment, as clinically indicated, and after discontinuation of sunitinib. Adjustments in medications for diabetes may be necessary to minimize the risk of hypoglycemia. In patients with pancreatic neuroendocrine tumors (pNET), pre-existing abnormalities in blood glucose were not present in all patients who experienced hypoglycemia.
    Headline
    major surgery, planned procedure
    General Information
    Do not administer sunitinib for at least 3 weeks prior to a planned procedure or elective surgery. After major surgery, wait at least 2 weeks and until adequate wound healing prior to restarting sunitinib therapy. Impaired wound healing can occur in patients who receive drugs that inhibit the vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) signaling pathway such as sunitinib. The safety of resuming treatment after resolution of wound healing complications has not been established.
    Headline
    heart failure, thoracic radiation therapy
    General Information
    Use sunitinib with caution in patients with a history of heart failure, or in patients with conditions that could impair left ventricular (LV) function including coronary artery disease, uncontrolled hypertension, thoracic radiation therapy and previous anthracycline exposure. Cardiovascular events, including heart failure, cardiomyopathy, myocardial ischemia, and myocardial infarction, some of which were fatal, have been reported with therapy. Consider monitoring the LV ejection fraction at baseline and periodically as clinically indicated; carefully monitor for clinical signs and symptoms of congestive heart failure (CHF). An interruption of therapy, dose reduction, or discontinuation of therapy may be necessary for a decrease in ejection fraction. Patients with cardiac events within 12 months of sunitinib therapy including myocardial infarction, severe/unstable angina, coronary/peripheral artery bypass graft, symptomatic CHF, cerebrovascular accident or transient ischemic attack, or pulmonary embolism were excluded from clinical trials; patients with prior anthracycline use or cardiac radiation were also excluded from some studies. It is unknown whether these conditions may increase the risk of LV dysfunction.
    Headline
    bradycardia, cardiomyopathy, congenital long QT syndrome, coronary artery disease, females, geriatric, hypocalcemia, hypokalemia, hypomagnesemia, QT prolongation
    General Information
    Use sunitinib with caution in people with baseline QT prolongation or who have conditions that may increase the risk of QT prolongation or torsade de pointes, including bradycardia, congenital long QT syndrome, hypocalcemia, hypokalemia, hypomagnesemia, geriatric adults, females, structural abnormalities that interfere with electrical conduction (e.g., cardiomyopathy, coronary artery disease, ischemic heart disease), or in those who have other additional risk factors for QT prolongation or torsade de pointes. The use of other medications that have been associated with QT prolongation or torsade de pointes may further increase risk. Monitor patients at increased risk for QT prolongation. Consider periodic monitoring of electrocardiograms and electrolytes (i.e., magnesium, potassium) in all patients); monitor more frequently when sunitinib is administered concomitantly with a strong CYP3A4 inhibitor or with other medications known to prolong the QT interval. Consider reducing the dose of sunitinib.
    Headline
    hypertension
    General Information
    Use sunitinib with caution in patients with hypertension; sunitinib frequently causes increases in blood pressure. Monitor blood pressure at baseline and as clinically indicated during treatment; initiate and/or adjust antihypertensive treatment as necessary. An interruption of therapy, dose reduction, or discontinuation of therapy may be necessary if hypertension occurs.
    Headline
    bleeding disorder
    General Information
    Use sunitinib with caution in patients with a bleeding disorder. Sunitinib treatment can cause serious cases of bleeding, including tumor-related hemorrhage, which may be fatal. Permanently discontinue sunitinib in patients who experience severe or life-threatening hemorrhage.
    Headline
    dental disease, dental work
    General Information
    Avoid invasive dental work while on sunitinib therapy if possible, especially in patients at higher risk for osteonecrosis of the jaw (ONJ) including those with concomitant exposure to bisphosphonates. If unavoidable, withhold sunitinib therapy for at least 3 weeks prior to scheduled dental surgery or invasive dental work. Use sunitinib with caution in patients with dental disease due to the risk of ONJ. Perform an oral examination prior to initiation of sunitinib therapy and periodically during treatment; advise patients regarding good oral hygiene practices. If ONJ occurs, hold sunitinib treatment until complete resolution; the safety of resuming sunitinib therapy after resolution of ONJ has not been established.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Sunitinib can cause fetal harm if used during pregnancy, based on its mechanism of action and data from animal studies. In rats and rabbits, administration of sunitinib during organogenesis resulted in teratogenicity (embryo-lethality, craniofacial malformations, and skeletal malformations) at exposures approximately 5.5 and 0.3 times, respectively, the combined AUC exposure (sunitinib plus its active metabolite) at the recommended daily dose. Advise pregnant individuals of the potential fetal risk.
    Headline
    people who can cause pregnancy in others, people who may become pregnant, reproductive risk
    General Information
    Counsel people who may become pregnant and people who can cause pregnancy in others about the reproductive risk associated with sunitinib. Sunitinib can cause fetal harm. Verify pregnancy status prior to treatment initiation. Advise people who may become pregnant to use effective contraception while taking sunitinib and for at least 4 weeks after the last dose. For those who can cause pregnancy in others, contraception should be used during sunitinib treatment and for 7 weeks after the last dose. Based on animal data, sunitinib may impair male and female fertility.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Advise patients to avoid breast-feeding while taking sunitinib and for at least 4 weeks after the last dose. Sunitinib has the potential to cause serious side effects in the breastfed child. There are no data on the presence of sunitinib or its metabolites in human milk, their effects on the breastfed child, or their effects on milk production. Sunitinib is present in animal milk.

Interactions
Adagrasib: (Major) Avoid concomitant use of adagrasib and sunitinib due to the potential for increased sunitinib exposure and additive risk for QT/QTc prolongation and torsade de pointes (TdP). If use is necessary, consider reducing the daily dose of sunitinib to a minimum of 37.5 mg for patients with GIST or RCC, and to a minimum of 25 mg for patients with pNET. Additionally, consider taking steps to minimize the risk for QT prolongation and TdP, such as electrolyte monitoring and repletion and ECG monitoring. Sunitinib is a CYP3A substrate, adagrasib is a strong CYP3A inhibitor, and both medications have been associated with QT interval prolongation. Coadministration with another strong CYP3A inhibitor increased exposure to sunitinib and its primary active metabolite by 51%.
Alfuzosin: (Moderate) Use caution and monitor for evidence of QT prolongation if sunitinib is administered with alfuzosin. Sunitinib can prolong the QT interval. Alfuzosin may also prolong the QT interval in a dose-dependent manner.
Amiodarone: (Major) Concomitant use of amiodarone and sunitinib increases the risk of QT/QTc prolongation and torsade de pointes (TdP). Avoid concomitant use if possible, especially in patients with additional risk factors for TdP. Consider taking steps to minimize the risk for QT/QTc interval prolongation and TdP, such as electrolyte monitoring and repletion and ECG monitoring, if concomitant use is necessary. Due to the extremely long half-life of amiodarone, a drug interaction is possible for days to weeks after drug discontinuation.
Amisulpride: (Major) Monitor ECGs for QT prolongation when amisulpride is administered with sunitinib. Amisulpride causes dose- and concentration- dependent QT prolongation. Sunitinib can prolong the QT interval.
Amoxicillin; Clarithromycin; Omeprazole: (Major) Avoid coadministration of clarithromycin with sunitinib due to increased sunitinib exposure as well as the risk of QT prolongation and torsade de pointes (TdP). Sunitinib is a CYP3A4 substrate that can cause QT prolongation. Clarithromycin is a strong CYP3A4 inhibitor associated with an established risk for QT prolongation and TdP. Coadministration with another strong CYP3A4 inhibitor increased exposure to sunitinib and its primary active metabolite by 51%.

Adverse Reactions
  Headline
  Adverse Reactions
  General Information
  Sunitinib prolongs the QT interval in a dose dependent manner, and torsade de pointes occurred in 20% from baseline and to below 50%. Left ventricular dysfunction was reported in 3 patients and CHF was reported in one patient. Patients with cardiac events within 12 months prior to sunitinib therapy were not included in clinical trials; it is unknown if patients with underlying cardiac disease may be at higher risk of developing drug-induced left ventricular dysfunction (see Contraindications/Precautions). An observational study of tyrosine kinase inhibitor treatment in patients with metastatic renal cell carcinoma revealed a possible increased risk for the development of cardiac events. Eighty-six patients who received sorafenib or sunitinib were observed for the development of heart failure, symptomatic arrhythmia, elevated cardiac enzymes, or acute coronary syndrome. Patients were included without regard to performance status or pre-existing cardiac condition. Cardiac events occurred in 11 out of 44 patients who received sunitinib. Six patients had a decrease in left ventricular ejection fraction and 6 experienced electrocardiogram changes at the time of event. Additionally, cardiac events occurred in 14 out of 56 patients who received sorafenib. Three of those patients had a decrease in left ventricular ejection fraction and 6 experienced electrocardiogram changes at the time of event. After treatment discontinuation and medical intervention, all patients with cardiac events were medically cleared to resume treatment. Overall survival was not significantly different in patients that experienced an event and those that did not. Monitor patients for signs and symptoms of heart failure. In the presence of clinical manifestations of CHF, discontinuation of sunitinib therapy is recommended. The dose of sunitinib should be interrupted and/or reduced in patients without clinical evidence of CHF but an ejection fraction that is between 20—50% of baseline.
 
Bleeding is one of the most common adverse events to sunitinib with bleeding events occurring in 37/202 (18%) gastrointestinal stromal tumors (GIST) patients participating in the randomized trial who received sunitinib; epistaxis was the most common hemorrhagic adverse event reported. In patients receiving sunitinib for treatment-naive metastatic renal cell carcinoma (MRCC), 112/375 patients (30%) had bleeding events. Less common bleeding events in MRCC or GIST patients treated with sunitinib included rectal bleeding, gingival bleeding, upper GI bleeding, genital bleeding, and wound bleeding. Most events in MRCC patients were Grade 1 or 2; there was one Grade 5 event (gastric bleed). In the GIST randomized trial, 14/202 patients (7%) receiving sunitinib had Grade 3 or 4 bleeding events. Tumor-related hemorrhage has been observed in patients treated with sunitinib. These events may occur suddenly, and in the case of pulmonary tumors may present as severe and life threatening hemoptysis or pulmonary hemorrhage. Fatal pulmonary hemorrhage was reported in 2 non-small cell lung cancer patients treated with sunitinib; sunitinib is not FDA-approved for the treatment of lung cancer. Both patients had squamous cell histology. Treatment-related Grade 3 and 4 tumor hemorrhage occurred in 5 of 202 (3%) patients receiving sunitinib in the GIST randomized trial. Tumor hemorrhages were noted as early as cycle 1 and as late as cycle 6. One of these 5 patients received no further treatment; none of the other 4 patients discontinued treatment or experienced a dose delay due to tumor hemorrhage. Tumor hemorrhage has not been reported during clinical trials in patients with MRCC. At higher sunitinib doses (>= 75 mg/day), tumor responses were often associated with reduced tumor vascularization and tumor necrosis, causing in organ perforation or fistula. Perform a complete blood count with platelet count and serum chemistries including phosphate at the beginning of each sunitinib treatment cycle.
 
Serious, sometimes fatal, gastrointestinal (GI) complications including GI perforation have occurred rarely in patients with intraabdominal malignancies treated with sunitinib.
 
Reductions in neutrophils, lymphocytes (lymphopenia), platelets, and hemoglobin are common. Among 202 patients who received sunitinib for gastrointestinal stromal tumors (GIST), 53% had reductions in neutrophils, 38% had reductions in lymphocytes, 38% had reductions in platelets, and 26% had reductions in hemoglobin (anemia). Among 375 patients who received sunitinib for metastatic renal cell cancer (MRCC), 77% had reductions in neutrophils, 78% had reductions in leukocytes, 68% had reductions in lymphocytes, 68% had reductions in platelets, and 79% had reductions in hemoglobin. Grade 4 neutropenia was reported in 2% of sunitinib-treated patients in the MRCC trial and in 2% of sunitinib-treated patients in the GIST randomized trial. Febrile neutropenia has also been reported. Grade 4 thrombocytopenia was reported in 1% of patients with GIST receiving sunitinib. Similarly, in a phase III trial of previously untreated patients with MRCC, comparing sunitinib with interferon alpha-2a, grade 3 or 4 neutropenia (18% vs. 9%) and thrombocytopenia (9% vs. 1%), as well as leukopenia (8% vs. 2%), were significantly more common among sunitinib treated patients (p 18 mcg/dl) following stimulation. Adrenal toxicity was noted in rats and monkeys exposed to sunitinib at plasma exposures as low as 0.7 times the AUC observed in clinical studies. Histological changes of the adrenal gland were characterized as hemorrhage, necrosis, congestion, hypertrophy, and inflammation. No evidence of adrenal hemorrhage or necrosis was found by CT/MRI obtained in 336 patients after exposure to one or more cycles of sunitinib. Monitor adrenal function in case of stress such as surgery, trauma, or severe infection.
 
Hypertension (all grades) was reported in 31/202 (15%) gastrointestinal stromal tumors (GIST) patients participating in the randomized trial who received sunitinib. Grade 3 hypertension was reported in 9 (4%) GIST patients; no grade 4 hypertension was reported. Of patients receiving sunitinib for treatment-naive metastatic renal cell carcinoma (MRCC), 111/375 patients (30%) experienced hypertension; grade 3 hypertension was observed in 12% of the 375 patients. Sunitinib dosing was reduced or temporarily delayed in none of the patients receiving sunitinib in the GIST randomized trial and in 18 of the MRCC patients. Two MRCC patients, including one with malignant hypertension, discontinued treatment due to hypertension. Severe hypertension (> 200 mg Hg systolic or > 110 mg Hg diastolic) occurred in 8/202 (4%) GIST patients receiving sunitinib and in 20/375 (5%) MRCC patients. Similarly, in a phase III trial of previously untreated patients with MRCC, comparing sunitinib with interferon alpha-2a, hypertension was significantly more common among sunitinib treated patients (12% for sunitinib vs. 1% for interferon, p= 75 mg/day) are associated with worsening and more severe dermatologic effects.
 
Electrolyte disturbances were reported in gastrointestinal stromal tumors (GIST) patients treated with sunitinib during the randomized clinical trial. These abnormalities included increased creatinine/azotemia (12%), hypokalemia (12%), hyperkalemia (6%), hypernatremia (10%), hyponatremia (6%), and hypophosphatemia (9%). Grade 4 abnormalities in serum creatinine occurred in 1% and in potassium occurred in 1% of patients. Among 375 patients in the metastatic renal cell cancer trial, 70% had increases in serum creatinine, 46% had abnormalities in uric acid (hyperuricemia), 49% had abnormalities in creatine kinase, and 31% had hypophosphatemia. Further, hypocalcemia (35%), hypoglycemia (19%), hyperglycemia (15%), hyponatremia (14%), hyperkalemia (11%), and hypernatremia (11%) occurred. Less than 1% had grade 4 abnormalities in the serum phosphorus, potassium, calcium, or sodium concentration. Grade 4 abnormalities in uric acid occurred in 12%.
 
Cases of hyperthyroidism, some followed by hypothyroidism, have been reported in clinical trials and through post-marketing experience. Acquired hypothyroidism was noted in 8 (4%) gastrointestinal stromal tumors (GIST) patients treated with sunitinib during the randomized clinical trial. Hypothyroidism was reported as an adverse reaction in 51 patients (14%) in the treatment-naive metastatic renal cell carcinoma (MRCC) study; some patients with no prior history of hypothyroidism were started on thyroid replacement therapy while on study. Of 42 patients in a phase 1/2 study, 15 developed hypothyroidism after a mean of 50 weeks (range, 12—94 weeks) of sunitinib receipt; all patients had a normal TSH concentration at baseline and had TSH normalization with typical levothyroxine doses. An additional 7 patients had at least 1 TSH concentration of 5—7 mU/L that subsequently normalized, and 4 patients had TSH suppression but discontinued the study before performance of thyroid function studies. Of note, 6 of the 15 patients with hypothyroidism had at least 1 TSH concentration = 50 mg/day. Asthenia appears to progressively worsen from Grade 1 during the first 2 weeks and to Grade 3 to 4 during weeks 3 and 4 of each cycle. It was associated with an increased length of rest period during daytime and sleepiness at night. Asthenia was fully reversible during the 2-week off cycle period of each cycle. Grade 3 to 4 asthenia occurred in 6 of 12 patients at 75—100mg/day but was restricted to 2 of 9 patients at the recommended dose of 50 mg/day.
 
In patients with metastatic renal cell carcinoma (MRCC) and gastrointestinal stromal tumors (GIST) treated with sunitinib, the following adverse reactions were reported: headache (18%), arthralgia (18%), back pain (17% MRCC, 3% had grade 3 or 4), myalgia/limb pain (14% GIST/17% MRCC), dyspnea (15% MRCC), cough (18% MRCC ), dizziness (7% MRCC), and dehydration (8% MRCC). During the postmarketing surveillance period, myopathy and/or rhabdomyolysis with or without acute renal failure have been reported. Of note, in a clinical trial, 49% of 375 sunitinib recipients had elevated creatine kinase concentrations as compared with 12% of 360 interferon alfa recipients. Manage patients with signs or symptoms of muscle toxicity as per standard medical practice. Urine discoloration (yellow) has been associated with transient skin coloration due to excretion of the drug and metabolites.
 
Proteinuria and nephrotic syndrome have been reported during the postmarketing surveillance period. A baseline urinalysis is recommended before sunitinib administration. Monitor patients for the development or worsening of proteinuria. The safety of continuing sunitinib in patients with moderate to severe proteinuria has not been systematically evaluated. Discontinue sunitinib in patients with nephrotic syndrome.
 
In clinical studies of sunitinib, seizures have been observed in patients with radiological evidence of brain metastasis. In addition, there have been rare (< 1%) reports of subjects presenting with seizures and radiological evidence of reversible posterior leukoencephalopathy syndrome (RPLS). None of these patients had a fatal outcome to the event. Patients with seizures and signs/symptoms consistent with RPLS such as hypertension, headache, decreased alertness, altered mental functioning, and visual loss including cortical blindness, should be controlled with medical management including control of hypertension. Temporary suspension of sunitinib is recommended; following resolution, treatment may be restarted if appropriate.
 
Five cases of microangiopathic hemolytic anemia (MAHA) have been reported among 12 patients receiving bevacizumab (10 mg/kg IV every 2 weeks) and sunitinib (50 mg PO once daily for 4 weeks, with 2 weeks off) in a phase I dose-escalation trial. Two cases were considered severe with evidence of thrombocytopenia, anemia, reticulocytosis, reductions in serum haptoglobin, schistocytes on peripheral smear, modest serum creatinine increases, severe hypertension, reversible posterior leukoencephalopathy syndrome, and proteinuria. The condition was reversible within three weeks of discontinuation of both drugs and did not require additional intervention. The concomitant use of sunitinib and bevacizumab is not recommended.

Description
  Text
  Sunitinib is an oral, multitargeted tyrosine kinase inhibitor with anti-angiogenic and anti-tumor activities due to selective inhibition of several receptor tyrosine kinases. Activation of gene pathways by hypoxia, such as vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) and epidermal growth factor (EGF), are common in solid tumors. These genes are implicated in tumor angiogenesis and proliferation. It is indicated for the treatment of progressive GI stromal tumor (GIST), adjuvant renal cell carcinoma (RCC) following nephrectomy in patients at high risk of recurrence, advanced RCC, and progressive pancreatic neuroendocrine tumor (pNET). Sunitinib carries a black-box warning for hepatotoxicity, which may be severe and in some cases fatal. Monitor hepatic function and interrupt, reduce, or discontinue dosing as recommended.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Sunitinib is an oral small molecule inhibitor of multiple receptor tyrosine kinases (RTKs) implicated in tumor growth, pathologic angiogenesis, and metastatic progression of cancer including platelet-derived growth factor receptors (PDGFR-alpha and PDGFR-beta), vascular endothelial growth factor receptors (VEGFR1, VEGFR2, and VEGFR3), stem cell factor receptor (KIT), Fms-like tyrosine kinase-3 (FLT3), colony stimulating factor receptor Type 1 (CSF-1R), and the glial cell-line derived neurotrophic factor receptor (RET). The inhibition of the activity of these RTKs by sunitinib has been demonstrated in biochemical and cellular assays, and the inhibition of function has been demonstrated in cell proliferation assays. Sunitinib inhibited the phosphorylation of PDGFR-beta, VEGFR2, and KIT in tumor xenografts expressing RTK targets in vivo. It demonstrated inhibition of tumor growth or tumor regression and/or inhibited metastases in some experimental models of cancer. Sunitinib inhibited the growth of tumor cells expressing dysregulated PDGFR, RET, and KIT in vitro, and inhibited PDGFR-beta- and VEGFR2-dependent tumor angiogenesis in vivo. The primary metabolite of sunitinib exhibits similar potency in biochemical and cellular assays.
 
Several adverse reactions of sunitinib (i.e., hair and skin discoloration) are associated with the multiple signaling pathways inhibition including KIT, PDGRF, and VEGFR. The graying of hair seen in patients may be a potential surrogate marker to monitor sunitinib biologic effects.

  Text
  Sunitinib is administered orally. Sunitinib and its primary active metabolite are 95% and 90% bound to human plasma protein in vitro, respectively, in a non-concentration dependent manner over the range of 100 ng/mL to 4,000 ng/mL. The apparent volume of distribution (Vd/F) for sunitinib was 2,230 L. After administration of a single oral dose to healthy volunteers, the terminal half-life of sunitinib is 40 to 60 hours; the terminal half-life of the primary active metabolite is 80 to 110 hours. With repeated daily administration, sunitinib accumulates 3- to 4-fold while the primary active metabolite accumulates 7- to 10-fold. Steady-state concentrations are achieved within 10 to 14 days; by day 14, combined plasma concentrations of sunitinib and its active metabolite ranged from 63 to 101 ng/mL. No significant changes in the pharmacokinetics of sunitinib or the active metabolite were observed with repeated daily administration or with repeated cycles. Sunitinib is primarily eliminated via the feces (61%), with 16% of an administered dose eliminated renally. In a human mass balance study of radiolabeled sunitinib, sunitinib and its active metabolite were the major compounds identified in pooled samples of plasma (92%), urine (86.4%), and feces (73.8%). Minor metabolites were identified in urine and feces but generally not found in plasma. Total oral clearance (CL/F) ranged from 34 L/hour to 62 L/hour, with an interpatient variability of 40%.
 
The pharmacokinetics in healthy volunteers were similar to the solid tumor patient populations tested, including patients with GI stromal tumor (GIST) and renal cell carcinoma (RCC). No significant changes in the pharmacokinetics of sunitinib or the primary active metabolite were observed with repeated daily administration or repeated cycles.
 
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes and drug transporters: CYP3A4
Sunitinib is metabolized by CYP3A4 to its primary active metabolite, which is further metabolized by CYP3A4; the primary active metabolite comprises 23% to 37% of total exposure.

Dosage Adjustment Guidelines
  Hepatic Impairment
  Baseline Hepatic Impairment
Mild to moderate hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh class A or B): No dose adjustment is required.
Severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh class C): Sunitinib has not been studied in these patients.
 
Treatment-Related Hepatotoxicity
Grade 3: Upon resolution to grade 1 or less or baseline, resume sunitinib at a reduced dose. Discontinue adjuvant sunitinib for patients with RCC and grade 3 hepatotoxicity at a reduced dose of 37.5 mg once daily. Discontinue sunitinib for patients with advanced RCC, GIST, or pNET and grade 3 hepatotoxicity at a reduced dose of 25 mg once daily. Permanently discontinue sunitinib for recurrent grade 3 hepatotoxicity despite dose reductions.
Grade 4: Permanently discontinue sunitinib.
  Renal Impairment
  Mild, moderate, or severe renal impairment (CrCl 80 mL/min or less), not on hemodialysis: No dose adjustment is recommended.
End-stage renal disease (ESRD) on hemodialysis: No starting dose adjustment is recommended. However, given the decreased exposure compared to patients with normal renal function, subsequent doses may be increased gradually up to 2-fold based on safety and tolerability.
