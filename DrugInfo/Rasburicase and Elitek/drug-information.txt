Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  General Information
  Rasburicase is contraindicated for use by patients with a known history of anaphylaxis or hypersensitivity reactions to rasburicase or to any of the excipients. Rasburicase should be immediately and permanently discontinued in any patient who develops clinical evidence of a serious hypersensitivity reaction. Patients with a mannitol hypersensitivity or yeast hypersensitivity may not be appropriate candidates for rasburicase. In each rasburicase vial, there is 10.6 mg of mannitol, and the drug is produced by a genetically modified Saccharomyces cerivisiae strain. Patients should be closely monitored for the onset of allergic-type undesirable effects such as hypotension, urticaria, acute bronchospasm, angina, or angioedema. Caution should be used in patients with a history of atopy or asthma, since they were excluded from clinical trials due to the protein nature of the drug and the allergic reactions noted with use of the non-recombinant product, which has been available outside the US.
 
Rasburicase is contraindicated for use in patients with a known history of hemolytic reactions or methemoglobinemia reactions to rasburicase or to any of the excipients. Rasburicase should be immediately and permanently discontinued in any patient who develops hemolytic or methemoglobinemia reactions to rasburicase or to any of the excipients. Rasburicase has been associated with hemolysis and thus, is contraindicated in patients with glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase (G6PD) deficiency. Patients with anemia before rasburicase receipt will likely have further reductions in their hemoglobin concentration if hemolysis occurs. Hydrogen peroxide, one of the by-products of breakdown of uric acid to allantoin, can induce hemolytic anemia or methemoglobinemia in patients with G6PD deficiency. Patients at higher risk of G6PD deficiency, such as black patients or patients of African or Mediterranean ancestry, should be screened prior to rasburicase administration. It is unknown if patients with deficiencies of cytochrome b5 reductase or of other enzymes with antioxidant activity are at an increased risk for methemoglobinemia or hemolytic anemia. Rasburicase should be administered with caution to patients with methemoglobin reductase deficiency. Appropriate patient monitoring and support measures such as transfusions or methylene-blue administration in the case of methemoglobinemia should be initiated.
 
Rasburicase should be used with caution in patients with bone marrow suppression or neutropenia, since hemolysis and neutropenia were noted in clinical trials.
 
Rasburicase is a FDA pregnancy risk category C drug. It is unknown if rasburicase can cause fetal harm or affect reproductive capacity. A pregnant women should receive rasburicase only if clearly needed. It is unknown whether or not rasburicase is excreted into breast milk. Due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in nursing babies, either breast-feeding or rasburicase should be discontinued. The importance of the drug to the mother should be considered.
 
There are insufficient data to determine the efficacy and safety of rasburicase in neonates, infants, and elderly patients. Seven infants between 1 and 6 months of age were included in clinical trials. Due to the small number of patients in this age range, their data was pooled into the < 2 year old group. Patients < 2 years of age had a higher mean uric acid AUC than children aged 2—17 years. A lower percentage of patients < 2 years of age also had a lower rate of success at achieving maintenance uric acid concentration by 48 hours (83%) than did the older pediatric patients (93%). In addition to the suggested lower efficacy of rasburicase in children < 2 years of age, a greater percentage of patients in this group experienced side effects like nausea, diarrhea, fever, and rash than did the older pediatric group.
 
Rasburicases will cause enzymatic degradation of the uric acid with blood samples left at room temperature, resulting in spuriously low uric acid levels. To ensure accurate measurements, blood must be collected into pre-chilled tubes containing heparin anticoagulant and immediately immersed and maintained in an ice water bath; plasma samples must be assayed within 4 hours of sample collection.

    laboratory test interference
    General Information
    Rasburicase causes laboratory test interference with uric acid measurements by enzymatically degrading uric acid in blood/plasma/serum samples left at room temperature; this results in low plasma uric acid assay readings. Special sample handling procedure must be followed to avoid ex vivo uric acid degradation.
    Headline
    serious hypersensitivity reactions or anaphylaxis
    General Information
    Rasburicase can cause serious hypersensitivity reactions or anaphylaxis; fatal reactions have been reported. Rasburicase is contraindicated in patients with a known history of anaphylaxis or hypersensitivity reactions to rasburicase or any component of the product. Permanently discontinue rasburicase if a patient experiences signs or symptoms of a severe hypersensitivity reaction such as acute bronchospasm, chest pain/tightness, dyspnea, hypoxia, hypotension, shock, and urticaria.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Rasburicase may cause fetal harm if used during pregnancy, based on data from animal studies. Rasburicase use has not been evaluated in pregnant women; weigh the benefits of therapy with the potential risk to the fetus in these patients. Multiple heart and great vessel malformations were observed in the offspring of pregnant rats who received IV rasburicase doses that resulted in 100 times the exposure seen with the recommended human dose. Additionally, decreased fetal body weights and heart and great vessel malformations occurred in the offspring of pregnant rabbits who received IV rasburicase doses that resulted in 5 to 54 times the exposure seen with the recommended human dose.
    Headline
    G6PD deficiency, hemolysis, history of significant adverse event(s) associated with previous use, methemoglobinemia
    General Information
    Rasburicase is contraindicated in patients with a history of significant adverse event(s) associated with previous use (e.g., hemolysis or methemoglobinemia) and in patients with glucose-6 phosphate dehydrogenase (G6PD) deficiency. Patients who have a high risk of G6PD deficiency, such as patients of African or Mediterranean ancestry, should be screened prior to starting rasburicase. Permanently discontinue rasburicase if a patient experiences hemolysis or methemoglobinemia; start appropriate monitoring and medical management (e.g., transfusion support, methylene blue).
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Advise patients that breast-feeding is not recommended during rasburicase treatment and for 2 weeks after the last dose due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in the breast-fed child. There are no data on the presence of rasburicase in human milk, the effects on the breast-fed child, or the effects on milk production.

Adverse Reactions
  Headline
  Adverse Reactions
  General Information
  Rasburicase may cause severe hypersensitivity reactions including anaphylactic shock and anaphylactoid reactions. Of the 703 patients for whom data on adverse events were available from clinical trials, anaphylaxis occurred in less than 1% of patients. Chest pain (unspecified), urticaria, rash (unspecified), edema, pruritus, hypotension, and respiratory distress such as dyspnea, wheezing, or bronchospasm may be signs or symptoms of allergic reactions. Approximately 1% of the 703 patients in clinical trials developed a rash. A severe rash, as defined by the National Cancer Institute Common Toxicity Criteria, developed in one patient. Severe hypersensitivity reactions may occur at any point during rasburicase treatment including initial receipt. Immediately and permanently discontinue rasburicase if a serious allergic reaction occurs.In clinical studies, two patients developed severe hemolysis within 2—4 days after rasburicase initiation. One of these patients was found to be G6PD deficient. Methemoglobinemia was reported in 2 patients that received rasburicase during clinical studies. The patients developed serious hypoxia that required medical intervention. Hemolysis or methemoglobinemia developed in less than 1% of 703 patients for which data on adverse events were available from clinical trials. Rasburicase should be permanently discontinued in any patient that develops either hemolysis or methemoglobinemia.The most common adverse events of any severity reported in clinical trials were nausea/vomiting, fever, headache, abdominal pain, constipation and diarrhea. A greater percentage of patients < 2 years of age versus patients aged 2 -17 years experienced vomiting (75% vs. 55%), diarrhea (63% vs. 20%), and fever (50% vs. 38%). Rasburicase was given before and along with anti-tumor therapy, which consisted of either systemic chemotherapy or steroids.Rasburicase is immunogenic and has elicited antibody formation. Immunogenicity to rasburicase may occur following the initial treatment course. Up to a 28-day period after rasburicase administration, antibodies to rasburicase developed in 0—14% of patients. In a recent study, repeated rasburicase administrations were effective in 11 of 12 patients. The repeat exposure was the second for 10 patients, the third for 1 patient, and the fourth for another. The sensitivity and specificity for antibody detection have not been fully evaluated, and the time for antibody development after rasburicase administration may be longer than 28 days. Thus, the true incidence of antibody production and the effect on subsequent rasburicase receipt has yet to be determined.

    hemolysis, methemoglobinemia
    General Information
    Hemolysis and methemoglobinemia have been reported with rasburicase therapy in clinical trials (n = 887); both hemolysis and methemoglobinemia occurred in less than 1% of patients. Some patients with methemoglobinemia experienced serious hypoxemia requiring intervention with medical support measures. Permanently discontinue rasburicase if a patient experiences hemolysis or methemoglobinemia and start appropriate monitoring and medical management (e.g., transfusion support for hemolysis and transfusion support and methylene-blue administration for methemoglobinemia). Severe hemolytic reactions have occurred within 2 to 4 days of starting rasburicase.

    abdominal pain, constipation, diarrhea, nausea, oral ulceration, vomiting
    General Information
    Gastrointestinal (GI) toxicity occurred with rasburicase (n = 92) or rasburicase plus allopurinol (combination therapy; n = 92) in adult patients with leukemia, lymphoma, or solid tumor malignancies at risk for hyperuricemia and tumor lysis syndrome in a multicenter, 3-arm, randomized study.     General Information
    Rasburicase causes laboratory test interference with uric acid measurements by enzymatically degrading uric acid in blood/plasma/serum samples left at room temperature; this results in low plasma uric acid assay readings. To ensure accurate measurements, collect blood into pre-chilled tubes containing heparin and immediately immerse and maintain the tube in an ice water bath. Prepare plasma samples by centrifugation in a pre-cooled centrifuge (4 degrees C). Keep the plasma samples in an ice water bath and analyze within 4 hours of collection.

Description
  Text
  Rasburicase catalyzes the enzymatic oxidation of uric acid to the inactive and more soluble metabolite, allantoin. It is a recombinant form of urate oxidase produced by cloning the gene for Aspergillus flavus urate oxidase for expression in Saccharomyces cerevisiae. Rasburicase is indicated for the initial management of plasma uric acid levels in pediatric and adult patients with leukemia, lymphoma, and solid tumor malignancies who are receiving anticancer therapy expected to result in tumor lysis and subsequent elevation of plasma uric acid. The uric acid response rate was significantly higher with rasburicase compared with allopurinol (87% vs. 66%; p = 0.001) in adult patients with active leukemia or lymphoma at high risk for hyperuricemia and tumor lysis syndrome in a randomized, 3-arm, phase III trial (n= 275). Treatment with rasburicase resulted in a 2.6-fold reduction in uric acid exposure compared with allopurinol in pediatric patients (age range, 0.3 to 17 years) with leukemia or lymphoma at high risk for tumor lysis in a multicenter, randomized trial (n = 52). Rasburicase was approved by the FDA in 2002 for pediatric patients and in 2009 for adult patients.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Rasburicase catalyzes the enzymatic oxidation of uric acid to the inactive and more soluble metabolite, allantoin. Allantoin is metabolized by peptide hydrolysis and excreted. Rasburicase is a recombinant form of urate oxidase produced by cloning the gene for Aspergillus flavus urate oxidase for expression in Saccharomyces cerevisiae. Humans do not produce urate oxidase due to a nonsense mutation in the coding region of the gene. In some hematologic malignancies and solid tumors, effective anticancer therapy causes a rapid lysis of malignant cells and an abrupt release of cellular contents into the blood resulting in tumor lysis syndrome (TLS). TLS is characterized by hyperuricemia and electrolyte abnormalities (e.g., hyperkalemia, hyperphosphatemia, hypocalcemia) and may lead to acute renal failure, arrhythmias, and/or death. Allopurinol inhibits the xanthine oxidase enzyme and prevents the formation of uric acid. In addition to a unique mechanism of action, rasburicase differs from allopurinol in that it degrades preexisting uric acid, reduces uric acid levels quickly (usually within 4 hours), and does not lead to xanthine accumulation.

Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes: none
Clinically relevant CYP450-mediated drug interactions are not expected in patients who receive the recommended rasburicase dose and dosing schedule. In vivo, rasburicase did not induce or inhibit the activity of CYP1A, CYP2A, CYP2B, CYP2C, CYP2E, or CYP3A.

Reconstitution of rasburicase vial:
Use the provided diluent. Add 1 mL of diluent to the 1.5-mg vial or 5 mL of diluent to the 7.5-mg vial for a final concentration of 1.5 mg/mL.
Do not shake or vortex the vial; gently swirl to mix.
Preparation of infusion:
Inject the calculated dose of reconstituted rasburicase solution into an infusion bag containing the appropriate volume of 0.9% Sodium Chloride injection, to achieve a final total volume of 50 mL.
Storage following infusion preparation: Store diluted infusion solution at 2 to 8 degrees C (36 to 46 degrees F); infuse within 24 hours of preparation.
 
Intravenous infusion:
Infuse the diluted solution IV over 30 minutes. Do not use filters during the infusion.
Infuse through a separate line; if the use of a separate line is not possible, flush the line with at least 15 mL of 0.9% Sodium Chloride injection prior to and after each infusion.
