Drug Class: Edetate calcium disodium, or calcium EDTA, is a parenteral drug used in the treatment of lead toxicity.

Routes: injection (IV/IM/SC); Common adult doses: 1500 mg/m2/day IV or IM.

Common Side Effects (excluding routine GI upset): anuria, glycosuria, hematuria, nephrotoxicity, oliguria, proteinuria, renal tubular necrosis, chills, fatigue, fever, malaise, myalgia, anemia, hypercalcemia, zinc deficiency, cheilitis, polydipsia, lacrimation, nasal congestion, rash (unspecified), sneezing

In an Overdose: Calcium EDTA reduces blood concentrations and depot stores of lead, and although it is used primarily in the treatment of lead poisoning, calcium EDTA also can be used as an antidote for other metal intoxications.

Key Nursing Considerations: Contraindications/precautions: Calcium EDTA is contraindicated for use in patients with anuria or severe oliguria. The drug should not be administered to patients with active renal disease because this can reduce the excretion of the chelate and increase the possibility of nephrotoxicity.

Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  General Information
  Calcium EDTA is contraindicated for use in patients with anuria or severe oliguria. The drug should not be administered to patients with active renal disease because this can reduce the excretion of the chelate and increase the possibility of nephrotoxicity. The EDTA-lead complex is highly nephrotoxic. In patients with anuria or oliguria, urine flow should be restored before reinstituting calcium EDTA therapy. Calcium EDTA should be used with caution in patients with dehydration secondary to vomiting or diarrhea, and urine flow should be reestablished before administration of the first dose of calcium EDTA. Once urine flow is reestablished, IV fluids must be restricted to basal water and electrolyte requirements.
 
Lead encephalopathy, although rare in adults, can occur more frequently in children and is likely to be overlooked. Intravenous administration of calcium EDTA can increase intracranial pressure. In patients with cerebral edema (increased intracranial pressure) or lead encephalopathy, lethal increases in intracranial pressure may occur with IV administration. Therefore, intramuscular administration is preferred in these patients, especially in children. If the IV route is used, avoid rapid infusion. The dosage schedule should be followed and the recommended daily dose should not be exceeded in any case.
 
Calcium EDTA is contraindicated in patients with hepatitis.
 
Calcium EDTA is classified as pregnancy category B. No adequate human studies have examined the effects of this drug on the human fetus, and animal reproduction studies have shown no adverse fetal effects.
 
It is not known whether calcium EDTA is excreted in breast milk, but because many drugs are excreted in breast milk, the drug should be used with caution in women who are breast-feeding.

    Advise patients requiring edetate calcium disodium, calcium EDTA for the treatment of lead poisoning to avoid breast-feeding. There are no data on the presence of calcium EDTA in human milk, its effects on the breast-fed child, or its effects on milk production. However, lead is excreted in human milk and can be toxic to the breast-feeding child. Adverse Reactions
  Headline
  Adverse Reactions
  General Information
  The calcium EDTA-lead complex is as nephrotoxic as the lead itself. Nephrotoxicity is manifest as renal tubular necrosis and is usually related to excessively high doses. This complication can lead to fatal nephrosis. Early signs of such adverse effects include glycosuria, proteinuria, changes in the distal renal tubules and glomeruli, an increase in the number of erythrocytes in the urine, or the presence of large renal epithelial cells. BUN and urinary sediment determinations should be instituted regularly to assess renal function. Polyuria and increased urgency also have been reported following calcium EDTA administration and, although rare, require medical attention.Lead encephalopathy occurs more frequently in children than adults. Following the intravenous administration of calcium EDTA, some patients with lead encephalopathy and cerebral edema have experienced lethal increased intracranial pressure. The intramuscular route is preferred for these patients. However, intramuscular administration is painful at the site of injection. If the intravenous route is necessary, infuse slowly and monitor the patient carefully. Do not exceed the recommended dose.An injection site reaction can occur during therapy with calcium EDTA. Phlebitis, and pain and burning at the site of calcium EDTA injection can occur, and extravasation at the site of injection should be avoided. The drug should be thoroughly diluted before administration, and concentration of solution should not exceed 0.5%. Patients should be monitored for an injection site reaction.Intravenous administration of calcium EDTA precipitated acute attacks of gout in three patients who had preexisting chronic lead nephropathy.A systemic febrile reaction (consisting of fever and chills, fatigue, headache, increased thirst), sometimes accompanied by a histamine-like reaction (manifested by sneezing, nasal congestion, and lacrimation), frequently has been reported 4—8 hours after IV infusion of calcium EDTA.Less frequently, parenteral administration of calcium EDTA has resulted in anorexia, nausea/vomiting, hypercalcemia, rash, dermatitis (which results in lesions similar to those seen with vitamin B6 deficiency and may be due to zinc depletion), arrhythmias, cheilosis, tremor, headache, peripheral neuropathy, arthralgia, transient bone marrow depression, anemia, zinc deficiency, and hypercalcemia.

Description
  Text
  Edetate calcium disodium, or calcium EDTA, is a parenteral drug used in the treatment of lead toxicity. It should not be confused with the noncalcium-containing salt (edetate disodium), which is used to treat hypercalcemia. Calcium EDTA reduces blood concentrations and depot stores of lead, and although it is used primarily in the treatment of lead poisoning, calcium EDTA also can be used as an antidote for other metal intoxications. Calcium EDTA was approved by the FDA for clinical use in 1953.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Calcium EDTA is a chelating agent with an affinity for divalent and trivalent metals. Once administered, the calcium component of calcium EDTA can be displaced by other divalent and trivalent metals, forming stable, soluble complexes that are then excreted by the kidneys. EDTA has a greater affinity for lead than for calcium and releases calcium to bind with lead. The noncalcium-containing formulation of EDTA should not be used to treat lead toxicity because it will preferentially bind calcium, and serious hypocalcemia can result.
 
One gram of edetate calcium disodium has the potential to combine with 620 mg of lead. Typically, however, only 3—5 mg of lead are excreted in response to a 1 g dose of the drug when administered to patients with acute lead poisoning or high levels of lead in the soft tissues.
 
Once calcium EDTA is circulating in the bloodstream and extracellular fluids, it can increase the urinary excretion of lead, but orally administered dosages of the drug increase the absorption of lead. Therefore, calcium EDTA is only administered subcutaneously, intravenously, or intramuscularly. The excretion of zinc is greatly increased following administration of calcium EDTA, and although mercury will displace calcium from its binding site on calcium EDTA, the drug is not effective in treating mercury poisoning, perhaps because mercury is too tightly bound to the ligands of the tissues or is sequestered in compartments of the body that calcium EDTA does not penetrate. Calcium EDTA is also not effective in the treatment of gold or arsenic poisoning.

  Text
  Calcium EDTA is administered intravenously or intramuscularly. The drug distributes predominantly into the extracellular fluid and does not enter erythrocytes or the CNS to a significant extent. Calcium EDTA is not metabolized and is excreted primarily by the kidneys, with 50% excreted in 1 hour and 95% within 24 hours. The half-life is 20 to 60 minutes.
 
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes: none

Administration
  General Information
  For storage information, see the specific product information within the How Supplied section.
 
NOTE: Edetate calcium disodium, calcium EDTA may aggravate symptoms associated with toxic lead levels in patients with blood lead levels greater than 70 mcg/dL. In these patients, calcium EDTA should be used in conjunction with dimercaprol.
NOTE: Ensure adequate diuresis with the use of IV fluids before therapy begins; however, excessive fluid intake should be avoided in patients with encephalopathy. Once urine flow is established, further IV fluids must be restricted to basal water and electrolyte requirements.

      Intravenous Administration
      Description
      NOTE: Lethal increases in intracranial pressure have been reported in patients with lead encephalopathy and cerebral edema following intravenous administration. The intramuscular route is preferred for these patients. If the intravenous route is necessary, infuse slowly and monitor the patient carefully.
Dilute the total daily dose of edetate calcium disodium, calcium EDTA injection in 250 to 500 mL of 5% Dextrose injection or 0.9% Sodium Chloride injection.
Intermittent infusion: Using an infusion pump, administer the diluted injection over 1 hour. NOTE: For the treatment of lead toxicity, the manufacturer recommends that the total daily dose be administered as a single infusion over 8 to 12 hours.
Continuous infusion: Infuse over 8 to 24 hours. The infusion should be interrupted for 1 hour before a blood lead concentration is measured to avoid a falsely elevated value.
      Name
      Intramuscular Administration
      Description
      Intramuscular injection is the preferred route of administration for patients at high risk for cerebral edema. Lethal increases in intracranial pressure have been reported in patients with lead encephalopathy and cerebral edema following the intravenous administration.
Intramuscular administration of calcium EDTA is painful. To minimize pain at the injection site, lidocaine or procaine may be added to edetate calcium disodium injection. A final lidocaine or procaine concentration of 0.5% (5 mg/mL) can be obtained as follows: 0.25 mL of 10% lidocaine solution per 5 mL of edetate calcium disodium; or 1 mL of 1% lidocaine or 1% procaine solution per ml of edetate calcium disodium.
Inject deeply into a well developed muscle. Aspirate prior to injection to avoid injection into a blood vessel. Massage injection site well following administration. Injection sites should be rotated.

Dosage Adjustment Guidelines
  Hepatic Impairment
  Specific dosage guidelines have not been established; however calcium EDTA is contraindicated in patients with hepatitis.
  Renal Impairment
  Specific dosage guidelines have not been established; however, dosage guidelines based on serum creatinine have been suggested for patients with lead nephropathy. These guidelines may also be useful for patients with preexisting renal impairment. The manufacturer states that edetate calcium disodium is contraindicated in patients with active renal disease.
