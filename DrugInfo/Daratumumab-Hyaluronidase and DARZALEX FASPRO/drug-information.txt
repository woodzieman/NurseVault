Drug Class: Daratumumab; hyaluronidase is a CD38-directed monoclonal antibody; the hyaluronidase component increases the dispersion and absorption of daratumumab allowing for subcutaneous administration.

Routes: injection (IV/IM/SC); Common adult doses: 1,800 mg daratumumab and 30,000 units hyaluronidase (15 mL) subcutaneously once weekly.

Common Side Effects (excluding routine GI upset): hemorrhoids, arthralgia, back pain, muscle cramps, musculoskeletal pain, cough, dyspnea, pulmonary edema, candidiasis, infection, influenza, laryngitis, pharyngitis, rhinitis, sepsis, sinusitis, tracheitis, anemia, leukopenia, lymphopenia, neutropenia, thrombocytopenia, asthenia, fatigue, gait disturbance, hypotonia, malaise, muscle paralysis, myopathy, tremor

In an Overdose: No overdose/antidote information is listed in this file.

          Dosage Description
          1,800 mg daratumumab and 30,000 units hyaluronidase subcutaneously weekly on weeks 1 to 8 (8 doses), 1,800 mg daratumumab and 30,000 units hyaluronidase every other week on weeks 9 to 24 (8 doses), and then 1,800 mg daratumumab and 30,000 units hyaluronidase every 4 weeks starting on week 25 until disease progression. Administer acetaminophen 650 to 1,000 mg PO, diphenhydramine 25 to 50 mg (or equivalent) PO or IV, and methylprednisolone 100 mg (or equivalent) PO or IV one to 3 hours prior to every injection; consider reducing the corticosteroid dose to methylprednisolone 60 mg (or equivalent) IV or PO after the second injection. Patients should also receive an oral corticosteroid (equivalent to methylprednisolone 20 mg) for 2 days beginning the day after every injection; consider discontinuing the post-treatment corticosteroid if the patient has no major systemic administration-related reactions with the first 3 doses. Short and long-acting bronchodilators and inhaled corticosteroids may be administered in patients with a history of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease; consider discontinuing these medications if the patient has no major systemic administration-related reaction with the first 4 injections. Begin antiviral prophylaxis within 1 week after starting daratumumab; hyaluronidase and continue for 3 months after therapy. The noninferiority of subcutaneous daratumumab compared with IV daratumumab was demonstrated in patients with relapsed or refractory multiple myeloma who had received at least 3 previous lines of therapy in a multinational, randomized, phase 3 trial (the COLUMBA trial). At a median follow-up of 29.3 months, the overall response rate was 43.7% in patients who received subcutaneous daratumumab compared with 39.8% in patients who received IV daratumumab in this trial. In the subcutaneous and IV daratumumab arms, respectively, the median progression-free survival times were 5.6 and 6.1 months and the median overall survival times were 28.2 and 25.6 months.

Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  Contraindications And Precautions
    Headline
    Drug Interactions
    General Information
    The coadministration of certain medications may lead to harm and require avoidance or therapy modification; review all drug interactions prior to concomitant use of other medications.
    Headline
    chronic obstructive pulmonary disease
    General Information
    Use daratumumab; hyaluronidase with caution in patients with a history of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD). Severe systemic administration-related reactions and local reactions have been reported with daratumumab; hyaluronidase therapy and may include anaphylaxis, bronchospasm, hypoxia, dyspnea, hypertension, hypotension, headache, tachycardia, laryngeal edema, wheezing, ocular adverse reactions, and respiratory symptoms. Most systemic administration-related reactions occur during the first injection; administer premedication (e.g., acetaminophen, diphenhydramine, and a corticosteroid) and postmedication (e.g., methylprednisolone or regimen-specific corticosteroid for combination regimens) as recommended. Consider giving additional post-injection medications such as short and long-acting bronchodilators and inhaled corticosteroids in patients with a history of COPD.
    Headline
    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components.
    Headline
    hepatitis B infection, varicella
    General Information
    Use daratumumab; hyaluronidase with caution in patients with a history of hepatitis B infection or in patients who might have a hepatitis B infection; reactivation of hepatitis B virus (HBV) has been reported in patients who received daratumumab; hyaluronidase therapy. Use daratumumab; hyaluronidase with caution in patients with a history of shingles (herpes zoster); recurrence of latent varicella-zoster virus may occur. Initiate antiviral prophylaxis to prevent herpes zoster reactivation within 1 week after starting daratumumab; hyaluronidase and continue for 3 months following the end of treatment.
    Headline
    cardiac disease
    General Information
    Daratumumab; hyaluronidase is not indicated for the treatment of patients with light-chain amyloidosis who have NYHA class IIIB or IV heart failure or Mayo Stage IIIB cardiac disease. Patients with NYHA class IIIA heart failure or Mayo Stage IIIA cardiac disease may be at greater risk of developing cardiotoxicity. Serious and fatal cardiac toxicity (e.g., cardiac arrest and arrhythmias) has been reported in patients with light-chain amyloidosis who received daratumumab/hyaluronidase, bortezomib, cyclophosphamide and dexamethasone. Monitor patients with cardiac involvement of light-chain amyloidosis more frequently for cardiac adverse reactions; administer supportive care as appropriate.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Based on its mechanism of action, daratumumab; hyaluronidase can cause fetal harm if administered during pregnancy. There are no human data to evaluate a drug-associated risk of major birth defects, miscarriage, or other adverse outcomes. Animal reproduction toxicity studies have not been conducted. Immunoglobulin G1 antibodies, such as daratumumab, are known to cross the placenta. Based on its mechanism of action, daratumumab may cause depletion of fetal CD38-positive immune cells and decreased bone density. Mice that were genetically modified to eliminate all CD38 expression (CD38 knockout mice) had reduced bone density, which recovered 5 months after birth. Data from studies using CD38 knockout animal models also suggest the CD38 is involved in the regulation of humoral immune responses (mice), feto-maternal immune tolerance (mice), and early embryonic development (frogs).
    Headline
    people who may become pregnant, reproductive risk
    General Information
    Counsel people who may become pregnant about the reproductive risk associated with daratumumab; hyaluronidase. Daratumumab; hyaluronidase can cause fetal harm. Advise people who may become pregnant to use effective contraception while taking daratumumab; hyaluronidase and for 3 months after the last dose.
    Headline
    neonates and infants exposed to this medication in utero
    General Information
    Defer administration of live vaccinations in neonates and infants exposed to this medication in utero until a hematology evaluation is completed. Immunoglobulin G1 monoclonal antibodies, such as daratumumab, are transferred across the placenta. Daratumumab; hyaluronidase may cause depletion of CD38-positive immune cells and decreased bone density.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Use daratumumab; hyaluronidase with caution during breast-feeding. It is unknown whether the drug is excreted in human milk or if it affects the breastfed child or milk production. While maternal immunoglobulin G (IgG) is present in breast milk, available data suggest minimal transfer of antibodies into infant circulation. Given daratumumab's large molecular size, levels in milk are expected to be low, and any ingested drug is likely to be partially degraded in the child's gastrointestinal tract, further limiting exposure. Consider the safety profiles of any concomitant medications when advising lactating patients.

    General Information

Description
  Text
  Daratumumab; hyaluronidase is a CD38-directed monoclonal antibody; the hyaluronidase component increases the dispersion and absorption of daratumumab allowing for subcutaneous administration. It is indicated for the treatment of adults with multiple myeloma as a single-agent and in combination with other anti-myeloma agents and as monotherapy for the treatment of adults with high-risk smoldering multiple myeloma. Daratumumab; hyaluronidase is also indicated in combination with bortezomib, cyclophosphamide, and dexamethasone for the treatment of newly diagnosed, light-chain amyloidosis. Because daratumumab binds to CD38 on red blood cells, it may interfere with the ability to determine a patient's blood type and result in a false positive indirect antiglobulin test (Coombs test). Additionally, daratumumab may interfere with the determination of complete response or disease progression in some patients. Patients should have their blood typed and cross-matched prior to starting treatment.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Daratumumab: Daratumumab is a human monoclonal antibody that binds to CD38 IgG1 (kappa subclass), a transmembrane glycoprotein, resulting in apotosis via Fc-mediated cross linking, antibody-dependent cell-mediated cytotoxicity, complement-dependent cytotoxicity, and antibody-dependent cellular phagocytosis. CD38 is expressed on myeloid-derived suppressor cells and some regulatory T-cells (CD38+Tregs) and B-cells (CD38+regs); these cells are sensitive to daratumumab.
Hyaluronidase: Recombinant human hyaluronidase is an endoglycosidase that is used to increase the dispersion and absorption of other drugs when administered subcutaneously. It is produced by Chinese Hamster Ovary cells containing a DNA plasmid encoding for a soluble fragment of human hyaluronidase.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Subcutaneous Route
    Description
    The absolute bioavailability was 69% following the subcutaneous administration of 1,800 mg daratumumab and 30,000 units hyaluronidase once weekly for 8 weeks compared with intravenous (IV) daratumumab 16 mg/kg. The noninferiority of subcutaneous daratumumab compared with IV daratumumab was demonstrated in patients with relapsed or refractory multiple myeloma who had received at least 3 previous lines of therapy in a multinational, randomized, phase 3 trial (the COLUMBA trial).
The observed maximum mean Cmin values (after the eighth dose) were 593 +/- 306, 654 +/- 243, and 597 +/- 232 mcg/mL following subcutaneous daratumumab; hyaluronidase in patients with multiple myeloma, smoldering multiple myeloma, and light-chain amyloidosis, respectively, and 522 +/- 226 mcg/mL in patients who received IV daratumumab in clinical trials; the geometric means ratio was 108% (90% CI, 96% to 122%) in patients with multiple myeloma. The maximum mean Cmin values (after the eighth dose) were 537 +/- 277 mcg/mL, 526 +/- 226 mcg/mL, 756 +/- 276 mcg/mL, and 526 +/- 209 mcg/mL following daratumumab; hyaluronidase in combination with pomalidomide and dexamethasone (Pd regimen), lenalidomide and dexamethasone (Rd regimen), carfilzomib plus dexamethasone (Kd regimen), and bortezomib, lenalidomide, and dexamethasone (VRd regimen; PERSEUS trial) respectively. The observed maximum mean Cmin value after the sixth dose was 407 +/- 183 mcg/mL following daratumumab; hyaluronidase in combination with VRd in the CEPHEUS trial.
The simulated median Cmax values (after the eighth dose) were 592, 780, and 729 mcg/mL following subcutaneous daratumumab; hyaluronidase in patients with multiple myeloma, smoldering multiple myeloma, and light-chain amyloidosis, respectively, and 688 mcg/mL in patients who received IV daratumumab. The Tmax occurred at 3 days and 4 days in patients with multiple myeloma and light-chain amyloidosis, respectively. Additionally, the simulated median AUC(0-7d) values (after the eighth dose) were 4,017, 5,043, and 4,855 mcg/mL x day following subcutaneous daratumumab; hyaluronidase in patients with multiple myeloma, smoldering multiple myeloma, and light-chain amyloidosis, respectively, and 4,019 mcg/mL x day in patients who received IV daratumumab. Daratumumab; hyaluronidase exposures were similar between patients receiving monotherapy and combination regimens.

Administration
  General Information
  For storage information, see the specific product information within the How Supplied section.
Premedication
Administer the following premedication 1 to 3 hours before each dose of daratumumab; hyaluronidase: acetaminophen 650 to 1,000 mg PO, diphenhydramine 25 to 50 mg (or equivalent) PO or IV, and a corticosteroid as described below.
Monotherapy: Methylprednisolone 100 mg (or equivalent) IV or PO prior to the first and second administrations and methylprednisolone 60 mg (or equivalent) IV or PO prior to subsequent doses. All patients should receive an oral corticosteroid (equivalent to methylprednisolone 20 mg) for 2 days beginning the day after every administration. If the patient does not experience a major systemic administration-related reaction after the first 3 doses, consider discontinuing corticosteroids.
Combination therapy: Dexamethasone 20 mg prior to every administration . Give the background regimen-specific dexamethasone dose as the daratumumab premedication corticosteroid when these drugs are scheduled on the same day. Consider giving a low-dose oral corticosteroid (equivalent to methylprednisolone 20 mg or less) on the day after every administration. If the patient does not experience a major systemic administration-related reaction after the first 3 doses, consider discontinuing corticosteroids (excluding any background regimen-specific corticosteroids).
Recommended Prophylaxis
Initiate antiviral prophylaxis to prevent herpes zoster reactivation within 1 week after starting daratumumab; hyaluronidase and continue for 3 months following the end of treatment.

    Administration Details
      Name
      Subcutaneous Administration
      Description
      Daratumumab; hyaluronidase is for subcutaneous administration only; do not administer intravenously.
Daratumumab; hyaluronidase is a ready-to-use solution and does not need to be diluted; it should be administered by a health care professional.
If a dose is missed, administer daratumumab; hyaluronidase as soon as possible and adjust the dosing schedule to maintain the dosing interval.
Preparation:
Allow the refrigerated daratumumab; hyaluronidase vial to warm to ambient temperature (15 to 30 degrees C; 59 to 86 degrees F); protect from light and do not shake the vial.
Using a stainless-steel transfer needle, withdraw 15 mL from the daratumumab; hyaluronidase vial into a polypropylene or polyethylene syringe; replace the transfer needle with a syringe closing cap.
Label the syringe with a peel off label to include the route of administration per institutional standards (e.g., for subcutaneous use only).
Storage: The unpunctured vial may be stored at ambient temperature and light for a maximum of 24 hours. If the prepared syringe solution is not used immediately, it may be stored refrigerated (2 to 8 degrees C; 36 to 46 degrees F) for up to 24 hours or at room temperature (15 to 25 degrees C; 59 to 77 degrees F) for up to 12 hours under ambient light; discard the syringe if not used within this timeframe.
Subcutaneous Injection:
If refrigerated, allow the syringe to acclimate to room temperature.
Immediately prior to injection, attach a stainless-steel hypodermic injection needle or a polypropylene, polyethylene, or polyvinyl chloride (PVC) subcutaneous infusion set to the syringe to avoid needle clogging.
Inject 15 mL of daratumumab; hyaluronidase subcutaneously into the subcutaneous tissue of the abdomen approximately 3 inches (7.5 cm) to the right or left of the navel over approximately 3 to 5 minutes; rotate injection sites for successive injections.
Do not inject daratumumab; hyaluronidase in areas where the skin is red, bruised, tender, hard, or scarred.
The injection delivery may be slowed down or paused if the patients has pain during the injection; select a second injection site on the opposite side of the abdomen to deliver the remainder of the dose if the pain is not alleviated by these measures.
Monitor for local and systemic administration-related reactions, especially following the first and second injections.
Do not administer other subcutaneous medications at the same site.
