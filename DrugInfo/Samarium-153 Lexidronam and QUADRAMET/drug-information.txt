Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  General Information
  Samarium-153 lexidronam causes bone marrow suppression. Because of the unknown potential for additive bone marrow suppression, samarium-153 lexidronam should not be administered concurrently with chemotherapy or external beam radiation therapy. Also, samarium-153 lexidronam should not be administered until adequate recovery of bone marrow in patients who have received chemotherapy or external beam radiation.
 
Safe and effective use of samarium-153 lexidronam in children under 16 years of age have not been established.
 
Use samarium-153 lexidronam cautiously in patients with congestive heart failure or renal impairment. Concomitant hydration is recommended with therapy to promote the urinary excretion of samarium-153 lexidronam. Additionally, use cautiously in patients with urinary incontinence because special precautions, such as bladder catheterization, should be taken to minimize the risk of radioactive contamination of clothing, bed linen, and the patient's environment.
 
Samarium-153 lexidronam should be administered with caution in patients with electrocardiograph (ECG) changes or cardiac arrhythmias. EDTMP is a chelating agent. Although the chelating effects have not been evaluated thoroughly in humans, dogs that received non-radioactive samarium EDTMP (6 times the human dose based on body weight, 3 times based on surface area) developed a variety of ECG changes (with or without hypocalcemia). Whether samarium-153 lexidronam causes ECG changes or cardiac arrhythmias has not been studied. Appropriate monitoring should be undertaken.
 
Samarium-153 should not be used in patients with phosphonate hypersensitivity. Ethylenediaminetetramethylenephosphonic acid (EDTMP) is present in Quadramet(R) and may cause a hypersensitivity reaction in patients with a history of phosphonate hypersensitivity. Samarium-153 lexidronam is contraindicated in patients who are sensitive to EDTMP or similar phosphonate compounds.
 
Samarium-153 should be used cautiously in patients with preexisting hypocalcemia or at risk for developing hypocalcemia because EDTMP chelates calcium.
 
Samarium-153 lexidronam is classified in FDA pregnancy risk category D. It can cause fetal harm when administered to a pregnant woman. Adequate and well controlled studies have not been conducted in animals or pregnant women. If this drug is used during pregnancy, or if a patient becomes pregnant after taking this drug, the patient should be apprised of the potential hazard to the fetus. Women of child-bearing potential should be advised to avoid becoming pregnant soon after receiving samarium-153 lexidronam. Men and women patients should use an effective method of contraception after receiving the drug.
 
Administration of radioisotopes like Samarium-153 lexidronam necessitate the cessation of breast-feeding. A nuclear medicine practitioner should be consulted to determine when radioactivity has cleared from breast milk, including screening of milk samples as needed. The mother should discard all milk pumped during the time of Samarium-153 lexidronam treatment and for the time that the radioactivity is still present in the milk.

Description
  Text
  NOTE: All products associated with this monograph are off-market. The content is retained for historical reference only and is no longer actively maintained.
 
Samarium-153 lexidronam is a parenteral radioisotope indicated for the relief of pain associated with metastatic bone cancer. The onset of analgesic effect is usually within 7 days, with maximum pain relief generally occurring 3 to 4 weeks after administration. It consists of a stable complex of radioactive samarium and a tetraphosphonate chelator, ethylenediaminetetramethylenephosphonic acid (EDTMP).

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  NOTE: All products associated with this monograph are off-market. The content is retained for historical reference only and is no longer actively maintained.
 
Samarium-153 lexidronam is a radioactive agent that emits both beta particles and a gamma photon. The mechanism by which samarium-153 lexidronam relieves pain associated with bone metastases is not known. Samarium-153 lexidronam has an affinity for bone and concentrates in areas of bone turnover in association with hydroxyapatite. In clinical studies, more samarium-153 lexidronam accumulates in osteoblastic lesions than in normal bone (lesion-to-normal bone ratio of approximately 5).

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Intravenous Route
    Description
    The greater the number of metastatic lesions, the more skeletal uptake of samarium-153 radioactivity. The relationship between skeletal uptake and the size of the metastatic lesions has not been studied. The total skeletal uptake of radioactivity is approximately 65.5% of the injected dose. The percentage of the injected dose taken up by bone ranges from 56.3% in a patient with 5 metastatic lesions to 76.7% in a patient with 52 metastatic lesions. If the number of metastatic lesions is fixed, over the range 0.1 to 3 mCi/kg, the percent of the injected dose taken up by bone is the same regardless of the dose.

  Text
  NOTE: All products associated with this monograph are off-market. The content is retained for historical reference only and is no longer actively maintained.
 
Samarium-153 lexidronam is administered intravenously. Human protein binding has not been studied, however, animal data show less than 0.5% of samarium-153 lexidronam is bound to protein. At physiologic pH, more than 90% of the complex is present as 153Sm[EDTMP]-5, and less than 10% as 153SmH[EDTMP]-4. Metabolic products of samarium-153 lexidronam were not detected in humans. The complex formed by samarium and ethylenediaminetetramethylenephosphonic acid (EDTMP) is excreted in the urine as an intact, single species that consists of 1 atom of samarium-153 and 1 molecule of EDTMP. Clearance of radioactivity from the blood demonstrates biexponential kinetics after IV injection. Over the first 30 minutes, the radioactivity in the blood decreased to approximately 15% of the injected dose with a half-life of 5.5 minutes (+/- 1.1 minutes). Thereafter, the radioactivity clears from the blood more slowly with a half-life of 65.4 minutes (+/- 9.6 minutes). Less than 1% of the injected dose remained in the blood 5 hours after injection. During the first 6 hours, approximately 34.5% is excreted in the urine. With greater number of metastatic lesions, the more skeletal uptake of samarium-153 activity, and the less radioactivity excreted.

NOTE: Samarium-153 lexidronam is a radioactive drug. To ensure minimum radiation exposure to occupational workers and to minimize radiation exposure to patients, observe appropriate precautions that are consistent with proper patient management. Radiopharmaceuticals should only be used by nuclear physicians and/or pharmacists who are qualified by training and experience in the safe use and handling of radioactive material, and whose experience and training have been approved by the appropriate governmental agency authorized to license the use of radiopharmaceuticals.
NOTE: Storage and disposal should be controlled in compliance with appropriate regulations of the governmental agency authorized to license the use of radiopharmaceuticals.
NOTE: Estimated absorbed radiation doses are based on clinical biodistribution studies using methods developed for radiation dose calculations by the Medical Internal Radiation Dose (MIRD) Committee of the Society of Nuclear Medicine. Radiation exposure is based on a urinary voiding interval of 4.8 hours. Radiation dose estimates for bone and marrow assume that radioactivity is deposited on bone surfaces, as noted in autoradiograms of biopsy bone samples in 7 patients who received samarium-153 lexidronam. Although electron emissions from 153Sm are abundant, with energies up to 810 keV, rapid blood clearance of samarium-153 lexidronam and low energy and abundant photon emissions generally result in low radiation doses to those parts of the body where the complex does not localize. However, enhanced localization will occur where blastic osseous lesions are present; thereby resulting in higher doses to the lesion than to normal bones and other organs.
