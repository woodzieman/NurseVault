Drug Class: Oxymetazoline is an alpha adrenoreceptor agonist that acts as a topically applied sympathomimetic vasoconstrictor.

Routes: nasal, topical, ophthalmic; Common adult doses: 8 drops/eye/day of the 0.025% ophthalmic solution; 1 drop/eye/day of the 0.1% ophthalmic solution; 6 sprays/nostril/day of the nasal spray; 1 topical application/day of the cream.

Common Side Effects (excluding routine GI upset): bradycardia, coma, hypertension, hypotension, hypothermia, lethargy, mydriasis, respiratory depression, sinus tachycardia, contact dermatitis, erythema, pruritus, nasal congestion, nasal irritation, rhinorrhea, sneezing, blurred vision, conjunctival hyperemia, headache, keratitis, ocular irritation, ocular pain, visual impairment, xerophthalmia

In an Overdose: No overdose/antidote information is listed in this file.

Key Nursing Considerations: Contraindications/precautions: Instruct patients with cardiovascular disease to seek immediate medical attention if their condition worsens. This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components.

Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  Contraindications And Precautions
    Headline
    Drug Interactions
    General Information
    The coadministration of certain medications may lead to harm and require avoidance or therapy modification; review all drug interactions prior to concomitant use of other medications.
    Headline
    cardiovascular disease
    General Information
    Oxymetazoline (an alpha-adrenergic agonist) acts as a vasoconstrictor, which may adversely affect blood pressure; thus, caution is advised when administering the drug to patients with severe or unstable cardiovascular disease (e.g. cerebrovascular disease, coronary artery disease, Raynaud phenomenon, orthostatic hypotension, and uncontrolled hypertension or hypotension). Instruct patients with cardiovascular disease to seek immediate medical attention if their condition worsens.
    Headline
    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components.
    Headline
    diabetes mellitus, prostatic hypertrophy, scleroderma, Sjogren syndrome, thromboangiitis obliterans, thyroid disease
    General Information
    Use oxymetazoline with caution in individuals with vascular and autoimmune diseases, such as diabetes mellitus, thyroid disease, prostatic hypertrophy, Sjogren syndrome, thromboangiitis obliterans (Buerger's disease), and scleroderma. Instruct individuals to seek immediate medical care if signs or symptoms of worsening vascular insufficiency develop.
    Headline
    General Information
    General Information
    Before prescribing oxymetazoline 0.1% ophthalmic solution for the treatment of blepharoptosis, consider that ptosis may be associated with neurologic or orbital diseases, such as stroke, cerebral aneurysm, Horner syndrome, myasthenia gravis, external ophthalmoplegia, orbital infection, and orbital masses. It is advised to evaluate the patient for these conditions in the presence of ptosis with decreased levator muscle function or other neurologic signs.
    Headline
    closed-angle glaucoma
    General Information
    Treatment with oxymetazoline may increase the risk of developing angle-closure glaucoma in patients with closed-angle glaucoma. Instruct patients to seek immediate medical care if signs or symptoms of acute angle-closure glaucoma develop.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    There are no adequate and well-controlled studies of oxymetazoline use during pregnancy to determine the risk of major birth defects or miscarriage. In clinical trials of oxymetazoline cream, 2 pregnancies were reported: 1 resulted in delivery of a healthy infant, and 1 resulted in a spontaneous abortion considered unrelated to treatment. An exploratory analysis of intranasal decongestant use during pregnancy identified a potential association between second-trimester exposure to oxymetazoline without first-trimester decongestant exposure, and renal collecting system anomalies. In another report, fetal distress occurred in a patient at 41 weeks of pregnancy after repeated use of oxymetazoline nasal spray at 5 times the recommended dose. The fetal distress resolved within hours, before delivery of a healthy infant. Estimated systemic exposure in this case was 8 to 18 times higher than plasma exposure after topical administration of oxymetazoline cream. In animal studies, oral administration of oxymetazoline to pregnant rats and rabbits produced no adverse developmental effects at systemic exposures 3 and 73 times, respectively, the maximum recommended human dose of oxymetazoline cream and 7 and 278 times the maximum recommended human ophthalmic dose.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Oxymetazoline is considered compatible with breast-feeding. Although the presence of oxymetazoline in human milk and its effects on milk production are unknown, it can be assumed that very little should reach the breastfed child because of the local administration and limited absorption into the lactating individual's bloodstream. Oxymetazoline is recommended over oral systemic decongestants such as pseudoephedrine during breastfeeding, and topical use on the face is unlikely to present a risk to the breastfed child.

    Headline
    bradycardia, coma, hypertension, hypotension, hypothermia, lethargy, mydriasis, nausea, respiratory depression, sinus tachycardia, vomiting
    General Information
    Serious and life-threatening adverse events requiring hospitalization have been reported to the FDA after children 5 years of age and younger accidentally ingested over-the-counter (OTC) oxymetazoline ophthalmic drops and nasal sprays. No deaths were reported; however, events such as nausea, vomiting, lethargy, sinus tachycardia, respiratory depression, bradycardia, hypotension, hypertension, sedation, somnolence, mydriasis, stupor, hypothermia, drooling, and coma have occurred. Serious adverse events can occur following ingestion of only a small amount (1 to 2 mL) in young children. Since most of these OTC products do not contain child-resistant closures, it is imperative that patients store these products out of reach of children. If a child accidentally swallows these drops, call the National Capital Poison Center (1-800-222-1222) and seek emergency medical attention immediately.

Description
  Text
  Oxymetazoline is an alpha adrenoreceptor agonist that acts as a topically applied sympathomimetic vasoconstrictor. Oxymetazoline is available as a topical cream, ophthalmic solution, and an intranasal spray. The topical cream is approved to treat persistent facial erythema in adults with rosacea. The oxymetazoline 0.1% ophthalmic solution is approved to treat adults with acquired blepharoptosis; while the 0.025% ophthalmic solution is used to relieve redness of the eye due to minor eye irritants. The intranasal formulation is indicated to treat nasal congestion associated with acute or chronic rhinitis, sinusitis, the common cold, hay fever, and other allergies. The maximum recommended treatment duration for the intranasal spray is 3 days; excessive or prolonged use can cause nasal mucosa irritation and recurrent nasal congestion. This drug was approved by the FDA in 1964.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Oxymetazoline stimulates alpha-adrenergic receptors which cause intense vasoconstriction. When administered intranasally, this action promotes drainage and improves ventilation. The 0.025% ophthalmic solution causes vasoconstriction of conjunctival blood vessels thereby providing relief from minor eye irritations. In patients with rosacea, topical use can reduce facial erythema.
 
For the treatment of blepharoptosis, the 0.1% ophthalmic solution targets a subset of adrenoreceptors in the upper eyelid tarsal smooth muscle (Mueller's muscle). Stimulation of these alpha-adrenoceptors causes contraction of the Mueller's muscle and subsequent opening of the eye.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Topical Route
    Description
    A pharmacokinetic study evaluating use of oxymetazoline topical cream in adults with rosacea identified measurable plasma drug concentrations in most subjects. After the first application, mean peak concentrations (Cmax) and exposure (AUC) were 60.5 +/- 53.9 pg/mL and 895 +/- 798 pg x hour/mL, respectively. After once daily applications for 28 days, the mean Cmax and AUC were 66.4 +/- 67.1 pg/mL and 1,050 +/- 992 pg x hour/mL, respectively; thus indicating little drug accumulation.
    Name
    Other Route(s)
    Description
    Ophthalmic Route
Following administration of a single drop of 0.1% ophthalmic solution in each eye (total oxymetazoline dose of 0.07 mg), the median time to reach peak oxymetazoline concentrations was 2 hours (range: 0.5 to 12 hours). The mean peak concentration (Cmax) and systemic exposure (AUC) were 30.5 +/- 12.7 pg/mL and 468 +/- 214 pg x hour/mL, respectively.
  Text
  Oxymetazoline is administered via the topical, ophthalmic, and intranasal routes. After systemic absorption, the drug is approximately 57% bound to human plasma proteins. In vitro studies using human liver microsomes showed oxymetazoline was minimally metabolized, with 96% of the parent drug remaining intact after 120-minute incubation. Excretion has not been determined; however, the mean terminal half-life in healthy adults is 8.3 hours (range: 5.6 to 13.9 hours).
 
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes and drug transporters: none
