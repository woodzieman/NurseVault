Drug Class: Cefprozil is an oral, semisynthetic, second-generation cephalosporin.

Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  General Information
  This drug does not treat viral infection (e.g., common cold). Prescribing cefprozil in the absence of a proven or strongly suspected bacterial infection or a prophylactic indication is unlikely to provide benefit to the patient and increases the risk of the development of drug-resistant bacteria. Patients should be told to complete the full course of treatment, even if they feel better earlier.
 
Cefprozil oral solution contains 28 mg of phenylalanine per 5 ml. Cefprozil should be avoided in patients with phenylketonuria.
 
Cefprozil should be used with caution in patients with renal disease or renal impairment since the drug is eliminated via renal mechanisms. Dosages may need to be reduced in these patients.
 
Cefprozil is contraindicated in patients with cephalosporin hypersensitivity or cephamycin hypersensitivity. Cefprozil should be used cautiously in patients with hypersensitivity to penicillin. The structural similarity between cefprozil and penicillin means that cross-reactivity can occur. Penicillins can cause a variety of hypersensitivity reactions ranging from mild rash to fatal anaphylaxis. Patients who have experienced severe penicillin hypersensitivity should not receive cefprozil. Cross-reactivity to cephalosporins is approximately 3—7% with a documented history to penicillin.
 
Almost all antibacterial agents have been associated with pseudomembranous colitis (antibiotic-associated colitis) which may range in severity from mild to life-threatening. In the colon, overgrowth of Clostridia may exist when normal flora is altered subsequent to antibacterial administration. The toxin produced by Clostridium difficile is a primary cause of pseudomembranous colitis. It is known that systemic use of antibiotics predisposes patients to development of pseudomembranous colitis. Consideration should be given to the diagnosis of pseudomembranous colitis in patients presenting with diarrhea following antibacterial administration. Systemic antibiotics should be prescribed with caution to patients with inflammatory bowel disease such as ulcerative colitis or other GI disease. If diarrhea develops during therapy, the drug should be discontinued. Following diagnosis of pseudomembranous colitis, therapeutic measures should be instituted. In milder cases, the colitis may respond to discontinuation of the offending agent. In moderate to severe cases, fluids and electrolytes, protein supplementation, and treatment with an antibacterial effective against Clostridium difficile may be warranted. Products inhibiting peristalsis are contraindicated in this clinical situation. Practitioners should be aware that antibiotic-associated colitis has been observed to occur over two months or more following discontinuation of systemic antibiotic therapy; a careful medical history should be taken.
 
No overall differences in safety or effectiveness were observed between elderly subjects and younger subjects in clinical studies of cefprozil. In clinical practice, no differences in the type of reported adverse reactions have been observed between elderly and younger patients, but greater sensitivity of some older individuals cannot be ruled out. Cefprozil is substantially excreted by the kidney, and the risk of toxic reactions to this drug may be greater in patients with impaired renal function. Because elderly patients are more likely to have decreased renal function, care should be taken in dose selection; monitoring of renal function may be useful.
 
All cephalosporins may rarely cause hypothrombinemia and have the potential to cause bleeding. Cephalosporins which contain the NMTT side chain (e.g., cefoperazone, cefamandole, cefotetan) have been associated with an increased risk for bleeding. Cephalosporins should be used cautiously in elderly patients and patients with a preexisting coagulopathy (e.g., vitamin K deficiency) since these patients are at a higher risk for developing bleeding complications.
 
Intramuscular injections should be administered cautiously to patients receiving cefprozil. IM injections may cause bleeding, bruising, or hematomas due to hypothrombinemia effect secondary to cephalosporin therapy.
 
Positive direct Coombs' tests have been reported in patients receiving cephalosporins. If hematological testing is done in patients receiving cephalosporins, a false-positive Coombs' test may be due to the antibiotic. Cephalosporins may also produce a false positive reaction for glucose in the urine copper reduction tests (Benedict's or Fehling's solution or with Clinitest tablets), but not with enzyme-based tests for glycosuria (e.g., Clinistix). A false negative reaction may occur in the ferricyanide test for blood glucose. Cefprozil in the blood does not interfere with assays of plasma or urine by the alkaline picrate method.
 
Cefprozil is classified as FDA pregnancy risk category B. Animal data reveal no teratogenic effects. There are, however, no adequate and well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Because animal studies are not always predictive of human response, this drug should be used during pregnancy only if clearly needed. Cefprozil has not been studied for use during labor and delivery. Treatment should be given only if clearly needed.
 
Cephalosporins should be used with caution during breast-feeding, considering the benefit to the mother. Most cephalosporins are excreted in the breast milk in small quantities. Rare potential complications in the nursing infant include alterations of gut flora that might result in diarrhea or other related complications (e.g., dehydration). Nevertheless, the use of most antibiotics is considered compatible with breast feeding. Cephalosporins are generally considered compatible for use for lactating women by the American Academy of Pediatrics and other expert opinions.
 
Cefprozil is approved for use in children, however, age limitations are set for certain conditions. For treating otitis media or acute sinusitis, safe and effective use has only been established in infants and children age 6 months to 12 years; efficacy is not established for infants below age 6 months. Safety and effectiveness in treating pharyngitis/tonsillitis, or uncomplicated skin and skin structure infections (SSSI) are only established in children age 2 to 12 years; cefprozil is not recommended for pharyngitis/tonsillitis or uncomplicated SSSI infections in children below 2 years. Cefprozil and other cephalosporins are not recommended in neonates; accumulation of cephalosporins has been reported in neonates due to prolonged drug half-life.

    Headline
    Drug Interactions
    General Information
    The coadministration of certain medications may lead to harm and require avoidance or therapy modification; review all drug interactions prior to concomitant use of other medications.
    Headline
    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components. Cefprozil is also contraindicated in individuals with known allergy to other cephalosporins. Before starting therapy with cefprozil, carefully inquire about previous hypersensitivity reactions to cephalosporins, penicillins, or carbapenems because cross-hypersensitivity among beta-lactam antibacterial drugs has been reported.
    Headline
    atopy
    General Information
    Hypersensitivity reactions to beta-lactams are more likely to occur in individuals with a history of sensitivity to multiple allergens (atopy). Before starting therapy with cefprozil, carefully inquire about previous hypersensitivity reactions to allergens.
    Headline
    colitis
    General Information
    Use cefprozil with caution in individuals with a history of gastrointestinal disease, particularly colitis.
    Headline
    hepatic failure, malnutrition
    General Information
    Monitor prothrombin time in individuals at risk of developing a prolonged prothrombin time, including those with hepatic failure, malnutrition, those receiving prolonged antimicrobial therapy, or those previously stabilized on anticoagulant therapy, and administer exogenous vitamin K if clinically indicated. Use of cephalosporins has been associated with reduced prothrombin activity.
    Headline
    renal failure, renal impairment, seizure disorder
    General Information
    Cefprozil dosage adjustment is required in individuals with severe renal impairment or renal failure. Seizures and other central nervous system adverse reactions have been reported with cephalosporins, particularly when recommended dosages of cephalosporins were exceeded due to renal impairment or in individuals with a history of seizure disorder. Maintain anticonvulsant therapy in individuals with a history of seizure disorder during treatment with cefprozil. Monitor prothrombin time in individuals at risk of developing a prolonged prothrombin time, including those with renal impairment, and administer exogenous vitamin K if clinically indicated. Use of cephalosporins has been associated with reduced prothrombin activity.
    Headline
    phenylketonuria
    General Information
    Cefprozil oral suspension contains phenylalanine. Phenylalanine can be harmful to individuals with phenylketonuria. In individuals with phenylketonuria, consider the combined daily amount of phenylalanine from all sources if using cefprozil oral suspension.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Data from published epidemiologic studies and pharmacovigilance case reports over several decades with cephalosporin use in human pregnancy have not established drug-associated risks or major birth defects, miscarriage, or adverse maternal or fetal outcomes. Additionally, no adverse developmental effects were observed in animals administered cefprozil at up to 18.5 times the maximum daily human dose.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Cefprozil is compatible with breast-feeding. Small amounts are present in human milk, with a low relative infant dose (RID) of 3.7%. Concentrations of cefprozil in human milk after administration of a 1 gram oral dose ranged from 0.25 to 3.3 mcg/mL, or less than 0.3% of the dose. Adverse effects in the breast-fed child are not expected. Occasionally, diarrhea and thrush caused by disruption of the child's gastrointestinal flora have been reported with cephalosporin use.

Interactions
Amikacin: (Minor) Cefprozil's product label states that cephalosporins may potentiate the adverse renal effects of nephrotoxic agents, such as aminoglycosides and loop diuretics. Carefully monitor renal function, especially during prolonged therapy or use of high aminoglycoside doses. The majority of reported cases involve the combination of aminoglycosides and cephalothin or cephaloridine, which are associated with dose-related nephrotoxicity as singular agents. Limited but conflicting data with other cephalosporins have been noted.

Desogestrel: Ethinyl Estradiol; Ferrous Bisglycinate: (Moderate) It would be prudent to recommend alternative or additional contraception when oral contraceptives (OCs) are used in conjunction with antibiotics. It was previously thought that antibiotics may decrease the effectiveness of OCs containing estrogens due to stimulation of metabolism or a reduction in enterohepatic circulation via changes in GI flora. One retrospective study reviewed the literature to determine the effects of oral antibiotics on the pharmacokinetics of contraceptive estrogens and progestins, and also examined clinical studies in which the incidence of pregnancy with OCs and antibiotics was reported. It was concluded that the antibiotics ampicillin, ciprofloxacin, clarithromycin, doxycycline, metronidazole, ofloxacin, roxithromycin, temafloxacin, and tetracycline did not alter plasma concentrations of OCs. Antituberculous drugs (e.g., rifampin) were the only agents associated with OC failure and pregnancy. Based on the study results, these authors recommended that back-up contraception may not be necessary if OCs are used reliably during oral antibiotic use. Another review concurred with these data, but noted that individual patients have been identified who experienced significant decreases in plasma concentrations of combined OC components and who appeared to ovulate; the agents most often associated with these changes were rifampin, tetracyclines, and penicillin derivatives. These authors concluded that because females most at risk for OC failure or noncompliance may not be easily identified and the true incidence of such events may be under-reported, and given the serious consequence of unwanted pregnancy, that recommending an additional method of contraception during short-term antibiotic use may be justified. During long-term antibiotic administration, the risk for drug interaction with OCs is less clear, but alternative or additional contraception may be advisable in selected circumstances. Data regarding progestin-only contraceptives or for newer combined contraceptive deliveries (e.g., patches, rings) are not available.

These authors concluded that because females most at risk for OC failure or noncompliance may not be easily identified and the true incidence of such events may be under-reported, and given the serious consequence of unwanted pregnancy, that recommending an additional method of contraception during short-term antibiotic use may be justified. During long-term antibiotic administration, the risk for drug interaction with OCs is less clear, but alternative or additional contraception may be advisable in selected circumstances. Data regarding progestin-only contraceptives or for newer combined contraceptive deliveries (e.g., patches, rings) are not available.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Cefprozil, a beta-lactam cephalosporin antibiotic like the penicillins, is mainly bactericidal. It inhibits the third and final stage of bacterial cell wall synthesis by preferentially binding to specific penicillin-binding proteins (PBPs) that are located inside the bacterial cell wall. Penicillin-binding proteins are responsible for several steps in the synthesis of the cell wall and are found in quantities of several hundred to several thousand molecules per bacterial cell. Penicillin-binding proteins vary among different bacterial species. Thus, the intrinsic activity of cefprozil as well as the other cephalosporins and penicillins against a particular organism depends on its ability to gain access to and bind with the necessary PBP. Like all beta-lactam antibiotics, cefprozil's ability to interfere with PBP-mediated cell wall synthesis ultimately leads to cell lysis. Lysis is mediated by bacterial cell wall autolytic enzymes (i.e., autolysins). The relationship between PBPs and autolysins is unclear, but it is possible that the beta-lactam antibiotic interferes with an autolysin inhibitor.
 
Pharmacodynamics
Beta-lactams, including cefprozil, exhibit concentration-independent or time-dependent killing. In vitro and in vivo animal studies have demonstrated that the major pharmacodynamic parameter that determines efficacy for beta-lactams is the amount of time free (non-protein bound) drug concentrations exceed the minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC) of the organism (free T above the MIC). This microbiological killing pattern is due to the mechanism of action, which is acylation of PBPs. There is a maximum proportion of PBPs that can be acylated; therefore, once maximum acylation has occurred, killing rates cannot increase. Free beta-lactam concentrations do not have to remain above the MIC for the entire dosing interval. The percentage of time required for both bacteriostatic and maximal bactericidal activity is different for the various classes of beta-lactams. Cephalosporins require free drug concentrations to be above the MIC for 35% to 40% of the dosing interval for bacteriostatic activity and 60% to 70% of the dosing interval for bactericidal activity.
 
Susceptibility
The susceptibility interpretive criteria for cefprozil are delineated by pathogen. The MICs are defined for Enterobacterales, H. influenzae and H. parainfluenzae as susceptible at 8 mcg/mL or less, intermediate at 16 mcg/mL, and resistant at 32 mcg/mL or more. The Clinical and Laboratory Standards Institute (CLSI) and the FDA differ on MIC interpretation for S. pneumoniae. The MICs are defined for S. pneumoniae by the FDA as susceptible at 0.5 mcg/mL or less and resistant at 1 mcg/mL or more based on an oral dosage of 500 mg every 12 hours for adults and 15 mg/kg/dose every 12 hours for pediatrics; however, the MICs are defined for S. pneumoniae by CLSI as susceptible at 2 mcg/mL, intermediate at 4 mcg/mL, and resistant at 8 mcg/mL or more. Oxacillin-susceptible staphylococci can be considered susceptible to cefprozil. For group A beta-hemolytic streptococci and non-meningitis S. pneumoniae isolates, penicillin susceptibility is a surrogate for cefprozil.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Oral Route
    Description
    Pharmacokinetic data on cefprozil are derived from the capsule form, however, under fasting conditions, the capsule, oral solution, tablet, and suspension formulations are bioequivalent. In fasting subjects, cefprozil is rapidly absorbed from the GI tract with approximately 95% of the dose absorbed. Average peak plasma concentrations of 6.1, 10.5, and 18.3 mcg/mL were obtained following doses of 250 mg, 500 mg, and 1 g, respectively. Peak serum levels of cefprozil occur 1.5 hours following an oral dose. In healthy elderly (>= 65 years of age) volunteers who received a single 1-g dose, the AUC was 35—60% higher than in healthy adult volunteers age 20—40 years. The average AUC in young and elderly female subjects was approximately 15—20% higher than in young and elderly male subjects. Ninety-five percent of an oral dose is fully absorbed. Food does not alter the rate or extent of absorption, however, there was an increase of 0.25—0.75 hours in the time to maximum plasma concentrations (Tmax). The manufacturer reports that the bioavailability of the capsule formulation was not affected by antacid administration; administration of antacids with other formulation has not been evaluated.

Dosage Adjustment Guidelines
  Hepatic Impairment
  The half-life of cefprozil is increased to 2 hours in patients with hepatic impairment. Specific guidelines for dosage adjustment in hepatic impairment are not available; it appears no adjustment is needed.
  Renal Impairment
  CrCl 30 mL/minute or more: No dosage adjustment is needed.
CrCl less than 30 mL/minute: Reduce recommended dose by 50%.
 
Intermittent hemodialysis
Cefprozil is partially removed by hemodialysis. A supplemental dose should be given after each hemodialysis session, or the dosage regimen should be timed so that a dose of cefprozil is scheduled at the end of the dialysis session.
