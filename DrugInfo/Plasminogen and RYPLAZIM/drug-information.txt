Drug Class: Plasminogen, human is plasma-derived human plasminogen approved for the treatment of patients with plasminogen deficiency type 1.

Routes: injection (IV/IM/SC); Common adult doses: 6.6 mg/kg/dose IV every 2 days.

Common Side Effects (excluding routine GI upset): bleeding, epistaxis, gi bleeding, hematuria, hemoptysis, vaginal bleeding, flank pain, gi obstruction, pelvic pain, urinary tract obstruction, arthralgia, back pain, dizziness, fatigue, headache

In an Overdose: No overdose/antidote information is listed in this file.

Key Nursing Considerations: Contraindications/precautions: This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components. Confirm healing of lesions or wounds suspected as a source of a recent bleeding event prior to initiating treatment with plasminogen, human. Infuse through a syringe disc filter using a separate infusion line placed in a peripheral vein. Withdraw 10 mL of 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection into a different syringe and remove any air bubbles.

    infection
    General Information
    Plasminogen is derived from human plasma and therefore carries a remote risk for transmission of viral infection and variant Creutzfeldt-Jakob disease (vCJD). There is a theoretical risk for transmission of Creutzfeldt-Jakob disease (CJD); however, if that risk truly exists it would be considered extremely low. It is also possible that unknown infectious agents are present in plasminogen. The risk of infectious agent transmission has been reduced by screening plasma donors, testing for the presence of current viral infections, and including virus inactivation/removal steps in the manufacturing process. Report all infections thought to be possibly transmitted by plasminogen, human to the manufacturer or FDA.

Description
  Text
  Plasminogen, human is plasma-derived human plasminogen approved for the treatment of patients with plasminogen deficiency type 1. Congenital plasminogen deficiency is a rare autosomal recessive disorder that leads to the development of wood-like pseudomembranes on mucosa. It is a systemic disease most commonly involving the eyes, gingiva, central nervous system, skin, respiratory tract, female genital tract, gastrointestinal system, and genitourinary system. There is no predisposition for thrombosis in patients with plasminogen deficiency. Plasminogen replacement therapy enables a temporary correction of plasminogen deficiency and reduction or resolution of extravascular lesions. Efficacy of plasminogen, human was established in a single-arm, open-label trial including 15 patients (age: 4 to 42 years) with a baseline plasminogen concentration 45% or less of normal and biallelic mutations in the plasminogen (PLG) gene. Resolution of 78% of the external lesions and 75% of the internal lesions occurred by the end of Week 48. No recurrent or new lesions developed through Week 48. All patients with any lesion at baseline had at least 50% improvement in the number and/or size of their lesions. Plasminogen, human was well-tolerated during clinical trials; however, there is risk of bleeding, particularly at lesion or wound sites. Additionally, tissue sloughing at mucosal sites may occur at initiation as plasminogen activity is restored and fibrinolysis occurs; this may lead to bleeding or organ obstruction. Patients with airway disease should initiate therapy in an appropriate clinical setting with personnel trained in airway management.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Plasminogen replacement therapy increases the plasma concentration of plasminogen, enabling a temporary correction of plasminogen deficiency and reduction or resolution of extravascular lesions in patients with plasminogen deficiency type 1.
 
Plasminogen is a protein involved in fibrinolysis, wound healing, cell migration, and tissue formation. Plasminogen is converted into plasmin, which functions to dissolve fibrin clots, cleaning and moistening the membrane surface. When plasminogen is deficient, membranes become thick and woody.

    After the first dose, pharmacokinetic parameters of baseline-adjusted plasminogen activity concentration in adults were as follows: Vd = 62.8 mL/kg; Cmax = 90.9%; clearance = 1.5 mL/kg/hour; half-life = 32.4 hours; mean residence time (MRT) = 29.7 hours.

      Name
      Intravenous Administration
      Description
      Reconstitution
Determine the number of vials required.
Allow vials to equilibrate to room temperature (at least 15 minutes if stored at 41 degrees F [5 degrees C]).
Withdraw 12.5 mL of Sterile Water for Injection for each vial using a sterile 20-mL syringe with an 18- to 22-gauge needle.
If using 10-mL vials of Sterile Water for Injection, withdraw 9 mL from the first vial and discard the needle. Attach a new sterile needle to the syringe and withdraw 3.5 mL Sterile Water for Injection from the second vial. Discard the Sterile Water for Injection vials. Repeat this process for each plasminogen vial.
If using 20- or 50-mL vials of Sterile Water for Injection, each plasminogen vial requires only 1 vial of Sterile Water for Injection.
Slowly add 12.5 mL Sterile Water for Injection to each plasminogen vial, directing the syringe toward the side of the vial to prevent foaming.
Gently swirl by rotating the vial slowly until the powder is fully dissolved. Do not shake. Discard the vial if plasminogen has not fully dissolved within 10 minutes.
Storage: Administer within 3 hours of reconstitution. Do not refrigerate after reconstitution.
 
Preparation
Slowly withdraw the reconstituted solution from each vial into an appropriately sized administration syringe using an 18- to 22- gauge needle. Each reconstituted vial will contain 5.5 mg/mL of plasminogen.
Do not mix with other medications.
 
Administration
Infuse through a syringe disc filter using a separate infusion line placed in a peripheral vein.
Withdraw 10 mL of 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection into a different syringe and remove any air bubbles.
Attach a syringe disc filter to the 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection syringe and the infusion tubing with the butterfly needle.
Inject the 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection through the syringe disc filter and butterfly needle tubing to remove air bubbles.
Remove the 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection syringe, leaving the disc filter attached to the tubing.
Attach the plasminogen administration syringe to the disc filter that is connected to the butterfly needle tubing.
Infuse the dose slowly over 10 to 30 minutes (approximately 5 mL/minute). Push the plunger of the syringe approximately 1 mL every 12 seconds.
