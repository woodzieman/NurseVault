          Dosage Description
          4 mg/m2 IV once every other week; higher doses are not recommended. Continue treatment for 2 doses after the achievement of a complete response (CR). Discontinue pentostatin in patients who do not achieve a CR or partial response (PR) after 6 months of therapy or at the end of 12 months of therapy if the best response is a PR. Prehydrate with 500 to 1,000 mL of IV fluids (e.g., 5% Dextrose and 0.45% Sodium Chloride injection) prior to the pentostatin infusion; give an additional 500 mL of IV fluids after the dose. Therapy interruption or discontinuation may be necessary in patients who experience severe toxicity. Clinical trials with pentostatin in hairy-cell leukemia resulted in a CR of 83% and a PR of 15%. Overall survival was higher than 98% at 9 years for patients achieving CR. The survival at 5 years for PR plateaued at higher than 80%. With a median follow-up of 9.3 years, the 5- and 10-year relapse-free survival rates were 85% and 67%, respectively. The mortality rate and incidence of subsequent malignancies were not higher than expected than the general population.

Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  General Information
  Dose-limiting severe renal, liver, pulmonary, and CNS toxicities occurred in phase 1 studies that used pentostatin at higher doses (20—50 mg/m2 in divided doses over 5 days). Acute pulmonary edema and hypotension leading to death have been reported in the literature in patients treated with pentostatin in combination with carmustine, etoposide, and high dose cyclophosphamide as part of the ablative regimen for bone marrow transplant. Pentostatin should be administered under the supervision of a physician qualified and experienced in the use of cancer chemotherapeutic agents. The use of higher doses than those specified is not recommended.
 
In a clinical investigation in patients with refractory chronic lymphocytic leukemia using pentostatin at the recommended dose in combination with fludarabine phosphate, 4 of 6 patients entered in the study had severe or fatal pulmonary toxicity. Pentostatin use with fludarabine is not recommended (see Interactions).
 
Pentostatin is contraindicated for use by patients who have demonstrated hypersensitivity to pentostatin. Pentostatin use by patients with mannitol hypersensitivity may be inappropriate, as each vial contains 50 mg of mannitol.
 
Pentostatin can cause bone marrow suppression during the first few courses of treatment. Administer pentostatin with extreme caution to patients who may have myelosuppression from prior cytotoxic or radiation therapy. Absolute neutrophil counts must be determined prior to administration of pentostatin, and frequent monitoring of complete blood counts during the initial treatment cycles is needed. If the initial neutrophil count is > 500 cells/mm3 and falls to = 2 weeks. The administration of live vaccines to immunocompromised patients should be avoided. Those undergoing chemotherapy should not be exposed to others who have recently received the oral poliovirus vaccine (OPV). Measles-mumps-rubella (MMR) vaccination is not contraindicated for the close contacts, including health care professionals, of immunocompromised patients. Passive immunoprophylaxis with immune globulins may be indicated for immunocompromised persons instead of, or in addition to, vaccination. When exposed to a vaccine-preventable disease such as measles, severely immunocompromised children should be considered susceptible regardless of their vaccination history.

    Headline
    serious adverse effects
    General Information
    Pentostatin may cause serious adverse effects including dose-limiting severe renal, liver, pulmonary, and central nervous system toxicities. Administration should only be given under the supervision of a physician qualified and experienced in the use of cancer chemotherapeutic agents. The use of higher than the FDA approved dosage is not recommended.
    Headline
    infection
    General Information
    Use pentostatin with caution in patients with an active infection; these patients may develop worsening of their condition. Evaluate the potential benefit for treatment compared with the potential risk to the patient prior to pentostatin initiation or continuation.
    Headline
    renal failure, renal impairment
    General Information
    Use pentostatin with caution in patients with renal impairment, including renal failure; data is insufficient to recommend a starting or a subsequent dose in patients with a creatinine clearance (CrCl) less than 60 mL/min. Evaluate renal function (e.g., serum creatinine level, CrCl) prior to each pentostatin dose and then during therapy as appropriate.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Pentostatin use during pregnancy may cause fetal harm. There are no adequate and well-controlled studies of pentostatin use during human pregnancy. In animal studies, doses equivalent to a human clinical dose on a mg/m2 basis caused maternal toxicity and fetal teratogenicity manifested by increased incidence of various skeletal malformations, early delivery, spontaneous abortions, and fetal death.
    Headline
    people who can cause pregnancy in others, people who may become pregnant, reproductive risk
    General Information
    Counsel patients about the reproductive risk associated with pentostatin. People who may become pregnant should avoid pregnancy and use effective contraception during and for 6 months after the last dose of pentostatin. People who can cause pregnancy in others should use effective contraception during treatment and for 3 months after the last dose. Individuals who become pregnant while receiving pentostatin should be apprised of the potential hazard to the fetus. The possible adverse effects on fertility have not been determined; advise patients on the potential risks of infertility.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in nursing infants from pentostatin, advise individuals to discontinue breast-feeding during treatment and for 1 week after the final dose. It is not known whether pentostatin is present in human milk, although many drugs are excreted in human milk.

  General Information
  NOTE: Withholding or discontinuation of individual doses may be needed when severe adverse reactions occur.Hematologic toxicity secondary to pentostatin therapy can be difficult to quantify, as some patients may already have a degree of myelosuppression. Bone marrow suppression, especially leukopenia, presents a major dose-limiting toxicity of pentostatin in patients with hairy-cell leukemia. Pentostatin therapy produces a high incidence of anemia (32%), neutropenia (45%) and thrombocytopenia (32%). Bleeding episodes have been reported. Pancytopenia recovers as the leukemia responds to treatment. Lymphopenia occurs universally, affects both B and T cells, and may last for months. Other hematologic toxicities (<=10%) reported include agranulocytosis, aplastic anemia, hemolytic anemia, and secondary malignancy specifically acute leukemia. Reports describe cases of thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura (TTP) and hemolytic-uremic syndrome (HUS) associated with the administration of pentostatin for cutaneous T-cell lymphoma. Obtain a complete blood count before each pentostatin dose and at other appropriate periods during therapy. Severe neutropenia has been observed following the early pentostatin treatment courses and therefore, frequent monitoring of complete blood counts is recommended during this time. Pentostatin dosage reductions are not recommended during treatment in patients with anemia and thrombocytopenia if patients can be otherwise supported hematologically. Temporarily withhold pentostatin if the absolute neutrophil count falls during treatment below 200 cells/mm3 in a patient who had an initial neutrophil count greater than 500 cells/mm3; pentostatin may be resumed when the count returns to predose concentrations. If hematologic parameters do not improve with subsequent courses, evaluate patients for disease status including a bone marrow examination.The immunosuppressant effect of pentostatin, coupled with neutropenia, leads to a high incidence of infection. Infections may be bacterial, but opportunistic viruses and fungi may be causative agents. Withhold pentostatin in patients with an active infection that occurs during treatment; pentostatin may be resumed when the infection is controlled.Dermatologic adverse events with pentostatin are common. A maculopapular rash with erythema occurs in up to 67% of patients. The skin rash (unspecified) may vary from mild to moderately severe. Rashes may worsen with continued treatment; withhold pentostatin in patients with severe rash. Other skin reactions include a bullous rash, ecchymosis, petechiae (< 3%), pruritus (21%), skin discoloration, urticaria (3—10%), xerosis (dry skin) (3—10%).Use of pentostatin at higher doses (20—50 mg/m2 in divided doses over 5 days) in phase I clinical trials resulted in dose-limiting and severe central nervous system (CNS) toxicity; the use of higher doses than those specified is not recommended. At current dosage levels, headache (17%), lethargy (62%), and fatigue (42%) occur and are commonly mild in nature. Additional CNS toxicities occurring less frequently (<= 10%) include abnormal dreams, amnesia, anxiety, ataxia, confusion, depression, dizziness, dysarthria, emotional lability, encephalopathy, hallucinations, hostility, hyperkinesis, insomnia, meningism, neuralgia, neuritis, neuropathy, neurosis, muscle paralysis, paresthesias, photosensitivity, seizures, somnolence, syncope, twitching, and vertigo. Withhold or discontinue pentostatin in patients showing evidence of nervous system toxicity.Ocular and otic adverse reactions have been reported in < 3% of patients treated with pentostatin during clinical trials including amblyopia, conjunctivitis, deafness/hearing loss, ear aches, labyrinthitis, lacrimation disorder, nonreactive eye, photophobia, retinopathy, tinnitus, unusual taste, visual impairment/vision abnormalities, watery eyes, and xerophthalmia.Anorexia occurs in more than 13% of patients receiving pentostatin. Diarrhea is also a common adverse event (17%). Nausea/vomiting may occur in up to 60% of patients but is usually mild-to-moderate and easily controlled. The onset of nausea/vomiting is 12—24 hours following the dose of pentostatin and may last for up to 48 hours. Abdominal pain (16%) and stomatitis (12%) have also been reported. Pentostatin therapy has been associated with elevated hepatic enzymes in 19% of patients, particularly with higher doses of pentostatin; elevations in liver function tests were generally reversible. Dose-limiting severe hepatic toxicity occurred in phase 1 studies that used pentostatin at higher doses (20—50 mg/m2 in divided doses over 5 days). The use of higher doses than those specified is not recommended. Other GI adverse reactions reported during clinical trials (<= 10%) include constipation, dental abnormalities, dyspepsia, dysphagia, flatulence, gingivitis, glossitis, and ileus.Several cardiovascular complications have been recorded during therapy with pentostatin (<= 10%) including angina, arrhythmias (i.e., AV block, bradycardia extrasystoles ventricular, tachycardia), abnormal ECG, cardiac arrest, chest pain (unspecified), heart failure, hypertension, hypotension, pericardial effusion, pulmonary embolism, deep venous thromboembolism, thrombo-phlebitis, and vasculitis. However, direct causality by pentostatin has not been established, and some of these effects were related to underlying cardiovascular disease. Direct cardiotoxicity of pentostatin seems unlikely. Edema has been reported in some patients including facial edema and peripheral edema (3—10%). Dose-limiting severe pulmonary toxicity occurred in phase 1 studies that used pentostatin at higher doses (20—50 mg/m2 in divided doses over 5 days). Acute pulmonary edema and hypotension leading to death have been reported in the literature in patients treated with pentostatin in combination with carmustine, etoposide, and high dose cyclophosphamide as part of the ablative regimen for bone marrow transplant. The use of higher doses than those specified is not recommended.Acute renal toxicity, although generally mild and reversible with use of the recommended pentostatin dose, has been reported. The use of higher doses than those specified is not recommended. Dose-limiting severe renal toxicity occurred in phase 1 studies that used pentostatin at higher doses (20—50 mg/m2 in divided doses over 5 days). Hydration during and following pentostatin administration should be given. The use of concomitant nephrotoxins should be avoided. Renal toxicity reported during clinical trials of pentostatin (< 3%) includes azotemia, nephropathy, renal failure (unspecified), renal insufficiency, and renal stones. Elevations in serum creatinine were usually minor and reversible, but some patients with normal renal function at treatment initiation had evidence of mild to moderate toxicity at a final assessment. Determine renal function with a serum creatinine and/or a creatinine clearance assay before pentostatin initiation. Also, assess serum creatinine before each pentostatin dose and at other appropriate periods during therapy. Withhold pentostatin in patients who have elevated serum creatinine concentrations; determine a creatinine clearance. There are insufficient data to recommend a starting or a subsequent dose for patients with a creatinine clearance < 60 ml/minute. Only treat patients with impaired renal function when the potential benefit justifies the potential risk. Two patients with creatinine clearances of 50—60 ml/minute achieved a complete response without unusual adverse events when treated with 2 mg/m2.Other frequent adverse reactions include asthenia/weakness (12%), arthralgia (6%), chills (19%), cough (20%), diaphoresis (8%), dyspnea (11%), fever (46%), and myalgia (19%). The following adverse reactions were reported in <=10% of patients treated with pentostatin in clinical trials: amenorrhea, arthritis, asthma, bronchospasm, libido decrease, flu-like symptoms, gout, hypercalcemia, hyponatremia, and laryngeal edema.Pentostatin-induced cellular destruction can result in tumor lysis syndrome (TLS) which includes hyperkalemia, hyperphosphatemia, hyperuricemia, and hypocalcemia. Uric acid nephropathy, acute renal failure due renal tubular obstruction, metabolic acidosis, and nephrolithiasis can also occur. These problems are more severe when a highly chemosensitive disease is treated (i.e., leukemia or lymphoma). Aggressive alkalinization of the urine and use of allopurinol can prevent urate nephropathy; close monitoring of serum electrolytes and renal function is recommended. The onset of TLS may be delayed in patients who receive pentostatin.

Description
  Text
  Pentostatin is a synthetic purine nucleoside antimetabolite. It is indicated for the treatment of hairy-cell leukemia in patients with active disease that is previously untreated or refractory to alpha-interferon therapy. Using a dose that is higher than the FDA-approved dosage is not recommended due to the potential for severe renal, hepatic, pulmonary, and nervous system toxicity. Additionally, the use of pentostatin in combination with fludarabine resulted in severe or fatal pulmonary toxicity in patients with refractory chronic lymphocytic leukemia; therefore, the use of pentostatin and fludarabine is not recommended.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Mechanism of Action: Pentostatin is a potent transition state inhibitor of the enzyme adenosine deaminase (ADA), which acts as a regulator on intracellular adenosine levels. Following inhibition, an increase in intracellular deoxyadenosine triphosphate (dATP) results in apoptosis of B and T cells. dATP is an allosteric inhibitor of ribonucleotide reductase. The ultimate effect on inhibition of ribonucleotide reductase and depletion of other deoxynucleotides affects DNA and RNA synthesis. This inhibition results in the arrest of cells in the G1 or S phase of the cell cycle and subsequent cell dysfunction and death. Pentostatin also decreases levels of nicotinamide, leading to depletion of nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide. Death of lymphocytes can be partly attributed to a reduction of this important vitamin. T lymphocytes have a much higher level of adenosine deaminase than B lymphocytes. This difference explains the increased susceptibility of T lymphocytes to the cytotoxic effects of pentostatin. Higher doses of pentostatin are required for cells containing greater amounts of adenosine deaminase. Other observed pharmacologic actions include inhibition of RNA transcription, production of DNA strand breaks, and incorporation of pentostatin triphosphate in DNA. Resistance mechanisms via p53 pathways are under investigation. The TP53 gene is mutated and/or deleted in approximately 10% to 15% of patients with CLL while 15% to 20% of patients exhibit a mutation in the gene encoding ATM (ataxia telangiectasia mutated), a kinase regulating p53. Such mutations result in impaired accumulation of p53 and thereby resistance to killing. Drug resistance also arises from low cellular capacity for purine analogue phosphorylation. The clinical response to purine analogues also appears to correlate with the expression of Bcl-2 proteins. Strategies for overcoming purine analogue resistance include the following: combine purine analogue with monoclonal antibodies, combine with Bcl-2 antisense oligonucleotide, or reestablishment of p53 function via insertion of peptides corresponding to the C-terminus of wild-type p53.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Intravenous Route
    Description
    Mean plasma concentrations of pentostatin are proportional to the dose. Distribution half-life has been recorded at 11 minutes following a dose of 4 mg/m2 infused over 5 minutes.
  Special Populations
    Name
    Renal Impairment
    Description
    There is a positive correlation between pentostatin clearance and renal function in patients with creatine clearance (CrCl) ranging from 60—130 ml/min with a half-life of 6 hours. For patients with CrCl < 50 ml/min, the half-life is 18 hours. Patients with elevated serum creatinine should have their dose withheld and a CrCl determined. There are insufficient data to recommend a starting or subsequent dose for patients with impaired renal function. In a study comparing the pharmacokinetics of pentostatin in patients with renal insufficiency to those with normal renal function, the mean plasma clearance in the normal renal function (NRF) patients was 114 ml/min compared to 72 ml/min in impaired renal function (IRF) patients. The mean half life was 5.35 hours (NRF) compared to 7.16 hours (IRF). This study revealed that CrCl predicts total pentostatin plasma clearance but not pentostatin renal clearance. Pentostatin total plasma clearance and renal clearance were lower in patients with impaired renal function.
  Text
  Pentostatin is administered intravenously. 
 
Plasma protein binding is only 4%. Pentostatin has limited penetration of the blood/brain barrier. Concentrations of pentostatin in cerebrospinal fluid reach 10—12% of those in plasma 2—4 hours after IV administration. CSF half-life is > 11 hours. Pentostatin undergoes little metabolism. Excretion is 90% renal, mainly as unchanged drug. The mean terminal half-life is 5.7 hours (range of 2.6—15 hours). Mean total clearance is independent of dosage, but correlates with creatinine clearance as 90% of the drug is excreted unchanged in urine.
