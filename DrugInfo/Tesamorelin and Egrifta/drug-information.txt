    Headline
    critical illness
    General Information
    The use of tesamorelin has not been studied in critically ill patients; however, increased mortality has been reported in individuals administered growth hormone (GH) with acute critical illness following abdominal surgery, coronary artery bypass graft surgery (CABG), multiple accidental trauma, and in individuals with acute respiratory failure (respiratory insufficiency). Since tesamorelin stimulates GH production, consider withholding therapy in the critically ill population.
    Headline
    pituitary insufficiency
    General Information
    Tesamorelin is contraindicated in individuals with hypopituitarism (pituitary insufficiency) due to hypophysectomy, pituitary tumor or surgery, head irradiation, or head trauma.
    Headline
    malignancy
    General Information
    Tesamorelin is contraindicated in individuals with active malignancy; any preexisting malignancy should be inactive and its treatment complete prior to instituting therapy. Tesamorelin induces the release of endogenous growth hormone (GH), a known growth factor. For individuals with a history of non-malignant neoplasms, initiate tesamorelin therapy after careful evaluation of the potential benefit of treatment. For individuals with a history of treated and stable malignancies, initiate tesamorelin therapy only after careful evaluation of the potential benefit of treatment relative to the risk of re-activation of the underlying malignancy. Carefully consider the decision to start treatment with tesamorelin based on the increased background risk of malignancies in HIV-positive individuals.
    Headline
    diabetes mellitus
    General Information
    Diabetes mellitus and diabetic retinopathy may result from treatment with tesamorelin. In clinical trials, reports of elevated HbA1c (6.5% or more) occurred more frequently in individuals treated with tesamorelin (4.5%) than with placebo (1.3%). Based on data from these trials, it was determined that tesamorelin therapy is associated with an increased risk of developing diabetes mellitus (intent-to-treat odds ratio = 3.3). The manufacturer recommends monitoring serum glucose status in all tesamorelin-treated individuals. Anti-diabetic medications may need to be adjusted. Additionally, health care providers are encouraged to monitor diabetic individuals treated with the drug for signs of developing or worsening retinopathy. Consider the risk to benefit ratio of continuing therapy in individuals who develop glucose intolerance or diabetes.
    Headline
    children, infants, neonates
    General Information
    Tesamorelin is only indicated for use in adults age 18 to 65 years. Safety and efficacy have not been established in adolescents, children, infants, or neonates. In pediatric patients with open epiphyses, tesamorelin may cause linear growth acceleration and excessive growth.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Tesamorelin is contraindicated for use during pregnancy. There is no benefit to modifying visceral adipose tissue during pregnancy, and such interventions may pose risks to the fetus. Administration to rats during organogenesis and lactation resulted in hydrocephalus in offspring at a dose approximately 2- and 4-times the clinical dose, respectively, based on measured drug exposure (AUC). If pregnancy occurs, discontinue tesamorelin therapy. Advise pregnant patients of the potential hazard to the fetus.

Interactions
Cortisone: (Moderate) Use caution when coadministering tesamorelin with cortisone as their concurrent use may decrease the effectiveness of the steroids. Tesamorelin stimulates the production of growth hormone, which is known to inhibit the enzyme 11-beta-hydroxysteroid dehydrogenase type 1 (11-beta-HSD-1); cortisone and prednisone require the 11-beta-HSD-1 enzyme for conversion to their active metabolites. Patients with hypoadrenalism receiving treatment with cortisone or prednisone may required increased maintenance or stress doses after initiation of tesamorelin.
Ezetimibe; Simvastatin: (Moderate) Use caution when coadministering tesamorelin with simvastatin as their concurrent use may alter simvastatin plasma concentrations. In a pharmacokinetic study, multiple 2 mg doses of tesamorelin administered with simvastatin resulted in an 8% decrease in simvastatin AUC and a 5% increase in simvastatin Cmax. The clinical impact of these pharmacokinetic changes are unknown; however, patients should be monitored for decreased simvastatin efficacy. Further, since simvastatin is a substrate for CYP3A4, it may be theorized that tesamorelin has little impact on CYP3A activity.
Lopinavir; Ritonavir: (Minor) Use caution when coadministering tesamorelin with ritonavir as their concurrent use may alter ritonavir plasma concentrations.     Headline
    diabetes mellitus, hyperglycemia, retinopathy
    General Information
    Tesamorelin stimulates growth hormone production and increases serum IGF-1 and, thus, may cause hyperglycemia. In clinical trials, mean concentrations of fasting blood glucose and fasting insulin were not significantly different between tesamorelin-treated and placebo-treated patients, but elevated HbA1c (6.5% or more) was reported more frequently during treatment with tesamorelin (4.5%) than with placebo (1.3%). Based on data from these trials, it was determined that the use of tesamorelin increases the risk of developing diabetes mellitus [OR = 3.3 (CI 1.4, 9.6)]. Additionally, patients with diabetes who receive tesamorelin are at increased risk of developing retinopathy; retinopathy was reported in 1.1% of patients receiving tesamorelin during a 52-week clinical trial; monitor patients with diabetes at regular intervals for the development or worsening of retinopathy. Determine a patient's glucose status before tesamorelin initiation and periodically during drug receipt. If glucose intolerance or diabetes develops, consider tesamorelin discontinuation if a clear efficacy response as judged by the degree of reduction in visceral adipose tissue by waist circumference or CT scan measurements is not obtained.

    Headline
    new primary malignancy
    General Information
    Tesamorelin increases the serum concentrations of two growth factors, growth hormone (GH) and insulin-like growth factor 1 (IGF-1) and may increase the risk of developing a new primary malignancy. The effect of prolonged elevations in IGF-1 concentrations on the development or progression of malignancies is unknown. During clinical trials, one patient was diagnosed with lung cancer 5 months after discontinuing tesamorelin. An analysis revealed the patient received tesamorelin for 3 months, during which his IGF-1 concentrations remained within normal range. The authors of the trial determined that the lung cancer was most likely unrelated to tesamorelin. Among tesamorelin recipients, 47.4% had IGF-1 concentrations greater than 2 standard deviation scores (SDS), and 35.6% had SDS greater than 3, as early as 13 weeks of treatment. After 52 weeks of therapy, 33.7% had IGF-1 SDS greater than 2, and 22.6% had IGF-1 SDS greater than 3. Closely monitor IGF-1 concentrations during tesamorelin receipt, and consider tesamorelin discontinuation in patients with persistent elevations of IGF-1 concentrations such as those greater than 3 SDS, particularly if the efficacy response based on visceral adipose tissue changes measured by waist circumference or CT scan is not robust. Use of tesamorelin is contraindicated by patients with a newly diagnosed or recurrent malignancy. Further, consider the increased background risk of malignancies in HIV-positive patients before tesamorelin initiation. Any preexisting malignancy needs to be inactive and its treatment complete before tesamorelin initiation; use tesamorelin only after careful evaluation of the potential benefit of treatment relative to the risk of reactivation of the underlying malignancy.

    General Information
    Antibody formation to tesamorelin may occur. In trials, anti-tesamorelin IgG antibodies were detected in 49.5% of patients treated for 26 weeks and in 47.4% of patients treated for 52 weeks. Description
  Text
  Tesamorelin is a synthetic growth hormone releasing factor (GRF) analog that is given subcutaneously. It is the first treatment indicated to reduce excess abdominal fat in HIV-infected patients with lipodystrophy. Treatment was well-tolerated during clinical trials with hypersensitivity and injection site reactions being the most commonly reported adverse events. Treatment is contraindicated in patients with disruption of the hypothalamic-pituitary axis, active malignancy, known hypersensitivity to the drug or mannitol, and in pregnant women. The drug was approved based on the results of 2 clinical trials. During these trials, HIV-infected adult patients treated with tesamorelin over 52 weeks experienced a 17.5% and 18.1% reduction in visceral adipose tissue (VAT) when compared with baseline (p less than 0.001). These results were achieved without affecting glucose homeostasis or subcutaneous or extremity fat reserves. Treatment is contraindicated in patients with disruption of hypothalamic-pituitary axis, active malignancy, known hypersensitivity to the drug or mannitol, and in pregnant women.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Tesamorelin is a synthetic analog of the hypothalamic produced peptide known as growth hormone releasing factor (GRF). It binds to growth hormone releasing factor receptors on the pituitary somatotroph cells. This binding stimulates the production and pulsatile release of endogenous growth hormone (GH). Growth hormone interacts with a number of target cells including chondrocytes, osteoblasts, myocytes, hepatocytes, and adipocytes to produce both anabolic and lipolytic pharmacologic effects. The lipolytic (lipid and carbohydrate metabolizing) effects are directly related to GH; however, the anabolic effects result from GH induced production of other hormones known as insulin-like growth factors, specifically insulin-like growth factor 1 (IGF-1). Administration of tesamorelin increases the release of both GH and IGF-1, thus producing both lipolytic and anabolic pharmacologic effects.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Subcutaneous Route
    Description
    The absolute bioavailability of tesamorelin is less than 4% following subcutaneous administration. In a pharmacokinetic study involving healthy volunteers, a single subcutaneous 2 mg dose using the 1 mg/vial (Egrifta) formulation resulted in a mean exposure (AUC) of 634.6 pg x hour/mL and a mean peak plasma concentration (Cmax) of 2,874.6 pg/mL. In another study involving healthy volunteers, a single subcutaneous 1.4 mg dose using the 2 mg/vial (Egrifta SV) formulation resulted in a mean AUC of 889.1 pg x hour/mL and a mean peak plasma concentration (Cmax) of 2,956.1 pg/mL. The systemic exposure (Cmax and AUC) of tesamorelin is similar between the 1.28 mg dose using the 11.6 mg/vial (Egrifta WR) formulation and the 2 mg dose from the 1 mg/vial (Egrifta) formulation. Following a single subcutaneous dose of 1.28 mg from the 11.6 mg/vial (Egrifta WR) formulation in healthy subjects, the mean AUC was 1,172 pg x hour/mL and the mean Cmax was 3,831 pg/mL. The time to reach the peak plasma concentration (Tmax) was 0.15 hours for all three formulations.

      NOTE: There are two formulations of tesamorelin available (Egrifta SV and Egrifta WR) with different strengths, dosages, reconstitution instructions, and storage requirements. These products are not substitutable.
 
Reconstitution of the 2 mg/vial formulation (Egrifta SV):
Add 0.5 mL of the provided diluent (Sterile Water for Injection) to the vial containing lyophilized powder. To avoid foaming, push the plunger in slowly with the needle on a slight angle so the sterile water goes down the inside wall of the tesamorelin vial.
Mix by gently rolling the vial between hands for 30 seconds. Do not shake the vial.
The reconstituted solution (concentration: 2 mg/0.5 mL) should be clear, colorless, and without particulate matter. Do not use if the solution contains particulates, is cloudy, or is discolored.
Place the needle cap on its side against a clean flat surface. Without touching the needle, hold the syringe and slide the needle carefully into the protective cap. Push the cap all the way or until it snaps shut. Do not touch the cap until it covers the needle completely. Remove the needle.
Place a new 1/2-inch 30-gauge needle onto the syringe.
Administer dose immediately after reconstitution, and discard any unused solution and diluent. If not used immediately, discard the reconstituted solution. Do not freeze or refrigerate.
 
Reconstitution of the 11.6 mg/vial formulation (Egrifta WR):
Available as a 28-day supply in two boxes containing:
four 11.6 mg single-patient-use vials (each vial provides 7 daily doses, mix only 1 vial per week)
one multi-dose 30 mL bottle of Bacteriostatic Water for Injection (to be used once weekly for mixing)
33 syringes, needles, and alcohol swab (do not use a syringe or needle more than 1 time)
Use a provided 3 mL syringe with needle attached (clear cap) for the weekly mixing.
To one of the vials containing the lyophilized powder, add 1.3 mL of the provided Bacteriostatic Water for Injection diluent to prepare 7 doses. Place the Bacteriostatic Water bottle back into the injection box to store for the next weekly mixing. Discard the Bacteriostatic Water bottle after 28 days of use.
Discard the 3 mL syringe and needle in a sharps container.
Mix the powder and diluent by moving the vial in a circle. Do not shake the vial.
Let the vial rest for a few seconds until the layer of foam separates and the solution becomes clear and colorless, with no particles. Do not use the vial if, after a few seconds, the solution looks cloudy, is discolored, or has particles in it.
Use a provided BD SafeGlide syringe with needle attached (orange cap) to obtain the daily dose from the reconstituted vial.
Insert the needle into the reconstituted vial and pull the plunger slowly to reach the 18 mark (0.18 mL). Remove the needles from the vial, turn the syringe upright, and tap with your finger to force any air bubbles to the top.
Adjust the plunger to reach exactly the 16 mark (0.16 mL) to provide the recommended 1.28 mg (0.16 mL) dose.
Store the reconstituted vial for the next daily injection in the Medication box at room temperature of 20 to 25 degrees C (68 to 77 degrees F). Discard the reconstituted viral after it has been used for 7 daily injections.
 
Subcutaneous injection:
Subcutaneously inject the reconstituted solution into the abdomen. Avoid injection into scar tissue, bruises or the navel. Injection sites should be rotated to different areas of the abdomen.
Slowly push the plunger all the way down until all the medicine in the syringe has been injected under the skin.
After removing the injection from the skin, flip back the needle shield until it snaps, covering the injection needle completely. Keep pressing until a click is heard, which means the injection needle is protected.
Using a piece of sterile gauze to rub the injection site clean. If there is bleeding, apply pressure to the injection site with gauze for 30 seconds. If bleeding continues, apply a bandage to the site.
Properly dispose of used syringes and needles in a sharps container.
