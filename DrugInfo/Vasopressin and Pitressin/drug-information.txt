Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  General Information
  Vasopressin should be administered with caution to patients with goiter associated with cardiac complications, seizure disorder, migraine, asthma, coronary thrombosis, angina, heart failure, or arteriosclerosis. Rapid increases in extracellular fluid could be hazardous to these patients. Periodic ECG monitoring is recommended. In addition, since vasopressin can precipitate angina or myocardial infarction, the drug should be used with extreme caution in patients with coronary artery disease or peripheral vascular disease.Patients with pre- or postoperative polyuria may have considerably less vasopressin requirements than normal. Vasopressin should be used cautiously in these patients.Vasopressin can have more potent effects in the elderly and in children. Use vasopressin cautiously in these patients.Vasopressin is contraindicated for use in patients with chronic nephritis accompanied by nitrogen retention. The hormone should not be administered to these patients until acceptable nitrogen concentrations are restored. Rapid increases in extracellular fluid could be hazardous to patients with chronic renal disease.Vasopressin is classified as pregnancy category C. No adequate studies have been conducted on the effects of this drug on the human fetus. Similarly, potential adverse effects to the nursing infant have not been studied. Therefore, vasopressin should be used during pregnancy or breast-feeding only when clearly needed.Patients receiving vasopressin should be monitored for signs of water intoxication which include listlessness, drowsiness, confusion, headache, anuria, and weight gain. Vasopressin-induced water intoxication can lead to seizures, coma, and death. Electrolyte imbalance also should be assessed periodically because hyponatremia can occur.

    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components. The Vasostrict 10 mL multi-dose vial contains chlorobutanol and is contraindicated in individuals with a known hypersensitivity to chlorobutanol.
    Headline
    coronary artery disease, heart failure
    General Information
    Use vasopressin with caution in individuals with heart failure or any state in which a rapid addition to extracellular water may produce hazard for an already overburdened system. Vasopressin may also decrease cardiac index. Do not use vasopressin in individuals with vascular disease, especially coronary artery disease, except with extreme caution. In such individuals, even small doses may precipitate anginal pain, and with larger doses, consider the possibility of myocardial infarction.
    Headline
    migraine, seizure disorder
    General Information
    Use vasopressin with caution in individuals with a seizure disorder or migraine. A rapid addition to extracellular water may exacerbate these conditions.
    Headline
    asthma
    General Information
    Use vasopressin with caution in individuals with asthma. A rapid addition to extracellular water may exacerbate this condition.
    Headline
    chronic nephritis
    General Information
    Vasopressin is contraindicated in chronic nephritis with nitrogen retention until reasonable nitrogen blood concentrations are attained.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    There are no available data on vasopressin use during pregnancy to inform a drug-associated risk of major birth defects, miscarriage, or other adverse outcomes. Animal reproduction studies have not been conducted. Because of increased clearance of vasopressin in the second and third trimester, the vasopressin dose may need to be increased in pregnant individuals. When used as a vasopressor, vasopressin may produce tonic uterine contractions that could threaten the continuation of pregnancy. Doses sufficient for an antidiuretic effect are not likely to such contractions.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Use vasopressin with caution during breast-feeding. There are no data on the presence of vasopressin in human milk, its effects on the breast-fed child, or its effects on milk production. Because vasopressin is not orally absorbed, it is unlikely to adversely affect a breastfed child. Consider the benefits of breast-feeding, the patient's clinical need for treatment, and any potential adverse effects on the breast-fed child from the medication or from the patient's underlying medical condition.

Adverse Reactions
  Headline
  Adverse Reactions
  General Information
  The most common adverse effects to vasopressin therapy include circumoral pallor, diaphoresis, injection site reaction, tremor, abdominal pain, flatulence, vertigo, eructation and/or nausea/vomiting. These adverse effects are mild and infrequent at low doses, however, at higher doses they increase in both frequency and severity. Diarrhea, uterine contractions, and intestinal hypermotility also have been reported. Administration of vasopressin with 1—2 glasses of water may prevent or alleviate these effects.Water intoxication can occur with vasopressin therapy. The risk of overhydration from vasopressin therapy is small in patients without excessive fluid intake. Signs of water intoxication include listlessness, drowsiness, confusion, headache, anuria, and weight gain. Vasopressin-induced water intoxication can also lead to seizures, coma, and death. Electrolyte balance should be assessed periodically because hyponatremia can occur.Large doses of vasopressin can cause hypertension, sinus bradycardia, arrhythmias, AV block, premature atrial contractions (PACs), coronary insufficiency, myocardial infarction, myocardial ischemia, and decreased cardiac output. Angina may also be induced by vasopressin especially in patients with mild forms of vascular disease. Coronary vasodilating agents such as amyl nitrite or nitroglycerin may be used to treat angina if it occurs.Hypersensitivity reactions can occur secondary to vasopressin therapy. Symptoms include angioedema, urticaria, bronchoconstriction, wheezing, circulatory collapse, cardiac arrest, and anaphylactic shock.Injection of vasopressin into the superior mesenteric artery for the treatment of upper GI tract hemorrhage has resulted in several adverse effects. Coronary thrombosis, venous thrombosis, infarction and necrosis of the small bowel, and peripheral emboli have all been reported following administration of vasopressin by this route.

Description
  Text
  Vasopressin is parenteral exogenous antidiuretic hormone (ADH) of the posterior pituitary gland indicated to increase blood pressure in adults with vasodilatory shock who remain hypotensive despite fluids and catecholamines and for prevention and treatment of postoperative abdominal distention, in abdominal roentgenography to dispel interfering gas shadows and in diabetes insipidus. Studies have demonstrated a catecholamine-sparing effect of vasopressin, suggesting early use of vasopressin in combination with norepinephrine may reduce the adrenergic burden associated with traditional vasopressors. Guidelines recommend adding vasopressin to norepinephrine instead of escalating the dose of norepinephrine in patients with septic shock and inadequate mean arterial pressures.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Vasopressin exerts its antidiuretic effects by increasing the resorption of water at the renal collecting ducts. Subsequently, urinary flow is reduced and urine osmolality is increased, thereby maintaining serum osmolality within an acceptable physiological range. At the cellular level, vasopressin stimulates adenylate cyclase activity. Increased adenylate cyclase activity increases the levels of cyclic adenosine monophosphate (cAMP) in the distal convoluted tubule and the collecting duct, thereby enhancing the permeability of the membrane to free water. Physiologic doses of vasopressin are administered to patients with neurohypophyseal diabetes insipidus to conserve the large amounts of water that are normally lost in the urine. In contrast to desmopressin, vasopressin induces the release of adrenocorticotropic hormone, increases plasma cortisol concentrations, and exhibits vasopressive and contractile action on visceral smooth muscle.
 
Vasopressin stimulates the contraction of vascular smooth muscle in coronary, splanchnic, GI, pancreatic, skin, and muscular vascular beds. This effect is most prominent in the capillaries, small arterioles, and venules with less effect on the smooth musculature of large veins. Neither adrenergic agents nor vascular denervation prevents the direct effect of vasopressin on smooth muscle contractile elements. This vasoconstrictive property has been used in managing massive GI hemorrhage; however, vasopressin is no longer indicated for this purpose.

    Pregnancy
The clearance of exogenous and endogenous vasopressin increases gradually over the course of pregnancy because of a spillover into blood of placental vasopressinase. During the first trimester of pregnancy, vasopressin clearance is only slightly increased. However, by the third trimester, clearance is increased 4-fold and at term up to 5-fold.
