Drug Class: Benznidazole is an antiprotozoal used for the treatment of Chagas disease or American trypanosomiasis, a parasitic infection caused by Trypanosoma cruzi.

Routes: oral; Common adult doses: Safety and efficacy have not been established; however, doses up to 8 mg/kg/day (Usual Max: 400 mg/day) PO have been used off-label.

    General Information

    Headline
    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components. Benznidazole is also contraindicated in individuals with a history of hypersensitivity to other nitroimidazole derivatives.
    Headline
    Cockayne syndrome
    General Information
    Benznidazole is contraindicated in individuals with Cockayne syndrome. Cases of severe irreversible hepatotoxicity and acute hepatic failure, including fatal outcomes with very rapid onset, have been reported in these individuals after treatment initiation with systemic metronidazole, another nitroimidazole agent, structurally related to benznidazole. The latency period from starting metronidazole to signs of liver failure was as short as 2 days.
    Headline
    bone marrow suppression
    General Information
    Use benznidazole only under strict medical supervision in individuals with hematological manifestations of bone marrow suppression. Hematological abnormalities, such as neutropenia, thrombocytopenia, anemia, and leukopenia, which resolved after treatment discontinuation, have been reported with benznidazole. Monitor complete blood count with differential before, during, and after benznidazole therapy.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Based on animal data, benznidazole may cause fetal harm if used during pregnancy. Published human data are insufficient to inform a drug-associated risk. In animal reproduction studies, oral administration of benznidazole to rats during organogenesis was associated with fetal malformations, such as anasarca, anophthalmia, and/or microphthalmia, at doses approximately 1 to 3 times the maximum recommended human dose (MRHD). Ventricular septal defects were observed when pregnant rabbits were given doses of approximately 0.3 to 1 times the MRHD. Effects on first generation offspring in rats exposed to benznidazole during gestation and through lactation included reduced fertility in males and reduced numbers of live embryos and fetuses in pregnant females at a dose equivalent to approximately 1.5 times the MRHD. There are risks to the fetus associated with Chagas disease. However, findings are inconsistent regarding risks of pregnancy loss, prematurity, and neonatal mortality in people with chronic Chagas disease. Treatment of chronic Chagas disease during pregnancy is not recommended due to the risk of embryo-fetal toxicity from benznidazole. For people with acute, symptomatic Chagas disease, consider the risks and benefits to the pregnant person and fetus prior to initiating treatment.
    Headline
    people who can cause pregnancy in others, people who may become pregnant, reproductive risk
    General Information
    Counsel people who may become pregnant and people who can cause pregnancy in others about the reproductive risk associated with benznidazole. Benznidazole may cause fetal harm. Verify pregnancy status prior to treatment initiation. Advise people who may become pregnant to use effective contraception while taking benznidazole and for 5 days after the last dose. Based on animal data, benznidazole may impair male fertility. In rats, the effect on fertility was reversible 22 weeks after dosing. The effects on humans are unknown.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Avoid the use of benznidazole during breast-feeding due to the potential for serious side effects in the breastfed child, and the transmission of Chagas disease. Limited data reports benznidazole is present in human milk at infant doses of 5.5% to 17% of the lactating individual's weight-adjusted dosage with a milk to plasma ratio of 0.3 to 2.79. There are no reports of adverse effects on the breastfed child or data on the effect of benznidazole on milk production.

Interactions
Disulfiram: (Contraindicated) Coadministration of benznidazole with disulfiram or in patients who have taken disulfiram within the last 2 weeks is contraindicated. Although not reported with benznidazole, psychotic reactions have been reported in patients concurrently taking disulfiram and other nitroimidazole agents, of which benznidazole is structurally similar to.

    genotoxicity
    General Information
    Genotoxicity of benznidazole has been demonstrated in human and animal studies. In a study evaluating the cytogenetic effect of benznidazole in pediatric subjects aged 11 months to 11 years with Chagas disease, a 2-fold increase in chromosomal aberrations was noted. The median incidence of micronucleated interphase lymphocytes in 20 subjects increased 2-fold compared to pre-dose values. In the same study, the mean incidence of chromosomal aberrations in 10 subjects also increased 2-fold compared with pre-dose values. Although not reported with benznidazole, carcinogenicity has been seen in mice and rats treated chronically with nitroimidazole derivatives, which are structurally related to benznidazole. It is not known whether benznidazole is associated with carcinogenicity in humans.

Description
  Text
  Benznidazole is an antiprotozoal used for the treatment of Chagas disease or American trypanosomiasis, a parasitic infection caused by Trypanosoma cruzi. Benznidazole is the first treatment for Chagas disease approved in the US. It was granted approval from the FDA based on the number of treated pediatric patients (6 to 12 years) who became Immunoglobulin G (IgG) antibody negative (55% to 60% vs. 5% to 15% for placebo) against the recombinant antigens of T. cruzi. Benznidazole is only FDA-approved for pediatric patients 2 to 12 years, although it is used off-label in patients ranging in age from infants to adults, and is recommended by the CDC as a treatment choice.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Benznidazole is an nitroimidazole antiprotozoal agent. It inhibits the synthesis of DNA, RNA, and proteins within the Trypanosoma cruzi parasite. Studies suggest that benznidazole is reduced by a Type I nitroreductase (NTR) enzyme of T. cruzi producing a series of short-lived intermediates that may promote damage to several macromolecules including DNA. In mammalian cells, however, benznidazole is metabolized by reduction of the nitro group to an amino group by a Type II NTR enzyme. The precise mechanism of action is unknown.
 
Benznidazole is active against all three stages, trypomastigotes, amastigotes, and epimastigotes, of T. cruzi. However, the sensitivity of T. cruzi strains to benznidazole from different geographic regions may vary. Animal studies suggest a potential for development of resistance to benznidazole. The mechanisms of drug resistance appear to be multifactorial and include decreased activity due to a mutation in the NTR (TcNTR) gene, higher efflux activity due to overexpression of TcPGP1 and TcPGP2 genes that encode P-glycoprotein as well as TcABCG1 genes that encode ATP-binding cassette transporters, and overexpression of other genes TcFeSOD-A and TcCyP19 that encode superoxide dismutase and cyclophilin, respectively, which have diverse biological function and may help parasite survival.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Oral Route
    Description
    The absorption of benznidazole from 3 different preparations (tablet, slurry with 100 mg tablets, slurry with 25 mg tablets) was comparable when given as a single dose under fasting conditions in healthy adult volunteers. Mean peak concentrations of 2.4 mg/L were achieved at a median of 2 hours for all 3 preparations. The absorption of benznidazole was not significantly affected when administered with food.
  Text
  Benznidazole is given orally. Protein binding is reported to be approximately 44% to 60%. The pathway of benznidazole metabolism is unknown. Benznidazole and unknown metabolites are reported to be excreted in the urine and feces. The elimination half-life of benznidazole is approximately 13 hours in healthy volunteers after a single dose.
 
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes: none
In vitro studies show that benznidazole is a P-glycoprotein (P-gp) substrate and does not notably induce CYP1A2, CYP2B6, and CYP3A4 at concentrations up to 100 micromolar.
