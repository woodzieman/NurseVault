Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  General Information
  Polyethylene glycol 3350 is contraindicated for use those with known polyethylene glycol hypersensitivity.
 
Polyethylene glycol 3350 solution is contraindicated for use in patients with known or suspected GI obstruction (i.e., ileus; GI perforation; gastric retention; toxic colitis or toxic megacolon). Patients with symptoms suggestive of bowel obstruction (i.e., acute abdomen or symptoms of appendicitis, abdominal pain, distension, nausea, or vomiting) should be evaluated prior to initiating laxative therapy.
 
In general, no special considerations exist for the use of polyethylene glycol as a laxative in geriatric patients. In clinical trials of polyethylene glycol 3350, elderly nursing home residents experienced a higher incidence of diarrhea versus younger adults at the recommended dose. If diarrhea occurs, discontinue laxative therapy.
 
Polyethylene glycol 3350 is a FDA pregnancy risk category C drug. Administer during pregnancy only if clearly needed. Animal reproduction studies have not been performed. While high-molecular weight polyethylene glycols are common food additives and are not significantly absorbed from the GI tract, it is not known whether this drug can cause fetal harm when given during pregnancy or if it can affect reproductive capacity.
 
The safest laxatives for use in lactating women are those that are not systemically absorbed. Polyethylene glycol 3350 (PEG 3350) is a common food additive and is not significantly absorbed from the GI tract. Only 0.2% of an oral dose would be absorbed systemically by the lactating mother. It is not known if PEG 3350 is distributed into breast-milk, but significant passage seems unlikely. The American Academy of Pediatrics has not determined if PEG 3350 is compatible with breast-feeding.
 
The safety and effectiveness of polyethylene glycol 3350 have not been established in adolescents, children, or infants. Data are limited at this time; the available data suggest that side effects (e.g., nausea, vomiting, irritability) may limit the use of PEG 3350 for constipation in some children, although the drug has been effective in short term use for treating fecal impaction. No specific dosage recommendations have been established for the use of this drug for chronic constipation in children.
 
Due to the potential choking hazard, use polyethylene glycol solution with caution in those patients prone to aspiration or regurgitation (e.g., coma, gag reflex depression, dysphagia, esophageal stricture, semiconscious, or impaired mental status, like dementia).

Adverse Reactions
  Headline
  Adverse Reactions
  General Information
  Polyethylene glycol 3350 (PEG 3350) is a relatively safe agent for the short-term treatment of constipation. Although animal experiments have proved the safety of chronic polyethylene glycol administration, the safety of using PEG 3350 for the long-term treatment of chronic constipation will require further human data. Clinical trials to date have evaluated the safety and efficacy of PEG 3350 in doses of 17 g/day for up to 6 months. Abdominal pain/bloating, cramping, flatulence, and rarely nausea/vomiting may occur. High doses (e.g., 34 g/day) may produce diarrhea and excessive stool frequency, particularly in elderly, debilitated patients, and may result in dehydration (rare). In some patients excessive stooling may cause fecal urgency or fecal incontinence. Discontinue PEG 3350 if diarrhea occurs. In children aged 3—18 years in a trial for fecal impaction, common side effects were similar to those seen in adults and included bloating and flatulence (18%), loose stools (13%) nausea/vomiting (5%) and abdominal cramps (5%).
 
Isolated cases of pruritus, urticaria, rhinorrhea, dermatitis and (rarely) anaphylactoid reactions have been reported which may represent allergic reactions. Polyethylene glycol compounds are found in numerous medications and food additives, and have been reported to cause allergic reactions.
 
Rare cases of neuropsychiatric events have been reported to the FDA via the Adverse Event Reporting System (AERS); a causal relationship between the use of polyethylene glycol oral laxatives and these events has not been determined.

Description
  Text
  Polyethylene glycol 3350 (PEG 3350) without electrolytes is an osmotic laxative used to treat constipation. Polyethylene glycols exhibit practically no systemic absorption from the intestine at molecular masses exceeding 3000. PEG 3350 without electrolytes is a gritless and tasteless preparation that draws water into the stool for ease of bowel movements. PEG 3350 single-ingredient products are approved as an over-the-counter (OTC) treatment of occasional constipation in adult and pediatric patients 17 years of age and older and has been used off-label in infants and children less than 17 years of age under the direct advice of a medical provider. PEG 3350 is also used to treat fecal impaction or chronic constipation. In several high-quality, placebo-controlled trials, PEG 3350 products consistently improve stool frequency and consistency even with long-term use. PEG 3350 also exhibits a side effect profile similar to placebo in most patient populations with use as directed, making it a preferred agent to treat constipation in both pediatric and adult patients. PEG 3350 can be a useful adjunct for constipation due to opiate analgesics; in adults, the American Gastroenterology Association (AGA) recommends the daily use of an osmotic laxative in combination with a stimulant laxative at least 2 to 3 times per week as first-line therapy in patients with opioid-induced constipation (OIC). PEG 3350 can be a useful adjunct for constipation-predominant irritable bowel syndrome (IBS-C) in adults to help improve stool frequency and consistency, but generally should not be used routinely as agents that target global IBS-C symptoms (e.g., guanylate cyclase-C agonists, lubiprostone) are preferred agents. In May 2019 the FDA determined that there is no meaningful difference between single-ingredient prescription-only PEG 3350 and nonprescription/over-the-counter (OTC) PEG 3350 products; patients requiring single-ingredient PEG 3350 may be encouraged to purchase PEG 3350 products without a prescription.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Polyethylene glycol 3350 is an osmotic agent which binds water and causes water to be retained within the stool. The lack of intestinal enzymatic degradation or bacterial metabolism of PEG 3350 contributes to the drug's effectiveness for idiopathic constipation; no tachyphylaxis was noted during clinical trials up to 24 months. In normal volunteers, low doses of PEG 3350 increased stool weight. In constipated patients, PEG 3350 softened stool consistency, increased stool frequency, and facilitated stool evacuation. PEG 3350 did not affect colonic transit time. Polyethylene glycol 3350 had no effect on the active absorption or secretion of glucose or electrolytes from the intestine.

Roughly 0.2% of a dose has been absorbed systemically in some studies of PEG 3350; the systemically absorbed amount is quickly excreted in the urine. Dosage Adjustment Guidelines
  Hepatic Impairment
  Specific guidelines for dosage adjustments in hepatic impairment are not available; it appears that no dosage adjustments are needed.

  Specific guidelines for dosage adjustments in renal impairment are not available; patients with renal disease or impairment should only use polyethylene glycol 3350 under supervision and advice of their doctor.
