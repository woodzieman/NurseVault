    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components.
    Headline
    closed-angle glaucoma, narrow iridocorneal angles
    General Information
    Cyclopentolate; phenylephrine should be used with caution in people with narrow angle glaucoma (closed-angle glaucoma) and those with anatomically narrow iridocorneal angles who are at risk for angle closure due to the potential of anticholinergic and sympathomimetic agents to precipitate acute angle closure. Mydriatic agents may cause a transient rise in intraocular pressure.
    Headline
    brain injury, children, Down syndrome, infants, neonates, spastic paralysis
    General Information
    Use of cyclopentolate; phenylephrine has been associated with psychotic reactions and behavioral disturbances in pediatric patients. Increased susceptibility to cyclopentolate has been reported in neonates, infants, young children, and children with spastic paralysis (e.g., cerebral palsy) or brain damage (brain injury). These disturbances include ataxia, incoherent speech, restlessness, hallucinations, hyperactivity, seizures, disorientation as to time and place. Pediatric patients may be more prone to heat intolerance due to the anticholinergic actions of cyclopentolate. Caution should be observed when considering use of cyclopentolate; phenylephrine in the presence of Down syndrome due to the potential increased sensitivity to anticholinergics.
    Headline
    conditions contributing to an elevated core body temperature, fever
    General Information
    Because of the risk of provoking hyperthermia, cyclopentolate; phenylephrine should be used with caution in patients, especially children, who have a fever or may be exposed to conditions contributing to an elevated core body temperature.
    Headline
    activities requiring coordination and concentration, driving or operating machinery
    General Information
    Patients should be advised not to drive or engage in driving or operating machinery or other activities requiring coordination and concentration while pupils are dilated from cyclopentolate; phenylephrine. Advise treated patients that they may experience sensitivity to light.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    No adequate human studies have examined the effects of cyclopentolate; phenylephrine on the fetus. Use during the first trimester for ophthalmic exam is often avoided when possible. However, even though systematic studies are lacking, decades of experience and the available case studies do not indicate an appreciable fetal risk of cyclopentolate use as a mydriatic when needed, given the limited duration of use during ophthalmic examination. Phenylephrine ophthalmic use during an ophthalmic exam at usual dosages is also unlikely to be of concern. When use is necessary during pregnancy, proper lacrimal occlusion after cyclopentolate; phenylephrine dosage will help limit the risk of maternal systemic absorption and fetal exposure.

    Adverse ocular effects associated with cyclopentolate; phenylephrine include increased intraocular pressure, burning and ocular irritation upon instillation, and superficial punctate keratitis. Description
  Text
  Cyclopentolate and phenylephrine are used together in an ophthalmic preparation for producing mydriasis for ophthalmologic examination. Cyclopentolate is a cycloplegic mydriatic agent that causes loss of accommodation and mydriasis. Phenylephrine is a sympathomimetic agent that causes vasoconstriction and mydriasis. The amount of mydriasis produced by this combination is greater than that produced by either agent alone. Cyclomydril was approved by the FDA in September 1975.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Cyclopentolate; phenylephrine is used in combination as an ophthalmic solution to produce mydriasis. The amount of mydriasis produced by this combination is greater than that produced by either agent alone. The concentrations of cyclopentolate and phenylephrine in the product have been selected to induce mydriasis with little accompanying cycloplegia.
Cyclopentolate: Cyclopentolate blocks the action of acetylcholine to produce relaxation of the sphincter muscle of the iris resulting in dilation of the pupil (mydriasis). This agent has an anticholinergic effect similar to atropine. Paralysis of accommodation (cycloplegia) is produced by blocking cholinergic stimulation of the ciliary muscle of the lens.
Phenylephrine: Phenylephrine is a sympathomimetic agent that causes vasoconstriction and mydriasis.
 
Pharmacodynamics
Cyclopentolate; phenylephrine has a rapid onset of action, producing the maximum mydriatic and cycloplegic effects within 15 to 60 minutes following topical application to the eye. The duration of both effects is normally 24 hours. However, mydriasis may persist for several days in selected patients.

Pharmacokinetics
  Text
  Cyclopentolate; phenylephrine is administered topically as an ophthalmic solution. Cyclopentolate; phenylephrine is absorbed rapidly (maximum mydriatic and cycloplegic effect within 15—60 minutes), works locally, and has minimal systemic absorption when precautions for lacrimal occlusion are followed to limit systemic effect. Systemic absorption via ocular administration is possible, however, and may result in adverse effects. Cyclopentolate may be absorbed systemically either by transcorneal absorption, direct topical absorption through the skin, or by absorption from the nasal or nasolacrimal system. Infants are particularly susceptible to systemic side effects. The duration of mydriatic and cycloplegic effect is normally 20—24 hours. However, mydriasis may persist for several days in selected patients.

    Ophthalmic Solution
Wash hands before and after use.
To prevent contamination or spread of infection, do not touch dropper tip to any surface. Do not use dropper for more than 1 person.
Tilt the patient's head back slightly and pull the lower eyelid down with the index finger to form a pouch.
Squeeze the prescribed number of drops into the pouch.
To avoid excessive systemic absorption, apply finger pressure on the lacrimal sac for 2 to 3 minutes following topical application to the eye.
In children, take care not to get into the mouth of the child or infant. Wash the hands of the child after administration.
Observe infants closely for at least 30 minutes after administration. Feeding intolerance may follow ophthalmic use of this product in infants. Withhold feeding for four (4) hours after examination.
The patient's eyes will be sensitive to light and vision will be blurry approximately 30 to 60 minutes following dilation with this product.
Patients should not drive of perform hazardous activities while eyes are dilated.
