Drug Class: Fruquintinib is a small molecule kinase inhibitor of vascular endothelial growth factor receptors (VEGF)-1, -2, and -3.

Routes: oral; Common adult doses: 5 mg/day PO.

Common Side Effects (excluding routine GI upset): bleeding, epistaxis, gi bleeding, prolonged bleeding time, gastrointestinal fistula, gi perforation, elevated hepatic enzymes, hepatotoxicity, hyperbilirubinemia, arthralgia, back pain, musculoskeletal pain, hypocalcemia, hypokalemia, hypomagnesemia, hyponatremia

      Heading
      for the treatment of metastatic colorectal cancer (mCRC) in patients who have previously been treated with fluoropyrimidine-, oxaliplatin-, and irinotecan-based chemotherapy, an anti-VEGF therapy, and an anti-EGFR therapy (if RAS wild-type and medically appropriate)
      Routes
        Heading
        Oral dosage
        Route Age Groups
          Heading
          Adults
          Dosage Description
          5 mg PO once daily for 21 days, repeated every 28 days. Continue until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity. Coadministration of certain drugs may need to be avoided or dosage adjustments may be necessary; review drug interactions. Treatment with fruquintinib significantly improved median overall survival (OS) (7.4 months vs. 4.8 months) and median progression-free survival (PFS) (3.7 months vs. 1.8 months) compared with placebo in patients with heavily pretreated mCRC in an international, multicenter, randomized, double-blind phase 3 clinical trial (FRESCO-2). Treatment with fruquintinib also significantly improved the median OS (9.3 months vs. 6.6 months) compared with placebo in patients with heavily pretreated mCRC in a multicenter, randomized, double-blind phase 3 clinical trial conducted in China (FRESNO); the median PFS was 3.7 months in the fruquintinib arm and 1.8 months in the placebo arm.

  Contraindications And Precautions
    Headline
    Drug Interactions
    General Information
    The coadministration of certain medications may lead to harm and require avoidance or therapy modification; review all drug interactions prior to concomitant use of other medications.
    Headline
    hypertension
    General Information
    Do not begin therapy with fruquintinib in patients with uncontrolled hypertension. Monitor blood pressure weekly for the first month, and at least monthly thereafter and as clinically indicated. Initiate or adjust antihypertensive therapy as appropriate. An interruption of therapy, dose reduction, or discontinuation of therapy may be necessary depending on the severity of hypertension.
    Headline
    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components. Patients with tartrazine dye hypersensitivity (FD and C Yellow No. 5) may experience allergic reactions including bronchial asthma when taking the 1-mg capsules of fruquintinib; the 1-mg capsules also contain FD and C Yellow No. 6 (sunset yellow FCF) which can also cause allergic reactions. While the incidence of tartrazine sensitivity is low, it is frequently seen in patients who also have aspirin or salicylate hypersensitivity.
    Headline
    Child-Pugh class C, hepatic failure
    General Information
    Fruquintinib is not recommended for use in patients with severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh class C; or, total bilirubin greater than 1.5 times and less than 3 times the upper limit of normal [ULN] and any AST) or hepatic failure. Monitor liver function tests (i.e., ALT, AST, and bilirubin) at baseline and periodically during treatment. An interruption of therapy, dose reduction, or discontinuation of therapy may be necessary if hepatotoxicity occurs. No dose adjustment is recommended for patients with mild hepatic impairment (total bilirubin less than or equal to ULN with AST greater than ULN, or total bilirubin greater than 1 to 1.5 times ULN with any AST); fruquintinib has not been sufficiently studied in patients with moderate hepatic impairment (total bilirubin greater than 1.5 to 3 times ULN and any AST).
    Headline
    renal impairment
    General Information
    Use fruquintinib with caution in patients with renal impairment; proteinuria has been reported with treatment. Monitor 24-hour urine protein at baseline and periodically during treatment. An interruption of therapy is necessary for proteinuria greater than or equal to 2 grams per 24 hours; a dose reduction or discontinuation of therapy may be necessary depending on the degree of proteinuria. Discontinue fruquintinib treatment in patients who develop nephrotic syndrome.
    Headline
    major surgery, planned procedure
    General Information
    Do not administer fruquintinib for at least 2 weeks prior to a major planned procedure. After major surgery, wait at least 2 weeks and until adequate wound healing prior to restarting fruquintinib therapy. Impaired wound healing can occur in patients who receive drugs that inhibit the vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) signaling pathway such as fruquintinib. The safety of resuming treatment after resolution of wound healing complications has not been established.
    Headline
    ischemic stroke, myocardial infarction, thromboembolism, thrombophilia
    General Information
    Carefully consider the use of fruquintinib in patients with a history of thrombophilia or a recent history of thromboembolism, myocardial infarction, or ischemic stroke; treatment may increase the risk of arterial thromboembolic events. Clinical trials of fruquintinib excluded patients with clinically significant cardiac disease, uncontrolled hypertension, or thromboembolic events in the previous 6 months. Discontinue fruquintinib in patients who develop arterial thromboembolic events.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Pregnancy should be avoided by females of reproductive potential during fruquintinib treatment and for at least 2 weeks after the last dose. Although there are no adequately controlled studies in pregnant women, fruquintinib can cause fetal harm or death when administered during pregnancy based on its mechanism of action and animal studies. Oral administration of fruquintinib to pregnant rats during organogenesis resulted in fetal external (edema and head and tail abnormalities), visceral, and skeletal malformations at approximately 0.2 times the recommended clinical dose based on BSA; an increase in postimplantation loss and a reduction in live fetuses was observed at doses approximately 0.5 times the recommended dose based on BSA. Women who are pregnant or who become pregnant while receiving fruquintinib should be apprised of the potential hazard to the fetus.
    Headline
    contraception requirements, male-mediated teratogenicity, pregnancy testing, reproductive risk
    General Information
    Counsel patients about the reproductive risk and contraception requirements during fruquintinib treatment. Fruquintinib can be teratogenic if taken by the mother during pregnancy. Females should avoid pregnancy and use effective contraception during and for at least 2 weeks after treatment with fruquintinib; due to the risk of male-mediated teratogenicity, males with female partners of reproductive potential should also use effective contraception during treatment and for at least 2 weeks after the last dose. Females of reproductive potential should undergo pregnancy testing prior to initiation of fruquintinib. Women who become pregnant while receiving fruquintinib should be apprised of the potential hazard to the fetus.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in nursing infants from fruquintinib, advise women to discontinue breast-feeding during treatment and for 2 weeks after the final dose. It is not known whether fruquintinib is present in human milk, although many drugs are excreted in human milk.

    hypertension, hypertensive crisis
    General Information
    Hypertension was reported in 38% to 61% of patients treated with fruquintinib in clinical trials, with a median onset of 14 days from the first dose (grade 3 or 4, 14% to 23%); hypertensive crisis occurred in 3 patients who received fruquintinib in a pooled safety analysis (0.3%). Do not initiate therapy with fruquintinib unless blood pressure is adequately controlled. Monitor blood pressure weekly for the first month, and at least monthly thereafter and as clinically indicated. Initiate or adjust antihypertensive therapy as appropriate. An interruption of therapy, dose reduction, or discontinuation of therapy may be necessary depending on the severity of hypertension.

    Headline
    bleeding, epistaxis, GI bleeding, prolonged bleeding time
    General Information
    Fruquintinib treatment can cause serious cases of bleeding, which may be fatal. Hemorrhage occurred in 28% of patients with metastatic colorectal cancer treated with fruquintinib in a randomized clinical trial (grade 3 or 4, 1.1%). Serious cases of hemorrhage (2.2%), epistaxis (3.9%), and GI bleeding (1.5%) were reported in another randomized clinical trial with a similar patient population. In a pooled safety analysis (n = 911), GI bleeding occurred in 6% of patients, including 13 patients (1%) who were grade 3 or higher and 2 patients with fatal hemorrhages. A prolonged bleeding time (activated partial thromboplastin time, aPTT) from baseline was reported in 21% of fruquintinib-treated patients in 1 randomized clinical trial compared with 18% of those who received placebo (grade 3 or 4, 2.7% vs. 1.5%). Monitor the INR in patients receiving anticoagulant therapy. Permanently discontinue fruquintinib in patients who experience severe or life-threatening hemorrhage.

    Headline
    infection
    General Information
    Treatment with fruquintinib increased the risk of infection compared with placebo in 3 randomized clinical trials (n = 391) (18% vs. 12%), including fatal infections (1% vs. 0.3%). In a pooled safety analysis of patients with metastatic colorectal cancer (n = 911), the most common infections were urinary tract infections (6.8%), upper respiratory tract infections (3.2%), and pneumonia (2.5%); fatal infections included pneumonia (0.4%), sepsis (0.2%), bacterial infection (0.1%), lower respiratory tract infection (0.1%), and septic shock (0.1%). Serious urinary tract infections (4.9% to 9%), upper respiratory tract infections (4.7%), and pneumonia (2.4% to 2.9%) were also reported in 2 randomized clinical trials. Interrupt fruquintinib therapy for grade 3 or 4 infections, or for worsening infections of any grade; treatment may be resumed at the same dose when the infection has resolved.

    General Information

Description
  Text
  Fruquintinib is a small molecule kinase inhibitor of vascular endothelial growth factor receptors (VEGF)-1, -2, and -3. It is indicated for the treatment of metastatic colorectal cancer patients who have been previously treated with fluoropyrimidine-, oxaliplatin-, and irinotecan-based chemotherapy, an anti-VEGF therapy, and, if RAS wild-type and medically appropriate, an anti-EGFR therapy. Patients should not receive fruquintinib for at least 2 weeks before elective surgery due to wound healing complications that may be associated with treatment; do not administer fruquintinib for at least 2 weeks after major surgery and until adequate wound healing has occurred.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Fruquintinib is a small molecule kinase inhibitor of vascular endothelial growth factor receptors (VEGFR)-1, -2, and -3 with IC50 values of 33, 35, and 0.5 nanomolar, respectively. In vitro, fruquintinib inhibits VEGF-mediated endothelial cell proliferation and tubular formation. In vitro and in vivo, fruquintinib inhibits VEGF-induced VEGFR-2 phosphorylation. In vivo studies showed fruquintinib inhibited tumor growth in a tumor xenograft mouse model of colon cancer.

    Description
    At steady-state at the recommended dosage, the geometric mean Cmax of fruquintinib is 300 ng/mL (CV, 28%) and AUC is 5,880 ng x hour/mL (CV, 29%). The fruquintinib Cmax and AUC are dose-proportional across the dosage range of 1 to 6 mg (0.2 to 1.2 times the recommended dosage. A high-fat meal (800 to 1,000 calories, 50% fat) did not have a clinically significant effect on the pharmacokinetics of fruquintinib.

Dosage Adjustment Guidelines
  Hepatic Impairment
  Baseline Hepatic Impairment
Mild hepatic impairment (total bilirubin less than or equal to the upper limit of normal (ULN) and AST greater than ULN; OR total bilirubin greater than 1 to 1.5 times ULN with any AST): No dosage adjustment recommended.
Moderate hepatic impairment (total bilirubin 1.6 to 3 times ULN and any AST): Fruquintinib has not been sufficiently studied in this population of patients.
Severe hepatic impairment (total bilirubin greater than 3 times ULN and any AST): Treatment with fruquintinib is not recommended.
 
Treatment-Related Hepatotoxicity
ALT/AST greater than 3 times ULN (or greater than 3 times baseline if baseline was abnormal); OR bilirubin greater than 1.5 times ULN (or greater than 1.5 times baseline if baseline was abnormal): Hold fruquintinib therapy. When AST/ALT and total bilirubin resolve to grade 1 or less (or baseline), resume treatment at the next lower dose level.
ALT/AST greater than 3 times ULN with concurrent total bilirubin greater than 2 times ULN (in the absence of cholestasis or hemolysis): Permanently discontinue fruquintinib therapy.
ALT/AST greater than 20 times ULN (or greater than 20 times baseline if baseline was abnormal); OR bilirubin greater than 10 times ULN (or greater than 10 times baseline if baseline was abnormal): Permanently discontinue fruquintinib therapy.
  Renal Impairment
  Specific guidelines for dosage adjustments in renal impairment are not available; it appears that no dosage adjustments are needed.
