    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components.
    Headline
    pulmonary disease
    General Information
    Use zenocutuzumab with caution in patients who have a history of pre-existing pulmonary disease; severe pneumonitis/interstitial lung disease has been reported in patients treated with zenocutuzumab. Monitor patients for new or worsening respiratory symptoms including fever, cough, or dyspnea. Immediately interrupt zenocutuzumab therapy in patients with new or worsening respiratory symptoms; a discontinuation of therapy may be necessary depending on the severity.
    Headline
    coronary artery disease, heart failure, hypertension, thoracic radiation therapy
    General Information
    Use zenocutuzumab with caution in patients with a history of heart failure, or in patients with conditions that could impair left ventricular function including coronary artery disease, uncontrolled hypertension, thoracic radiation therapy and previous anthracycline exposure. Zenocutuzumab can cause heart failure and has not been studied in patients with a history of clinically significant heart disease or in those who have a left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) less than 50% at baseline. Assess LVEF at baseline and monitor at regular intervals during treatment and as clinically indicated. An interruption or discontinuation of therapy may be necessary if the LVEF decreases below 50%. Permanently discontinue zenocutuzumab in patients with symptomatic congestive heart failure.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Zenocutuzumab can cause fetal harm or death based on its mechanism of action. There are no available data on the use of zenocutuzumab during human pregnancy. Literature reports have linked the use of HER2-directed antibodies during pregnancy (alone or in combination with chemotherapy) to oligohydramnios, which can manifest as fatal pulmonary hypoplasia, skeletal abnormalities, and neonatal death. In some cases, the amniotic fluid index increased after stopping the HER2-directed antibody. Human IgG1 can cross the placenta; because zenocutuzumab is an IgG antibody, it has the potential to reach the fetus. Monitor patients who have received zenocutuzumab during pregnancy (or within 2 months prior to conception) for oligohydramnios. If oligohydramnios occurs, perform fetal testing appropriate for gestational age and consistent with standard of care. Animal data show that inhibition of HER2 and/or HER3 can impair embryo-fetal development, including cardiac, vascular, and neuronal development, and can cause embryolethality. Pregnant individuals should be counseled about the potential fetal hazard.
    Headline
    embryo-fetal toxicity, people who may become pregnant, reproductive risk
    General Information
    Counsel people who may become pregnant about the reproductive risk and embryo-fetal toxicity associated with zenocutuzumab. Zenocutuzumab may cause fetal harm. Verify pregnancy status prior to treatment initiation. Advise people who may become pregnant to use effective contraception while taking zenocutuzumab and for 2 months after the last dose.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Advise patients to avoid breast-feeding while taking zenocutuzumab and for 2 months after the last dose. There are no data on the presence of zenocutuzumab in human milk, its effects on the breastfed child, or its effects on milk production. Maternal IgG is known to be present in human milk. The effects of local gastrointestinal exposure and limited systemic exposure in the breastfed child are unknown.

  Adverse Reactions
    Headline
    infusion-related reactions
    General Information
    Grade 1 or 2 infusion-related reactions occurred in 13% of patients in a pooled safety population of a multicenter, open-label, multicohort clinical trial (n = 175); most (91%) reactions occurred during the first infusion. The median time to onset was 63 minutes (range, 13 minutes to 240 minutes) from the start of the infusion. Symptoms included back pain, chills, cough, diarrhea, dyspnea, facial edema, fatigue, fever, nausea, noncardiac chest pain, oropharyngeal discomfort, paresthesia, and vomiting. An interruption of the infusion is necessary for grade 1 to 3 infusion reactions; it may be resumed at a reduced rate after symptom resolution. Permanently discontinue zenocutuzumab for grade 4 or life-threatening infusion reactions or for anaphylaxis.

    General Information
    Heart failure without a decrease in left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) occurred in 1.7% of patients treated with zenocutuzumab in a pooled safety population of a multicenter, open-label, multicohort clinical trial (n = 175), including 1 death (0.6%). A grade 2 decrease in LVEF (LVEF 40% to 50%; 10% to 19% decrease from baseline) occurred in 2% of evaluable patients. Zenocutuzumab has not been studied in patients with a history of clinically significant heart disease or in those who have a left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) less than 50% at baseline. An interruption or discontinuation of therapy may be necessary if the LVEF decreases below 50%. Permanently discontinue zenocutuzumab in patients with symptomatic congestive heart failure. Carotid artery stenosis was additionally reported in 1 patient with non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) who received zenocutuzumab.

Description
  Text
  Zenocutuzumab is a bispecific HER2- and HER3-directed antibody indicated for the treatment of advanced, unresectable or metastatic cholangiocarcinoma, non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), or pancreatic adenocarcinoma harboring a neuregulin 1 (NRG1) gene fusion with disease progression on or after prior systemic therapy. It carries a black box warning for risk of embryofetal toxicity; effective contraception is required for people who can get pregnant during treatment and for 2 months after the last dose. Zenocutuzumab was approved by the FDA in December 2024.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Zenocutuzumab-zbco is a low-fucose humanized immunoglobulin G1 (IgG1) bispecific HER2-and HER3-directed antibody that binds to the extracellular domains of HER2 and HER3 expressed on the surface of cells, including tumor cells. It inhibits HER2:HER3 dimerization and prevents neuregulin 1 (NRG1) from binding to HER3 and mediates antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC). Chimeric NRG1 has an EGF-like domain which allows ligand binding via the ERBB family of receptor tyrosine kinases (e.g., EGFR, HER2, HER3, and HER4), primarily to HER3 and HER4. HER3 has little intrinsic enzyme activity, but when phosphorylated by NRG1 binding activates downstream signal transduction pathways including the phosphoinositide 3-kinase (PI3K) pathway, leading to tumor cell proliferation and survival. Zenocutuzumab decreased cell proliferation and signaling through the PI3K-AKT-mammalian target of rapamycin (mTOR) pathway and showed antitumor activity in mouse models of NRG1 fusion-positive lung and pancreatic cancers.

Dilution:
Only use infusion bags made of polyvinylchloride (PVC), polyolefin or polyolefin/polyamide coextruded plastic.
Withdraw and discard 37.5 mL of 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection from a 250 mL infusion bag.
Withdraw 37.5 mL of zenocutuzumab from 2 vials and add to the infusion bag. The final volume should be 250 mL. Discard any unused portion left in the vial.
Gently invert the bag to mix. Do not shake.
Storage following dilution: The diluted infusion may be stored for 2 hours prior to administration at room temperature (15 to 25 degrees C or 59 to 77 degrees F) or for 23 hours refrigerated at 2 to 8 degrees C (36 to 46 degrees F). Diluted zenocutuzumab must be fully infused by 6 hours from the end of preparation if stored at room temperature or 28 hours from the end of preparation if refrigerated. If the infusion time will exceed this due to, for example, an infusion-related reaction, the bag must be discarded and a new bag prepared.
 
Intravenous Infusion:
If the IV bag was refrigerated, allow it to come to room temperature prior to administration (approximately 30 minutes).
Administer using an infusion set made of either PVC, polyethylene (PE), polyurethane (PUR) or polybutadiene (PB) with an in-line, sterile, non-pyrogenic, low protein-binding 0.2 micrometer polyethersulfone (PES) filter.
Administer as an IV infusion over 4 hours.
