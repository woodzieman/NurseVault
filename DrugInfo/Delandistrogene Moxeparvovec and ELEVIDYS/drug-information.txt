    Headline
    Treatment-Related Restrictions of Use
    General Information
    Delandistrogene moxeparvovec is contraindicated in patients with deletions in exon 8 and/or exon 9 of the DMD gene due to the risk of severe immune-mediated myositis. In clinical trials, cases of immune-mediated myositis were reported approximately 1 month after infusion in patients with such deletions. These immune responses are believed to stem from a T-cell-mediated reaction triggered by lack of self-tolerance to the transgene-encoded region corresponding to exons 1 to 17 of the DMD gene. While data are limited, patients with mutations in exons 1 to 17 or 59 to 71 may also be at increased risk for developing serious immune-mediated myositis.
Preexisting neutralizing anti-adeno-associated viral vector (AAV) antibody formation may impede transgene expression at desired therapeutic concentrations in AAV-vector based gene therapies. Measure baseline anti-AAVrh74 antibody titers using a Total Binding Antibody enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA). Therapy is not recommended for patients with anti-AAVrh74 total binding antibody titers of 1:400 or more.
Delandistrogene moxeparvovec is not recommended in individuals with recent vaccination (within 4 weeks of treatment) due to immunogenicity and potential safety concerns.
    Headline
    acute hepatic failure, hepatic disease, hepatotoxicity
    General Information
    Acute serious liver injury or hepatotoxicity, including life-threatening and fatal acute hepatic failure, has occurred with delandistrogene moxeparvovec therapy. Individuals with pre-existing liver impairment, chronic hepatic disease, or acute liver disease (e.g., acute hepatic viral infection) may be at higher risk of serious liver injury and acute liver failure. Postpone delandistrogene moxeparvovec therapy in these individuals until the acute liver disease is resolved or controlled. The onset of liver injury typically begins within 8 weeks after administration of delandistrogene moxeparvovec. Most cases resolve spontaneously or with systemic corticosteroids and resolve without clinical sequelae within 2 months. In non-ambulatory individuals, acute liver failure with fatal outcomes has occurred in the clinical and postmarketing settings. Life-threatening mesenteric vein thrombosis, complicated by bowel ischemia and necrosis, and portal hypertension have been reported following acute liver injury in non-ambulatory individuals. Prior to delandistrogene moxeparvovec administration, perform liver enzyme test. Monitor liver function (AST, ALT, GGT, albumin, aPTT, INR, and total bilirubin) weekly for the first 3 months following the infusion. Continue monitoring if clinically indicated, until results are unremarkable (normal clinical exam, GGT, and total bilirubin concentrations return to near baseline). Individuals should maintain proximity to an appropriate health care facility for at least 2 months after the infusion. Systemic corticosteroid treatment is recommended for individuals before and after delandistrogene moxeparvovec infusion; adjust corticosteroid regimen when indicated. If acute serious liver injury is suspected, a consultation with a specialist is recommended.
    Headline
    left ventricular dysfunction
    General Information
    Monitor troponin-I prior to delandistrogene moxeparvovec infusion and weekly for the first month after administration; continue monitoring if clinically indicated. Increase the frequency of monitoring if cardiac symptoms, such as chest pain or shortness of breath, occur. Acute serious myocarditis and troponin-I elevations have been reported in clinical trials within 24 hours to more than 1 year after delandistrogene moxeparvovec infusion. Patients with pre-existing left ventricular dysfunction may be at increased risk of adverse outcomes if myocarditis develops. Patients with moderate to severe left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) impairment have not been studied in clinical trials with delandistrogene moxeparvovec.
    Headline
    infection
    General Information
    Due to the increased risk of a serious systemic immune response, delay delandistrogene moxeparvovec therapy in individuals with recent infection (within 4 weeks) or active infections until the infection has resolved. Increased susceptibility to serious infections may occur due to concomitant administration of corticosteroid and additional immunosuppressants and delandistrogene moxeparvovec. Respiratory infections, including fatal outcomes, have occurred in individuals taking immunosuppressant corticosteroids required for delandistrogene moxeparvovec administration. Clinical signs or symptoms of infection should be absent at the time of administration. Monitor individuals for signs and symptoms of infection before and after administration and treat appropriately. Administer immunization according to the best clinical practices and immunization guidelines prior to initiation of the corticosteroid regimen required before delandistrogene moxeparvovec infusion.

    Headline
    dysphagia, dyspnea, myalgia, myasthenia, myopathy
    General Information
    Immune-mediated myositis (inflammatory myopathy) has been reported approximately 1 month after delandistrogene moxeparvovec infusion in patients with deletion mutations involving exon 8 and/or exon 9 in the DMD gene. Symptoms of severe muscle weakness (myasthenia), including dysphagia, dyspnea and hypophonia, were observed. In 1 patient, a life-threatening case of immune-mediated myositis occurred. Symptoms resolved during hospitalization after additional immunomodulatory treatment and muscle strength gradually improved, but did not return to baseline. Advise patients to contact a health care provider immediately if they experience any unexplained increased muscle pain (myalgia), tenderness, or weakness, including dysphagia, dyspnea or hypophonia; these may be symptoms of myositis. If these symptoms occur, consider additional immunomodulatory treatment (immunosuppressants [e.g., calcineurin-inhibitor] in addition to corticosteroids) based on patient's clinical presentation and medical history. Immune-mediated myositis has been reported during postmarketing experience with delandistrogene moxeparvovec.

Description
  Text
  Delandistrogene moxeparvovec is an adeno-associated virus vector-based gene therapy FDA-approved for the treatment Duchenne muscular dystrophy (DMD) in individuals who are ambulatory and have a confirmed mutation in the DMD gene. DMD is a rare and serious genetic condition that primarily affects males. About 1 in every 3,300 boys are affected by this disorder. Affected individuals typically lack functional dystrophin protein. This leads to the development of symptoms in early childhood that often progress to trouble walking and a loss of ambulation in adolescence and ultimately, life-threatening heart and respiratory conditions that limit life expectancy to early adulthood. In clinical trials, delandistrogene moxeparvovec has shown to increase the micro-dystrophin protein expression in muscle biopsies. Acute serious liver injury, including life-threatening and fatal acute hepatic failure, has occurred with delandistrogene moxeparvovec therapy. Individuals with pre-existing liver impairment may be at higher risk. Monitor liver function (liver function tests, clinical exam), platelet counts, and troponin-I. Delandistrogene moxeparvovec was FDA-approved in 2023.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Delandistrogene moxeparvovec is a gene therapy product comprised of a non-replicating, recombinant, adeno-associated virus (AAV) serotype rh74 (AAVrh74) capsid and a ssDNA expression cassette flanked by inverted terminal repeats (IRTs) derived from AAV2. The cassette contains an MHCK7 gene regulatory component comprising a creatine kinase 7 promoter and an alpha-myosin heavy chain enhancer and the DNA transgene encoding the engineered delandistrogene moxeparvovec micro-dystrophin protein. AAVrh74 serotype transduction has been demonstrated in skeletal muscle cells in clinical and nonclinical studies. AAVrh74 serotype transduction has been demonstrated in cardiac and diaphragm muscle cells in nonclinical studies. The MHCK7 promoter/enhancer drives transgene expression and has been shown in animal models to drive transgenic delandistrogene moxeparvovec micro-dystrophin protein expression predominantly in skeletal muscle, including diaphragm, and cardiac muscle. In clinical studies, muscle biopsy analyses have confirmed delandistrogene moxeparvovec micro-dystrophin expression in skeletal muscle. Duchenne muscular dystrophy (DMD) is caused by a mutation in the DMD gene resulting in lack of functional dystrophin protein. Delandistrogene moxeparvovec carries a transgene encoding a micro-dystrophin protein consisting of selected domains of dystrophin expressed in normal muscle cells. Delandistrogene moxeparvovec micro-dystrophin has been demonstrated to localize to the sarcolemma.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Intravenous Route
    Description
    In clinical muscle biopsies at week 12 post-dose, evaluation of delandistrogene moxeparvovec vector genome exposure in clinical muscle biopsies expressed as copies per nucleus revealed drug distribution and transduction with a mean change from baseline of 2.91 and 3.44 copies per nucleus in 2 studies at the recommended dose of 1.33 x 10^14 vg/kg. In 3 studies, the Cmax in the serum was 0.0055 x 10^13 copies/mL and 2.78 x 10^6 copies/mL in the urine, 7.86 x 10^7 copies/mL in the saliva, and 4.87 x 10^7 copies/mcg in the feces. The Tmax was 5.8 hours post-dose (serum), 6.7 hours (saliva), 6.5 hours (urine) and 13 days (feces). It took a median time of 55 days post-dose to achieve first below limit of quantification (BLOQ) sample followed by 2 consecutive BLOQ samples for serum. The median time to achieve complete elimination as the first BLOQ sample followed by 2 consecutive BLOQ samples was 49.8 days (saliva), 78 days (urine), and 162 days (feces) post-dose.
  Text
  Delandistrogene moxeparvovec is administered intravenously. After IV administration, delandistrogene moxeparvovec vector genome undergoes distribution via systemic circulation and distributes into target muscle tissues. Delandistrogene moxeparvovec capsid proteins are broken down through proteasomal degradation after anti-adeno-associated viral vector (AAV) entry into target cells. Thus, is not likely to exhibit the drug-drug interaction potential mediated by known drug metabolizing enzymes (cytochrome P450-based) and drug transporters. Delandistrogene moxeparvovec is eliminated in the urine and feces. The estimated elimination half-life in serum is approximately 12 hours and most of the drug is expected to be cleared from the serum by 1-week post-dose. In the excreta, the estimated elimination half-life of delandistrogene moxeparvovec vector genome is 40 hours (urine), 55 hours (feces), and 60 hours (saliva).

Preparation
Thaw before use.
When thawed in the refrigerator, delandistrogene moxeparvovec vials are stable for up to 14 days in the refrigerator (2 to 8 degrees C [36 to 46 degrees F]) when stored in the upright position.
Frozen delandistrogene moxeparvovec vials will thaw in approximately 2 hours when placed at room temperature (up to 25 degrees C [77 degrees F]) when removed from original packaging.
Inspect vials to ensure no ice crystals are present prior to preparation.
When thawed, swirl gently. Do NOT shake, refreeze, or place back in the refrigerator.
Remove the plastic flip-off cap from the vials and disinfect the rubber stopper.
Withdraw 10 mL of delandistrogene moxeparvovec from each vial provided in the kit. Do NOT use filter needles during preparation. Multiple syringes will be required to withdraw the required volume. Remove air from the syringes and cap the syringes.
Maintain syringes at room temperature prior to and during administration.
Storage: Sealed delandistrogene moxeparvovec thawed vials are stable up to 24 hours at room temperature (up to 25 degrees C [77 degrees F)] when stored in the upright position. Use within 6 hours of drawing into syringe. Discard the delandistrogene moxeparvovec-containing syringe(s) if drug infusion has not started within the 6-hour timeframe.
 
Intravenous (IV) Infusion
Administer in a setting where treatment for infusion-related reactions is immediately available.
Administer delandistrogene moxeparvovec as a single-dose IV infusion through a peripheral venous catheter.
Consider application of a topical anesthetic to the infusion site prior to IV administration.
Insertion of a back-up catheter is recommended.
Flush the IV access line with 0.9% sodium chloride injection prior to the delandistrogene moxeparvovec infusion at the same infusion rate.
Administer delandistrogene moxeparvovec via IV infusion using a syringe infusion pump with an in-line 0.2-micron filter for approximately 1 to 2 hours, or longer at care team discretion, through a peripheral limb vein.
Infuse at a rate of less than 10 mL/kg/hour. Do NOT administer as an IV push. Do NOT infuse in the same IV access line with any other product. Discard unused delandistrogene moxeparvovec.
Flush the IV access line with 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection after the delandistrogene moxeparvovec infusion.
In the event of an infusion-related reaction during administration:
Slow or stop the infusion. Discontinue the infusion for anaphylaxis.
Administer treatment as needed to manage the infusion-related reaction.
The infusion may be restarted at a lower rate after the infusion-related reaction has resolved.
If the infusion is stopped and restarted, delandistrogene moxeparvovec should be infused within 12 hours after drawing into the syringe.

    Visually inspect each vial of delandistrogene moxeparvovec. It is a clear, colorless liquid that may have some opalescence and may contain white to off-white particles. Do not use if the suspension in the vials is cloudy or discolored.

For patients receiving corticosteroids at baseline, taper off the additional peri-delandistrogene moxeparvovec corticosteroids (back to baseline corticosteroid dose) over 2 weeks, or longer as needed. Recommended pre-and post-infusion corticosteroid dosing
Daily or intermittent baseline corticosteroid dosing: Start 1 day prior to infusion: 1 mg/kg/day (prednisone equivalent) and continue baseline dose. Max: 60 mg/day (prednisone equivalent).

No baseline corticosteroid dosing: Start 1 week prior to infusion: 1.5 mg/kg/day (prednisone equivalent). Max: 60 mg/day (prednisone equivalent).

Recommended corticosteroid regimen dose modification for liver function abnormalities after delandistrogene moxeparvovec infusion
Modify oral corticosteroid doses for individuals with liver function abnormalities (GGT 3 times or more baseline value, total bilirubin more than the upper limits of normal) following delandistrogene moxeparvovec infusion.
Baseline + 1 mg/kg/day: Increase to 2 mg/kg/day (prednisone equivalent) and continue baseline dose. Max: 120 mg/day (prednisone equivalent).
Baseline + 1 mg/kg/day taken on days without high-dose corticosteroid treatment: Increase to 2 mg/kg/day (prednisone equivalent) taken on days without high-dose corticosteroid treatment and continue baseline dose. Max: 120 mg/day (prednisone equivalent).
1.5 mg/kg/day: Increase to 2 mg/kg/day (prednisone equivalent). Max: 120 mg/day (prednisone equivalent).
Consider IV bolus corticosteroids instead of oral corticosteroids for GGT or bilirubin elevations that do not respond after 1 week of oral corticosteroids.

Dosage Adjustment Guidelines
  Hepatic Impairment
  Specific guidelines for dosage adjustments in hepatic impairment are not available; postpone delandistrogene moxeparvovec administration in individuals with acute liver disease until resolved or controlled. Carefully consider delandistrogene moxeparvovec therapy in patients with preexisting hepatic impairment or chronic hepatic viral infection as these individuals may be at increased risk of acute serious liver injury or acute liver failure. The safety and efficacy of delandistrogene moxeparvovec therapy in individuals with hepatic impairment or elevated GGT have not been studied. In clinical trials, increased liver function tests were commonly reported in individuals after delandistrogene moxeparvovec infusion.
  Renal Impairment
  Specific guidelines for dosage adjustments in renal impairment are not available; it appears that no dosage adjustments are needed.
