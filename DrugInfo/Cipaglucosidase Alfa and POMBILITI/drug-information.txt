    General Information
    Severe infusion-related reactions have occurred in patients treated with cipaglucosidase alfa. Infusion-related reactions may occur during or within a few hours after infusion and are more likely to occur with higher infusion rates. Consider premedication with antihistamines, antipyretics, and/or corticosteroids; premedication is recommended in patients who received it prior to previous enzyme replacement therapy. If a severe infusion-related reaction occurs, immediately discontinue the infusion and initiate appropriate medical treatment. Patients with advanced Pompe disease may have compromised cardiorespiratory function, predisposing them to a higher risk of severe complications from infusion-related reactions.

    pregnancy
    General Information
    Cipaglucosidase alfa, in combination with miglustat, is contraindicated during pregnancy. Based on findings from animal studies, the combination may cause embryo-fetal harm. Great vessel and cardiac malformations were increased in the offspring of pregnant rabbits exposed to cipaglucosidase alfa and miglustat at 16 times and 3 times, respectively, the maximum recommended human dose (MRHD) based on AUC. In a pre-and post-natal development study in rats, pup mortality was increased with cipaglucosidase alfa alone; maternal and pup mortality was increased with cipaglucosidase alfa and miglustat in combination.

    General Information
    Antibody formation can occur with cipaglucosidase alfa use. Antidrug antibodies (ADA) were present in 85% of enzyme replacement therapy (ERT)-experienced patients at baseline and 89% of patients 52 weeks after treatment. Patients who experienced an infusion-related reaction post-treatment were tested for anti-cipaglucosidase alfa IgE antibodies after the occurrence of the reaction. It is unknown if the development of ADA has an impact on clinical efficacy. Antibodies to cipaglucosidase alfa were cross reactive to alglucosidase alfa.

Description
  Text
  Cipaglucosidase alfa is a hydrolytic lysosomal glycogen-specific enzyme indicated for the treatment of late-onset acid alpha-glucosidase deficiency (Pompe disease) in adult patients who are not improving on their current enzyme replacement therapy (ERT). Cipaglucosidase alfa is to be used in combination with miglustat, an enzyme stabilizer. Pompe disease is caused by a genetic deficiency in acid alpha-glucosidase, an enzyme required for lysosomal glycogen degradation. Accumulation of glycogen in skeletal and heart muscle results in muscular weakness and respiratory or heart failure. Treatment with cipaglucosidase alfa and miglustat was compared to a non-U.S. approved alglucosidase alfa product and placebo in a study of 123 randomized patients (95 were ERT-experienced and 28 were ERT-naive). Key efficacy endpoints included assessment of sitting forced vital capacity (FVC) (% predicted) and 6-minute walk distance (6MWD). The mean change in sitting FVC from baseline at week 52 was -1.1% in patients treated with cipaglucosidase alfa in combination with miglustat compared to -3.3% in patients receiving the comparator; the estimated treatment different was 2.3% (95% CI: 0.02, 4.62). Patients treated with cipaglucosidase alfa and miglustat walked on average 21 meters farther from baseline as compared to those in the comparator group who walked 8 meters farther from baseline; the estimated treatment difference was 14 meters (95% CI: -1, 28). The ERT-experienced patients treated with cipaglucosidase alfa in combination with miglustat showed a numerically favorable change in sitting FVC and 6MWD from baseline at Week 52. Cipaglucosidase alfa may cause embryo-fetal harm; verify pregnancy status in patients of reproductive potential prior to initiating therapy. Serious hypersensitivity reactions including anaphylaxis and infusion-related reactions can occur. Patients susceptible to fluid volume overload or with compromised cardiac or respiratory function may be at risk for serious acute cardiorespiratory failure. Premedication with antihistamines, antipyretics, and/or corticosteroids is recommended and resuscitative equipment should be readily available during administration.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Cipaglucosidase alfa provides an exogenous source of the lysosomal enzyme acid alpha-glucosidase (GAA), which is required for lysosomal glycogen degradation. After binding to mannose 6-phosphate (M6P) receptors on the cellular surface, cipaglucosidase alfa is internalized and transported into lysosomes where it undergoes proteolytic cleavage that results in increased enzymatic activity and the cleavage of glycogen. Miglustat then binds with, stabilizes, and reduces inactivation of cipaglucosidase alfa in the blood after infusion.

  Cipaglucosidase alfa is administered intravenously. Volume of distribution ranges from 2 to 4.7 L in patients with late-onset Pompe disease. The metabolic pathway of cipaglucosidase has not been characterized, but the protein portion is expected to be metabolized into small peptides and amino acids via catabolic pathways. Mean cipaglucosidase clearance when administered with miglustat is 0.8 L/hour and mean half-life is 2.1 hours in enzyme replacement therapy (ERT)-experienced patients.

      Description
      Reconstitution
Allow vials to reach room temperature outside the refrigerator for approximately 30 minutes.
Inject 7.2 mL Sterile Water for Injection into each vial, down the inside wall to avoid foaming (not directly onto the lyophilized powder).
Tilt and roll vial gently until fully dissolved, which takes about 2 minutes. Do not invert, swirl, or shake.
Each vial yields 15 mg/mL after reconstitution.
Visually inspect the reconstituted solution for particulate matter and discoloration; discard if foreign matter is observed or the solution is discolored. The reconstituted solution is clear to opalescent, colorless to yellowish that is essentially particle free.
Storage: If immediate use is not possible, store for up to 24 hours refrigerated (2 to 8 degrees C [36 to 46 degrees F]). Do not freeze.
 
Dilution
Withdraw airspace within a bag of 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection. Remove an equal volume of 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection from the bag that will be replaced by the total volume (mL) of reconstituted cipaglucosidase alfa.
Slowly withdraw 7 mL of reconstituted solution from each of the vials until the patient's dose is obtained. Discard any remaining reconstituted solution in the last vial.
Add the reconstituted solution slowly and directly into the infusion bag. Gently invert infusion bag to mix the solution to prevent foaming; avoid vigorous shaking or agitation.
After dilution, the solution will have a final concentration of 0.5 to 4 mg/mL of cipaglucosidase alfa.
Do not use a pneumatic tube to transport the infusion bag.
Storage: Administer the diluted solution at room temperature without delay. If immediate use is not possible, store for up to 16 hours refrigerated (2 to 8 degrees C [36 to 46 degrees F]). Storage at room temperature is not recommended. Do not freeze. After removal from the refrigerator, complete infusion within 6 hours. Do not restore in the refrigerator. Discard the diluted solution if refrigerated more than 16 hours or if the diluted solution is not able to be completely infused within 6 hours after removal from the refrigerator.
 
IV Infusion
Consider pretreatment with antihistamines, antipyretics, and/or corticosteroids. If premedication was used with previous enzyme replacement therapy (ERT), pretreat with antihistamines, antipyretics, and/or corticosteroids prior to cipaglucosidase alfa administration.
Prior to administration, inspect the infusion bag for foaming. If foaming is present, let foam dissipate before administration.
If the diluted solution has been refrigerated, allow solution to equilibrate to room temperature for 30 minutes prior to infusion.
Use an in-line, low protein binding, 0.2 micron filter.
Infuse over approximately 4 hours. Infuse at an initial rate of 1 mg/kg/hour, gradually increasing the infusion rate by 2 mg/kg/hour every 30 minutes if there are no signs of hypersensitivity or infusion-related reactions until a maximum rate of 7 mg/kg/hour is reached; then, maintain the infusion rate at 7 mg/kg/hour until the infusion is complete.
If a severe infusion-related or hypersensitivity reaction occurs, immediately discontinue the infusion and initiate appropriate medical treatment.
Consider the risks and benefits of readministering cipaglucosidase alfa after severe hypersensitivity reactions (including anaphylaxis).
Some patients have been rechallenged using slower infusion rates and lower than recommended dosages. A desensitization procedure may also be considered. If readministration occurs, ensure the patient tolerates the infusion before the dose and/or rate is increased to the approved recommended dosage.
If a mild-to-moderate infusion-related or hypersensitivity reaction occurs, temporarily hold the infusion or slow the infusion and initiate appropriate medical treatment.
If symptoms persist despite temporarily holding or slowing the infusion, stop the infusion for 30 to 60 minutes and monitor the patient. Consider reinitiating the infusion at a reduced rate if symptoms have improved. If symptoms continue to persist, discontinue the infusion, and consider reinitiating the infusion within 7 to 14 days with appropriate premedication.
If symptoms subside after holding or slowing the infusion, increase the infusion rate to the rate at which the reaction occurred and consider continuing to increase the rate (every 30 minutes) in a stepwise manner until reaching the target infusion rate. Closely monitor the patient.
Discard any unused diluted product after 6 hours.
Do not infuse cipaglucosidase alfa in the same line with other products.
