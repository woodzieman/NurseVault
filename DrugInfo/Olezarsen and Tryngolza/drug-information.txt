    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    There are no available data on the use of olezarsen during human pregnancy. Triglyceride concentrations increase during the third trimester of pregnancy, placing individuals with underlying defects in triglyceride metabolism at risk for severe gestational hypertriglyceridemia and acute pancreatitis. In animal reproduction studies of an unconjugated antisense oligonucleotide (ASO), which shares the same nucleotide sequence as olezarsen but lacks the N-acetylgalactosamine (GalNAc) moiety, subcutaneous administration to pregnant rabbits and mice throughout organogenesis produced no adverse developmental effects at doses up to 21 times or 20 times, respectively, the maximum recommended human dose (MRHD). In a pre- and postnatal toxicity study in mice administered an unconjugated ASO from gestation day 6 through lactation day 21, offspring had lower body weights throughout their lives and slight delays in the attainment of morphological and developmental landmarks at a dose of 87.5 mg/kg/week (21 times the MRHD).
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Use olezarsen with caution during breast-feeding. There are no data on the presence of olezarsen in human milk, its effects on the breast-fed child, or its effects on milk production. Unconjugated antisense oligonucleotide (ASO) was present in low concentrations in the milk of lactating mice. Oligonucleotide-based products typically have poor bioavailability, and it is unlikely that a low concentration of olezarsen in milk will lead to a clinically relevant concentration in the breast-fed child. Consider the benefits of breast-feeding, the patient's clinical need for treatment, and any potential adverse effects on the breast-fed child from the medication or from the patient's underlying medical condition.

    Headline
    elevated hepatic enzymes
    General Information
    Elevated hepatic enzymes occurred with olezarsen treatment. Mean hepatic enzyme values increased from baseline but remained within the normal range. These increases occurred within the first 6 months of treatment and then stabilized. In individuals with severe hypertriglyceridemia (sHTG), elevations in hepatic enzymes to 3 times the upper limit of normal (ULN) or greater occurred more frequently with olezarsen 80 mg (7%) than with olezarsen 50 mg (3%) or placebo (3%). Hepatic enzyme values returned toward baseline after olezarsen discontinuation. Consider liver enzyme testing before initiating olezarsen or increasing the dose and as clinically indicated thereafter. If persistent elevations in liver enzymes occur (e.g., 3 times the ULN or greater), consider dose interruption and/or dose reduction. Discontinue olezarsen promptly if serious hepatic injury with clinical symptoms and/or hyperbilirubinemia or jaundice occurs.

Description
  Text
  Olezarsen is an apolipoprotein C-III-directed antisense oligonucleotide indicated as an adjunct to diet to reduce triglycerides in individuals with familial chylomicronemia syndrome (FCS) and to reduce triglycerides and the risk of acute pancreatitis in individuals with severe hypertriglyceridemia (triglycerides of 500 mg/dL or greater). It is designated an orphan drug by the FDA for FCS. Olezarsen is the first drug shown to reduce the risk of acute pancreatitis in adults with severe hypertriglyceridemia. Olezarsen can cause dose-related increases in liver enzymes. The most common adverse reactions in clinical trials were injection site reactions. Olezarsen was FDA-approved in December 2024.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Olezarsen is an antisense oligonucleotide (ASO) conjugated to a ligand containing 3 N-acetylgalactosamine (GalNAc3) residues to enable delivery to hepatocytes. Olezarsen binds to apolipoprotein C-III (apoC-III) mRNA, leading to mRNA degradation and a subsequent reduction in serum apoC-III protein concentrations. Reduced apoC-III protein concentrations increase the clearance of plasma triglycerides and very low-density lipoproteins (VLDL). ApoC-III is synthesized primarily in the liver and, to a lesser extent, in the intestines. In plasma, apoC-III is transported by triglyceride-rich lipoproteins (TRLs) and high-density lipoproteins (HDL). ApoC-III inhibits lipoprotein lipase (LPL) activity and reduces hepatic uptake of TRL remnants, contributing to hypertriglyceridemia.
 
Pharmacodynamics
Olezarsen decreased fasting apoC-III concentrations after administration of 80 mg subcutaneously every 4 weeks in individuals with familial chylomicronemia syndrome (FCS). Compared with placebo, fasting apoC-III concentrations decreased by 57% at 1 month, 69% at 3 months, 72% at 6 months, and 80% at 12 months. In 2 placebo-controlled clinical trials in individuals with severe hypertriglyceridemia, placebo-corrected reductions in fasting apoC-III concentrations after administration of olezarsen 50 mg subcutaneously every 4 weeks ranged from 57% to 68% at Month 6 and 53% to 67% at Month 12. With olezarsen 80 mg administered subcutaneously every 4 weeks, placebo-corrected reductions ranged from 63% to 77% at Month 6 and 58% to 72% at Month 12.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Subcutaneous Route
    Description
    After subcutaneous administration, olezarsen Tmax is approximately 2 hours. At steady state, the geometric mean Cmax is 659 nanograms/mL and AUC is 8,030 nanograms x hour/mL after olezarsen 80 mg subcutaneously once monthly in individuals with familial chylomicronemia syndrome (FCS). In individuals with severe hypertriglyceridemia (sHTG), the geometric mean Cmax is 157 nanogram/mL and AUC is 3,470 nanogram x hour/mL after olezarsen 50 mg subcutaneously once monthly and 251 nanogram/mL and 5,550 nanogram x hour/mL, respectively, after olezarsen 80 mg subcutaneously once monthly. Olezarsen Cmax and AUC increase dose proportionally after single subcutaneous doses ranging from 10 to 120 mg (0.13 to 1.5 times the approved recommended dose) in healthy volunteers. Olezarsen does not accumulate with repeat dosing.

  Text
  Olezarsen is administered subcutaneously. The apparent central Vd for olezarsen is 96.1 L in individuals with familial chylomicronemia syndrome (FCS) and 127 L in individuals with severe hypertriglyceridemia (sHTG). The apparent peripheral Vd is 3,920 and 6,660 L, respectively. Olezarsen is more than 99% bound to plasma proteins in vitro. After subcutaneous dosing, olezarsen primarily distributes to the liver and kidneys. Metabolism of olezarsen is by endo- and exonucleases to short oligonucleotide fragments of varying sizes within the liver. Less than 1% of an administered olezarsen dose is eliminated unchanged in urine within 24 hours. The terminal elimination half-life of olezarsen is approximately 4 weeks.

    Administration Details
      Name
      Subcutaneous Administration
      Description
      Allow the autoinjector to reach room temperature by removing it from the refrigerator approximately 30 minutes before administration. Do not use external heat sources to warm the autoinjector.
Administer by subcutaneous injection into the abdomen (except within 2 inches [5 cm] of the navel) or the front of the thigh. The back of the upper arm may be used when administered by a caregiver or health care provider.
Avoid injection into skin that is bruised, tender, erythematous, indurated, scarred, or otherwise damaged.
Administer using the single-dose autoinjector according to the manufacturer's Instructions for Use.
Storage: The autoinjector is for single use only. Store in the refrigerator at 2 to 8 degrees C (36 to 46 degrees F) in the original container to protect from light. Do not freeze. It may also be stored at room temperature of 15 to 30 degrees C (59 to 86 degrees F) in the original carton for up to 6 weeks. If not used within 6 weeks, discard.
    Name
    Injectable Administration
    Description
    Visually inspect parenteral products for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration whenever solution and container permit. Olezarsen is a clear and colorless to yellow solution. Do not use if the solution is cloudy, discolored, or contains particulate matter.
Missed dose: Administer the missed dose as soon as possible. Resume dosing at monthly intervals from the date of the most recently administered dose.
