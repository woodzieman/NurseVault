Drug Class: Teplizumab is a CD3-directed monoclonal antibody indicated to delay the onset of Stage 3 type 1 diabetes (T1D) in individuals with Stage 2 T1D.

Indication Specific Dosing
  General Information
  General Dosing Information
Patient selection for individuals with Stage 2 type 1 diabetes (T1D) diagnosis based on the confirmation of:
At least 2 positive pancreatic islet cell autoantibodies.
Dysglycemia without overt hyperglycemia using an oral glucose tolerance test. If an oral glucose tolerance test is not available, an alternative method for diagnosing dysglycemia without overt hyperglycemia may be appropriate. In adults 18 years or older, recommend following the definition of dysglycemia used in clinical trials to diagnose dysglycemia prior to use of teplizumab due to the lack of specificity of other measures.
Ensure the individual's diagnosis confirms an autoimmune origin and does not suggest type 2 diabetes or other forms of diabetes, including genetic forms of diabetes, maturity-onset diabetes of the young (MODY), latent autoimmune diabetes in adults (LADA), or diabetes secondary to medications or surgery.
Patient selection with Stage 3 T1D:
Select pediatric patients with recently diagnosed Stage 3 T1D (within the last 8 weeks)
At least 1 positive pancreatic islet cell autoantibody
Peak C-peptid 0.2 picomoles/mL or greater on a mixed-meal tolerance test. If a mixed meal tolerance test is not available, an alternative method for measuring peak C-peptide 0.2 picomoles/mL or greater may be appropriate.

    Treatment-Related Restrictions of Use
    General Information
    The safety of immunization with live-attenuated vaccines in teplizumab-treated patients has not been studied. Additionally, teplizumab may interfere with the immune response to vaccination and decrease vaccine efficacy. Administer all age-appropriate vaccinations prior to starting teplizumab. Inactivated or mRNA vaccinations are not recommended within the 2 weeks prior to initiation of treatment, during treatment, or up to 6 weeks after completion of treatment. Live-attenuated vaccinations are not recommended within the 8 weeks prior to teplizumab initiation, during treatment, or up to 52 weeks after treatment.
    Headline
    adrenal insufficiency, anemia, cardiovascular disease, Down syndrome, immunosuppression, infection, neutropenia, thrombocytopenia, viral infection, viral reactivation
    General Information
    Serious, life-threatening cases of viral reactivation, including Epstein-Barr virus (EBV) and cytomegalovirus (CMV) reactivation, have been reported with teplizumab. Individuals with immunosuppression, including individuals with Down syndrome, may be at increased risk. Serious cases have also been reported in adults with higher body surface area or comorbid conditions such as adrenal insufficiency or cardiovascular disease. The majority of serious viral reactivation cases were reported in individuals who continued treatment with teplizumab despite persistent, severe lymphopenia. Severe lymphopenia may be prolonged in adults, which may increase the risk for serious infections. Teplizumab therapy is not recommended in individuals with active serious infection or chronic infection, other than localized skin infections, and is contraindicated in individuals who are immunocompromised or have active viral infection, such as EBV or CMV infection. Prior to therapy, evaluate for active EBV and CMV infection and confirm absence of active infection on assessment of viral load, such as PCR testing. Before initiating teplizumab, obtain a complete blood cell count. Initiation of teplizumab therapy is not recommended in people with a pre-existing lymphopenia (lymphocyte count less than 1,000 lymphocytes/mcL), anemia (hemoglobin less than 10 g/dL), thrombocytopenia (platelet count less than 150,000 platelets/mcL), and/or neutropenia (absolute neutrophil count less than 1,500 cells/mcL).
    Headline
    hepatic failure
    General Information
    Before initiating teplizumab, look for evidence of hepatic failure or impairment by monitoring liver function tests. Initiation of teplizumab therapy is not recommended in patients whose alanine aminotransferase (ALT) or aspartate aminotransferase (AST) concentrations are greater than 2 times the upper limit of normal (ULN) or bilirubin concentration is greater than 1.5 times the ULN.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Avoid the use of teplizumab during pregnancy and at least 30 days prior to planned pregnancy. Available data on the use of teplizumab during pregnancy are insufficient to evaluate a drug-associated risk of major birth defects, miscarriage, or other adverse outcomes. Monoclonal antibodies can be actively transported across the placenta, with transport increasing as pregnancy progresses. Teplizumab may cause immunosuppression in an infant exposed to the drug in utero. Report pregnancies to Provention Bio, Inc.'s Adverse Event reporting line at 1-800-633-1610.
    Headline
    people who may become pregnant, reproductive risk
    General Information
    Counsel people who may become pregnant about the reproductive risk associated with teplizumab. There are insufficient data on the use of teplizumab during pregnancy to inform of drug-associated risks. However, exposure may cause immunosuppression in the exposed infant. To minimize fetal exposure, avoid the use of teplizumab for at least 30 days (6 half-lives) prior to a planned pregnancy.
    Headline
    neonates and infants exposed to this medication in utero
    General Information
    Teplizumab may interfere with the immune response of neonates and infants exposed to this medication in utero. Transport of endogenous IgG antibodies across the placenta increases as pregnancy progresses and peaks during the third trimester. There are insufficient data regarding infant teplizumab serum levels at birth and the duration of persistence in the serum after in utero exposure to identify a specific time frame to delay live virus immunizations. Consider the risks and benefits prior to administering live or live-attenuated vaccines in neonates and infants exposed to this medication in utero.

    Headline
    arthralgia, cytokine release syndrome, fatigue, myalgia
    General Information
    Cytokine release syndrome (CRS) has been reported with teplizumab; manifestations typically occur during the first 5 days of treatment. In clinical trials, CRS was reported in 2% to 9% of teplizumab-treated individuals. Of the teplizumab-treated patients who developed CRS, 13% had serious adverse reactions. CRS manifestations in teplizumab-treated patients included fever, nausea with or without vomiting, fatigue, headache, myalgia, arthralgia, increased alanine aminotransferase (ALT) and/or aspartate aminotransferase (AST), and increased total bilirubin. Liver transaminase elevations were observed in 56% of teplizumab-treated patients who experienced CRS. Specifically, 64% were up to 2.5 times the upper limit of normal (ULN), 32% were more than 2.5 to 5 times the ULN, and 4.5% were 5 to 10 times the ULN. To reduce the risk of CRS, premedicate with antipyretics, antihistamines, and/or antiemetics prior to teplizumab administration. Monitor liver transaminases and serum bilirubin at baseline and during treatment as clinically indicated. Discontinue teplizumab in patients who develop elevated ALT or AST more than 5 times the ULN or bilirubin more than 3 times the ULN. For severe CRS, consider temporarily pausing dosing for 1 to 2 days and, if symptoms have resolved or significantly improved, subsequently administering the remaining doses to complete the full course of treatment on consecutive days or discontinuing treatment.

    Headline
    anemia, leukopenia, lymphopenia, neutropenia, thrombocytopenia
    General Information
    In clinical trials, lymphopenia was reported in 73% to 78% of teplizumab-treated adult and pediatric patients; severe lymphopenia (less than 500 cells/microL lasting 1 week or longer) occurred in 0.9% to 1.4% of teplizumab-treated patients. Of the teplizumab-treated patients, 0.5% permanently discontinued teplizumab therapy because of lymphopenia. For most teplizumab-treated patients who develop lymphopenia, lymphocyte concentrations began to recover after the fifth day of treatment and returned to baseline by Week 6 without dose interruption. Severe lymphopenia may be more prolonged in adults. In pediatric patients (age: 8 to 17 years), lymphopenia was reported in 51% of individuals. Lymphocyte count nadir occurred at approximately Day 4 of treatment, with recovery and return to baseline by Week 4. Leukopenia (21%) and neutropenia (5%) were also reported in adult and pediatric patients during clinical trials. In clinical trials with pediatric patients (age: 8 to 17 years), leukopenia (34%) and neutropenia (23%) were reported. Anemia was reported in 27% of teplizumab-treated patients compared to 21% of patients receiving placebo. In pediatric patients (age: 8 to 17 years), a decrease in hemoglobin was reported in 5% of individuals. Thrombocytopenia was reported in 13% of teplizumab-treated patients compared to 5% of patients receiving placebo. Anemia and thrombocytopenia recovery occurred within 2 to 4 weeks of treatment. In clinical trials, 1.8% of teplizumab-treated patients discontinued treatment due to hemoglobin less than 8.5 g/dL (or a decrease of more than 2 g/dL to a value less than 10 g/dL) and 1% discontinued teplizumab due to a platelet count less than 50,000 platelets/microL. Obtain a complete blood count before initiating teplizumab. Monitor lymphocyte counts regularly during treatment and monitor for lymphocyte recovery after completion of teplizumab. Permanently discontinue teplizumab in patients who develop prolonged severe lymphopenia.

    Headline
    angioedema, bronchospasm, edema, rash, serum sickness, urticaria
    General Information
    During clinical trials, acute hypersensitivity reactions have been reported in teplizumab-treated patients. If severe hypersensitivity reactions occur, discontinue use of teplizumab and treat promptly. Rash was reported in 36% of teplizumab-treated adult and pediatric patients compared to 0% of individuals receiving placebo or control, with 33 excess cases of rash per 100 patients. In pediatric patients (age: 8 to 17 years), rash (51%) and hypersensitivity reactions (1%) were reported. One individual experienced a delayed hypersensitivity; this individual had a prior history of allergic rhinitis and presented with rash and arthralgia 16 days after completing course 1. The event resolved with antihistamine treatment. The majority of rashes observed with teplizumab treatment were not serious and resolved without intervention; however, 0.3% of teplizumab-treated patients compared to 0% of patients receiving placebo experienced a serious rash. Serum sickness was reported in 2% of teplizumab-treated patients compared to 0% of patients receiving placebo. The patient who developed serum sickness had a prior history of positive anti-nuclear antibody and presented with arthralgias, elevated C-reactive protein, and low C4 complement 5 days after completing teplizumab therapy. The illness resolved in 2.5 months. Anaphylaxis, with hypoxia and bronchospasm, was observed in 1 teplizumab-treated patient who was hospitalized. Angioedema (periorbital and facial) (0.3%), peripheral edema and generalized edema (1.6%), and urticaria (1.9%) were reported in teplizumab-treated adult and pediatric patients. Vomiting and bronchospasm were also reported as acute hypersensitivity reactions.

    antibody formation
    General Information
    As with all therapeutic proteins, there is the potential for immunogenicity and antibody formation. In a placebo-controlled trial in individuals 8 years and older with stage 2 type 1 diabetes, anti-drug antibodies (ADAs) were observed in approximately 57% of teplizumab-treated patients, 46% of whom developed neutralizing antibodies. Description
  Text
  Teplizumab is a CD3-directed monoclonal antibody indicated to delay the onset of Stage 3 type 1 diabetes (T1D) in individuals with Stage 2 T1D. It is also indicated to delay the decline in endogenous insulin production in individuals recently diagnosed with Stage 3 T1D. There is limited evidence of safety and effectiveness in individuals 45 year and older with Stage 2 T1D. Teplizumab is not effective as a disease modifying therapy in non-autoimmune dysglycemic conditions. In a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial of 76 patients 8 to 49 years with Stage 2 T1D, the median time from randomization to Stage 3 T1D diagnosis was 50 months in the teplizumab group and 25 months in the placebo group. With a median follow-up time of 51 months, teplizumab therapy resulted in a statistically significant delay in the development of Stage 3 T1D, hazard ratio 0.41 (95% CI: 0.22 to 0.78; p = 0.0066). In pediatric patients (age: 8 to 17 years) who were recently diagnosed with Stage 3 T1D within 6 weeks, two 12-day courses of teplizumab showed a statistically significant reduction in C-peptide decline at Week 78, supporting delayed decline in endogenous insulin production. Teplizumab is associated with increased risk of viral reactivation, including serious, life-threatening EBV and CMV reactivation, cytokine release syndrome, serious infections, lymphopenia, hypersensitivity reactions, and hepatic enzyme elevations; premedication and monitoring of lymphocyte counts, liver enzymes, bilirubin, and symptoms of viral reactivation are recommended. Teplizumab was FDA-approved in 2022.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Teplizumab binds to CD3, a cell surface antigen on T lymphocytes, on the surface of both CD4+ and CD8+ T cells during treatment with internalization of the teplizumab/CD3 complex from the surface of T cells. Teplizumab's mechanism may involve partial agonistic signaling and deactivation of pancreatic beta cell autoreactive T lymphocytes. Teplizumab increases the proportion of regulatory T cells and exhausted CD8+ T cells in peripheral blood.

      Intravenous Administration
      Description
      Dilution
Teplizumab must be diluted prior to use.
If the calculated dose is 2,000 mcg or less: Prepare 1 sterile syringe (polypropylene (PP), polycarbonate (PC), or glass), or 1 sterile glass vial with 18 mL of 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection, or one 50 mL or smaller polyvinylchloride (PVC) with di-(2-ethylhexyl)phthalate (DEHP) infusion bag with 18 mL of 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection.
If the calculated dose is greater than 2,000 mcg: Prepare 2 sterile syringes (PP, PC, or glass), 2 sterile glass vials with 18 mL of 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection, or two 50 mL or smaller PVC with DEHP infusion bags with 18 mL of 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection.
Remove 2 mL of teplizumab from the single-dose vial and slowly add to the sterile syringe (PP, PC, or glass), glass vial, or PVC with DEHP infusion bag containing 18 mL of 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection. Gently mix by slowly swirling the vial or rocking the infusion bag or syringe. The resulting 20 mL diluted solution contains 100 mcg/mL of teplizumab. If preparing a dose of more than 2,000 mcg, repeat the process with the second teplizumab vial and the second syringe, vial, or infusion bag.
 
Preparation
Calculate the volume of teplizumab 100 mcg/mL dilution needed for the infusion. Volume of 100 mcg/mL dilution = Dose (mcg)/100 mcg/mL
Infusion bag for IV administration
Using an appropriately sized syringe, withdraw the volume of diluted teplizumab solution required for that day's calculated dose from the 100 mcg/mL solution.
Slowly add the contents of the syringe containing the diluted teplizumab solution to a PVC with DEHP infusion bag containing 25 mL of 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection. For a calculated dose greater than 2,000 mcg, add the cumulative volume to a single infusion bag. Gently mix by rocking the infusion bag. Do not shake.
Discard the unused portion of the remaining diluted teplizumab solution in the syringe, sterile glass vial, or PVC with DEHP infusion bag.
Storage: If the teplizumab infusion bag is not used immediately, store at room temperature between 15 and 25 degrees C (59 to 77 degrees F). Discard if the infusion is unable to be completed within 6 hours of preparation.
Syringe for IV infusion via syringe pump
Use an appropriately sized sterile syringe (up to 60 mL) that is compatible with a syringe pump. The syringe pump should support rates as low as 1 mL/hour.
The final concentration range is 15 to 60 mcg/mL with a maximum infusion volume of 60 mL. A lower volume may be used to fit syringe pump technical constraints, provided the concentration remains between 15 and 60 mcg/mL.
If the calculated dose is 900 mcg or less:
Calculate the maximum volume that can be administered for the calculated dose using a minimum infusion concentration of 15 mcg/mL. Volume = Dose (mcg)/minimum infusion concentration 15 mcg/mL
Calculate the volume of 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection to be added to the infusion syringe: Volume of 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection (mL) = Volume of infusion (mL) - Volume of 100 mcg/mL dilution (mL)
If the calculated dose is greater than 900 mcg:
The maximum infusion volume is capped at 60 mL.
Volume of 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection (mL) = 60 mL - Volume of 100 mcg/mL dilution (mL)
Measure the appropriate volume of 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection from the above calculation into the infusion syringe.
Using an appropriately sized syringe, withdraw the volume of teplizumab 100 mcg/mL dilution required for that day's calculated dose and add it to the infusion syringe. For doses greater than 2,000 mcg, add the cumulative volume from both prepared 100 mcg/mL dilutions to a single infusion syringe.
Gently rock the infusion syringe to ensure the solution is mixed. Do not shake.
Storage: If not used immediately, store in a refrigerator between 2 and 8 degrees C (36 to 46 degrees F) for up to 12 hours, followed by a maximum of 6 hours at room temperature between 15 and 25 degrees C (59 to 77 degrees F), which includes infusion time. Discard if the infusion is unable to be completed within 15 hours of preparation.
 
Administration
Use of an in-line filter is not recommended. If necessary, use a polyethylene sulfone (PES) filter. Do not use light protected (colored) infusion sets.
Administration time may be slowed for individual tolerability.
Infusion bag administration
Prime the PCV with DEHP IV infusion set with the diluted teplizumab infusion solution. Do not waste any infusion solution during the priming process.
Minimum administration duration:
30 minutes in adult and pediatric patients 8 years and older
120 minutes in pediatric patients 1 to 7 years
After infusion, flush the IV set with a volume of 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection greater than or equal to the priming volume at the same infusion rate to ensure the full dose is given.
Syringe administration
Prime the IV extension set with the teplizumab infusion solution. Do not waste any infusion solution during the priming process.
Attach the infusion syringe to the syringe pump.
Minimum administration duration:
30 minutes in adult and pediatric patients 8 years and older
120 minutes in pediatric patients 1 to 7 years
Do not manually push the syringe.
After infusion, flush the IV extension set with a volume of 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection greater than or equal to the priming volume at the same infusion rate to ensure the full dose is given.

Premedicate with a nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID) or acetaminophen and an antihistamine, and consider premedicating with an antiemetic, prior to infusion for the first 5 days. If needed, administer additional doses of premedication.
Missed dose: Do NOT administer 2 doses on the same day. If a planned infusion is missed, resume dosing by administering all remaining doses on consecutive days to complete the treatment course.

Dosage Adjustment Guidelines
  Hepatic Impairment
  Specific guidelines for dosage adjustments in hepatic impairment are not available; it appears that no dosage adjustments are needed. Teplizumab therapy is not recommended in patients with hepatic disease whose alanine aminotransferase (ALT) or aspartate aminotransferase (AST) concentrations are greater than 2 times the upper limit of normal (ULN) or bilirubin concentration is greater than 1.5 times the ULN.
  Renal Impairment
  Specific guidelines for dosage adjustments in renal impairment are not available; it appears that no dosage adjustments are needed.
