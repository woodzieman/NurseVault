Drug Class: Brimonidine is a selective alpha-2 adrenergic agonist indicated for use as an ophthalmic agent to reduce intraocular pressure in patients with open-angle glaucoma and ocular hypertension, and as a topical agent to treat persistent facial erythema of rosacea.

Routes: ophthalmic, topical; Common adult doses: 3 drops/day/affected eye 0.1%, 0.15%, or 0.2% ophthalmic solution; 4 drops/day/affected eye 0.025% ophthalmic solution; 1 application/day topically brimonidine gel.

Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  General Information
  The use of brimonidine is contraindicated in patients with documented brimonidine hypersensitivity and in patients taking MAOI therapy (see Drug Interactions).Although brimonidine has minimal effects on blood pressure and other cardiopulmonary hemodynamics, it should be used with caution in patients with severe cardiac disease, cerebrovascular disease or coronary artery disease.Some brimonidine ophthalmic solutions (i.e., Alphagan®) contain benzalkonium chloride, a preservative that may be absorbed by soft contact lenses. Patients should remove contact lenses prior to instilling Alphagan® and wait 15 minutes before replacing them. The original Alphagan® formulation (0.2% brimonidine), preserved with benzalkonium chloride, has been discontinued; however a generic equivalent is available. Purite®, a preservative in the Alphagan® P formulation (0.1% and 0.15% brimonidine), is used to reduce the incidence of adverse effects associated with benzalkonium chloride.The hypotensive effects of brimonidine may decrease over time in some patients. The initial lowering of intraocular pressure (IOP) seen in the first month of therapy may not reflect long-term effects and patients should be closely monitored for increased IOP. This decrease in effect may occur at any time during therapy.Brimonidine should be used with caution in patients with Raynaud's disease, thromboangiitis obliterans (Buerger's disease), depression and orthostatic hypotension.Brimonidine has not been studied in patients with renal impairment or hepatic disease. It should be used carefully in these patient populations.Brimonidine is classified as Pregnancy Category B. Animal studies show that brimonidine crosses the placenta and enters fetal circulation to a limited degree; however, no harm to the fetus was noted. No studies of brimonidine in pregnant women have been performed. Brimonidine should be used in during pregnancy only if the potential benefit to the mother justifies the risk to the fetus.It is not known if brimonidine is excreted into breast milk. A decision to continue breast-feeding or discontinuing brimonidine should weigh the risks to the infant versus the importance of the drug to the mother.Brimonidine 0.2% ophthalmic formulation has been evaluated in children aged 2—7 years; the frequency of drug discontinuation due to somnolence is approximately 16%. The incidence of somnolence during brimonidine therapy appears to be substantially higher (50—83% frequency) in children aged = 7 years (25% frequency). The safety and effectiveness of brimonidine in children < 2 years has not been established, and is not recommended. Brimonidine use in young children and infants has been associated with agitation, apnea, bradycardia, convulsions, cyanosis, depression, dyspnea, emotional instability, hypotension, hypothermia, hypotonia, hypoventilation, irritability, lethargy, somnolence, and stupor. In neonates, brimonidine has been reported to rarely cause apnea, hypotension, hypothermia, and hypotonia.

    General Information
    Although brimonidine has minimal effects on blood pressure and other cardiopulmonary hemodynamics, it should be used with caution in patients with severe, unstable, or uncontrolled cardiovascular disease or coronary artery disease.

    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components.

    General Information
    Some brimonidine ophthalmic solutions contain benzalkonium chloride, a preservative that may be absorbed by soft contact lenses. Instruct patients to remove contact lenses before instilling brimonidine ophthalmic solutions containing benzalkonium chloride.
    Headline
    depression, orthostatic hypotension, Raynaud's phenomenon, scleroderma, Sjogren syndrome, thromboangiitis obliterans
    General Information
    Use brimonidine with caution in individuals with Raynaud phenomenon, thromboangiitis obliterans (Buerger's disease), depression, scleroderma, Sjogren syndrome, and orthostatic hypotension.
    Headline
    children, infants, neonates
    General Information
    Brimonidine 0.1%, 0.15%, and 0.2% ophthalmic solutions are contraindicated for use in neonates, infants, and children younger than 2 years. During postmarketing use of these ophthalmic solutions in infants, the following adverse events were noted: apnea, bradycardia, coma, hypotension, hypothermia, hypotonia, lethargy, pallor, respiratory depression, and somnolence. Brimonidine 0.025% ophthalmic solution is not indicated for use in patients younger than 5 years of age, and the topical gel is only approved for use in adults 18 years or older.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Brimonidine should be used during pregnancy only if the potential benefit justifies the potential risk to the fetus. Limited available data from postmarketing safety reports and published literature with brimonidine tartrate ophthalmic solution during pregnancy are insufficient to inform a drug-associated risk of pregnancy-related adverse outcomes including miscarriage, stillbirth, or congenital anomaly. There are no adequate and well-controlled studies of the use of topical brimonidine gel during human pregnancy. In animal studies, brimonidine crossed the placenta and entered into the fetal circulation to a limited extent. Brimonidine was not teratogenic following oral administration of doses up to 2.5 mg/kg/day in pregnant rats during gestation days 6 to 15 and 5 mg/kg/day in pregnant rabbits during gestation days 6 to 18. 55747
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Use brimonidine with caution during breast-feeding. There are no data on the presence of brimonidine in human milk after topical or ophthalmic use. 55747   However, limited data in breast-feeding individuals using brimonidine ophthalmic products have not demonstrated adverse reactions in the breastfed child. The FDA-approved labeling for both topical and ophthalmic brimonidine products recommends avoiding brimonidine use during breast-feeding due to the potential to cause serious side effects, including central nervous system depression and apnea, in the breastfed child. These types of effects have been reported during accidental oral ingestion by pediatric subjects less than 2 years of age. During ophthalmic administration in the lactating individual, systemic absorption can be minimized by applying pressure over the tear duct for at least 1 minute after ophthalmic administration and removing any excess solution. Consider the benefits of breast-feeding, the patient's clinical need for treatment, and any potential adverse effects on the breastfed child from the medication or from the underlying maternal condition.

Description
  Text
  Brimonidine is a selective alpha-2 adrenergic agonist indicated for use as an ophthalmic agent to reduce intraocular pressure in patients with open-angle glaucoma and ocular hypertension, and as a topical agent to treat persistent facial erythema of rosacea. In addition, a lower strength ophthalmic solution (0.025%) has been approved to relieve ocular redness associated with minor ocular irritation. When administered via the ophthalmic route to patients with open-angle glaucoma and ocular hypertension, brimonidine reduces intraocular pressure by approximately 2 to 6 mmHg. Because this reduction in intraocular pressure is achieved with only minimal effect on cardiovascular or pulmonary hemodynamics, brimonidine may be a treatment option for patients in whom topical beta-blocker therapy is contraindicated. When administered topically to patients with acne rosacea, brimonidine significantly improved facial redness as compared to vehicle control.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Brimonidine is a potent alpha-2 adrenergic receptor agonist that shows up to 1,700-fold selectivity for alpha-2 receptors over alpha-1 receptors. When administered via the ophthalmic route, brimonidine decreases intraocular pressure by 2 mechanisms: reducing aqueous humor production (primary short-term mechanism) and stimulating aqueous humor outflow through the uveoscleral pathway (primary long-term mechanism). In addition to lowering intraocular pressure, data from animal studies suggest brimonidine may provide a neuroprotective effect against the progressive neuropathy that is associated with glaucoma.  When administered topically, brimonidine binds to alpha-adrenergic receptors on smooth muscles surrounding the vessels of the superficial and deep dermal plexuses. By binding to these receptors, brimonidine causes vasoconstriction, thereby diverting blood flow away from the central face and reducing facial erythema associated with rosacea.
