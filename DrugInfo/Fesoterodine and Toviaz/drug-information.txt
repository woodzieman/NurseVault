Drug Class: Fesoterodine is an oral selective muscarinic receptor antagonist; the active metabolite of fesoterodine is responsible for the therapeutic actions of the drug.

Routes: oral; Common adult doses: 8 mg/day PO.

Common Side Effects (excluding routine GI upset): anaphylactoid reactions, angioedema, pruritus, rash, urticaria, weight gain, dysuria, infection, urinary retention, back pain, confusion, dizziness, drowsiness, headache, insomnia, blurred vision, myopia, xerophthalmia, angina, chest pain (unspecified), palpitations, qt prolongation, sinus tachycardia

In an Overdose: No overdose/antidote information is listed in this file.

Key Nursing Considerations: Contraindications/precautions: Fesoterodine is contraindicated in patients who have demonstrated a hypersensitivity to the drug or its ingredients. Due to its anticholinergic effects, fesoterodine is contraindicated in patients with urinary retention.

Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  General Information
  Fesoterodine is contraindicated in patients who have demonstrated a hypersensitivity to the drug or its ingredients.
 
Due to its anticholinergic effects, fesoterodine is contraindicated in patients with urinary retention. Anticholinergics may precipitate urinary retention in patients with preexisting urinary tract obstruction or prostatic hypertrophy; caution is warranted.
 
Because the antispasmodic effect of fesoterodine on smooth muscle can delay gastric emptying, fesoterodine is contraindicated for use in patients with gastric retention (e.g., gastroparesis, GI obstruction, pyloric stenosis). Caution is advisable when fesoterodine is used in patients with decreased gastrointestinal motility (e.g., severe constipation). Fesoterodine is not recommended for use in the setting of ileus, toxic megacolon or severe ulcerative colitis. In general, gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD) may be aggravated by the use of anticholinergic medications.
 
Fesoterodine is contraindicated in patients with uncontrolled narrow or closed-angle glaucoma. Fesoterodine may increase intraocular pressure and aqueous outflow resistance in patients with closed-angle glaucoma and caution is advised in patients with treated disease. Fesoterodine is not contraindicated in patients with chronic open-angle glaucoma. The anticholinergic effects of fesoterodine may make the eyes dry, and this may cause irritation for wearers of contact lenses.
 
Patients should be advised to use caution when driving or operating machinery while receiving fesoterodine since anticholinergic drugs can cause dizziness or somnolence. Patients should also be informed of the possibility for enhanced drowsiness or dizziness with concurrent use of alcohol.
 
Fesoterodine is not recommended for use in patients with severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh C) because the drug has not been studied in this patient population. The pharmacokinetics of fesoterodine are altered in the presence of moderate hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh B). Caution is advised in those with mild to moderate hepatic disease; however, dosage adjustments are not necessary.
 
Plasma levels of the active metabolite of fesoterodine are increased in the presence of mild to severe renal impairment. Dosage adjustments are required for those with severe renal disease. Caution is advised in those with mild to moderate renal impairment, although dosage adjustments are not necessary. There are no data available in patients with renal failure or those receiving dialysis.
 
Anticholinergics may exacerbate the clinical symptoms of patients with myasthenia gravis or autonomic neuropathy. Caution is advisable during use of fesoterodine in patients with myasthenia gravis.
 
Due to its anticholinergic effects, fesoterodine may cause decreased sweating. Extreme temperature variations (e.g., ambient temperature increase) should be avoided to prevent hyperthermia.
 
The safety and efficacy of fesoterodine is comparable between elderly patients and younger adults. However, a higher incidence of anticholinergic effects and urinary tract infections occurred in patients 75 years and older compared to younger patients during clinical trials (see Adverse Reactions). Careful titration of dosage is advisable in the elderly.
 
Safe and effective use of fesoterodine in children has not been established.
 
Fesoterodine is classified as pregnancy category C. In vitro data indicate that fesoterodine is not mutagenic or genotoxic. Animal studies suggest that there is no effect on reproductive function, fertility, or early embryonic development. There are no studies of fesoterodine in pregnant women. The drug should be avoided during pregnancy unless the benefit of therapy outweighs the potential risk to the fetus.
 
It is not known if fesoterodine is excreted into breast milk. Therefore, fesoterodine should be avoided during breast-feeding unless the benefit of therapy outweighs the potential risk to the nursing infant.

    The coadministration of certain medications may lead to harm and require avoidance or therapy modification; review all drug interactions prior to concomitant use of other medications.
    Headline
    bladder obstruction, prostatic hypertrophy, urinary retention
    General Information
    Fesoterodine is contraindicated in people with urinary retention. Older adults and people with clinically significant bladder obstruction or incomplete bladder emptying, such as people with benign prostatic hypertrophy (BPH), may be at increased risk of urinary retention. Monitor fesoterodine recipients for symptoms of urinary retention and consider discontinuation of treatment if urinary retention occurs.
    Headline
    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components. Fesoterodine is also contraindicated in people with hypersensitivity reactions to tolterodine. Fesoterodine and tolterodine are chemically related and both share the same active metabolite, 5-hydroxymethyl tolterodine. There is little evidence in the literature regarding cross-reactivity among the antimuscarinics for overactive bladder (OAB) if an allergic response has previously been noted, unless the medications share chemical similarities, like chemical structure or similar metabolites. Some publications recommend avoidance of other agents in the class if a serious allergic response has occurred.
    Headline
    constipation, gastroparesis, GI obstruction
    General Information
    Fesoterodine is contraindicated in people with gastric retention (i.e., gastroparesis). Like other anticholinergic agents, fesoterodine may delay gastric emptying. The use of fesoterodine is not recommended in people with decreased gastrointestinal motility (GI obstruction) such as severe constipation due to the risk of gastric retention.
    Headline
    Child-Pugh class C, hepatic failure
    General Information
    Fesoterodine is not recommended for use in patients with severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh class C), including hepatic failure, because the drug has not been studied in this patient population. In subjects with moderate (Child-Pugh class B) hepatic impairment, exposure to the active metabolite was increased as compared to healthy subjects. However, no dosage adjustment is recommended in people with mild or moderate hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh class A and B).
    Headline
    renal failure, renal impairment
    General Information
    Plasma concentrations of the active metabolite of fesoterodine are increased in the presence of renal impairment. Dosage adjustments are required in adults with renal failure or severe renal impairment (CrCl less than 30 mL/minute). Caution is advised in adults with mild to moderate renal impairment, but dosage adjustments are not necessary. In pediatric patients, dosage adjustments are required based on the weight of the patient and the degree of renal impairment. Use is not recommended for pediatric patients weighing more than 25 and up to 35 kg with eGFR less than 30 mL/minute/1.73 m2, those weighing more than 35 kg with eGFR less than 15 mL/minute/1.73 m2, or any pediatric patient requiring dialysis..
    Headline
    myasthenia gravis
    General Information
    Use fesoterodine with caution in people with myasthenia gravis as the anticholinergic activity may exacerbate the clinical symptoms of the disease.
    Headline
    closed-angle glaucoma
    General Information
    Fesoterodine is contraindicated in individuals with uncontrolled narrow-angle glaucoma (closed-angle glaucoma). Fesoterodine can worsen controlled (treated) narrow-angle glaucoma and should be used with caution in treated individuals.
    Headline
    activities requiring coordination and concentration, driving or operating machinery
    General Information
    Advise fesoterodine recipients to avoid driving or operating machinery or competing activities requiring coordination and concentration until they know how fesoterodine will affect them. Anticholinergic drugs like fesoterodine can cause central nervous (CNS) effects, including somnolence, dizziness, and blurred vision. Monitor for signs of anticholinergic CNS effects, particularly after initiating fesoterodine treatment or with a dosage increase. Consider a dosage reduction or discontinuation of fesoterodine if a patient experiences adverse CNS effects.
    Headline
    geriatric
    General Information
    The Beers Criteria state that antimuscarinics with strong anticholinergic properties, such as fesoterodine, are considered potentially inappropriate medications (PIMs) in geriatric adults with dementia/cognitive impairment (adverse CNS effects) or delirium/high risk of delirium (new-onset or worsening delirium). During clinical trials, the safety and efficacy of fesoterodine were comparable between geriatric and younger adults.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    There are no available data with fesoterodine use during pregnancy to evaluate for a drug-associated risk of major birth defects, miscarriage or adverse maternal or fetal outcomes. In animal reproduction studies, oral administration of fesoterodine to pregnant mice and rabbits during organogenesis resulted in fetotoxicity at maternal exposures that were 6- and 3-times respectively the maximum recommended human dose (MRHD) of 8 mg/day, based on AUC.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Use fesoterodine with caution during breast-feeding. There is no information on the presence of fesoterodine in human milk, the effects on the breastfed child, or the effects on milk production. Fesoterodine is rapidly metabolized to tolterodine, the active metabolite. One resource states that it is unlikely that concentrations of fesoterodine or tolterodine will be high enough to produce adverse effects in the breastfed infant. Long-term use of fesoterodine might reduce milk production. The breastfed infant should be monitored for anticholinergic side effects and weight gain. Consider the developmental and health benefits of breast-feeding along with the clinical need for fesoterodine and any potential adverse effects on the breastfed child from the drug or from the underlying maternal condition.

Description
  Text
  Fesoterodine is an oral selective muscarinic receptor antagonist; the active metabolite of fesoterodine is responsible for the therapeutic actions of the drug. Fesoterodine is indicated for the treatment of adults with overactive bladder (OAB) with symptoms of urge urinary incontinence, urgency, and frequency. It is also indicated for the treatment of neurogenic detrusor overactivity (NDO) in pediatric patients 6 years and older that weigh more than 25 kg. During adult clinical trials, a reduction in the number of urge urinary incontinence episodes per 24 hours was observed for both doses compared to placebo as early as 2 weeks after treatment initiation. In pediatric clinical trials (n = 82), treatment with fesoterodine resulted in improvements from baseline to week 12 in the primary efficacy endpoint, maximum cystometric bladder capacity (MCBC), with numerically higher changes from baseline for fesoterodine 8 mg/day than for fesoterodine 4 mg/day. As with other anticholinergic drugs, common adverse effects include dry mouth and constipation; monitor for urinary retention. Per guidelines for idiopathic OAB, either bladder-specific antimuscarinics or beta-3 adrenergic receptor agonists may be used initially as monotherapy or in combination with behavioral interventions depending on the patient's needs and treatment goals. When using a bladder-specific antimuscarinic, use of extended-release oral formulations (e.g., fesoterodine) is preferred to immediate-release products as these formulations may limit side effects such as dry mouth; transdermal therapy (i.e., oxybutynin) may also be offered. Patients experiencing intolerance or side effects may respond well to dose reduction or selection of a different bladder-specific antimuscarinic agent. Clinicians may consider combination therapy with an antimuscarinic and beta-3 agonists for patients refractory to monotherapy with either therapy alone. Fesoterodine was initially FDA approved in 2008.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Fesoterodine and its active metabolite, 5-hydroxymethyltolterodine, are competitive muscarinic receptor antagonists. Fesoterodine is rapidly hydrolyzed to its active metabolite, 5-hydroxymethyltolterodine, which is responsible for the therapeutic action of the drug. Actions on the bladder include inhibition of bladder contraction and a decrease in detrusor pressure. A urodynamic study revealed that administration of fesoterodine increased the volume at first detrusor contraction as well as bladder capacity. Other antimuscarinic effects may include constipation, blurred vision, and xerostomia.

  Text
  Fesoterodine is administered orally. After an oral dose, it is rapidly hydrolyzed to the active metabolite, 5-hydroxymethyltolterodine, which is responsible for the therapeutic action of the drug. The steady-state volume of distribution in adults is 169 L. Protein-binding is about 50%. Hepatic CYP2D6 and CYP3A4 are involved in the metabolism of 5-hydroxymethyltolterodine to carboxy, carboxy-N-desisopropyl, and N-desisopropyl metabolites which do not significantly contribute to the anticholinergic effects of the drug. About 70% of a dose is excreted renally as 5-hydroxymethyltolterodine (16%), carboxy metabolite (34%), carboxy-N-desisopropyl metabolite (18%), and N-desisopropyl metabolite (1%). About 7% is excreted in the feces. The half-life in adults who are extensive (normal) CYP2D6 metabolizers is approximately 7 hours.
 
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes and drug transporters: CYP2D6, CYP3A4
Fesoterodine is rapidly hydrolyzed to its active metabolite, 5-hydroxymethyltolterodine, which is metabolized via hepatic CYP2D6 and CYP3A4. Adult patients and pediatric patients weighing more than 35 kg receiving strong CYP3A4 inhibitors concurrently should not receive more than 4 mg/day of fesoterodine. Pediatric patients of lower weight should not receive fesoterodine if they are taking strong CYP3A4 inhibitors. According to the product labeling, no dosage adjustments are recommended during concurrent use of moderate CYP3A4 inhibitors or inducers. No dosage adjustments are recommended in people receiving CYP2D6 inhibitors. The active metabolite of fesoterodine does not have the potential to inhibit or induce CYP450 enzyme systems.

Dosage Adjustment Guidelines
  Hepatic Impairment
  Mild to moderate hepatic impairment (Child Pugh A or B): No dosage adjustments are needed.
Severe hepatic impairment (Child Pugh C): Fesoterodine is not recommended for use in this patient population.
  Renal Impairment
  Adults
CrCl 30 mL/minute or more: No dosage adjustments are needed.
CrCl less than 30 mL/minute: Dosage should not exceed 4 mg PO once daily.
 
Pediatric patients weighing more than 35 kg
eGFR 30 mL/minute/1.73 m2 or more: No dosage adjustments are needed.
eGFR 15 to 29 mL/minute/1.73 m2: Dosage should not exceed 4 mg PO once daily.
eGFR less than 15 mL/minute/1.73 m2 or requiring dialysis: Use is not recommended.
 
Pediatric patients weighing 26 to 35 kg
eGFR 30 mL/minute/1.73 m2 or more: Dosage should not exceed 4 mg PO once daily.
eGFR less than 30 mL/minute/1.73 m2 or requiring dialysis: Use is not recommended.
