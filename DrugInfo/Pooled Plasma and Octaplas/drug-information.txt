    hypocalcemia
    General Information
    Citrate toxicity can occur with the administration of solvent/detergent treated, pooled plasma at rates more than 1 mL/kg/minute (0.02 to 0.025 mmol citrate/kg/minute). Symptoms of citrate toxicity (hypocalcemia) include fatigue, paresthesia, and muscle spasms, especially in patients with liver dysfunction. To minimize citrate toxicity, administer calcium gluconate intravenously into another vein. Citrate toxicity was reported in 2 patients in an open-label single-arm postmarketing study (n = 41).

Description
  Text
  Solvent/detergent treated, pooled plasma is a parenteral, solvent/detergent treated ABO blood group-type specific pooled human plasma indicated for the replacement of multiple coagulation factors in patients with acquired deficiencies due to liver disease and those undergoing cardiac surgery or liver transplantation and for plasma exchange in patients with thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura (TTP). It is made from ABO blood group-type specific pooled human plasma, which is frozen to preserve the content of labile coagulation factors. Solvent/detergent treated, pooled plasma can serve as an alternative to fresh frozen plasma (FFP). Solvent/detergent treated, pooled plasma contains plasma proteins such as albumin, immunoglobulins, other globulins, coagulation factors, complement proteins, and protease inhibitors. The content and distribution of plasma proteins found in solvent/detergent treated, pooled plasma are similar to reference ranges for healthy donors, with the exceptions of protein S and alpha 2-antiplasmin, which are labile to solvent/detergent treatment and are controlled to ensure concentrations of 0.4 International Units/mL or more.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Solvent/detergent treated, pooled plasma replaces human plasma proteins. The active ingredient comprises plasma proteins such as albumin, immunoglobulins, other globulins, coagulation factors, complement proteins, and protease inhibitors. The content and distribution of plasma proteins in solvent/detergent treated, pooled plasma are comparable to reference ranges for healthy blood donors, with the exceptions of protein S and alpha 2-antiplasmin.

Pharmacokinetics
  Text
  Solvent/detergent treated, pooled human plasma is administered intravenously. Content and distribution of plasma proteins and coagulation factors are comparable to reference ranges for normal human plasma, except for protein S and alpha 2-antiplasmin, which are labile to solvent detergent treatment and therefore controlled to ensure concentrations in the final product of 0.4 International Units/mL or more. With a mean total protein content of 57 mg/mL, albumin comprises about 50% and immunoglobulin classes A, G, and M comprise about 3%, 12%, and 1%, respectively.

  Route Specific Administrations
    Administration Details
      Name
      Intravenous Administration
      Description
      Visually inspect parenteral products for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration whenever solution and container permit. Do not use if turbid, cloudy, or has deposits.
Do not shake.
Storage: Use thawed product within 8 hours if stored at 20 to 25 degrees C (68 to 77 degrees F) or 24 hours if stored at 1 to 6 degrees C (33.8 to 42.8 degrees F). Do not refreeze thawed product. Discard unused product.
 
Thawing using a water bath:
Thaw in the outer wrapper in a circulating water bath at 30 to 37 degrees C (86 to 98.6 degrees F). An overwrap bag may be used to provide further protection of contents if needed.
Prevent water from contaminating the entry port.
The minimum thawing time is 30 minutes at 37 degrees C (98.6 degrees F). Thawing time depends on the number of bags in the water bath but should not exceed 60 minutes.
 
Thawing using dry tempering system:
Place solvent/detergent treated, pooled plasma bags between the heating plates according to the manufacturer's instructions; thaw at 30 to 37 degrees C (86 to 98.6 degrees F).
Remove bags when thawing is complete.
Monitor and record the thawing process using the thawing device printer or barcode scanner recommended by the device manufacturer.
 
IV Infusion:
After thawing, administer using an infusion set with a filter.
Do not give drugs containing calcium in the same IV line as solvent/detergent treated, pooled plasma as precipitation may occur.
Administer at a rate not to exceed 1 mL/kg/minute to minimize the risk of citrate toxicity.
