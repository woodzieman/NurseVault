Drug Class: Obecabtagene autoleucel is a chimeric antigen receptor (CAR) T-cell gene therapy.

Routes: oral; Common adult doses: 410 X 10^6 CAR-positive viable T-cells as the total dose for split dose IV administration; dosage regimen determined by bone marrow blast percentage obtained within 7 days of lymphodepletion.

Common Side Effects (excluding routine GI upset): anemia, bone marrow suppression, leukopenia, lymphopenia, neutropenia, thrombocytopenia, graft-versus-host disease (gvhd), hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis, macrophage activation syndrome, arrhythmia exacerbation, heart failure, hypotension, palpitations, sinus tachycardia, weight loss, infusion-related reactions, rash, skin ulcer

In an Overdose: No overdose/antidote information is listed in this file.

Key Nursing Considerations: Contraindications/precautions: This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components. Serious hypersensitivity reactions, including anaphylaxis, may occur due to dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) in obecabtagene autoleucel.

          Dosage Description
          10 x 10^6 CAR-positive viable T-cells on day 1; refer to Release for Infusion Certificate for the exact volume to be administered on day 1. On day 10 (+/- 2 days), the dose administered is 400 x 10^6 CAR-positive viable T-cells supplied in 2 or more separate infusion bags. The dosage regimen will be determined by the tumor burden assessed by bone marrow blast percentage from a sample obtained within 7 days prior to the start of lymphodepletion; see the Release for Infusion certificate and Dose Schedule Planner for the actual cell counts and volumes to be infused and to select the appropriate dosage regimen. The total dose is 410 x 10^6 CAR-positive viable T-cells supplied in 3 to 5 color-coded infusion bags for split dose IV administration. Premedicate patients with acetaminophen approximately 30 minutes prior to the obecabtagene autoleucel infusion. Confirm the availability of tocilizumab prior to infusion. Following leukapheresis and T-cell collection, administer lymphodepleting chemotherapy with fludarabine (30 mg/m2 IV daily for 4 doses; given on days -6, -5, -4, -3) and cyclophosphamide (500 mg/m2 IV daily for 2 doses on day -6 and -5) prior to obecabtagene autoleucel administration; infuse obecabtagene autoleucel 3 days (+/- 1 day; minimum, 48 hours) after completion of fludarabine. The rate of complete remission (CR) achieved within 3 months after infusion was 42% and the median duration of CR was 14.1 months in 65 evaluable patients with relapsed or refractory acute lymphoblastic lymphoma who received obecabtagene autoleucel in a noncomparative clinical trial (FELIX). Bridging chemotherapy between leukapheresis and lymphodepleting chemotherapy was permitted; 91% of patients received bridging therapy.

    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components. Serious hypersensitivity reactions, including anaphylaxis, may occur due to dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) in obecabtagene autoleucel.

    General Information
    General Information
    Vaccination with live viral vaccines during or following treatment with obecabtagene autoleucel has not been studied. Live virus vaccination is not recommended for at least 6 weeks prior to the start of lymphodepleting chemotherapy, during obecabtagene autoleucel treatment, and until immune recovery after obecabtagene autoleucel treatment.
    Headline
    cytokine release syndrome
    General Information
    Cytokine release syndrome (CRS) is a potentially fatal reaction that has been reported with obecabtagene autoleucel therapy. Do not administer obecabtagene autoleucel in patients with active infection or inflammatory disorder. Promptly evaluate patients for hospitalization and initiate treatment of CRS with supportive care and further management per current practice guidelines.
    Headline
    activities requiring coordination and concentration, driving or operating machinery, neurological disease, neurotoxicity
    General Information
    Use obecabtagene autoleucel with caution in patients with a history of neurological disease (e.g., seizures, stroke, or memory loss). Severe neurotoxicity (e.g., immune effector cell-associated neurotoxicity syndrome [ICANS], encephalopathy) has been reported with obecabtagene autoleucel therapy. Neurologic toxicity may occur concurrently with cytokine release syndrome (CRS) or after CRS resolves. Advise patients to avoid driving or operating machinery or performing activities requiring coordination and concentration for at least 2 weeks after the obecabtagene autoleucel infusion due to the risk of neurologic events (e.g., mental status changes, seizures) and altered or decreased consciousness or coordination. Promptly evaluate patients for hospitalization and initiate treatment of neurotoxicity/ICANS with supportive care and further management per current practice guidelines. Provide intensive care supportive therapy in patients who develop severe or life-threatening neurologic toxicities.
    Headline
    inflammatory disorder
    General Information
    Do not administer obecabtagene autoleucel in patients with active infection or inflammatory disorder. Use obecabtagene autoleucel with caution in patients with recent infection. Serious and fatal infection including bacterial infection, fungal infection, and viral infection have been reported with obecabtagene autoleucel therapy. Administer prophylactic antimicrobials according to local guidelines. Viral infection and reactivation may occur in patients treated with drugs directed against B cells, such as obecabtagene autoleucel. There is no experience with manufacturing obecabtagene autoleucel for patients with human immunodeficiency virus infection or with active hepatitis B or hepatitis C infection.
    Headline
    new primary hematologic malignancy
    General Information
    New primary hematologic malignancy has occurred following treatment with BCMA- and CD19-directed genetically modified autologous T cell immunotherapies, including obecabtagene autoleucel. Mature T cell malignancies, including CAR-positive tumors, may present as soon as weeks following infusion, and may include fatal outcomes; life-long monitoring is recommended following obecabtagene autoleucel therapy.
    Headline
    blood, organ, tissue, or cell donor
    General Information
    Patients must avoid being a blood, organ, tissue, or cell donor during treatment with obecabtagene autoleucel.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Obecabtagene autoleucel is not recommended for individuals who are pregnant, and pregnancy after administration should be discussed with the treating physician. There are limited data on obecabtagene autoleucel use in pregnancy. No animal reproductive and developmental toxicity studies have been conducted. In the FELIX study, 1 patient became pregnant 6 months after treatment and had a premature delivery at 30 weeks. It is not known if obecabtagene autoleucel has the potential to be transferred to the fetus. However, based on its mechanism of action, fetal toxicity, including B-cell lymphocytopenia and hypogammaglobulinemia, may occur if the transduced cells cross the placenta.
    Headline
    people who can cause pregnancy in others, people who may become pregnant, reproductive risk
    General Information
    Counsel people who may become pregnant and people who can cause pregnancy in others about the reproductive risk associated with obecabtagene autoleucel. Based on its mechanism of action, obecabtagene autoleucel has the potential to cause fetal harm. Verify pregnancy status prior to treatment. Follow contraceptive guidance specific to the agents used in patients who receive lymphodepleting chemotherapy. There is insufficient exposure data to provide a recommendation concerning the duration of contraception required after treatment with obecabtagene autoleucel. There are no data on the effects of obecabtagene autoleucel on fertility.
    Headline

There is no information on the presence of obecabtagene autoleucel in human milk, its effects on the breastfed child, or its effects on milk production. Interactions
Cholera Vaccine: (Moderate) Patients receiving immunosuppressant medications may have a diminished response to the live cholera vaccine. When feasible, administer indicated vaccines prior to initiating immunosuppressant medications. Counsel patients receiving immunosuppressant medications about the possibility of a diminished vaccine response and to continue to follow precautions to avoid exposure to cholera bacteria after receiving the vaccine.
Dengue Tetravalent Vaccine, Live: (Contraindicated) Avoid administration of the live dengue virus vaccine with immunosuppressive drug therapy and prior to immune recovery following treatment with immunosuppressive drug therapy. When feasible, administer indicated live virus vaccines at least 4 weeks before planned immunosuppression or wait until at least 3 months after discontinuation. The time to restoration of immune competence may be longer in some patients. Patients with altered immunocompetence may be at increased risk for severe adverse reactions due to uninhibited growth of the attenuated live virus. Additionally, vaccines may be less effective if administered during a period of altered immunocompetence.

    Headline
    cytokine release syndrome
    General Information
    Cytokine release syndrome (CRS) occurred in 75% (grade 3, 3%) of patients with relapsed or refractory B-cell precursor acute lymphoblastic leukemia who received obecabtagene autoleucel in a single-arm study. Symptoms of CRS included fever (100%), hypotension (35%), and hypoxia (19%). The median CRS time to onset was 8 days with a median duration of 5 days. After the first infusion but before the second infusion, 68% of patients experienced CRS with a median time to onset of 6 days. At the first sign of CRS, evaluate patients for hospitalization and initiate supportive care treatment based on severity and consider further management per current practice guidelines; a delay in the second split dose or a discontinuation of therapy may be necessary based on severity. Evaluate for and treat other causes of fever, hypoxia, and hypotension.

    Headline
    anxiety, aphasia, confusion, delirium, dizziness, drowsiness, encephalopathy, headache, immune effector cell-associated neurotoxicity syndrome, impaired cognition, insomnia, lethargy, neurotoxicity, posterior reversible encephalopathy syndrome, seizures, tremor
    General Information
    Neurotoxicity including immune effector cell-associated neurotoxicity syndrome (ICANS) occurred in 64% (grade 3 or higher, 12%) of patients with relapsed or refractory B-cell precursor acute lymphoblastic leukemia (B-ALL) who received obecabtagene autoleucel; some cases were fatal. Neurologic toxicity may occur concurrently with cytokine release syndrome (CRS) or after CRS resolves. Forty-two percent (42%) of patients in the noncomparative trial received prophylactic anti-epileptic medications; all patients who developed neurotoxicity received treatment with high-dose corticosteroids. Symptoms of neurotoxicity included ICANS (24% to 38%; grade 3 or higher, 7%), headache (22% to 34%), encephalopathy (21% to 33%; grade 3 or higher, 4%), dizziness (14% to 22%), tremor (8% to 13%), anxiety (9%), insomnia (9%), and delirium (5% to 8%). The term encephalopathy included aphasia, cognitive disorder/impaired cognition, confusion, altered consciousness, attention disturbance, dysarthria, dysgraphia, lethargy, memory impairment, mental status changes, posterior reversible encephalopathy syndrome, and somnolence/drowsiness. The median time to onset of neurotoxicity was 10 days with a median duration of 13 days. After the first infusion but before the second infusion, 55% of patients experienced neurotoxicity with a median time to onset of 6 days. Motor impairment (6%), delirium (5%), and seizures (2%) were also reported in B-ALL patients who received obecabtagene autoleucel in a single-arm study.

    anemia, bone marrow suppression, leukopenia, lymphopenia, neutropenia, thrombocytopenia
    General Information
    Grade 3 or 4 lymphopenia (90%), leukopenia (87%), neutropenia (72%), thrombocytopenia (48%), and anemia (43%) that worsened from baseline were reported in B-ALL patients who received obecabtagene autoleucel in a single-arm study. Prolonged bone marrow suppression following lymphodepleting chemotherapy and obecabtagene autoleucel administration was also reported in clinical trials. Grade 3 or higher cytopenias not resolved by day 30 were reported in 71% of patients with relapsed or refractory B-cell precursor acute lymphoblastic leukemia (B-ALL) including neutropenia (66%) and thrombocytopenia (54%); grade 3 or higher cytopenias persisted beyond day 50 in 27% of patients including neutropenia (17%) and thrombocytopenia (15%). Additionally, grade 3 or higher febrile neutropenia was reported in 26% of patients. Febrile neutropenia sometimes occurred concurrently with cytokine release syndrome.

    Headline
    laboratory test interference
    General Information
    False-positive HIV test results have been reported in patients who received treatment with chimeric antigen receptor (CAR) T-cell immunotherapy, such as obecabtagene autoleucel. The use of gammaretroviral or lentiviral vectors to reprogram T-cells as part of CAR T-cell therapy has resulted in laboratory test interference with HIV-1 nucleic acid amplification testing (NAAT). False test results may occur due to vector interference with long terminal repeat (LTR) genomes in HIV NAAT. Alternative testing methods for HIV (e.g., assays that target p24 antigen and anti-HIV-1 antibodies or the integrase gene) should be performed in patients who have received CAR T-cell therapy.

Description
  Text
  Obecabtagene autoleucel is a chimeric antigen receptor (CAR) T-cell gene therapy. It is CD19-directed immunotherapy that works by using a patient's own genetically altered immune cells to kill B-cell cancer cells in the blood. Obecabtagene autoleucel is indicated for use in adult patients with relapsed or refractory B-cell precursor acute lymphoblastic leukemia. Obecabtagene autoleucel has black box warnings for cytokine release syndrome, severe neurotoxicity, and new T-cell malignancies. Obecabtagene autoleucel was FDA-approved in November 2024.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Obecabtagene autoleucel is a chimeric antigen receptor (CAR) T-cell therapy that works by redirecting T-cells to target the CD19 antigen on B-cells in patients with hematologic malignancies. The CD3-zeta domain activates downstream signaling cascades that lead to T-cell activation. Proliferation and persistence following activation are enhanced by the presence of the 4-1BB costimulatory domain. The binding of CAR to CD19 activates obecabtagene autoleucel and promotes cell expansion and differentiation and triggers the lysis of CD19-expressing cells.
This immunotherapy involves removing, genetically modifying, and then re-infusing a patient's own T-cells. During the manufacturing process, the cells are genetically modified using lentiviral transduction to express a CAR comprised of a murine anti-CD19 single chain variable fragment linked to 4-IBB and CD3-zeta costimulatory domains.
To produce CAR T-cell therapy, T-cells are collected from the blood by leukapheresis; enriched; activated; transduced with the replication incompetent lentiviral vector containing the CD19 CAR transgene; expanded in a cell culture; and then washed, formulated into a suspension, and cryopreserved.

    Pharmacodynamics
Peak levels of cytokines (e.g., IL-2, IL-5, IL-6, IL-7, IL-8, IL-10, IL-15, TNF-alpha, IFN-gamma, and granulocyte-macrophage colony-stimulating factors) were evaluated pre- and post-obecabtagene autoleucel infusion over 3 months; peak plasma cytokine elevations were observed within the first month after the infusion and levels returned to baseline by Month 3. Due to the on-target effect of obecabtagene autoleucel, the expected period of B-cell aplasia was observed in 93.1% and 80% of patients at 3 months and 6 months, respectively. Obecabtagene autoleucel expansion was not substantially different in responding (CR/Cri) and nonresponding patients. The maximum observed persistency of obecabtagene autoleucel was 36.5 months, with 75.9% of patients who had ongoing remission at the last laboratory assessment with ongoing CAR-T persistency.
 
Immunogenicity
Four percent of patients with relapsed or refractory B-cell precursor acute lymphoblastic leukemia who received obecabtagene autoleucel in a single-arm study tested positive in the cellular immunogenicity readout measured by interferon gamma production after the infusion. There was no clinically significant effect of humoral and cellular immunogenicity on pharmacokinetics or pharmacodynamics of obecabtagene autoleucel.
 
Tumor Burden
Persistency was not impacted by tumor burden. Patients who received a first split dose of 10 x 10^6 cells (greater than 20% blasts) had a higher expansion of CAR T cells (Cmax and AUC) compared to patients who received a first split dose of 100 x 10^6 cells (less than or equal to 20% blasts).
 
Effects of Cytokine Release Syndrome
Patients who experienced cytokine release syndrome (CRS) had 1.8-fold higher mean bone marrow blast percentage and higher CAR T-cell expansion (5-fold higher Cmax and 6.8-fold higher AUC(0-28 days)) compared with patients without CRS.
 
Effects of Immune Effector Cell-Associated Neurotoxicity Syndrome
Patients who experience immune effector cell-associated neurotoxicity syndrome (ICANS) have 1.8-fold higher mean bone marrow blast percentage and higher CAR T-cell expansion (3.3-fold higher Cmax and 2.9-fold higher AUC(0-28 days)) compared with patients without ICANS.

    Administration Details
      Name
      Intravenous Administration
      Description
      Obecabtagene autoleucel is for autologous and intravenous use only.
Confirm availability of obecabtagene autoleucel prior to beginning lymphodepleting chemotherapy.
Obecabtagene autoleucel is shipped directly to the health care facility in the vapor phase of a liquid nitrogen shipper, with each infusion bag packed individually within an overwrap and then enclosed within a metal cassette.
Prior to delivery, ensure the presence of onsite storage conditions in the vapor phase of liquid nitrogen (below negative 150 degrees C).
Transfer the infusion bag(s) within their metal cassette(s) to onsite storage below negative 150 degrees C until ready for thaw and administration. Transfer time out of the vapor phase liquid nitrogen environment should not exceed 90 seconds to avoid premature thawing.
Confirm the infusion time with the patient in advance and adjust the start time for thaw so that the recipient will be ready, and the dose will be infused within 60 minutes of thaw.
The total dose is supplied in 3 to 5 color-coded infusion bags (Blue, Red, Orange) for split dose administration.
The Release for Infusion certificate will contain the volume and total CD19 CAR-positive viable T cell number contained in each infusion bag, the dose to be administered on a given day, and the number of bags required to deliver the dose.
Record the blast percentage from the patient's bone marrow assessment, the bag serial number(s), the number of each bag type required for each dose, and the volume to administer via syringe (for the 10 x 10^6 dose, Blue Bag) on the Dose Schedule Planner provided with the Release for Infusion certificate.
Ensure tocilizumab and emergency equipment are available prior to the infusion and during the recovery period.
 
Preparation
Transfer only the cassette(s)/infusion bag(s) required for the given dosing day from the onsite vapor phase liquid nitrogen storage to the bag thaw location via an appropriate transfer vessel (i.e., a vapor phase liquid nitrogen shipper, maintaining a temperature below minus 150 degrees C).
If more than one infusion bag is required on a given dosing day, thaw each infusion bag one at a time and continuously monitor during the thawing process; do NOT remove subsequent bags from the vapor phase liquid nitrogen storage until the infusion of the previous bag is complete.
Leave the infusion bag in its overwrap and thaw at 37 degrees C using a water bath or dry thaw method until there are no visible frozen clumps left in the infusion bag.
Do not irradiate.
Gently massage each bag for 2 to 8 minutes until the cells have just thawed.
Remove from the water bath or thaw device immediately after thawing is complete and carefully remove the infusion bag from the overwrap.
Gently mix the contents of the bag to disperse clumps of cellular material.
Prime the tubing with normal saline and immediately administer to the patient.
Do not re-freeze or refrigerate the thawed product.
