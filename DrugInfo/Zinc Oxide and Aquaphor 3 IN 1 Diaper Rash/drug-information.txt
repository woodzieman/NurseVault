Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  General Information
  There are no contraindications associated with the use of topical zinc oxide products. However, type 4 or delayed hypersensitivity reactions may occur in patients with latex hypersensitivity who use zinc oxide topical products concomitantly. This occurs as a result of the chemicals used in the manufacture of latex chemically reacting in the presence of zinc oxide. These chemicals have been documented to cause allergic contact dermatitis.No problems would be expected with appropriate use of topical zinc oxide during pregnancy or breast-feeding (see Zinc Salts monograph).

Description
  Text
  Zinc oxide is a topical preparation that has astringent, antiseptic, anti-inflammatory, and skin protectant properties. It is commonly used in the prevention and treatment of various skin conditions, such as minor skin irritation, diaper dermatitis, incontinence-related dermatitis, or Rhus dermatitis due to poison ivy, oak, or sumac. Topical zinc oxide is also used as a sunscreen for the prevention of sunburn. It is incorporated into numerous topical preparations, including ointments, creams, pastes, lotions, powders, and sunscreen products, many of which are available over the counter (OTC). Zinc oxide may stain or damage some fabrics or surfaces.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Topical zinc oxide has astringent, antiseptic, anti-inflammatory, and skin protectant properties. When applied topically, zinc oxide creates a barrier to protect the skin surface from moisture, feces, urine, and irritation, preventing injury or allowing injured skin to heal. As a sunscreen, zinc oxide reflects, absorbs, and scatters ultraviolet (UV) wavelengths, specifically UVA. Zinc oxide is thought to exert its antimicrobial activity through membrane disruption, reactive oxygen species (ROS) production, and release of toxic zinc ions that lead to oxidative stress, DNA damage, and cell membrane disruption. Topical zinc oxide has also been shown to promote wound healing by aiding in tissue regeneration through activation of fibroblasts, endothelial cells, and macrophages where fibroblast proliferate. Zinc oxide also enhances reepithelialization and reduces inflammation.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Topical Route
    Description
    Zinc oxide is insoluble in water, but highly soluble in acidic or alkaline solutions. As a result of this insolubility, systemic absorption of zinc oxide following topical application is minimal.
  Text
  Zinc oxide is administered topically. While absorption of zinc into the skin does occur, the extent of absorption is minimal. Studies evaluating dermal exposure demonstrate that most zinc oxide remains on the skin surface or within the stratum corneum, skin folds, skin furrows and hair follicles, with limited penetration into the viable epidermis or dermis. If transdermal absorption occurs, zinc oxide will dissociate into zinc and oxygen ions. Once in the systemic blood circulation, zinc is primarily bound to albumin and is transported to the liver where some is stored, and the rest delivered to extrahepatic tissue. Other organs containing zinc include lungs, kidneys, spleen, and heart. Zinc oxide is primarily eliminated in feces and, to a lesser extent, the urine.

      Description
      Zinc oxide skin protectant
Gently cleanse the affected area.
Pat dry with a clean, soft cloth or allow to air dry.
Apply zinc oxide liberally as often as necessary to the affected area.
Advise individuals to stop use and contact health care provider if condition worsens or symptoms persist for more than 7 days, or symptoms clear up and then return within a few days.
For diaper rash: Change wet and soiled diapers promptly, cleanse the diaper area, and allow to dry. Apply liberally as often as necessary, with each diaper change, especially at bedtime or anytime when exposure to wet diapers may be prolonged.
For adult incontinence: Generously apply to affected areas as needed, particularly after an incontinence episode. Use a dry tissue to remove the excess product from the skin.
 
Zinc oxide sunscreen
Shake well before each use.
Do not apply to damaged or broken skin.
Apply zinc oxide liberally 15 minutes before sun exposure.
Reapply after 80 minutes of swimming or sweating, immediately after towel drying, and/or at least every 2 hours.
Advise individuals to use other sun protection measures, such as limiting the time in the sun, especially between 10 a.m. and 2 p.m., and wearing long-sleeve shirts, pants, hats, and sunglasses.
Protect from excessive heat and direct sunlight.
