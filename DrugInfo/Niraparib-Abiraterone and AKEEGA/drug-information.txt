          Dosage Description
          200 mg niraparib/1,000 mg abiraterone PO once daily in combination with prednisone 10 mg PO once daily and androgen deprivation therapy (ADT) (e.g., concurrent gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH) analog or bilateral orchiectomy), until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity. In an exploratory analysis, rPFS and overall survival were not significantly different in patients without BRCA mutations.

  Contraindications And Precautions
    Headline
    Drug Interactions
    General Information
    The coadministration of certain medications may lead to harm and require avoidance or therapy modification; review all drug interactions prior to concomitant use of other medications.
    Headline
    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components.
    Headline
    drug-induced bone marrow suppression
    General Information
    Do not begin therapy with niraparib; abiraterone in patients who have not adequately recovered from drug-induced bone marrow suppression. Monitor complete blood counts weekly for the first month of treatment with niraparib; abiraterone, every 2 weeks for the next 2 months, monthly for the remainder of the first year, and then every other month and as clinically indicated. An interruption of therapy, dose reduction, or discontinuation of therapy may be necessary for patients who develop anemia, neutropenia, or thrombocytopenia. If hematologic toxicity occurs and does not resolve within 28 days of treatment interruption, discontinue treatment with niraparib; abiraterone and refer the patient to a hematologist for further investigation including a bone marrow biopsy and cytogenetics.
    Headline
    cardiovascular disease, hypokalemia
    General Information
    Abiraterone may cause hypertension, hypokalemia, and fluid retention due to increased mineralocorticoid levels resulting from CYP17 inhibition; hypertension and hypertensive crisis have been reported in patients treated with niraparib in clinical trials. Prolongation of the QT interval and torsade de pointes (TdP) have been reported in patients who develop hypokalemia while taking abiraterone in postmarketing experience. Closely monitor patients who have underlying medical conditions that may be affected, including those with cardiovascular disease, heart failure, recent myocardial infarction, or cardiac arrhythmias. Monitor patients for hypertension, hypokalemia, and fluid retention at least once a month; control hypertension and correct hypokalemia before and during treatment with niraparib; abiraterone. The safety of niraparib; abiraterone in patients with left ventricular ejection fraction less than 50% or NYHA Class II to IV heart failure has not been established because these patients were excluded from the randomized clinical trials.
    Headline
    Child-Pugh class B, Child-Pugh class C, hepatic failure
    General Information
    Avoid the use of niraparib; abiraterone in patients with moderate or severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh class B, Child-Pugh class C) due to increased exposure of both drugs which may increase the incidence and severity of adverse reactions; data on drug exposure are not available for patients with hepatic failure. Additionally, hepatotoxicity has been reported in patients treated with abiraterone in postmarketing experience. Monitor transaminases (ALT and AST) and total bilirubin at baseline, every 2 weeks for the next 3 months, and then monthly thereafter; additionally, monitor liver function tests as clinically indicated if signs or symptoms of hepatotoxicity develop. An interruption of therapy, dose reduction, or discontinuation of therapy may be necessary for patients who develop transaminitis or hyperbilirubinemia. Permanently discontinue niraparib; abiraterone for patients with ALT more than 3 times the upper limit of normal (ULN) and concurrent total bilirubin greater than 2 times ULN in the absence of biliary obstruction or other causes, or in patients with ALT/AST of 20 times ULN or more for any reason.
    Headline
    adrenal insufficiency, infection, pituitary insufficiency
    General Information
    Use niraparib; abiraterone with caution in patients with adrenal insufficiency or pituitary insufficiency. Adrenocortical insufficiency has been reported in patients receiving abiraterone in combination with prednisone; adverse reactions associated with mineralocorticoid excess due to abiraterone treatment may mask symptoms. Monitor patients for signs and symptoms of adrenocortical insufficiency, especially after corticosteroid withdrawal or dose reductions, as well as during and after unusual stress including concurrent infection. If clinically indicated, perform appropriate tests to confirm the diagnosis of adrenocortical insufficiency. Increased dosage of corticosteroids may be indicated before, during, and after stressful situations.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Pregnancy should be avoided by female partners of men receiving therapy with niraparib; abiraterone during treatment and for 4 months after the last dose. Instruct pregnant women or females of reproductive potential to NOT handle abiraterone tablets without protection (e.g., gloves). Although there are no adequately controlled studies in pregnant women, niraparib; abiraterone can cause fetal harm or death when administered during pregnancy based on its mechanism of action and animal studies. If the partner of a patient is exposed to abiraterone during pregnancy, apprise her of the potential hazard to the fetus. When administered to rats during organogenesis in an embryo-fetal developmental toxicity study, embryo-fetal lethality (increased post-implantation loss, resorptions, decreased live fetuses), fetal developmental delay (skeletal effects), and urogenital effects (bilateral ureter dilation) occurred at systemic exposures of approximately 0.03 times the AUC in patients. Decreased fetal anogenital distance occurred at systemic exposures of approximately 0.1 times the AUC in patients, and decreased fetal body weight occurred at approximately 0.3 times the AUC in patients.
    Headline
    diabetes mellitus
    General Information
    Use niraparib; abiraterone with caution in patients who have diabetes mellitus; severe hypoglycemia has occurred when abiraterone was administered to patients with diabetes receiving concomitant thiazolidinediones or repaglinide. Monitor blood glucose in patients with diabetes during and after discontinuation of niraparib; abiraterone treatment; adjust antidiabetic drug therapy as necessary to minimize the risk of hypoglycemia.
    Headline
    contraception requirements, infertility, male-mediated teratogenicity, reproductive risk
    General Information
    Counsel patients about the reproductive risk and contraception requirements during niraparib; abiraterone treatment. Based on findings of male-mediated teratogenicity in animal reproduction studies and its mechanism of action, males with female partners of reproductive potential should avoid pregnancy and use effective contraception during and for 4 months after treatment with niraparib; abiraterone. Although there are no data regarding the effect of niraparib; abiraterone on human fertility, male infertility and impaired reproductive function has been observed in animal studies.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    The safety and efficacy of niraparib; abiraterone have not been established in females. It is not known whether either abiraterone or niraparib are present in human milk, although many drugs are excreted in human milk. Due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in nursing infants from niraparib; abiraterone, women should avoid breast-feeding if receiving niraparib; abiraterone.

Acetaminophen; Caffeine; Dihydrocodeine: (Moderate) Concomitant use of dihydrocodeine with abiraterone may increase dihydrocodeine plasma concentrations, but decrease the plasma concentration of the active metabolite, dihydromorphine, resulting in reduced efficacy or symptoms of opioid withdrawal. If coadministration is necessary, monitor patients closely at frequent intervals and consider a dosage increase of dihydrocodeine until stable drug effects are achieved. Discontinuation of abiraterone could decrease dihydrocodeine plasma concentrations and increase dihydromorphine plasma concentrations resulting in prolonged opioid adverse reactions, including hypotension, respiratory depression, profound sedation, coma, and death. If abiraterone is discontinued, monitor the patient carefully and consider reducing the opioid dosage if appropriate. Dihydrocodeine is primarily metabolized by CYP2D6 to dihydromorphine, and by CYP3A4. Abiraterone is a moderate inhibitor of CYP2D6.
Acetaminophen; Chlorpheniramine; Dextromethorphan: (Moderate) Abiraterone inhbits CYP2D6 and dextromethorphan is a CYP2D6 substrate. Monitor for dextromethorphan-related side effects, such as drowsiness, nausea or vomiting, sweating, restlessness, or tremor. Description
  Text
  Niraparib; Abiraterone is a fixed-dose combination of niraparib, a PARP inhibitor, and abiraterone, a CYP17 inhibitor. It is indicated for the treatment of adult patients with deleterious or suspected deleterious BCRA2-mutated metastatic castration-sensitive prostate cancer and BRCA-mutated metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer, both in combination with prednisone. Niraparib; Abiraterone should be used with caution in patients with a history of cardiovascular disease, as it may cause hypertension, hypokalemia, and fluid retention and its safety in patients with heart failure was not established in clinical trials.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Niraparib inhibits poly (ADP-ribose) polymerase (PARP) enzyme 1 (PARP1) and PARP enzyme 2 (PARP2). PARP enzymes are involved in normal cellular homeostasis, such as DNA transcription, cell cycle regulation, and DNA repair. In vitro, niraparib-induced cytotoxicity resulted in DNA damage, apoptosis and cell death due to increased formation of PARP-DNA complexes; cytotoxicity was observed in tumor cell lines irrespective of BRCA1/2 deficiencies. Niraparib decreased tumor growth in mouse xenograft models of human cancer with BCRA1/2 deficiencies and in human patient-derived xenograft tumor models with homologous recombination deficiency that had either mutated or wild type BRCA1/2.
Abiraterone acetate is converted to abiraterone, an androgen biosynthesis inhibitor. Abiraterone inhibits 17 alpha-hydroxylase/C17,20-lyase (CYP17) which decreases testosterone concentrations by catalyzing the conversion of pregnenolone and progesterone to their 17alpha-hydroxy derivatives, and the subsequent formation of dehydroepiandrosterone (DHEA) and androstenedione, respectively, by C17, 20 lyase activity; DHEA and androstenedione are androgens and are precursors of testosterone. In addition to reduced testosterone concentrations, inhibition of CYP17 by abiraterone can result in increased mineralocorticoid production by the adrenals. Concurrent corticosteroid (e.g., prednisone) administration suppresses adrenocorticotropic hormone (ACTH), helping to reduce the incidence and severity of adverse reactions related to mineralocorticoid excess.
Androgen sensitive prostate cancer responds to treatment that decreases androgen concentrations. Androgen deprivation therapies such as treatment with GnRH agonists or orchiectomy decrease androgen production in the testes but do not affect androgen production by the adrenals or in the tumor. In contrast, CYP17, inhibited by abiraterone, is expressed in testicular, adrenal, and prostate tumor tissues. It is not necessary to monitor the effect of abiraterone on serum testosterone concentrations. Changes in serum prostate specific antigen (PSA) concentrations may be observed but have not been shown to correlate with clinical benefit in individual patients.
In mouse xenograft models of prostate cancer, the combination of niraparib and abiraterone increased antitumor activity compared to either drug alone.

Abiraterone concentrations were also proportional to dose in the range of 250 mg to 1,000 mg.
Taking niraparib; abiraterone with meals may result in increased and highly variable exposures. Administration of abiraterone acetate with food increases the AUC by up to 10-fold and the Cmax by up to 17-fold compared to a fasting state, depending on the fat content of the meal.

  Text
  Niraparib; abiraterone is administered orally. Niraparib is 83% bound to human plasma proteins while abiraterone is more than 99% bound to human plasma proteins albumin and alpha-1 acid glycoprotein. The apparent volume of distribution of niraparib and abiraterone are 1,117 liters and 25,774 liters, respectively. The mean half-life of niraparib when given in combination with abiraterone was approximately 62 hours (CV, 42%) and the apparent CL/F was 16.7 liters/hour (CV, 27%). The mean half-life of abiraterone when given in combination with niraparib was approximately 20 hours (CV, 7.8%) and the apparent CL/F was 1,673 liters/hour (CV, 24%). Approximately 48% (range, 33% to 60%) of a radiolabeled dose of niraparib and approximately 5% of a radiolabeled dose of abiraterone were recovered in urine; about 39% (range, 28% to 47%) of radiolabeled niraparib and about 8% of radiolabeled abiraterone were recovered in feces. Unchanged niraparib, abiraterone acetate, and abiraterone accounted for 19%, 55%, and 22% of the administered doses recovered in feces, respectively; unchanged niraparib accounted for 11% of the administered dose recovered in urine.
 
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes and drug transporters: CYP3A4, SULT2A1, CYP2D6, CYP2C8
Niraparib is metabolized by carboxylesterases. Abiraterone acetate is rapidly converted to abiraterone in vivo by CYP3A4 and SULT2A1. In vitro, niraparib induces CYP1A2 and inhibits BCRP, MATE1, and MATE2. Abiraterone acetate is a substrate of CYP3A4, although coadministration with a strong CYP3A4 inhibitor did not have a clinically meaningful effect on its pharmacokinetics; coadministration with a strong CYP3A4 inducer decreased the mean AUC of abiraterone by 55%. Additionally, abiraterone inhibits CYP2D6 (moderate) and CYP2C8 (weak). It also has the potential to inhibit CYP1A2, CYP2D6, CYP2C8, and to a lesser extent CYP2C9, CYP2C19, and CYP3A4/5.

Dosage Adjustment Guidelines
  Hepatic Impairment
  Baseline Hepatic Impairment
Mild hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh A): No dosage modification is necessary.
Moderate to severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh B or C): Avoid use of niraparib; abiraterone.
Treatment-Related Hepatotoxicity
ALT and/or AST greater than 5 times the upper limit of normal (ULN) or total bilirubin greater than 3 times ULN: Hold niraparib; abiraterone treatment and closely monitor liver function. When ALT and AST resolve to 2.5 times ULN or less and total bilirubin to 1.5 times ULN or less, resume treatment at a reduced dose of niraparib 100 mg/abiraterone 500 mg PO once daily. Monitor transaminases every 2 weeks for 3 months and monthly thereafter; monitor more frequently as clinically indicated.
ALT or AST 20 times ULN or higher; OR ALT 3 times ULN and total bilirubin more than 2 times ULN in the absence of biliary obstruction or other causes; OR recurrent hepatotoxicity at a reduced dose of niraparib 100 mg/abiraterone 500 mg: Permanently discontinue therapy.
  Renal Impairment
  Baseline Renal Impairment
Mild to moderate renal impairment (CrCl 30 to 90 mL/min): No dosage modification is recommended.
Severe renal impairment (CrCl 15 to 30 mL/min): The use of niraparib; abiraterone has not been studied in this population. Monitor patients for increased adverse reactions.
