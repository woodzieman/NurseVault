    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    In general, topical antifungals have limited to no systemic absorption and may be used during pregnancy. There are no adequate or well-controlled studies of efinaconazole use during human pregnancy to inform any drug-associated risks of major birth defects, miscarriage, or other adverse outcomes. In animal reproduction studies, efinaconazole did not cause any fetal malformations when administered to pregnant rabbits and rats during the period of organogenesis at subcutaneous doses up to 112 and 154 times, respectively, the maximum recommended human dose (MRHD) based on AUC comparisons. Embryo-fetal toxicities (embryo-fetal deaths, decreased live fetuses, placental effects) were observed only in rats in the presence of maternal toxicity at systemic exposures 559 times the MRHD based on AUC comparisons. Subcutaneous efinaconazole administration to pregnant rats from the beginning of organogenesis through the end of lactation did not cause embryo-fetal toxicity or developmental effects at systemic exposures 17 times the MRHD based on AUC comparisons.
    Headline

    General Information
    Efinaconazole is compatible with breast-feeding. It is unknown if efinaconazole is excreted in human milk. However, maternal systemic exposure is very low after topical application to the toenails; it is unlikely that a measurable amount of drug enters human milk.

Description
  Text
  Efinaconazole is a topical azole antifungal solution indicated for the treatment of onychomycosis of the toenails caused by Trichophyton rubrum and Trichophyton mentagrophytes in adults and pediatric patients 6 years and older. Approval was based on the results of 2 randomized, double-blind, vehicle-controlled clinical trials involving adults with onychomycosis of the toenail. In these trials, complete cure (defined as 0% clinical involvement of the target toenail plus negative KOH and negative culture) at 4 weeks post-treatment was achieved in 17.8% and 15.2% of efinaconazole recipients compared to 3.3% and 5.5% of patients receiving the vehicle solution.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Efinaconazole exerts its antifungal activity by inhibiting fungal lanosterol 14-alpha-demethylase, an enzyme necessary for the biosynthesis of ergosterol. Ergosterol is an essential component of fungal cell membranes. By decreasing ergosterol concentrations, the fungal cell membrane permeability is increased, resulting in leakage of cellular contents.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Topical Route
    Description
    Pharmacokinetic parameters were evaluated in 18 patients with severe onychomycosis. The drug was administered topically once daily for 28 days to patients' 10 toenails and 0.5 cm of adjacent skin. On treatment day 28, the mean plasma Cmax was 0.67 +/- 0.37 ng/mL and the mean AUC was 12.15 +/- 6.91 ng x hour/mL. At steady state, plasma drug concentrations generally remain consistent over a 24-hour dosing interval.
  Special Populations
    Name
    Pediatrics
    Description
    In a pharmacokinetic study in 17 pediatric subjects 12 to 16 years of age with moderate to severe onychomycosis, the plasma concentrations of efinaconazole were relatively flat over a 24-hour dosing interval. The mean plasma Cmax and AUC for efinaconazole on day 28 were 0.55 ng/mL and 11.4 ng x hour/mL, respectively.
  Text
  Efinaconazole is applied topically to toenails. In healthy volunteers, the plasma half-life of efinaconazole, after 7 daily applications to all 10 toenails, was 29.9 hours. Distribution, metabolism, and excretion data are not available.
 
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes: None
Based on data from in vitro studies, therapeutic concentrations of efinaconazole do NOT inhibit CYP1A2, CYP2A6, CYP2C8, CYP2C9, CYP2C19, CYP2D6, CYP2PE1, nor CYP3A4. In addition, the drug does NOT induce CYP1A2 or CYP3A4.

  Route Specific Administrations
    Administration Details
      Name
      Other Topical Formulations
      Description
      Topical Nail Solution
Clean and dry toenails prior to application. Instruct patients to wait at least 10 minutes after showering, bathing, or washing before applying.
Hold the bottle upside down directly over the affected toenail. Lightly squeeze the bottle to release 1 drop onto the toenail. For the big toenail, squeeze a second drop to the end of the toenail.
Spread the drop(s) around the affected toenail(s) using the integrated brush applicator. Ensure the entire toenail (toenail bed, folds, hyponychium, and undersurface of the toenail plate) is completely covered.
Allow solution to dry completely.
Wash hands with soap and water after the application process.
