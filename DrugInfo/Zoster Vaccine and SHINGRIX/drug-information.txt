    Headline
    immunosuppression
    General Information
    Patients with significant immunosuppression may have a suboptimal antibody response to the zoster vaccine, recombinant. Immunosuppressed persons may include patients with human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) infection; severe combined immunodeficiency (SCID); hypogammaglobulinemia; agammaglobulinemia; altered immune states due to generalized neoplastic disease; or an immune system compromised by radiation therapy or drug therapy (e.g., chemotherapy or corticosteroid therapy with greater than physiologic doses). Short-term (less than 2 weeks) corticosteroid therapy or intra-articular, bursal, or tendon injections with corticosteroids should not be immunosuppressive. To maximize the immunologic response to the vaccine, consider delaying immunization of patients with HIV until virologic suppression on antiretroviral therapy is achieved or wait for immune reconstitution in those who had a CD4 count less than 200 cells/mm3.

Consider the benefits of breast-feeding, the risk of potential drug exposure in the child, and the risk of not vaccinating the lactating individual.

    General Information
    Overall, 17,041 adults aged 50 years and older received at least 1 dose of zoster vaccine, recombinant in 17 clinical studies. Safety was evaluated by pooling data from 2 placebo-controlled clinical studies involving 29.305 subjects aged 50 years and older who received at least 1 dose of zoster vaccine, recombinant (n = 14,645) or placebo (n = 14,660).     Headline
    edema, erythema, injection site reaction, pruritus
    General Information
    During the zoster vaccine, recombinant clinical trials, injection site reaction was commonly reported. The most frequently reported localized reactions were pain at the injection site (overall incidence 69% to 88%; grade 3 incidence 4% to 11%), edema or swelling (overall incidence 10% to 31%; swelling greater than 100 mm 0.1% to 2%), and erythema or redness (overall incidence 20% to 39%; redness greater than 100 mm 1% to 3%). During postmarketing surveillance, decreased mobility of the injected arm persisting for 1 week or longer and injection site induration were reported.

    General Information
    Guillain-Barre syndrome (GBS) has been reported with zoster vaccine, recombinant. Description
  Text
  Zoster vaccine, recombinant is an adjuvanted vaccine for the prevention of herpes zoster (shingles). Herpes zoster results from reactivation of latent varicella-zoster virus and typically presents as a painful rash; postherpetic neuralgia, characterized by persistent nerve pain after herpes zoster, is the most common complication. The vaccine has been shown to enhance varicella-zoster virus-specific immune responses, which is thought to be the mechanism by which it protects against herpes zoster. In a randomized, placebo-controlled trial, zoster vaccine, recombinant reduced the risk of herpes zoster by 97.2% (95% CI 93.7% to 99.0%) in adults 50 years and older; pooled data from pivotal studies demonstrated 91.3% efficacy (95% CI 86.9% to 94.5%) in adults 70 years and older. In adults 70 years and older, pooled vaccine efficacy against postherpetic neuralgia was 88.8% (95% CI 68.7% to 97.1%). In immunocompromised adults 18 years and older who had undergone autologous hematopoietic stem cell transplantation, vaccine efficacy against herpes zoster was 68.2% (95% CI 55.5% to 77.6%); a post hoc analysis in adults with hematologic malignancies demonstrated an efficacy of 87.2% (95% CI 44.2% to 98.6%). Long-term follow-up of 7,273 recipients extended from a median of 5.6 years to a median of 11.4 years after vaccination, with herpes zoster incidence rates remaining lower than those observed in the placebo groups of the original pivotal studies across the evaluated age groups. Zoster vaccine, recombinant is not indicated for prevention of primary varicella infection and is contraindicated in individuals with a history of a severe allergic reaction, such as anaphylaxis, to any vaccine component or after a previous dose. Zoster vaccine, recombinant, adjuvanted was initially FDA-approved in 2017.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  The risk of developing herpes zoster increases with age and immunosuppression due to disease or therapy and appears to be related to a decline in varicella-zoster virus (VZV)-specific cell-mediated immunity. Boosting the VZV-specific immune response is thought to be the mechanism by which zoster vaccine, recombinant protects against herpes zoster and its complications.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Intramuscular Route
    Description
    Efficacy of 2 doses of zoster vaccine, recombinant was studied in individuals aged 50 years and older and 70 years and older. Efficacy was evaluated in a modified Total Vaccinated Cohort that included individuals who received 2 doses of zoster vaccine, recombinant and did not develop a confirmed case of herpes zoster (HZ) within 1 month after the second dose. Vaccine efficacy was determined by comparing the incidence of confirmed HZ during follow-up between the vaccine and placebo groups. Compared with placebo, zoster vaccine, recombinant reduced the risk of developing HZ by 97.2% (95% CI: 93.7; 99) in patients 50 years and older. Over a median of 3.1 years, individuals were followed prospectively for the development of postherpetic neuralgia (PHN), a herpes zoster-related complication. No cases of PHN were reported in the vaccine group compared with 18 cases reported in the placebo group. In a second study of individuals 70 years and older, zoster vaccine, recombinant reduced the risk of developing HZ by 89.8% (95% CI: 84.3; 93.7) compared with placebo. Over a median of 3.9 years, individuals were followed prospectively for the development of postherpetic neuralgia (PHN). Four cases of PHN were reported in the vaccine group compared with 28 cases reported in the placebo group.

Administration
  General Information
  For storage information, see the specific product information within the How Supplied section.
Verify immunization status in the state or local immunization information system in addition to the health care electronic record. If this vaccine has been previously given, question the patient, parent, or guardian about any symptoms or signs of an adverse reaction.
Advise individuals on the benefits and risks of the vaccine. Provide a copy of the Vaccine Information Statement (VIS).
Record manufacturer, lot number, administration date, and the name and address of the person administering the vaccine.

      Intramuscular Administration
      Description
      Preparation
Vial
Prepare zoster vaccine, recombinant by reconstituting the lyophilized varicella zoster virus glycoprotein E (gE) antigen component with the accompanying AS01B adjuvant suspension component.
Using a sterile needle and sterile syringe, withdraw the entire contents of the vial of the adjuvant suspension component (vial 1 of 2) by slightly tilting the vial.
Slowly transfer the entire contents of the syringe into the lyophilized gE antigen component vial (vial 2 of 2).
Gently swirl the vial until the contents are thoroughly mixed and the powder is completely dissolved. Do not shake vigorously.
Withdraw 0.5 mL of reconstituted product for the dose.
Storage of reconstituted vaccine: If possible, administer immediately after reconstitution; however, reconstituted vaccine may be stored refrigerated between 2 and 8 degrees C (36 and 46 degrees F) for up to 6 hours.
Prefilled syringe
Attach a sterile needle to the prefilled syringe.
Storage: The prefilled syringe may be kept at a temperature between 8 and 25 degrees C (46 and 77 degrees F) for up to 72 hours.
Administration
For IM use only.
The preferred site for administration is the deltoid region of the upper arm.

    Description
    Visually inspect parenteral products for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration whenever solution and container permit. The reconstituted vaccine and prefilled syringe should be an opalescent, colorless to pale brownish liquid. If discoloration or visible particulate matter are present, discard the vaccine dose.
Do not mix with any other vaccine or product in the same syringe.
