    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Discontinue enlicitide when pregnancy is recognized unless the benefits of therapy outweigh the potential risks to the fetus. There are insufficient data on the use of enlicitide during pregnancy to evaluate for a drug associated risk of major birth defects, miscarriage, or adverse outcomes for the pregnant individual or fetus. Due to the lack of human data, guidelines recommend avoiding the use of other PCSK9 inhibitors during pregnancy. FDA-approved labeling advises considering the individual's ongoing need for therapy. Enlicitide may cause fetal harm based upon its mechanism of action. Enlicitide increases LDL-C uptake and reduces LDL-C concentrations in the circulation, which decreases cholesterol and possibly other biologically active substances derived from cholesterol. Cholesterol and other products of the cholesterol biosynthesis pathway are essential components for fetal development, including synthesis of steroids and cell membranes. Animal data indicate that enlicitide crosses the placenta. Enlicitide was detected in the fetal plasma of rats at concentrations that were 3 to 5% of mean maternal plasma concentrations. In animal reproduction studies, no adverse embryofetal developmental effects were observed in pregnant rats and rabbits administered enlicitide subcutaneously during organogenesis (up to 58- and 51-fold, respectively, the maximum recommended human dose, based on AUC). Consider the benefits and risks of enlicitide when prescribing during pregnancy.

    Use caution enlicitide with caution during breast-feeding. Description
  Text
  Enlicitide is a proprotein convertase subtilisin/kexin type 9 (PCSK9) inhibitor indicated as an adjunct to diet and exercise to reduce low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) in adults with hypercholesterolemia, including heterozygous familial hypercholesterolemia (HeFH). In clinical trials, enlicitide decreased LDL-C concentrations by approximately 57% to 65%. The most common adverse reactions reported with enlicitide are dizziness and diarrhea. Enlicitide must be administered in the morning on an empty stomach with water, black coffee, or plain tea. At least 30 minutes should elapse after taking enlicitide before eating or drinking beverages other than water, black coffee, or plain tea.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Enlicitide is a macrocyclic peptide that binds to proprotein convertase/subtilisin kexin type 9 (PCSK9) and inhibits PCSK9 binding to low-density lipoprotein receptors (LDLR) on the surface of hepatocytes. When PCSK9 binds to LDLR, it promotes LDLR degradation within the liver, increasing LDL-C concentrations. Thus, by inhibiting the binding of PCSK9 to LDLR, enlicitide increases the number of LDLRs available to clear LDL-C, thereby, lowering LDL-C concentrations.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Oral Route
    Description
    The oral bioavailability of enlicitide is approximately 1%. Enlicitide undergoes rapid absorption with a Tmax of approximately 0.5 to 1 hour following oral administration on an empty stomach in the morning with a meal given at least 30 minutes after the enlicitide dose. To facilitate oral absorption, each enlicitide tablet contains sodium caprate. Sodium caprate is a permeation enhancer that temporarily loosens the tight junctions between epithelial cells in the intestines. Following enlicitide 20 mg once daily, steady-state occurs after approximately 7 days of therapy and the accumulation ratio is 1.42. At steady-state, the geometric means (% coefficient variation) of the AUC and Cmax are 308 nanomolar per hour (28%) and 15.5 nanomolar (29%), respectively. Enlicitide plasma exposures were found to be less than dose-proportional between 0.25 times and 15 times the recommended dose of 20 mg once daily in individuals with hypercholesterolemia. Administration of enlicitide on an empty stomach in the morning, at least 30 minutes before a meal, does not affect the exposure of enlicitide. However, administration 30 minutes after a meal reduces steady-state exposure and Cmax by 48% and 50%, respectively, and delays Tmax. The administration of enlicitide with black coffee or plain tea does not result in a clinically meaningful change in enlicitide exposure.

    Renal Impairment
    Description
    Enlicitide steady-state exposures are expected to be 5%, 11%, and 26% higher in individuals with mild (eGFR 60 to 89 mL/minute), moderate (eGFR 30 to 59 mL/minute), and severe renal impairment (eGFR less than 30 mL/minute) compared to individuals with normal renal function; however, these increases in exposure are not clinically significant. Following administration of a single dose to individuals with end-stage renal disease (ESRD) receiving hemodialysis, enlicitide exposure (AUC0-inf) was 17% and 75% higher when administered before hemodialysis and after hemodialysis, respectively, compared to individuals with normal renal function; these increases in exposure are not clinically significant.

  Text
  Enlicitide is administered orally. The steady-state Vd is 2,834 L and the blood to plasma ratio is approximately 0.6. Enlicitide plasma protein binding is concentration dependent and ranges from 32% bound at 1 micromolar to 93% bound at 2 nanomolar. Enlicitide undergoes minimal metabolism and does not have any known major metabolites. In individuals with hypercholesterolemia treated with 20 mg of enlicitide once daily, the apparent clearance at steady-state is approximately 41 L/hour. Enlicitide clearance increases with increasing concentration, resulting in nonlinear pharmacokinetics. The primary route of elimination is via glomerular filtration, with approximately 73% and 8% of an intravenous enlicitide dose excreted in urine and feces, respectively. The effective half-life is approximately 14 hours and the terminal half-life is approximately 244 hours.
 
Pharmacodynamics
At 0.5 to 1 hour (Tmax for enlicitide) post-administration of single doses of enlicitide 10 mg or more, there is a maximum reduction in plasma concentrations of free proprotein convertase/subtilisin kexin type 9 (PCSK9) of more than 90%. Based upon exposure-response analysis across enlicitide daily doses ranging from 0.5 times to 1.5 times the recommended dose, the 20 mg dose achieves near-maximal reductions in plasma LDL-C in individuals with hypercholesterolemia.
 
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes and drug transporters: none
