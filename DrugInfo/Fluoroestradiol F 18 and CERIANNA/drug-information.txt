    Treatment-Related Restrictions of Use
    General Information
    Fluoroestradiol F 18 is indicated for use with positron emission tomography (PET) to detect estrogen receptor (ER)-positive lesions as an adjunct to biopsy in individuals with recurrent or metastatic breast cancer; it is not useful for imaging other receptors, such as human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2) and the progesterone receptor (PR). Further, the uptake of fluoroestradiol F 18 is not specific for breast cancer and may occur in other ER-positive tumors that develop outside of the breasts (e.g., uterus, ovaries). Use tissue biopsy to confirm the recurrence of breast cancer and to verify estrogen receptor status by pathology. Do not use fluoroestradiol F 18 in lieu of tissue biopsy when a biopsy is indicated.

Interactions
Elacestrant: (Major) Hold elacestrant for at least 11 days (5 biological half-lives) prior to completing an imaging study with fluoroestradiol F 18. Medications that bind to the estrogen receptor (ER), such as elacestrant, may compete with fluoroestradiol F18 binding and reduce the detection of ER-positive lesions.

Description
  Text
  Fluoroestradiol F 18 is a parenteral radioactive diagnostic agent indicated for use with positron emission tomography (PET) imaging for the detection of estrogen receptor (ER)-positive lesions as an adjunct to biopsy in patients with recurrent or metastatic breast cancer. The uptake of fluoroestradiol F 18 is not specific for breast cancer and may occur in a variety of ER-positive tumors that arise outside of the breast, including from the uterus and ovaries. Therefore, tissue biopsy is necessary to confirm recurrence of breast cancer and to verify ER status by pathology. Fluoroestradiol F 18 is not useful for imaging other receptors, such as human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2) and the progesterone receptor (PR).

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Fluoroestradiol F 18 is a radiolabeled, synthetic estrogen analog. After intravenous administration, fluoroestradiol F18 is taken up by tumors containing estrogen receptors. Uptake of fluoroestradiol F 18 depends on estrogen receptor (ER) density and function in tumors and physiologic tissue. The F 18 isotope produces a positron signal that is detected by a positron emission tomography (PET) scanner. Detection of ER-positive tumors should be based on comparison with tissue background outside of organs with high physiologic uptake and regions with high activity due to hepatobiliary and urinary excretion. The uptake of fluoroestradiol F 18 is not specific for breast cancer and may occur in a variety of ER-positive tumors that arise outside of the breast, including from the uterus and ovaries. Therefore, tissue biopsy is necessary to confirm recurrence of breast cancer and to verify ER status by pathology. Fluoroestradiol F 18 is not useful for imaging other receptors, such as human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2) and the progesterone receptor (PR).

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Intravenous Route
    Description
    The amount of radiation absorbed after exposure to a 222 MBq (6 mCi) dose of fluoroestradiol F 18 is estimated to be 4.9 mSv, with the highest magnitudes of radiation absorbed in the liver (0.126 mGy/MBq) and gallbladder (0.102 mGy/MBq). A positron emission tomography (PET) or computerized tomography (CT) scan will increase radiation exposure by an amount dependent on the settings used. The physical half-life of fluoroestradiol F 18 is 109.8 minutes.

  Text
  Fluoroestradiol F 18 is administered intravenously. After intravenous administration, fluoroestradiol F 18 is 95% bound to plasma proteins. Fluoroestradiol F 18 primarily distributes to the hepatobiliary system, but also to the small and large intestines, heart wall, blood, kidneys, uterus, and bladder. Fluoroestradiol F 18 is metabolized in the liver. At 20 minutes after injection, approximately 20% of circulating radioactivity in the plasma is non-metabolized fluoroestradiol F 18; at 2 hours after injection, circulating fluoroestradiol F 18 levels are less than 5% of peak concentration. Elimination occurs by biliary and urinary excretion.

Administration
  General Information
  For storage information, see the specific product information within the How Supplied section.
 
NOTE: Image patients with fluoroestradiol F 18 before starting systemic endocrine therapies that target estrogen receptors (e.g., estrogen receptor modulators and estrogen receptor down-regulators).
NOTE: Ensure the patient is adequately hydrated by encouraging patient hydration before and after fluoroestradiol F 18 administration. In addition, encourage frequent voiding during the first hours after fluoroestradiol F 18 administration.
NOTE: Fluoroestradiol F 18 is a radioactive material. To ensure minimum radiation exposure to occupational workers and to minimize radiation exposure to patients, observe appropriate precautions that are consistent with proper patient management. Radiopharmaceuticals should only be used by nuclear physicians and/or radiopharmacists who are qualified by training and experience in the safe use and handling of radioactive material, and whose experience and training have been approved by the appropriate governmental agency authorized to license the use of radiopharmaceuticals.
