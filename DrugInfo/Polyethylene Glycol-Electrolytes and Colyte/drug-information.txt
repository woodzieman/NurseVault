Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  General Information
  Polyethylene glycol; electrolytes solution is contraindicated for use in those with known polyethylene glycol hypersensitivity or with hypersensitivity to any of the components of the specific commercial products.
 
Polyethylene glycol; electrolytes solution is contraindicated for use in patients with known or suspected GI obstruction or ileus, GI perforation, gastric retention, toxic colitis, or toxic megacolon. Patients with symptoms suggestive of bowel obstruction (e.g., acute abdomen or symptoms of appendicitis, abdominal pain, distension, nausea, or vomiting) should be evaluated prior to initiating bowel evacuation. Use polyethylene glycol; electrolytes solution with caution in the presence of severe ulcerative colitis or other conditions where there may be compromised integrity of the bowel wall (e.g., diverticulitis). The rapidity of administration of the PEG; electrolytes solution may lead to very rare instances of bowel perforation in such patients. Administration of PEG; electrolytes solution should be slowed or temporarily discontinued if the patient experiences severe bloating, distention, or abdominal pain.
 
There have been rare reports of generalized tonic-clonic seizures associated with use of polyethylene glycol colon preparation products in patients with no prior history of seizure disorder. The seizure cases were associated with electrolyte abnormalities (e.g., hyponatremia, hypokalemia). The neurologic abnormalities resolved with correction of fluid and electrolyte abnormalities. Therefore, polyethylene glycol; electrolytes preparations should be used with caution in patients using concomitant medications that increase the risk of electrolyte abnormalities (see Drug Interactions) or in patients with known or suspected hyponatremia. Consider performing baseline and post-colonoscopy laboratory tests (sodium, potassium, calcium, creatinine, and BUN) in these patients.
 
Moviprep® brand polyethylene glycol; electrolytes solution, contains phenylalanine (a maximum of 2.33 mg of phenylalanine per treatment). Moviprep® should be avoided in patients who have phenylketonuria or who must restrict intake of phenylalanine.
 
Since Moviprep® brand polyethylene glycol; electrolytes solution contains sodium ascorbate and ascorbic acid, Moviprep® should be used with caution in patients with G6PD deficiency (glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase deficiency) especially G6PD deficiency patients with an active infection, with a history of hemolysis, or taking concomitant medications known to precipitate hemolytic reactions.
 
In children and infants = 60 years old (see Adverse Reactions).

    Headline
    decreased gastrointestinal wall integrity, GI obstruction, inflammatory bowel disease, intestinal perforation, toxic megacolon
    General Information
    Polyethylene glycol; electrolytes solution is contraindicated for use in patients with known or suspected GI obstruction or ileus, bowel or intestinal perforation, gastric retention, toxic colitis or toxic megacolon. Patients with symptoms suggestive of bowel obstruction should be evaluated prior to initiating bowel evacuation. Use with caution in patients with inflammatory bowel disease or other conditions where there may be decreased gastrointestinal wall integrity. Administration of osmotic laxative products may produce colonic mucosal aphthous ulcerations, and there have been reports of more serious cases of ischemic colitis requiring hospitalization. Concurrent use of stimulant laxatives may increase this risk. Consider the potential for mucosal ulcerations resulting from this bowel preparation when interpreting colonoscopy findings in patients with known or suspected inflammatory bowel disease. If gastrointestinal obstruction or perforation is suspected, perform appropriate diagnostic studies to rule out these conditions before administering polyethylene glycol; electrolytes solution. Especially use with caution in patients with severe active ulcerative colitis.
    Headline
    electrolyte imbalance, seizures
    General Information
    People with electrolyte imbalance should have them corrected prior to bowel preparation. There have been rare reports of generalized tonic-clonic seizures associated with use of polyethylene glycol colon preparation products in patients with no prior history of seizure disorder. The seizures were associated with electrolyte imbalance and low serum osmolality. The neurologic abnormalities resolved with correction of fluid and electrolyte abnormalities. Use polyethylene glycol; electrolytes preparations with caution in people with a history of seizures, a seizure disorder, that have conditions that may lower the seizure threshold or that are associated with an increased seizure risk (e.g., withdrawing from alcohol or benzodiazepines, taking medications that lower the seizure threshold or that may produce electrolyte abnormalities, or in people with known or suspected hyponatremia). Consider performing baseline and post-colonoscopy laboratory tests (sodium, potassium, calcium, creatinine, and BUN) in these patients.
    Headline
    cardiac arrhythmias, cardiomyopathy, heart failure, myocardial infarction, QT prolongation, unstable angina
    General Information
    Consider performing baseline and post-colonoscopy ECGs in people at increased risk of serious cardiac arrhythmias. There have been rare reports of serious arrhythmias associated with the use of ionic osmotic laxative products for bowel preparation. Use polyethylene glycol; electrolytes products with caution in people at increased risk of arrhythmias.(e.g., history of QT prolongation, uncontrolled cardiac arrhythmias, recent history of myocardial infarction, unstable angina, congestive heart failure, cardiomyopathy).
    Headline
    renal failure, renal impairment
    General Information
    Bowel preparations containing polyethylene glycol; electrolytes should be used with caution in patients with renal impairment, renal failure or in patients on concomitant medications that may affect renal function. Because such patients may be at an increased risk of developing electrolyte abnormalities associated with bowel preparation, advise on the importance of adequate hydration. In addition, consider performing baseline and post-colonoscopy laboratory tests (sodium, potassium, calcium, creatinine, and BUN) in these patients.
    Headline
    aspiration, dysphagia, gag reflex depression
    General Information
    Use polyethylene glycol; electrolytes products with caution in people with gag reflex depression, dysphagia, in unconscious, or semiconscious patients and other individuals prone to regurgitation or aspiration (e.g., gastroesophageal reflux disease). Observe these patients during administration of polyethylene glycol; electrolytes solution, especially if it is administered via nasogastric tube. Do not combine polyethylene glycol; electrolytes solution with starch-based thickeners. When polyethylene glycol (PEG) is mixed with starch-thickened liquids, it reduces the viscosity of the starch-thickened liquid. When a PEG-based product used for another indication was mixed in starch-based pre-thickened liquids used in patients with dysphagia, thinning of the liquid occurred and cases of choking and potential aspiration were reported.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    There are no data on the use of polyethylene glycol; electrolytes during pregnancy to evaluate for a drug-associated risk of fetal harm. Animal studies have not been done. Polyethylene glycol; electrolytes is not significantly absorbed from the GI tract. When use is absolutely necessary, evidence appears to favor polyethylene glycol; electrolyte solutions as preferred agents versus sodium phosphate (which might have a higher association with fluid and electrolyte abnormalities) when complete bowel evacuation is necessary during pregnancy.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Polyethylene glycol; electrolytes is compatible with breast-feeding. The safest bowel evacuants for use during breast-feeding are those that are not systemically absorbed. Polyethylene glycol 3350 is a common food additive and is not significantly absorbed from the GI tract. Only 0.2% of an oral dose would be absorbed systemically by the lactating individual and significant passage to the milk seems unlikely. Consensus documents indicate that polyethylene glycol-containing bowel preparations represent a low risk when used during breast-feeding.

Description
  Text
  Polyethylene glycol (PEG) and electrolytes are used together primarily for bowel evacuation and cleaning before GI examination. PEG 3350-electrolytes solutions have been used medicinally for decades. Polyethylene glycol compounds exhibit practically no systemic absorption from the intestine at molecular masses exceeding 3000, and thus act as osmotic laxatives. Electrolytes are added to the solution to maintain water and electrolyte balance in most patients, even after administration of large volumes. NuLytely, when compared to GoLytely or CoLyte, contains 52% less sodium and no sodium sulfate; the formula may be more palatable for some patients. Other than this consideration, the products are interchangeable for therapeutic use.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Mechanism of Action: Polyethylene glycol; electrolytes solution contains polyethylene glycol 3350 (PEG 3350), an osmotic agent that binds water and causes water to be retained within the stool. The lack of intestinal enzymatic degradation or bacterial metabolism of PEG 3350 contributes to the drug's effectiveness; tachyphylaxis does not normally occur with intermittent use. PEG 3350 softens stool consistency, increases stool frequency, and facilitates stool evacuation. PEG 3350 does not affect colonic transit time. A balance of electrolytes (e.g., sodium, chloride, potassium and bicarbonate) are added to the solution to counteract losses resulting from bowel evacuation. The concentration of electrolytes in the polyethylene glycol; electrolytes solution causes no net absorption or secretion of electrolytes; thus water and electrolyte balance are usually maintained even after administration of large volumes.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Oral Route
    Description
    After oral administration, the dosage essentially remains within the GI tract. Complete recovery of an administered dose occurs. Roughly 0.2% of a dose has been absorbed systemically in some studies of PEG 3350. PEG 3350 is not fermented within the GI tract by the colonic microflora and no metabolism occurs locally.
  Text
  Polyethylene glycol; electrolytes solution is administered orally as a solution in water. Any systemically absorbed dose is quickly excreted in the urine.

Administration
  General Information
  For storage information, see specific product information within the How Supplied section.
  Route Specific Administrations
    Name
    Oral Administration
    Description
    For oral use only. The first bowel movement should occur approximately 1 hour after the start of solution administration.
Patients should follow fasting instructions and instructions for clear liquids as their practitioner prescribes.
When used as a bowel prep, various regimens have been used. Administration regimens may vary by the specific product formulation. Follow individual product labeling for proper administration technique.
For example, some products may be split-dosed and administered partly on the evening prior and then partly on the morning of the procedure.
Other formulations must be administered as a full one-time dose. In this case, one method is to schedule patients for the exam midmorning or later, allowing the patients 3 hours for drinking the full dose and an additional 1-hour period for complete bowel evacuation.
Another method is to give the solution the evening before the examination, particularly if the patient is to have a barium enema.
Reconstitute the specific product prescribed according to the package instructions with water only. Do not reconstitute with other liquids and/or add starch-based thickeners to the mixing container as this can lead to choking and aspiration in certain patients.
The solution is more palatable if chilled before administration. When reconstituted, keep solution refrigerated. Depending on the product, use within 24 to 48 hours of reconstitution. Discard unused portion.

Dosage Adjustment Guidelines
  Hepatic Impairment
  Specific guidelines for dosage adjustments in hepatic impairment are not available; it appears that no dosage adjustments are needed.
  Renal Impairment
  Specific guidelines for dosage adjustments in renal impairment are not available; it appears that no dosage adjustments are needed. However, due to an increased risk of developing electrolyte abnormalities and serious adverse events use caution when prescribing polyethylene glycol; electrolytes to patients with renal impairment (see Contraindications/Precautions).
