Description
  Text
  NOTE: All products associated with this monograph are off-market. The content is retained for historical reference only and is no longer actively maintained.
 
Sodium thiosulfate 25% and salicylic acid 1% are used together in a prescription topical preparation for the treatment of tinea versicolor. Tinea versicolor is a chronic fungal infection of the skin caused by Malassezia furfur. Sodium thiosulfate used topically retards the growth of fungus. Salicylic acid is added for its skin penetrating and keratolyic action. Sulfur and salicylic acid topical preparations have long been included as potential alternatives for the topical treatment of the disease. Most patients are treated with topical agents alone. While sodium thiosulfate; salicylic acid is effective, recurrence is common after treatment. Treatments of choice usually include selenium sulfide lotions or shampoos, azole antifungal topical preparations, or terbinafine topical preparations. Oral antifungal regimens may lead to a lower recurrence rate but are usually reserved for patients with extensive disease or recurrent infections or infections failing topical treatments.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  NOTE: All products associated with this monograph are off-market. The content is retained for historical reference only and is no longer actively maintained.
 
Sodium thiosulfate and salicylic acid are used together to treat tinea versicolor, caused by Malassezia furfur. Sodium thiosulfate is a topical antifungal agent with fungistatic properties. Sodium thiosulfate only keeps the fungus from growing and spreading. Topical salicylic acid at concentrations of 1% and higher exhibits keratolytic action by causing desquamation of the horny layer of skin; this action allows the sodium thiosulfate to penetrate the skin and also hastens skin turnover to help erradicate the dermal layers in which the fungus exists. Salicylic acid also has weak antifungal and antibacterial activity.

    Description
    Both agents, sodium thiosulfate and salicylic acid, are known to exhibit some degree of percutaneous absorption; however systemic exposure of these agents is considered negligible with normal prescription use.       Name
      Cream/Ointment/Lotion Formulations
      Description
      For external use only. Do not use on inflamed, broken, burned or infected skin. Avoid contact with the eyes or mucus membranes.
Shake lotion well before use.
Thoroughly wash, rinse and dry the affected areas before application.
Apply a thin film to the affected area and other susceptible areas as directed.
Continue administration for the duration of treatment recommended by the prescriber, even if the condition has improved.
