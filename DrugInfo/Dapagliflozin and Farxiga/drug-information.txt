  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  Contraindications And Precautions
    Headline
    Drug Interactions
    General Information
    The coadministration of certain medications may lead to harm and require avoidance or therapy modification; review all drug interactions prior to concomitant use of other medications.
    Headline
    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components.
    Headline
    alcohol use disorder, fever, hypovolemia, ketogenic diet, pancreatitis, surgery
    General Information
    Withhold dapagliflozin, if possible, in temporary clinical situations that could predispose patients to ketoacidosis such as underinsulinization due to insulin dose reduction or missed insulin doses, acute fever, reduced caloric intake, ketogenic diet, surgery, hypovolemia, alcohol use disorder, and pancreatitis. For patients who undergo scheduled surgery, dapagliflozin should be temporarily discontinued at least 3 days before surgery. Resume dapagliflozin when the patient is clinically stable and has resumed oral intake. Reports of ketoacidosis, a serious, life-threatening condition requiring urgent hospitalization, have been identified in postmarketing surveillance of people with type 1 and type 2 diabetes mellitus receiving sodium glucose co-transporter-2 (SGLT2) inhibitors, including dapagliflozin. Fatal cases of ketoacidosis have been reported. The risk of ketoacidosis may be greater with higher SGLT2 doses. Consider ketone monitoring in people at risk for ketoacidosis if indicated by the clinical situation.
    Headline
    Treatment-Related Restrictions of Use
    General Information
    Dapagliflozin is not indicated to improve glycemic control of people with type 1 diabetes mellitus and should not be used for the treatment of diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA). Dapagliflozin significantly increases the risk of diabetic ketoacidosis in patients with type 1 diabetes.
Dapagliflozin is not recommended for the treatment of chronic kidney disease in patients with polycystic kidney disease (i.e., autosomal dominant polycystic kidney disease) or patients requiring or with a recent history of immunosuppressive therapy for kidney disease; dapagliflozin is not expected to be effective in these populations.
    Headline
    renal failure, renal impairment
    General Information
    Before the initiation of dapagliflozin treatment and periodically during therapy, obtain an estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) to assess renal function. Use of dapagliflozin is not recommended in patients being treated for type 2 diabetes mellitus without established CV disease or multiple CV risk factors if eGFR is below 45 mL/minute/1.73 m2. However, guidelines recommend use of dapagliflozin in all patients with an eGFR 20 mL/minute/1.73 m2 or greater, T2DM, and CKD for CV and renal protection, regardless of glycemia or presence of albuminuria. The guidelines further state that while dapagliflozin should not be initiated in these patients if the eGFR is less than 20 mL/minute/1.73 m2, it may be continued if tolerated for CV and renal protection until dialysis is initiated. In people with type 2 diabetes mellitus, the pharmacodynamic response to dapagliflozin declines with increasing severity of renal impairment. In patients being treated for other indications (i.e., heart failure, chronic kidney disease) with eGFR less than 25 mL/minute/1.73 m2, initiation of dapagliflozin is not recommended; however, patients may continue on therapy to reduce the risk of eGFR decline, ESRD, CV death, and hospitalization for heart failure. The impact of renal failure or dialysis on dapagliflozin is unknown. Dapagliflozin causes intravascular volume depletion; renal function abnormalities can occur. Initiation of SGLT2 inhibitors, including dapagliflozin, causes a small increase in serum creatinine and decrease in eGFR. In patients with normal or mildly impaired renal function at baseline, these changes in serum creatinine and eGFR generally occur within weeks of starting therapy and then stabilize. The acute effect on eGFR reverses after treatment discontinuation, suggesting acute hemodynamic changes play a role in the renal function changes observed with dapagliflozin. Increases that do not fit this pattern should prompt further evaluation to exclude the possibility of acute kidney injury. According to guidelines, randomized clinical outcome trials of advanced kidney disease or high cardiovascular disease risk with normal kidney function have not shown that SGLT2 inhibitors promote acute kidney injury. Despite these findings, the manufacturer recommends that before initiating dapagliflozin, consider factors that may predispose patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus to acute kidney injury before initiating dapagliflozin, including hypovolemia; chronic renal insufficiency; older adult age; congestive heart failure; pre-existing renal disease; and concomitant medications such as diuretics, ACE inhibitors, angiotensin II receptor blockers (ARBs), and NSAIDs. Consider temporarily discontinuing dapagliflozin in any setting of reduced oral intake such as acute illness or fasting, or with fluid loss such as gastrointestinal illness or excessive heat exposure. Monitor patients for signs and symptoms of acute kidney injury. If acute kidney injury occurs, discontinue dapagliflozin promptly and institute treatment.
    Headline
    geriatric
    General Information
    The Beers Criteria states that SGLT2 inhibitors should be used with caution in geriatric adults; monitor for urogenital infections and ketoacidosis. Older adults may be at increased risk of urogenital infections, particularly women in the first month of treatment. An increased risk of euglycemic diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA) has also been seen in older adults. In clinical trials for treatment of type 2 diabetes mellitus, and after controlling for level of renal function (eGFR), efficacy of dapagliflozin was similar for older and younger adults. However, the incidence of hypotension was higher in geriatric adults aged 65 years and older. In clinical trials assessing dapagliflozin for heart failure and chronic kidney disease, safety and efficacy were similar between older and younger adults.
    Headline
    genitourinary tract infection
    General Information
    People with a history of chronic or recurrent genitourinary tract infection are more likely to develop genitourinary infections when using dapagliflozin or other sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 (SGLT2) inhibitors. Monitor treated individuals for signs and symptoms of genitourinary infections (e.g., urinary tract infection, genital mycotic infection), and treat promptly, if indicated. Serious genitourinary infections, including urosepsis, pyelonephritis, and necrotizing fasciitis of the perineum (Fournier's gangrene), have occurred in individuals receiving SGLT-2 inhibitors. Some cases have resulted in hospitalization, multiple surgical procedures, or death. If Fournier's gangrene is suspected, discontinue the SGLT-2 inhibitor and promptly institute appropriate medical and/or surgical intervention.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    There are no adequate and well-controlled studies of dapagliflozin during human pregnancy. During pregnancy, consider appropriate alternative therapies, especially during the second and third trimesters, when the potential risks to human kidney development are of concern. When dapagliflozin was administered to juvenile rats during periods of animal development that correspond to the late second and third trimester of human development, increased incidence and/or severity of renal pelvic and tubular dilatation were evident at the lowest tested dose which was approximately 15 times human clinical exposure from a 10 mg dose. When dapagliflozin was studied in rabbits during intervals coinciding with the first trimester period of organogenesis in humans, no developmental toxicities were observed at any dose tested. The American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists (ACOG) and the American Diabetes Association (ADA) continue to recommend human insulin as the standard of care in pregnant individuals with diabetes mellitus or gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM) requiring medical therapy; insulin does not cross the placenta.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Avoid dapagliflozin while breast-feeding due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in the breastfed infant. There is no information regarding the presence of dapagliflozin in human milk, the effects on breastfed infants, or the effects on milk production. Dapagliflozin is an uncharged molecule that is 91% protein bound in plasma, so it is unlikely to pass into the breast milk in important amounts. However, there is concern for risk to the developing human kidney with dapagliflozin exposure.  Alternatives, such as insulin, metformin, gluburide, glipizide, and acarbose may be considered. If dapagliflozin is discontinued and blood glucose is not controlled on diet and exercise alone, insulin therapy should be considered. Other oral hypoglycemics may also be considered. Metformin monotherapy may be appropriate for some patients as available studies indicate low excretion in milk and that maternal use during breast-feeding is not expected to result in side effects to a healthy nursing infant. Some experts recommend using metformin with caution if the patient is breastfeeding a newborn or a premature neonate with reduced renal function. Because acarbose has limited systemic absorption, which results in minimal maternal plasma concentrations, clinically significant exposure via breast milk is not expected; therefore, this agent may be an alternative if postprandial glucose control is needed. Glyburide may be a suitable alternative since it was not detected in the breast milk of lactating women who received single and multiple doses of glyburide. If any oral hypoglycemics are used during breast-feeding, the breastfed infant should be monitored for signs of hypoglycemia, such as increased fussiness or somnolence.

Acebutolol: (Moderate) Increased frequency of blood glucose monitoring may be required when a beta blocker is given with antidiabetic agents. Since beta blockers inhibit the release of catecholamines, these medications may hide symptoms of hypoglycemia such as tremor, tachycardia, and blood pressure changes. Other symptoms, like headache, dizziness, nervousness, mood changes, or hunger are not blunted. Beta-blockers also exert complex actions on the body's ability to regulate blood glucose. Some beta-blockers, particularly non-selective beta-blockers such as propranolol, have been noted to potentiate insulin-induced hypoglycemia and a delay in recovery of blood glucose to normal levels. Hyperglycemia has been reported as well and is possibly due to beta-2 receptor blockade in the beta cells of the pancreas. A selective beta-blocker may be preferred in patients with diabetes mellitus, if appropriate for the patient's condition. Selective beta-blockers, such as atenolol or metoprolol, do not appear to potentiate insulin-induced hypoglycemia. While beta-blockers may have negative effects on glycemic control, they reduce the risk of cardiovascular disease and stroke in patients with diabetes and their use should not be avoided in patients with compelling indications for beta-blocker therapy when no other contraindications are present.

Atenolol: (Moderate) Increased frequency of blood glucose monitoring may be required when a beta blocker is given with antidiabetic agents. Since beta blockers inhibit the release of catecholamines, these medications may hide symptoms of hypoglycemia such as tremor, tachycardia, and blood pressure changes. Other symptoms, like headache, dizziness, nervousness, mood changes, or hunger are not blunted. Beta-blockers also exert complex actions on the body's ability to regulate blood glucose. Some beta-blockers, particularly non-selective beta-blockers such as propranolol, have been noted to potentiate insulin-induced hypoglycemia and a delay in recovery of blood glucose to normal levels. Hyperglycemia has been reported as well and is possibly due to beta-2 receptor blockade in the beta cells of the pancreas. A selective beta-blocker may be preferred in patients with diabetes mellitus, if appropriate for the patient's condition. Selective beta-blockers, such as atenolol or metoprolol, do not appear to potentiate insulin-induced hypoglycemia. While beta-blockers may have negative effects on glycemic control, they reduce the risk of cardiovascular disease and stroke in patients with diabetes and their use should not be avoided in patients with compelling indications for beta-blocker therapy when no other contraindications are present.

Garlic, Allium sativum: (Moderate) Patients receiving antidiabetic agents should use dietary supplements of Garlic, Allium sativum with caution. Constituents in garlic might have some antidiabetic activity, and may increase serum insulin levels and increase glycogen storage in the liver. Monitor blood glucose and glycemic control. Patients with diabetes should inform their health care professionals of their intent to ingest garlic dietary supplements. Some patients may require adjustment to their hypoglycemic medications over time. One study stated that additional garlic supplementation (0.05 to 1.5 grams PO per day) contributed to improved blood glucose control in patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus within 1 to 2 weeks, and had positive effects on total cholesterol and high/low density lipoprotein regulation over time. It is unclear if hemoglobin A1C is improved or if improvements are sustained with continued treatment beyond 24 weeks. Other reviews suggest that garlic may provide modest improvements in blood lipids, but few studies demonstrate decreases in blood glucose in diabetic and non-diabetic patients. More controlled trials are needed to discern if garlic has an effect on blood glucose in patients with diabetes. When garlic is used in foods or as a seasoning, or at doses of 50 mg/day or less, it is unlikely that blood glucose levels are affected to any clinically significant degree.

Labetalol: (Moderate) Increased frequency of blood glucose monitoring may be required when a beta blocker is given with antidiabetic agents. Since beta blockers inhibit the release of catecholamines, these medications may hide symptoms of hypoglycemia such as tremor, tachycardia, and blood pressure changes. Other symptoms, like headache, dizziness, nervousness, mood changes, or hunger are not blunted. Beta-blockers also exert complex actions on the body's ability to regulate blood glucose. Some beta-blockers, particularly non-selective beta-blockers such as propranolol, have been noted to potentiate insulin-induced hypoglycemia and a delay in recovery of blood glucose to normal levels. Hyperglycemia has been reported as well and is possibly due to beta-2 receptor blockade in the beta cells of the pancreas. A selective beta-blocker may be preferred in patients with diabetes mellitus, if appropriate for the patient's condition. Selective beta-blockers, such as atenolol or metoprolol, do not appear to potentiate insulin-induced hypoglycemia. While beta-blockers may have negative effects on glycemic control, they reduce the risk of cardiovascular disease and stroke in patients with diabetes and their use should not be avoided in patients with compelling indications for beta-blocker therapy when no other contraindications are present.

Metoprolol: (Moderate) Increased frequency of blood glucose monitoring may be required when a beta blocker is given with antidiabetic agents. Since beta blockers inhibit the release of catecholamines, these medications may hide symptoms of hypoglycemia such as tremor, tachycardia, and blood pressure changes. Other symptoms, like headache, dizziness, nervousness, mood changes, or hunger are not blunted. Beta-blockers also exert complex actions on the body's ability to regulate blood glucose. Some beta-blockers, particularly non-selective beta-blockers such as propranolol, have been noted to potentiate insulin-induced hypoglycemia and a delay in recovery of blood glucose to normal levels. Hyperglycemia has been reported as well and is possibly due to beta-2 receptor blockade in the beta cells of the pancreas. A selective beta-blocker may be preferred in patients with diabetes mellitus, if appropriate for the patient's condition. Selective beta-blockers, such as atenolol or metoprolol, do not appear to potentiate insulin-induced hypoglycemia. While beta-blockers may have negative effects on glycemic control, they reduce the risk of cardiovascular disease and stroke in patients with diabetes and their use should not be avoided in patients with compelling indications for beta-blocker therapy when no other contraindications are present.

    General Information

Description
  Text
  Dapagliflozin is an oral sodium-glucose cotransporter 2 (SGLT2) inhibitor indicated as an adjunct to diet and exercise to improve glycemic control in adults and pediatric individuals 10 years of age and older with type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM); to reduce the risk of hospitalization for heart failure (HF) in adults with T2DM and either established cardiovascular (CV) disease or multiple CV risk factors; to reduce the risk of CV death, hospitalization for HF, and urgent HF visits in adults with HF; and to reduce the risk of sustained estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) decline, end-stage kidney disease (ESKD), CV death, and hospitalization for HF in adults with chronic kidney disease (CKD) at risk of progression. Dapagliflozin is not recommended or expected to be effective for improving glycemic control in adults with T2DM and eGFR less than 45 mL/minute/1.73 m2 or for treating CKD in individuals with polycystic kidney disease or in those requiring/recent history of immunosuppressive therapy for the treatment of chronic kidney disease (CKD). Dapagliflozin was superior to placebo in reducing the incidence of the primary composite endpoint of hospitalization for HF or CV death (HR 0.83 [95% CI 0.73, 0.95]), which reflected a lower rate of hospitalization for HF in subjects randomized to dapagliflozin (HR 0.73 [95% CI 0.61, 0.88]), with no change in the risk of CV death. In the DAPA-HF trial, dapagliflozin was superior to placebo in reducing the primary composite endpoint of hospitalization for HF, urgent HF visit, or CV death (HR 0.74 [95% CI 0.65, 0.85]). The DAPA-CKD trial (n = 4,304) in adults with (68%) or without type 2 DM and eGFR of 25 to 75 mL/minute/1.73 m2 and elevated urinary albumin excretion (urine albumin creatinine ratio of 200 to 5,000 mg/g) was stopped early due to overwhelming efficacy; dapagliflozin added to background therapy reduced the primary composite outcome of sustained decline in eGFR of at least 50%, ESKD, or death from renal or CV causes (HR 0.56 [95% CI 0.45, 0.68] p less than 0.001). Dapagliflozin also reduced the incidence of the composite endpoint of CV death or hospitalization for HF (HR 0.71 [95% CI 0.55, 0.92] p less than 0.009). First-line T2DM therapy depends on comorbidities, individual-centered treatment factors, and management needs. In adults with T2DM and established atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD) or indicators of high ASCVD risk, a glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonist (GLP-1 RA) and/or SGLT2 inhibitor with proven CV benefit (e.g., canagliflozin, empagliflozin) should be initiated as a first-line therapy independent of A1C goal or other antihyperglycemic treatments, including metformin. In adults with T2DM and HF, an SGLT2 inhibitor with proven benefit (e.g., canagliflozin, dapagliflozin, empagliflozin, and ertugliflozin) should be initiated as first-line therapy to reduce hospitalizations for HF. In adults with T2DM and CKD, an SGLT2 inhibitor with primary evidence (e.g., canagliflozin, dapagliflozin, empagliflozin) of reducing CKD progression should be initiated as first-line therapy; however, the glycemic benefits of SGLT2 inhibitors are reduced at eGFR less than 45 mL/minute per 1.73 m2. In individuals with T2DM who do not have ASCVD/indicators of high-risk, HF, or CKD and who need to minimize hypoglycemia and/or promote weight loss, SGLT2 inhibitors are generally recommended as a second or third-line option as add-on to metformin therapy, as they have a high efficacy for glucose lowering and an intermediate efficacy for weight loss. Dapagliflozin was initially FDA-approved in 2014.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Dapagliflozin is an inhibitor of sodium-glucose co-transporter 2 (SGLT2), the transporter responsible for reabsorbing the majority of glucose filtered by the tubular lumen in the kidney. SGLT2 is expressed in the proximal renal tubules. By inhibiting SGLT2, dapagliflozin reduces the reabsorption of filtered glucose and lowers the renal threshold for glucose, and thereby promotes urinary glucose excretion, improving blood glucose control. In adults with type 2 diabetes mellitus, dapagliflozin 10 mg/day for 12 weeks resulted in excretion of approximately 70 g of glucose in the urine per day at week 12. A near maximum glucose excretion was observed at the dapagliflozin dose of 20 mg/day. This urinary glucose excretion with dapagliflozin also results in increased urinary volume.
 
Through inhibition of glucose and sodium reabsorption in the proximal renal tubule, SGLT2 inhibitors exert osmotic diuretic and natriuretic effects causing plasma volume contraction and reducing both systolic and diastolic blood pressure. Though the exact mechanism for the beneficial cardiorenal effects seen in clinical studies of SGLT2 inhibitor therapy is not fully known, it is believed to be, in part, related to glycosuria and natriuresis; these effects lead to uricosuria and reduction in plasma uric acid, and a reduction in plasma volume, lowering of cardiac preload, and reduced arterial pressure and stiffness possibly resulting in afterload reduction. Additional proposed mechanisms may involve increased circulating concentrations of beta-hydroxybutyrate, a ketone body and an improved source of adenosine triphosphate (ATP), which may result in an increase in cardiac efficiency; increased erythropoietin concentrations and related cardioprotective effects; elevated hemoglobin concentrations leading to improved myocardium oxygenation; inhibition of the myocardial sodium-hydrogen exchanger resulting in increased mitochondrial calcium, reduced oxidative stress, and improved mitochondrial function; potential reduction in sympathetic nervous system activation; enhancing endothelium-dependent vasodilation through increased endothelial nitric oxide synthetase; and decreasing intraglomerular pressure which is believed to be mediated by increased tubuloglomerular feedback.

Overall, dapagliflozin is unlikely to affect the pharmacokinetics of concurrently administered medications that are P-gp, OCT2, OAT1, or OAT3 substrates.

Dosage Adjustment Guidelines
  Hepatic Impairment
  No dosage adjustment is needed in patients with mild, moderate, or severe hepatic impairment. The use of dapagliflozin has not been studied in patients with severe hepatic impairment and therefore the benefit-risk for the use of dapagliflozin in patients with severe hepatic impairment should be individually assessed.
  Renal Impairment
  Treatment of type 2 diabetes mellitus
eGFR 45 mL/minute/1.73 m2 or more: No dosage adjustment needed.
eGFR 25 to 44 mL/minute/1.73 m2: Not recommended for glycemic control; however, guidelines recommend use in all patients with T2DM and CKD for cardiovascular and renal protection regardless of glycemia or presence of albuminuria; once initiated dapagliflozin may be continued at 10 mg PO once daily at lower levels of eGFR if tolerated and until kidney replacement therapy is initiated.
 
eGFR less than 25 mL/minute/1.73 m2: Do not initiate therapy; may continue if eGFR falls after initiation unless not tolerated or kidney replacement therapy is initiated.
 
Other indications (i.e., heart failure, chronic kidney disease)
eGFR 25 mL/minute/1.73 m2 or more: No dosage adjustment needed.
eGFR less than 25 mL/minute/1.73 m2: Initiation is not recommended; however, patients may continue on 10 mg PO once daily to reduce the risk of eGFR decline, ESRD, cardiovascular death, and hospitalization for heart failure.
 
Intermittent hemodialysis
Efficacy and safety studies did not include patients on dialysis. The impact of hemodialysis on dapagliflozin is not known.
