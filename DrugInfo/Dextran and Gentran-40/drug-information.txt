Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  General Information
  The possibility of circulatory overload is one of the major concerns for administration of dextran. These effects are variable with the different molecular weights of dextran preparations. An absolute contraindication is recommended for dextran 70 and 75 in patients with severe heart failure or severe renal failure, while caution should be exercised during use of dextran 40, 70, or 75 in patients with renal disease or cardiac failure. Dextran 40 is also contraindicated in patients with renal disease accompanied by severe oliguria or anuria, and in patients with pulmonary edema.Dextran 70 or 75 can prolong bleeding time through interference with platelet function, and it should be used with caution in patients with thrombocytopenia and not at all in those with severe coagulopathy. Dextran 40 appears to have little effect on bleeding time at the recommended dosage, but it should be used with caution in patients with coagulopathy (e.g., hemophilia), thrombocytopenia or hypofibrinogenemia. Dextran 40 also can increase blood loss because of increased perfusion pressure and improved microcirculatory flow. When patients are dehydrated, adequate fluid intake is essential; renal failure is possible in severe dehydration because of increased urine viscosity and specific gravity.Intramuscular injections should not be administered to patients receiving dextran. IM injections may cause bleeding, bruising, or hematomas due to thrombocytopenia secondary to dextran therapy.Severe anaphylactoid reactions can occur during treatment with dextran 40, 70, or 75, indicating hypersensitivity to dextran. Close observation of the patient is required during the first few minutes of infusion and a determination made as to whether hypotension results from shock or from dextran. Alternative methods for maintaining circulation and for resuscitation should be available.Patients undergoing bowel surgery or who have pathological abdominal conditions should be treated cautiously with dextran 70 or 75 because of possible adverse GI effects.Dextran preparations are classified as pregnancy category C. Full evaluations of safety during pregnancy have not been made. Preparations should be used only when clearly needed.No evaluation has been done on the distribution of dextran products into breast milk. Dextran should be avoided during breast-feeding, and alternative methods of feeding are recommended.Cautious use of dextran is recommended in patients with a dietary sodium restriction; 500 ml of dextran preparations in 0.9% sodium chloride contain 77 mEq of sodium and chloride.

    Headline
    Treatment-Related Restrictions of Use
    General Information
    In individuals with normal hemostasis, dosage of dextran 40 approximating 15 mL/kg of body weight may prolong bleeding time and depress platelet function. Dosages in this range also markedly decrease factor VIII, and decrease factors V and IX to a greater degree than would be expected to occur from hemodilution alone. Since these changes tend to be more pronounced after trauma or major surgery, observe these individuals for early signs of bleeding complications.
    Headline
    acute heart failure, edema, heart failure
    General Information
    Dextran 40 is contraindicated in individuals with marked cardiac decompensation or acute heart failure. Use sodium-containing dextran 40 solutions with great care, if at all, in individuals with heart failure or clinical states in which there exists edema with sodium retention. The intravenous administration of dextran 40 can cause fluid and/or solute overloading resulting in overhydration and congested states or pulmonary edema; the risk of solute overload causing congested states with peripheral and pulmonary edema is directly proportional to the electrolyte concentrations of such solutions.
    Headline
    bleeding, bleeding disorder
    General Information
    Dextran 40 is contraindicated in individuals with marked hemostatic defects of all types (e.g., thrombocytopenia, hypofibrinogenemia, etc.), including those caused by medications (e.g., heparin, warfarin, etc.), or bleeding disorder. Dextran may interfere to some extent with platelet function; therefore, use dextran 40 with caution in cases with thrombocytopenia. Use dextran 40 with caution in individuals with active bleeding as the resulting increase in perfusion pressure and improved microcirculatory flow may result in additional blood loss. Take care to prevent a depression of hematocrit below 30% by volume.
    Headline
    anuria, hypovolemia, renal disease
    General Information
    Dextran 40 is contraindicated in individuals with renal disease with severe oliguria or anuria. Take special care with dextran 40 use in individuals with impaired renal clearance of dextran due to the possibility of circulatory overload. Do not exceed the recommended dextran 40 dose in individuals with advanced renal disease, since excessive doses may precipitate renal failure. Renal excretion of dextran 40 causes elevations of the specific gravity of the urine. In the presence of adequate urine flow, only minor elevation will occur, whereas in individuals with reduced urine output, urine viscosity and specific gravity can be increased markedly. It is recommended, when desired, to assess an individual's state of hydration by determination of urine or serum osmolarity. If hypovolemia is present, administer additional fluid; an osmotic diuretic, such as mannitol, may also be administered to maintain an adequate urine flow. Because dextran 40 is a hypertonic colloid solution, it attracts water from the extravascular space; consider fluid shifts when dextran 40 is used for individuals with hypovolemia where additional fluid therapy will be needed. Use sodium-containing dextran 40 solutions with great care, if at all, in individuals with severe renal insufficiency; administration of solutions containing sodium ions to individuals with diminished renal function may result in sodium retention.
    Headline
    diabetes mellitus
    General Information
    Use dextrose-containing dextran 40 solutions with caution in individuals with known subclinical or overt diabetes mellitus.

Adverse Reactions
  Headline
  Adverse Reactions
  General Information
  Anaphylactoid reactions can occur with any of the dextran preparations. Close observation during the first few minutes of administration is essential. Allergic reactions include urticaria, nasal congestion, wheezing, tightness of the chest, nausea and vomiting, and hypotension, which can be mild or severe. Dextran therapy should be stopped at the first sign of allergic reactions. Symptoms can be alleviated by parenteral epinephrine. Dextran 40 has less potential for causing these adverse reactions.Dextran 40 could cause renal failure. Molecular weight must be under 50,000 for excretion by the kidneys, so this reaction does not apply to dextran 70 or 75. Dextran 40 undergoes rapid urinary excretion, increasing viscosity and specific gravity of urine. Patients with a reduced flow of urine are especially susceptible to renal tubular obstruction. Adequate hydration is essential.Adverse GI effects have been reported from use of dextran 70 or 75 including abdominal pain and nausea/vomiting.Dextran 70 or 75 can cause prolonged bleeding time through interference with platelet function. Dextran 40 appears to have little effect on bleeding time at the recommended dosage, but it should be used with caution in some patients (see Preacuations).
  Adverse Reactions
    Headline
    anaphylactic shock, anaphylactoid reactions, cardiac arrest, hypotension, nasal congestion, urticaria, wheezing
    General Information
    Severe and fatal anaphylactoid reactions/anaphylactic shock (dextran-induced anaphylactoid reactions, DIAR), consisting of cardiac arrest and respiratory arrest, have been reported with IV dextran. Most reaction have occurred in patients not previously exposed to IV dextran during the early infusion period. Other allergic reactions consist of urticaria, nasal congestion, wheezing, tightness of the chest, and hypotension. Close observation during the first few minutes of administration is essential; discontinue therapy at the first sign of allergic reaction. Symptoms can be alleviated with administration of parenteral epinephrine, while other means of treatment for shock are instituted. Dextran 40 has less potential for causing these adverse reactions, compared to other Dextran preparations.

Description
  Text
  Dextran is a branched polysaccharide used intravenously as a plasma volume expander. It is formed by a bacterium, Leuconostoc mesenteroides, which contributes to its antigenicity; however, due to improved preparation techniques, the incidence of hypersensitivity reactions is reduced. Dextran is available in various molecular weights, and these products exhibit different osmotic and pharmacologic properties. Dextran 40 contains molecules of molecular weight (MW) 40,000 daltons while dextran 70 contains molecules of 70,000 daltons. Both types of products contain molecules of varying molecular weights, some lower and some higher than the stated label. Dextran is used clinically as an adjunct in the management or prevention of shock caused by hemorrhage, burns, surgery, or trauma, and in the prophylaxis of venous thrombosis and pulmonary embolism in patients undergoing surgery that carries a high risk of thromboembolic complications (e.g., hip surgery). Dextran was first approved in 1951 as a 6% solution. Dextran 70/75 was approved by the FDA in 1953 and Dextran 40 in 1967.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  The colloidal osmotic effect exerted by dextran draws fluid into the vascular system from the interstitial spaces, resulting in increased circulating blood volume. This increased volume causes an increase in central venous pressure, cardiac output, stroke volume, blood pressure, urinary output, and capillary perfusion, and a decrease in heart rate, peripheral resistance, and blood viscosity. Dextran 70 and 75 both exert osmotic and pharmacologic effects similar to those of albumin. Unlike the higher MW products, dextran 40 not only corrects hypovolemia, but also improves microcirculation independently of its volume-expanding effects. The exact mechanism of this activity is unknown, but it is believed to occur by minimizing erythrocyte aggregation and/or decreasing blood viscosity. Dextran 40 is also believed to coat erythrocytes, which maintains erythrocyte electronegativity and the ability to repel one another, and reduces erythrocyte rigidity to aid in passage through capillaries.

Pharmacokinetics
  Text
  Dextran is administered intravenously. Following a single infusion, circulating blood volume is increased maximally within a few minutes following infusion of dextran 40 and within 1 hour after dextran 70 or 75. Volume expansion can persist for 24 hours or longer. Molecules of the lowest molecular weight contained in dextran 40 are excreted rapidly via the kidney, leaving molecules with higher molecular weight. Molecules that are over molecular weight 50,000 (which includes most of those in dextran 70 and 75) are slowly converted to glucose and subsequently metabolized to carbon dioxide and water. In patients with normal renal function, 70% of a dose of dextran 40 and 50% of dextran 70 or 75 is excreted in the urine within 24 hours.
