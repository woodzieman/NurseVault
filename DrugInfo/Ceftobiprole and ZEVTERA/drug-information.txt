Drug Class: Ceftobiprole is a fifth-generation cephalosporin antibacterial indicated for the treatment of S. aureus bacteremia, including those with right-sided infective endocarditis, acute bacterial skin and skin structure infections, and community-acquired bacterial pneumonia.

Routes: injection (IV/IM/SC); Common adult doses: 2,668 mg/day IV.

Common Side Effects (excluding routine GI upset): anxiety, asterixis, coma, dizziness, encephalopathy, headache, insomnia, irritability, myoclonia, seizures, bronchospasm, dyspnea, wheezing, dysgeusia, cholestasis, elevated hepatic enzymes, hyperbilirubinemia, c. difficile-associated diarrhea, candidiasis, infection, pseudomembranous colitis, superinfection, agranulocytosis, anemia, aplastic anemia, bleeding, leukopenia, lymphopenia, neutropenia, prolonged bleeding time

In an Overdose: No overdose/antidote information is listed in this file.

Key Nursing Considerations: Contraindications/precautions: This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components. Ceftobiprole is contraindicated in individuals with a known history of severe hypersensitivity to ceftobiprole or other cephalosporins.

    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    There are no available data with ceftobiprole use in pregnancy to evaluate for a drug-associated risk of major birth defects, miscarriage, or adverse maternal or fetal outcomes. Data over several decades with cephalosporin use during pregnancy have not established drug-associated risks of major birth defects, miscarriage, or adverse maternal or fetal outcomes. Intravenous administration of ceftobiprole to pregnant rats and monkeys during organogenesis at doses approximately 1.4 and 0.9 times, respectively, the maximum recommended human dose (MRHD) showed no evidence of adverse fetal developmental outcomes. Some evidence of maternal toxicity, including a slight reduction in body weight and food consumption, was noted in pregnant monkeys at 0.9 times the MRHD; this dose was associated with a slight increase in abortion rate (approximately 10% above historical controls). Intravenous administration of ceftobiprole medocaril to rats 2 weeks prior to mating, during organogenesis, and through lactation resulted in no adverse fertility or fetal development effects in offspring at doses approximately 1.4 times the MRHD. Maternal toxicity, including mortality, reduced food consumption, and body weight gain in surviving females during gestation, was observed at this dose. Effects in the offspring, including a decrease in the number of pups born per litter and decreased postnatal survival up to 4 days after birth, were also observed at this dose during the post-natal pre-weaning period. No adverse effects were observed in surviving pups during the postnatal postweaning development period (days 5 to 22).

Interactions
Sodium picosulfate; Magnesium oxide; Anhydrous citric acid: (Major) Prior or concomitant use of antibiotics with sodium picosulfate; magnesium oxide; anhydrous citric acid may reduce efficacy of the bowel preparation as conversion of sodium picosulfate to its active metabolite bis-(p-hydroxy-phenyl)-pyridyl-2-methane (BHPM) is mediated by colonic bacteria. If possible, avoid coadministration. Certain antibiotics (i.e., tetracyclines and quinolones) may chelate with the magnesium in sodium picosulfate; magnesium oxide; anhydrous citric acid solution. Therefore, these antibiotics should be taken at least 2 hours before and not less than 6 hours after the administration of sodium picosulfate; magnesium oxide; anhydrous citric acid solution.

Description
  Text
  Ceftobiprole is a fifth-generation cephalosporin antibacterial indicated for the treatment of S. aureus bacteremia, including those with right-sided infective endocarditis, acute bacterial skin and skin structure infections, and community-acquired bacterial pneumonia. Ceftobiprole is active against methicillin-susceptible S. aureus (MRSA), methicillin-resistant S. aureus (MRSA), and a number of gram-negative organisms. In adult clinical trials, an increase in mortality was seen in subjects with ventilator-associated bacterial pneumonia (VABP) who were treated with ceftobiprole vs. comparator-treated subjects; the cause of this increased mortality has not been established. The safety and effectiveness of ceftobiprole for the treatment of VABP have not been established and the use of ceftobiprole for VABP is not approved. Ceftobiprole medocaril, the water-soluble prodrug for intravenous administration, is converted to ceftobiprole, the active moiety, upon infusion by type A esterases. Ceftobiprole was FDA-approved in April 2024.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Ceftobiprole is a cephalosporin antibacterial agent with bactericidal action resulting from the inhibition of cell wall biosynthesis mediated through binding to penicillin-binding proteins (PBPs). It has a high affinity for S. aureus PBPs (1 to 4), including PBP2a in methicillin-resistant S. aureus, as well as PBP2x and PBP2b in penicillin-resistant S. pneumoniae.
 
Pharmacodynamics
Beta-lactams, including ceftobiprole, exhibit concentration-independent or time-dependent killing. In vitro and in vivo animal studies have demonstrated that the major pharmacodynamic parameter that determines efficacy for beta-lactams is the amount of time free (non-protein bound) drug concentrations exceed the minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC) of the organism (free T above MIC). This microbiological killing pattern is due to the mechanism of action, which is acylation of PBPs. There is a maximum proportion of PBPs that can be acylated; therefore, once maximum acylation has occurred, killing rates cannot increase. Free beta-lactam concentrations do not have to remain above the MIC for the entire dosing interval. The percentage of time required for both bacteriostatic and maximal bactericidal activity is different for the various classes of beta-lactams. Cephalosporins require free drug concentrations to be above the MIC for 35% to 40% of the dosing interval for bacteriostatic activity and 60% to 70% of the dosing interval for bactericidal activity.
 
Susceptibility
The susceptibility interpretive criteria for ceftobiprole are delineated by pathogen. The breakpoints are based on a dose of 667 mg IV every 6 hours for 8 days and then 667 mg IV every 8 hours for S. aureus bacteremia and endocarditis and 667 mg IV every 8 hours for other indications, including community-acquired pneumonia and skin and skin structure infections. The MICs are defined for S. aureus (MSSA and MRSA) as susceptible at 2 mcg/mL or less, intermediate at 4 mcg/mL, and resistant at 8 mcg/mL or more. The MICs are defined for S. pneumoniae as susceptible at 0.5 mcg/mL or less, intermediate at 1 mcg/mL, and resistant at 2 mcg/mL or more. The MICs are defined for S. pyogenes as susceptible at 0.5 mcg/mL or less. The MICs are defined for Enterobacterales, H. influenzae, and H. parainfluenzae as susceptible at 0.25 mcg/mL or less, intermediate at 0.5 mcg/mL, and resistant at 1 mcg/mL or more.
 
Resistance
Ceftobiprole does not have activity in the presence of extended-spectrum beta-lactamases (ESBLs) from the TEM, SHV, or CTX-M families, serine carbapenemases (such as KPC), class B metallo-beta-lactamases, class C (AmpC) cephalosporinases if expressed at high concentrations, and Ambler class D beta-lactamases including carbapenemases.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Intravenous Route
    Description
    Ceftobiprole exhibits linear and time-independent pharmacokinetics. The Cmax and AUC increase proportionally with the dose over a range of 125 to 1,000 mg (0.25 to 2 times the highest approved recommended adult dose). After multiple doses, the mean Cmax is 33 mcg/mL and the mean AUC is 102 mcg x hours/mL. Ceftobiprole pharmacokinetic parameters were similar after single or multiple doses.

    Name
    Renal Impairment
    Description
    No clinically significant differences in the pharmacokinetics of ceftobiprole were observed in adult subjects with mild renal impairment (CrCl 50 to 80 mL/minute). Ceftobiprole AUC increased 2.5-fold in adults with moderate renal impairment (CrCl 30 to 49 mL/minute) and 3.3-fold in severe renal impairment (CrCl less than 30 mL/minute). No clinically significant differences in the pharmacokinetics of ceftobiprole were observed in adults with end-stage renal disease (CrCl less than 15 mL/minute) compared to healthy adults based on limited pharmacokinetic data as well as pharmacokinetic modeling and simulation. Ceftobiprole is removed by hemodialysis. Pharmacokinetic data in pediatric patients with renal impairment are unavailable; however, similar to adults, use in children 2 to 17 years with renal impairment (eGFR 15 to 49 mL/minute/1.73 m2) is expected to increase ceftobiprole AUC. The impact of any renal impairment in children younger than 2 years or those aged 2 to 17 years with eGFR less than 15 mL/minute/1.73 m2, with or without hemodialysis, on ceftobiprole pharmacokinetics is unknown.

  Text
  Ceftobiprole medocaril, the prodrug of the active moiety ceftobiprole, is administered intravenously. Ceftobiprole is 16% bound to human plasma protein. The distribution of ceftobiprole to epithelial lining fluid (ELF) is described as a mean ELF to unbound plasma concentration ratio of 0.15 to 0.19. The mean Vd at steady-state is 18 L. Ceftobiprole medocaril is rapidly converted by type A esterases to the active drug, ceftobiprole. Ceftobiprole is primarily eliminated renally with 83% excreted as unchanged drug in the urine. It is minimally metabolized. The mean half-life of ceftobiprole in adults is 3.3 hours.
 
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes and drug transporters: BSEP, MRP2, OATP1B1, OATP1B3
Ceftobiprole is an in vitro inhibitor of BSEP, MRP2, OATP1B1, and OATP1B3.

      Reconstitution
Reconstitute the lyophilized powder with the appropriate diluent for the population. The volume of the resulting reconstituted solution is approximately 10.6 mL.

Further dilute the reconstituted solution prior to administration.
Storage: The reconstituted solution may be stored refrigerated for up to 24 hours or at room temperature for up to 1 hour. Discard any unused reconstituted solution.
 
Dilution
Dilute the reconstituted vial with the appropriate diluent to the appropriate volume for the population. Doses are expressed as ceftobiprole medocaril sodium.
Adults: Withdraw 10 mL of the reconstituted solution and add to a 250 mL infusion bag of 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection or 5% Dextrose Injection to yield a concentration of 2.67 mg/mL (667 mg/250 mL).
For adults with renal impairment (CrCl less than 30 mL/minute): Withdraw 5 mL of the reconstituted solution and add to a 125 mL infusion bag of 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection or 5% Dextrose Injection to yield a concentration of 2.67 mg/mL (333 mg/125 mL).
Children and Adolescents 12 to 17 years: Withdraw the appropriate dose and volume based on weight and add to an infusion bag of up to 250 mL of 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection or 5% Dextrose Injection to yield a maximum concentration of 2.67 mg/mL or 667 mg/250 mL.
Infants and Children 3 months to 11 years: Withdraw the appropriate dose and volume based on weight and add to an infusion bag, bottle, or syringe of up to 125 mL of 5% Dextrose Injection to yield a maximum concentration of 5.33 mg/mL or 667 mg/125 mL.
Do not exceed a dose of 267 mg for administration via a 50 mL syringe.
Gently invert the diluted solution 5 to 10 times to form a homogenous solution. Avoid vigorous agitation to prevent foaming.
Storage: Do not expose the diluted solution to direct sunlight. Protect the diluted solution from light when stored under refrigeration. Diluted solutions with a concentration of 2.67 mg/mL that were reconstituted and diluted with 5% Dextrose Injection may be stored for 6 hours at 25 degrees C or 94 hours at 2 to 8 degrees C. Diluted solutions with a concentration of 2.67 mg/mL that were reconstituted with 5% Dextrose Injection and diluted with 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection may be stored for 4 hours at 25 degrees C or 24 hours at 2 to 8 degrees C. Diluted solutions with a concentration of 2.67 mg/mL that were reconstituted with Sterile Water for Injection and diluted with 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection or 5% Dextrose Injection may be stored for 6 hours at 25 degrees C or 94 hours at 2 to 8 degrees C. Diluted solutions with a concentration of 5.33 mg/mL that were reconstituted and diluted with 5% Dextrose Injection may be stored for 6 hours at 25 degrees C or 24 hours at 2 to 8 degrees C. Do not freeze.
 
Intravenous Infusion
Allow diluted solutions stored in the refrigerator to equilibrate to room temperature prior to administration.
Do not expose the diluted solution to direct sunlight; however, the infusion solution does not need to be protected from light during administration.
Administer by IV infusion over 2 hours.

Dosage Adjustment Guidelines
  Hepatic Impairment
  Specific guidelines for dosage adjustments in hepatic impairment are not available; it appears that no dosage adjustments are needed.
  Renal Impairment
  NOTE: Doses are expressed as ceftobiprole medocaril sodium; 667 mg of ceftobiprole medocaril sodium, a prodrug, is equivalent to 500 mg of ceftobiprole.
Adults
CrCl more than 150 mL/minute: 667 mg IV every 6 hours for all indications (augmented renal clearance).
CrCl 50 to 150 mL/minute: For bacteremia and endocarditis, 667 mg IV every 6 hours for days 1 to 8, then 667 mg IV every 8 hours starting on day 9 (no dosage adjustment necessary). For other indications, 667 mg IV every 8 hours (no dosage adjustment necessary).
CrCl 30 to 49 mL/minute: For bacteremia and endocarditis, 667 mg IV every 8 hours for days 1 to 8, then 667 mg IV every 12 hours starting on day 9. For other indications, 667 mg IV every 12 hours.
CrCl 15 to 29 mL/minute: For bacteremia and endocarditis, 333 mg IV every 8 hours for days 1 to 8, then 333 mg IV every 12 hours starting on day 9. For other indications, 333 mg IV every 12 hours.
CrCl less than 15 mL/minute: 333 mg IV every 24 hours for all indications.
 
Children and Adolescents 12 to 17 years
eGFR 30 to 49 mL/minute/1.73 m2: 10 mg/kg/dose (Max: 667 mg/dose) IV every 12 hours.
eGFR 15 to 29 mL/minute/1.73 m2: 10 mg/kg/dose (Max: 333 mg/dose) IV every 12 hours.
eGFR less than 15 mL/minute/1.73 m2: There is insufficient information to recommend dosage adjustments.
 
Children 6 to 11 years
eGFR 30 to 49 mL/minute/1.73 m2: 10 mg/kg/dose (Max: 667 mg/dose) IV every 12 hours.
eGFR 15 to 29 mL/minute/1.73 m2: 10 mg/kg/dose (Max: 333 mg/dose) IV every 24 hours.
eGFR less than 15 mL/minute/1.73 m2: There is insufficient information to recommend dosage adjustments.
 
Children 2 to 5 years
eGFR 30 to 49 mL/minute/1.73 m2: 13.3 mg/kg/dose (Max: 667 mg/dose) IV every 12 hours.
eGFR 15 to 29 mL/minute/1.73 m2: 13.3 mg/kg/dose (Max: 333 mg/dose) IV every 24 hours.
eGFR less than 15 mL/minute/1.73 m2: There is insufficient information to recommend dosage adjustments.
 
Infants and Children younger than 2 years
There is insufficient information to recommend dosage adjustments in infants and children younger than 2 years with any degree of renal impairment.
 
Intermittent hemodialysis
Ceftobiprole is removed by dialysis. For adults, 333 mg IV every 24 hours given after hemodialysis on dialysis days is recommended for all indications. There is insufficient information to recommend dosage adjustments in pediatric populations.
