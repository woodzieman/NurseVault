Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  General Information
  Flutamide is contraindicated in patients with severe hepatic disease. Flutamide has been associated with severe liver toxicity that in some cases resulted in hospitalization or death. Flutamide is not recommended in patients whose ALT values exceed twice the upper limit of normal. Serum transaminase levels should be measured monthly for the first 4 months of therapy, and periodically thereafter. Liver function tests should be obtained at the first sign and symptoms of hepatic dysfunction. If at anytime a patient has jaundice or their ALT rises above 2-times the upper limit of normal, flutamide should be immediately discontinued with close follow-up of liver function tests.One metabolite of flutamide is 4-nitro-3-fluoro-methylaniline. Several toxicities consistent with aniline exposure, including methemoglobinemia, hemolytic anemia, and cholestatic jaundice have been observed in both animals and humans after flutamide administration. In patients with characteristics that make them susceptible to aniline toxicity including tobacco smoking, glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase deficiency (G6PD deficiency), or a certain hemoglobinopathy (hemoglobin M disease), monitoring of methemoglobin concentrations should be considered.The manufacturer considers flutamide to be contraindicated in females, particularly for non-serious or non-life threatening conditions. There are reports of the use of flutamide in women for hirsutism and polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS); however, the potential serious risks (i.e., life-threatening hepatotoxicity) of this drug should be carefully considered before prescribing flutamide to any woman. Flutamide is classified in FDA pregnancy risk category D. The benefits of therapy do not outweigh the risk of toxicity during pregnancy and flutamide should not be used. It should be noted that anovulatory females with polycystic ovary disease may become ovulatory while receiving flutamide; adequate contraception is recommended. Given its indication for use, flutamide would not be expected to be used during lactation. Use during breast-feeding is not indicated.Flutamide tablets contain lactose; patients with lactase deficiency should take appropriate precautions with use.

    General Information
    Flutamide is not FDA-approved for use in females.
    Headline
    Child-Pugh class B, Child-Pugh class C, hepatic failure, hepatotoxicity
    General Information
    Flutamide is contraindicated in patients with severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh class C) or hepatic failure; hepatotoxicity has been reported in patients treated with flutamide. Use flutamide with caution in patients with moderate hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh class B), as treatment is not recommended in patients with ALT levels 2 times the upper limit of normal (ULN). A discontinuation of therapy may be necessary if hepatotoxicity occurs.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Flutamide may cause fetal harm when administered during pregnancy, based on animal data and its mechanism of action. Advise pregnant people of the potential fetal risk. There was decreased 24-hour survival in the offspring of pregnant rats treated with flutamide at doses of 30, 100, or 200 mg/kg/day (approximately 3, 9, and 19 times the human dose). A slight increase in minor variations in the development of the sternebrae and vertebrae was seen in fetuses of cats treated with 2 higher doses. Feminization of the male cats also occurred at the 2 higher doses. There was a decreased survival rate in the offspring of rabbits receiving the highest dose (15 mg/kg/day, equal to 1.4 times the human dose).
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Avoid the use of flutamide during breast-feeding. There are no data on the presence of flutamide in human milk, its effects on the breastfed child, or its effects on milk production. Flutamide possesses anti-androgenic activity.
    Headline
    people who can cause pregnancy in others, people who may become pregnant, reproductive risk
    General Information
    Counsel people who may become pregnant and people who can cause pregnancy in others about the reproductive risk associated with flutamide. Flutamide can cause fetal harm. Advise people who may become pregnant to use effective contraception while taking flutamide.  Flutamide may cause male infertility. Reduced sperm counts were observed during a 6-week flutamide therapy study in normal human volunteers.

Aprepitant, Fosaprepitant: (Major) Use caution if flutamide and aprepitant are used concurrently and monitor for a possible decrease in the efficacy of aprepitant as well as an increase in flutamide-related adverse effects for several days after administration of a multi-day aprepitant regimen. After administration, fosaprepitant is rapidly converted to aprepitant and shares the same drug interactions. Flutamide is a CYP3A4 substrate in vitro. Aprepitant, when administered as a 3-day oral regimen (125 mg/80 mg/80 mg), is a moderate CYP3A4 inhibitor and inducer; substitution of fosaprepitant 115 mg IV on day 1 of the 3-day regimen may lessen the inhibitory effects of CYP3A4. The AUC of a single dose of another CYP3A4 substrate, midazolam, increased by 2.3-fold and 3.3-fold on days 1 and 5, respectively, when coadministered with a 5-day oral aprepitant regimen. After a 3-day oral aprepitant regimen, the AUC of midazolam increased by 25% on day 4, and decreased by 19% and 4% on days 8 and 15, respectively, when given on days 1, 4, 8, and 15. As a single 40-mg oral dose, the inhibitory effect of aprepitant on CYP3A4 is weak, with the AUC of midazolam increased by 1.2-fold; the midazolam AUC increased by 1.5-fold after a single 125-mg dose of oral aprepitant. After single doses of IV fosaprepitant, the midazolam AUC increased by 1.8-fold (150 mg) and 1.6-fold (100 mg); less than a 2-fold increase in the midazolam AUC is not considered clinically important. Additionally, flutamide is a moderate in vitro CYP3A4 inducer and aprepitant is a CYP3A4 substrate. When a single dose of aprepitant (375 mg, or 3 times the maximum recommended dose) was administered on day 9 of a 14-day rifampin regimen (a strong CYP3A4 inducer), the AUC of aprepitant decreased approximately 11-fold and the mean terminal half-life decreased by 3-fold. The manufacturer of aprepitant recommends avoidance of administration with strong CYP3A4 inducers, but does not provide guidance for weak-to-moderate inducers.

Adverse Reactions
  Headline
  Adverse Reactions
  General Information
  Adverse effects associated with flutamide therapy have been reported during combination therapy with flutamide and LHRH agonists. The most common adverse effects attributed to the flutamide/LHRH agonist combination include libido decrease, impotence, and hot flashes. These adverse reactions are associated with low serum androgen concentrations and are generally related with LHRH agonist therapy.Higher incidences of diarrhea have been reported after the addition of flutamide to LHRH agonist therapy. Diarrhea occurs in 12% of patients and may be linked to lactose intolerance since each tablet contains a considerable amount of lactose. The diarrhea will stop when flutamide is stopped. Additionally, it may be advisable to use appropriate precautions when administering to patients with lactose intolerance (lactase deficiency).Reports of hospitalization and, rarely, death due to hepatic failure have occurred during flutamide post-marketing surveillance. Flutamide hepatotoxicity appears to be due to hepatic necrosis. In addition, reversible hepatitis has been reported in patients receiving flutamide. Flutamide-induced hepatotoxicity presents as abdominal pain, anorexia, cholestasis with jaundice, elevated hepatic enzymes, fatigue, flu-like symptoms, hyperbilirubinemia, nausea/vomiting, right, upper quadrant tenderness, and/or urine discoloration; the urine was noted to change to an amber or yellow-green appearance that can be attributed to the flutamide and/or its metabolites. Flutamide-induced hepatotoxicity can progress to hepatic encephalopathy and death related to acute liver failure. Hepatotoxicity is reversible in some cases if flutamide is discontinued promptly. Approximately half the reported cases occurred in the first 3 months of flutamide treatment. Flutamide should not be used in patients with ALT values 2-times the upper limit of normal and patients should undergo periodic monitoring of liver function tests (see Contraindications). Liver function tests should be obtained at the onset of symptoms.One metabolite of flutamide is 4-nitro-3-fluoro-methylaniline. Several toxicities consistent with aniline exposure, including methemoglobinemia, hemolytic anemia, and cholestatic jaundice have been observed in both animals and humans after flutamide administration. In patients with characteristics that make them susceptible to aniline toxicity including tobacco smoking, glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase deficiency (G6PD deficiency), or a certain hemoglobinopathy (hemoglobin M disease), monitoring of methemoglobin concentrations should be considered.A number of other adverse reactions have been reported with flutamide/LHRH agonist combination therapy, although a causal relationship of these reaction and drug therapy has not been established. Adverse reactions include gynecomastia, mastalgia (pain at the nipple), anemia (macrocytic anemia), leukopenia, sulfhemoglobinemia, and edema.Various types of photosensitivity (including erythema, ulceration, bullous eruptions, and epidermal necrolysis) have been reported during flutamide therapy. The incidence of such reactions is not known.Secondary malignancy of the breast has been reported in men treated with flutamide.

Description
  Text
  Flutamide is an oral acetanilid, nonsteroidal antiandrogen. It is indicated in combination with LHRH agonists for the treatment of locally confined Stage B to C prostate cancer and Stage D metastatic prostate cancer. Due to the risk of hepatotoxicity, flutamide is contraindicated in patients with severe hepatic impairment; liver function tests should be monitored during treatment. Flutamide was FDA approved in 1989.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Flutamide is an acetanilid, nonsteroidal, orally active antiandrogen that inhibits androgen uptake and inhibits nuclear binding of androgen in target tissues. It interferes with testosterone at the cellular level, which can complement medical castration achieved with LHRH agonists, which suppresses testicular androgen production by inhibiting luteinizing hormone secretion.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Oral Route
    Description
    Flutamide is rapidly and completely absorbed; food does not affect the bioavailability of flutamide. The biologically active alpha-hydroxylated metabolite reaches maximum plasma concentrations (Cmax) in approximately 2 hours and was present at higher concentrations than the parent flutamide in all tissues studied. Total drug levels were highest 6 hours after administration in all tissues and declined at roughly similar rates to low levels at 18 hours. The plasma half-life of the alpha-hydroxylated metabolite is approximately 6 hours.

    Renal Impairment
    Description
    Chronic renal insufficiency does not appear to affect the exposure of flutamide or its biologically active alpha-hydroxylated metabolite. The half-life of the active metabolite after a single 250-mg dose was slightly prolonged in subjects with a creatinine clearance less than 29 mL/min. Flutamide and its active metabolite are not cleared by hemodialysis.
    Name
    Geriatric
    Description
    In normal geriatric volunteers, the Cmax of flutamide and hydroxyflutamide were 25.2 ng/mL (+/- 34.2 ng/mL) and 894 ng/mL (+/- 406 ng/mL), respectively, after a single dose and 113 ng/mL (+/- 213 ng/mL) and 1,629 ng/mL (+/- 586 ng/mL), respectively, at steady-state. The Tmax of flutamide after a single dose was 1.9 hours (+/- 0.7 hours) and for hydroxyflutamide was 2.7 hours (+/- 1 hour). At steady-state, the Tmax was 1.3 hours (+/- 0.7 hours) and 1.9 hours (+/- 0.6 hours), respectively. The trough (Cmin) of hydroxyflutamide at steady-state was 673 ng/mL (+/- 316 ng/mL). At the recommended dose (250 mg PO three times daily), both flutamide and its active metabolite approached steady state plasma concentrations after the fourth dose; the half-life of the active metabolite in geriatric volunteers is 8.1 hours (+/- 1.3 hours) after a single dose and 9.6 hours (+/- 2.5 hours) at steady-state.

  Text
  Flutamide is administered orally. At steady-state, flutamide is 94% to 96% protein bound; its active metabolite is 92% to 94% protein bound. The only site of preferential drug accumulation is the prostate. Flutamide is rapidly and extensively metabolized to at least 6 metabolites; within 1 hour of administration, only 2.5% of radiolabeled flutamide (parent drug) was present in the plasma. The primary metabolite in plasma is a biologically active alpha-hydroxylated derivative and accounts for 23% of the plasma tritium 1 hour after administration. Flutamide and its metabolites are mainly excreted in the urine, with only 4.2% of a single dose excreted in the feces over 72 hours; the major urinary metabolite is 2-amino-5-nitro-4-(trifluoromethyl)phenol.
 
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes and transporters: CYP1A2, CYP3A4
In vitro, CYP1A2 is the major pathway for metabolism, while CYP3A4 is a minor pathway.
 
Pharmacodynamics
Elevations of plasma testosterone and estradiol levels have been noted following flutamide administration.
