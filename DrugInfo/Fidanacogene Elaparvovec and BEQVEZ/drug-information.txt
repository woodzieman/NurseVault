    Headline
    coagulopathy, ethanol ingestion, geriatric, hepatic disease, hepatic encephalopathy, hepatitis, hypoalbuminemia, jaundice, metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis, metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease, portal hypertension
    General Information
    Prior to administration of fidanacogene elaparvovec, perform liver health assessments, including enzyme testing [i.e., alanine aminotransferase (ALT), aspartate aminotransferase (AST), alkaline phosphatase, total bilirubin, and albumin], hepatitis B or C serologies, and hepatic ultrasound and elastography. In case of radiological liver abnormalities and/or sustained liver enzyme elevations, consider a consultation with hepatologist to assess eligibility for fidanacogene elaparvovec. Do not administer fidanacogene elaparvovec to persons with current liver-related coagulopathy, hypoalbuminemia, persistent jaundice, cirrhosis, portal hypertension, splenomegaly, hepatic encephalopathy, hepatic fibrosis, or active viral hepatitis. Integration of liver-targeting adeno-associated viral (AAV) vector DNA into the genome may carry the theoretical risk of developing a new primary malignancy of hepatocellular carcinoma. Persons with preexisting risk factors for hepatocellular carcinoma, such as advanced (geriatric) age and hepatic disease, including hepatitis B or C, metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD), chronic ethanol ingestion, and metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis (MASH), should receive annual liver ultrasound screenings and alpha-fetoprotein (AFP) testing for 5 years after fidanacogene elaparvovec administration. For the first year after fidanacogene elaparvovec administration, advise individuals to limit ethanol ingestion, as alcohol may impact liver enzyme elevation and potentially reduce factor IX activity over time.
    Headline
    factor IX inhibitors, laboratory test interference
    General Information
    Perform factor IX inhibitor testing prior to fidanacogene elaparvovec administration. Do not administer fidanacogene elaparvovec to persons with a positive test (0.6 or more Bethesda Units) or a prior history for factor IX inhibitors. The safety and effectiveness of fidanacogene elaparvovec in persons with prior or active factor IX inhibitors have not been established. Also monitor for development of factor IX inhibitors after administration of fidanacogene elaparvovec. Perform an assay that detects factor IX inhibitors if bleeding is not controlled, or plasma factor IX activity concentrations decrease. When using an in vitro activated partial thromboplastin time (aPTT)-based one-stage clotting assay (OSA) for determining factor IX activity, plasma factor IX activity results can be affected by both the type of aPTT reagent and the reference standard used in the assay. Higher inter-laboratory and inter-reagent variability in OSA results is observed at the lower factor IX activity concentrations (0.025 IU/mL). This is important to consider particularly when changing the laboratory and/or reagents used in the assay. Therefore, when possible, use the same laboratory to monitor factor IX activity over time, particularly during the timeframe for corticosteroid treatment decision-making, to minimize the impact of inter-laboratory variability. In a clinical study of fidanacogene elaparvovec, silica-based OSA returned consistently higher values of factor IX activity compared to ellagic acid-based OSA and chromogenic substrate assay (CSA). Generally, values of the ellagic acid-based OSA aligned with values of CSA. Based on clinical trials, the approximate conversion factor between a silica-based OSA and ellagic acid-based OSA/CSA is 2. Use of exogenous factor IX concentrates before and after fidanacogene elaparvovec may result in laboratory test interference and impede assessment of endogenous fidanacogene elaparvovec-derived factor IX activity.
    Headline
    human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) infection
    General Information
    Perform human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) infection testing prior to fidanacogene elaparvovec administration. Do not administer fidanacogene elaparvovec to persons with a CD4+ cell count less than 200 mm3 or a viral load of 20 copies/mL or more in case of serological evidence of HIV-1 or HIV-2 infection. Clinical studies of fidanacogene elaparvovec included a limited number of persons living with HIV, which precludes a determination of whether the efficacy and safety data differ when compared to persons without HIV infection.

    Headline
    accidental exposure
    General Information
    Fidanacogene elaparvovec contains genetically modified vectors. Wear personal protective equipment (including gloves, safety goggles, laboratory coat, and sleeves) while preparing or administering fidanacogene elaparvovec to avoid accidental exposure. Fidanacogene elaparvovec may be transmitted to persons other than the individual receiving the treatment through exposure to excretions and secretions. Temporary vector shedding of intravenously administered AAV-based gene therapies occurs primarily through urine and feces, and to some extent saliva, mucus, and semen. To minimize the risk of transmission to other persons, instruct individuals and/or their caregivers regarding proper hand hygiene when coming into direct contact with secretions or excretions and proper handling of any materials that have come into contact with bodily waste or fluids; recommended procedures include storage of waste material in sealed bags prior to disposal into regular trash. Follow these precautions for 6 months after fidanacogene elaparvovec, especially in the case of pregnancy or immunodeficiency of close contacts.

    Headline
    infusion-related reactions
    General Information
    Infusion-related reactions, including hypersensitivity and anaphylaxis, may occur with fidanacogene elaparvovec administration. Closely monitor for clinical signs and symptoms of infusion-related reactions throughout the infusion period and for at least 3 hours after the end of the infusion. Symptoms of hypersensitivity may include hypotension, fever, palpitation, nausea, vomiting, chills, or headache. In the event of an infusion-related reaction during administration, slow or stop the infusion; the infusion may be restarted at a slower rate once the infusion reaction has resolved. Consider treatment with an antihistamine, corticosteroid, or other measures for management of an infusion-related reaction.

    Headline
    elevated hepatic enzymes, hepatotoxicity
    General Information
    Intravenous administration of a liver-directed adeno-associated virus (AAV) vector could potentially lead to liver transaminase elevations. Transaminase elevations, particularly when observed in the first 4 months after fidanacogene elaparvovec administration, are presumed to occur due to immune-mediated injury of transduced hepatocytes (hepatotoxicity) and may reduce the therapeutic efficacy of the AAV-vector based gene therapy. Liver enzyme elevation may result in decrease in factor IX activity. Monitor alanine aminotransferase (ALT) and aspartate aminotransferase (AST) concentrations once or twice weekly for at least 4 months after administration of fidanacogene elaparvovec. For a single increase in either ALT or AST of 1.5-fold or more from baseline after screening and prior to fidanacogene elaparvovec administration even if within normal range or for consecutive increases in ALT, AST, or both on 2 subsequent blood tests even if within normal range, consider initiating a course of corticosteroids. Start to taper prednisone/prednisolone when the ALT and/or AST have declined for at least 2 consecutive lab draws and/or the concentrations have begun to normalize and any decline in factor IX activity has plateaued. The recommended treatment regimen for oral corticosteroids is prednisone or prednisolone 60 to 100 mg/day (based on body weight) for 1 week, 60 mg/day PO for 1 week, 40 mg/day PO for 1 week, 30 mg/day PO for 2 weeks, 20 mg/day PO until ALT returns to baseline, then reduce dosage by 5 mg/week until 10 mg/day is achieved, and then reduce dosage by 2.5 mg/week up to 5 mg/day. If there is persistent transaminase elevation while on oral corticosteroid treatment alone, consult with a hepatologist as required to discuss use of combined oral and intravenous corticosteroids (i.e., methylprednisolone). Monitor for and manage adverse reactions due to corticosteroid use. If concomitant corticosteroid therapy is needed after fidanacogene elaparvovec administration, delay administration of live vaccines until the individual is weaned off corticosteroids. Elevated hepatic enzymes were reported as adverse reactions in 13.3% to 53.3% of fidanacogene elaparvovec-treated subjects in clinical studies; not all transaminase elevations were reported as adverse reactions. In clinical study 1, 28 subjects (62%) received corticosteroids for transaminase elevation and/or decline in factor IX activity. The mean time to corticosteroid initiation was 45 days, and the mean duration of corticosteroid treatment was 113 days (range: 41 to 276 days). In clinical study 2, 3 subjects (20%) received corticosteroids for transaminase elevation and/or decline in factor IX activity with time to initiation and duration of corticosteroid use within the range seen in clinical study 1.

    Headline
    antibody formation
    General Information
    Fidanacogene elaparvovec may generate immunity in the form of neutralizing antibody formation against the vector capsid, the transgene (virus-derived factor IX), and as a cellular response against the transduced cells producing factor IX. In clinical trials, subjects were required to screen negative for anti-adeno-associated virus serotype Rh74var (AAVRh74var) neutralizing antibodies and negative for factor IX inhibitors in a Nijmegen modified Bethesda assay after a lifetime minimum of 50 exposure days to factor IX replacement therapy. No subjects developed factor IX inhibitors during the clinical studies using fidanacogene elaparvovec. A sustained increase in neutralizing anti-AAVRh74var antibodies has been observed after fidanacogene elaparvovec administration in all subjects who participated in clinical studies and had neutralizing antibody assessment. Mean anti-AAVRh74var antibody titers at weeks 52 and 156 after fidanacogene elaparvovec administration were 101,230.98 and 132,325.56, respectively. No subjects who received fidanacogene elaparvovec in clinical trials developed factor IX inhibitors. Fidanacogene elaparvovec-treated subjects were tested for cellular immune responses to overall capsid pool and overall factor IX pool using an IFN-gamma enzyme-linked immunosorbent spot (ELISpot) assay. ELISpot results did not show a trend of presumed T-cell response (based on limited positive ELISpot) as a function of time during the 1-year post-infusion period in clinical studies.

Fidanacogene elaparvovec is an adeno-associated virus vector-based gene therapy indicated for the treatment of moderate to severe hemophilia B (congenital factor IX deficiency) in those who currently use factor IX prophylaxis therapy, have current or historical life-threatening hemorrhage, have repeated, or serious spontaneous bleeding episodes and who do not have neutralizing antibodies to adeno-associated virus serotype Rh74var (AAVRh74var) capsid as detected by an FDA-approved test. Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  NOTE: All products associated with this monograph are off-market. The content is retained for historical reference only and is no longer actively maintained.
 
Fidanacogene elaparvovec is an adeno-associated virus (AAV) vector-based gene therapy that consists of a recombinant viral capsid (AAVRh74var) derived from naturally occurring AAV serotype Rh74 vector containing the human coagulation factor IX transgene modified to a high-specific factor IX activity variant (FIX-R338L). Fidanacogene elaparvovec introduces a functional copy of FIX-R338L (hFIX Padua) into the transduced cells. The AAVRh74var capsid transduces hepatocytes, the natural site of factor IX synthesis. A single dose of fidanacogene elaparvovec results in cell transduction and an increase in circulating factor IX activity in persons with hemophilia B.

    Description
    The maximum vector DNA concentrations (Cmax) after fidanacogene elaparvovec administration were found in plasma followed by saliva, peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMC), semen, and urine. The mean time to maximum vector DNA concentration (Tmax) was 1.2 days for plasma and saliva, 7.4 days for PBMC, 3.8 days for semen, and 1.6 days for urine. Full vector DNA clearance, defined as 3 consecutive negative results (i.e., below quantification limit), occurred within a mean of 1 to 4 months after fidanacogene elaparvovec administration for plasma, saliva, and semen; PBMC was the slowest fluid to full clearance with a mean of 12 months. Peak vector DNA concentration in the urine was very low relative to plasma and declined to full clearance within a mean of 4 weeks after fidanacogene elaparvovec administration.

Product vial thawing
Remove the inner carton from the outer carton.
Thaw fidanacogene elaparvovec vials for 1 hour at room temperature (15 to 30 degrees C [59 to 86 degrees F]) in the upright orientation in the inner carton.
Vials may be gently swirled; do not shake or invert vials.
Ensure there are no visible ice crystals present in the suspension prior to use.

Remove the calculated volume of fidanacogene elaparvovec from the vials using aseptic technique and add to diluent solution (0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection with 0.25% human serum albumin) for a total infusion volume of 200 mL.
For some individuals, there will be an extra vial that may not need to be used.
Gently mix the suspension for infusion. Do not shake it.
Ensure the suspension for infusion is equilibrated to room temperature before administration.

After the entire content of the bag is infused, flush the IV infusion line using local site procedures.
