        Dosage Description
        Recommended starting dose is 19 mg subcutaneously once weekly. Dose may be increased (maximum, 108 mg) after a minimum of 2 weeks based on medical judgment to reduce or to maintain hematocrit at the recommended level below 45%. Increase doses as follows (allow a minimum of 2 weeks between increases): dose 9.5 mg, increase to 19 mg; dose 19 mg, increase to 28 mg; dose 28 mg, increase to 41 mg; dose 41 mg, increase to 54 mg; dose 54 mg, increase to 69 mg; dose 69 mg, increase to 82 mg; dose 82 mg, increase to 95 mg; dose 95 mg, increase to 108 mg. If the weekly dose is greater than 82 mg, administer the first injection (54 mg) on day 1 and the second injection (remainder of the dose) on day 4 or 5. Dose reduction or discontinuation may be necessary if toxicity occurs. In a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled phase 3 study (VERIFY), patients received rusfertide or placebo during a 32-week period; the experimental group received rusfertide 19 mg subcutaneously weekly, titrated to keep hematocrit levels below 45%. The proportion of patients achieving a response (defined as absence of phlebotomy eligibility) between weeks 20 and 32 was 76.9% in the rusfertide arm and 32.9% in the placebo arm. In addition, a greater proportion of patients maintained a hematocrit lower than 45% from weeks 0 to 32 with rusfertide (62.6% vs. 14.4%) compared with placebo. The mean change from baseline in hematocrit levels (weeks 0 to 32) increased in the placebo arm but remained approximately 0 or lower in the rusfertide arm. During weeks 32 to 52, when patients in the placebo arm were switched to open-label rusfertide, the mean change in hematocrit decreased rapidly.

    anemia, thrombocytosis
    General Information
    Anemia (16%), including decreased hemoglobin levels, and thrombocytosis (8%), including increased platelet counts, were reported in patients who received rusfertide in a clinical trial. Within 4 weeks of treatment initiation, platelet counts increased by an average of 31% from baseline; 36% of patients had platelet counts that exceeded 600 x 10^9 cells/L and 6% had platelet counts that exceeded 1,000 x 10^9 cells/L. Platelet counts generally plateaued on treatment by week 8. Platelet elevations associated with treatment may require cytoreductive therapy initiation, modification, or rusfertide dose modifications or discontinuation.

Description
  Text
  Rusfertide is a hepcidin mimetic indicated for the treatment of erythrocytosis in adults with polycythemia vera. It may cause new or worsening thrombocytosis, injection site reactions, and embryo-fetal toxicity. Rusfertide was approved by the FDA in August 2026.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Rusfertide is a mimetic of the endogenous hormone hepcidin that blocks the iron transporter ferroportin. Inhibition of ferroportin by rusfertide reduces availability of iron for production of red blood cells, resulting in lower hematocrit levels. Polycythemia vera is a chronic myeloproliferative neoplasm typically driven by a constitutively activating JAK2 mutation, resulting in erythrocytosis that increases thromboembolic risk. By binding and inhibiting ferroportin, rusfertide restricts iron export to the plasma, inducing functional iron restriction that limits the iron substrate available for erythropoiesis, thereby reducing red blood cell production and lowering hematocrit independent of any direct marrow-suppressive or antineoplastic effect.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Subcutaneous Route
    Description
    Following subcutaneous administration, rusfertide is absorbed with a median time to peak plasma concentration (Tmax) of 24 hours (range, 4 to 48 hours) after a 19 mg dose; absolute bioavailability is approximately 51%. Cmax and AUC increase less than dose-proportionally over the dose range of 9.5 mg to 54 mg. Steady state concentrations are achieved after approximately 3 once weekly doses; mean accumulation ratios following once weekly 54 mg dosing are 1.6 for AUC and 1.3 for Cmax. Steady-state exposures are similar regardless of whether the injection is given into the abdomen, thigh, or upper arm.

  Text
  Rusfertide is administered subcutaneously. It is highly bound to plasma proteins (greater than 99%), with a blood-to-plasma ratio of 2.7; the mean apparent volume of distribution is 38.4 +/- 19.1 L. Rusfertide is catabolized into smaller peptides via 2 independent pathways: proteolysis by trypsin-like proteases, forming metabolite M1 (a minor metabolite, less than 1% of total drug-related exposure), which undergoes further proteolysis to form M4; and hydroxylation, forming metabolite M9. M4 and M9 are pharmacologically active, with potencies approximately 65% and 14% of rusfertide, respectively, and account for approximately 15% and 31% of total drug-related exposure at steady state. Following subcutaneous administration, urine concentrations of rusfertide are below the detection limit. The mean apparent clearance is 0.930 +/- 0.233 L/hour, and the mean elimination half-life is 28.6 +/- 11.3 hours.
Pharmacodynamics: Maximum reduction in serum iron occurs approximately 24 to 48 hours after a single dose; the 41 mg and 54 mg doses produce a more sustained reduction, lasting up to 96 hours. With repeated weekly dosing, mean serum ferritin rises progressively over time (21 mcg/L at baseline to 174 mcg/L at week 52). At 1.3 times the geometric mean Cmax achieved with the maximum recommended weekly dose (108 mg), clinically significant QTc prolongation was not observed.
 
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes and drug transporters: None

    Administration Details
      Name
      Subcutaneous Administration
      Description
      A patient or caregiver may administer rusfertide after being properly trained on preparation and administration.
For detailed instructions on the preparation and administration, see the Instructions for Use provided by the manufacturer.
Rusfertide is available in more than 1 dosage strength; match the correct strength to prescribed dose. The prescribed dose may require more than 1 injection.
Weekly doses exceeding 54 mg are administered as 2 injections. If the weekly dose is greater than 82 mg, administer the first injection on day 1 and the second injection on day 4 or 5.
Rusfertide is supplied as a single-dose vial of powder for reconstitution with a liquid diluent syringe. Additional supplies needed that are not included in the product carton are a vial adapter (13 mm), needle (27G 1/2 inch), alcohol swabs, and sharps container.
Preparation
Determine the number of vials needed for the full dose.
Attach the vial adapter to the vial(s) and attach the diluent syringe to the vial adapter.
Inject all of the diluent from the syringe into vial containing lyophilized powder; do not remove the syringe from the vial adapter.
Gently swirl the vial to reconstitute; may take about 2 minutes. Do not shake.
Set the vial on a clean surface to allow the liquid to settle and any excess foam to disappear; may take about 1 minute.
Withdraw all of the reconstituted solution from the vial into the syringe and administer as soon as possible. Discard reconstituted solution if not used within 4 hours.
Storage after reconstitution: May store at room temperature between 20 to 25 degrees C (68 to 77 degrees F) for up to 4 hours.
Administration
Attach the needle to the syringe.
Do not select a site where the skin is tender, bruised, red, irritated, hard or broken.
Administer subcutaneously into the abdomen at least 2 inches from the navel, outer area of upper arm, or front of the thigh. The outer area of the upper arms may be used only if the injection is being given by a caregiver. Do not inject into the same spot twice in a row; change/rotate sites between injections.
Discard the empty medicine vial and the syringe and needle into the sharps disposal container.

    Description
    If a once a week injection is missed by 1 to 4 days, administer the missed injection immediately and then resume regular dosing schedule. If missed by more than 4 days, administer the missed injection immediately and the next injection 3 days later; then resume the regular dosing schedule.
If a 2-times a week injection is missed by 1 to 2 days, administer the missed injection immediately and the next injection 3 days later; then resume the regular dosing schedule. If missed by more than 2 days, skip the missed injection and continue the regular dosing schedule.
Visually inspect parenteral products for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration whenever solution and container permit.
