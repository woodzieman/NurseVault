    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components.
    Headline
    cardiovascular disease, thrombophilia, tobacco smoking
    General Information
    It is not known if the risks associated with deucravacitinib, a tyrosine kinase 2 (TYK2) inhibitor, are similar to those observed with Janus Kinase (JAK) inhibitors; however, TYK2 is a member of the JAK family. Therefore, caution is advised when administering deucravacitinib to patients at increased risk of thrombosis (thrombophilia) and thromboembolism. In a large, randomized, postmarketing safety trial of a JAK inhibitor in rheumatoid arthritis (RA) patients 50 years of age and older with at least one cardiovascular (CV) risk factor, higher rates of all-cause mortality (including sudden CV death), major adverse CV events (MACE), overall thrombosis, deep venous thrombosis (DVT), pulmonary embolism (PE), and malignancies were observed in patients treated with the JAK inhibitor as compared to those treated with tumor necrosis factor (TNF) inhibitors. Patients who are current or past smokers are at additional increased risk. Consider the benefits and risks before initiation or continuation of therapy, particularly in patients with a history of current or past tobacco smoking and in patients with cardiovascular disease or other cardiovascular risk factors. Patients should be informed about the symptoms of serious cardiovascular events and the steps to take if they occur. Deucravacitinib is not approved for use in RA.
    Headline
    Treatment-Related Restrictions of Use
    General Information
    Prior to initiating deucravacitinib, complete all age-appropriate immunizations according to current guidelines, including prophylactic herpes zoster vaccination. Avoid the use of live vaccines in individuals treated with deucravacitinib, as the response to live or non-live vaccines has not been evaluated.
    Headline
    malignancy
    General Information
    Deucravacitinib may increase the risk for a new primary malignancy. During the 0- to 52-week treatment period of the 2 clinical trials, malignancies were reported in 3 drug recipients, including single cases each of breast cancer, hepatocellular carcinoma, and lymphoma (treatment weeks 24,32, and 25, respectively). A total of 3 patients developed lymphoma (0.1 per 100 patient-years) while receiving deucravacitinib during clinical trials (treatment weeks 25, 77, and 98); 1 individual from one of the 0- to 52-week clinical trials and 2 individuals from the open-label extension trial. Consider the benefits and risks of treatment with deucravacitinib in patients with a known malignancy (other than a successfully treated non-melanoma skin cancer) and patients who develop a malignancy while receiving deucravacitinib.
    Headline
    Child-Pugh class C, hepatic failure
    General Information
    Avoid use of deucravacitinib in patients with severe hepatic disease (Child-Pugh class C) or hepatic failure. No dose adjustments are needed in patients with mild (Child-Pugh class A) or moderate (Child-Pugh class B) hepatic impairment. During clinical trials, increases in hepatic enzymes greater than 3-times the upper limit of normal (ULN) were observed more frequently in deucravacitinib-treated patients than in those who received the placebo. Evaluate liver function tests at baseline and thereafter in patients with known or suspected hepatic disease according to routine patient management. If treatment-related increases in hepatic enzymes occur and drug-induced hepatic injury is suspected, stop deucravacitinib until a diagnosis of hepatic injury is excluded.
    Headline
    infection
    General Information
    Deucravacitinib may increase the risk of infections. Do not administer deucravacitinib to patients with an active or serious infection until the infection resolves or is adequately treated. Consider the risks and benefits of treatment before initiating deucravacitinib in individuals with chronic or recurrent infections, history of serious or opportunistic infections, underlying condition(s) that may predispose them to an infection (i.e., immunosuppression), or known or suspected exposure to tuberculosis. Evaluate individuals for latent or active tuberculosis infection prior to initiating treatment. Consider viral hepatitis screening and monitoring according to clinical guidelines. Deucravacitinib is not recommended for use in individuals with active tuberculosis, active hepatitis B virus (HBV), or active hepatitis C virus (HCV) infections. In individuals with latent tuberculosis or a history of tuberculosis in whom an adequate course of treatment cannot be confirmed, initiate antituberculosis therapy before starting deucravacitinib. Closely monitor individuals for signs and symptoms of infection during and after treatment with deucravacitinib. If signs of viral hepatitis reactivation occur, consult a hepatologist for advice. The impact of deucravacitinib on reactivation of chronic hepatitis is unknown, as patients with positive screening tests for HBV or HCV, chronic hepatitis B, or untreated hepatitis C were excluded from clinical trials. Individuals who develop a new infection during treatment should undergo prompt and complete diagnostic testing, and initiate appropriate antimicrobial therapy. In individuals who develop a serious infection, treatment with deucravacitinib should be interrupted and not resumed until the infection has resolved or is adequately treated. Herpes virus reactivation (e.g., herpes zoster, herpes simplex) was reported during deucravacitinib clinical trials.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Available data regarding the use of deucravacitinib during pregnancy are insufficient to determine a drug-associated risk of major birth defects, miscarriage, or adverse outcomes. In rats and rabbits, no effects on embryo-fetal development were observed following administration of deucravacitinib during organogenesis at exposures 211 times and 72 times the exposure (AUC) at the maximum recommended human dose (MRHD), respectively. In a pre- and post-natal development study in rats, deucravacitinib exposure from gestation day 6 through lactation day 20 was not associated with medication-related effects on postnatal development, neurobehavioral performance, or reproductive performance in offspring at exposures 15 times the MRHD based on AUC. At exposures 87 times the MRHD in rats, offspring had reduced body weight gain during the pre-weaning period, which gradually normalized to control levels after weaning. Report exposures during pregnancy to the manufacturer's Adverse Event reporting line at 1-800-721-5072.
    Headline
    diabetes mellitus
    General Information
    Use deucravacitinib with caution in people with diabetes mellitus. Hypoglycemia, including severe hypoglycemia, has been reported in individuals with diabetes mellitus who started other JAK inhibitors. During treatment with deucravacitinib, consider increased blood glucose monitoring as clinically indicated in individuals with diabetes. Advise individuals to notify their health care provider if they develop signs or symptoms of hypoglycemia.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Use deucravacitinib with caution during breast-feeding. There are no data on the presence of deucravacitinib in human milk, its effects on the breastfed child, or its effects on milk production. Deucravacitinib is present in animal milk. Consider the benefits of breast-feeding, the clinical need for treatment, and any potential adverse effects on the breastfed child from the medication or from the patient's underlying medical condition.

Adverse Reactions
  Headline
  Adverse Reactions
  Adverse Reactions
    Headline
    General Information
    General Information
    The overall safety profile of deucravacitinib in individuals with active psoriatic arthritis was generally consistent with the safety profile observed in individuals with plaque psoriasis.
    Headline
    infection, laryngitis, pharyngitis, rhinitis, sinusitis, tracheitis
    General Information
    In the 16-week placebo-controlled period of the 2 clinical trials for plaque psoriasis, an infection developed in 29% of individuals treated with deucravacitinib (116 events per 100 person-years) and 22% of individuals in the placebo group (83.7 events per 100 person-years). Upper respiratory tract infections (i.e., nasopharyngitis, pharyngitis, sinusitis, rhinitis, rhinotracheitis, tracheitis, laryngitis, tonsillitis) were the most frequently reported, occurring in 19.2% of deucravacitinib-treated patients and 14.8% of patients who received placebo. Herpes simplex infections (i.e., oral herpes, genital herpes, herpes simplex, and herpes virus infection) were reported in 17 individuals in the deucravacitinib group (2%; 6.8 per 100 patients-years) and 1 individual in the placebo group (0.2%; 0.8 per 100 patient-years). Herpes zoster was reported in less than 1% of people who received deucravacitinib. Multidermatomal herpes zoster was reported in 1 immunocompetent individual treated with deucravacitinib. The majority of these infections were mild to moderate in severity and did not lead to treatment discontinuation. Serious infections were reported in 5 deucravacitinib-treated individuals (2 per 100 patient-years) and 2 individuals who received placebo (1.6 per 100 patient-years). The most common serious infections reported during the 52-week treatment period were pneumonia and COVID-19. One individual, who did not have latent tuberculosis, developed active tuberculosis after receiving 54 weeks of deucravacitinib.

    elevated hepatic enzymes
    General Information
    Elevated hepatic enzymes were observed in individuals treated with deucravacitinib during clinical trials. In the 16-week placebo-controlled period for the treatment of plaque psoriasis, ALT elevations of at least 3-times the upper limit of normal (ULN) were reported in 9 deucravacitinib-treated individuals (3.6 per 100 patient-years) and 2 individuals in the placebo group (1.6 per 100 patient-years). AST elevations of at least 3-times the ULN were noted in 13 individuals who received deucravacitinib (5.2 per 100 patient-years) and 2 individuals who received placebo (1.6 per 100 patient-years). Evaluate liver function tests (LFTs) prior to starting and periodically throughout deucravacitinib therapy in individuals with known or suspected hepatic disease. If increases in liver enzymes occur during therapy and deucravacitinib-induced liver injury is suspected, interrupt therapy until a diagnosis of liver injury is excluded.

Description
  Text
  Deucravacitinib is an oral tyrosine kinase (TYK2) inhibitor indicated for the treatment of active psoriatic arthritis and moderate to severe plaque psoriasis in individuals who are candidates for systemic therapy or phototherapy. It is not recommended for use in combination with other potent immunosuppressants when used for plaque psoriasis. Clinical trials in adults with moderate to severe plaque psoriasis demonstrated that deucravacitinib 6 mg once daily significantly improved disease severity compared with placebo and apremilast, as measured by higher rates of Psoriasis Area and Severity Index (PASI) 75 and Static Physician's Global Assessment (sPGA) 0/1 responses at Week 16. In 2 randomized, placebo-controlled trials in adults with active psoriatic arthritis, deucravacitinib significantly improved disease activity, with a greater proportion of individuals achieving American College of Rheumatology 20% improvement criteria (ACR20) at Week 16 compared with placebo. Similar to other immunosuppressants, deucravacitinib may increase the risk of infections. Individuals should be evaluated for active and latent tuberculosis (TB) infection and complete all immunizations according to current immunization guidelines prior to initiating deucravacitinib. Individuals who are positive for TB should receive an adequate course of anti-TB treatment before initiating deucravacitinib. Deucravacitinib should not be administered to those with severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh class C). Deucravacitinib was FDA-approved in 2022.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Deucravacitinib is a tyrosine kinase 2 (TYK2) inhibitor. TYK2, a member of the Janus kinase (JAK) family, pairs with JAK1 to mediate multiple cytokine pathways and also pairs with JAK2 to transmit signals through activation of Signal Transducers and Activators of Transcription (STATs). By binding to the regulatory domain of TYK2, deucravacitinib stabilizes an inhibitory interaction between the regulatory and catalytic domains of the enzyme. This results in allosteric inhibition of receptor-mediated activation of TYK2. Deucravacitinib inhibits interluekin-2 (IL-2), IL-23, and type I interferon (IFN-alpha and IFN-beta) downstream signaling, but does not interfere with JAK2- dependent hematopoietic functions. The exact mechanism linking inhibition of TYK2 to therapeutic effect in plaque psoriasis or active psoriatic arthritis is not currently known.
 
Pharmacodynamics
Deucravacitinib therapy is associated with a dose-dependent decrease in psoriasis-associated gene expression in psoriatic skin, including IL-23 pathway and type I IFN pathway regulated genes. Following 16-weeks of oral deucravacitinib therapy, circulating IL-17A, IL-19, and beta-defensin were reduced. In individuals with active psoriatic arthritis, reductions in biomarkers including C-reactive protein (CRP), matrix metalloproteinase-3 (MMP3), matrix metalloproteinase-1 (MMP1), type I collagen degradation product (C1M), and TNF-alpha were also observed. The relationship between these pharmacodynamic markers and the mechanism by which deucravacitinib exerts its clinical effects is unknown.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Oral Route
    Description
    The absolute oral bioavailability of deucravacitinib is 99% and the median time to reach peak concentration (Tmax) ranges from 2 to 3 hours in healthy individuals. Maximum plasma concentrations (Cmax) and systemic exposure (AUC) of deucravacitinib increase proportionally over a dosing range from 3 to 36 mg (i.e., 0.5- to 6-times the approved recommended dose). Drug accumulation is less than 1.4-fold with once daily dosing. At steady state, the Cmax and AUC of deucravacitinib following oral administration of 6 mg once daily are 45 nanograms/mL and 473 nanograms x hour/mL, respectively, in individuals with plaque psoriasis, and 53 nanograms/mL and 598 nanograms x hour/mL, respectively, in individuals with psoriatic arthritis. The Cmax and AUC of the active metabolite, BMT-153261, at steady state following oral administration of 6 mg once daily are 5 nanograms/mL and 95 nanograms x hour/mL, respectively, in individuals with plaque psoriasis and 19 nanograms/mL and 209 nanograms x hour/mL, respectively, in individuals with psoriatic arthritis. Administration of the dose with a high-fat, high-calorie meal (951 kcal, 52% fat, 33% carbohydrate, 15% protein) does not significantly affect the pharmacokinetics of deucravacitinib. When administered with food, the Cmax and AUC of deucravacitinib are decreased by approximately 24% and 11%, respectively, and the Tmax is prolonged by 1 hour. The Cmax and AUC of BMT-153261 when administered with food are decreased by approximately 23% and 10%, respectively, and the Tmax is prolonged by 2 hours.

  Deucravacitinib is administered orally. Once in systemic circulation, the drug has a Vd at steady state of 140 L and is 82% to 90% bound to plasma proteins; the blood-to-plasma concentration ratio is 1.26. Deucravacitinib is metabolized by CYP1A2 to form the major metabolite BMT-153261. It also undergoes metabolism by CYP2B6, CYP2D6, carboxylesterase (CES) 2, and uridine glucuronyl transferase (UGT) 1A9. The metabolite BMT-153261 has comparable potency to the parent drug, but its circulating exposure only accounts for approximately 20% of the total systemic exposure of drug-related components. The drug and its metabolites are eliminated in the urine and feces. After a single dose, approximately 13% and 26% of the dose is excreted unchanged in the urine and feces, respectively; approximately 6% and 12% of the dose is eliminated as BMT-153261 in the urine and feces, respectively. The terminal half-life of deucravacitinib is 10 hours.
