Drug Class: Guselkumab is a human monoclonal antibody that selectively binds the p19 subunit of interleukin-23 (IL-23) and inhibits IL-23-mediated inflammatory signaling, Guselkumab is approved for the treatment of moderate to severely active ulcerative colitis (UC), moderate to severely active Crohn disease...

Routes: injection (IV/IM/SC); Common adult doses: Plaque Psoriasis and Psoriatic Arthritis: 100 mg/dose subcutaneously.

Common Side Effects (excluding routine GI upset): candidiasis, fever, infection, influenza, pharyngitis, arthralgia, headache, migraine, bleeding, edema, erythema, fatigue, hematoma, injection site reaction, pruritus, skin discoloration, urticaria

In an Overdose: No overdose/antidote information is listed in this file.

Key Nursing Considerations: Contraindications/precautions: This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components. Update immunizations in agreement with current immunization guidelines prior to initiating guselkumab therapy.

    Headline
    General Information
    General Information
    Update immunizations in agreement with current immunization guidelines prior to initiating guselkumab therapy. Avoid the use of live vaccines in patients receiving guselkumab.
Before starting guselkumab, test potential drug recipients for hepatitis B (surface antigen and core antibody), hepatitis C (IgG), and HIV (HIV-1 and HIV-2 antibodies and HIV-1 antigen). Consider ongoing screening (e.g., annually) in people who are at increased risk for HIV, hepatitis B, or hepatitis C infection. Retest for HIV in any person who displays symptoms or other conditions that may suggest HIV. In patients found to be HIV positive, consider involving relevant specialists to ensure the HIV viral load is suppressed through antiretroviral therapy. Similarly, retest for viral hepatitis in any person who develops unexplained elevations in hepatic enzymes. It is recommended to consult a hepatologist when using biological therapy in patients with hepatitis B or C, whether acute (newly diagnosed) or chronic.
    Headline
    infection
    General Information
    Avoid starting guselkumab in patients with any clinically significant active infection until the infection resolves or is adequately treated. In people with a chronic infection or history of recurrent infection, consider the risks and benefits of treatment with guselkumab. Instruct patients to seek medical advice if signs or symptoms of an infection develop. If a severe or clinically important infection develops or if an infection is not responding to standard therapy, discontinue guselkumab until the infection resolves. Evaluate patients for tuberculosis (TB) infection prior to initiating treatment. Do not administer guselkumab to patients with active TB. Initiate treatment of latent TB prior to administering guselkumab. Consider anti-TB therapy prior to starting guselkumab in patients with a past history of latent or active TB in whom an adequate course of treatment cannot be confirmed. Monitor all patients for signs and symptoms of active TB during and after guselkumab treatment.
    Headline
    hepatic disease
    General Information
    Consider using an alternative to guselkumab in individuals with evidence of acute hepatic disease or cirrhosis. For the treatment of plaque psoriasis or psoriatic arthritis, evaluate liver enzymes and bilirubin prior to initiating treatment with guselkumab and periodically thereafter, if clinically indicated. In individuals with ulcerative colitis or Crohn disease, evaluate liver enzymes and bilirubin at baseline, for 16 weeks of treatment, and periodically thereafter. If liver enzyme elevation is observed during treatment, promptly investigate the cause to identify potential cases of drug-induced liver injury. Interrupt treatment if drug-induced liver injury is suspected, until this diagnosis is excluded.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    There are no available data regarding guselkumab use during human pregnancy to inform a drug-associated risk of major birth defects, miscarriage, or other adverse outcomes. Human IgG antibodies are known to cross the placental barrier. Transport of these IgG antibodies increases as pregnancy progresses, and peaks during the third trimester. Consider the potential clinical impact of in utero drug exposure and the impact of the medication on the health of the pregnant individual. Published data suggest that the risk of adverse pregnancy outcomes in people with inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) is associated with increased disease activity. Adverse pregnancy outcomes include preterm delivery (before 37 weeks gestation), low birth weight (less than 2,500 g) infants, and small for gestational age at birth. In a combined embryofetal development and pre-and postnatal development study in animals, no adverse developmental effects were observed in offspring born to pregnant cynomolgus monkeys after subcutaneous administration of guselkumab during organogenesis through parturition at doses up to 18-times the exposure in humans administered 200 mg intravenously and 16-times the exposure to the 400 mg dose given subcutaneously. Offspring deaths were observed at 4- to 18-times the exposure in humans administered 200 mg intravenously and 4- to 16-times the exposure to the 400 mg dose given subcutaneously. The clinical significance of these findings is not known. There is a pregnancy exposure registry that monitors outcomes in pregnancies exposed to guselkumab; patients are encouraged to enroll in the registry by visiting www.mothertobaby.org/ongoing-study/tremfya-guselkumab, by calling 1-877-311-8972, or by emailing MotherToBaby@health.ucsd.edu.
    Headline
    neonates and infants exposed to this medication in utero
    General Information
    Consider the potential clinical impact of guselkumab exposure to neonates and infants exposed to this medication in utero. Transport of endogenous IgG antibodies across the placenta increases as pregnancy progresses, and peaks during the third trimester. Therefore, it is expected that guselkumab may be present in infants exposed in utero. No data are available on the response to live or inactive vaccines after exposure.
    Headline
    planned procedure
    General Information
    Patients who undergo surgery while taking biologic therapy, such as guselkumab, may be at higher risk for postoperative infections. For a planned procedure, balance the risk of postoperative infection against the risk of developing a severe or unstable disease by stopping the biologic therapy. Recommendations for the use of immunomodulatory agents in the perioperative setting vary. The American Academy of Dermatology and National Psoriasis Foundation recommend patients with psoriasis and psoriatic arthritis continue to receive IL-23 inhibitors through low-risk surgeries or procedures. They also suggest that IL-23 inhibitors may be safely continued through intermediate- and high-risk surgeries on a case-by-case basis; however, The American College of Rheumatology recommends holding guselkumab for 9 weeks before undergoing total knee or total hip arthroplasties. The British Association of Dermatologists advise stopping biologic therapy 3- to 5-times the half-life or the length of the treatment cycle (whichever is longer) between the last dose and a planned surgery. Restart the biologic therapy postoperatively if there is no evidence of infection and wound healing is satisfactory.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Use guselkumab with caution during breast-feeding. There are no data on the presence of guselkumab in human milk, the effects on the breastfed child, or the effects on milk production. Guselkumab was not detected in the milk of lactating cynomolgus monkeys. Due to species-specific differences in lactation physiology, animal data may not reliably predict drug levels in human milk. Maternal IgG is known to be present in human milk. Absorption by the infant is probably minimal; waiting for at least 2 weeks postpartum to resume therapy may minimize transfer to the infant. The Global Consensus Group for Pregnancy and Inflammatory Bowel Disease considers guselkumab use to be acceptable during breast-feeding. The developmental and health benefits of breast-feeding should be considered along with the clinical need for guselkumab and any potential adverse effects on the breastfed child from guselkumab or the underlying maternal condition.

Abatacept: (Major) Concomitant use of abatacept with other biologic agents, such as guselkumab, is not recommended because of the possibility of additive immunosuppression and increased risk of infection. There is insufficient experience to assess the safety and efficacy of abatacept administered concurrently with guselkumab.
Acetaminophen; Codeine: (Moderate) Clinically relevant drug interactions may occur when guselkumab is administered with sensitive substrates of CYP2D6, such as codeine. Description
  Text
  Guselkumab is a human monoclonal antibody that selectively binds the p19 subunit of interleukin-23 (IL-23) and inhibits IL-23-mediated inflammatory signaling, Guselkumab is approved for the treatment of moderate to severely active ulcerative colitis (UC), moderate to severely active Crohn disease (CD), moderate-to-severe active psoriatic arthritis (PsA), and moderate-to-severe plaque psoriasis in people who are candidates for systemic therapy or phototherapy. A large proportion of people with moderate-to-severe plaque psoriasis treated with guselkumab achieve skin clearance as measured by PASI 90 responses. In the treatment of PsA, guselkumab not only improves signs and symptoms of PsA, but also results in radiographic response, inhibiting progression of joint and structural damage, and may be used as monotherapy or in combination conventional disease-modifying antirheumatic drug (csDMARD, e.g., methotrexate). The ideal therapy for psoriasis or PsA is individualized, and considers treat-to-target strategies and severity of disease, including the presence or absence of psoriatic arthritis and the person's comorbidities and treatment preferences. In adults with moderately to severely active UC or CD, guselkumab is a guideline-recommended advanced therapy for induction and maintenance of remission. For CD, the AGA categorizes guselkumab as a higher-efficacy option in both advanced-therapy-naive and previously exposed adults. For UC, the AGA categorizes it as a higher-efficacy option in advanced-therapy-naive adults and an intermediate-efficacy option after previous advanced-therapy exposure. Guselkumab uniquely offers a fully subcutaneous regimen for both induction and maintenance treatments for UC and CD. Guselkumab may increase susceptibility to infection, and individuals should be evaluated for active or latent tuberculosis (TB) before treatment is initiated. Guselkumab was FDA-approved in 2017.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Guselkumab is a human IgG1 lambda monoclonal antibody that selectively binds to the p19 subunit of interleukin 23 (IL-23) cytokine, inhibiting its interaction with the IL-23 receptor. A naturally occurring cytokine, IL-23 is involved in normal inflammatory and immune responses. By inhibiting the interaction of IL-23 with its receptor, guselkumab blocks the release of proinflammatory cytokines and chemokines. In addition, guselkumab reduces serum concentrations of IL-17A, IL-17F, and IL-22 relative to pretreatment concentrations in patients with psoriasis. Serum concentrations of acute phase proteins C-reactive protein, serum amyloid A, and IL-6, and Th17 effector cytokines IL-17A, IL-17F, and IL-22 were reduced in psoriatic arthritis patients treated with guselkumab. The relationship between these pharmacodynamic markers and the mechanism by which guselkumab exerts its clinical effect is not fully understood.

  Guselkumab is administered subcutaneously and intravenously. The drug exhibits linear pharmacokinetics in both healthy subjects and subjects with psoriasis. The apparent Vd in adults with psoriasis, ulcerative colitis, and Crohn disease are 13.5 L, 10.1 L, and 11.4 L, respectively. The pharmacokinetics of guselkumab in subjects with psoriatic arthritis are reported to be similar to those with plaque psoriasis.
 
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes and drug transporters: CYP1A2, CYP2C9, CYP2C19, CYP2D6, CYP3A4
The formation of CYP450 enzymes can be altered by increased levels of certain cytokines [e.g., interleukin (IL)-1, IL-6, IL-10, TNF-alpha, interferon] during chronic inflammation. The administration of guselkumab may, in theory, normalize the formation of CYP450 enzymes. Data from drug-drug interaction studies involving subjects with moderate-to-severe plaque psoriasis suggest guselkumab has a low potential for clinically relevant interactions with substrates of CYP3A4, CYP2C9, CYP2C19, and CYP1A2. However, the results were highly variable because of the limited number of subjects in the study. The potential for guselkumab interaction with substrates of drugs metabolized by CYP2D6 cannot be ruled out.

You will hear the first 'click' when the injection begins. Keep holding the pen firmly against the skin for about 10 seconds and you will hear a second 'click'. Keep pushing.
The injection is complete when the plunger rod stops moving and fills the viewing window.
Lift the injector straight up off the skin.
Do not rub the injection site; slight bleeding may occur. If needed, cover with a bandage.
Disposal: Dispose of the used prefilled pen properly in an FDA-cleared sharps disposal container right away after use. Do not re-use the prefilled pen. No preservatives are present; discard any unused portion.
