      Heading
      for the treatment of relapsed or refractory diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL) not otherwise specified including DLBCL arising from low grade lymphoma in patients who are ineligible for autologous stem cell transplant (ASCT), in combination with lenalidomide
      Routes
        Heading

        Route Age Groups
          Heading
          Adults
          Dosage Description
          12 mg/kg (actual body weight) IV on days 1, 4, 8, 15, 22 of cycle 1; 12 mg/kg IV on days 1, 8, 15, 22 of cycles 2 and 3; and 12 mg/kg IV on days 1 and 15 of cycle 4 and beyond until disease progression; administer in combination with lenalidomide (25 mg PO daily on days 1 to 21 for a maximum of 12 cycles). Treatment cycles are repeated every 28 days. Premedicate with acetaminophen, a H1-antihistamine, a H2-antihistamine, and/or a glucocorticosteroid 30 minutes to 2 hours prior to the first 3 infusions. Continue premedication prior to each subsequent infusion if an infusion-related reaction occurs; premedication is optional in patients who do not experience an infusion-related reaction. Therapy interruption or discontinuation may be necessary in patients who develop toxicity. At the final 5-year follow-up analysis in adult patients with relapsed or refractory DLBCL who received tafasitamab plus lenalidomide in a single-arm, phase 2 trial (L-MIND), the objective response rate was 57.5%, with a complete response rate of 41.3%. After a median follow-up of 44 months, the median duration of response was not reached. The median progression-free survival time was 11.6 months with a median follow-up of 45.6 months and the median overall survival time was 33.5 months following a median follow-up of 65.6 months.

After a median follow-up of 14.1 months, progression-free survival (PFS) time was significantly longer in adult patients with relapsed or refractory FL who received tafasitamab plus lenalidomide and rituximab compared with placebo plus lenalidomide and rituximab (22.4 months vs. 13.9 months); the overall response rate was 84% versus 72%, respectively. At the time of the PFS analysis, the median overall survival time had not been reached in either arm.

    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components.
    Headline
    infection
    General Information
    Use tafasitamab with caution in patients with an active infection or who have recently had an infection. Fatal and serious infection, including opportunistic infection, has been reported in patients during and after tafasitamab therapy. Monitor patients for signs and symptoms of infection and manage infections as appropriate. Consider infection prophylaxis per institutional guidelines and/or treatment with subcutaneous or intravenous immunoglobulin as appropriate.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Tafasitamab may cause fetal harm if administered during pregnancy, based on its mechanism of action. There are no available human or animal data to evaluate a drug-associated risk. Immunoglobulin G monoclonal antibodies are transferred across the placenta; therefore, tafasitamab may cause fetal CD19-positive, B-cell depletion.
    Headline
    people who may become pregnant, reproductive risk
    General Information
    Counsel people who may become pregnant about the reproductive risk associated with tafasitamab. Tafasitamab may cause fetal harm. Verify pregnancy status prior to treatment initiation and throughout treatment based on the requirements of agents used in combination. Advise people who may become pregnant to use effective contraception while taking tafasitamab and for 3 months after the last dose.
    Headline
    neonates and infants exposed to this medication in utero
    General Information
    It is unclear how the immune system of neonates and infants exposed to this medication in utero will react to subsequent administration of a live vaccine. Tafasitamab is an immunomodulatory agent and may cause depletion of fetal CD19 positive immune cells. IgG monoclonal antibodies cross the placenta during pregnancy. Defer administering live vaccines to those exposed to this medication in utero until a hematology evaluation is completed.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Advise patients to avoid breast-feeding while taking tafasitamab and for 3 months after the last dose due to the potential for serious adverse effects in the breast-fed child. There is no data on the presence of tafasitamab in human milk, its effects on the breast-fed child, or its effects on milk production. Human IgG is present in human milk, but the effects of local gastrointestinal exposure and limited systemic exposure in the breast-fed child are unknown.

Interactions
Cholera Vaccine: (Moderate) Patients receiving immunosuppressant medications may have a diminished response to the live cholera vaccine. When feasible, administer indicated vaccines prior to initiating immunosuppressant medications. Counsel patients receiving immunosuppressant medications about the possibility of a diminished vaccine response and to continue to follow precautions to avoid exposure to cholera bacteria after receiving the vaccine.
Dengue Tetravalent Vaccine, Live: (Contraindicated) Avoid administration of the live dengue virus vaccine with immunosuppressive drug therapy and prior to immune recovery following treatment with immunosuppressive drug therapy. When feasible, administer indicated live virus vaccines at least 4 weeks before planned immunosuppression or wait until at least 3 months after discontinuation. The time to restoration of immune competence may be longer in some patients. Patients with altered immunocompetence may be at increased risk for severe adverse reactions due to uninhibited growth of the attenuated live virus. Additionally, vaccines may be less effective if administered during a period of altered immunocompetence.
SARS-CoV-2 (COVID-19) vaccines: (Moderate) Patients receiving immunosuppressant medications may have a diminished response to the SARS-CoV-2 virus vaccine. Adverse Reactions

  Adverse Reactions
    Headline
    infection, laryngitis, pharyngitis, progressive multifocal leukoencephalopathy, sepsis, sinusitis
    General Information
    Infection (73%; grade 3 or higher, 30%) including respiratory tract infection (51%; grade 3 or 4, 12%), urinary tract infection (17%; grade 3 or 4, 4.9%), and sepsis (4.9%) were reported in patients who received tafasitamab plus lenalidomide in a nonrandomized trial. One case of progressive multifocal leukoencephalopathy (PML) was also reported in this trial. In a separate randomized trial, grade 3 or higher infection occurred in 24% of patients treated with tafasitamab plus lenalidomide and rituximab. Respiratory infections were reported in 56% (grade 3 or 4, 18%), which included COVID-19 infection (34%; grade 3 or 4, 10%), pneumonia (18%; grade 3 or 4, 14%), and upper respiratory infection (17%; grade 3 or 4, 1.1%). Opportunistic infections, including herpes simplex or zoster infection (5%), fungal pneumonia (1.1%), and cytomegalovirus (CMV) reactivation, occurred in 6% of patients in the same population. Infection related death occurred in 1.1% to 2.5% of patients treated with tafasitamab in both clinical trials. The term respiratory tract infection included bronchitis, pneumonia, nasopharyngitis, sinusitis, and laryngitis. Consider infection prophylaxis per institutional guidelines; consider intravenous immunoglobulin as appropriate.

    anemia, lymphopenia, neutropenia, thrombocytopenia
    General Information
    Severe myelosuppression can occur with tafasitamab therapy, including neutropenia, lymphopenia, thrombocytopenia, and anemia. Neutropenia (51%; grade 3 or 4, 49%), anemia (36%; grade 3, 7%), thrombocytopenia (31%; grade 3 or 4, 17%), febrile neutropenia (grade 3 or 4, 12%), and lymphopenia (6%) were reported in patients with relapsed or refractory diffuse large B-cell lymphoma who received tafasitamab plus lenalidomide in a nonrandomized trial. Decreases from baseline in neutrophils (75%; grade 3 or 4, 48%), hemoglobin (60%; grade 3 or 4, 9%), lymphocytes (57%; grade 3 or 4, 22%), and platelets (40%; grade 3 or 4, 8%) were reported in patients with relapsed or refractory follicular lymphoma who received tafasitamab in combination with lenalidomide and rituximab in a separate randomized clinical trial; febrile neutropenia was also reported in 4.4% of patients in the same population. Consider granulocyte colony stimulating factor administration as appropriate. Therapy interruption or discontinuation may be necessary in patients who develop severe myelosuppression.

Description
  Text
  Tafasitamab-cxix is a CD19-directed cytolytic monoclonal antibody indicated in combination with lenalidomide for the treatment of relapsed or refractory diffuse large B-cell lymphoma in adult patients who are ineligible for an autologous stem-cell transplant and in combination with lenalidomide and rituximab for the treatment of relapsed or refractory follicular lymphoma in adult patients. Myelosuppression and infection have been reported with tafasitamab therapy.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Tafasitamab is a humanized IgG1CD19-directed cytolytic monoclonal antibody that contains an IgG1/2 hybrid Fc-domain with 2 amino acid substitutions to modify the Fc-mediated functions of the antibody. It works by binding to the CD19 antigen expressed on the surface of pre-B and mature B lymphocytes causing B-cell lysis through apoptosis and immune effector mechanisms, including antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC) and antibody-dependent cellular phagocytosis. In vitro, the combination of tafasitamab and lenalidomide increased ADCC activity in diffuse large B-cell lymphoma tumor cells compared with tafasitamab or lenalidomide alone.

Administration
  General Information
  For storage information, see the specific product information within the How Supplied section.
Premedication
Premedicate with acetaminophen, a histamine H1 receptor antagonist, a histamine H2 receptor antagonist, and/or a glucocorticosteroid 30 minutes to 2 hours prior to the first 3 tafasitamab infusions.
Premedication is optional for subsequent infusions in patients who do not experience an infusion-related reaction; continue premedication prior to each infusion if an infusion-related reaction is experienced.
  Route Specific Administrations
    Administration Details
      Name
      Intravenous Administration
      Description
      Reconstitution
Add 5 mL of Sterile Water for injection toward the side of the tafasitamab lyophilized powder vial for a final concentration of 40 mg/mL.
Gently swirl until the powder is completely dissolved (this may take up to 5 minutes); do not shake or swirl vigorously.
The reconstituted solution should be clear to slightly yellow.
Storage following reconstitution: Store up to 12 hours at room temperature (20 to 25 degrees C; 68 to 77 degrees F) or under refrigeration (2 to 8 degrees C; 36 to 46 degrees F); protect from light.
Dilution
Calculate the required dosage and determine the volume required from the reconstituted vial.
Remove the calculated amount from a 250 mL bag of 0.9% Sodium Chloride injection; add the calculated drug volume to the infusion bag for a final infusion bag concentration between 2 and 8 mg/mL.
Discard any unused portion of drug remaining in the vial.
Gently mix the contents of the infusion bag by slowly inverting the bag; do not shake.
Storage of diluted admixture: Store up to 12 hours at room temperature or up to 18 hours under refrigeration; protect from light and do not freeze.
Intravenous (IV) infusion
Infuse the first dose of tafasitamab at an IV infusion rate of 70 mL/hr for the first 30 minutes, then increase the rate to ensure the entire dose is administered within 1.5 to 2.5 hours.
All subsequent doses may be infused IV over 1.5 to 2 hours.
Monitor during the infusion for infusion-related reactions.
Do not administer other drugs through the same infusion line.
Tafasitamab is compatible with infusion containers made of polypropylene (PP), polyvinylchloride (PVC), polyethylene (PE), polyethylenterephthalate (PET), or glass and infusion sets made of polyurethane (PUR) or PVC.
