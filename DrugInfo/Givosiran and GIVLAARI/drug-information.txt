Drug Class: Givosiran is a parenteral aminolevulinate synthase 1-directed small interfering RNA (siRNA) indicated for the treatment of acute hepatic porphyria (AHP) in adults.

Routes: injection (IV/IM/SC); Common adult doses: 2.5 mg/kg/dose subcutaneously once monthly.

Common Side Effects (excluding routine GI upset): erythema, injection site reaction, pruritus, rash, skin discoloration, urticaria

In an Overdose: No overdose/antidote information is listed in this file.

    Headline
    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components.
    Headline
    hyperhomocysteinemia
    General Information
    Measure blood homocysteine concentrations at baseline and monitor for changes during treatment with givosiran. In individuals with hyperhomocysteinemia, assess folate, vitamin B12, and vitamin B6. Consider supplementation with vitamin B6, as monotherapy or part of a multivitamin preparation. Elevated blood homocysteine concentrations have occurred in individuals receiving givosiran. The clinical significance of the homocysteine elevations during treatment with givosiran is unknown.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    There are no available data on the use of givosiran during pregnancy to inform a drug-associated risk of major birth defects, miscarriage, or other adverse outcomes. Weigh the benefits of treatment against the potential risks to the fetus. Acute porphyria attacks during pregnancy, often triggered by hormonal changes, occur in approximately 24% to 95% of patients with acute hepatic porphyria (AHP) and have been associated with maternal mortality rates of 2% to 42%. Pregnancy in patients with AHP is also associated with increased risks of spontaneous abortion, hypertension, and low birth weight infants. In animal studies, subcutaneous administration of givosiran to pregnant rabbits during organogenesis produced adverse developmental findings (e.g., skeletal variations) at doses associated with maternal toxicity.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Use givosiran with caution during breast-feeding. There are no data on the presence of givosiran in human milk, its effects on the breastfed child, or its effects on milk production. Consider the benefits of breast-feeding, the patient's clinical need for treatment, and any potential adverse effects on the breastfed child from the medication or from the patient's underlying medical condition.

Discontinue all serotonergic agents and initiate symptomatic treatment if serotonin syndrome occurs.

Description
  Text
  Givosiran is a parenteral aminolevulinate synthase 1-directed small interfering RNA (siRNA) indicated for the treatment of acute hepatic porphyria (AHP) in adults. In the ENVISION trial (n = 94), a double-blind, placebo-controlled study, patients with acute hepatic porphyria, including acute intermittent porphyria (n = 89), variegate porphyria (n = 2), hereditary coproporphyria (n = 1), and no identified mutation (n = 2), having a minimum of 2 porphyria attacks requiring hospitalization, urgent healthcare visit, or intravenous hemin administration at home in the 6 months before study entry were included. On average, givosiran-treated patients experienced 70% (95% CI 60% to 80%) fewer porphyria attacks compared to placebo. Givosiran use also resulted in a reduction in hemin use as well as urinary aminolevulinic acid (ALA) and porphobilinogen (PBG), which are neurotoxic intermediates associated with porphyria attacks and other disease manifestations. Serious adverse events, including anaphylaxis, hepatic transaminase elevation, and serum creatinine elevation with associated glomerular filtration rate decrease have been reported with givosiran. Givosiran is intended for administration by a healthcare professional only and where medical support is available to appropriately manage anaphylactic reactions during administration. Liver and renal function monitoring are recommended.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Givosiran is a double-stranded small interfering RNA (siRNA) that ultimately decreases circulating concentrations of the neurotoxic heme intermediates aminolevulinic acid (ALA) and porphobilinogen (PBG), which are associated with acute attacks and other disease manifestations of acute hepatic porphyria. Givosiran binds to and silences aminolevulinate synthase 1 (ALAS1) mRNA in hepatocytes, thereby inhibiting the translation and expression of the ALAS1 protein and reducing the elevated concentrations of hepatic ALAS1 mRNA. This prevents ALA formation and PBG production and decreases 5-ALA concentrations.

Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes and drug transporters: CYP1A2, CYP2D6
Givosiran is not a substrate of CYP isoenzymes. Givosiran does not directly inhibit or induce CYP isoenzymes; however, because of its pharmacological effects on the hepatic heme biosynthesis pathway, it has the potential to reduce the activity of CYP isoenzymes in the liver.       Preparation
Withdraw the required drug volume using a 21-gauge or larger needle. Divide doses requiring volumes more than 1.5 mL into multiple syringes.
Replace the 21-gauge or larger needle with either a 25- or 27-gauge needle with 1/2- or 5/8-inch needle length.
Avoid having givosiran on the needle tip until the needle is in the subcutaneous space.
Storage: Vials are single-dose. Discard any unused portion.
 
Administration
Administer into the abdomen, the back or side of the upper arms, or the thighs. If injecting into the abdomen, avoid a 5 cm diameter circle around the navel.
If more than 1 injection is needed for a single dose, use injection sites at least 2 cm apart from previous injection locations.
Rotate injection sites.
Never inject into scar tissue or areas that are reddened, inflamed, or swollen.
    Name
    Injectable Administration
    Description
    Visually inspect parenteral products for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration whenever solution and container permit. Givosiran is a clear, colorless to yellow solution.
Givosiran is intended for administration by a healthcare professional only.
Ensure medical support is available to appropriately manage anaphylactic reactions when administering givosiran.
Administer givosiran as soon as possible after a missed dose. Resume dosing at monthly intervals after administration of the missed dose.

Dosage Adjustment Guidelines
  Hepatic Impairment
  Interrupt or discontinue treatment for severe or clinically significant transaminase elevations. In patients with severe or clinically significant transaminase elevations, who have dose interruption and subsequent improvement, reduce the givosiran dose to 1.25 mg/kg/dose subcutaneously once monthly when resuming therapy. In patients who resume therapy at the reduced dose without recurrence of severe or clinically significant transaminase elevations, the dose may be increased to the recommended dose of 2.5 mg/kg/dose subcutaneously once monthly.
  Renal Impairment
  Specific guidelines for dosage adjustments in renal impairment are not available; it appears that no dosage adjustments are needed.
