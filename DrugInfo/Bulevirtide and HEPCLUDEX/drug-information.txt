Description
  Text
  Bulevirtide is a subcutaneous selective hepatitis B virus (HBV)/hepatitis delta virus (HDV) entry inhibitor indicated for the treatment of chronic HDV infection in individuals without cirrhosis or with compensated cirrhosis. The indication was approved under accelerated approval based on decreases in HDV RNA concentrations and alanine aminotransferase (ALT) normalization; improvement in disease-related clinical outcomes has not been established. Bulevirtide is the first FDA-approved treatment for chronic HDV infection. In a multicenter, randomized, open-label, parallel-arm trial in 100 adults with chronic HDV infection without cirrhosis or with compensated cirrhosis, participants were randomized to immediate treatment with bulevirtide or delayed treatment. At Week 48, a combined response, defined as undetectable HDV RNA concentrations or a decline in HDV RNA concentrations from baseline of at least 2 log10 International Units/mL plus ALT normalization, occurred in 48% of participants receiving bulevirtide compared with 2% of those in the delayed treatment group. Undetectable HDV RNA concentrations occurred in 20% vs. 0% of participants at Week 48 and increased to 36% and 50% at Weeks 96 and 144, respectively, in the bulevirtide group. Bulevirtide carries a boxed warning regarding severe acute exacerbations of hepatitis D and hepatitis B following treatment discontinuation, particularly in individuals with cirrhosis. Bulevirtide was FDA-approved in May 2026.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Bulevirtide inhibits hepatitis delta virus (HDV) infection by binding to sodium taurocholate co-transporting polypeptide (NTCP) on the plasma membrane of hepatocytes and blocking HDV attachment to NTCP. Bulevirtide is a synthetic 47-amino acid lipopeptide derived from the hepatitis B virus surface antigen (HBsAg) preS1 domain.
 
Antiviral Activity
In primary human hepatocytes, bulevirtide demonstrated antiviral activity against laboratory-generated and clinical HDV isolates representing HDV genotypes 1 through 8 and HBV genotypes A through H. Median EC50 values ranged from 0.4 to 0.52 nM across in vitro models.
 
Antiviral Resistance
No bulevirtide-resistant HBV or HDV variants have been identified in cell culture or clinical studies. In clinical trials, no baseline polymorphisms or treatment-emergent substitutions in the bulevirtide region of HBsAg or in HDAg were associated with virologic nonresponse or breakthrough, and identified variants remained susceptible to bulevirtide in cell culture assays.
 
Cross-Resistance
Cross-resistance between bulevirtide and nucleos(t)ide analog reverse transcriptase inhibitors approved for chronic hepatitis B infection is not expected because of their different mechanisms of action.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Subcutaneous Route
    Description
    The median Tmax of bulevirtide is 3 hours (range, 1 to 4 hours), and the absolute bioavailability is 57%. After subcutaneous administration in adults with HDV infection, the geometric mean Cmax was 184 nanograms/mL, and the geometric mean AUC was 1,935 nanogram x hour/mL. Systemic exposure increased in a more than dose-proportional manner with increasing doses; at steady state, AUC and Cmax were approximately 2-fold higher than after the first dose.

    The steady-state pharmacokinetics of bulevirtide were approximately 27% lower in individuals with moderate hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh class B) than in individuals with normal hepatic function.     Description
    No clinically significant differences in bulevirtide pharmacokinetics were observed in individuals with severe renal impairment (CrCl 15 to 29 mL/minute) and those with normal renal function. Bulevirtide has not been studied in end-stage renal disease (CrCl less than 15 mL/minute), including those receiving dialysis. However, because bulevirtide is more than 99% protein bound, dialysis is not expected to significantly affect drug exposure.

  Text
  Bulevirtide is administered by subcutaneous injection. Plasma protein binding is more than 99%. Bulevirtide is catabolized by peptidases to amino acids; no active metabolites are expected. The mean terminal plasma half-life is 3 hours (range, 2 to 6 hours). The major route of elimination is excretion as smaller peptides and amino acids.
