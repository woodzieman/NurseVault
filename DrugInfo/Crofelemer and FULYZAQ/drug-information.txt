Routes: oral; Common adult doses: 250 mg/day PO.

Common Side Effects (excluding routine GI upset): elevated hepatic enzymes, hemorrhoids, hyperbilirubinemia, cough, infection, pharyngitis, sinusitis, acne vulgaris, anxiety, arthralgia, back pain, contact dermatitis, depression, dizziness, increased urinary frequency, leukopenia, musculoskeletal pain, nephrolithiasis

    Headline

    Headline
    Black patients
    General Information
    In the clinical trial, treatment with crofelemer was comparatively less effective in Black patients than in non-African Americans. Among other racial subgroups, no differences in the consistency of the crofelemer treatment effect have been observed.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Oral crofelemer has minimal systemic absorption; use during pregnancy is not expected to result in fetal drug exposure. In reproductive toxicology studies, crofelemer was not teratogenic and produced no embryo-fetal toxicity in pregnant rats at oral doses up to 177 times the recommended human daily dose. In pregnant rabbits, doses 96 times the recommended human daily dose were not teratogenic but were associated with increased fetal resorption and abortion relative to controls. It is unclear whether this finding reflects a direct embryo-fetal effect or is secondary to the maternal toxicity (e.g., decreased body weight, decreased food consumption) observed at these doses.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Use crofelemer with caution during breast-feeding. There are no data on the presence of crofelemer in human milk, its effects on the breastfed child, or its effects on milk production. However, oral crofelemer has minimal systemic absorption, making it unlikely to significantly transfer into human milk. Consider the benefits of breast-feeding, the patient's clinical need for treatment, and any potential adverse effects on the breastfed child from the medication or from the patient's underlying medical condition.

Description
  Text
  Crofelemer is a botanical prescription drug compound derived from the red sap of the Croton lechleri plant of Latin and South America. It is indicated as an anti-diarrheal for the symptomatic relief of non-infectious diarrhea in adult patients with HIV/AIDS on anti-retroviral therapy. Crofelemer does not affect gut motility and is not absorbed systemically to any significant level. It instead acts locally, inhibiting the secretion of chloride ions via the cystic fibrosis transmembrane regulator (CFTR) chloride channels and the calcium-activated chloride channels (CaCC) from the luminal membrane of intestinal cells. By blocking these channels, crofelemer reduces gastrointestinal fluid accumulation. Crofelemer received initial FDA approval in December 2012.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Crofelemer is an inhibitor of both the cyclic adenosine monophosphate (cAMP)-stimulated cystic fibrosis transmembrane conductance regulator (CFTR) chloride ion channel, and the calcium-activated chloride ion channels (CaCC) at the luminal membrane of enterocytes. These channels regulate fluid secretion by intestinal epithelial cells. Crofelemer acts by blocking the channels and accompanying high volume water loss in diarrhea, normalizing the flow of chloride ions and water in the GI tract.

Pharmacokinetics

    Description
    The absorption of crofelemer is minimal following oral dosing in healthy adults and HIV-positive patients and concentrations of crofelemer in plasma are below the level of quantitation (i.e., < 50 ng/mL). Therefore, standard pharmacokinetic parameters such as area under the curve (AUC), maximum concentration (Cmax), and half-life cannot be estimated.
