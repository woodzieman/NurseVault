Drug Class: Racepinephrine, a racemic mixture of epinephrine, is an inhalation sympathomimetic bronchodilator agent.

Routes: inhalation; Common adult doses: 12 inhalations/day.

Common Side Effects (excluding routine GI upset): angina, arrhythmia exacerbation, chest pain (unspecified), hemoptysis, hypertension, intracranial bleeding, myocardial infarction, pallor, palpitations, peripheral vasoconstriction, sinus tachycardia, stroke, anxiety, dizziness, headache, insomnia, restlessness, seizures, tremor, weakness

In an Overdose: No overdose/antidote information is listed in this file.

Key Nursing Considerations: Contraindications/precautions: This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components. Racepinephrine should not be used without a prescription (over the counter, or OTC) unless the patient has been told by their health care provider that they have asthma.

  Contraindications And Precautions
    Headline

    Headline
    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components.
    Headline
    General Information
    General Information
    Racepinephrine should not be used without a prescription (over the counter, or OTC) unless the patient has been told by their health care provider that they have asthma. Also, if the patient has ever been hospitalized for asthma, they are encouraged not to use racepinephrine over-the-counter (OTC), and to seek medical evaluation and prescription instead. Asthma can be a life threatening condition. Signs of asthma exacerbation include: if symptoms are not relieved in 20 minutes, symptoms get worse, the patient needs more than 12 racepinephrine inhalations in 24 hours, the patient uses more than 9 inhalations in 24 hours for more than 3 days a week, or if the patient experiences more than two asthma attacks per week. The patient should consult their health care provider and stop use of racepinephrine if asthma worsens.
    Headline
    cardiac disease, hypertension
    General Information
    Use of racepinephrine inhalations may cause increased blood pressure or heart rate, and may increase the risk for myocardial infarction (MI) or stroke. This risk for MI or stroke increases if the individual has a history of hypertension or cardiac disease, takes this product more frequently than recommended, exceeds the recommended dose, or administers this drug using a non-approved route, such as via an electronic atomizer or nebulizer.
    Headline
    closed-angle glaucoma, diabetes mellitus, prostatic hypertrophy, psychiatric disorder, seizures, thyroid disease
    General Information
    Non-selective sympathomimetic agents such as racepinephrine can aggravate a variety of health conditions, and should be used cautiously in patients with diabetes mellitus, closed-angle glaucoma, thyroid disease (especially hyperthyroidism), a psychiatric disorder or emotional condition, a history of seizures, or trouble with urination due to prostatic hypertrophy.
    Headline
    children, infants, neonates
    General Information
    Children 4 years of age and older should be supervised by an adult during the use of racepinephrine. Racepinephrine should not be used without a prescription in children less than 4 years of age; very young children, infants, or neonates should receive evaluation and advice of a health care provider for the treatment of asthma symptoms. Additionally, administration via a hand-held rubber bulb nebulizer may be challenging in very young children. The FDA has noted that administration via atomizers or mechanical nebulizers is not recommended, as such adminstration may alter the pharmacokinetic, distribution, administered dosage, and safety profile of racepinephrine inhalation solution, and may increase the risk of more serious side effects.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    There are no adequate human data on racepinephrine use during pregnancy to assess a drug-associated risk of major birth defects, miscarriage, or other adverse pregnancy outcomes. Among short-acting bronchodilators, albuterol has substantially greater pregnancy experience and is preferred because available pregnancy safety data are generally reassuring. Racepinephrine may increase heart rate and blood pressure in the pregnant person. Because racepinephrine is racemic epinephrine with beta-adrenergic activity, it may also inhibit uterine contractility; however, the clinical relevance of this effect with inhaled racepinephrine has not been established.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Racepinephrine is compatible with breast-feeding. There are no data on the presence of racepinephrine in human milk, its effects on the breastfed child, or its effects on milk production. Racepinephrine is racemic epinephrine. Epinephrine exposure is expected to be very low in the breastfed child due to its poor bioavailability and short half-life. Inhaled epinephrine is unlikely to interfere with breast-feeding.

Description
  Text
  Racepinephrine, a racemic mixture of epinephrine, is an inhalation sympathomimetic bronchodilator agent. Racepinephrine is about one-half as active as epinephrine and the products are labeled for the temporary relief of shortness of breath, chest tightness, and wheezing due to bronchial asthma in adults and children 4 years of age and older. Racepinephrine is about one-half as active as epinephrine. Epinephrine and related epinephrine products have been available for inhalation since the early 1940's. However, concerns over the safety of these products have led to multiple revisions in U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) drug monograph status, including the removal of most non-prescription metered-dose inhaler (MDI) products from the market in 1996. In the United States, racepinephrine is available as an oral inhalation solution (Asthmanefrin, S2 brands) that can be purchased without a prescription (over the counter, or OTC) for use in hand-held rubber bulb nebulizer; these products remain in the final monograph for non-prescription (OTC) bronchodilator drug products and are generally recognized as safe and effective (GRASE). These products have not been evaluated for safety and efficacy via the new drug application (NDA) process, and thus are not approved to be delivered via modern electronic nebulizer or aerolizer devices. The use of non-prescription racepinephrine is generally not recommended in treatment guidelines. Racepinephrine is generally reserved for the acute relief of asthma when selective prescription short-acting beta2-agonists (SABAs) are not available; if symptoms of bronchial asthma are not relieved within 20 minutes or if they become worse, patients should be instructed to discontinue use of the product immediately and seek medical assistance. Excessive use of racepinephrine may result in nervousness, tachycardia, and possibly, other adverse cardiac effects. Adverse events after the marketing of these OTC products have been reported to the FDA. The FDA is reexamining the use of OTC bronchodilator drugs in hand-held rubber bulb nebulizers; the FDA has recommended that these inhalational products be removed from the OTC monograph.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Racepinephrine, a racemic mixture of epinephrine, is an inhalation sympathomimetic bronchodilator agent. Epinephrine is a nonselective adrenergic agonist; it stimulates alpha1-, alpha2-, beta1-, and beta2-adrenergic receptors, although the degree of stimulation at these receptors may vary depending on the dose administered (i.e., the circulating concentration of epinephrine at the receptor). Stimulation of beta2-receptors by epinephrine leads to arteriolar vasodilation, bronchial smooth muscle relaxation, and increased glycogenolysis. Subsequent to binding at the adrenergic receptor, the intracellular actions of epinephrine are mediated by cyclic adenosine monophosphate (cAMP). The production of cAMP is augmented by beta-stimulation and attenuated by alpha-stimulation. Epinephrine primarily exerts its relaxant effect on bronchial smooth muscle via stimulation of beta2-receptors. Beta2-stimulation also prevents mast cell secretion of histamine and other autocoids, thus antagonizing its effect on end organs and reversing bronchoconstriction and edema. Furthermore, alpha-stimulation may decrease secretions from the bronchial mucosa, attenuating the development of edema. There is some evidence that racepinephrine is effective as a treatment of some pulmonary conditions such as bronchiolitis in children.

Pharmacokinetics

    Name
    Inhalation Route
    Description
    After inhalation of a usual dose via a hand-held bulb nebulizer, racepinephrine, like epinephrine, produces an onset of action usually within 1 to 5 minutes. Inhalational pharmacokinetics of epinephrine are, like other inhaled medications, determined by factors such as the inhaled particle size, the reliability of the device for delivery, and, the rate of delivery via inhalation. Since racepinephrine is regulated under the non-prescription (OTC) bronchodilator drug monograph via the U.S. Food and Drug Administration, pharmacokinetic characteristics required of a new drug application (NDA) process for inhalational drugs, such as assessments of inhalational reliability, specifications of target dose per inhalation, and delivered dose uniformity have not been tested or documented.
  Text
  Racepinephrine is a racemic mixture of epinephrine that is administered by oral inhalation. The drug is distributed throughout the body. Epinephrine crosses the placenta but does not penetrate the blood-brain barrier to a great extent. Epinephrine will distribute into breast milk. The pharmacologic activity of epinephrine is rapidly terminated by uptake and metabolism in the synaptic cleft. Circulating drug is metabolized by the enzymes catechol-O-methyltransferase (COMT) and monoamine oxidase (MAO) in the liver and in other tissues. These inactive metabolites are then conjugated to either sulfates or glucuronides and renally excreted. Minimal amounts of the drug are excreted unchanged in the urine.

Racepinephrine is used in an aqueous solution at a concentration equivalent to 1 percent epinephrine.
Add 0.5 mL (contents of one vial) of racepinephrine solution to the hand-held rubber bulb nebulizer.
The use of this product by children should be supervised by an adult.
During clinical use, heart rate and blood pressure monitoring may be advised, as heart rate and blood pressure may increase.
Storage: Protect unopened vials from light and store at cool temperature between 2 and 20 degrees C (36 to 68 degrees F). Do not freeze. The sterile vials of inhalation solution are each individually wrapped in foil pouches which should remain sealed until the time of use; do not use a vial if the foil pouch has been opened. Do not use the product if it is brown in color or if product color is pinkish or darker than slightly yellow, or if the product is cloudy or if a precipitate is noted. The vials do not contain a preservative. Discard any opened, unused solution.
    Name
    Inhalation Administration
    Description
    Racepinephrine inhalation products should only be used in hand-held bulb nebulizers; safety and efficacy in modern electronic or continuous nebulizer machines is not established, and may result in alterations in dosage that may increase the risk for serious adverse reactions, including cardiovascular events.
