Drug Class: Pemigatinib is an oral small molecule kinase inhibitor that targets fibroblast growth factor receptors (FGFR) 1, 2, and 3.

Routes: oral; Common adult doses: 13.5 mg PO once daily.

      for the treatment of previously treated, unresectable locally advanced or metastatic cholangiocarcinoma with a fibroblast growth factor receptor 2 (FGFR2) fusion or other rearrangement

          Heading

          Dosage Description
          13.5 mg PO once daily on days 1 to 14, followed by 7 days of rest, every 21 days until disease progression. Coadministration of certain drugs may need to be avoided or dosage adjustments may be necessary; review drug interactions. Therapy may need to be interrupted, dose reduced, or discontinued in patients who develop severe toxicity. Treatment with pemigatinib resulted in an objective response rate of 36% (complete response, 2.8%) for a median duration of 9.1 months in patients with locally advanced unresectable or metastatic cholangiocarcinoma with an FGFR2 fusion or non-fusion rearrangement whose disease had progressed on or after at least 1 prior therapy in a multicenter, noncomparative trial (the FIGHT-202 study); the median time to response was 2.7 months (range, 0.7 to 6.9 months). The duration of response was at least 6 months in 63% of patients, and was at least 12 months in 18% of patients.

      Heading
      for the treatment of relapsed or refractory myeloid/lymphoid neoplasms with FGFR1 rearrangement
      Routes
        Heading
        Oral dosage
        Route Age Groups
          Heading
          Adults
          Dosage Description
          13.5 mg PO once daily until disease progression. Coadministration of certain drugs may need to be avoided or dosage adjustments may be necessary; review drug interactions. Therapy may need to be interrupted, dose reduced, or discontinued in patients who develop severe toxicity. In a multicenter, single-arm, phase 2 (FIGHT-203) trial, pemigatinib was evaluated in 28 patients with myeloid/lymphoid neoplasms with FGFR1 rearrangement (8p11 rearrangement based on cytogenetic evaluation) using 2 dosing schedules: 13.5 mg PO once daily continuously (recommended schedule) or 13.5 mg PO once daily for 14 days (days 1 to 14) repeated every 21 days until disease progression, unacceptable toxicity, or allogeneic hematopoietic stem-cell transplantation (allo-HSCT). The median duration of treatment was 205 (range, 30 to 1,347) days. Overall, the complete cytogenetic response rate was 79% in 28 patients with myeloid/lymphoid neoplasms with FGFR1 rearrangement. The complete response (CR) rate was 78% in 18 patients with chronic phase in the marrow with or without extramedullary disease (EMD); the median time to CR was 104 (range, 44 to 435) days and the median duration of CR was not reached (range, 1 to more than 988 days). The CR rate was 50% in 4 patients with blast phase in the marrow with or without EMD; the CR lasted more than 1 day and 94 days. Additionally, 1 (33%) of 3 patients with EMD only achieved a CR that lasted more than 64 days. Patients in this study had relapsed disease after allo-HSCT or a disease modifying therapy, or they were not a candidate for allo-HSCT or other disease modifying therapies.

Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  Contraindications And Precautions
    Headline
    Drug Interactions
    General Information
    The coadministration of certain medications may lead to harm and require avoidance or therapy modification; review all drug interactions prior to concomitant use of other medications.
    Headline
    cataract surgery, myopia, ocular trauma, posterior vitreous detachment, retinal detachment
    General Information
    Use pemigatinib with caution in patients with a history of high myopia, posterior vitreous detachment (PVD), ocular trauma, previous cataract surgery, or a personal or family history of retinal detachment as these may increase the risk for retinal detachment. Pemigatinib can cause retinal pigment epithelial detachment (RPED) (retinal detachment), which may cause symptoms including blurred vision, visual floaters, or photopsia. The median time to onset of RPED was 56 days. Dry eye symptoms were also commonly reported; treat with ocular demulcents as needed. Perform a comprehensive ophthalmological examination including optical coherence tomography (OCT) prior to beginning treatment with pemigatinib, every 2 months for the first 6 months of treatment, and every 3 months thereafter during treatment. If visual symptoms occur, urgently refer patients for an ophthalmologic evaluation, with follow-up every 3 weeks until resolution or discontinuation of pemigatinib. An interruption of therapy, dose reduction, or discontinuation of therapy may be necessary if RPED occurs.
    Headline
    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components.
    Headline
    hyperphosphatemia
    General Information
    Do not use pemigatinib in patients with hyperphosphatemia. Pemigatinib can cause hyperphosphatemia leading to soft tissue mineralization, cutaneous calcification, calcinosis, and non-uremic calciphylaxis. Monitor patients for hyperphosphatemia during treatment and follow dose modification guidelines when required; an interruption of therapy, dose reduction, or discontinuation of therapy may be necessary if hyperphosphatemia occurs.
    Headline
    renal failure, renal impairment
    General Information
    Reduce the dose of pemigatinib for patients with severe renal impairment (eGFR 15 to 29 mL/min); however, a dose adjustment is not recommended for patients with end-stage renal disease (eGFR less than 15 mL/min) receiving intermittent hemodialysis. Data are not available for patients with renal failure. Consider alternative markers of renal function if persistent treatment-related elevations in serum creatinine are observed; serum creatinine may appear increased without affecting glomerular function due to an inhibition of renal tubular secretion of creatinine via OCT2 and MATE1 renal transporters.
    Headline
    Child-Pugh class C, hepatic failure
    General Information
    Reduce the dose of pemigatinib for patients with severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh class C; or total bilirubin greater than 3 times the upper limit of normal [ULN] with any AST); data are not available for patients with hepatic failure.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Pregnancy should be avoided by females of reproductive potential during pemigatinib treatment and for at least 1 week after the last dose. Although there are no adequately controlled studies in pregnant women, pemigatinib can cause fetal harm or death when administered during pregnancy based on its mechanism of action and animal studies. Women who are pregnant or who become pregnant while receiving pemigatinib should be apprised of the potential hazard to the fetus. Administration of pemigatinib to pregnant rats during organogenesis resulted in 100% embryofetal mortality due to postimplantation loss at doses resulting in approximately 0.6 times the human exposure based on AUC at the recommended dose of 13.5 mg once daily. Fetal survival was unaffected at doses resulting in approximately 0.2 times the human exposure based on the recommended dose, but there was reduced mean fetal body weight and an increase in fetal skeletal and visceral malformations, major blood vessel variations, and reduced ossification.
    Headline
    contraception requirements, male-mediated teratogenicity, pregnancy testing, reproductive risk
    General Information
    Counsel patients about the reproductive risk and contraception requirements during pemigatinib treatment. Pemigatinib can be teratogenic if taken by the mother during pregnancy. Females of reproductive potential should avoid pregnancy and use effective contraception during and for at least 1 week after treatment with pemigatinib. Due to the risk of male-mediated teratogenicity, males with female partners of reproductive potential should also use effective contraception during and for at least 1 week after treatment with pemigatinib. Females of reproductive potential should undergo pregnancy testing prior to initiation of pemigatinib. Women who become pregnant while receiving pemigatinib should be apprised of the potential hazard to the fetus.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in nursing infants from pemigatinib, advise women to discontinue breast-feeding during treatment and for 1 week after the final dose. It is not known whether pemigatinib is present in human milk, although many drugs are excreted in human milk.

Interactions
Adagrasib: (Major) Avoid coadministration of pemigatinib and adagrasib due to the risk of increased pemigatinib exposure which may increase the risk of adverse reactions. If coadministration is unavoidable, reduce the dose of pemigatinib to 9 mg PO once daily if original dose was 13.5 mg per day and to 4.5 mg PO once daily if original dose was 9 mg per day. If adagrasib is discontinued, resume the original pemigatinib dose after 3 elimination half-lives of adagrasib. Pemigatinib is a CYP3A substrate and adagrasib is a strong CYP3A inhibitor.     blurred vision, photopsia, retinal detachment
    General Information
    Ophthalmologic adverse events have been reported with pemigatinib therapy. Comprehensive ophthalmological examinations including optical coherence tomography (OCT) are recommended prior to starting therapy and continuing during therapy. Retinal pigment epithelial detachment (RPED) occurred in 11% (grade 3 or 4, 1.3%) of patients treated with pemigatinib across clinical trials (n = 635); symptoms may include blurred vision, visual floaters, or photopsia. The median time to onset of RPED was 56 days. A dose reduction was required in 3.1% of patients, resulting in resolution or improvement to grade 1 in 76% of these patients; a permanent discontinuation of therapy was necessary for 0.2% of patients. An interruption of therapy, dose reduction, or discontinuation of therapy may be necessary if RPED occurs. The term RPED included maculopathy, retinal detachment, retinal disorder, retinal thickening, and the presence of subretinal fluid. The incidence of asymptomatic RPED with pemigatinib is unknown as clinical trials did not conduct routine monitoring including OCT. Additionally, blurred vision (21%; grade 3 or 4, 2.9%) and trichiasis (18%; grade 3 or 4, 2.9%) occurred in patients with relapsed or refractory myeloid/lymphoid neoplasms with FGFR1 rearrangement who received pemigatinib (n = 34) in a clinical trial.

Description
  Text
  Pemigatinib is an oral small molecule kinase inhibitor that targets fibroblast growth factor receptors (FGFR) 1, 2, and 3. It is indicated for the treatment of adults with previously treated, unresectable locally advanced or metastatic cholangiocarcinoma with an FGFR2 fusion or other rearrangement as detected by an FDA-approved test. Pemigatinib is also indicated for the treatment of adults with relapsed or refractory myeloid/lymphoid neoplasms with FGFR1 rearrangement. Comprehensive ophthalmological examinations are necessary prior to beginning therapy and throughout treatment due to the risk of retinal pigment epithelial detachment. Increases in serum phosphate levels are also a pharmacodynamic effect of pemigatinib; monitor for hyperphosphatemia.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Pemigatinib is a small molecule kinase inhibitor. It inhibits the phosphorylation and signaling of fibroblast growth factor receptors (FGFR) 1, 2, and 3 and decreases cell viability in cancer cell lines with activating FGFR amplifications and fusions that result in constitutive activation of FGFR signaling. Constitutive FGFR signaling can support the proliferation and survival of malignant cells. The IC50 values for FGFR 1, 2, and 3 were less than 2 nanoMolar; pemigatinib also inhibited FGFR4 in vitro at a concentration approximately 100 times higher than those that inhibit FGFR 1, 2, and 3. It exhibited anti-tumor activity in mouse xenograft models of human tumors with FGFR1, FGFR2, or FGFR3 alterations resulting in constitutive FGFR activation including a patient-derived xenograft model of cholangiocarcinoma that expressed an oncogenic FGFR2-Transformer-2 beta homolog (TRAb2) fusion protein and a patient-derived KG1 leukemia model that carries a translocation of FGFR1 (FGFR1OP2-FGFR1).

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics

    Oral Route
    Description
    The geometric mean steady-state pemigatinib AUC was 2,620 nanoMolar x hour (CV, 54%) and Cmax was 236 nanoMolar (CV, 56%) for a dose of 13.5 mg once daily. The median time to achieve peak plasma concentrations (Tmax) was 1.13 hours (range, 0.5 to 6 hours). Steady-state concentrations increased proportionally over a range of 1 to 20 mg. With repeated once-daily dosing, the median accumulation ratio of pemigatinib was 1.63 (range, 0.63 to 3.28).
The administration of pemigatinib with a high-fat and high-calorie meal (approximately 1,000 calories; protein, 150 calories; carbohydrate, 250 calories; fat, 500 to 600 calories) did not have a clinically significant effect on the pharmacokinetics of pemigatinib.

  Text
  Pemigatinib is administered orally. It is 90.6% bound to human plasma proteins in vitro, independent of concentration. The estimated apparent volume of distribution was 235 liters (CV, 60.8%) after a 13.5 mg oral dose of pemigatinib. The geometric mean elimination half-life of pemigatinib was 15.4 hours (CV, 51.6%) and the geometric mean apparent clearance was 10.6 liters/hour (CV, 54%). Steady-state was achieved within 4 days following repeated once-daily dosing. After a single radiolabeled dose of pemigatinib (11 mg), 82.4% of the dose was recovered in feces (unchanged drug, 1.4%), and 12.6% in urine (unchanged drug, 1%).
 
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes: CYP3A4, P-glycoprotein (P-gp), BCRP, OCT2, MATE1
In vitro, pemigatinib is predominantly metabolized by CYP3A4. Administration of pemigatinib with strong and moderate CYP3A inducers should be avoided; a dose reduction is necessary if concomitant strong and moderate CYP3A inhibitors is necessary. In a human mass balance study, the major drug-related moiety in plasma was unchanged pemigatinib. Pemigatinib is a substrate of both P-gp and BCRP in vitro, although P-gp or BCRP inhibitors are not expected to affect pemigatinib exposure at clinically relevant concentrations. In vitro, pemigatinib also inhibits P-gp, OCT2, and MATE1.

Dosage Adjustment Guidelines
  Hepatic Impairment
  Mild (total bilirubin 1.1 to 1.5 times the upper limit of normal [ULN] or AST greater than ULN) or moderate (total bilirubin 1.6 to 3 times ULN with any AST) hepatic impairment: No dosage adjustment is recommended.Severe hepatic impairment (total bilirubin greater than 3 times ULN with any AST): Reduce the dose of pemigatinib to 9 mg PO once daily for first 14 days of each 21-day cycle in patients with cholangiocarcinoma OR 9 mg PO once daily in patients with myeloid/lymphoid neoplasms.
  Renal Impairment
  Mild to moderate renal impairment (estimated glomerular filtration rate [eGFR]*, 30 to 89 mL/min/1.73 m2): No dosage adjustment is recommended.Severe renal impairment (eGFR, 15 to 29 mL/min/1.73 m2): Reduce the dose of pemigatinib to 9 mg PO once daily for first 14 days of each 21-day cycle in patients with cholangiocarcinoma OR 9 mg PO once daily in patients with myeloid/lymphoid neoplasms.End-stage renal disease (eGFR, less than 15 mL/min/1.73 m2) on intermittent hemodialysis: No dosage adjustment is recommended.
*eGFR was estimated using the Modification of Diet in Renal Disease equation.
