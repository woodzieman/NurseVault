          3.2 mg/m2 IV over 60 minutes on day 1, every 21 days until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity. Treatment of progressive, metastatic SCLC with lurbinectedin resulted in an investigator-assessed partial response rate of 45% for a median duration of 6.2 months in patients whose chemotherapy-free interval was 90 days or more in one cohort of a multicenter, noncomparative study; 44% of patients remained in a partial response for 6 months or more. In patients with a chemotherapy-free interval of fewer than 90 days, the investigator-assessed partial response rate was 22% for a median duration of 4.7 months; 10% of patients remained in a partial response for 6 months or more.

Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  Contraindications And Precautions
    Headline
    Drug Interactions
    General Information
    The coadministration of certain medications may lead to harm and require avoidance or therapy modification; review all drug interactions prior to concomitant use of other medications.
    Headline
    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components.
    Headline
    neutropenia, thrombocytopenia
    General Information
    Do not administer lurbinectedin to patients with neutropenia (ANC less than 1,500 cells/mm3) or thrombocytopenia (platelets less than 100,000 cells/mm3); treatment can cause myelosuppression. Monitor complete blood counts with differential prior to each administration of lurbinectedin; the use of G-CSF is recommended for patients with a neutrophil count less than 500 cells/mm3 or any value less than the lower limit of normal. An interruption of therapy, dose reduction, or discontinuation of therapy may be necessary for patients who develop neutropenia or thrombocytopenia.
    Headline
    Child-Pugh class B, Child-Pugh class C, hepatic failure
    General Information
    Avoid the use of lurbinectedin in patients with severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh class C; total bilirubin greater than 3 times the upper limit of normal [ULN]); if treatment with lurbinectedin is unavoidable, a dose reduction is necessary. Additionally, reduce the dose of lurbinectedin for patients with moderate hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh class B; total bilirubin greater than 1.5 to 3 times ULN and any AST). Monitor for an increase in the incidence and severity of treatment-related adverse reactions in these patients; lurbinectedin has not been studied in patients with hepatic failure. Additionally, lurbinectedin can cause hepatotoxicity; monitor liver function tests prior to initiating therapy, periodically during treatment, and as clinically indicated. An interruption of therapy, dose reduction, or discontinuation of therapy may be necessary for patients who develop hepatic impairment.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Avoid the use of lurbinectedin during pregnancy. Lurbinectedin may cause fetal harm, based on findings from animal data and its mechanism of action. Intravenous administration of a single lurbinectedin dose (approximately 0.2 times the 3.2 mg/m2 clinical dose) to pregnant rats during organogenesis resulted in 100% post-implantation loss.
    Headline
    people who can cause pregnancy in others, people who may become pregnant, reproductive risk
    General Information
    Counsel people who may become pregnant and people who can cause pregnancy in others about the reproductive risk associated with lurbinectedin. Lurbinectedin may cause embryo-lethality. Verify pregnancy status prior to treatment initiation. Advise people who may become pregnant to use effective contraception while taking lurbinectedin and for 7 months after the last dose. For those that can cause pregnancy in others, contraception should be used during lurbinectedin treatment and for 4 months after the last dose.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Advise patients to avoid breast-feeding while taking lurbinectedin and for 2 weeks after the last dose. There are no data on the presence of lurbinectedin in human milk, its effects on the breastfed child, or its effects on milk production. However, lurbinectedin has the potential to cause serious side effects in the breastfed child.

    anemia
    General Information
    Lurbinectedin can cause severe and fatal bone marrow suppression including anemia. Grade 3 or 4 anemia occurred in 17% of patients with advanced solid tumors treated with lurbinectedin monotherapy in a pooled safety population. In patients with SCLC who received lurbinectedin, serious cases of anemia occurred in at least 3% of patients; the incidence was higher in patients older than 65 years of age (8%). Decreased hemoglobin from baseline occurred in 74% (grade 3 or 4, 10%) of patients with platinum-resistant SCLC treated with lurbinectedin. An interruption of therapy, dose reduction, or permanent discontinuation may be necessary based on severity of myelosuppression.

    Headline
    bone marrow suppression, leukopenia, lymphopenia, neutropenia
    General Information
    Lurbinectedin can cause severe and fatal bone marrow suppression including febrile neutropenia and sepsis, lymphopenia, and leukopenia. Grade 3 or 4 neutropenia occurred in 41% of patients with advanced solid tumors treated with single-agent lurbinectedin monotherapy in a pooled safety population; the median time to onset was 15 days and the median duration was 7 days. In patients with SCLC who received single-agent lurbinectedin, serious cases of neutropenia occurred in at least 3% of patients; the incidence was higher in patients older than 65 years of age (11%). Febrile neutropenia occurred in 7% of patients in the pooled safety population and in at least 3% of patients from the SCLC cohort; serious cases of neutropenic fever were also more common in geriatric patients (11%). A decrease from baseline in leukocytes (79%; grade 3 or 4, 29%) and lymphocytes (79%; grade 3 or 4, 43%) also occurred in single-agent lurbinectedin-treated patients with SCLC. Primary granulocyte-colony stimulating factor (G-CSF) was administered to 84% of patients with SCLC treated with lurbinectedin plus intravenous atezolizumab in the IMforte trial. Decreases from baseline in neutrophils occurred in 36% (grade 3 or 4, 18%) of patients treated with lurbinectedin plus intravenous atezolizumab in the IMforte trial with a median time to onset of grade 3 or 4 decrease in neutrophils of 31 days and a median duration of 10 days; febrile neutropenia occurred in 1.7% of patients and was fatal in 1 patient in the same population. A decrease from baseline in lymphocytes also occurred in 55% (grade 3 or 4, 17%) of patients treated with lurbinectedin plus intravenous atezolizumab in the IMforte trial. Administer prophylactic G-CSF with lurbinectedin in combination with atezolizumab or atezolizumab; hyaluronidase to reduce the risk of febrile neutropenia. Description
  Text
  Lurbinectedin is an intravenous alkylating agent. It is indicated for the treatment of metastatic small cell lung cancer (SCLC) with disease progression on or after platinum-based chemotherapy and in combination with atezolizumab or atezolizumab; hyaluronidase for the maintenance treatment of adult patients with extensive-stage SCLC whose disease has not progressed after first-line induction therapy with atezolizumab or atezolizumab; hyaluronidase, carboplatin, and etoposide. Treatment with lurbinectedin is associated with potentially severe myelosuppression; it is more common in patients 65 years of age or older. Monitor blood counts prior to each dose of lurbinectedin and do not initiate or continue treatment until the neutrophil count is at least 1,500 cells/mm3 and the platelet count is at least 100,000 cells/mm3.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Lurbinectedin is an alkylating agent that binds guanine residues in the minor groove of DNA, forming adducts and resulting in a bending of the DNA helix towards the major groove. Adduct formation triggers a cascade of events that can affect the subsequent activity of DNA binding proteins, including some transcription factors, and DNA repair pathways, resulting in perturbation of the cell cycle and eventual cell death. Lurbinectedin inhibited human monocyte activity in vitro and reduced macrophage infiltration in implanted tumors in mice.

Dosage Adjustment Guidelines
  Hepatic Impairment
  Baseline Hepatic Impairment
Mild hepatic impairment (AST more than the upper limit of normal [ULN] and total bilirubin within normal limits; OR total bilirubin 1.1 to 1.5 times ULN and any AST): No dosage adjustments are recommended.
Moderate hepatic impairment (total bilirubin more than 1.5 times to 3 times ULN and any AST): 1.6 mg/m2 IV every 21 days.
Severe hepatic impairment (total bilirubin more than 3 times ULN): Avoid lurbinectedin; if administration cannot be avoided, the recommended dose is 1.6 mg/m2 IV every 21 days.
Treatment-Related Hepatotoxicity
Grade 2: Hold lurbinectedin therapy. When hepatotoxicity resolves to grade 1 or less, resume treatment at the same dose. Permanently discontinue lurbinectedin treatment in patients unable to tolerate a dose of 2 mg/m2 or in patients requiring a dose delay of more than 2 weeks.
Grade 3: Hold lurbinectedin therapy. When hepatotoxicity resolves to grade 1 or less, resume treatment at a reduced dose or permanently discontinue therapy. Permanently discontinue lurbinectedin treatment in patients unable to tolerate a dose of 2 mg/m2 every 21 days or in patients requiring a dose delay of more than 2 weeks.
  Renal Impairment
  Specific guidelines for dosage adjustments in renal impairment are not available; it appears that no dosage adjustments are needed.
