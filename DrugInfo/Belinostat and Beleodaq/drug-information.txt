        Dosage Description
        1,000 mg/m2 IV over 30 minutes once daily on days 1, 2, 3, 4, and 5 repeated every 21 days until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity. The initial belinostat starting dose is 750 mg/m2 in patients who are homozygous for the UGT1A1*28 allele. Interruption, dose reduction, or discontinuation of therapy may be necessary in patients who develop toxicities. The absolute neutrophil count should be 1,000 cells/mm3 or greater and the platelet count should be 50,000 cells/mm3 or greater before the start of each new cycle. The overall response rate (ORR) (primary end point) assessed by an independent review committee was 25.8% following treatment with belinostat in patients with relapsed or refractory PTCL in a multinational, single-arm, phase II trial (n = 129; the BELIEF trial). The complete response (CR) was 10.8% and the median duration of response (DOR) was 8.4 months. Additionally, the median progression-free survival and overall survival times were 1.6 months and 7.9 months, respectively. In this trial, the median number of prior therapies was 2 (range, 1 to 8 therapies); 20.8% of patients had previously had a hematopoietic stem-cell transplant. The ORR was 25% in patients with relapsed or refractory PTCL who received belinostat (median of 2 cycles of therapy; range 1 to 9 cycles) in another multinational, phase II trial (n = 24); 8.3% of these patients achieved a CR. The median DOR was 109 days, and the median time to progression was 82 days. Patients in this study had received a median of 3 prior therapies (range, 1 to 9 therapies).

    Headline
    infection
    General Information
    Do not administer belinostat to patients with an active infection. Use caution in patients with a history of extensive or intensive chemotherapy, as they may be at higher risk of life-threatening infections. Serious and fatal infections, including pneumonia and sepsis, have occurred during treatment with belinostat.
    Headline
    Child-Pugh class B, Child-Pugh class C, hepatic failure
    General Information
    Avoid use of belinostat in patients with severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh class C) including hepatic failure. An initial dose reduction is required in patients with moderate (Child-Pugh class B) hepatic impairment (total bilirubin greater than 1.5 to 3 times ULN and any AST); these patients may be at increased risk of adverse reactions. Abnormal liver function tests and fatal hepatic toxicity have been observed in patients receiving belinostat. An interruption of therapy, dose reduction, or discontinuation of therapy may be necessary in patients who develop hepatic toxicity.
    Headline
    UGT1A1 poor metabolizer
    General Information
    Patients with a known UGT1A1 poor metabolizer status (e.g., patients with UGT1A1*28 allele) could have reduced belinostat clearance. An initial dose reduction is required in patients known to be homozygous for the UGT1A1*28 allele to minimize dose limiting toxicities.
    Headline
    renal failure, renal impairment
    General Information
    Avoid use of belinostat in patients with severe renal impairment (CrCl less than 30 mL/min) including renal failure. An initial dose reduction is required in patients with moderate renal impairment (CrCl 30 to 60 mL/min); these patients may be at increased risk of adverse reactions.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Based on its mechanism of action, belinostat can cause teratogenicity and/or embryo-fetal death if administered during pregnancy. Reproductive animal studies have not been conducted with belinostat and there is no data of belinostat use during human pregnancy; however, it is a genotoxic drug that actively targets dividing cells.
    Headline
    people who can cause pregnancy in others, people who may become pregnant, reproductive risk
    General Information
    Counsel people who may become pregnant and people who can cause pregnancy in others about the reproductive risk associated with belinostat. Belinostat may cause fetal harm. Verify pregnancy status prior to treatment initiation. Advise people who may become pregnant to use effective contraception while taking belinostat and for 6 months after the last dose. For those that can cause pregnancy in others, contraception should be used during belinostat treatment and for 3 months after the last dose. Belinostat may cause male infertility, based on data from animal studies. It is not known if infertility is reversible.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Advise patients to avoid breast-feeding while taking belinostat and for 2 weeks after the last dose. There are no data on the presence of belinostat in human milk, its effects on the breast-fed child, or its effects on milk production. However, belinostat has the potential to cause serious side effects in the breast-fed child.

Description
  Text
  Belinostat (PXD-101) is a class I/II intravenous histone deacetylase (HDAC) inhibitor. HDAC plays an important role in the epigenetic silencing of tumor suppressor genes. The overall response rate was 25% following a median of 2 cycles of belinostat in 24 patients with relapsed or refractory peripheral T-cell lymphoma (PTCL) in a multinational, 2-cohort, phase II trial. In July 2014, belinostat (Beleodaq) was approved by the FDA for the treatment of relapsed or refractory PTCL. It was an accelerated approval based on tumor response rate and duration of response; however, continued approval may be dependent upon verification and description of clinical benefit in confirmatory trials.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Belinostat is a class I and II inhibitor of the histone deacetylase (HDAC) family of enzymes. HDACs are enzymes that catalyze the removal of acetyl groups from the lysine residues of proteins, including histones and transcription factors. Overexpression of HDACs or an abnormal recruitment of HDACs to oncogenic transcription factors is present in some cancer cells. This causes hypoacetylation of core nucleosomal histones resulting in a condensed chromatin structure and repression of gene transcription. Inhibition of HDAC activity produces an accumulation of acetyl groups on the histone lysine residues resulting in an open chromatin structure and transcriptional activation. In many different malignant cell lines, HDAC inhibitors have been shown to activate differentiation, inhibit the cell cycle, and induce apoptosis. In vivo, HDAC inhibitors have exhibited stimulation of the immune system and blockage of angiogenesis. In vitro, belinostat has shown preferential cytotoxicity towards tumor cells compared to normal cells, inducing cell cycle arrest and/or apoptosis of some transformed cells at nanomolar concentrations (< 250 nanomolar).

Pharmacokinetics
  Special Populations
    Name
    Hepatic Impairment
    Description
    Steady state clearance of belinostat decreased and AUC increased as a function of the degree of liver dysfunction; Cmax, half-life and apparent volume of distribution remained unchanged. Increases in belinostat exposure were correlated with worsening liver function. Mean belinostat clearance was reduced by 33% in severe hepatic impairment (total bilirubin more than 3 to 10 times upper limit of normal [ULN]), 24% in moderate hepatic impairment (total bilirubin more than 1.5 to 3 times ULN and any AST), and 18% in mild hepatic impairment (total bilirubin with normal limits and any AST or total bilirubin 1 to 1.5 times ULN).
    Name
    Renal Impairment
    Description
    In patients with mild renal impairment (CrCl 60 to 90 mL/min), belinostat AUC increased by 1.3-fold and Cmax increased by 1.7-fold while total clearance was unchanged. In patients with moderate renal impairment (CrCl 30 to less than 60 mL/min), belinostat AUC and Cmax each increased by 1.7-fold and total clearance was reduced by 26%. The effect of severe renal impairment (CrCl less than 30 mL/min) on belinostat pharmacokinetics is unknown.
  Text
  Belinostat is administered intravenously. It has limited tissue distribution, with 92.9% to 95.8% of drug bound to plasma proteins. In pooled data from a phase I/II trial, the total mean plasma clearance was 1,240 mL/min and the elimination half-life was 1.1 hours following belinostat doses ranging from 150 to 1,200 mg/m2. The total clearance approximates average hepatic blood flow (1,500 mL/min), suggesting high hepatic extraction. Belinostat is primarily metabolized in the liver via UGT1A1; CYP2A6, CYP2C9, and CYP3A4 are also involved in hepatic metabolism of belinostat. Identified metabolites include belinostat amide and belinostat acid, methyl belinostat, and 3-(anilinosulfonyl)-benzenecarboxylic acid (3-ASBA). Following a single, radiolabeled 1,500-mg dose of belinostat IV given over 30 minutes in patients with recurrent or progressive malignancy, the mean percent of radioactivity recovered over 168 hours were 9.7% +/- 6.5% and 84.8% +/- 9.8% in the feces and urine, respectively. Unchanged belinostat accounted for 1.7% of the radioactivity recovered in the urine.
 
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes or transporters: UGT1A1, P-gp, CYP2C8, CYP2C9
Belinostat is primarily metabolized by UGT1A1; avoid concomitant administration with strong UGT1A1 inhibitors. In vitro studies have demonstrated it also undergoes hepatic metabolism by CYP2A6, CYP2C9, and CYP3A4 and that belinostat and its metabolites inhibit CYP2C8 and CYP2C9. Belinostat is a P-glycoprotein (P-gp) substrate; it is not a P-gp inhibitor.

  Baseline Hepatic Impairment:
Mild hepatic impairment (total bilirubin 1.5 times the upper limit of normal [ULN] or less and any AST): No dosage adjustment is recommended.
Moderate hepatic impairment (total bilirubin more than 1.5 to 3 times ULN and any AST): 500 mg/m2 IV once daily on days 1, 2, 3, 4, and 5 of a 21-day cycle.
Severe hepatic impairment (total bilirubin more than 3 times ULN and any AST): Treatment with belinostat is not recommended.
Treatment-Related Hepatotoxicity
Grade 3 or 4 treatment-related hepatotoxicity: Hold therapy until the toxicity resolves to grade 2 or less; decrease the belinostat dose by 25% (e.g., from 1,000 mg/m2 to 750 mg/m2). Discontinue therapy in patients who have a recurrence of grade 3 or 4 toxicity following 2 dose reductions.
  Renal Impairment
  Baseline Renal Impairment
Mild renal impairment (CrCl 60 to less than 90 mL/min): No dosage adjustment is recommended.
Moderate renal impairment (CrCl 30 to less than 60 mL/min): 500 mg/m2 IV once daily on days 1, 2, 3, 4, and 5 of a 21-day cycle.
Severe renal impairment (CrCl less than 30 mL/min): Treatment with belinostat is not recommended.
