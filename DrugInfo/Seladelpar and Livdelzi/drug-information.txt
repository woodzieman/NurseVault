    Headline
    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components.
    Headline
    biliary obstruction
    General Information
    Avoid the use of seladelpar in individuals with complete biliary obstruction. Interrupt seladelpar use if biliary obstruction is suspected and treat as clinically indicated.
    Headline
    geriatric
    General Information
    Clinical studies using seladelpar did not include a sufficient number of geriatric adults 75 years of age and older to determine whether they respond differently than younger adults. Monitor individuals older than 75 years closely for adverse events.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    There are insufficient data on seladelpar use during human pregnancy to determine the risk of major birth defects, miscarriage, or other adverse pregnancy outcomes. In animal studies, no malformations or effects on embryo-fetal survival were observed in pregnant rats or rabbits at exposures up to 176 and 49 times, respectively, the recommended human dose based on AUC. Reduced fetal growth was observed in rabbits at 49 times the recommended human dose, but not at 3 times the recommended dose. In a rat pre- and postnatal development study, maternal administration from organogenesis through lactation was associated with reduced postnatal growth and pre-weaning survival in offspring at 115 times the recommended human dose based on AUC; these effects were not observed at 16 times the recommended dose. Report pregnancies to the manufacturer at 1-800-445-3235.
    Headline
    Child-Pugh class A, Child-Pugh class B, Child-Pugh class C, decompensated hepatic cirrhosis
    General Information
    Monitor individuals who are receiving seladelpar for hepatotoxicity. Obtain baseline laboratory levels at initiation and monitor thereafter according to routine patient management. Interrupt seladelpar treatment if liver enzyme levels worsen or the individual develops signs and symptoms of hepatitis (jaundice, right upper quadrant pain, eosinophilia). Consider permanent discontinuation if liver tests worsen after restarting seladelpar. While no dosage adjustment is needed in individuals with Child-Pugh class A hepatic impairment, monitor these individuals for evidence of hepatic decompensation during seladelpar treatment. Use of seladelpar is not recommended in people who have or develop decompensated hepatic cirrhosis (e.g., ascites, variceal bleeding, hepatic encephalopathy). Consider discontinuing seladelpar if the individual progresses to moderate or severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh class B or Child-Pugh class C) as safety and efficacy in these individuals have not been established.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Use seladelpar with caution during breast-feeding. There are no data on the presence of seladelpar or its metabolites in either human or animal milk, their effects on the breastfed child, or their effects on milk production. Consider the benefits of breast-feeding, the patient's clinical need for treatment, and any potential adverse effects on the breastfed child from the medication or from the patient's underlying medical condition.

    Headline
    elevated hepatic enzymes
    General Information
    Elevated hepatic enzymes have been associated with seladelpar use. Dose-related increases in AST and ALT at levels greater than 3 times the upper limit of normal in primary biliary cholangitis (PBC) patients receiving 50 mg once daily (5 times the recommended dose) and 200 mg once daily (20 times the recommended dose) have occurred. Transaminase levels returned to pre-treatment levels after seladelpar was discontinued. Using seladelpar 10 mg once daily did not show a pattern for an increase in transaminase levels. Obtain baseline laboratory values at initiation and monitor according to routine patient management. Interrupt seladelpar treatment if liver tests (ALT, AST, total bilirubin, alkaline phosphatase) worsen or if the patient develops signs and symptoms of clinical hepatitis (jaundice, right upper quadrant pain, eosinophilia). Consider permanent discontinuation if liver tests worsen after restarting seladelpar. A decrease in estimated glomerular filtration rate (up to 25% decrease) has been reported in 10% of patients receiving seladelpar during clinical trials.

Description
  Text
  Seladelpar is a peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor (PPAR)-delta agonist indicated for the treatment of primary biliary cholangitis (PBC) in combination with ursodeoxycholic acid (ursodiol or UDCA) in adults who have an inadequate response to UDCA, or as monotherapy in people unable to tolerate UDCA. This indication is approved under accelerated approval based on a reduction of alkaline phosphatase (ALP) during clinical trials. Improvement in survival or the prevention of liver decompensation events has not been demonstrated. Continued approval for these indications may be contingent upon verification of clinical benefit in confirmatory trials. Seladelpar is not recommended in patients who have developed decompensated cirrhosis (e.g., ascites, variceal bleeding, hepatic encephalopathy). Seladelpar was FDA-approved in August 2024.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Seladelpar is a peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor (PPAR)-delta agonist. The mechanism by which seladelpar works in patients with primary biliary cholangitis (PBC) is not well understood. The potential mechanism of action includes inhibition of bile acid synthesis through the activation of PPAR-delta receptor. PPAR-delta is a nuclear receptor expressed in most tissues, including the liver. Studies have shown PPAR-delta activation by seladelpar reduces bile acid synthesis through Fibroblast Growth Factor 21 dependent down-regulation of CYP7A1, the key enzyme for the synthesis of bile acids from cholesterol.
Seladelpar treatment results in a reduction of alkaline phosphatase (ALP). In patients with PBC treated with 10 mg/day of seladelpar, a greater reduction in mean alkaline phosphatase (ALP) from baseline was observed as early as 1 month after treatment compared to the placebo group and lower ALP was generally maintained through month 12. In another study in which patients with PBC were treated with 2, 5, or 10 mg/day of seladelpar, a dose dependent reduction in mean ALP was observed. Bilirubin and alkaline phosphatase (ALP) concentrations are often used to monitor patients with PBC, even though the relationship of these markers to clinical outcomes, including mortality, has not been well-defined.

    Description
    The median Tmax was 1.5 hours for seladelpar. A high-fat meal did not produce any clinically significant differences in seladelpar pharmacokinetics. Seladelpar steady-state was achieved by day 4 and AUC increase was less than 30% following once daily dosing. The mean Cmax and AUC were 90.5 ng/mL and 817 ng/mL x hour, respectively. CYP2C9 activity is decreased in individuals with genetic variants such as CYP2C9*2 and CYP2C9*3. Approximately 2% to 3% of White subjects, 0.5% to 4% of Asian subjects, and less than 1 % of African Americans lack the capacity to metabolize CYP2C9 substrates and are classified as poor metabolizers (PMs).   Text
  Seladelpar is administered orally. Seladelpar plasma protein binding is greater than 99%. The Vd of seladelpar is approximately 133.2 L. Seladelpar is primarily metabolized by the liver into 3 major metabolites, seladelpar sulfoxide (M1), desethyl-seladelpar (M2), and desethyl-seladelpar sulfoxide (M3). None of the major metabolites have pharmacological activity. Approximately 73% of a seladelpar dose is eliminated in the urine (0.01% unchanged) and 20% (2% unchanged) in the feces, primarily as metabolites. The oral clearance of seladelpar is 12 L/hour. In primary biliary cirrhosis (PBC) subjects, the half-life of seladelpar was 3.8 to 6.7 hours.
 
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes and drug transporters: CYP2C8, CYP2C9, CYP3A4, BCRP, P-gp, OAT3, OATP1B1, OATP1B3
Seladelpar is a substrate CYP2C8, CYP2C9, CYP3A4, and other transporters including BCRP, P-gp, OAT3, OATP1B1, and OATP1B3 during in vitro studies. Seladelpar is not a substrate of MATE1, MATE2-K, OAT1, OCT1, or OCT2 transporters. In vitro, seladelpar did not inhibit CYP enzymes (1A2, 2B6, 2C8, 2C19, 2D6, 3A4), UGTs, P-gp, MATEs, OAT1, OAT3, OCT1, or OCT2.

Dosage Adjustment Guidelines
  Hepatic Impairment
  No dosage adjustment is recommended for PBC patients with mild hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh A). Use of seladelpar is not recommended in patients who have or develop decompensated cirrhosis (ascites, variceal bleeding, hepatic encephalopathy). Monitor patients with cirrhosis for evidence of decompensation. Consider discontinuing seladelpar if the patient progresses to moderate or severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh B or C).
  Renal Impairment
  No dosage adjustment is needed in patients with mild, moderate, or severe renal impairment. Patients with end-stage renal disease on dialysis have not been studied.
