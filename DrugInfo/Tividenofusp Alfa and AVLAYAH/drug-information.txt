    serious hypersensitivity reactions or anaphylaxis
    General Information
    Serious hypersensitivity reactions or anaphylaxis have been reported with tividenofusp alfa and other enzyme replacement therapies. Anaphylaxis has occurred during the early course of enzyme replacement therapy and after an extended duration of therapy. Administer under the supervision of a health care provider knowledgeable in the management of hypersensitivity reactions. Initiate therapy in a health care setting with appropriate medical monitoring and support measures, including cardiopulmonary resuscitation equipment. Inform individuals receiving tividenofusp alfa or their caregivers of the symptoms of severe hypersensitivity reactions, including anaphylaxis, and to seek immediate medical attention if symptoms occur. If a severe hypersensitivity reaction or infusion-associated reaction (IAR) occurs, discontinue tividenofusp alfa immediately and initiate appropriate medical treatment, including epinephrine. Consider the risks and benefits of readministering tividenofusp alfa after a severe reaction and monitor individuals closely if the decision is made to readminister treatment. Consider reducing the infusion rate and/or dose with readministration. In the event of a mild to moderate hypersensitivity reaction or IAR, temporarily hold the infusion and/or reduce the infusion rate, then titrate up to the recommended infusion rate as tolerated. If the dose has been decreased due to an adverse reaction, evaluate when it is appropriate to increase the dose and follow the recommended dose escalation regimen to achieve a maintenance dose of 15 mg/kg once weekly.

Description
  Text
  Tividenofusp alfa is a hydrolytic lysosomal glycosaminoglycan (GAG)-specific enzyme indicated for the treatment of neurologic manifestations of mucopolysaccharidosis II (Hunter syndrome) when initiated in presymptomatic or symptomatic individuals prior to advanced neurologic impairment. Individuals with this syndrome have a genetic deficiency of iduronate-2-sulfatase (IDS), a lysosomal enzyme. Deficiency of IDS causes an accumulation of glycosaminoglycans (GAGs) in cells throughout the body. The buildup of these GAGs produces a variety of physical and neurological problems including abnormal joint function, respiratory problems, learning disabilities, and hearing loss. Tividenofusp alfa provides an exogenous source of IDS to replace deficient enzyme activity and promote degradation of accumulated heparan sulfate (HS) and dermatan sulfate. In clinical trials, treatment with tividenofusp alfa resulted in a mean 91% reduction in CSF HS from baseline and 93% of individuals had CSF HS concentrations below the upper limit of normal by Week 24. Anaphylaxis has occurred with tividenofusp alfa administration. Therapy must be initiated in a health care setting with appropriate medical monitoring and support measures, including access to cardiopulmonary resuscitation equipment. Tividenofusp alfa was FDA-approved in March 2026.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Tividenofusp alfa is a recombinant fusion protein designed to replace deficient iduronate-2-sulfatase (IDS) activity in individuals with Hunter syndrome. Hunter syndrome is an X-linked lysosomal storage disorder characterized by impaired degradation of heparan sulfate (HS) and dermatan sulfate (DS), with consequent glycosaminoglycan (GAG) accumulation in multiple organs and tissues, including the central nervous system. The molecule combines IDS with an Fc domain that binds the transferrin receptor, enabling receptor-mediated transport across the blood-brain barrier as well as delivery to peripheral tissues. After cellular uptake, tividenofusp alfa is internalized via the mannose-6-phosphate receptor and transported to lysosomes, where it is expected to restore enzymatic catabolism of accumulated substrates and reduce pathologic glycosaminoglycan burden.
 
Pharmacodynamics
In clinical studies, tividenofusp alfa treatment was associated with reductions in CSF HS and with decreases from baseline in urinary HS (86%), DS (91%), and total urine GAGs (57%), with many individuals reaching total urine GAG concentrations below the upper limit of normal by Week 24. Higher serum tividenofusp alfa exposure appeared to be associated with greater reductions in CSF and urine HS, with the maximal effect achieved at the recommended maintenance dosage of 15 mg/kg once weekly. However, the relationship between changes in CSF HS, urine HS, urine DS, and total urine GAG levels and the clinical response has not been established, and the time to maximal pharmacodynamic effect and the exposure-response relationship for safety and effectiveness remain incompletely characterized.

    Description
    The pharmacokinetics of tividenofusp was evaluated in pediatric patients with Hunter syndrome aged 3 months to 13 years. The Cmax increased proportionally with dose, while the AUC increased in a greater-than-dose proportional manner across the dose range of 3 to 30 mg/kg (0.2 to 2 times the approved maintenance dosage). From Week 1 (recommended starting dose) to Week 24 (recommended maintenance dose), the geometric mean (range) Cmax was 33.1 (19.9 to 50.3) mcg/mL and 204 (19.5 to 615) mcg/mL, respectively; the geometric mean (range) AUC was 277 (82.4 to 446) mcg x hour/mL and 3,000 (839 to 12,100) mcg x hour/mL, respectively.

Preparation of Infusion
Use polypropylene syringes and infusion bags composed of polyvinylchloride (PVC) or polyolefins (PO), such as polyethylene (PE) and polypropylene (PP).
Determine the number of tividenofusp alfa vials to be reconstituted based on the individual's weight. Round the number of vials up to the next whole number.
Remove vials from the refrigerator and allow to warm to room temperature for 15 to 30 minutes. Do not use an external source of heat to warm the vials.
Reconstitute each vial with 5.2 mL of Sterile Water for Injection. Inject diluent onto the side of each vial to avoid foaming. Do not inject forcefully or directly onto the powder.
Gently swirl each vial to completely dissolve the powder. Do not invert or shake the vial. Each vial will contain 30 mg/mL of tividenofusp alfa.
The solution should be clear to slightly opalescent and colorless to slightly brown or yellow, and free of visible particles. Discard if cloudy, discolored, or contains particles.
Dilute the reconstituted tividenofusp alfa solution in 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection to a final concentration between 0.6 and 15 mg/mL.
Determine the appropriate volume of the infusion bag based on individual's weight.
Remove any airspace within the infusion bag.
Withdraw a volume of 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection from the infusion bag equivalent to the volume of tividenofusp alfa to be added.
Slowly withdraw the required volume of reconstituted solution from the tividenofusp alfa vial(s). Do not administer more than 1 dose from each vial.
Slowly inject the required volume into the infusion bag. Avoid introducing air into the bag.
Gently invert the infusion bag to mix the solution. Do not shake.
Storage: May store reconstituted tividenofusp alfa vials at room temperature for up to 4 hours. Do not shake or freeze. If the diluted tividenofusp alfa infusion bag is not used immediately, it may be stored in the refrigerator at 2 to 8 degrees C (36 to 46 degrees F) for up to 24 hours. After removal of the diluted solution from the refrigerator, completely infuse within 10 hours. Do not store back into the refrigerator. Discard the diluted solution if refrigerated more than 24 hours or if the diluted solution cannot be completely infused within 10 hours after removal from the refrigerator. Do not shake and do not freeze.
 
Intravenous Infusion
Infuse using only infusion sets composed of PVC or PE and filter membranes composed of polyethersulfone (PES).
If refrigerated, allow the solution to warm to room temperature prior to infusion.
Use a dedicated infusion line equipped with a sterile, non-pyrogenic, low protein-binding, 0.2 micron, in-line filter to administer tividenofusp alfa.
Infuse over approximately 4 hours. Increase the initial infusion rate to the subsequent infusion rate every hour based on individual tolerance. The total infusion time should not exceed 8 hours.
Total infusion volume 25 mL: Infuse at a rate of 2.5 mL/hour for the first hour, 5 mL/hour for the second hour, 10 mL/hour for the third hour to completion.
Total infusion volume 50 mL: Infuse at a rate of 5 mL/hour for the first hour, 10 mL/hour for the second hour, 20 mL/hour for the third hour to completion.
Total infusion volume 100 mL: Infuse at a rate of 10 mL/hour for the first hour, 20 mL/hour for the second hour, 40 mL/hour for the third hour to completion.
Total infusion volume 250 mL: Infuse at a rate of 25 mL/hour for the first hour, 50 mL/hour for the second hour, 100 mL/hour for the third hour to completion.
Discontinue tividenofusp alfa if a severe hypersensitivity reaction or severe infusion-associated reaction (IAR) occurs and immediately initiate appropriate medical treatment. If the decision to readminister is made, re-evaluate pretreatment medications, slow the infusion rate, and/or reduce the dose. If a mild to moderate hypersensitivity reaction or mild to moderate IAR occurs, temporarily hold the infusion and/or reduce the infusion rate by at least 50% from the current rate, then titrate up to the recommended infusion rate as tolerated.
Flush the line at the end of the infusion with 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection using the final infusion rate that was used to administer tividenofusp alfa.
Do not administer tividenofusp alfa in the same intravenous infusion line with other products.
Missed dose: If a dose is missed, skip the missed dose. Do not double the dose. Restart tividenofusp alfa treatment as soon as possible, maintaining the 1-week interval between doses thereafter. Resume dosing at the last administered dosage following the recommended infusion rate.
