    General Information
    There is a potential for immunogenicity with beremagene geperpavec treatment. Antibody formation against the viral vector (HSV-1) and transgene protein (COL7) was evaluated in a subset of subjects in the randomized, intra-subject placebo-controlled clinical study. Description
  Text
  Beremagene geperpavec is a herpes-simplex virus type 1 (HSV-1) vector-based topical gene therapy indicated for the treatment of wounds in individuals with dystrophic epidermolysis bullosa (DEB). Mutations in the collagen type VII alpha 1 chain (COL7A1) gene cause DEB by preventing the formation of anchoring fibrils (COL7), which are essential molecules for maintaining the integrity of the skin and to hold the epidermis and dermis together. Beremagene geperpavec increases COL7 production and secretion, which can improve wound healing in patients with dystrophic epidermolysis bullosa. Patients with autosomal dominant DEB (DDEB) have lower than normal functional anchoring fibrils, and patients with autosomal recessive DEB (RDEB) have no functioning anchoring fibrils. The efficacy of beremagene geperpavec was established in a randomized, double-blind, intra-subject placebo-controlled trial, in which beremagene geperpavec was compared to placebo for 26 weeks in patients with DEB (n = 31; 30 patients with RDEB and 1 patient with DDEB). At 10 weeks, 68% of patients treated with beremagene geperpavec had complete wound closure, compared to 23% of patients treated with placebo (treatment difference, 45% (95% CI: 22% to 69%); p= 0.003). At 24 weeks, 65% of patients treated with beremagene geperpavec had 100% complete wound closure, compared to 26% of placebo-treated patients (treatment difference, 39% (95% CI: 14% to 63%); p= 0.012). Patients and caregivers should be advised to avoid contact with treated wounds and dressings after treatment. Beremagene geperpavec was FDA-approved in 2023.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Beremagene geperpavec is a live, replication defective, HSV-1 based vector that has been genetically modified to express the human type VII collagen (COL7) protein. Upon topical application, beremagene geperpavec can transduce both keratinocytes and fibroblasts. Following entry of beremagene geperpavec into the cells, the vector genome is deposited in the nucleus to transcribe collagen type VII alpha 1 chain (COL7A1) genes. The resulting transcription allows for production and secretion of COL7 by the cell in its mature form. These COL7 molecules arrange themselves into long, thin bundles that form anchoring fibrils, which hold the epidermis and dermis together and are essential for maintaining the integrity of the skin. Dystrophic epidermolysis bullosa (DEB) results from reduced or absent levels of COL7A1 caused by mutations in the COL7A1 gene.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Topical Route
    Description
    Following topical application of beremagene geperpavec in clinical studies (n = 9), viral vector DNA was detected in skin swab samples in all patients with maximum concentrations ranging from 5.1 x 10^4 to 4.1 x 10^8 vector genomes. Negative shedding was confirmed in 6 patients with three measurements below the limit of detection within 8 weeks of treatment with beremagene geperpavec. During a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial (n = 31), skin swabs from 19 of the 31 subjects (61%) were positive for viral vector following treatment with beremagene geperpavec. Negative shedding from skin swabs was achieved in 16 out of 19 patients within 6 weeks of treatment with beremagene geperpavec. Most wound dressings (94%, 29/31) contained a range of detectable vector genomes, but no extracellular infectious particles were detected on the skin surface after beremagene geperpavec application. During a clinical study, linear deposition of the non-collagenous domain 1 and domain 2 of the type VII alpha 1 chain gene was observed at the dermal-epidermal junction in skin biopsies harvested after beremagene geperpavec treatment.
  Text
  Beremagene geperpavec is administered topically. Specific pharmacokinetic information is not available. The potential for systemic exposure to beremagene geperpavec was minimal. During a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial (n = 31), beremagene geperpavec genomes were below the limit of detection in all blood samples, and all but 1 urine sample. Viral vector DNA was not detected in blood or urine in initial clinical studies.

Administration
  General Information
  For storage information, see the specific product information within the How Supplied section.
Beremagene geperpavec may be administered by a health care professional, patient, or caregiver either in a health care setting or in a home setting.
Follow universal biohazard precautions for handling.
Avoid direct contact with beremagene geperpavec-treated wounds and dressings until next dressing change following treatment application. Wear protective gloves during beremagene geperpavec application, wound dressing change, and wound dressing disposal. In the event of beremagene geperpavec accidental exposure, flush the affected area with clean water for at least 15 minutes.
Individuals who are pregnant should not prepare or apply beremagene geperpavec and avoid all direct contact with treated wounds and/or dressings.

Gel Mixture Vial Preparation
Prior to handling beremagene geperpavec, wash hands thoroughly and wear protective gloves.
Remove both beremagene geperpavec and excipient gel vial from carton and thaw at room temperature for at least 20 minutes.
Prior to mixing, visually inspect that both vials are completely thawed and in liquid form. Beremagene geperpavec is an opalescent yellow to colorless suspension and the excipient gel is a clear viscous gel solution.
Invert beremagene geperpavec suspension vial 4 to 5 times. Do not invert the vial containing excipient gel.
Remove the vial caps and clean each stopper with a 70% isopropyl alcohol pad.
Using aseptic technique, attach an 18-guage needle to the 3 mL preparation syringe.
Hold the beremagene geperpavec suspension vial at 45 to 90 degrees and withdraw 1 mL of suspension into the preparation syringe.
Without engaging the safety lock, remove the preparation syringe and needle from the vial and discard the vial into the biohazard waste.
Transfer the entire contents of beremagene geperpavec suspension from the preparation syringe to the vial containing excipient gel. Without removing the needle from the vial, remove the air from above the liquid by pulling back the plunger to the 1 mL mark.
Remove the preparation syringe and needle from the vial and engage the safety lock; discard syringe and needle in the biohazard waste.
Place a 70% isopropyl alcohol pad on top of the prepared vial stopper, hold tightly in place, and shake the vial vigorously for 10 seconds.
The final mixture of beremagene geperpavec gel contains 5 x 10^9 plaque forming units (PFU) in a volume of 2.5 mL.
Storage: Beremagene geperpavec gel mixture does not contain any preservatives. Discard after 8 hours at room temperature (20 to 25 degrees C [68 to 77 degrees F]) or after 48 hours refrigerated (2 to 8 degrees C [35.6 to 46.4 degrees F]).
 
Administration Syringe Preparation
Determine the volume of gel mixture and the number of administration syringes needed based on the wound area size. Each prepared 1 mL administration syringe contains 0.5 mL of beremagene geperpavec gel.
Connect an 18-guage needle to the 1 mL administration syringe and insert needle into the vial containing beremagene geperpavec gel mixture.
Tilt the vial 45 to 90 degrees and withdraw 0.5 mL of beremagene geperpavec gel mixture. Do not remove the needle from the vial; lift the tip above the gel mixture and disconnect the 1 mL syringe containing beremagene geperpavec mixture and leave the needle in the vial stopper.
Air pockets may form in the administration syringe; do not flick the syringe to remove the air pocket. Manipulate the plunger up and down until all air pockets have been removed.
Place a syringe cap on the syringe containing beremagene geperpavec and place the capped syringe into a sealable bag.
If additional administration syringes are required, the process can be repeated up to 3 times by attaching a new 1 mL administration syringe to the needle in the beremagene geperpavec gel vial stopper. Cap each additional syringe and place in the sealable bag. Label each syringe as syringe #1, #2, #3, and #4.
Discard any unused remaining gel mixture remaining in the vial and the attached needle into a biohazard container.
Storage: Store filled and capped administration syringes in a sealable bag and place the bag into an insulated secondary container at 2 to 8 degrees C (35.6 to 46.4 degrees F) for transportation to the site of administration. Discard administration syringes after 8 hours at room temperature (20 to 25 degrees C [68 to 77 degrees F]) or after 48 hours refrigerated (2 to 8 degrees C [35.6 to 46.4 degrees F]).
 
Topical Gel Administration
Wear protective gloves during administration and disposal.
Remove the cap from the administration syringe.
Apply evenly spaced droplets of beremagene geperpavec to selected wounds 1 cm apart in a grid like pattern. Avoid contact of the syringe with the skin.

Apply total beremagene geperpavec dose based on the wound area in cm2. Apply beremagene geperpavec to wounds until they are closed before selecting a new wound to treat. Prioritize previously treated wounds if they re-open.

Wound area 20 to 40 cm2: 0.4 mL containing 8 x 10^8 PFU beremagene geperpavec once weekly.
Wound area 40 to 60 cm2: 0.6 mL containing 1.2 x 10^9 PFU beremagene geperpavec once weekly.
Wound area greater than 60 cm2
Adults and Pediatric patients 3 years and older: Maximum 2 mL containing 4 x 10^9 PFU beremagene geperpavec once weekly.
Pediatric patients younger than 3 years: Maximum 1 mL containing 2 x 10^9 PFU beremagene geperpavec once weekly.
Cut a hydrophobic dressing slightly larger than the wound and place on top of the beremagene geperpavec treated wound.
Cut a standard dressing slightly larger than the hydrophobic dressing and place on top of the hydrophobic dressing.
The dressing should be left in place until the next dressing change following beremagene geperpavec application. Once the dressings are removed, the individual may continue with their standard of care.
Discard any unused administration syringes and all materials that may have come in contact with beremagene geperpavec suspension or gel into a biohazard container.
Missed dose: If a dose is missed, apply beremagene geperpavec as soon as possible and resume weekly dosing thereafter.
