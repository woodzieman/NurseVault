Drug Class: Pegaspargase is an asparagine specific enzyme that is indicated for the first-line treatment of acute lymphoblastic leukemia (ALL) in adults and pediatric patients and ALL in adults and pediatric patients who experienced hypersensitivity to native forms of L-asparaginase.

Routes: injection (IV/IM/SC); Common adult doses: Older than 21 years: 2,000 units/m2 IV or IM repeated no sooner than every 14 days.21 years and younger: 2,500 units/m2 IV or IM repeated no sooner than every 14 days.

Common Side Effects (excluding routine GI upset): encephalopathy, hyperammonemia, hyperamylasemia, pancreatitis, elevated hepatic enzymes, hyperbilirubinemia, hypoalbuminemia, sinusoidal obstruction syndrome (sos), veno-occlusive disease (vod), bleeding, coagulopathy, disseminated intravascular coagulation (dic), epistaxis, gi bleeding, hematoma, intracranial bleeding, prolonged bleeding time, anaphylactic shock, anaphylactoid reactions, bronchospasm, erythema, hypotension, infusion-related reactions, laryngeal edema, rash, urticaria, colitis, enterocolitis

In an Overdose: No overdose/antidote information is listed in this file.

Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  General Information
  NOTE: L-Asparaginase is discussed in a separate monograph.
 
Pegaspargase is contraindicated in patients who have had previous serious allergic reactions (e.g., urticaria, acute bronchospasm, laryngeal edema, or hypotension) or other unacceptable reactions to pegaspargase. Patients previously hypersensitive to native L-asparaginase (i.e., those with E. coli protein hypersensitivity) may be treated with pegaspargase; however, they are at increased risk for allergic reactions compared to non-hypersensitive patients. Monitor patients for 1 hour following administration of pegaspargase in a setting that has resuscitation equipment and other agents necessary for the treatment of anaphylactic reactions (e.g., epinephrine, oxygen, intravenous corticosteroids, antihistamines) readily available. Discontinue pegaspargase treatment in patients who experience anaphylaxis or other serious allergic reactions. Of 128 patients who received pegaspargase intramuscularly, a hypersensitivity reaction occurred in 30% of patients previously hypersensitive to native L-asparaginase as compared with 11% of non-hypersensitive patients. Data are limited regarding hypersensitivity likelihood after intravenous pegaspargase administration.
 
Pegaspargase is contraindicated in patients with pancreatitis, who develop pancreatitis during treatment, or with a history of pancreatitis with prior L-asparaginase therapy. Evaluate patients with abdominal pain during pegaspargase therapy; pancreatitis can occur during pegaspargase therapy. Discontinue pegaspargase in patients who develop pancreatitis.
 
Pegaspargase is contraindicated in patients who have had significant hemorrhagic events (e.g., bleeding, coagulopathy, disseminated intravascular coagulation (DIC)) or thromboembolic disease, including coronary thrombosis or mesenteric thrombosis, associated with prior L-asparaginase therapy or experience such events during pegaspargase. Pegaspargase can cause impaired protein synthesis by the liver, leading to decreased production of insulin, albumin, lipoproteins, clotting factors, and endogenous anticoagulants such as proteins C and S and antithrombin III. Clinicians may expect to see coagulopathies, thrombosis, or bleeding due to impaired protein synthesis during pegaspargase therapy. Monitoring of coagulation parameters is warranted during pegaspargase therapy. Treatment with fresh-frozen plasma to replace coagulation factors may be necessary in patients with severe or symptomatic coagulopathy. Patients with hemophilia or who are receiving anticoagulant therapy, aspirin, or NSAIDs may be at increased risk for bleeding problems (see Interactions). Patients with limited hepatic synthetic function due to hepatic disease may be unable to tolerate the effects of pegaspargase. In addition, intramuscular injections may cause bleeding, bruising, or hematomas due to coagulopathy secondary to pegaspargase therapy.
 
Pegaspargase may be administered intravenously, and intravenous administration may reduce the number of injections needed to administer pegaspargase (see Administration). However, intramuscular administration is the preferred route of administration of pegaspargase because of the lower incidence of adverse effects such as hepatotoxicity, coagulopathy, gastrointestinal disorders, and renal disorders.
 
Patients with diabetes mellitus may be at increased risk of adverse reactions due to alteration in insulin production or pancreatic insult. Blood sugar should be closely monitored during pegaspargase therapy.
 
Pegaspargase is classified as FDA pregnancy-risk category C. Animal reproductive studies have not been performed with pegaspargase. It is also not known if pegaspargase can cause fetal harm when administered to a pregnant woman or can affect fertility. Pegaspargase should be given to a pregnant woman only if clearly needed.
 
It is uncertain if pegaspargase is distributed into breast milk. The potential for serious adverse effects on the infant indicates that breast-feeding should be discontinued during pegaspargase therapy.
 
Hyperkalemia, hyperphosphatemia, hyperuricemia, hypocalcemia, and decreased urine output may be indicative of pegaspargase-induced tumor lysis syndrome (TLS). Appropriate measures (e.g. aggressive hydration and allopurinol) must be taken to prevent severe electrolyte imbalances and renal toxicity during and following chemotherapy administration in patients with large chemosensitive tumors.
 
Pegaspargase has been reported to have immunosuppressive activity in animal models. The possibility that pegaspargase therapy may predispose patients to infections should be considered. Patients with a history of varicella zoster, other herpes infection (e.g., herpes simplex), or other viral infection are at risk for reactivation of the infection when treated with chemotherapy.
 
Use care to avoid accidental exposure to pegaspargase during preparation, handling, and administration. The use of protective gowns, gloves and goggles is recommended. Pegaspargase injection may be irritating to eyes, skin, and upper respiratory tract. Inhalation of dust or vapors and contact with skin or mucous membranes, especially ocular exposure, must be avoided. Should accidental ocular exposure occur, copious irrigation for at least 15 minutes with water, normal saline, or balance salt ophthalmic irrigating solution should be instituted immediately followed by prompt ophthalmologic examination. If accidental skin contact occurs, the affected area should be washed immediately with soap and water. If inhaled, remove from exposure and seek medical attention.
 
Vaccination during chemotherapy or radiation therapy should be avoided because the antibody response is suboptimal. When chemotherapy is being planned, vaccination should precede the initiation of chemotherapy by >= 2 weeks. The administration of live vaccines to immunocompromised patients should be avoided. Those undergoing chemotherapy should not be exposed to others who have recently received the oral poliovirus vaccine (OPV). Measles-mumps-rubella (MMR) vaccination is not contraindicated for the close contacts, including health care professionals, of immunocompromised patients. Passive immunoprophylaxis with immune globulins may be indicated for immunocompromised persons instead of, or in addition to, vaccination. When exposed to a vaccine-preventable disease such as measles, severely immunocompromised children should be considered susceptible regardless of their vaccination history.

    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components. Administer pegaspargase in a clinical setting that has resuscitation equipment and other treatment necessary to treat anaphylaxis (e.g., epinephrine, oxygen, IV steroids, and antihistamines).     Headline
    history of significant adverse event(s) associated with previous use, pancreatitis
    General Information
    Pegaspargase is contraindicated for use in patients with a history of significant adverse event(s) associated with previous use, including patients with a history of a serious bleeding event or serious thrombosis with prior asparaginase therapy. Use of pegaspargase is contraindicated in patients with a history of pancreatitis. Therapy interruption or permanent discontinuation may be necessary in patients who develop pancreatitis, bleeding, or clotting. Consider appropriate replacement therapy in patients with severe or symptomatic coagulopathy.
    Headline
    Child-Pugh class C, hepatic failure
    General Information
    Use of pegaspargase is contraindicated in patients who have a history of severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh class C) including hepatic failure. Obtain liver function tests (LFTs) prior to each dose and then at least weekly during cycles of therapy that include pegaspargase and for 6 weeks after the last pegaspargase dose; increased monitoring is recommended in patients who develop abnormal LFTs. Frequently monitor for signs and symptoms of hepatic veno-occlusive disease (e.g., rapid weight gain, fluid retention with ascites, hepatomegaly, rapid increase of bilirubin level). Therapy interruption or discontinuation may be necessary in patients who develop hepatotoxicity.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Pegaspargase may cause fetal harm when administered during pregnancy, based on animal data. There are published literature reports of fetal toxicity when pregnant rabbits received L-asparaginase or when pregnant rats were deprived of dietary asparagine. Human data is not available. Advise pregnant people of the potential fetal risk.
    Headline
    people who may become pregnant, reproductive risk
    General Information
    Counsel people who may become pregnant about the reproductive risk associated with pegaspargase. Verify pregnancy status prior to treatment initiation. Advise people who may become pregnant to use effective contraception while taking pegaspargase and for 3 months after the last dose.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Advise patients to avoid breast-feeding while taking pegaspargase and for 1 month after the last dose, due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in the breast-fed child. There are no data on the presence of pegaspargase in human milk, its effects on the breast-fed child, or its effects on milk production.

Methotrexate: (Major) L-Asparaginase with methotrexate has shown both therapeutic synergistic and antagonistic effects depending upon the schedule of administration of these agents. When methotrexate is given 3-24 hours prior to L-Asparaginase Escherichia coli, the L-asparaginase blocks the antifolate effects of methotrexate and decreases methotrexate toxicity. If L-asparaginase is given prior to methotrexate, the efficacy of methotrexate is decreased. This could be due to inhibition of protein synthesis preventing progression to the S-phase of the cell cycle. Alternatively, L-asparaginase pretreatment may inhibit methotrexate polyglutamation, which is required for intracellular retention of methotrexate. Cells are refractory to methotrexate for up to 10 days following a single dose of L-asparaginase. Adverse Reactions
  Headline
  Adverse Reactions
  General Information
  NOTE: L-Asparaginase is discussed in a separate monograph.
 
Serious and sometimes fatal hypersensitivity reactions including anaphylactic shock, hypotension, angioedema, or bronchospasm may occur during pegaspargase therapy. Other manifestations include anaphylactoid reactions, chills, edema, erythema, fever, dyspnea, laryngeal edema, pruritus, and rash (unspecified). Urticaria occurs in the majority of reported hypersensitivity reactions. Rarely, a serum sickness-type reaction with arthralgia, proteinuria, and fever may develop after an extended course of treatment. When used as part of first-line treatment of acute lymphocytic leukemia (ALL) during clinical trials, the incidence of clinical allergic reactions was 3% (2 of 58 patients); both occurred during the first delayed intensification phase of the study. The incidence of pegaspargase hypersensitivity reactions was 30% in patients with hypersensitivity to E. coli L-asparaginase and 11% in nohyperbilirubinemian-hypersensitive, previously treated patients when given IM. In 5 previously-treated, E. coli L-asparaginase hypersensitive patients, the incidence of allergic reactions was 60% when pegaspargase was administered intravenously, while the incidence was only 12% in non-hypersensitive, previously treated patients. Of 35 patients in clinical trials with previous hypersensitivity reactions to E. coli L-asparaginase only, 5 (14%) had severe hypersensitivity reactions to pegaspargase. Of 27 patients in clinical trials with previous hypersensitivity to both E. coli and Erwinia L-asparaginase 7 (26%) had severe hypersensitivity reactions. The overall incidence of hypersensitivity reactions requiring discontinuation of pegaspargase in 174 previously treated patients was 9% (19% in 62 hypersensitive and 3% in 112 non-hypersensitive patients). Of the 9% hypersensitivity reactions leading to discontinuation of pegaspargase; 1% were anaphylactic reactions.
 
Hepatic adverse reactions are common with pegaspargase therapy. Elevated hepatic enzymes include elevated transaminases (11%, grade 3/4 3%) and hyperbilirubinemia (1%; grade 3/4 2%) in patients treated with pegaspargase as part of first line treatment; similar incidence was seen in clinical trials in patients with previously treated acute lymphocytic leukemia (ALL). L-Asparaginase inhibits hepatic protein synthesis. Hypoalbuminemia may occur and can be severe resulting in peripheral edema or ascites. Decreases in plasma cholesterol-binding proteins, possibly leading to hyperlipidemia and hypercholesterolemia have been reported. Coagulopathy (7%; grade 3/4 2%) due to decreased synthesis of hepatic clotting factors, including fibrinogen, and factors II, V, VII, VIII, and IX, resulting in prolonged prothrombin time or partial thromboplastin time or hypofibrinogenemia has been reported during clinical trials for first line ALL therapy and previously treated ALL patients. Factors V and VIII are markedly affected with variable decreases in factors VII and X. In rare instances, bleeding associated with coagulopathy has been reported, with some patients experiencing fatal bleeding (e.g., intracranial bleeding) associated with hypofibrinogenemia or hypoprothrombinemia. Inhibition of anticoagulant protein synthesis (e.g., proteins C and S and antithrombin III) may lead to thrombosis. The reduction in coagulation inhibitors by L-asparaginase may be greater than decreases in procoagulant factors leading to a hypercoagulable state. Thrombosis has been reported in 2—4% of patients treated with pegaspargase during clinical trials. Superficial thrombo-phlebitis, deep venous thrombosis, sagittal sinus thrombosis, venous catheter thrombosis, and atrial thrombosis have occurred. Central nervous system thrombosis was reported in 2 of 58 patients treated with pegaspargase during clinical trials for first-line therapy for ALL. Antithrombin III levels may fall to 2-times the upper limit of normal, pancreatic inflammation, and pseudocyst. One patient also developed hyperammonemia and encephalopathy. Six patients became symptomatic after their initial dose.
 
Pegaspargase inhibits the synthesis of insulin. Over time, hyperglycemia (3—5%) will occur as insulin production declines and hypoinsulinemia develops. Hyperglycemia is usually transient and usually resolved upon discontinuation pegaspargase; however, short-term insulin therapy may be necessary. Fatal diabetic ketoacidosis has occurred.
 
Mild nausea/vomiting may be seen during pegaspargase therapy. Anorexia, abdominal cramps, general malaise, and weight loss also have been observed. An injection site reaction consisting of pain, redness, or swelling may occur.
 
CNS toxicity of pegaspargase is more common in adults than children. Lethargy and somnolence are most common. Other CNS reactions include agitation, EEG changes, depression, emotional lability (mood changes), fatigue, hallucinations, irritability, paresthesias, and somnolence. In rare instances, a parkinsonian-like syndrome with tremor and progressive muscle tone (hypertonia) has occurred. About 25% of patients experience cerebral dysfunction characterized confusion, impaired cognition, stupor, or coma. These adverse reactions usually resolve spontaneously once pegaspargase therapy is stopped. Treatment with L-asparaginase is associated with an increase in blood ammonia levels during the conversion of asparagine to aspartic acid. Neurotoxicity may also be due to decreased levels of glutamine and L-asparagine in the CNS. Some clinicians have used 'asparagine rescue' to treat acute CNS toxicity. Doses of asparagine used were 1—2 mmol/kg/day as a continuous IV infusion for 5 days. CNS symptoms may also be related to possible CNS bleeding or thrombosis especially seizures or headache. Status epilepticus and temporal lobe seizures have been reported.
 
Azotemia, usually pre-renal, occurs frequently with pegaspargase therapy. Acute renal failure (unspecified) and fatal renal insufficiency have been reported during pegaspargase treatment. Proteinuria occurs infrequently.
 
Pegaspargase has very little bone marrow suppression effects. If myelosuppression occurs, it is generally transient; slight anemia may occur. Marked leukopenia has been reported with L-asparaginase therapy and white blood cell counts should be monitored.
 
Pegaspargase-induced cellular destruction can result in tumor lysis syndrome (TLS) which includes hyperkalemia, hyperphosphatemia, hyperuricemia, and hypocalcemia. Uric acid nephropathy, acute renal failure due renal tubular obstruction, metabolic acidosis, and nephrolithiasis can also occur. These problems are more severe when a highly chemosensitive disease is treated (i.e., leukemia or lymphoma). Aggressive alkalinization of the urine and use of allopurinol can prevent urate nephropathy; close monitoring of serum electrolytes and renal function is recommended.
 
Because pegaspargase is a therapeutic protein, there is the potential for the development of binding and/or neutralizing antibody formation to pegaspargase. During clinical trials for the first line treatment of acute lymphocytic leukemia (ALL), 'high-titer' antibody formation was 2% during induction (n=48), 10% during delayed intensification I (n=50), and 11% during delayed intensification II (n=44). There is insufficient data to determine whether the development of antibodies is associated with an increased risk of clinical allergic reactions, altered pharmacokinetics, or loss of anti-leukemic activity.

Description
  Text
  Pegaspargase is an asparagine specific enzyme that is indicated for the first-line treatment of acute lymphoblastic leukemia (ALL) in adults and pediatric patients and ALL in adults and pediatric patients who experienced hypersensitivity to native forms of L-asparaginase. It is used in combination with multi-agent chemotherapy. Use of pegaspargase is contraindicated in patients with severe hepatic impairment, a history of pancreatitis, or who have had a serious thrombosis or hemorrhagic event related to prior L-asparaginase therapy.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Pegaspargase contains an Escherichia (E.) coli-derived asparagine specific enzyme as a conjugate of L-asparaginase (L-asparagine amidohydrolase) and monomethoxypolyethylene glycol (mPEG). L-asparaginase is a tetrameric enzyme that catalyzes the conversion of the amino acid L-asparagine into aspartic acid and ammonia. The depletion of plasma L-asparagine causes selective killing of leukemic cells that are deficient in asparagine synthetase and depend on exogenous L-asparagine for survival.

Dosage Adjustment Guidelines
  Hepatic Impairment
  Specific guidelines for dosage adjustments in baseline mild or moderate hepatic impairment are not available; it appears that no dosage adjustments are needed. Use of pegaspargase in patients with a history of severe hepatic impairment is contraindicated.
 
Treatment-Related HepatotoxicityTotal bilirubin level greater than 3- to 10-times the upper limit of normal (ULN): Hold pegaspargase; resume therapy when the total bilirubin levels decrease to 1.5-times the ULN or less.Total bilirubin level greater than 10-times the ULN: Discontinue pegaspargase; do not make up for missed doses.
