Indication Specific Dosing
  General Information
  General Dosing Information
All infants should receive protection against RSV-associated lower respiratory tract infection (LRTI) with either maternal RSV vaccination or administration of nirsevimab or clesrovimab to the infant. No product is preferred; base selection on parental preference, product availability, and timing of birth relative to the RSV season.
Pregnant individuals who erroneously received Arexvy do not need to receive a dose of Abrysvo; however, nirsevimab is recommended for the infant (if younger than 8 months).
Administer nirsevimab to infants who erroneously received the RSV vaccine instead of nirsevimab.

    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components. The Arexvy stopper of the vial containing lyophilized antigen component or adjuvant suspension component and the Arexvy tip cap and plunger stopper of the prefilled syringe containing the adjuvant suspension component are not made with natural rubber latex.
    Headline
    children, infants, neonates
    General Information
    Safety and efficacy of respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) vaccine have not been established in neonates, infants, children, or adolescents; however, evidence from an animal model strongly suggests that the RSV vaccine would be unsafe in patients younger than 2 years due to an increased risk of enhanced respiratory disease. The RSV vaccine is not indicated for use in pediatric patients.

    General Information
    General Information
    The respiratory syncytial virus vaccine may not protect all vaccine recipients. 69069
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    There are no data on the presence of the respiratory syncytial virus vaccine in human milk, its effects on the breast-fed child, or its effects on milk production. Consider the benefits of breast-feeding, the risk of potential drug exposure in the child, and the risk of not vaccinating a lactating individual.
    Headline
    immunosuppression
    General Information
    Immunocompromised individuals, including those receiving immunosuppressive therapy, may have a diminished immune response to the respiratory syncytial virus vaccine. Studies evaluating Abrysvo and Arexvy in adults with immunosuppression assessed immune responses following 1 or 2 doses; the 2-dose regimens were unapproved dosing regimens. These studies did not establish the effectiveness of either vaccine in immunocompromised individuals.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    In clinical trials for the respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) vaccine that is indicated in pregnant individuals (Abrysvo), there was an increased incidence of preterm birth in those who received the vaccine compared to those who received placebo. However, data are insufficient to establish or exclude a causal relationship between preterm birth and RSV vaccination during pregnancy. To avoid the potential risk of preterm birth, vaccinate pregnant patients between 32 and 36 weeks gestation. The RSV vaccine has not been studied in pregnant individuals less than 24 weeks gestation or those at increased risk for preterm birth. Additionally, pre-eclampsia and gestational hypertension were reported more frequently in pregnant individuals who received the RSV vaccine compared to the placebo group. In neonates born to those who were vaccinated, low birth weight and jaundice occurred more frequently. There was no evidence of an increased risk of congenital anomalies or fetal deaths associated with vaccine administration. A pregnancy exposure registry is available; encourage individuals who receive the RSV vaccine during pregnancy to enroll in the registry by calling 1-800-616-3791. Arexvy and Mresvia are not approved for use in pregnant individuals.

    Headline
    edema, erythema, injection site reaction
    General Information
    Injection site reaction was commonly reported during clinical trials for respiratory syncytial virus vaccine. The most frequent solicited local adverse reactions reported after a dose of Arexvy were pain (overall incidence, 61% to 76%; grade 3 incidence, 1% to 3%), erythema (greater than 20 mm, 7.5% to 13%; greater than 100 mm, 0.2% to 0.5%), and edema or swelling (greater than 20 mm, 5.5% to 10%; greater than 100 mm, 0.1% to 0.2%). The most frequent solicited local adverse reactions reported during the 7 days after a dose of Abrysvo were pain (overall incidence, 10.6% to 46.8%; mild, 9.4% to 36.1%; moderate, 1.1% to 5.5%; severe, up to 0.1%), redness or erythema (overall incidence, 2.7% to 7.2%; mild, 1.5% to 5%; moderate, 1% to 2.2%; severe, 0.1%), and edema or swelling (overall incidence, 2.5% to 7.1%; mild 2.5 cm to 5 cm, 1.5% to 4.1%; moderate 5 cm to 10 cm, 0.9% to 2.9%; severe greater than 10 cm, up to 0.2%). In a study in immunocompromised individuals who received 2 doses of Abrysvo, local reactions were higher after Dose 2 (34.3% to 46.8%) than Dose 1 (19.6% to 26%). The most frequent solicited local adverse reactions reported during the 7 days after a dose of Mresvia were pain (overall incidence, 55.9% to 73.9%; grade 3 incidence, 1.6% to 1.7%), redness or erythema (2.5 cm or more, 2% to 2.4%; greater than 10 cm, 0.6%), edema or swelling (2.5 cm or more, 3.7% to 4.6%; greater than 10 cm, 0.2% to 0.9%), and axillary swelling or tenderness (overall incidence, 15.2% to 17.1%; grade 3, 0.6% to 0.8%).

    Headline
    atrial fibrillation
    General Information
    There is insufficient data to determine a causal relationship of atrial fibrillation to respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) vaccine. Atrial fibrillation was reported within 30 days of Arexvy administration in 10 patients age 60 years and older (7 serious events) who received respiratory syncytial virus vaccine (n = 12,467), compared to 4 patients (1 serious event) who received placebo (n = 12,499). One non-serious case of atrial fibrillation occurred in a 45-year-old individual 6 days after vaccination. The individual had a history of paroxysmal atrial fibrillation, paroxysmal supraventricular tachycardia, hypercholesterolemia, insulin resistance, and obstructive sleep apnea. The event was self-limited, resolved spontaneously within 4 days, and did not require changes to the atrial fibrillation medication regimen. There were no cases of atrial fibrillation in patients 50 to 59 years receiving Arexvy. Within 6 months of administration, serious events of atrial fibrillation were reported in 13 patients age 60 years and older who received Arexvy, compared to 15 participants who received placebo. Atrial fibrillation was reported in 11 vaccine recipients (5 serious events) with 30 days of Abrysvo administration (n = 18,574), compared to 3 patients (2 serious events) who received placebo (n = 18,288). In a study with immunocompromised individuals, 1 individual reported atrial fibrillation and atrial flutter with symptom onset 15 days after vaccine administration of Abrysvo.

    Headline
    Guillain-Barre syndrome
    General Information
    In clinical trials, Guillain-Barre syndrome (GBS) was reported in 1 participant beginning 9 days after receiving Arexvy and in 1 participant 7 days after receiving Abrysvo (with diagnosis later revised to chronic inflammatory demyelinating polyneuropathy). The results of a postmarketing observational study suggest an increased risk of GBS during the 42 days after vaccination. The risk of GBS after vaccination was assessed in self-controlled case series analyses. For individuals 65 years and older vaccinated with Abrysvo, the incidence rate ratio (GBS cases in the risk window/control window) was 2.02 (95% CI: 0.93, 4.4) with an estimated 9 excess cases of GBS per million doses administered. Description
  Text
  Respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) vaccine is indicated for the prevention of lower respiratory tract disease (LRTD) caused by RSV. RSV-associated LRTD, including pneumonia and bronchiolitis, disproportionately affects older adults and leads to increased risk of hospitalization and death. Arexvy significantly reduced the risk of developing first episode RSV-associated LRTD by 82.6% (96.95% CI, 57.9% to 94.1%) compared with placebo in individuals 60 years and older. The Arexvy vaccine produced strong immune responses against RSV in adults aged 18 to 59 with health conditions, similar to or better than those seen in adults aged 60 and older. Mresvia significantly reduced the risk of developing first episode RSV-associated LRTD by 62.5% (95% CI, 47.7% to 73.1%) compared with placebo in individuals 60 years and older after 8.6 months of follow-up. At 29 days after vaccination with Mresvia, non-inferiority was demonstrated for the nAb Geometric Mean Titers (GMTs) for RSV subgroups RSV-A and RSV-B in individuals 18 to 59 years compared to individuals 60 years and older. Abrysvo significantly reduced the risk of developing first episode RSV-associated LRTD by 66.7% (96.66% CI, 28.8% to 85.8%) for LRTD with 2 or more symptoms, and by 85.7% (96.66% CI, 32% to 98.7%) for LRTD with 3 or more symptoms. At the end of the first RSV season, the reduced risk was 65.1% and 88.9%, respectively. In pregnant individuals, Abrysvo reduced the risk of severe LRTD by 81.8% within 90 days after birth and 69.4% within 180 days after birth. Within a subgroup of pregnant individuals who were 32 to 36 weeks gestational age, Abrysvo reduced the risk of LRTD by 34.7% and reduced the risk of severe LRTD by 91.1% within 90 days after birth when compared to placebo. Within 180 days after birth, Abrysvo reduced the risk of LRTD by 57.3% and by 76.5% for severe LRTD, when compared to placebo. The most commonly reported adverse drug reactions in clinical trials include injection site pain, fatigue, muscle pain, headache, and joint pain. RSV vaccine was FDA-approved in 2023.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Respiratory syncytial virus vaccine induces an immune response against recombinant respiratory syncytial virus glycoprotein F stabilized in pre-fusion conformation (Arexvy and Mresvia, RSVPreF; Abrysvo, recombinant RSV preF A and RSV preF B) that protects against lower respiratory tract disease (LRTD) caused by respiratory syncytial virus (RSV). When Abrysvo is administered to pregnant individuals, antibodies to RSV antigens are transferred transplacentally to protect infants younger than 6 months of age.

      Reconstitution (Arexvy Vial and Adjuvant Suspension Component Vial)
Prepare respiratory syncytial virus vaccine by reconstituting the vial of lyophilized antigen component (a sterile white powder) with the accompanying vial of adjuvant suspension component (an opalescent, colorless to pale brownish sterile liquid). Use only the supplied adjuvant suspension component for reconstitution.

Gently swirl the vial until powder is completely dissolved. Do not shake vigorously.
Withdraw 0.5 mL of reconstituted product for the dose.
Storage of reconstituted Arexvy vaccine: If possible, administer immediately after reconstitution; however, reconstituted vaccine may be stored refrigerated between 2 and 8 degrees C (36 and 46 degrees F) or at room temperature [up to 25 degrees C (77 degrees F)] for up to 4 hours. Reconstitution (Arexvy Vial and Adjuvant Suspension Component Prefilled Syringe)
Prepare the respiratory syncytial virus vaccine by reconstituting the vial of lyophilized antigen component (a sterile white powder) with the accompanying prefilled syringe of adjuvant suspension component (an opalescent, colorless to pale brownish sterile liquid).
Hold the prefilled syringe of adjuvant suspension component by the barrel and unscrew the syringe cap by twisting it counterclockwise.
Attach a sterile needle by rotating the needle clockwise until you feel it lock.
Slowly transfer the contents of the prefilled syringe into the vial containing the lyophilized antigen component.
Gently swirl the vial until the powder is completely dissolved. Do not shake vigorously.
Withdraw the entire contents of approximately 0.5 mL from the vial containing the reconstituted vaccine into the same syringe and administer intramuscularly.

Reconstitution (Abrysvo Vial and Sterile Water Prefilled Syringe)
Supplied in a kit that includes a vial of Lyophilized Antigen Component (sterile white powder), a prefilled syringe containing the Sterile Water Diluent Component, and a vial adapter.
Remove the flip top cap from the vial of Lyophilized Antigen Component.
Peel off the top cover from the vial adapter packaging.
While keeping the vial adapter in its packaging, center the adapter over the vial's stopper and attach to the vial with a straight downward push. Remove the packaging.
Hold the syringe of Sterile Water Diluent Component by the Luer lock adapter. Twist to remove the syringe cap. Connect the syringe to the vial adapter by turning the Luer lock.
Inject the entire contents of the syringe into the vial.
Hold the plunger rod down and gently swirl the vial until the powder is completely dissolved (less than 1 minute). Do not shake.
Invert the vial completely and slowly withdraw the entire contents into the syringe for an approximately 0.5 mL dose.
Twist to disconnect the syringe from the vial adapter.
Attach a sterile needle for intramuscular injection.
Storage of reconstituted Abrysvo vaccine: Administer vaccine immediately after reconstitution or store at room temperature between 15 and 30 degrees C (59 and 86 degrees F) and use within 4 hours.
 
Reconstitution (Abrysvo Vial and Sterile Water Diluent Vial)

Using a sterile needle and sterile syringe, withdraw the entire contents of the vial containing the Sterile Water Diluent Component.
Inject the entire contents into the vial containing the Lyophilized Antigen Component (white powder).
Gently swirl the vial in a circular motion until the powder is completely dissolved. Do not shake.
Withdraw 0.5 mL from the vial containing the reconstituted vaccine.
Storage of reconstituted Abrysvo vaccine: Administer vaccine immediately after reconstitution or store at room temperature between 15 and 30 degrees C (59 and 86 degrees F) and use within 4 hours.

Preparation (Mresvia)
Prefilled syringes are supplied as a frozen suspension that must be thawed prior to administration. After thawing, do not refreeze. Do not return to the refrigerator after standing at room temperature. Do not shake. Protect from light.
Thawing under refrigeration [2 to 8 degrees C (36 to 46 degrees F)]: Thaw carton of 1 prefilled syringe, carton of 2 prefilled syringes, or 1 syringe (removed from carton) for 100 minutes. Thaw carton of 10 prefilled syringes for 160 minutes.
Thawing at room temperature [15 to 25 degrees C (59 to 77 degrees F)]: Thaw carton of 1 prefilled syringe, carton of 2 prefilled syringes, or 1 syringe (removed from carton) for 40 minutes. Thaw carton of 10 prefilled syringes for 80 minutes.
Storage of thawed Mresvia vaccine: Prefilled syringes may be stored refrigerated [2 to 8 degrees C (36 to 46 degrees F)] for up to 90 days prior to use. Prefilled syringes may be stored at room temperature [8 to 25 degrees C (46 to 77 degrees F)] for a total of 24 hours after removal from refrigerated conditions. Discard the prefilled syringe if not used within this time.
