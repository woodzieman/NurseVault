Drug Class: Treosulfan is an intravenous alkylating drug indicated for use in combination with fludarabine as a preparative regimen for allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplantation in adult and pediatric patients 1 year and older with acute myeloid leukemia or myelodysplastic syndrome.

Routes: injection (IV/IM/SC); Common adult doses: 10 g/m2 IV.

Common Side Effects (excluding routine GI upset): anemia, bone marrow suppression, graft failure, neutropenia, thrombocytopenia, weight loss, agitation, dizziness, headache, insomnia, paresthesias, peripheral neuropathy, seizures, vertigo, chills, fatigue, fever, infection, flushing, heart failure, hypertension, hypotension, pericardial effusion, sinus tachycardia, thromboembolism, elevated hepatic enzymes, hepatotoxicity, hyperbilirubinemia, cough, dysphonia

  Contraindications And Precautions
    Headline
    Drug Interactions
    General Information
    The coadministration of certain medications may lead to harm and require avoidance or therapy modification; review all drug interactions prior to concomitant use of other medications.
    Headline
    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components.
    Headline
    bone marrow suppression
    General Information
    Profound bone marrow suppression with pancytopenia is the desired therapeutic effect of treosulfan-based preparative regimens. Do not begin the preparative regimen if the stem cell donor is not available.
    Headline
    Treatment-Related Restrictions of Use
    General Information
    Increased morbidity and mortality were observed at dosages higher than recommended in a clinical trial (MC-FludT.14/L Trial 1). Avoid exceeding the recommended treosulfan dosage of 10 g/m2 daily for 3 consecutive days.
    Headline
    seizure disorder
    General Information
    Consider clonazepam prophylaxis for patients at higher risk for seizures such as those with seizure disorder and infants. Seizures have been reported in patients following treatment with treosulfan.
    Headline
    chromosomal instability syndrome
    General Information
    The risk of secondary malignancy is increased in patients with any chromosomal instability syndrome (DNA breakage disorders) such as Fanconi anemia; the safety and efficacy of treosulfan have not been established for patients with these disorders.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Pregnancy should be avoided by individuals of reproductive potential during treosulfan treatment. Although there are no adequately controlled studies in pregnant people, treosulfan can cause fetal harm or death when administered during pregnancy based on its mechanism of action because it is genotoxic and affects dividing cells. Individuals who are pregnant or who become pregnant while receiving treosulfan should be apprised of the potential hazard to the fetus.
    Headline
    people who can cause pregnancy in others, people who may become pregnant, reproductive risk
    General Information
    Counsel people about the reproductive risk associated with treosulfan treatment. People who may become pregnant should avoid pregnancy and use effective contraception during treatment and for 6 months after treatment with treosulfan. People who can cause pregnancy in others should use effective contraception during treatment and for 3 months after treatment with treosulfan. Individuals who may become pregnant should undergo pregnancy testing within 7 days prior to initiation of treosulfan. Individuals who become pregnant while receiving treosulfan should be apprised of the potential hazard to the fetus. Although there are no data regarding the effect of treosulfan on human fertility, male and female infertility has been observed in animal studies.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in nursing infants from treosulfan, advise individuals to discontinue breast-feeding during treatment and for 1 week after the final dose. It is not known whether treosulfan is present in human milk, although many drugs are excreted in human milk.

Interactions
Acetaminophen; Caffeine; Dihydrocodeine: (Moderate) Consider a reduced dose of dihydrocodeine with frequent monitoring for respiratory depression and sedation if concurrent use of treosulfan is necessary. If treosulfan is discontinued, consider increasing the dihydrocodeine dose until stable drug effects are achieved and monitor for evidence of opioid withdrawal. Concomitant use of dihydrocodeine with treosulfan may increase dihydrocodeine plasma concentrations, resulting in greater metabolism by CYP2D6, increased dihydromorphine concentrations, and prolonged opioid adverse reactions, including hypotension, respiratory depression, profound sedation, coma, and death. If treosulfan is discontinued, monitor the patient carefully and consider increasing the opioid dosage if appropriate. Dihydrocodeine is partially metabolized via CYP3A and treosulfan is a weak CYP3A inhibitor.

It is recommended to avoid this combination when codeine is being used for cough. Description
  Text
  Treosulfan is an intravenous alkylating drug indicated for use in combination with fludarabine as a preparative regimen for allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplantation in adult and pediatric patients 1 year and older with acute myeloid leukemia or myelodysplastic syndrome. Profound myelosuppression is the desired therapeutic effect of treosulfan-based preparative regimens; hematopoietic stem cell transplantation is required to prevent potentially fatal complications of prolonged myelosuppression. Treosulfan was approved by the FDA in January 2025.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Treosulfan is an alkylating agent. Alkylating agents act by cross-linking strands of DNA and are nonspecific for cell cycle phase. DNA alkylation is thought to be responsible for the cytotoxic activities of treosulfan. Treosulfan showed hematopoietic stem cell depleting activity as well as immunosuppressive and antitumor activity in mouse models of leukemia.

  Text
  Treosulfan is administered intravenously. It does not bind to plasma protein. The mean volume of distribution is approximately 41 liters (CV, 19%). Treosulfan is a prodrug; the pharmacologically inactive treosulfan is converted spontaneously under physiological conditions into the active monoepoxide intermediate and finally to active L-diepoxibutane. The mean terminal half-life is 1.7 (standard deviation, +/- 0.4) hours. A median of 42% of the dose is excreted unchanged in the urine within 24 hours and 89% of this unchanged fraction is excreted within the first 8 hours after administration.
Pharmacodynamics: Increase in treosulfan exposure is associated with an increased incidence of infection-related death. Treosulfan time course of pharmacodynamic response is unknown.
 
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes and drug transporters: CYP2C19, CYP3A4
Treosulfan is a weak inhibitor of CYP2C19 and CYP3A4 (using midazolam as substrate). Treosulfan is a CYP2D6 substrate and its monoepoxide intermediate is a CYP2C8 substrate.
