    aseptic meningitis
    General Information
    Aseptic meningitis was reported in individuals treated with zilganersen during clinical trials. One individual experienced aseptic meningitis requiring dose interruption and pretreatment with IV dexamethasone prior to subsequent administration of zilganersen. Despite corticosteroid premedication, CSF white blood cells and protein increased with continued exposure but the individual remained asymptomatic and did not require discontinuation from treatment. Pleocytosis (increased white blood cell count in CSF) was observed in 29% of individuals (n = 7) after administration of the first 2 to 4 doses of zilganersen 50 mg compared to 18% of individuals (n = 3) in the control group during the main study and 6 individuals treated with zilganersen during the open-label extension period.

    General Information
    Treatment with zilganersen may result in antibody formation. Overall, 28.1% (9/32) of zilganersen treated individuals developed treatment-emergent anti-drug antibodies (ADA). Of these, 2 were transient and 7 were persistent. The presence of ADA had no apparent effect on the safety or efficacy profile of zilganersen. Description
  Text
  Zilganersen is an antisense oligonucleotide indicated for the treatment of Alexander disease. Alexander disease is a rare, progressive neurological disorder caused by changes in the GFAP gene. These changes affect astrocytes, support cells in the central nervous system, and can lead to abnormal protein accumulation, white-matter damage, and impaired nervous-system function. Symptoms may include developmental delay, loss of previously acquired skills, seizures, muscle stiffness or weakness, movement and coordination problems, difficulty swallowing, speech impairment, and breathing problems. Symptoms and severity vary among individuals. In a clinical trial of pediatric and adult individuals with Alexander disease, intrathecal zilganersen every 12 weeks significantly slowed the decline in gait speed compared with the control group at Week 61. A subgroup of individuals aged 2 to 4 years had an observed improvement in the Gross Motor Function Measure-88 subscales for standing, walking, running, and jumping while individuals in the control arm had an observed decline. The most common adverse reactions include vomiting, back pain, cough, headache, and post-lumbar puncture syndrome. Zilganersen was FDA approved in September 2026.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Alexander disease is associated with pathogenic variants in the GFAP gene, which can lead to abnormal accumulation of glial fibrillary acidic protein (GFAP) in astrocytes. This accumulation contributes to astrocyte dysfunction, formation of Rosenthal fibers, and damage to myelin and white matter, particularly in the central nervous system. Zilganersen is a GFAP-directed antisense oligonucleotide. It binds to GFAP precursor messenger RNA, promotes its degradation, and reduces GFAP protein synthesis. By lowering GFAP production, zilganersen is intended to reduce the abnormal GFAP burden associated with Alexander disease.
Pharmacodynamics
Plasma GFAP is an indirect measure of target engagement. After 5 doses (week 61), the geometric mean ratio to baseline in plasma GFAP was 33.6% lower in individuals receiving zilganersen compared to those in the control group.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Other Route(s)
    Description
    Intrathecal Route
The pharmacokinetic properties of zilganersen were evaluated after intrathecal administration of multiple doses of 50 mg given as a bolus every 12 weeks in pediatric and adult individuals (2 years and older) with Alexander disease. Zilganersen was quantifiable in the CSF 84 days after injection. The steady-state CSF trough concentration was 1.21 nanograms/mL. The steady-state trough plasma concentration and steady-state maximum plasma concentration (Cmax) were 0.252 nanograms/mL and 908 nanograms/mL, respectively. The AUC was 7,716 nanograms x hour/mL. The median Tmax was 4.03 hours (range: 1 to 23.7 hours). No evidence of time-dependent plasma pharmacokinetics was observed between the first and sixth administrations of zilganersen.

  Text
  Zilganersen is administered intrathecally. In vitro, greater than 98% of zilganersen binds to human plasma proteins. After intrathecal administration of zilganersen into the CSF, zilganersen distributes into the systemic circulation and tissues with a central compartment volume of 7.95 L and a peripheral compartment volume of 315 L. Zilganersen undergoes endonuclease and exonuclease-mediated cleavage to shorter oligonucleotide metabolites. It is not a CYP substrate, and CYP450-mediated metabolism is not expected. Zilganersen and its metabolites are likely eliminated primarily by urinary excretion. The urinary excretion of unchanged zilganersen has not been characterized. The terminal elimination half-life in plasma is approximately 1 month.

      Administer intrathecally after dilution via lumbar puncture performed by, or under the direction of, a health care provider experienced in performing lumbar punctures.
 
Dilution
The diluent vial contains more volume than needed. Excess must be removed prior to the dilution.
Use a 3 mL and/or 10 mL syringe with an 18G to 22G needle. Withdraw excess aCSF diluent from the vial based on the individual's age. Keep the diluent vial containing the remaining diluent. Discard the syringe with the excess diluent.
Younger than 2 years: remove 12.6 mL of excess diluent
2 to 7 years: remove 7 mL of excess diluent
8 years and older: remove 1.4 mL of excess diluent
Withdraw 2.8 mL of zilganersen from the vial using a new 3 mL syringe. Add to the diluent vial.
Gently invert the vial several times to mix the solution.
Attach an 18G or 19G filter needle (5-micron) to a 20 mL syringe. Withdraw the recommended dose volume of diluted zilganersen from the vial based on the individual's age.
Younger than 2 years: 10 mL
2 to 7 years: 15 mL
8 years and older: 20 mL
Remove the filter needle. Attach a syringe tip cap to the prepared dosing syringe.
Discard any unused portion of the diluted solution and any unused contents of the zilganersen vial.
Storage: Once drawn into the dosing syringe, the solution must be administered within 4 hours if kept at room temperature up to 30 degrees C (86 degrees F) or 24 hours if refrigerated at 2 to 8 degrees C (36 to 46 degrees F). Do not freeze. Do not expose to heat. Protect the prepared dosing syringe from light.
 
Administration
Consider sedation or local anesthesia if indicated by the individual's clinical condition.
Consider imaging to guide intrathecal administration.
Evaluate the individual prior to intrathecal injection for factors that increase risks associated with lumbar puncture.
If the syringe is refrigerated, allow it to warm to room temperature for at least 30 minutes prior to use. Do not use external heat sources to warm the syringe.
Prior to administration, remove a volume of the patient's CSF approximately equal to the intended dose volume as appropriate for age using a lumbar puncture needle.
Younger than 2 years: 10 mL
2 to 7 years: 15 mL
8 years and older: 20 mL
Administer zilganersen as an intrathecal bolus injection over 1 to 3 minutes using the lumbar puncture needle.
Do not flush the needle or any attached extension catheters, if used, after administration.
Discard the dosing syringe.
Missed dose: Administer zilganersen as soon as possible. Resume treatment at the recommended dosing frequency from the date of the most recently administered dose.
