Drug Class: Crinecerfont is a corticotropin-releasing factor (CRF) type 1 receptor antagonist indicated for classic congenital adrenal hyperplasia (CAH) as an adjunctive treatment to glucocorticoid replacement.

Routes: oral; Common adult doses: 200 mg/day PO.

Common Side Effects (excluding routine GI upset): dizziness, fatigue, headache, restlessness, arthralgia, back pain, myalgia

In an Overdose: No overdose/antidote information is listed in this file.

Key Nursing Considerations: Contraindications/precautions: This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components. Crinecerfont is not recommended in individuals with severe renal impairment or end-stage renal disease (renal failure).

    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Available data on the use of crinecerfont during human pregnancy are insufficient to evaluate a drug-associated risk of major birth defects, miscarriage, or other adverse outcomes. Oral administration to pregnant rabbits at doses 2-fold higher than human exposure at the maximum recommended human dose (MRHD) resulted in a low incidence of poly-malformations (craniofacial defects) and slightly lower mean fetal weights. No developmental toxicity was observed in rats at doses 4-fold higher than the MRHD. A pregnancy registry is available for individuals who receive crinecerfont during pregnancy and for those who become pregnant while receiving crinecerfont. Exposure can be reported by calling 1-855-276-7489.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Use crinecerfont with caution during breast-feeding. There are no data on the presence of crinecerfont in human milk, its effects on the breast-fed child, or its effect on milk production. Crinecerfont is present in animal milk. Monitor the breast-fed child for signs of adrenal insufficiency such as weakness, poor feeding, and weight loss. Consider the benefits of breast-feeding, the patient's clinical need for treatment, and any potential adverse effects on the breast-fed child from the medication or from the patient's underlying medical condition.

Description
  Text
  Crinecerfont is a corticotropin-releasing factor (CRF) type 1 receptor antagonist indicated for classic congenital adrenal hyperplasia (CAH) as an adjunctive treatment to glucocorticoid replacement. Congenital adrenal hyperplasia is a rare genetic condition affecting the adrenal glands. Individuals with CAH do not produce enough cortisol and produce an excess of androgens. They require high doses of glucocorticoids, which increase cortisol and help reduce the excess androgen concentrations. Treatment with crinecerfont reduces excessive adrenal androgen production, therefore reducing the amount of glucocorticoid treatment needed. In a clinical trial of 182 adults, crinecerfont treatment reduced the daily glucocorticoid dose by 27% while maintaining control of androstenedione concentrations, compared to a 10% daily glucocorticoid dose reduction in the placebo group. In a clinical trial of 103 pediatric patients, crinecerfont-treated patients experienced a statistically significant reduction from baseline in serum androstenedione, compared to an average increase from baseline in the placebo group. Monitor individuals for acute adrenal insufficiency or adrenal crisis, especially during times of increased cortisol need (acute intercurrent illness, serious trauma, or surgical procedures). Crinecerfont was FDA-approved in December 2024.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Crinecerfont is a selective corticotropin-releasing factor (CRF) type 1 receptor antagonist that blocks the binding of CRF to CRF type 1 receptors in the pituitary, but not CRF type 2 receptors. Crinecerfont binding to CRF type 1 receptors inhibits adrenocorticotropic hormone (ACTH) secretion from the pituitary, thereby reducing ACTH-mediated adrenal androgen production.
 
Pharmacodynamics
In clinical trials of adult and pediatric patients with congenital adrenal hyperplasia, administration of crinecerfont for 4 weeks during the initial glucocorticoid stable period led to median reductions in ACTH concentrations of 65% to 72%.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Oral Route
    Description
    The Tmax of crinecerfont is 4 hours after oral administration. The Cmax and AUC of crinecerfont increase dose-proportionally over the approved recommended dosage range. With twice daily dosing, steady state conditions are achieved in approximately 7 days with an accumulation ration of 1.4. There are no clinically significant differences in Cmax and AUC after oral capsule and oral solution administration. In adult patients receiving crinecerfont 100 mg by mouth twice daily, AUC was 72,846 ng x hour/mL and Cmax was 4,231 ng/mL. After administration of the capsule with food, the Cmax increased 4.9-fold and AUC increased 3.3-fold with a high-fat meal (800 to 1,000 calories, 50% fat), compared to fasted conditions. After administration of the oral solution with food, the Cmax increased 8.6-fold and AUC increased 8.3-fold with a high-fat meal (800 to 1,000 calories, 50% fat), compared to fasted conditions.

  Text
  Crinecerfont is administered orally. The mean apparent Vd (CV%) of crinecerfont in adults with congenital adrenal hyperplasia (CAH) is 852 L (31%) and plasma protein binding is 99.9% or more. Crinecerfont is metabolized primarily by CYP3A4 and to a lesser extent by CYP2B6 in vitro. Additionally, CYP2C8 and CYP2C19 may have minor contributions to its metabolism. After administration of a single oral 100 mg dose of radiolabeled crinecerfont, approximately 47.3% (2.7% as unchanged) of the dose was recovered in feces and 2% (amount as unchanged is undetectable) in urine. The effective half-life is approximately 14 hours with a mean (CV%) apparent clearance of 3.5 L/hour (37%).
 
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes and drug transporters: CYP34A
Crinecerfont is a CYP3A4 substrate. Concomitant use of crinecerfont with a strong or moderate CYP3A4 inducer decreases crinecerfont exposure, which may reduce its efficacy. Coadministration of rifampin decreased crinecerfont Cmax by 23% and decreased AUC by 62%. Crinecerfont Cmax and AUC increased by 25% and 45%, respectively, when used concomitantly with ketoconazole. No clinically significant differences in the pharmacokinetics of midazolam were observed when co-administered with crinecerfont. Concomitant use did not affect the pharmacokinetics of oral contraceptives containing ethinyl estradiol and levonorgestrel.
