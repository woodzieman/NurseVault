Drug Class: Pembrolizumab; berahyaluronidase alfa-pmph is a subcutaneous programmed death receptor-1 (PD-1) blocking monoclonal antibody; it is a combination of berahyaluronidase alfa, an endoglycosidase, and pembrolizumab.

Routes: injection (IV/IM/SC); Common adult doses: Every 3 weeks: Pembrolizumab 395 mg with 4,800 units berahyaluronidase alfa subcutaneously.

Common Side Effects (excluding routine GI upset): colitis, enterocolitis, ascites, biliary obstruction, cholangitis, elevated hepatic enzymes, hepatic failure, hepatitis, hepatotoxicity, hyperbilirubinemia, adrenocortical insufficiency, hypophysitis, photophobia, hypoparathyroidism, hypothyroidism, thyroiditis, diabetes mellitus, diabetic ketoacidosis, hyperglycemia, hypoglycemia, acute kidney injury, azotemia, interstitial nephritis, oliguria, renal failure, urinary retention, anaphylactoid reactions, chills, flushing, hypotension

In an Overdose: No overdose/antidote information is listed in this file.

Key Nursing Considerations: Contraindications/precautions: This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components. Use pembrolizumab; berahyaluronidase alfa with caution in patients with a history of autoimmune disease, including inflammatory bowel disease.

          Pembrolizumab 395 mg with 4,800 units berahyaluronidase alfa subcutaneously every 3 weeks OR pembrolizumab 790 mg with 9,600 units of berahyaluronidase alfa subcutaneously every 6 weeks until disease recurrence or unacceptable toxicity. Treatment with IV pembrolizumab significantly improved the median progression-free survival (PFS) compared with ipilimumab (4.1 to 5.5 months vs. 2.8 months) in patients with ipilimumab-naive unresectable or metastatic melanoma who had received no more than 1 prior systemic treatment for metastatic myeloma in a multicenter, randomized, open-label clinical trial (KEYNOTE-006); overall survival (OS) was also significantly improved. In patients with unresectable stage III or IV melanoma who had disease progression following ipilimumab and had received prior treatment with a BRAF- and/or MEK-inhibitor (if BRAF V600 mutant-positive), treatment with IV pembrolizumab significantly improved median PFS compared with investigator-choice chemotherapy (ICC) (2.9 months vs. 2.7 months) in a second interim analysis of a multinational, randomized, phase 2 trial (the KEYNOTE-002 trial); the ORR was 21% to 25% versus 4%, respectively (CR, 2% to 3% vs. 0%). ICC consisted of carboplatin/paclitaxel, carboplatin monotherapy, paclitaxel monotherapy, dacarbazine monotherapy, or temozolomide monotherapy. At the final analysis, patients who received pembrolizumab had a median OS of 13.4 to 14.7 months compared with 11 months for those who received ICC. Treatment crossover to pembrolizumab occurred in 55% of patients who received ICC.

Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  Contraindications And Precautions
    Headline
    Drug Interactions
    General Information
    The coadministration of certain medications may lead to harm and require avoidance or therapy modification; review all drug interactions prior to concomitant use of other medications.
    Headline
    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components.
    Headline
    autoimmune disease, inflammatory bowel disease
    General Information
    Use pembrolizumab; berahyaluronidase alfa with caution in patients with a history of autoimmune disease, including inflammatory bowel disease. Immune-mediated reactions, which may be severe or fatal, can occur in patients treated with drugs that bind to either the programmed death-receptor 1 (PD-1) or the PD-ligand 1 (PD-L1), thus removing inhibition of the immune response, such as pembrolizumab. These immune-mediated reactions can occur in any organ system or tissue and can also affect more than one body system simultaneously. Although usually occurring during treatment with PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors, immune-mediated reactions can occur at any time after starting therapy including after discontinuation of therapy. Because early identification and management is critical to safe usage of PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors, closely monitor patients for signs and symptoms that may be clinical manifestations of underlying immune-mediated reactions. Initiate an appropriate workup to exclude alternative etiologies including infection in cases of suspected immune-mediated reaction. Begin medical management promptly, including specialty consultation as appropriate. An interruption or discontinuation of pembrolizumab; berahyaluronidase alfa therapy may be necessary, depending on the severity of the reaction. In general, if an interruption or discontinuation of therapy is necessary, administer systemic corticosteroids (1 to 2 mg/kg per day of prednisone or equivalent); upon improvement to grade 1 or less, begin a steroid taper over at least 1 month. Consider additional systemic immunosuppression in patients whose symptoms are not controlled with corticosteroid therapy.
    Headline
    pulmonary disease, thoracic radiation therapy
    General Information
    Use pembrolizumab; berahyaluronidase alfa with caution in patients who have a history of pre-existing pulmonary disease; immune-mediated pneumonitis/interstitial lung disease has been reported in patients treated with IV pembrolizumab. The incidence is higher in patients who have received prior thoracic radiation therapy, except in patients with classical Hodgkin lymphoma, where pneumonitis rates were similar regardless of prior thoracic radiation. Monitor patients for signs and symptoms of pneumonitis (e.g., new or worsening cough, chest pain, shortness of breath). If confirmed, pembrolizumab; berahyaluronidase alfa therapy may need to be interrupted or discontinued depending on severity; treatment with systemic corticosteroids may be necessary.
    Headline
    allogeneic stem cell transplant
    General Information
    Perform a risk/benefit analysis prior to starting treatment with a PD-1/PD-L1 blocking antibody, such as pembrolizumab; berahyaluronidase alfa, in patients who may receive or who have a history of undergoing an allogeneic stem cell transplant (SCT). Fatal and other serious complications including hyperacute, acute, and chronic graft-versus-host-disease, sinusoidal obstruction syndrome (SOS) previously termed veno-occlusive disease (VOD) after reduced intensity conditioning, and a steroid-requiring febrile syndrome (without an identified infectious cause) have been reported in patients who receive an allogeneic SCT before or after being treated with a PD-1/PD-L1 blocking antibody. Monitor patients closely for evidence of transplant-related complications and intervene promptly. Complications may occur despite intervening therapy between PD-1/PD-L1 blockade and allogeneic SCT.
    Headline
    organ transplant, tissue transplant
    General Information
    Use pembrolizumab; berahyaluronidase alfa with caution in patients who have previously had a solid organ transplant or tissue transplant (including corneal transplant). Immune-mediated organ transplant rejection has been reported with PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitor therapy.
    Headline
    neurological disease
    General Information
    Use pembrolizumab; berahyaluronidase alfa with caution in patients who have a history of neurological disease such as myasthenia gravis or Guillain-Barre syndrome. Immune-mediated neurotoxicity has been reported with pembrolizumab or other PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors. Pembrolizumab; berahyaluronidase alfa may need to be interrupted or discontinued depending on the severity of the neurotoxicity; treatment with systemic corticosteroids may also be necessary.
    Headline
    obesity
    General Information
    Monitor patients with obesity as well as those who are considered overweight for an increased risk of infusion-related reactions when treated with pembrolizumab; berahyaluronidase alfa. Patients with a high BMI have a greater risk even if they have a low metabolic risk (i.e., fewer than 2 of diabetes, dyslipidemia, hypertension).
    Headline
    cardiac disease
    General Information
    Use pembrolizumab; berahyaluronidase alfa with caution in patients with cardiovascular risk factors such as hypertension, heart failure, cardiac disease, and myocardial infarction as they are at increased risk of cardiac immune-related adverse reactions such as myocarditis, vasculitis, and pericarditis.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Pembrolizumab; berahyaluronidase alfa can cause fetal harm when used during pregnancy based on its mechanism of action. There are no available data on the use of pembrolizumab; berahyaluronidase alfa during human pregnancy. A central function of the PD-1/PD-L1 pathway is to preserve pregnancy by maintaining immune tolerance to the fetus. Animal studies indicate that PD-1/PD-L1 inhibition may increase the risk of immune-mediated rejection of the developing fetus, resulting in fetal death. Human IgG4 antibodies are known to cross the placenta, so pembrolizumab; berahyaluronidase alfa has the potential to be transmitted to the fetus. Fetal exposure to pembrolizumab; berahyaluronidase alfa may increase the risk of developing immune-mediated disorders or altering the normal immune response. Patients should be counseled on the potential fetal risks.
    Headline
    people who may become pregnant, reproductive risk
    General Information
    Counsel people who may become pregnant about the reproductive risk associated with pembrolizumab; berahyaluronidase alfa. Pembrolizumab can cause fetal harm. Verify pregnancy status prior to treatment initiation. Advise people who may become pregnant to use effective contraception while taking pembrolizumab; berahyaluronidase alfa and for 4 months after the last dose.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Advise patients to avoid breast-feeding while taking pembrolizumab; berahyaluronidase alfa and for 4 months after the last dose due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in the breastfed child. There are no data on the presence of pembrolizumab in human milk, its effects on the breast-fed child, or its effects on milk production. Maternal IgG is known to be present in human milk. Pembrolizumab is a large protein molecule (molecular weight of 149,000 Daltons). It is unknown if pembrolizumab is present in human milk; the amount of drug in milk is likely to be very low. Pembrolizumab may be partially destroyed in the gastrointestinal tract, resulting in minimal absorption. The effects of localized gastrointestinal exposure and limited systemic exposure in the breastfed child to pembrolizumab are unknown.

    interstitial lung disease, pneumonitis
    General Information
    Immune-mediated interstitial lung disease/pneumonitis occurred in 5% (grade 3, 2%; grade 5, 0.4%) of patients (n = 251) treated with pembrolizumab; berahyaluronidase alfa in combination with chemotherapy in 1 randomized clinical trial. The incidence of pneumonitis is higher in patients who have received prior thoracic radiation. Pneumonitis was reported in 3.4% (grade 3 or 4, 1.2%; grade 5, 0.1%) of patients who received IV pembrolizumab monotherapy across clinical trials (n = 2,799). Systemic corticosteroids were required in 67% of patients who developed pneumonitis with resolution occurring in 59% of those patients. All patients who had pembrolizumab therapy held for pneumonitis resumed therapy after symptom improvement; of these, 23% had a recurrence of pneumonitis. If confirmed, pembrolizumab; berahyaluronidase alfa may need to be interrupted or discontinued; treatment with systemic corticosteroids and other systemic immunosuppressants may be necessary.

Description
  Text
  Pembrolizumab; berahyaluronidase alfa-pmph is a subcutaneous programmed death receptor-1 (PD-1) blocking monoclonal antibody; it is a combination of berahyaluronidase alfa, an endoglycosidase, and pembrolizumab. It is indicated for the treatment of adult and pediatric (12 years and older) solid tumor indications including certain types of breast cancer, biliary tract cancer, cervical cancer, cutaneous squamous cell carcinoma, endometrial cancer, esophageal cancer, gastric cancer, head and neck cancer, hepatocellular cancer, melanoma, Merkel cell carcinoma, mesothelioma, microsatellite instability-high or mismatch repair deficient solid tumors (including colorectal cancer), lung cancer, ovarian cancer, renal cell carcinoma, tumor mutational burden-high solid tumors, and urothelial carcinoma. Immune-mediated adverse reactions including colitis, pneumonitis, hepatitis, hypophysitis, nephritis, hypothyroidism/hyperthyroidism, and encephalitis have been reported with pembrolizumab; berahyaluronidase alfa use; treatment with high-dose corticosteroids may be necessary for patients who develop immune-mediated toxicity. Pembrolizumab; berahyaluronidase alfa was approved by the FDA in September 2025.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Pembrolizumab; berahyaluronidase alfa is a combination of pembrolizumab, a humanized monoclonal IgG4 kappa antibody, and berahyaluronidase, an endoglycosidase. Berahyaluronidase, a variant of human hyaluronidase PH20, increases permeability of the subcutaneous tissue by depolymerizing hyaluronan, a polysaccharide found in the extracellular matrix of subcutaneous tissue, and facilitates delivery of pembrolizumab administered subcutaneously. At the doses administered, the effects of berahyaluronidase are reversible and permeability of the subcutaneous tissue is restored within 24 to 48 hours. Pembrolizumab binds to the programmed death receptor-1 (PD-1) found on T-cells and blocks the interaction of PD-1 with its ligands, PD-L1 and PD-L2, on the tumor cell. Melanoma and some other solid tumors suppress cytotoxic T-cell activity by over-expressing PD-L1 on the cell surface. Blocking the PD-1/PD-L1 pathway inhibits T-cell proliferation and cytokine production. In syngeneic mouse tumor models, inhibiting PD-1 activity resulted in deceased tumor growth. Pembrolizumab differs from ipilimumab, another monoclonal antibody that binds to the cytotoxic T-lymphocyte Antigen-4 (CTLA-4) and blocks the interaction of CTLA-4 with its ligands, in that pembrolizumab blocks the PD-1/PD-L1 interaction peripherally at the tumor site whereas ipilimumab blocks the CTLA4/ligand interaction primarily in the lymphoid organs. Combination treatment of a PD-1 blocking antibody and lenvatinib decreased tumor-associated macrophages, increased activated cytotoxic T cells, and reduced tumor growth in syngeneic mouse tumor models compared to either treatment alone.

Do not dilute pembrolizumab; berahyaluronidase alfa. Do not shake.
If stored in the refrigerator, allow vial to come to room temperature.

Every 3-week dosing (395 mg pembrolizumab; 4,800 units berahyaluronidase alfa): Withdraw 2.4 mL into syringe.
Every 6-week dosing (790 mg pembrolizumab; 9,600 units berahyaluronidase alfa): Withdraw 4.8 mL into syringe.

Storage after preparation: If not used immediately, store with transfer needle and cap in place at room temperature at 20 to 25 degrees C (68 to 77 degrees F) for up to 8 hours, or refrigerated at 2 to 8 degrees C (36 to 46 degrees F) for up to 24 hours. The 24-hour period may include up to 8 hours at room temperature.

If stored in the refrigerator, allow the solution to come to room temperature for at least 30 minutes before administration.
Change the needle to a 25 to 30 gauge, half-inch, stainless steel hypodermic needle immediately prior to administration.
Do not inject into areas where the skin is tender, red, bruised, or hard. Alternate injection sites; ensure each injection site is at least 2.5 cm from the previous injection site.
Inject the appropriate volume of pembrolizumab; berahyaluronidase alfa subcutaneously into the thigh or abdomen:
Every 3-week dosing (395 mg pembrolizumab; 4,800 units berahyaluronidase alfa): 2.4 mL over 1 minute.
Every 6-week dosing (790 mg pembrolizumab; 9,600 units berahyaluronidase alfa): 4.8 mL over 2 minutes.
