    Headline
    infection
    General Information
    Avoid using deuruxolitinib in patients with an active, serious infection, including localized infections. Serious infections have been reported in patients receiving deuruxolitinib. The risk and benefits of treatment should be considered prior to initiating deuruxolitinib in patients: 1) with chronic or recurrent infection; 2) who have been exposed to tuberculosis; 3) with a history of a serious or an opportunistic infection; 4) who have resided or traveled in areas of endemic tuberculosis or endemic mycoses; or 5) with underlying conditions that may predispose them to infection (e.g., diabetes mellitus, human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) infection, chronic pulmonary disease, low lymphocyte counts, and pre-existing immunosuppression). Closely monitor patients for signs and symptoms of infection during and after treatment with deuruxolitinib. If a patient develops a serious or opportunistic infection, stop therapy until the infection resolves or is treated adequately. A patient who develops a new infection during treatment with deuruxolitinib should undergo prompt and complete diagnostic testing appropriate for an immunocompromised patient; appropriate antimicrobial therapy should be initiated, and the patient should be closely monitored.
    Headline
    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components.
    Headline
    Child-Pugh class C, hepatic failure
    General Information
    Deuruxolitinib is not recommended for use in patients with severe hepatic disease (Child-Pugh class C) or hepatic failure; however, no dose adjustments are needed in patients with mild or moderate hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh class A or Child-Pugh class B). Perform viral hepatitis screening in accordance with clinical guidelines before starting therapy with deuruxolitinib. Patients with evidence of HIV, hepatitis B, or hepatitis C infection were excluded from clinical trials. Use of deuruxolitinib is not recommended in patients with hepatitis B or C infection.
    Headline
    malignancy, tobacco smoking
    General Information
    Deuruxolitinib may increase the risk for a new primary malignancy. Malignancies, including non-melanoma skin cancer (NMSC), were observed in clinical trials of deuruxolitinib. In a large, randomized, postmarketing safety study of another JAK inhibitor in rheumatoid arthritis (RA) patients, a higher rate of malignancies (excluding non-melanoma skin cancer (NMSC)) and lymphoma was observed in patients treated with the JAK inhibitor compared to those treated with TNF blockers. Patients with a history of current or past tobacco smoking were at increased risk of lung cancer and overall cancers. Consider the risks and benefits of deuruxolitinib prior to initiating or continuing therapy in patients with a known malignancy other than a successfully treated NMSC. Non-melanoma skin cancer has been reported with deuruxolitinib use so periodic skin examination is recommended for patients who are at increased risk for skin cancer.

    General Information
    General Information
    Before starting deuruxolitinib, all necessary immunizations should be completed, including varicella zoster or prophylactic herpes zoster vaccination, in accordance with current immunization guidelines. Avoid use of live vaccines during or immediately prior to deuruxolitinib treatment.
    Headline
    cardiovascular disease, mortality, serious cardiovascular events, thromboembolism, thrombophilia
    General Information
    Avoid use of deuruxolitinib in patients who are at an increased risk of thrombosis (thrombophilia) and thromboembolism, including those with thromboembolic disease. Thrombosis, including pulmonary embolism (PE), deep vein thrombosis (DVT) and cerebral venous sinus thrombosis, has been reported with deuruxolitinib therapy. Thrombosis, including DVT, PE, and arterial thrombosis have been reported with other JAK inhibitors used to treat inflammatory conditions. Many of these adverse reactions were serious and some were fatal. In a large, randomized, postmarketing safety trial of another JAK inhibitor in subjects with RA 50 years of age and older with at least one cardiovascular (CV) risk factor, a higher rate of major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE) defined as CV death, non-fatal myocardial infarction, and non-fatal stroke was also observed for the JAK inhibitor compared to TNF blockers. An increased risk of all-cause mortality (including sudden CV death) was also observed for the JAK inhibitor compared to TNF blockers. Patients who are current or past smokers are at additional increased risk. Consider the benefits and risks before initiation or continuation of therapy, particularly in patients who are current or past smokers and in patients with cardiovascular disease or other cardiovascular risk factors. Patients should be informed about the symptoms of serious cardiovascular events and the steps to take if they occur. Advise patients of the potential increased risk for MACE and to seek emergency medical attention if they experience signs and symptoms of a heart attack, stroke, or blood clot. If a thromboembolic event occurs, discontinue the drug.
    Headline
    diverticulitis, peptic ulcer disease
    General Information
    Gastrointestinal (GI) perforations have been reported in clinical trials with deuruxolitinib. Monitor patients treated with deuruxolitinib who may be at increased risk for GI perforation (e.g., patients with a history of diverticulitis or peptic ulcer disease). For early identification of GI perforation, promptly evaluate patients presenting with new onset abdominal symptoms.

    renal failure, renal impairment
    General Information
    Deuruxolitinib is not recommended for use in patients with severe renal impairment or end-stage renal disease/renal failure (eGFR less than 30 mL/minute). No adjustment of dosage is required in patients with mild or moderate renal impairment.
    Headline
    CYP2C9 poor metabolizer
    General Information
    Deuruxolitinib is contraindicated in patients who are known or suspected to be a CYP2C9 poor metabolizer or patients who are on concomitant moderate or strong CYP2C9 inhibitors. Prior to deuruxolitinib treatment, patients should be tested for CYP2C9 variants to determine if they are poor metabolizers.
    Headline
    anemia, lymphopenia, neutropenia
    General Information
    Do not initiate deuruxolitinib in patients with anemia, defined as a hemoglobin less than 8 g/dL. If the hemoglobin decreases to less than 8 g/dL, interrupt dosing until hemoglobin values have normalized. Do not initiate deuruxolitinib in patients with lymphopenia or neutropenia, defined as an absolute lymphocyte count (ALC) less than 500 cells/mm3 or an absolute neutrophil count (ANC) less than 1,000 cells/mm3, respectively. Avoid or interrupt treatment in patients with an ANC less than 1,000 cells/mm3 or an ALC less than 500 cells/mm3 until the counts have recovered to values above these thresholds. Laboratory evaluations may be extended for patients on chronic therapy who develop hematologic abnormalities.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Based on animal reproduction studies, deuruxolitinib may cause fetal harm during pregnancy. Available data from pregnancies reported in clinical trials with deuruxolitinib are not sufficient to evaluate for a drug-associated risk for major birth defects, miscarriage, or adverse maternal or fetal outcomes. In animal reproduction studies, reduced fetal weight and increased skeletal malformation were observed in pregnant rats given oral deuruxolitinib at a dose 4.8 times the maximum recommended human dose (MRHD) during the period of organogenesis. In pregnant rabbits given oral deuruxolitinib at a dose 0.3 times the MRHD during the period of organogenesis, maternal toxicity, reduced fetal weight, and increased post-implantation loss was observed. Pregnant rats given oral deuruxolitinib at a dose 5 times the MRHD during pregnancy and lactation periods resulted in maternal toxicity, decreased pup survival, and adverse effects on postnatal development. Advise pregnant women of the potential risk to the fetus. Consider pregnancy planning and prevention for females of reproductive potential.
    Headline
    people who may become pregnant, reproductive risk
    General Information
    Counsel people who may become pregnant about the reproductive risk associated with deuruxolitinib. Based on animal studies, deuruxolitinib may cause fetal harm when administered during pregnancy.
    Headline
    diabetes mellitus
    General Information
    Use deuruxolitinib with caution in people with diabetes mellitus. Hypoglycemia, including severe hypoglycemia, has been reported in individuals with diabetes mellitus who have started other JAK inhibitors. During treatment with deuruxolitinib, consider increased blood glucose monitoring as clinically indicated in individuals with diabetes. Advise individuals to notify their health care provider if they develop signs or symptoms of hypoglycemia.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Avoid breast-feeding while taking deuruxolitinib and for 1 day after the last dose. Deuruxolitinib has the potential to cause serious side effects in the breastfed child. There are no data on the presence of deuruxolitinib in human milk, its effects on the breastfed child, or its effects on milk production. Deuruxolitinib is present in animal milk.

Description
  Text
  Deuruxolitinib is an oral Janus kinase (JAK) inhibitor for the treatment of severe alopecia areata in adults. Janus kinases are intracellular enzymes that transmit signals arising from cytokine interactions on the cellular membrane to influence cellular processes of immune cell function. Janus kinase (JAK) inhibitors target the JAK1 and JAK2 pathways, which are implicated in the pathogenesis of alopecia areata. The FDA-approved prescribing information contains a Black Box Warning for serious infections, mortality, malignancy, major adverse cardiac events (MACE), and thrombosis. Deuruxolitinib was FDA-approved in July 2024.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Deuruxolitinib is a Janus kinase (JAK) inhibitor. JAKs mediate the signaling of a number of cytokines and growth factors important for hematopoiesis and immune function. JAK signaling involves recruitment of STATs (signal transducers and activators of transcription) to cytokine receptors, activation and subsequent localization of STATs to the nucleus leading to modulation of gene expression. In an in vitro kinase activity assay, deuruxolitinib had greater inhibitory potency for JAK1, JAK2 and TYK2 relative to JAK3. The relevance of inhibition of JAK enzymes to therapeutic effectiveness is not currently known.

  Text
  Deuruxolitinib is administered orally. The steady state volume of distribution is approximately 50L. Plasma protein binding is 91.5% and the blood to plasma concentration ratio is approximately 1.3. After a single dose of radiolabeled deuruxolitinib, there was no unchanged dose recovered in either urine or feces. Deuruxolitinib is primarily metabolized by CYP2C9 (76%) and CYP3A4 (21%) and to a lesser extent by CYP1A2 (3%). The two most abundant human metabolites are C-21714 and C-21717, each of which accounts for approximately 5% of total drug-related AUC. Both metabolites are approximately 10-fold less pharmacologically active than deuruxolitinib. The mean elimination half-life is approximately 4 hours.
 
Affected cytochrome P450 (CYP450) isoenzymes and drug transporters: CYP2C9, CYP3A4, and CYP1A2
In vitro studies indicate that deuruxolitinib is not an inhibitor of CYP1A2, CYP2B6, CYP2C8, CYP2C19, CYP2D6, CYP3A4, OATP1B1, OATP1B3, and OCT1 but is an inhibitor of BCRP, BSEP, OAT3 and MATE2-K. Deuruxolitinib is not an inducer of CYP1A2 or CYP2B6, CYP2C8 or CYP2C19. Deuruxolitinib is a substrate of BCRP and MDR1 but not a substrate of the uptake transporters OATP1B1 and OATP1B3.
