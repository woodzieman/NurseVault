    Headline
    Drug Interactions
    General Information
    The coadministration of certain medications may lead to harm and require avoidance or therapy modification; review all drug interactions prior to concomitant use of other medications.
    Headline
    serious hypersensitivity reactions or anaphylaxis
    General Information
    Serious hypersensitivity reactions or anaphylaxis have been reported during or after the administration of raxibacumab infusion. Administration of raxibacumab requires a specialized care setting where appropriate equipment, medication (including epinephrine) and personnel trained in the management of hypersensitivity, anaphylaxis, and shock are available. Diphenhydramine premedication does not prevent anaphylaxis and may mask or delay the onset of symptoms of hypersensitivity. Monitor patients closely during the infusion and for a period of time after administration. If hypersensitivity reactions or anaphylaxis occur, interrupt or stop the raxibacumab infusion immediately and treat appropriately.
    Headline
    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Available data on the use of raxibacumab during pregnancy are insufficient to evaluate a drug-associated risk of major birth defects, miscarriage, or other adverse outcomes. Limited data from case reports of anthrax infection in pregnant individuals indicate that infection during pregnancy is associated with a high risk of maternal, fetal, and neonatal deaths, particularly in the absence of treatment. In animal studies, raxibacumab was not associated with teratogenicity or other adverse developmental outcomes at 3 times the human maximum plasma concentration at the maximum recommended adult dose. Maternal toxicity was observed as reduced body weight gain late in gestation, but there was no difference in mean total weight gain.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Use raxibacumab with caution during breast-feeding. There are no data on the presence of raxibacumab in human milk, its effects on the breast-fed child, or its effect on milk production. Maternal IgG is present in human milk. Consider the benefits of breast-feeding, the patient's clinical need for treatment, and any potential adverse effects on the breast-fed child from the medication or from the patient's underlying medical condition.

Adverse Reactions

  Adverse Reactions
    Headline
    anaphylactoid reactions, chills, erythema, hypotension, maculopapular rash, pruritus, rash, serious hypersensitivity reactions or anaphylaxis, urticaria
    General Information
    Serious hypersensitivity reactions or anaphylaxis have been reported during or after the administration of raxibacumab infusion. Premedicate with diphenhydramine within 1 hour prior to administration to reduce the risk or occurrence and/or the severity of a hypersensitivity reaction. Rash, urticaria, pruritus, chills, chest and throat tightness, lip and throat swelling, and hypotension, were reported in 4.5% of healthy adults during clinical trials. Anaphylaxis (anaphylactoid reactions) occurred in 2 patients during the raxibacumab infusion. Some patients required interruption or discontinuation of the raxibacumab infusion as well as additional treatment that included steroids, diphenhydramine, H2 blockers, and/or intravenous fluids. Monitor patients closely during the infusion and for a period of time after administration. If hypersensitivity reactions or anaphylaxis occur, interrupt or stop the raxibacumab infusion immediately and treat appropriately. Rash, including erythematous rash (erythema) and maculopapular rash, was reported in 3.2% of healthy adults, and pruritus was reported in 2.5% of healthy adults during clinical trials. Mild to moderate infusion-related rashes were reported in 22.2% of patients who did not receive diphenhydramine as compared to 3.3% of those premedicated.

Description
  Text
  Raxibacumab is a human monoclonal antibody against Bacillus anthracis protective antigen for the treatment of inhalation anthrax. It was developed for emergency use, and 20,000 doses of the drug were sent to the US Strategic National Stockpile in the first quarter of 2009. Specifically, it was developed under a contract entered into in 2006 with the Biomedical Advanced Research and Development Authority of the Office of the Assistant Secretary for Preparedness and Response, U.S. Department of Health and Human Services. Raxibacumab is the first monoclonal antibody approved under the FDA’s Animal Efficacy Rule, which allows efficacy findings from adequate and well-controlled animal studies to support FDA approval when it is not feasible or ethical to conduct trials in humans. In this case, because inhalational anthrax is a rare and lethal disease, it is not possible to conduct adequate efficacy trials in humans. Raxibacumab was FDA approved for the treatment and prophylaxis of inhalational anthrax in adult and pediatric patients in December 2012.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Raxibacumab is a monoclonal antibody that binds the protective antigen (PA) of Bacillus anthracis. This inhibits the binding of PA to its cellular receptors, preventing the intracellular entry of the anthrax lethal factor and edema factor, the enzymatic toxin components responsible for the pathogenic effects of anthrax toxin.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Intravenous Route
    Description
    The pharmacokinetics of raxibacumab are linear in single IV doses up to 40 mg/kg. In healthy volunteers, the mean Cmax was 1020.3 +/- 140.6 mcg/ml and the mean AUC was 15,845 +/- 4333.5 mcg x day/ml in single-dose studies of 40 mg/kg IV. Because efficacy cannot be tested in humans, a comparison to exposures in animals in necessary. Humans achieve similar or greater systemic exposure after a single 40 mg/kg IV dose compared to animal models. Although raxibacumab is intended for single dose administration, the pharmacokinetics after a second dose were studied. When a second dose was administered 14 days after the first dose (n=23), the mean concentration at 28 days after the second dose was approximately twice the mean concentration at 14 days after the first dose. In a study of 20 healthy patients receiving a second dose of raxibacumab at least 4 months after their first dose, no statistically significant differences were noted in the AUC, CL, or half-life. The mean Cmax after the second dose was 15% lower than with the first dose.

      Name
      Intravenous Administration
      Description
      Dilution:
Each single-use vial contains 1700 mg/34 mL (50 mg/mL).
For adult patients, dilute raxibacumab to a final volume of 250 mL with 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection.
For pediatric patients, dilute raxibacumab to a final volume based on patient weight:
Less than 1 kg: 7 mL with 0.45% or 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection.
1 to 1.9 kg: 15 mL with 0.45% or 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection.
2 to 2.9 kg: 20 mL with 0.45% or 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection.
3 to 4.9 kg: 25 mL with 0.45% or 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection.
5 to 10 kg: 40 mL with 0.45% or 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection.
10.1 to 14.9 kg: 50 mL with 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection.
15 to 29.9 kg: 100 mL with 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection.
30 to 40 kg: 125 mL with 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection.
More than 40 kg: 150 mL with 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection.
For patients requiring maximal fluid restriction, dilution factors can be adjusted to a maximal concentration of 32 mg/mL.
Based on the total infusion volume necessary, prepare either a syringe or an infusion bag as appropriate.
 
Syringe preparation:
Select an appropriate size syringe for the total volume of infusion to be administered.
Using the selected syringe, withdraw the appropriate volume of raxibacumab from the raxibacumab vial(s).
Withdraw an appropriate amount of compatible solution to prepare a total infusion volume.
Gently mix the solution. Do not shake.
Discard any unused portion remaining in the raxibacumab vial(s).
Storage: The prepared solution is stable for 8 hours at room temperature.
 
Infusion bag preparation:
Select an appropriate size infusion bag of compatible solution and withdraw a volume of solution from the bag equal to the calculated volume (mL) of raxibacumab. Discard the withdrawn solution.
Withdraw the required volume of raxibacumab from the vial(s).
Transfer the required volume of raxibacumab to the selected infusion bag.
Gently invert the bag to mix the solution. Do not shake.
Discard any unused portion of the remaining raxibacumab vial(s).
Storage: The prepared solution is stable for 8 hours at room temperature.
 
Intravenous infusion:
Administer in a monitored setting appropriately equipped to manage hypersensitivity, anaphylaxis, and shock.
Premedicate with diphenhydramine within 1 hour prior to the raxibacumab infusion. Diphenhydramine 25 to 50 mg should be used for adults. Specific diphenhydramine dosing is not described for pediatric patients. Diphenhydramine route of administration (oral or IV) should be based on the temporal proximity to the start of the raxibacumab infusion.
Infuse raxibacumab IV over 2 hours and 15 minutes.
For adult patients, infuse at 15 mL/hour for the first 20 minutes, then 125 mL/hour.
For pediatric patients, the infusion rate is based on patient weight:
Less than 1 kg: 0.5 mL/hour for the first 20 minutes, then 3.5 mL/hour.
1 to 1.9 kg: 1 mL/hour for the first 20 minutes, then 7 mL/hour.
2 to 2.9 kg: 1.2 mL/hour for the first 20 minutes, then 10 mL/hour.
3 to 4.9 kg: 1.5 mL/hour for the first 20 minutes, then 12 mL/hour.
5 to 10 kg: 3 mL/hour for the first 20 minutes, then 20 mL/hour.
10.1 to 14.9 kg: 6 mL/hour for the first 20 minutes, then 25 mL/hour.
15 to 29.9 kg: 6 mL/hour for the first 20 minutes, then 50 mL/hour.
30 to 40 kg: 15 mL/hour for the first 20 minutes, then 62.5 mL/hour.
More than 40 kg: 15 mL/hour for the first 20 minutes, then 75 mL/hour.
Interrupt or stop the infusion and treat appropriately if hypersensitivity reactions or anaphylaxis occur.
