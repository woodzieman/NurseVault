Drug Class: Fosfomycin is an epoxide antibacterial indicated for the treatment of uncomplicated and complicated urinary tract infection (UTI), including pyelonephritis.

Routes: injection (IV/IM/SC), oral; Common adult doses: 18 g/day IV and 3 g PO as a single dose; however, doses up to 3 g PO once daily have been used off-label.

Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  General Information
  It is unknown if fosfomycin is excreted in human breast milk. Because many drugs are excreted in breast milk, use of fosfomycin in breast-feeding mothers should be undertaken with caution.The safe and effective use of fosfomycin in children under 12 years of age has not been established in adequate and well-controlled studies.Fosfomycin is classified pregnancy category B. There are, however, no adequate and well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Fosfomycin should be used during pregnancy only if clearly needed.Almost all antibacterial agents have been associated with pseudomembranous colitis (antibiotic-associated colitis) which may range in severity from mild to life-threatening. In the colon, overgrowth of Clostridia may exist when normal flora is altered subsequent to antibacterial administration. The toxin produced by Clostridium difficile is a primary cause of pseudomembranous colitis. It is known that systemic use of antibiotics predisposes patients to development of pseudomembranous colitis. Consideration should be given to the diagnosis of pseudomembranous colitis in patients presenting with diarrhea following antibacterial administration. Systemic antibiotics should be prescribed with caution to patients with inflammatory bowel disease such as ulcerative colitis or other GI disease. If diarrhea develops during therapy, the drug should be discontinued. Following diagnosis of pseudomembranous colitis, therapeutic measures should be instituted. In milder cases, the colitis may respond to discontinuation of the offending agent. In moderate to severe cases, fluids and electrolytes, protein supplementation, and treatment with an antibacterial effective against Clostridium difficile may be warranted. Products inhibiting peristalsis are contraindicated in this clinical situation. Practitioners should be aware that antibiotic-associated colitis has been observed to occur over two months or more following discontinuation of systemic antibiotic therapy; a careful medical history should be taken.

    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components. Before initiating therapy with fosfomycin, it is important to inquire about previous hypersensitivity reactions to oral or parenteral fosfomycin.
    Headline
    heart failure, hepatic cirrhosis, hepatic failure, hyperaldosteronism, hypernatremia, hypertension, pulmonary edema, sodium restriction
    General Information
    Monitor for signs of edema during intravenous fosfomycin therapy, particularly in individuals requiring sodium restriction or those predisposed to fluid overload, such as individuals with heart failure, hepatic cirrhosis, severe hepatic failure, hypertension, hyperaldosteronism, hypernatremia, or pulmonary edema. Each vial of fosfomycin injection contains a high sodium load; therefore, a low-sodium diet is recommended during intravenous fosfomycin treatment. Also, minimize concomitant administration of other sodium-containing medications during intravenous fosfomycin treatment. Monitor serum electrolyte concentrations (i.e., sodium, potassium, calcium, magnesium, and phosphorus) and fluid status during intravenous fosfomycin treatment. Electrolyte supplementation may be necessary in some cases.
    Headline
    bradycardia, cardiomyopathy, congenital long QT syndrome, coronary artery disease, females, geriatric, hypocalcemia, hypokalemia, hypomagnesemia, QT prolongation, ventricular arrhythmias
    General Information
    Avoid use of intravenous fosfomycin in people with baseline QT prolongation or who have conditions that may increase the risk of QT prolongation or torsade de pointes, including bradycardia, congenital long QT syndrome, hypocalcemia, hypokalemia, hypomagnesemia, geriatric adults, females, structural abnormalities that interfere with electrical conduction (e.g., cardiomyopathy, coronary artery disease, ischemic heart disease), or in those who have other additional risk factors for QT prolongation or torsade de pointes. The use of other medications that have been associated with QT prolongation or torsade de pointes may further increase risk. Also avoid use of intravenous fosfomycin in individuals with ventricular arrhythmias, including a history of torsade de pointes. The risk of QT prolongation with intravenous fosfomycin may also be increased in individuals with conditions that have the potential to cause electrolyte imbalance. Monitor electrolytes during treatment with intravenous fosfomycin.
    Headline
    bone marrow suppression
    General Information
    Monitor complete blood counts during intravenous fosfomycin therapy, particularly in individuals with pre-existing bone marrow suppression or other conditions or concomitant medications that may predispose to bone marrow suppression. Neutropenia has been reported with intravenous fosfomycin therapy.
    Headline
    renal failure, renal impairment
    General Information
    Intravenous fosfomycin dosage adjustment is required in individuals with renal impairment or renal failure, specifically estimated CrCl of 50 mL/minute or less. Monitor estimated CrCl and adjust the intravenous fosfomycin dosage accordingly. Due to the potential to cause electrolyte imbalance, renal impairment may also increase the risk of QT prolongation with intravenous fosfomycin. Monitor for signs of edema during intravenous fosfomycin therapy, particularly in individuals predisposed to fluid overload, such as individuals with renal impairment. Monitor serum electrolyte concentrations (i.e., sodium, potassium, calcium, magnesium, and phosphorus) and fluid status during intravenous fosfomycin treatment. Electrolyte supplementation may be necessary in some cases.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Available human data on fosfomycin use during pregnancy are limited but reassuring. Fosfomycin crosses the placenta; however, single-dose administration is generally considered safe during pregnancy. Observational data suggest that single-dose oral fosfomycin use during pregnancy is well tolerated, and guidelines recommend single-dose fosfomycin for the treatment of asymptomatic bacteriuria in pregnant individuals. In population-based cohort study, first-trimester exposure to oral fosfomycin (n = 2724) was not associated with an increased risk of major congenital abnormalities compared with nitrofurantoin exposure (n = 650; 2% vs. 2.5%; adjusted OR = 0.8 [0.44 to 1.47]) or with no exposure to either drug (n = 133,502; 2% vs. 2.1%; adjusted OR = 0 .97 [0.73 to 1.3]). A smaller prospective observational cohort study also found no increase risk of major birth defects or spontaneous abortion after first-trimester fosfomycin exposure. Major birth defects occurred in 1 of 146 fosfomycin-exposed infants (0.7%; 95% CI, 0.04 to 4.33%) compared with 15 of 399 unexposed infants (3.8%; 95% CI, 2.2% to 6.26%). Spontaneous abortion occurred in 5 of 152 fosfomycin-exposed pregnancies versus 53 of 456 comparison pregnancies, with cumulative incidences of 6.2% and 23.1%, respectively (adjusted HR, 0.35; 95% CI, 0.14 to 0.9). Animal data meeting current standards for developmental toxicity studies are lacking. Limited published animal data found no malformations after intravenous or intraperitoneal fosfomycin sodium administration in rabbits or rats, respectively, although fetotoxicity was observed; the clinical relevance of these findings is uncertain.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Use oral fosfomycin with caution during breast-feeding. FDA-approved labeling for intravenous fosfomycin recommends avoiding breast-feeding during treatment and for 24 hours after the last dose due to the potential for adverse reactions in the breastfed child. Limited data suggest that fosfomycin is present in human milk at low concentrations. After administration of 1 to 2 g of fosfomycin injection in 2 lactating individuals, colostrum concentrations averaged 4.8 mg/L and milk concentrations averaged 3.6 mg/L. Fosfomycin absorption is largely dependent on its salt form. Fosfomycin present in human milk would likely be in the poorly absorbed calcium salt form due to the calcium content of milk, reducing the potential for exposure via breast milk. It is unlikely to cause adverse effects in the breastfed child.

Description
  Text
  Fosfomycin is an epoxide antibacterial indicated for the treatment of uncomplicated and complicated urinary tract infection (UTI), including pyelonephritis. Fosfomycin tromethamine is converted to the free acid, fosfomycin, after oral administration. Fosfomycin is bactericidal in the urine at therapeutic doses. There is no known cross-resistance between fosfomycin and other antibacterial drug classes. Intravenous fosfomycin is associated with elevated hepatic enzymes, neutropenia, QT prolongation, and electrolyte abnormalities; fosfomycin tromethamine has lower bioavailability and is not associated with these adverse events. Fosfomycin was FDA-approved in 1996.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Fosfomycin is an epoxide antibacterial that disrupts bacterial cell wall synthesis by covalently binding and irreversibly inhibiting phosphoenolpyruvate transferase (MurA) by acting as a phosphoenolpyruvate analogue resulting in inactivation of the enzyme enolpyruvyl transferase, which is required for peptidoglycan formation. Therefore, fosfomycin inhibits the first step in peptidoglycan biosynthesis, resulting in bacterial cell lysis and death. Transport of fosfomycin into the bacterial cell well occurs via 2 different transport systems, glycerol-3-phosphate (GlpT) and/or hexose-6-phosphate (UhpT). It also reduces adherence of bacteria to uroepithelial cells.
 
Pharmacodynamics
For fosfomycin, the ratio of area under the concentration-time curve to minimal inhibitory concentration (AUC/MIC) appears to best correlate with antibacterial activity. However, some studies have suggested that the amount of time free (non-protein bound) drug concentrations exceed the minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC) of the organism (free T above MIC) may be important.
 
Susceptibility
The susceptibility interpretive criteria for fosfomycin are delineated by pathogen. For intravenous fosfomycin, the MICs are defined for E. coli as susceptible at 8 mcg/mL or less, intermediate at 16 mcg/mL, and resistant at 32 mcg/mL or more. The MICs are defined for K. pneumoniae as susceptible at 32 mcg/mL or less, intermediate at 64 mcg/mL, and resistant at 128 mcg/mL or more. These susceptibility interpretive criteria are based on a dosage of fosfomycin 6 g IV every 8 hours infused over 1 hour in individuals with an estimated creatinine clearance of 50 mL/minute. For oral fosfomycin, the MICs are defined for urinary isolates of E.coli and E. faecalis as susceptible at 64 mcg/mL or less, intermediate at 128 mcg/mL, and resistant at 256 mcg/mL or more.
 
Resistance
Resistance to fosfomycin may occur by chromosomal mutations leading to alterations of bacterial transport systems and/or modifications of the fosfomycin binding site in MurA (Cys115). Mutations in the structural transport genes (glpT and uhpT), transport regulatory genes (uhpA, uhpB, uhpC), and genes involved in cAMP synthesis (cyaA, ptsI) can cause a decrease in fosfomycin uptake. Inactivation of cAMP-CRP may also diminish transport system activity. Plasmid-borne resistance may result in enzymatic inactivation of fosfomycin by binding to glutathione or by cleavage of the carbon-phosphorus-bond in the fosfomycin molecule. The enzymes responsible for this type of resistance are fosfomycin hydrolyzing enzymes (FosA, FosB, FosX) and fosfomycin kinases (FomA, FomB, and FosC).

    Description

Dosage Adjustment Guidelines
  Hepatic Impairment
  Specific guidelines for dosage adjustments in hepatic impairment are not available; it appears that no dosage adjustments are needed.
  Renal Impairment
  Oral
Specific guidelines for dosage adjustments in renal impairment are not available; it appears that no dosage adjustments are needed.
 
Intravenous
CrCl more than 50 mL/minute: No dosage adjustment is needed.
CrCl 41 to 50 mL/minute: 6 g IV loading dose, then 4 g IV every 8 hours.
CrCl 31 to 40 mL/minute: 6 g IV loading dose, then 3 g IV every 8 hours.
CrCl 21 to 30 mL/minute: 6 g IV loading dose, then 5 g IV every 24 hours.
CrCl 11 to 20 mL/minute: 6 g IV loading dose, then 3 g IV every 24 hours.
 
Intermittent Hemodialysis
Approximately 60% to 80% of the administered fosfomycin dose is removed by hemodialysis. Administer intravenous fosfomycin after hemodialysis on dialysis days.
