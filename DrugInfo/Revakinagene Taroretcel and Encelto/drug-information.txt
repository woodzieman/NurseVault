Drug Class: Revakinagene taroretcel is an allogeneic encapsulated cell-based gene therapy indicated for the treatment of idiopathic macular telangiectasia type 2 (MacTel).

Routes: ophthalmic; Common adult doses: One implant per affected eye.

Common Side Effects (excluding routine GI upset): implant migration, keratoconjunctivitis, ocular discharge, ocular irritation, ocular pain, ocular pruritus, uveitis, vitreous hemorrhage

In an Overdose: No overdose/antidote information is listed in this file.

Key Nursing Considerations: Contraindications/precautions: Delayed dark adaption may impair one's ability to see objects, pedestrians, or road signs when moving rapidly from a brightly lit environment to a dimly lit environment.     pregnancy
    General Information
    There are no data on the use of revakinagene taroretcel during pregnancy. Endogenous ciliary neurotropic factor (CNTF) is naturally found in maternal plasma, placental cells, and umbilical cord blood. It is not known if the use of revakinagene taroretcel increases CNTF above naturally occurring levels in these tissues. In animal reproduction studies, subcutaneous administration of recombinant human ciliary neurotropic factor (rhCNTF) to pregnant rats and rabbits demonstrated no evidence of teratogenic effects on the fetus. However, when administered to rabbits at a dose level of 10 mcg/kg/day, a decrease in implantations and live fetuses was observed. When administered to rats at a dose level of 100 mcg/kg/day, a decrease in corpora lutea was observed.

    General Information
    Vitreous hemorrhage (8.5%), which may result in temporary vision loss, has occurred following revakinagene taroretcel implantation. Patients receiving antithrombotic medication (e.g., oral anticoagulants, aspirin, nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs) may be at increased risk of vitreous hemorrhage. To reduce the risk of vitreous hemorrhage, interrupt antithrombotic medications prior to the revakinagene taroretcel implantation. Vitrectomy surgery may be necessary to clear severe, recurrent, or non-clearing vitreous hemorrhage. If the patient has a late onset vitreous hemorrhage (greater than one year following revakinagene taroretcel implantation surgery), examine the revakinagene taroretcel implantation site for possible implant migration or extrusion. If implant migration or extrusion has occurred, surgically reposition revakinagene taroretcel. Implant extrusion through the initial scleral wound has occurred following revakinagene taroretcel implantation. Signs and symptoms of implant extrusion include recurrent uveitis, vitreous hemorrhage, ocular pain (15%) more than one year after implantation, or visibility of titanium fixation loop under the conjunctiva. To reduce the risk of implant extrusion, carefully follow the specific surgical steps for revakinagene taroretcel. Ocular discomfort (9%), dry eye (keratoconjunctivitis) (6%), ocular pruritus (9%), ocular irritation (5.1%), and ocular discharge (3.4%) were also reported during clinical trials of revakinagene taroretcel.

Description
  Text
  Revakinagene taroretcel is an allogeneic encapsulated cell-based gene therapy indicated for the treatment of idiopathic macular telangiectasia type 2 (MacTel). Macular telangiectasia type 2 is a rare eye disease where blood vessels in the macula become dilated and leak, causing central vision loss. The efficacy of revakinagene taroretcel was evaluated in two phase studies, and demonstrated that revakinagene taroretcel significantly slowed the loss of macular photoreceptors in MacTel patients over 24 months. Therapy is delivered by an intravitreal implant inserted surgically by a qualified ophthalmologist. Revakinagene taroretcel was FDA-approved in 2025.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Revakinagene taroretcel is an allogeneic encapsulated cell-based gene therapy. It secretes recombinant human ciliary neurotrophic factor (rhCNTF), which is one of several neurotrophic factors endogenously produced by neurons and supporting glial cells. Exogenous ciliary neurotrophic factor (CNTF) is thought to initially target Muller glia to trigger a cascade of signaling events that may promote photoreceptor survival; however, the mechanism of action for revakinagene taroretcel is not completely understood.

Systemic exposure of recombinant human ciliary neurotropic factor was measured in 2 distribution studies in rabbits and in 2 toxicology studies in minipigs. Overall, there was no evidence of systemic exposure to recombinant human ciliary neurotropic factor after implantation of revakinagene taroretcel in rabbits for periods up to 9 months or in minipigs for periods of up to 6 months. Following intraocular implantation of a single revakinagene taroretcel dose in rabbits at 12 weeks, the mean Cmax of recombinant human ciliary neurotropic factor in the vitreous and aqueous was 2.0 and 0.3 ng/mL, respectively, and below the level of quantitation in the serum and contralateral, untreated eye. Similarly, in human patients, recombinant human ciliary neurotropic factor levels were below the limit for the level of quantitation in the serum.

    Intravitreal Administration
NOTE: Implant insertion is a surgical procedure performed under aseptic conditions by a qualified ophthalmologist. Refer to the product "Instructions for Use" for detailed guidance.
Pre-Surgical Preparation
Inspect the revakinagene taroretcel implant packaging for any signs of damage or leakage.
Verify the use-by date.
Confirm that the disposable temperature recording device displays a green check-mark at the top of the screen.
Ensure the liquid medium is at the correct pH using the provided pH color guide reference card.
Prepare the surgical field properly.
Surgical Steps
Preparing the Surgical Site
Create a 7x7 mm peritomy of the conjunctiva and Tenon's capsule at the selected implantation site.
Place a corneal-limbal traction suture in the selected surgical quadrant (either inferotemporal or inferonasal).
Maintain hemostasis of the underlying sclera and conjunctiva.
Using an MVR and 15-degree blade, create a 3.0 mm full-thickness sclerotomy 3.75 mm posterior and parallel to the limbus (Figure 2). Do not insert the revakinagene taroretcel implant outside of the pars plana.
Confirm:
The incision is full thickness.
There is an adequate hemostasis.
There is no spanning uveal tissue.
Preparing the revakinagene taroretcel implant
Open the inner container and expose the upper compartment and luer lock cap.
Unlock the luer lock cap by turning it counterclockwise once.
Lift the luer lock cap vertically to remove the revakinagene taroretcel implant (attached to the gripper).
Rinse the revakinagene taroretcel implant with at least 5 mL of sterile Balanced Saline Solution (BSS).
Keep the revakinagene taroretcel implant moist by applying BSS every 10 minutes until insertion.
While holding the luer lock cap, pass a double-armed 9-0 polypropylene suture needle through the revakinagene taroretcel implant's fixation loop.
Implantation of the revakinagene taroretcel implant
Gently open the sclerotomy incision and insert the revakinagene taroretcel implant perpendicularly into the eye.
Ensure only the fixation loop is exposed.
Release the revakinagene taroretcel implant from the gripper by squeezing the indicated region with forceps or a fine needle holder.
Securing the Implant
Secure the revakinagene taroretcel implant by creating a 3-1-1 anchor knot with the polypropylene suture at the apex of the fixation loop.
Confirm the revakinagene taroretcel implant is centered in the incision.
Pass each suture arm centrally through either side of the wound at 90-99% scleral depth.
Pull up the suture ends and confirm that the fixation loop is at the proper depth (90 to 99%).
Tie down the suture to the sclera with a 3-1-1 knot, ensuring the knot is placed away from the incision.
If a suture breaks, leave the tail as long as possible and lay it flat.
Take a 2.0 mm scleral bite at 50 to 75% depth beyond the sclerotomy on each side.
Closing the Incision
Close the scleral incision with 9-0 nylon sutures, ensuring:
The polypropylene suture is captured to prevent irritation and erosion.
All nylon suture knots are rotated into the sclera.
The closure is watertight.
Pull the polypropylene suture end taut and cut it flush to the sclera.
Close the conjunctiva and Tenon's capsule using 6-0 plain gut or chromic suture, or 7-0 Vicryl suture or similar.
Ensure Tenon's capsule covers the insertion site and use 3-point fixation and scleral bites.
Administer sub-conjunctival steroid injection: dexamethasone, 2 mg/0.5 mL (4 mg/mL) or equivalent. If the case is complicated and inflammation is anticipated, a higher dose of dexamethasone (0.5 cc of 10 mg/mL) or equivalent may be used, at the surgeon's discretion.
Perform indirect ophthalmoscopy to confirm placement of the revakinagene taroretcel implant in the vitreous and that there are no intraocular complications. Failure to perform indirect ophthalmoscopy can lead to unidentified malpositioning of the revakinagene taroretcel implant and intraocular complications.
Post-Operative Wound Care
The patient is to use:
A topical antibiotic solution at a frequency of 1 drop four times a day for 7 days.
A steroid drop taper of prednisolone acetate 1% (or equivalent) starting the day after surgery with the following taper:
1 drop four times a day for the first 7 days;
1 drop three times a day for the next 7 days;
1 drop two times a day for the next 7 days;
1 drop once a day for the last 7 days.
 
Removal Procedure
Preparing the Surgical Site
Create a 7x7 mm peritomy of the conjunctiva and Tenon's capsule to expose the insertion site.
Place a corneal-limbal traction suture in the quadrant where the revakinagene taroretcel implant is located.
Maintain hemostasis of the sclera and surrounding conjunctiva.
Establishing Infusion & Vitrectomy
Place an infusion cannula in the inferior quadrant (opposite the revakinagene taroretcel implant).
Confirm the infusion line is positioned within the vitreous cavity before opening the infusion.
Insert two superior cannulas following normal pars plana vitrectomy protocol.
Perform a thorough vitrectomy to remove vitreous surrounding the revakinagene taroretcel implant without disrupting the hollow fiber membrane.
Reopening the Sclerotomy
Locate the revakinagene taroretcel implant incision and remove the two nylon sutures while leaving the polypropylene suture intact.
Using an MVR blade, carefully dissect open the original scleral incision down to the revakinagene taroretcel implant cap at the base of the fixation loop.
Extend the incision along the entire 3.0 mm length to full thickness.
Cut the polypropylene anchor suture on the anterior side of the knot.
Turn off or lower infusion pressure.
Removing the revakinagene taroretcel implant
Fully open the pars plana sclerotomy and confirm there is no spanning uveal tissue.
Identify and grasp the fixation loop.
Cut off the remaining polypropylene knot.
Remove the revakinagene taroretcel implant from the eye.
Inspect the revakinagene taroretcel implant capsule for any damage or penetration.
Do not discard or dispose of the revakinagene taroretcel implant. Call and report to 1-833-963-9275. The appropriate action will be taken to initiate the return of the revakinagene taroretcel implant and possible replacement.
Closing the Incision
Remove any prolapsed vitreous.
Close the sclerotomy with interrupted 7-0 Vicryl sutures for a watertight closure.
Remove the infusion line and additional cannulas.
Close the conjunctiva with 6-0 plain gut sutures or equivalent.
Post-Operative Wound Care
The patient is to use:
A topical antibiotic solution at a frequency of 1 drop four times a day for 7 days.
A steroid drop taper of prednisolone acetate 1% (or equivalent) starting the day after surgery with the following taper:
1 drop four times a day for the first 7 days;
1 drop three times a day for the next 7 days;
1 drop two times a day for the next 7 days;
1 drop once a day for the last 7 days.
