Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  General Information
  Latanoprost should not be used in patients with closed-angle glaucoma, or inflammatory or neovascular glaucoma. There is limited experience with latanoprost in these patients.Latanoprost should be used with caution in patients with aphakia, pseudophakic patients with a torn posterior lens capsule, and patients with known risk factors for macular edema. Macular edema, including cystoid macular edema, has been reported during treatment with this drug.Latanoprost may gradually change eye color, increasing the amount of brown pigment in the iris. This change may be permanent. Patients should be informed of the possibility of iridal discoloration. Some patients may also develop photophobia and may be more sensitive to sunlight (UV) exposure.Latanoprost should be used with caution in patients with active intraocular inflammation (e.g., iritis, uveitis).Latanoprost eye solution is formulated with the preservative benzalkonium chloride, which may be absorbed by soft contact lenses. Users of soft contact lenses should not administer latanoprost while wearing the lenses.The use of multiple dose containers of ophthalmic products has been associated with bacterial keratitis. Inadvertent contamination of the latanoprost containers may increase the risk of infection in ocular surgery patients, or in patients who develop an ocular infection or ocular trauma, including corneal abrasion. If there is any damage to the ocular epithelial surface, latanoprost should be used with caution.Latanoprost is classified as pregnancy risk category C. Although no adequate human studies have examined the effects of this drug on the fetus, animal reproduction studies have shown adverse fetal effects. Therefore, in making the decision to administer this drug during pregnancy, the potential risks to the fetus must be weighed against the potential benefits to the mother. It is not known if latanoprost or its metabolites are excreted in breast milk. Because of the potential risk for adverse reactions in nursing infants, latanoprost is not recommended in breast-feeding women.The safety and efficacy of latanoprost has not been established in children.Latanoprost should be used cautiously in patients with renal disease (e.g., renal failure, renal impairment) or hepatic disease. There have been no studies on safe use in these patients.

    Headline
    contact lenses
    General Information
    Persons wearing contact lenses should be instructed to remove them before instilling latanoprost ophthalmic drops. The ophthalmic solution is formulated with the preservative benzalkonium chloride, which may be absorbed by soft contact lenses. Lenses may be reinserted 15 minutes after drug administration.
    Headline
    herpes simplex virus epithelial keratitis, iritis, uveitis
    General Information
    Use latanoprost with caution in individuals with active intraocular inflammation (e.g., iritis, uveitis). Use of latanoprost in these individuals may exacerbate inflammation. The use of multiple dose containers of ophthalmic products has been associated with bacterial keratitis. Inadvertent contamination of the latanoprost containers may increase the risk of infection in individuals undergoing ocular surgery, or in individuals who develop an ocular infection or ocular trauma, including corneal abrasion. If there is any damage to the ocular epithelial surface, use latanoprost with caution. Reactivation of herpes simplex keratitis has been reported during latanoprost therapy. Use caution in individuals with a history of herpes simplex virus epithelial keratitis; avoid use in individuals with active herpes simplex virus epithelial keratitis due to the potential for exacerbation of inflammation.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    There are no adequate and well-controlled studies to evaluate the use of latanoprost during pregnancy. The use of ophthalmic prostaglandin analogs during pregnancy is controversial. While theoretically, prostaglandin analogs could increase uterine tone and induce premature labor, the doses used for labor induction are significantly higher than those in ophthalmic formulations. There have been no systemic side effects attributed to topical prostaglandins in clinical trials. Due to their short half-life, significant systemic exposure is unlikely. Limited experience with latanoprost use during human pregnancy has not resulted in clinically significant risk to the fetus. In an observational study involving 11 pregnant individuals taking latanoprost for glaucoma, there were no congenital anomalies in 9 cases with complete follow-up, 1 case was lost to follow-up, and 1 case experienced an early spontaneous abortion. In animal studies involving rats and rabbits, the administration of intravenous latanoprost at clinically relevant doses resulted in fetal malformation, embryo fetal lethality, and spontaneous abortion.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Guidelines consider prostaglandin eye drops compatible with breast-feeding. There are no data on the presence of latanoprost in human milk, its effects on the breast-fed child, or its effects on milk production. However, there is minimal systemic absorption of latanoprost after ophthalmic administration; therefore, clinically significant amounts of the drug are not expected to be bioavailable to a breastfed child via human milk. To further minimize the amount of drug that reaches the systemic circulation and human milk, apply pressure over the tear duct by the corner of the eye for 1 minute after ophthalmic administration.

Adverse Reactions
  Headline
  Adverse Reactions
  General Information
  Ocular adverse reactions (ADRs) associated with latanoprost therapy were reported in 5—15% of treated eyes in a 6-month, multi-center, double-blind, active-controlled study performed by the manufacturer. These ADRs include blurred vision, conjunctival hyperemia, foreign body sensation, iridal discoloration, ocular irritation (itching, burning, stinging), and punctate corneal keratopathy. Iridal discoloration happens slowly and may not be noticeable for several months to several years. This change is caused by an increase in the amount of brown pigment in the iris due to an increased number of melanosomes in melanocytes. The increase in brown pigment has not been shown to progress further upon discontinuation of treatment, however the resultant color change may be permanent. Latanoprost may gradually change eyelashes and vellus hair; these changes include increased length, thickness, pigmentation, number of lashes or hairs (hypertrichosis), and misdirected growth of lashes. Eyelid skin darkening has also been reported. Adverse reactions reported in 1—4% of patients include xerophthalmia, lacrimation, ocular pain, eyelid crusting, blepharedema, blepharitis, eyelid discomfort, and photophobia. Other ADRs reported in < 1% of patients include conjunctivitis, diplopia, and discharge from the eye. Although serious, there were extremely rare reports of the following adverse reactions: retinal artery embolus, retinal detachment, and vitreous hemorrhage from diabetic retinopathy.Systemic adverse reactions were reported during therapy with latanoprost, however, a cause and effect relationship was not demonstrated. The most common systemic adverse reaction to latanoprost was upper respiratory tract infection/cold/flu which occurred at a rate of 4%. Other systemic ADRs which occurred at a rate of 1—2% include myalgia, joint pain, back pain, angina pectoris, and rash/allergic skin reaction.Post-marketing use of latanoprost has identified other events that have been included in package labeling due to either their frequency, seriousness of the event, or a possible causal relationship to the drug: corneal edema and erosions, herpes keratitis, keratitis, ocular inflammation (iritis/uveitis), macular edema (including cystoid macular edema), toxic epidermal necrolysis, asthma or asthma exacerbations (bronchospasm), and dyspnea. Since the reporting of these events is voluntary, no estimates of their incidence with latanoprost use can be made.

Description
  Text
  Latanoprost, an analog of prostaglandin F2alpha, is a prodrug used to reduce elevated intraocular pressure (IOP) in patients with open-angle glaucoma or ocular hypertension. Studies have shown that latanoprost administered once daily is at least as effective as timolol in lowering intraocular pressure. When latanoprost and timolol were used in combination, a complete or almost complete additive effect in reducing intraocular pressure has been observed. Treatment has been associated with increased pigmentation of the iris, periorbital tissue (eyelid) and eyelashes.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Latanoprost is a selective agonist at a subtype of prostaglandin receptors known as the FP receptor. By acting on the FP receptor, latanoprost increases the outflow of aqueous humor, thereby reducing intraocular pressure. According to the manufacturer, studies in both animals and man suggest that increased uveoscleral outflow is the primary mechanism of action.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Other Route(s)
    Description
    Ophthalmic Route
Following ocular administration, latanoprost is absorbed through the cornea where the isopropyl ester prodrug is hydrolyzed to the acid form to become biologically active. Peak aqueous humor concentrations are reached about 2 hours after topical administration. Reduction of intraocular pressure starts approximately 3 to 4 hours after administration and peaks after 8 to 12 hours. Plasma levels of the acid of latanoprost can only be measured during the first hour after local administration.
  Text
  Latanoprost is administered topically to the eye as an isopropyl ester prodrug. Once in systemic circulation, the biologically active latanoprost acid is primarily metabolized by the liver to the 1,2-dinor and 1,2,3,4- tetranor metabolites via fatty acid beta-oxidation with an elimination half-life of 17 minutes. The metabolites are mainly eliminated by the kidneys, with 88% of the topically administered dose being recovered in the urine.

  Route Specific Administrations
    Name
    Ophthalmic Administration
    Description
    Instruct the patient on proper instillation of the eye solution.
Wash hands before and after use.
Remove contact lenses before instilling ophthalmic drops. Lenses may be reinserted 15 minutes after drug administration.
Tilt the head back slightly and pull the lower eyelid down with the index finger to form a pouch. Squeeze the prescribed number of drops into the pouch and gently close eyes for 1 to 2 minutes. Do not blink.
Care should be taken to avoid contamination. Do not touch the tip of the dropper to the eye, fingertips, or other surfaces.
Latanoprost may be used concomitantly with other ophthalmic drug products to lower intraocular pressure. If more than one topical ophthalmic drug is being used, the drugs should be administered at least 5 minutes apart. In vitro studies have shown precipitation occurs when ophthalmic drugs that contain thimerosal are mixed with latanoprost.
A delivery aid (i.e., Xal-Ease) is available for administering the ophthalmic solution.
