Drug Class: Vadadustat is an oral hypoxia-inducible factor prolyl hydroxylase inhibitor indicated for the treatment of anemia due to chronic kidney disease (CKD) in adults who have been receiving dialysis for at least 3 months.

Routes: oral; Common adult doses: 600 mg/day PO.

Indication Specific Dosing
  General Information
  General Dosing Information
Correct and exclude other causes of anemia (e.g., vitamin deficiency, metabolic, or chronic inflammatory conditions, bleeding) prior to initiating treatment with vadadustat.
Evaluate iron studies before and during treatment with vadadustat. Administer supplemental iron therapy when serum ferritin is less than 100 mcg/L or when serum transferrin saturation is less than 20%.
Assess serum alanine aminotransferase (ALT), aspartate aminotransferase (AST), and bilirubin prior to initiation of vadadustat, monthly for the first 6 months, and then as clinically indicated.
 
Transitioning from an erythropoiesis-stimulating agent (ESA)
Considering the gradual rise in hemoglobin with vadadustat, red blood cell (RBC) transfusions or ESA treatment may be considered during the transition phase if hemoglobin values decrease below 9 g/dL or hemoglobin response is considered not acceptable.
Continue vadadustat treatment during the transfusion period in persons receiving RBC transfusions.
Interrupt vadadustat treatment for persons receiving temporary ESA rescue treatment. Resume vadadustat at the prior dose or with a dose that is 150 mg more than the prior dose, with subsequent titration, when hemoglobin concentrations are 10 g/dL or more and sufficient time after the last ESA dose has elapsed.
Darbepoetin alfa: Restart vadadustat 7 days after the darbepoetin alfa last dose.
Epoetin alfa: Restart vadadustat 2 days after the last epoetin alfa dose.
Methoxy polyethylene glycol-epoetin beta: Restart vadadustat 14 days after the last methoxy polyethylene glycol-epoetin beta dose.

        300 mg PO once daily, initially.     Hypersensitivity

    Headline
    Treatment-Related Restrictions of Use
    General Information
    The safety of vadadustat has not been established for the treatment of anemia due to chronic kidney disease (CKD) in adults not on dialysis, and its use is not recommended in this population. In clinical trials in adults with anemia of CKD who were not on dialysis, an increased risk of mortality, stroke, myocardial infarction, serious acute kidney injury, serious hepatic injury, and serious gastrointestinal erosions was observed with vadadustat.
    Headline
    cardiovascular disease, mortality, myocardial infarction, stroke, thromboembolism
    General Information
    Avoid use of vadadustat in individuals with a history of myocardial infarction, cerebrovascular event, or acute coronary syndrome within 3 months prior to starting vadadustat. Individuals with cardiovascular disease are at increased risk for arterial and venous thrombotic events. These events may be associated with mortality and include myocardial infarction, stroke, venous thromboembolism, and vascular access thrombosis. A hemoglobin increase of more than 1 g/dL over 2 weeks may contribute to the risk of development of these thrombotic events. Targeting a hemoglobin concentration more than 11 g/dL is expected to further increase the risk of death and arterial and venous thrombotic events (as occurs with erythropoiesis stimulating agents, which also increase erythropoietin concentrations). Clinical trials have not identified a target hemoglobin concentration, vadadustat dose, or dosing strategy that does not increase the risk of thrombotic vascular events. Use the lowest dose of vadadustat that is sufficient to reduce the need for red blood cell transfusions. Adhere to dosing and hemoglobin monitoring recommendations to avoid excessive erythropoiesis. Advise individuals to seek immediate medical attention if they develop signs or symptoms of myocardial infarction, stroke, venous thromboembolism, or thrombosis of vascular access; evaluate and manage promptly if these occur.
    Headline
    hypertension
    General Information
    Vadadustat is contraindicated in individuals with uncontrolled hypertension. Periodically monitor blood pressure and adjust or initiate anti-hypertensive therapy as needed during vadadustat treatment.
    Headline
    ethanol ingestion, gastrointestinal lesion, tobacco smoking
    General Information
    Individuals with a history of gastrointestinal lesion, peptic ulcer disease, concomitant use of medications that increase the risk of gastrointestinal erosion, or current tobacco smoking or ethanol ingestion are at increased risk for gastrointestinal erosions with vadadustat. Advise individuals of the signs and symptoms of gastric and esophageal erosions and gastrointestinal bleeding and to seek prompt medical care if these occur. Gastric or esophageal erosions were observed with vadadustat in clinical trials.
    Headline
    malignancy
    General Information
    Vadadustat is not recommended and has not been studied in individuals with active malignancy; increased hypoxia inducible factor (HIF)-1 concentrations may be associated with unfavorable effects on cancer growth.
    Headline
    hepatic disease
    General Information
    Vadadustat use is not recommended in individuals with cirrhosis or active, acute hepatic disease. Measure alanine aminotransferase (ALT), aspartate aminotransferase (AST), and bilirubin prior to the initiation of vadadustat, monthly for the first 6 months, and then as clinically indicated.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Use vadadustat during pregnancy only if the benefit justifies the potential risk to the fetus. Available data with vadadustat use during pregnancy are insufficient to evaluate a drug-associated risk of major birth defects, miscarriage, or other adverse outcomes. In animal studies, vadadustat administration to rats and rabbits during organogenesis was not teratogenic, but was associated with reduced fetal weight at doses that caused maternal toxicity. There are risks to the pregnant individual and fetus associated with chronic kidney disease, including maternal hypertension, preeclampsia, miscarriage, stillbirth, preterm delivery, low birth weight, and polyhydramnios. There is a pregnancy exposure registry that monitors pregnancy outcomes in individuals exposed to vadadustat during pregnancy. Health care providers and pregnant individuals are encouraged to report pregnancies by calling 1-844-445-3799.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Advise patients to avoid breast-feeding while taking vadadustat and for 2 days after the last dose. Vadadustat has the potential to cause serious side effects in the breast-fed child. There are no data on the presence of vadadustat in human milk, its effects on the breast-fed child, or its effect on milk production. Vadadustat is present in animal milk.

Description
  Text
  Vadadustat is an oral hypoxia-inducible factor prolyl hydroxylase inhibitor indicated for the treatment of anemia due to chronic kidney disease (CKD) in adults who have been receiving dialysis for at least 3 months. Vadadustat has not been shown to improve quality of life, fatigue, or well-being. Vadadustat is not indicated as a substitute for red blood cell transfusions in persons who require immediate correction of anemia or for the treatment of anemia in CKD in persons not receiving dialysis. In large clinical trials in adults with anemia of CKD who were not receiving dialysis, an increased risk of mortality, stroke, serious acute kidney injury, serious hepatic injury, and serious gastrointestinal erosions was observed in subjects treated with vadadustat compared to darbepoetin. Vadadustat is associated with an increased risk of thrombotic vascular events, including major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE). No trial has identified a hemoglobin target concentration, vadadustat dose, or dosing strategy that does not increase these risks.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Vadadustat is a reversible inhibitor of hypoxia-inducible factor (HIF), prolyl 4-hydroxylases (PH)1, PH2, and PH3. This activity results in the stabilization and nuclear accumulation of HIF-1-alpha and HIF-2-alpha transcription factors, and increased production of erythropoietin.

  Text
  Vadadustat is administered orally. Protein binding of vadadustat is 99.5% or more in human plasma. Vadadustat does not distribute into red blood cells. Vadadustat is primarily metabolized via glucuronidation by UDP-glucuronosyltransferase (UGT) enzymes. After a single radiolabeled oral dose to healthy adults, 85.9% of the total dose was recovered; 58.9% in urine (less than 1% of total dose unchanged) and 26.9% in feces (9% of total dose unchanged). The mean half-life of vadadustat in individuals on chronic hemodialysis was 13.9 hours.

Vadadustat is a substrate of OAT1 and OAT3 and an OAT3 and BCRP inhibitor.
