Drug Class: Brompheniramine and pseudoephedrine combination products are used to temporarily to relieve allergic symptoms (sneezing, itching, watery eyes), and/or nasal congestion due to allergic rhinitis (hay fever) or the common cold.

Routes: oral; Common adult doses: 24 mg/day PO brompheniramine and 240 mg/day PO pseudoephedrine.

Common Side Effects (excluding routine GI upset): anxiety, delirium, dizziness, drowsiness, euphoria, excitability, fatigue, hallucinations, headache, insomnia, irritability, lethargy, psychosis, restlessness, seizures, tremor, weakness, angina, arrhythmia exacerbation, hypertension, hypotension, myocardial infarction, palpitations, premature ventricular contractions (pvcs), sinus tachycardia, stroke, supraventricular tachycardia (svt), contact dermatitis, erythema, rash (unspecified)

In an Overdose: Seizures may occur, but have more commonly occurred with excessive dosage, overdosage, or in patients with renal failure receiving the maximum dosage (without dosage adjustment). Adverse cardiovascular responses to antihistamines are likely to be associated with the anticholinergic properties or quinidine-like effects of H1-antagonists such as brompheniramine, and are typically seen in overdosage situations.

Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  General Information
  NOTE: This monograph discusses the contraindications and precautions for the brompheniramine and pseudoephedrine combination products. Clinicians may wish to consult the individual monographs for more information about each agent.
 
Combination products containing brompheniramine; pseudoephedrine is contraindicated in patients with asthma. Combination products containing brompheniramine;pseudoephedrine should be avoided when possible in patients with or chronic cough due to bronchitis, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) (e.g., emphysema or chronic bronchitis). The anticholinergic activity of H1-antagonists may result in thickened bronchial secretions in the respiratory tract thereby aggravating an acute asthmatic attack or COPD. Although H1-antagonists should be avoided during an acute asthmatic attack, these anticholinergic effects do not preclude the use of H1-antagonists in all asthmatic or COPD patients, particularly if thickened bronchial secretions are not a primary component of the illness. Antihistamines are contraindicated in patients with status asthmaticus. Antihistamines should be used under the direction of a physician in patients who have conditions such COPD; self-medication is not recommended.
 
Combination products containing brompheniramine; pseudoephedrine should be used with caution in patients with cardiac disease or hypertension due to both components. Although cardiovascular effects are uncommon, the quinidine-like local anesthetic and anticholinergic effects of H1-antagonists such as brompheniramine may result in adverse cardiac effects including tachycardia, ECG changes, hypotension, and arrhythmias. Since pseudoephedrine is a vasoconstrictor and may increase heart rate and blood pressure via sympathomimetic effects, it is contraindicated for use in patients with uncontrolled or severe hypertension or severe coronary artery disease (including acute myocardial infarction, angina, or history of myocardial infarction); use with caution in patients with acute cardiac arrhythmias (e.g., tachycardia). Considerable caution should be used in patients with controlled or mild hypertension, heart failure, cardiomyopathy, or other cardiac disease. Well-controlled hypertensive adult patients receiving pseudoephedrine at recommended doses (240 mg/day PO) generally do not appear at risk for significant elevations in blood pressure ; however, small increases in blood pressure and heart rate may occur. Although considered safe in the general population of controlled hypertensives, increased blood pressure (especially systolic hypertension) has been reported in individual patients receiving pseudoephedrine. When pseudoephedrine is used at higher than recommended doses, when combined with a MAO inhibitor (see Drug Interactions), or when used in the setting of substance abuse or overdosage, it may result in hypertension or hypertensive crisis. Brompheniramine; pseudoephedrine is contraindicated in patients receiving MAOI therapy (see Drug Interactions).
 
Brompheniramine; pseudoephedrine should be used with caution in elderly patients and in patients with renal impairment. Elderly patients are at increased risk for anticholinergic adverse effects due to brompheniramine, since there is a decline in endogenous cholinergic activity with aging. Patients aged more than 60 years are more likely to have decreased renal clearance of pseudoephedrine as well as adverse reactions to sympathomimetic amines. In general, dose selection for the elderly patient should be cautious, usually starting at the low end of the dosing range. Pseudoephedrine is primarily eliminated by renal excretion; pseudoephedrine should be used with caution in patients with renal impairment and dosage reduction is warranted. Due to decreased elimination and potential pseudoephedrine drug accumulation, patients with renal failure may at increased risk for drug-related toxicity.
 
Combination products containing brompheniramine; pseudoephedrine should be used with caution in patients with conditions which would be worsened by its anticholinergic or sympathomimetic effects. Pseudoephedrine is not recommended for use in patients with hyperthyroidism or diabetes mellitus because sympathomimetics can exacerbate these conditions; it is contraindicated for use in closed-angle glaucoma and urinary retention due to prostatic hypertrophy. Although brompheniramine has only moderate anticholinergic effects, a worsening of symptoms may be seen in patients who have glaucoma, increased intraocular pressure, bladder obstruction, GI obstruction or ileus, benign prostatic hypertrophy, or urinary retention; do not use in patients with peptic ulcer disease. These precautions become most significant when using antihistamines with substantial anticholinergic activity such as those in the ethanolamine and phenothiazine groups. Brompheniramine should be used conservatively in patients with closed-angle glaucoma or increased intraocular pressure. An increase in intraocular pressure may occur from the anticholinergic actions of the drug, precipitating an acute attack of glaucoma. Other ocular effects resulting from the anticholinergic effects of brompheniramine include dry eyes or blurred vision. This may be of significance in wearers of contact lenses.
 
Brompheniramine may induce paradoxical CNS stimulation in children. There have been a number of cases of respiratory depression, sleep apnea, and SIDS in infants receiving phenothiazine antihistamines. The mechanism of this reaction is not yet known; therefore, antihistamines should be used with extreme caution in children with a family history of SIDS or sleep apnea. Brompheniramine should not be used in premature or full-term neonates due to the possibility of paradoxical CNS stimulation or seizures. In addition, the adverse effects of sympathomimetics such as pseudoephedrine can be severe, especially in infants and toddlers; CNS stimulation, hypertension and tachycardia may occur. In January 2007, the CDC warned caregivers and healthcare providers of the risk for serious injury or fatal overdose from the administration of cough and cold products to children and infants less than 2 years of age. This warning followed an investigation of the deaths of three (3) infants less than 6 months of age that were attributed to the inadvertent inappropriate use of these products. The symptoms preceding these deaths have not been clearly defined, and there is a lack of conclusive data describing the exact cause of death. The report estimated that 1519 children less than 2 years of age were treated in emergency departments during 2004—2005 for adverse events related to cough and cold medications. In October 2007, the FDA Nonprescription Drug Advisory Committee and the Pediatric Advisory Committee recommended that nonprescription cough and cold products containing pseudoephedrine, dextromethorphan, chlorpheniramine, diphenhydramine, brompheniramine, phenylephrine, clemastine, or guaifenesin not be used in children less than 6 years of age. In January 2008, the FDA issued a Public Health Advisory recommending that OTC cough and cold products not be used in infants and children less than 2 years. An official ruling regarding the use of these products in children greater than 2 years has not yet been announced. The FDA recommends that if parents and caregivers use cough and cold products in children greater than 2 years, labels should be read carefully, caution should be used when administering multiple products, and only measuring devices specifically designed for use with medications should be used. While some combination cough/cold products containing these ingredients are available by prescription only and are not necessarily under scrutiny by the FDA, clinicians should thoroughly assess each patient's use of similar products, both prescription and nonprescription, to avoid duplication of therapy and the potential for inadvertent overdose.
 
Combination products containing brompheniramine; pseudoephedrine should be administered during pregnancy only when the benefits of therapy outweigh the potential risks to the fetus (Pregnancy Category C), and use should be limited to short-term, 'as needed' administration under the prescription of a qualified health-care professional. Non-pharmacologic methods (e.g., fluids, rest) are recommended to be tried first for symptomatic relief of rhinitis or allergies during pregnancy. Due to the brompheniramine component, the combination of brompheniramine; pseudoephedrine is not recommended during the first-trimester or during the last 2 weeks of pregnancy (see Brompheniramine monograph for more specific information). Brompheniramine and pseudoephedrine are both classified as FDA Pregnancy Category C drugs (see Brompheniramine and Pseudoephedrine monographs for more specific information). Use of brompheniramine or pseudoephedrine during pregnancy should be avoided unless the potential benefits outweigh the unknown potential risks to the fetus. When these products are used in pregnant women, extended-release products should be avoided to limit fetal exposure.
 
Combination products containing brompheniramine; pseudoephedrine is contraindicated during breast-feeding. Many H1-antagonists are excreted in breast-milk and in general, these medications are not recommended for use during breast-feeding. Side effects in breast-feeding infants may manifest as irritability, disturbed sleeping patterns, drowsiness, hyperexcitability, or excessive crying. Because of the potential for serious adverse reactions in nursing infants, a decision should be made whether to discontinue nursing or brompheniramine products, taking into account the importance of the drug to the mother. Pseudoephedrine is excreted into breast milk. Peak milk concentrations occur 1—1.5 hours after a maternal oral dosage, and peak milk concentrations usually exceed those of maternal plasma. Sympathomimetic adverse effects (irritability, excessive crying, and altered sleeping patterns) have been reported in a breast-fed infant following maternal administration of pseudoephedrine; symptoms resolved within 12 hours of drug discontinuation.
 
Some brompheniramine-pseudoephedrine products contain aspartame and thus contain phenylalanine. Use with caution in those patients with phenylketonuria. For example, Rondec chewable tablets contain aspartame.
 
Brompheniramine is an antihistamine and should be administered with caution in patients with a seizure disorder. Antihistamines may precipitate epileptiform seizures in patients with focal lesions of the cerebral cortex.
 
Combination products containing brompheniramine should be used with caution in patients with significant hepatic disease. Brompheniramine is extensively metabolized by the liver; therefore, the metabolism of brompheniramine may be reduced in the presence of hepatic impairment.
 
Brompheniramine can cause drowsiness, while pseudoephedrine is a CNS stimulant. Patients receiving brompheniramine-pseudoephedrine should be advised to avoid driving or operating machinery until the effects of the drug combination are known.

Many experts recommend against pseudoephedrine use in severe acute or chronic kidney disease (CKD) or renal failure.

    Headline
    geriatric
    General Information
    The Beers Criteria identifies first-generation antihistamines as potentially inappropriate medications (PIMs) for geriatric adults, recommending their avoidance due to their high anticholinergic properties, decreased clearance in older age, the development of tolerance when used as sleep aids, and an increased risk of anticholinergic effects and toxicity compared to younger individuals. These medications should particularly be avoided in patients with dementia or cognitive impairment (due to adverse CNS effects), those at high risk for delirium (which can worsen or trigger new-onset delirium), and men with lower urinary tract symptoms or benign prostatic hyperplasia (due to risks of urinary retention or hesitancy).
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Combination products containing brompheniramine; pseudoephedrine are not recommended for use during pregnancy. Non-pharmacologic methods (e.g., fluids, rest) are recommended to be tried first for symptomatic relief of colds or allergies during pregnancy. Pregnant patients should see their health care professional for a proper diagnosis and for treatment recommendations. Pseudoephedrine may reduce blood flow to the placenta and the fetus, and there is some evidence use may be associated with birth defects if used during early pregnancy. Evidence from case-control studies in human pregnancy indicate there may be an increased risk of gastroschisis, small intestinal atresia, and hemifacial microsomia in babies exposed in utero to pseudoephedrine, particularly in the first trimester. No well controlled trials of brompheniramine use during pregnancy are available. Generally, avoid brompheniramine use since there are antihistamine alternatives available with more data for use during pregnancy. Loratadine and oral cetirizine are acceptable antihistamine alternatives based on their excellent safety data and recommendation in multiple guidelines for use during pregnancy.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Brompheniramine; pseudoephedrine combination products should be used with caution during breast-feeding. Pseudoephedrine is excreted into breast milk. Peak milk concentrations occur 1 to 1.5 hours after a maternal oral dosage, and peak milk concentrations usually exceed those of maternal plasma. The total amount of pseudoephedrine (measured by AUC) in milk is 2 to 3 times that of plasma. However, only 0.5% of a maternal dose would probably be ingested by an infant during breast-feeding within any 24 hours. Pseudoephedrine may reduce milk production. Milk production over a 24-hour period was reduced by an average of 24% compared to placebo after a single 60 mg dose of pseudoephedrine. Based on concentrations in breast milk and assuming a maternal dose of 240 mg pseudoephedrine daily, it was estimated that an infant would receive 4.3% of the maternal weight-adjusted dose. It is suggested to avoid pseudoephedrine use  if lactation is not yet well established or if there are difficulties producing sufficient milk. Sympathomimetic adverse effects (irritability, excessive crying, and altered sleeping patterns) have been reported in a breastfed infant following maternal administration of pseudoephedrine; symptoms resolved within 12 hours of drug discontinuation. Small, occasional doses of brompheniramine would not be expected to cause adverse effects in breastfed infants; insignificant amounts of brompheniramine are secreted in the breast milk. Larger doses or more prolonged use may cause effects in the infant or decrease the milk supply. Side effects in breast-feeding infants may manifest as irritability, disturbed sleeping patterns, drowsiness, hyperexcitability, or excessive crying. Antihistamines can lower basal prolactin secretion and may interfere with the establishment of lactation. Consider treatment alternatives to brompheniramine; pseudoephedrine. Guidelines recommend loratadine or cetirizine at the lowest dose as preferred antihistamines because they are safer with only low levels found in breast milk.

Adverse Reactions
  Headline
  Adverse Reactions
  General Information
  Brompheniramine can cause CNS depressant effects, while pseudoephedrine can induce CNS stimulation in some patients. CNS effects such as anxiety, restlessness, headache, lightheadedness, dizziness, insomnia, irritability, and psychological disturbances including hallucinations and psychosis have been reported during therapy with pseudoephedrine. Elderly patients appear to be more sensitive to the CNS stimulation effects of the drug and may also have reduced elimination of pseudoephedrine due to age-related declines in renal function. Seizures may occur, but have more commonly occurred with excessive dosage, overdosage, or in patients with renal failure receiving the maximum dosage (without dosage adjustment). Due to decreased elimination and potential pseudoephedrine drug accumulation, patients with renal failure may at increased risk for drug-related toxicity, including neurotoxicity. The dosage of pseudoephedrine should be reduced in patients with renal impairment. Drug-induced CNS depression, such as drowsiness, fatigue, or sedation, can occur during therapy with propylamine antihistamines such as brompheniramine. There is considerable individual patient response to sedative effects, so patients should be warned of the possible impairment of mental acuity. These side effects may disappear after a few days of medication; although, if symptoms persist or are severe, a reduction in dosage or a change to another H1-antagonist may be necessary. Other possible CNS effects due to H1-antagonists include disturbed coordination, dizziness, headache, lethargy, and muscular weakness. H1-antagonists such as brompheniramine can cause a paradoxical CNS stimulation or excitability; however, this is more likely to occur in children. Symptoms may include delirium, euphoria, insomnia, nervousness, restlessness, tremor, and in severe cases, seizures.
 
Adverse cardiovascular reactions can occur with antihistamine and sympathomimetic products, including brompheniramine; pseudoephedrine. As with other sympathomimetics, cardiovascular adverse effects may occur during pseudoephedrine therapy including angina, cardiac arrhythmias (or arrhythmia exacerbation), hypertension, myocardial infarction, or stroke; these effects generally occur at excessive dosage or in patients at higher risk (see Contraindications). Pseudoephedrine and phenylephrine appear to have a lower propensity to cause hypertension and potential sequelae (e.g., stroke, hypertensive crisis, intracranial bleeding) compared to ephedrine or phenylpropanolamine. Well-controlled hypertensive patients receiving pseudoephedrine at recommended doses (240 mg/day PO) do not appear at high risk for significant elevations in blood pressure, however, increased blood pressure (especially systolic hypertension) has been reported in some patients. Although infrequent, cardiac arrhythmias secondary to pseudoephedrine may occur in the general population at therapeutic doses and include palpitations, premature ventricular contractions (PVCs), supraventricular tachycardia (SVT), and sinus tachycardia. Adverse cardiovascular responses to antihistamines are likely to be associated with the anticholinergic properties or quinidine-like effects of H1-antagonists such as brompheniramine, and are typically seen in overdosage situations. These responses may include cardiac arrhythmias (e.g., extrasystoles, AV block), ECG changes (e.g., widened QRS), palpitations, and/or sinus tachycardia. Following antihistamine overdosage, alpha-adrenergic blockade may lead to hypotension; hypertension can also occur, but is usually not of clinical significance.
 
Several significant dermatological reactions have been reported infrequently with pseudoephedrine use. These reactions include: fixed drug eruption or exanthema, erythema, contact dermatitis, and other rash (unspecified). In general, the onset of the skin reactions occurs within 24 hours of administration and resolves in 2—3 days following drug discontinuation.
 
Gastrointestinal adverse reactions may occur during brompheniramine; pseudoephedrine therapy. Pseudoephedrine can produce GI effects such as nausea/vomiting, anorexia, and ischemic colitis. Ischemic colitis has been associated with the use of pseudoephedrine and may present with symptoms of abdominal pain and bloody diarrhea. Colitis may result from reversible splanchnic arterial vasoconstriction and may occur with acute or chronic use; the ischemic symptoms usually resolve upon discontinuation of pseudoephedrine. H1-antagonists such as brompheniramine can cause adverse GI effects including abdominal pain, anorexia, constipation, diarrhea, dyspepsia, nausea/vomiting, and xerostomia. Gastrointestinal adverse reactions may be relieved by taking the drug with meals or milk.
 
Respiratory adverse reactions to brompheniramine; pseudoephedrine are primarily due to the anticholinergic effects of brompheniramine and include: dryness of the pharynx, thickening of bronchial secretions, tightness of the chest, and wheezing.
 
Genitourinary system adverse reactions to brompheniramine; pseudoephedrine products include dysuria and urinary retention.
 
The brompheniramine component of this product can cause ocular and otic adverse effects due to moderate anticholinergic activity and may include acute labyrinthitis, blurred vision, diplopia, ocular hypertension, photophobia, tinnitus, vertigo, visual impairment, and xerophthalmia. Elderly patients are more susceptible to these effects since endogenous cholinergic activity declines with age. Ocular effects can occur with pseudoephedrine products; these can include increased intraocular pressure (ocular hypertension) and photophobia.
 
Other anticholinergic effects that can occur with the brompheniramine component of this combination product include chills, diaphoresis, facial dyskinesia, faintness, impotence (erectile dysfunction), and menstrual irregularity (e.g., early menses).

Description
  Text
  Brompheniramine and pseudoephedrine combination products are used to temporarily to relieve allergic symptoms (sneezing, itching, watery eyes), and/or nasal congestion due to allergic rhinitis (hay fever) or the common cold. Brompheniramine is a first-generation, sedating antihistamine (H1-blocker) of the propylamine class used to relieve histamine-mediated allergic symptoms and also acts to dry nasal and sinus passages. Pseudoephedrine is a sympathomimetic amine used for its nasal and sinus decongestant properties.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Products containing brompheniramine and pseudoephedrine provide antihistaminic and decongestant properties to relieve the allergic and congestant symptoms associated with vasomotor or allergic rhinitis.
•Brompheniramine: Brompheniramine is an antagonist at H1-receptors. Brompheniramine does not prevent the release of histamine, as do cromolyn and nedocromil, but rather competes with free histamine for binding at H1-receptor sites. Brompheniramine competitively antagonizes the effects of histamine on H1-receptors in the GI tract, uterus, large blood vessels, and bronchial smooth muscle. Blockade of H1-receptors suppresses the edema, flare, and pruritus that results from histaminic activity. The anticholinergic activity of propylamine derivatives such as brompheniramine is moderate. This anticholinergic action appears to be due to a central antimuscarinic effect. Sedative effects from brompheniramine result from antagonism at central histaminergic receptors. Chronic administration may lead to some degree of tolerance, but the sedative effects may also be reduced.
•Pseudoephedrine: Pseudoephedrine is an agonist at both alpha- and, to a lesser degree, beta-adrenergic receptors. Like ephedrine, pseudoephedrine also has an indirect effect by releasing norepinephrine from its storage sites. By stimulating alpha-adrenergic receptors in the mucosa of the respiratory tract, pseudoephedrine shrinks swollen nasal mucous membranes; reduces tissue hyperemia, edema, and nasal congestion; and increases nasal airway patency. Also, drainage of sinus secretions is increased, and obstructed eustachian ostia may be opened. Oral administration of pseudoephedrine usually produces negligible effects on blood pressure. In some patients, especially those with preexisting cardiac disease receiving higher doses, pseudoephedrine may increase blood pressure or irritability of the heart muscle and may affect ventricular conduction.

  Combination products containing brompheniramine and pseudoephedrine are given orally.
Brompheniramine: Brompheniramine is extensively metabolized. Metabolites are primarily excreted renally. The half-life of brompheniramine ranges from about 12 to 35 hours in healthy adults.
Pseudoephedrine: Pseudoephedrine is presumed to cross the placenta, blood brain barrier, and may be distributed into breast milk. Pseudoephedrine is incompletely metabolized in the liver to norpseudoephedrine, the primary active metabolite of the parent. The drug and metabolite are excreted in the urine; with 55 to 75% excreted as unchanged drug. The elimination half-life of the drug ranges from 9 to 16 hours dependent primarily upon urinary pH. The rate of urinary excretion is accelerated upon urinary acidification to a pH near 5. Upon alkalization of the urine to a pH of approximately 8, some of the drug is reabsorbed into the kidney tubule and the rate of urinary excretion is slowed.

Dosage Adjustment Guidelines
  Hepatic Impairment
  Specific guidelines for dosage adjustments in hepatic impairment are not available. No specific hepatic impairment precautions are noted.
  Renal Impairment
  No dosage adjustments for brompheniramine are required.
Use pseudoephedrine with caution in people with severe renal impairment (CrCl less than 30 mL/minute); dosage reduction has been suggested for these individuals. Accumulation is expected in people with renal failure and an increased risk for pseudoephedrine toxicity. Many experts recommend against pseudoephedrine use in severe acute or chronic kidney disease (CKD) or renal failure.
 
Intermittent hemodialysisPseudoephedrine is not usually recommended for people with renal failure on dialysis. Hemodialysis is only minimally effective at removing pseudoephedrine from the circulation. Do not give supplemental doses. Do not give a supplemental dosage for brompheniramine.
