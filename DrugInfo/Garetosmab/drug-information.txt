    pregnancy
    General Information
    Garetosmab is contraindicated during pregnancy. Fetal harm may occur based on animal studies and its mechanism of action. No human pregnancy exposure data are available to evaluate drug-associated risks of major birth defects, miscarriage, or other adverse outcomes. Intravenous administration of garetosmab to pregnant cynomolgus monkeys resulted in embryo-fetal toxicity, infant mortality, and postnatal malformations at exposures equivalent to or higher than those in individuals receiving the recommended human doses of garetosmab 10 mg/kg or 3 mg/kg every 4 weeks, respectively. If pregnancy occurs during treatment, discontinue garetosmab immediately.
    Headline
    people who can cause pregnancy in others, people who may become pregnant, reproductive risk
    General Information
    Counsel people who may become pregnant and people who can cause pregnancy in others about the reproductive risk associated with garetosmab. Garetosmab may cause fetal harm. Verify pregnancy status prior to treatment initiation. Advise people who may become pregnant to use effective contraception while taking garetosmab and for 6 months after the last dose. Based on animal data, garetosmab may impair male fertility. In sexually mature male cynomolgus monkeys, minimal to moderate seminiferous tubular degeneration was observed and was reversible after a treatment-free recovery period. Changes in sperm concentration, sperm motility, or testosterone concentrations were not observed. In male rats, intravenous garetosmab administration resulted in inflammation, granulomas, and epithelial degeneration of the male reproductive tract associated with decreased sperm motility at exposures less than the recommended human doses.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Advise individuals to avoid breast-feeding while taking garetosmab and for 6 months after the last dose. There are no data on the presence of garetosmab in human milk, its effects on the breastfed child, or its effects on milk production. Maternal immunoglobulin G (IgG) is known to be present in human milk; the effects of local gastrointestinal exposure and limited systemic exposure to garetosmab in the breastfed child are unknown. Because of the potential for serious adverse reactions in the breastfed child, including skin and soft tissue infections, and the potential for effects on male reproductive organ development based on the mechanism of action, breast-feeding is not recommended during treatment and for 6 months after the last dose.

Description
  Text
  Garetosmab is an intravenous (IV), human immunoglobulin G4 (IgG4) monoclonal antibody and activin signaling inhibitor indicated to reduce formation of new heterotopic ossification (HO) lesions and clinician-assessed flare-ups in adults with fibrodysplasia ossificans progressiva (FOP). It specifically binds activin A and inhibits activin A-mediated signaling through mutant activin receptor type 1 (ACVR1) reducing the formation and progression of new heterotopic lesions. In a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial (n = 63), garetosmab 10 mg/kg and 3 mg/kg IV every 4 weeks reduced new HO lesions at Week 56 by 90% and 94%, respectively, vs. placebo (rate ratios 0.10 [95% CI, 0.01 to 0.76] and 0.06 [95% CI, 0 to 0.73]). Clinician-assessed flare-ups were also significantly reduced with the 10 mg/kg dose (rate ratio 0.12 [95% CI, 0.03 to 0.42]). Clinically important safety considerations include embryo-fetal toxicity, skin and soft tissue infections, and epistaxis. Garetosmab is contraindicated during pregnancy. Garetosmab was approved by the FDA in August 2026.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Fibrodysplasia ossificans progressiva (FOP) is caused by gain-of-function mutations in the intracellular domain of activin receptor type 1 (ACVR1, also known as ALK2), a bone morphogenetic protein (BMP) type I receptor that is expressed in many tissues, including skeletal muscle and cartilage. In people with FOP, mutant ACVR1 is activated by activin A resulting in heterotopic ossification. Garetosmab is a human immunoglobulin G4 (IgG4) monoclonal antibody that specifically binds to and inhibits signaling by activin A to block the activation of FOP-mutant ACVR1 and reduce the formation and progression of new heterotopic bone lesions.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Intravenous Route
    Description
    Following multiple IV doses of garetosmab 10 mg/kg or 3 mg/kg administered every 4 weeks in people with fibrodysplasia ossificans progressiva (FOP), the steady-state exposures were approximately dose proportional. The mean (SD) trough concentrations at steady-state were 99 (34) mg/L or 27 (10) mg/L following 10 mg/kg or 3 mg/kg every 4 weeks, respectively. Steady-state concentrations were attained by approximately 12 weeks with an accumulation ratio of approximately 1.7-fold.

    Hepatic Impairment
    Description
    No clinically meaningful differences in garetosmab exposure were observed in adults with mild to moderate hepatic impairment (total bilirubin more than 1.5 to 3 times the upper limit of normal (ULN) and any AST), following administration of 10 mg/kg or 3 mg/kg every 4 weeks. The effect of severe hepatic impairment (total bilirubin more than 3 times the ULN and any AST) on garetosmab pharmacokinetics is unknown. Because garetosmab is degraded by widely distributed proteolytic enzymes rather than processes restricted to hepatic tissue, changes in hepatic function are not expected to impact its pharmacokinetics.

  Text
  Garetosmab is administered intravenously. The estimated mean (SD) Vd is 5.1 (1.2) L. Garetosmab is expected to be degraded into small peptides and amino acids through catabolic pathways in the same manner as endogenous immunoglobulin G (IgG). Elimination occurs through parallel linear and nonlinear pathways. At higher concentrations, elimination occurs predominantly through a linear, non-saturable proteolytic pathway, while at lower concentrations, non-linear, target-mediated clearance predominates. After the last dose of garetosmab 10 mg/kg or 3 mg/kg administered every 4 weeks, the median time to a non-detectable concentration was approximately 24 weeks and 17 weeks, respectively, based on population pharmacokinetic analysis.

Administration

  Route Specific Administrations
    Administration Details
      Name
      Intravenous Administration
      Description
      Preparation
Withdraw the required volume of garetosmab and transfer into an IV infusion bag [polyvinyl chloride (PVC), polyolefin (PO), or ethyl vinyl acetate (EVA)] containing a minimum of 50 mL and a maximum of 100 mL of 5% Dextrose Injection to prepare a diluted solution with a final concentration ranging from 0.9 mg/mL to 25 mg/mL.
Mix the diluted solution by gentle inversion; do not shake.
Storage: Use diluted solution immediately. If not used immediately, store the diluted solution refrigerated at 2 to 8 degrees C (36 to 46 degrees F) for no more than 24 hours or at room temperature up to 25 degrees C (77 degrees F) for no more than 16 hours from the time of preparation to the start of infusion. Do not freeze the diluted solution.
 
Administration
If refrigerated, allow the diluted solution to come to room temperature prior to administration.
Administer diluted solution by IV infusion over 60 minutes using an infusion set constructed of polyvinyl chloride (PVC), polyethylene (PE)-lined PVC, or polyurethane (PU). A sterile, in-line or add-on, 0.2-micron to 5-micron polyethersulfone (PES) or polyamide (PA) filter is recommended.
Garetosmab should be administered using an infusion pump to control the infusion rate.
Upon completion of the infusion, flush the infusion line with an adequate volume of sterile 5% Dextrose Injection to ensure that the entire contents of the infusion bag are administered.
Do not mix other medications with garetosmab or administer other medications concurrently via the same infusion line.
    Name
    Injectable Administration
    Description
    Garetosmab should only be administered via intravenous route.
Visually inspect parenteral products for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration whenever solution and container permit. Garetosmab is a clear to slightly opalescent, colorless to pale yellow solution; discard the vial if the solution is cloudy, discolored, or contains particulate matter.
Each vial is intended for one-time use only.
Missed dose: If a dose of garetosmab is missed, administer the dose as soon as possible. Thereafter, garetosmab should be scheduled once every 4 weeks from the date of the last dose.
