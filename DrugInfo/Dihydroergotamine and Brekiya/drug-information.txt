Drug Class: Dihydroergotamine is a parenteral and nasally administered ergotamine derivative indicated for the treatment of acute migraine with or without aura and cluster headache.

Routes: injection (IV/IM/SC), nasal; Common adult doses: 3 mg/day IM or subcutaneous or 2 mg/day IV and 6 mg/week IV/IM/subcutaneous; 3 mg/day and 4 mg/week intranasal (Migranal and generic equivalents) or 2.9 mg/day and 4.35 mg/week intranasal (Trudhesa) or 10.4 mg/day intranasal (Atzumi).

Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  General Information
  Dihydroergotamine is contraindicated in patients with known ergot alkaloid hypersensitivity because they may develop an allergic or otherwise serious adverse reaction. Patients should be advised not to exceed the recommended dosage of dihydroergotamine during use. Patients should report any of the following symptoms: abdominal pain, anxiety, chest pain, myalgia, severe nausea and vomiting, numbness, tingling, or vision changes. If signs and symptoms of impaired circulation occur, the medication should be discontinued, and affected extremities should be kept warm.
 
Dihydroergotamine is contraindicated in patients with basilar/hemiplegic migraine headaches. Treatment for migraine-induced stroke has not been formally evaluated and ergot alkaloids for abortive therapy of the migraine should be avoided because they may further reduce cerebral blood flow to the ischemic area. Dihydroergotamine is not recommended for migraine prophylaxis.
 
Due to its potent alpha-adrenergic stimulation, dihydroergotamine is contraindicated in patients with conditions that predispose them to vasospastic reactions. Dihydroergotamine is contraindicated in peripheral vascular disease (including thromboangiitis obliterans (Buerger's disease), luetic arteritis, severe arteriosclerosis, thrombophlebitis, and Raynaud's disease), ischemic heart disease (including unstable or vasospastic angina, or predisposition to vasospastic reactions), sepsis, history of myocardial infarction, or uncontrolled hypertension. It is recommended that dihydroergotamine not be given to patients in whom unrecognized coronary artery disease (CAD) is predicted by the presence of cardiac disease risk factors (e.g., hypertension, hypercholesterolemia, current tobacco smoking, obesity, diabetes mellitus, strong family history of CAD, postmenopausal females, or males over 40 years of age), unless a cardiovascular evaluation provides sufficient evidence that the patient is reasonably free of ischemic heart disease. The elderly may be more susceptible to vasoconstrictive effects of ergot alkaloids. In general, dihydroergotamine dosing should be more cautious in the elderly, starting at the lower end of the dose range to account for differences in renal, hepatic, or cardiac systems as well as concomitant disease states and medications. Coadministration of dihydroergotamine with potent inhibitors of CYP3A4 is contraindicated due to the risk of acute ergot toxicity (see Drug Interactions). Serious and/or life-threatening peripheral ischemia has been associated with the coadministration of dihydroergotamine with potent CYP3A4 inhibitors including protease inhibitors and macrolide antibiotics, with some cases resulting in limb amputation. Cerebral ischemia has occurred rarely in patients taking ergotamine with protease inhibitors (one case fatal). Because CYP3A4 inhibition can elevate serum dihydroergotamine concentrations, the risk for vasospasm leading to cerebral ischemia and/or ischemia of the extremities is increased.
 
Dihydroergotamine is extensively metabolized in the liver and excreted in the bile. The manufacturer considers severe hepatic impairment or severe pruritus (i.e., biliary tract disease or cholestasis) a relative contraindication to use. Those with hepatic disease may be at increased risk for developing ergot toxicity due to systemic accumulation of the drug.
 
Dihydroergotamine is hepatically metabolized and excreted renally. It should not be used in cases of severe renal impairment or in renal failure. Dihydroergotamine is relatively contraindicated in patients renal disease due to the increased risk of toxic accumulation of the drug and its metabolites.
 
Dihydroergotamine is classified in FDA pregnancy risk category X. Although teratogenic effects are unknown, dihydroergotamine and other ergot alkaloids should not be administered during pregnancy because of the drugs oxytocic and uterine-stimulating activities.
 
There is no direct evidence relating to dihydroergotamine use in nursing mothers. However, ergotamine (a related drug) is known to be excreted into breast milk and may cause vomiting, diarrhea, weak pulse, and unstable blood pressure in nursing infants. Dihydroergotamine is contraindicated in breast-feeding women. Ergot drugs are also known to inhibit lactation.
 
The combination of dihydroergotamine and heparin is not recommended for intramuscular administration. There appears to be an increased risk of injection site reactions with this route of administration.
 
Dihydroergotamine should be used with caution in children and adolescents. Safe and effective use has not been established, but children > 6 years old have been treated for severe migraine headaches with this drug. It is recommended that dihydroergotamine use in older children is reserved for cases where less toxic medications have failed to provide relief. Do not use in very young children or infants.
 
Long-term use of ergot alkaloids such as ergotamine has been associated with abnormal heart valve findings including aortic and/or mitral regurgitation, mitral stenosis, severe tricuspid regurgitation, and pulmonary regurgitation. Chronic use of ergotamine preparations has caused fibrotic thickening of the aortic, mitral, tricuspid, and/or pulmonary valves. Retroperitoneal fibrosis and pulmonary fibrosis have occurred in rare instances. Caffeine; ergotamine preparations should be used cautiously in those with pulmonary disease or valvular heart disease.
 
Because dihydroergotamine may cause vasoconstriction, patients should be advised to inform their health care provider of usage prior to any surgeries or related procedures. Dihydroergotamine use is contraindicated in patients who have recently undergone vascular surgery. Ergot alkaloids may cause problems with plastic, cosmetic, or reconstructive surgery in particular; cyanosis, ischemia, and vasoconstriction in the skin or other tissues may occur if dihydroergotamine has been used within 48 hours of the surgical procedure.

    Headline
    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components. Dihydroergotamine is also contraindicated in individuals with known hypersensitivity to ergot alkaloids. The dihydroergotamine prefilled autoinjector for subcutaneous injection is also contraindicated in individuals with latex hypersensitivity; the rigid needle shield of the autoinjector contains a needle cover that contains dry natural rubber, which is made from latex.
    Headline
    coadministration with certain medications
    General Information
    Coadministration with certain medications, specifically strong CYP3A4 inhibitors is contraindicated with dihydroergotamine; serious and life-threatening peripheral ischemia has been reported with the coadministration of dihydroergotamine and strong CYP3A4 inhibitors. Because CYP3A4 inhibition increases serum dihydroergotamine concentrations, the risk for vasospasm leading to cerebral ischemia and/or ischemia of the extremities is increased.
    Headline
    coronary artery disease, coronary artery vasospasm, diabetes mellitus, family history of ischemic heart disease, hypercholesterolemia, hypertension, males, obesity, peripheral arteriosclerosis, postmenopausal females, tobacco smoking
    General Information
    Dihydroergotamine is contraindicated in individuals with ischemic coronary artery disease (CAD) (i.e., angina pectoris, history of myocardial infarction, documented silent ischemia), coronary artery vasospasm (including Prinzmetal angina), uncontrolled hypertension, or peripheral arteriosclerosis. Perform a cardiovascular (CV) evaluation prior to dihydroergotamine initiation to ensure the individual is free of coronary artery and ischemic myocardial disease, or other significant underlying cardiovascular disease. Do not administer dihydroergotamine if the individual's medical history (including risk factors) or electrocardiographic investigation findings are consistent with coronary artery vasospasm or myocardial ischemia. It is strongly recommended to administer the first dose of dihydroergotamine in the setting of an equipped health care facility in individuals with risk factors predictive of coronary artery disease (e.g., hypertension, hypercholesterolemia, tobacco smoking, obesity, diabetes mellitus, strong family history of ischemic heart disease, postmenopausal females, or males who are older than 40 years) who are determined to have a satisfactory CV evaluation, unless the individual has previously received dihydroergotamine. During the interval immediately after the first use of dihydroergotamine, an electrocardiogram is recommended in those individuals with risk factors because ischemia can occur in the absence of clinical symptoms. It is recommended that individuals who are intermittent long-term users of dihydroergotamine and who have or acquire risk factors predictive of coronary artery disease undergo periodic interval cardiovascular evaluation as they continue to use dihydroergotamine. Significant blood pressure elevation has been reported on rare occasions in individuals with or without a history of hypertension receiving dihydroergotamine.
    Headline

    General Information
    Dihydroergotamine is not indicated for the preventive treatment of migraine or management of basilar/hemiplegic migraine. Some dihydroergotamine products are contraindicated for the prophylactic therapy of migraine or in individuals with basilar/hemiplegic migraine.

    Headline
    renal failure, renal impairment
    General Information
    Dihydroergotamine is contraindicated in individuals with severe renal impairment or renal failure.
    Headline
    sepsis, vascular surgery
    General Information
    Dihydroergotamine is contraindicated after vascular surgery and in individuals with sepsis.
    Headline
    labor, obstetric delivery, pregnancy
    General Information
    Avoid use of dihydroergotamine during pregnancy, including labor and obstetric delivery, due to its oxytocic effects and an observed increased risk of preterm delivery. Available human data do not indicate an increased risk of major congenital malformations or miscarriage. In animal studies, dihydroergotamine was associated with adverse embryo-fetal effects (e.g., reduced fetal weight, decreased skeletal ossification) in rats and rabbits, and with reduced offspring weight and impaired reproductive function in rats exposed during pregnancy and lactation, at doses below those used clinically and not associated with maternal toxicity.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Advise patients to avoid breast-feeding while taking dihydroergotamine and for 3 days after the last dose. Counsel patients to discard breast milk expressed during this period. There are no data on the presence of dihydroergotamine in human milk; however, the related drug ergotamine is known to be excreted in breast milk. Serious adverse effects have been reported in breastfed infants exposed to ergotamine, including diarrhea, vomiting, weak pulse, and blood pressure instability. Dihydroergotamine may also decrease milk production by reducing prolactin levels.

  Adverse Reactions
  General Information
  Ergot toxicity is characterized by severe peripheral vasoconstriction. In the peripheral circulation, this effect is characterized by pain in the extremities, myalgia, numbness and tingling in fingers and toes, cyanosis, and/or cold extremities. Dihydroergotamine can also cause coronary vasospasm. Serious cardiac events, including angina, myocardial infarction, ventricular tachycardia, ventricular fibrillation, and death have been reported to occur following administration of dihydroergotamine mesylate injection. Although no deaths have been reported in patients receiving the nasal spray formulation, the potential for adverse cardiac effects exists. Patients who experience signs or symptoms suggestive of angina following administration of dihydroergotamine should be evaluated for the presence of coronary artery disease or a predisposition to variant angina before receiving additional doses.Significant elevations in blood pressure, occasionally leading to hypertension, have been reported in patients treated with dihydroergotamine. Increased blood pressure has occurred in patients with and without a history of hypertension.Adverse cerebrovascular events, including intracranial bleeding, subarachnoid hemorrhage, and stroke, have been reported in patients treated with dihydroergotamine injection. It is possible that in many cases, the patients' symptoms were reflective of a primary cerebrovascular event and that dihydroergotamine was administered in the incorrect belief that the symptoms were due to migraine. Nevertheless, patients with migraine may be at increased risk for certain cerebrovascular events.Dihydroergotamine can cause histamine release, leading to localized edema and pruritus, sinus tachycardia or sinus bradycardia, weakness in the legs, and/or dizziness.About 30% of patients treated with dihydroergotamine nasal spray have reported throat irritation, nasal irritation, and/or dysgeusia. Specific symptoms have included nasal congestion, burning sensation, dryness, discharge, epistaxis, pain, or soreness. The symptoms were primarily mild to moderate in severity and were transient. In about 70% of cases, symptoms resolved within four hours after dosing.All ergot alkaloids can cause nausea or vomiting, although dihydroergotamine causes less nausea/vomiting than ergotamine.Injection site reaction including hematomas, irritation, mild pain or burning, and echymosis can occur following parenteral administration of dihydroergotamine. Local reactions are more common with IM injections or when dihydroergotamine is used in combination with heparin.Excessive dosing or prolonged administration of dihydroergotamine may inhibit lactation. This effect is related to the suppression of prolactin.Although specific effects of dihydroergotamine are not attributable to teratogenesis, it is known that the drug may induce fetal harm. Retarded fetal growth and an increase in intrauterine fetal death, including fetal abortion, have been seen in animals exposed to ergot alkaloids during gestation. Ergot alkaloids stimulate uterine contractions and are potent vasoconstrictors of the placental vascular bed.There have been reports of pulmonary fibrosis and retroperitoneal fibrosis in patients following prolonged daily use of injectable dihydroergotamine mesylate. Rarely, the long-term daily use of other ergot alkaloids has been associated with the development of cardiac valvulopathy (cardiac valvular fibrosis), including fibrotic thickening of the aortic, mitral, tricuspid, and/or pulmonary valves. Such effects have included aortic and/or mitral regurgitation, mitral stenosis, severe tricuspid regurgitation, and pulmonary regurgitation. Rare cases have also been reported in association with the use of injectable dihydroergotamine mesylate; however, in those cases, patients also received drugs known to be associated with cardiac valvular fibrosis.

Description
  Text
  Dihydroergotamine is a parenteral and nasally administered ergotamine derivative indicated for the treatment of acute migraine with or without aura and cluster headache. Dihydroergotamine is not indicated for the treatment of hemiplegic or basilar migraine or for migraine prophylaxis. Guidelines classify intranasal and parenteral dihydroergotamine as having established and probable efficacy, respectively, for the treatment of acute migraine. Serious adverse cardiac and cerebrovascular events, including acute myocardial infarction, ventricular tachycardia and ventricular fibrillation, coronary artery vasospasm, transient myocardial ischemia, cerebral hemorrhage, subarachnoid hemorrhage, and stroke, have occurred after the use of dihydroergotamine; some of these events have been fatal. Prior to initiation of dihydroergotamine, a cardiovascular evaluation is recommended to determine if the patient is free of coronary artery and ischemic myocardial disease or other significant underlying cardiovascular disease. Dihydroergotamine was FDA-approved in 1946.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Dihydroergotamine binds with high affinity to 5-HT1D receptors. It also binds with high affinity to serotonin 5-HT1A, 5-HT2A, and 5-HT2C receptors, alpha1 and alpha2-adrenergic receptors, and dopamine D2L and D3 receptors. The therapeutic activity of dihydroergotamine in migraine is generally attributed to the agonist effect at 5HT1D receptors. It is hypothesized that activation of 5-HT1D receptors located on intracranial blood vessels, including those on arterio-venous anastomoses, leads to vasoconstriction, which correlates with the relief of migraine headache. An alternative hypothesis suggests that activation of 5-HT1D receptors on sensory nerve endings of the trigeminal system results in the inhibition of pro-inflammatory neuropeptide release.

    Intranasal Route
Absorption of dihydroergotamine is variable after intranasal administration.   Text
  Dihydroergotamine is administered parenterally or intranasally. Dihydroergotamine is 93% bound to plasma protein, and the apparent steady-state Vd is approximately 800 L. The major metabolite, 8'-beta-hydroxy dihydroergotamine, exhibits affinity equivalent to its parent for adrenergic and 5-HT receptors and demonstrates equivalent potency in several venoconstrictor activity models, in vivo and in vitro. The other metabolites, dihydrolysergic acid, dihydrolysergic amide, and a metabolite formed by oxidative opening of the proline ring, are of minor importance. The major excretory route of dihydroergotamine is via the bile in the feces. The total body clearance is 1.5 L/minute, which reflects mainly hepatic clearance. Only 6% to 7% of unchanged dihydroergotamine is excreted in the urine after intramuscular injection. The renal clearance (0.1 L/minute) is unaffected by the route of dihydroergotamine administration. The decline of plasma dihydroergotamine after intramuscular or intravenous administration is multi-exponential with a terminal half-life of about 9 hours. The decline of plasma dihydroergotamine after nasal administration is biphasic with a half-life of about 10 to 13 hours.
