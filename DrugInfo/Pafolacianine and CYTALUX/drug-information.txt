    General Information
    Infusion-related reactions (17%) reported in subjects receiving pafolacianine in clinical studies include nausea (13%), vomiting (5%), abdominal pain (2%), flushing (2%), hypersensitivity (2%), dyspepsia (1%), chest pain (unspecified) (1%), hypertension (1%), and other infusion-related reactions (2%). Reactions typically occurred within 15 minutes of the start of infusion. Pafolacianine infusion interruption or discontinuation due to adverse reactions occurred in 11% of all patients. Description
  Text
  Pafolacianine is an intravenous optical imaging agent indicated for intraoperative identification of malignant lesions as an adjunct in adults with ovarian cancer or malignant and non-malignant pulmonary lesions known or suspected as lung cancer. Pafolacianine is used with a near infrared (NIR) imaging system cleared by the FDA for specific use with pafolacianine. Pafolacianine should only be used by surgeons who have completed a training program on the use of NIR imaging systems for fluorescence imaging during surgery. Infusion-related reactions have been reported with pafolacianine; consider prophylaxis against infusion-related reactions by preemptively administering antihistamines and antinausea medications.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Pafolacianine is a fluorescent drug that targets folate receptors (FR), which may be overexpressed in ovarian cancer. Pafolacianine binds to FR-expressing cancer cells, internalizes via receptor-mediated endocytosis, and accumulates intracellularly. Accumulation of pafolacianine in these cancer tissues can assist in the intraoperative identification of the malignant lesions when used in conjunction with a near infrared (NIR) imaging system cleared by the FDA for specific use with pafolacianine. NIR imaging devices which excite at 760 to 785 nm and detect emission at 790 to 815 nm are suitable for use with pafolacianine. Pafolacianine absorbs light in the NIR region within a range of 760 to 785 nm (peak 776 nm) and emits fluorescence within a range of 790 to 815 nm (peak 796 nm). The mechanism of pafolacianine detection of lung lesions is not well understood. The density of FR in malignant lesions in the lung is generally similar to that of normal tissue.

  Text
  Pafolacianine is administered intravenously. The mean Vd of pafolacianine is 17.1 +/- 5.99 L, indicating distribution into tissues. Plasma protein binding of pafolacianine is 93.7%. No notable partitioning into red blood cells has been observed. The elimination half-life of pafolacianine is 0.44 +/- 0.23 hours and mean plasma clearance is 28.6 +/- 4.97 L/hour. Pafolacianine does not undergo metabolism by cytochrome P450 (CYP) enzymes. After administration of a single IV infusion of radiolabeled pafolacianine, approximately 35% of the dose was recovered in the urine (19.1%) and feces (15.8%) after 3 to 5 weeks.

      Dilution
Thaw frozen pafolacianine vial in the original carton at room temperature between 20 and 25 degrees C (68 to 77 degrees F) for at least 90 minutes.
Hand shake or vortex the thawed solution for 60 seconds.
Withdraw the calculated volume of pafolacianine for a dose of 0.025 mg/kg. Discard any unused portion in the vial.
Add pafolacianine to 250 mL of 5% Dextrose Injection.
Use only 5% Dextrose Injection for dilution. Use of an incorrect diluent to prepare pafolacianine infusion can cause the aggregation of pafolacianine and may induce infusion reactions, such as nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, or rash. Do not use other diluents.
Gently swirl the bag by hand for 1 minute to mix the solution. The mixture should be light blue/green to clear and not contain any visible particulate matter.
Protect the infusion bag from light using a light-blocking cover during infusion and storage.
Storage: If not used immediately, store the diluted solution under refrigeration at 2 to 8 degrees C (36 to 46 degrees F) for up to 24 hours. Once the bag is removed from refrigeration, the infusion must be completed within 3 hours.
 
Intravenous Infusion
Pafolacianine is to be used with a near infrared (NIR) imaging system cleared by the FDA for specific use with pafolacianine. Clinical data demonstrates that NIR imaging devices which excite at 760 to 785 nm and detect emission at 794 to 796 nm are suitable for use with pafolacianine.
Administer IV over 60 minutes using a dedicated infusion line, 1 to 9 hours before surgery.
If an infusion-related reaction occurs during administration, interrupt the infusion and treat with antihistamine and/or antinausea medications as necessary. Complete the infusion within 3 hours of the start of the initial administration.
