Drug Class: Tepotinib is a kinase inhibitor that targets mesenchymal-epithelial transition (MET), a proto-oncogene that induces downstream signaling through the RAS-RAF and PI3K pathways.

Routes: oral; Common adult doses: 450 mg PO once daily.

Common Side Effects (excluding routine GI upset): elevated hepatic enzymes, hepatic failure, hepatotoxicity, arthralgia, back pain, bone pain, musculoskeletal pain, myalgia

In an Overdose: No overdose/antidote information is listed in this file.

Key Nursing Considerations: Contraindications/precautions: Monitor patients for new or worsening respiratory symptoms including fever, cough, or dyspnea. Immediately interrupt tepotinib therapy in patients with new or worsening respiratory symptoms and permanently discontinue therapy if no other potential causes are identified.

          Dosage Description
          450 mg PO once daily until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity. Coadministration of certain drugs may need to be avoided or dosage adjustments may be necessary; review drug interactions. In a multicenter, multicohort, noncomparative, open-label phase 2 study of patients with advanced or metastatic NSCLC with MET exon 14 skipping alterations and EGFR wild-type and ALK-negative status (VISION), treatment with tepotinib resulted in an overall response rate (ORR) of 57% in patients who were treatment naive (n = 164) and an ORR of 45% in those who were previously treated (n = 149); 84% of patients who were previously treated received prior platinum-based chemotherapy. The median duration of response was 46.4 months in patients who were treatment naive and 12.6 months in those who were previously treated; 66% of patients in each group had a duration of response of at least 6 months, while the duration was at least 12 months in 40% and 36% of patients, respectively.

    Headline
    pulmonary disease
    General Information
    Use tepotinib with caution in patients who have a history of pre-existing pulmonary disease; severe pneumonitis/interstitial lung disease has been reported in patients treated with tepotinib. Monitor patients for new or worsening respiratory symptoms including fever, cough, or dyspnea. Immediately interrupt tepotinib therapy in patients with new or worsening respiratory symptoms and permanently discontinue therapy if no other potential causes are identified.
    Headline
    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Pregnancy should be avoided by females of reproductive potential during tepotinib treatment and for at least 1 week after the last dose. Although there are no adequately controlled studies in pregnant women, tepotinib can cause fetal harm or death when administered during pregnancy based on its mechanism of action and animal studies. Women who are pregnant or who become pregnant while receiving tepotinib should be apprised of the potential hazard to the fetus. Oral administration of tepotinib to pregnant rabbits during organogenesis resulted in teratogenicity (e.g., skeletal malformations including malrotations of the fore and/or hind paws with concomitant misshapen scapular and/or malpositioned clavicle and/or calcaneous and/or talus, and spina bifida) and fetal death at exposures less than the human exposure based on the AUC at the 450-mg clinical dose.
    Headline
    contraception requirements, male-mediated teratogenicity, pregnancy testing, reproductive risk
    General Information
    Counsel patients about the reproductive risk and contraception requirements during tepotinib treatment. Tepotinib can be teratogenic if taken by the mother during pregnancy. Females of reproductive potential should avoid pregnancy and use effective contraception during and for 1 week after treatment with tepotinib; due to the risk of male-mediated teratogenicity, men with female partners of reproductive potential should also use effective contraception during and for 1 week after treatment. Females of reproductive potential should undergo pregnancy testing prior to initiation of tepotinib. Women who become pregnant while receiving tepotinib should be apprised of the potential hazard to the fetus.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in nursing infants from tepotinib, advise women to discontinue breast-feeding during treatment and for 1 week after the final dose. It is not known whether tepotinib is present in human milk.

Abacavir; Dolutegravir; lamiVUDine: (Moderate) Monitor for increased toxicity of dolutegravir if coadministered with tepotinib. Concurrent use may increase the plasma concentrations of dolutegravir. Dolutegravir is a P-gp substrate and tepotinib is a P-gp inhibitor.
Afatinib: (Moderate) If the concomitant use of tepotinib and afatinib is necessary, monitor for afatinib-related adverse reactions. If the original dose of afatinib is not tolerated, consider reducing the daily dose of afatinib by 10 mg; resume the previous dose of afatinib as tolerated after discontinuation of tepotinib. The manufacturer of afatinib recommends permanent discontinuation of therapy for severe or intolerant adverse drug reactions at a dose of 20 mg per day, but does not address a minimum dose otherwise. Afatinib is a P-gp substrate and tepotinib is a P-gp inhibitor. Administration with another P-gp inhibitor, given 1 hour before a single dose of afatinib, increased afatinib exposure by 48%; there was no change in afatinib exposure when the P-gp inhibitor was administered at the same time as afatinib or 6 hours later. In healthy subjects, the relative bioavailability for AUC and Cmax of afatinib was 119% and 104%, respectively, when coadministered with the same P-gp inhibitor, and 111% and 105% when the inhibitor was administered 6 hours after afatinib.

Description
  Text
  Tepotinib is a kinase inhibitor that targets mesenchymal-epithelial transition (MET), a proto-oncogene that induces downstream signaling through the RAS-RAF and PI3K pathways. It is approved for the treatment of adult patients with metastatic non-small cell lung cancer harboring MET exon 14 skipping alterations. Tepotinib received accelerated approval based on an overall response rate of 43 months in both treatment-naive and previously treated patients, for a median duration of 10.8 and 11.1 months, respectively in a multicenter, multicohort, open-label study. Hepatotoxicity can occur; liver function tests should be monitored at baseline and periodically during treatment.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Tepotinib is a kinase inhibitor that targets MET, including the mutant variant produced by exon 14 skipping. Tepotinib inhibits hepatocyte growth factor (HGF)-dependent and -independent MET phosphorylation and MET-dependent downstream signaling pathways. MET is a proto-oncogene that induces downstream signaling through the RAS-RAF and PI3K pathways. MET exon 14 skipping leads to impaired MET ubiquitination, decreased MET turnover, and increased signaling leading to increased cell proliferation, survival, and metastasis. Tepotinib also inhibited melatonin 2 and imidazoline 1 receptors at clinically achievable concentrations. In vitro, tepotinib inhibited tumor cell proliferation, anchorage-independent growth, and migration of MET-dependent tumor cells. In mice implanted with tumor cell lines with oncogenic activation of MET (including MET exon 14 skipping alterations), tepotinib inhibited tumor growth, led to sustained inhibition of MET phosphorylation, and in one model, decreased the formation of metastases.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Oral Route
    Description
    The geometric mean Cmax of tepotinib at steady state was 1,291 (CV, 48.1%) after a 450-mg once daily dose administered to cancer patients; the AUC was 27,438 ng x hour/mL (CV, 51.7%). Tepotinib exposure (Cmax and AUC) increases proportionally with dose over a range of 27 mg to 450 mg. After multiple daily doses of tepotinib, the median accumulation was 2.5-fold for Cmax and 3.3-fold for AUC. The median Tmax of tepotinib is 8 hours (range, 6 to 12 hours). The geometric mean absolute bioavailability of tepotinib in the fed state was 71.6% (CV, 10.8%) in healthy subjects.
The mean Cmax of tepotinib increased by 2-fold and the AUC by 1.6-fold following administration of a high-fat, high-calorie meal (approximately 800 to 1,000 calories; 150 calories protein, 250 calories carbohydrates, and 500 to 600 calories fat). The median Tmax decreased from 12 hours to 8 hours.

  Tepotinib is administered orally. It is 98% protein bound at clinically relevant exposures, independent of drug concentration. The geometric mean apparent volume of distribution of tepotinib is 1,038 liters (CV, 24.3%). Affected cytochrome P450 (CYP450) isoenzymes and drug transporters: CYP2C8, CYP3A4, P-glycoprotein (P-gp)
Tepotinib is primarily metabolized by CYP3A4 and CYP2C8; one major circulating plasma metabolite has been identified (M506). Tepotinib is also a P-gp substrate and a P-gp inhibitor. Tepotinib may also inhibit intestinal BCRP at clinically relevant concentrations.

Dosage Adjustment Guidelines
  Hepatic Impairment
  Baseline Hepatic Impairment
Mild to moderate hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh A or B): No dosage adjustment is recommended.
Severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh C): The pharmacokinetics and safety of tepotinib have not been studied.
 
Treatment-Related Hepatotoxicity
Grade 3 increased ALT/AST OR increased bilirubin: Hold tepotinib therapy until recovery to baseline ALT/AST or bilirubin. If recovery occurs within 7 days, resume treatment at the same dose. If recovery occurs after 7 days, resume tepotinib at a reduced dose of 225 mg PO once daily. Permanently discontinue tepotinib therapy in patients unable to tolerate a dose of 225 mg once daily.
Grade 4 increased ALT/AST OR increased bilirubin: Permanently discontinue tepotinib therapy.
ALT/AST more than 3 times the upper limit of normal (ULN) with total bilirubin more than 2 times ULN, in the absence of cholestasis or hemolysis: Permanently discontinue tepotinib therapy.
  Renal Impairment
  Mild to moderate renal impairment (CrCl 30 to 89 mL/min): No dosage adjustment is recommended.
Severe renal impairment (CrCl less than 30 mL/min): The recommended dose for tepotinib has not been established.
