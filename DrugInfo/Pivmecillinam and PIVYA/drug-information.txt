Drug Class: Pivmecillinam is the oral prodrug of mecillinam, an amidinopenicillin antibiotic, indicated for uncomplicated urinary tract infections (UTIs).

Routes: oral; Common adult doses: 555 mg/day PO.

Common Side Effects (excluding routine GI upset): dizziness, headache, vertigo, esophageal ulceration, oral ulceration, c. difficile-associated diarrhea, candidiasis, pseudomembranous colitis, superinfection

In an Overdose: No overdose/antidote information is listed in this file.

    General Information

Description
  Text
  Pivmecillinam is the oral prodrug of mecillinam, an amidinopenicillin antibiotic, indicated for uncomplicated urinary tract infections (UTIs). Mecillinam has a targeted spectrum of activity and is mainly active against gram-negative bacteria. Unlike the majority of other beta-lactam agents, which preferentially bind gram-negative penicillin-binding protein (PBP)-1A, PBP-1B or PBP-3, mecillinam exerts high specificity against PBP-2 in the gram-negative cell wall. This results in a low cross-resistance with certain other beta-lactam antibiotics. Pivmecillinam may cause carnitine deficiency, especially with long-term use, and acute attacks of porphyria and use is contraindicated in persons with primary or secondary carnitine deficiency and in those with porphyria. Severe cutaneous adverse reactions (SCAR) have also been reported with pivmecillinam use. Pivmecillinam was FDA-approved in April 2024.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Pivmecillinam, a penicillin antibiotic, is the prodrug containing the pivaloyloxymethylester of the amidinopenicillanic acid, mecillinam. Mecillinam is the active antibacterial agent and is mainly active against gram-negative bacteria. Beta-lactam antibiotics, such as mecillinam, are mainly bactericidal. Like other penicillins, mecillinam inhibits the third and final stage of bacterial cell wall synthesis by preferentially binding to specific penicillin-binding proteins (PBPs) that are located inside the bacterial cell wall. PBPs are responsible for several steps in the synthesis of the cell wall and are found in quantities of several hundred to several thousand molecules per bacterial cell. PBPs vary among different bacterial species. Thus, the intrinsic activity of mecillinam, as well as other penicillins, against a particular organism depends on its ability to gain access to and bind with the necessary PBP. Like all beta-lactam antibiotics, mecillinam's ability to interfere with PBP-mediated cell wall synthesis ultimately leads to cell lysis. Lysis is mediated by bacterial cell wall autolytic enzymes (i.e., autolysins). The relationship between PBPs and autolysins is unclear, but it is possible that the beta-lactam antibiotic interferes with an autolysin inhibitor. Prevention of the autolysin response to beta-lactam antibiotic exposure through loss of autolytic activity (mutation) or inactivation of autolysin (low-medium pH) by the microorganism can lead to tolerance to the beta-lactam antibiotic resulting in bacteriostatic activity. Unlike the majority of other beta-lactam agents, which preferentially bind gram-negative PBP-1A, PBP-1B, or PBP-3, mecillinam exerts high specificity against PBP-2 in the gram-negative cell wall.
 
Beta-lactams, including mecillinam, exhibit concentration-independent or time-dependent killing. In vitro and in vivo animal studies have demonstrated that the major pharmacodynamic parameter that determines efficacy for beta-lactams is the amount of time free (non-protein bound) drug concentrations exceed the minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC) of the organism (free T above MIC). This microbiological killing pattern is due to the mechanism of action, which is acylation of PBPs. There is a maximum proportion of PBPs that can be acylated; therefore, once maximum acylation has occurred, killing rates cannot increase. Free beta-lactam concentrations do not have to remain above the MIC for the entire dosing interval. The percentage of time required for both bacteriostatic and maximal bactericidal activity is different for the various classes of beta-lactams.
 
Mecillinam demonstrates in vitro activity against Enterobacterales in the presence of some beta-lactamases and extended-spectrum beta-lactamases (ESBLs) of the following groups: CTX-M, SHV, TEM, and AmpC. The inhibitory action of mecillinam on PBP-2 results in low cross-resistance with certain beta-lactams.
 
The susceptibility interpretive criteria for pivmecillinam are delineated by pathogen. The MICs are defined for mecillinam for Enterobacterales as susceptible at 8 mcg/mL or less, intermediate at 16 mcg/mL, and resistant at 32 mcg/mL or more based on a dose 185 mg PO 3 times daily. This is clinically applicable only to E. coli isolates from the urinary tract.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics

    Oral Route
    Description
    Pivmecillinam is well absorbed. The estimated oral bioavailability of mecillinam is 25% to 35% based on comparison of dose normalized exposure after 185 mg oral pivmecillinam administration and 200 mg IV mecillinam administration. After a single oral dose of pivmecillinam, the mecillinam Cmax is 1.7 mcg/mL and AUC is 214 mcg x minute/mL. Tmax is 90 minutes and there is no clinically significant accumulation of mecillinam. Food does not significantly affect mecillinam pharmacokinetics.

  Pivmecillinam, the prodrug of the active antibacterial moiety mecillinam, is administered orally. Pivmecillinam is subsequently rapidly hydrolyzed to mecillinam and pivalic acid by non-specific esterases present in blood, gastrointestinal mucosa, and other tissues. Mecillinam plasma protein binding is less than 25% and the apparent Vd is 51 L. Mecillinam undergoes minimal hepatic metabolism and the primary route of elimination is urinary excretion, primarily as mecillinam (80% of dose). Oral clearance of pivmecillinam is 580 mL/minute and the terminal half-life of mecillinam is 61 minutes after a single pivmecillinam dose.
 
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes and drug transporters: OAT3, MATE1, MATE2-K
In vitro, mecillinam is a substrate of OAT3, MATE1, and MATE2-K.
