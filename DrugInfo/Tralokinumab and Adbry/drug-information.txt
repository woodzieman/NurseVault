    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Data on the use of tralokinumab during pregnancy are limited and do not allow for a definitive assessment of the risk for major birth defects, miscarriage, or other adverse outcomes. As human IgG antibodies can cross the placenta, tralokinumab may be transmitted from the pregnant individual to the fetus. As the pregnancy progresses, transport of IgG antibodies across the placenta increases and peaks during the third trimester. Tralokinumab may be present in neonates exposed in utero. Consider the clinical impact of tralokinumab in neonates exposed in utero. In a study with monkeys, intravenous administration of tralokinumab during organogenesis through parturition at weekly doses up to 10 times the MRHD (based on mg/kg) showed no adverse effects on offspring development from birth to 6 months of age. There is a pregnancy exposure registry available that monitors pregnancy outcomes in people exposed to tralokinumab. Care teams may enroll patients or patients may self-enroll by visiting mothertobaby.org/ongoing-study/adbry-tralokinumab or by calling 1-877-311-8972.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Use tralokinumab with caution during breast-feeding. There are no data on the presence of tralokinumab in human milk, its effects on the breast-fed child, or its effect on milk production. Human IgG is present in human milk. If tralokinumab is present in human milk, the effects of local gastrointestinal exposure and limited systemic exposure on the breast-fed child are unknown. Consider the benefits of breast-feeding, the patient's clinical need for treatment, and any potential adverse effects on the breast-fed child from the medication or from the patient's underlying medical condition.

    conjunctivitis, keratitis, keratoconjunctivitis
    General Information
    Conjunctivitis and keratitis were reported more frequently in adult patients receiving tralokinumab in clinical trials compared with placebo-treated patients. Most subjects with conjunctivitis or keratitis recovered or were recovering during the treatment period. Advise patients to report any new onset or worsening eye symptoms to their health care provider. In a pooled analysis of 5 atopic dermatitis trials in which 1,964 adult subjects were treated with tralokinumab, with or without concomitant topical corticosteroids, conjunctivitis was reported in 7.5% of patients receiving tralokinumab in the initial treatment period of up to 16 weeks compared with 3.1% of patients receiving placebo. Conjunctivitis led to discontinuation of treatment in 2 subjects during the initial treatment period. During the maintenance treatment period of the monotherapy trials, conjunctivitis was reported in 8.9% of subjects treated with tralokinumab 300 mg every other week (20 events per 100 subject-years of exposure) and in 6.3% of subjects treated with tralokinumab 300 mg every 4 weeks (14 events per 100 subject-years of exposure). Conjunctivitis was reported as a severe event in 1 subject and led to discontinuation of treatment in another subject during the maintenance treatment period. A similar pattern was seen during the continuation trial. Keratoconjunctivitis (including 1 atopic keratoconjunctivitis) was reported in 5 (0.3%) subjects treated with tralokinumab (1.2 events per 100 subject-years of exposure), and in no subjects treated with placebo in the pooled trials. Keratoconjunctivitis was reported as resolved in 2 out of the 5 subjects by the end of trials. Keratitis (including 1 ulcerative keratitis) was reported in 4 (0.2%) subjects treated with tralokinumab (0.9 events per 100 subject-years of exposure) and 1 subject treated with placebo (0.6 events per 100 subject-years of exposure) during the initial treatment period of up to 16 weeks in the pooled trials. Keratitis resolved in all but 1 subject by the end of the trials. There were no events that were serious or led to the discontinuation of treatment. In the initial period of the monotherapy trials, a keratitis rate of 2 events per 100 subject-years of exposure was reported. During the maintenance treatment period of the monotherapy trials in the tralokinumab 300 mg every other week group, keratitis was reported in 1 (0.6%) subject (ulcerative, severe, resolved after discontinuation) at an exposure-adjusted event rate of 1.2 per 100 subject-years, and keratoconjunctivitis (not serious or severe, resolved, not led to discontinuation) was reported in 3 (1.9%) subjects (3.6 events per 100 subject-years of exposure). No events of keratitis or keratoconjunctivitis were reported in tralokinumab every 4 weeks or placebo groups. No additional keratitis events were reported for patients receiving combination tralokinumab plus topical corticosteroids in the 16-week continuation period.

    General Information
    As with all therapeutic proteins, immunogenicity or antibody formation may occur with tralokinumab. In adult clinical trials, the incidence of anti-drug antibodies (ADA) during the initial 16-week treatment period was 1.4% for subjects treated with tralokinumab 300 mg every other week and 1.3% for subjects treated with placebo; neutralizing antibodies were seen in 0.1% of subjects treated with tralokinumab and 0.2% of subjects treated with placebo. Description
  Text
  Tralokinumab, an interleukin-13 antagonist, is a human monoclonal antibody of the IgG4 subclass. It is indicated for the treatment of moderate-to-severe atopic dermatitis (AD) in adults and pediatric patients 12 years and older whose disease is not adequately controlled with other topical prescription therapies or when use of those therapies is not advised. Tralokinumab is the first FDA-approved biologic that specifically binds to and inhibits the IL-13 cytokine, a key driver of atopic dermatitis signs and symptoms. Tralokinumab is administered as a subcutaneous injection and may be used with or without topical corticosteroid therapy. It may also be utilized with topical calcineurin inhibitors; however, use of these topical agents should be reserved for problem areas only (i.e., face, neck, intertriginous and genital areas). In 2 randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled clinical trials (n = 1,568) with tralokinumab monotherapy in adult patients, 16% to 21% of patients treated with tralokinumab achieved clear or almost clear skin (IGA 1/1) at week 16 vs. 7% to 9% of placebo-treated patients. Additionally, 25% to 33% of patients treated with tralokinumab achieved an improvement of 75% or more in the Eczema Area and Severity Index score (EASI-75) vs. 10% to 13% of placebo-treated patients. The efficacy of tralokinumab was established in a multicenter, randomized, double-blind study in 298 pediatric patients 12 to 17 years with moderate-to-severe AD. At week 16, 21% of patients treated with tralokinumab achieved clear or almost clear skin (IGA 0 or 1) compared to 4% of placebo-treated patients. Additionally, 29% of patients treated with tralokinumab achieved an EASI-75 vs. 6% of placebo-treated patients. Tralokinumab may alter a patient's immunity and increase the risk of infection following administration of live vaccines; complete all age-appropriate vaccinations prior to initiating therapy.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Tralokinumab is a human IgG4 monoclonal antibody that specifically binds to human interleukin-13 (IL-13) and inhibits its interaction with the IL-13 receptor alpha1 and alpha2 subunits (IL-13R alpha1 and IL-13R alpha2). IL-13 is a naturally occurring cytokine of the Type 2 immune response. Tralokinumab inhibits the bioactivity of IL-13 by blocking IL-13 interaction with IL-13R alpha1/IL-4R alpha receptor complex. Tralokinumab inhibits IL-13-induced responses, including the release of proinflammatory cytokines, chemokines and IgE.
Tralokinumab was associated with decreased concentrations of Th2 and Th22 immunity biomarkers in the blood, such as thymus and activation-regulated chemokine (TARC/CCL17), periostin, IL-22, lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) and serum IgE. It also decreased expression of keratin 16 and Ki-67 in AD skin, and upregulated protein expression of loricrin. Tralokinumab suppressed expression of genes in the Th2 pathway, including CCL17, CCL18 and CCL26 as well as markers of Th17- and Th22-regulated genes in lesional skin. The clinical relevance of these biomarkers is unknown.

    Description
    The absolute bioavailability of tralokinumab is estimated to be 76%. The Tmax was 5 to 8 days after administration. Preparation
Remove the carton containing the tralokinumab from the refrigerator at least 30 minutes for prefilled syringes and 45 minutes for autoinjectors prior to administration. Lay the carton on a flat surface and allow the prefilled syringes or autoinjectors to reach room temperature of 20 to 25 degrees C (68 to 77 degrees F). Do not microwave the syringes or autoinjectors, run hot water over them, or leave them in direct sunlight. Do not shake the syringes or autoinjectors.
Do not remove the needle cover until right before tralokinumab is administered.
After removal from the refrigerator, prefilled syringes or autoinjectors may be kept at room temperature up to 30 degrees C (86 degrees F) and must be used within 14 days or discarded. Do not put syringes or autoinjectors that have reached room temperature back into the refrigerator.
The drug solution does not contain preservatives and each prefilled syringe or autoinjector is for single-use only; therefore, discard any unused product.
 
Subcutaneous injection for Adults (autoinjector and prefilled syringe)
Administer subcutaneously into the thigh or abdomen, except for the 2 inches (5 cm) around the navel. The upper arm can also be used if a caregiver administers the injection.
For the initial 600 mg dose, administer each of four tralokinumab 150-mg injections using prefilled syringes or two tralokinumab 300-mg injections using the autoinjectors at different injection sites within the same body area. Each injection site should be at least 1 inch from the prior injection site.
For the subsequent 300 mg doses, administer each of the two tralokinumab 150-mg injections using prefilled syringes or one tralokinumab 300-mg autoinjector at different injection sites within the same body area. Each injection site should be at least 1 inch from the prior injection site.
Rotate the body area with each subsequent set of injections; do not use the same body area 2 times in a row. DO NOT inject tralokinumab into skin that is tender, damaged, bruised, or scarred.
 
Subcutaneous injection for pediatric patients 12 years and older (prefilled syringe only)
Administer subcutaneously into the thigh or abdomen, except for the 2 inches (5 cm) around the navel. The upper arm can also be used if a caregiver administers the injection.
For the initial 300 mg dose, administer each of the injections two tralokinumab 150 mg using only prefilled syringes at different injection sites within the same body area. Each injection site should be at least 1 inch from the prior injection site.
For the subsequent 150 mg doses, administer one tralokinumab 150 mg injections using a prefilled syringe at different injection sites within the same body area.
Rotate the body area with each subsequent set of injections; do not use the same body are 2 times in a row. DO NOT inject tralokinumab into skin that is tender, damaged, bruised, or scarred.
 
Instructions for use
Autoinjectors
NOTE: Tralokinumab autoinjectors are for use in adults only.
Wash hands and clean the injection site(s) with an alcohol wipe. Allow the alcohol to dry before injecting. Do not touch or blow on the injection site after it has been cleansed.
When ready to inject, remove the needle cap by pulling it straight off. Do not put the needle cap back on after it has been removed. Do not hold the plunger rod or plunger head while removing the needle cover. Do not touch the needle or allow it to touch any surface; throw it away if this does occur.
Gently pinch and hold a fold of cleaned skin.
Place the needle guard of the autoinjector flat against your skin at a 90-degree angle.
Press the autoinjector down firmly and hold it in place. You will hear a click to let you know that the injection has started, and the yellow plunger will start to move. Hold until the yellow plunger is at the bottom of the viewing window. Keep pressing for another 5 seconds.
Pull the autoinjector straight away from the injection area. The needle guard will slide down and lock into place over the needle.
Lightly press a cotton ball or gauze onto the injection site if you see any blood. Do not rub the injection site. If needed, cover the injection site with a small bandage.
Autoinjectors are single-use only; the drug solution does not contain preservatives. Immediately discard autoinjector and any unused product in a proper sharps disposal container.
Prefilled syringe
Wash your hands and clean the injection site(s) with an alcohol wipe. Allow the alcohol to dry before injecting. Do not touch or blow on the injection site after it has been cleansed.
When ready to inject, remove the needle cap by pulling it straight off. Do not put the needle cap back on after it has been removed. Do not hold the plunger rod or plunger head while removing the needle cover. Do not touch the needle or allow it to touch any surface; throw it away if this does occur.
Gently pinch and hold a fold of cleaned skin.
Insert the needle completely into the fold of the skin at a 45-degree angle.
Push the plunger rod down slowly and steadily as far as it will go until the syringe is empty.
Lift thumb off of the plunger and the needle will automatically retract, move back into the syringe, and lock into place.
Lightly press a cotton ball or gauze onto the injection site if you see any blood. Do not rub the injection site. If needed, cover the injection site with a small bandage.
Prefilled syringes are single-use only; the drug solution does not contain preservatives. Immediately discard syringe and any unused product in a proper sharps disposal container.
Missed dose: Administer as soon as possible and then resume dosing at the regular scheduled time.
