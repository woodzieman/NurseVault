Indication Specific Dosing
  General Information
  The estimated LDL-C reduction with evolocumab is approximately 58% to 64%. Due to low-density lipoprotein receptor (LDLR) gene variants, a lower mean LDL-C reduction occurs in homozygous familial hypercholesterolemia (21% to 31% LDL-C reduction).
Evolocumab is recommended as second-line therapy in addition to maximally tolerated statin therapy in individuals with atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD) who require additional lipid-lowering therapy to achieve lipoprotein goals (e.g., LDL-C, non-HDL-C). Factors to consider include the benefit of ASCVD risk reduction, degree of desired LDL-C reduction, and administration by subcutaneous injection.

    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components. The evolocumab prefilled single-dose autoinjector and prefilled single-dose syringe formulations that contain dry natural rubber (a derivative of latex) in the needle cover may cause an allergic reaction in individuals with latex hypersensitivity. Instruct individuals to inform their healthcare provider if they are sensitive to latex. Consider prescribing a formulation of evolocumab that does not contain dry natural rubber for individuals that are sensitive to latex.
    Headline
    magnetic resonance imaging (MRI)
    General Information
    Do not expose the Pushtronex system, a single-use on-body infuser for evolocumab, to a magnetic resonance environment, such as magnetic resonance imaging (MRI).
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Due to a lack of human data, guidelines recommend avoiding the use of evolocumab during pregnancy. In animal studies, no effects on pregnancy or offspring development were observed after evolocumab exposure from organogenesis through parturition at dose exposures up to 12 times the maximum recommended human dose. However, in a similar study with another PCSK9 inhibitor, humoral immune suppression was observed in infant monkeys at exposures that were greater than those expected clinically. Immune suppression of offspring was not evaluated in animal studies with evolocumab. Measurable evolocumab serum concentrations were observed in infant monkeys at birth at comparable concentrations as maternal serum, indicating that evolocumab, like other IgG antibodies, crosses the placental barrier. Monoclonal antibodies are transported across the placenta in increasing amounts, especially near term. Before administering evolocumab during pregnancy, consider the benefits and risks of treatment. There is a pregnancy exposure registry that monitors outcomes in pregnant patients exposed to evolocumab; information about the registry can be obtained at https://wwwext.amgen.com/products/global-patient-safety/adverse-event-reporting or by calling 1-800-772-6436.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Use evolocumab with caution during breast-feeding. Some guidelines recommend avoidance during lactation due to a lack of data. Data suggest that IgG antibodies that are present in human milk do not enter the neonatal and infant circulation in substantial amounts. Evolocumab has a large molecular weight, making it unlikely to enter the milk in significant amounts. In addition, its low oral bioavailability limits absorption in the breastfed child's gut.

    antibody formation
    General Information
    Antibody formation may occur with evolocumab. In a pool of placebo- and active-controlled clinical trials, 0.3% of adult subjects treated with at least 1 dose of evolocumab tested positive for binding antibody development. Subjects who tested positive for binding antibodies were tested for neutralizing antibodies, and none of the patients were positive for neutralizing antibodies. The development of anti-evolocumab antibodies was not detected in pediatric clinical trials. There is no evidence that the presence of anti-drug antibodies impacts the pharmacokinetics, clinical response, or safety of evolocumab. The observed incidence of anti-drug antibodies is highly dependent on the sensitivity and specificity of the assay. Differences in assay methods preclude meaningful comparisons of the incidence of anti-drug antibodies in these studies with the incidence of anti-drug antibodies in other studies, including evolocumab.

Description
  Text
  Evolocumab is a proprotein convertase subtilisin/kexin type 9 (PCSK9) inhibitor indicated as an adjunct to diet and exercise for the treatment of primary hypercholesterolemia, including heterozygous familial hypercholesterolemia (HeFH), and for the treatment of homozygous familial hypercholesterolemia (HoFH). Additionally, evolocumab is also indicated to reduce the risk of myocardial infarction, stroke, and coronary revascularization in individuals at increased risk for these events. During various clinical trials, evolocumab produced negative mean differences from placebo from baseline in LDL-C ranging from 31% to 71%. Evolocumab is recommended as second-line therapy in addition to maximally tolerated statin therapy in individuals with clinical atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD) and comorbidities that still require 25% or greater LDL-C reduction. Factors to consider include cost, benefit of ASCVD risk reduction, dosing frequency requirements, and administration by subcutaneous injection. Evolocumab was FDA-approved in 2015.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Evolocumab is a human monoclonal IgG2 antibody that binds to and inhibits proprotein convertase subtilisin/kexin type 9 (PCSK9) binding to low-density lipoprotein receptors (LDLR). PCSK9 binds to LDLR on the hepatocyte surface to promote LDLR degradation within the liver. LDLR is the primary receptor that clears circulating LDL; with evolocumab inhibiting the binding of PCSK9 to LDLR, the number of LDLRs available to clear LDL increases, thereby lowering LDL-C concentrations.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Subcutaneous Route
    Description
    The absolute bioavailability after subcutaneous administration is 72%. The median Tmax after administration of single doses of 140 mg or 420 mg to adult patients is 3 to 4 days. Maximum suppression of free circulating PCSK9 occurs within 4 hours after a single subcutaneous dose of evolocumab 140 mg or 420 mg and returns to baseline once evolocumab concentrations are undetectable. Evolocumab exhibits non-linear pharmacokinetics. Following administration of a subcutaneous dose of 140 mg and 420 mg to healthy adults, the mean Cmax was 18.6 mcg/mL and 59 mcg/mL, respectively. An approximate 2- to 3-fold accumulation is observed in trough serum concentrations after 140 mg doses administered every 2 weeks (Cmin 7.21) or 420 mg doses administered monthly (Cmin 11.2). Steady state is reached within 12 weeks of dosing.
  Special Populations
    Name
    Hepatic Impairment
    Description
    The mean Cmax and AUC are lower (20% to 30% and 40% to 50%, respectively) in patients with mild and moderate hepatic impairment. Evolocumab has not been studied in patients with severe hepatic impairment.
    Name
    Renal Impairment
    Description
    Since monoclonal antibodies are not known to be eliminated via renal pathways, renal function is not expected to impact the pharmacokinetics of evolocumab. In a clinical trial of 18 adult patients with either normal renal function (eGFR 90 mL/minute/1.73 m2 or more; n = 6), severe renal impairment (eGFR less than 30 mL/minute/1.73 m2; n = 6), or end-stage renal disease (ESRD) receiving hemodialysis (n = 6), exposure to evolocumab after a single 140 mg subcutaneous dose was decreased in patients with severe renal impairment or ESRD receiving hemodialysis. Reductions in PCSK9 concentrations in patients with severe renal impairment or ESRD receiving hemodialysis were similar to those with normal renal function.

  Text
  Evolocumab is administered subcutaneously. The Vd following a single subcutaneous dose of 420 mg was 3.3 L. At low concentrations, evolocumab is eliminated primarily via saturable binding to proprotein convertase subtilisin/kexin type 9 (PCSK9). At higher concentrations, evolocumab is eliminated mainly through a non-saturable proteolytic pathway. The estimated effective half-life of evolocumab is 11 to 17 days. Maximum LDL-C reduction occurred 2 weeks after a single dose of 140 mg and 3 weeks after a single dose of 420 mg.

    Administration Details
      Name
      Subcutaneous Administration
      Description
      If stored in the refrigerator, allow at least 30 minutes for the single-use prefilled syringe, pen, or autoinjector to reach room temperature prior to administration. Allow the solution to warm up naturally. Do not warm by using a heat source or leave in direct sunlight. If the solution has not reached room temperature, it may cause discomfort and may take longer to inject the full dose or result in the full dose not being administered.
Do not shake.
Administer into areas of the abdomen (except for a 2-inch area around the umbilicus), thigh, or upper arm that are not tender, bruised, red, hard, raised, thick, or scaly. Avoid areas with scars, stretch marks, moles, wrinkles, or excessive hair.
Rotate the site with each injection to avoid irritation.
Do not administer with other injectable drugs at the same injection site.
The 420 mg dose can be administered as 3 injections, consecutively within 30 minutes, utilizing the single-use prefilled auto-injector, syringe, or pen.
Single-use prefilled syringe administration
Do not remove the gray needle cap until ready to inject. Do not leave the grey cap off for more than 5 minutes; this can dry out the medication.
Hold the syringe by the barrel and pull the gray needle cap straight off. It is normal to see a drop of solution at the end of the needle. Do not try to remove any air bubbles in the syringe.
Pinch the skin injection site to create a firm surface approximately 2 inches wide. Hold the pinch and insert the needle into the skin using a 45 to 90-degree angle.
Using slow and constant pressure, push the plunger rod all the way down until the syringe is empty.
When done, release the plunger, and gently lift the syringe off the skin.
Dispose the used prefilled syringe in an appropriate sharps container.
Storage: Store in the refrigerator at 2 to 8 degrees C (36 to 46 degrees F) in the original carton to protect from light. Do not freeze. If needed, may keep at room temperature at 20 to 25 degrees C (68 to 77 degrees F) in the original carton for 30 days. If not used within 30 days, discard evolocumab.
Single-use prefilled pen administration
Do not remove the cap until ready to inject and able to inject within 5 minutes.
Pull hard or twist the cap to remove it. Do not touch the yellow safety guard. It is okay to see a few drops of medicine come out of the needle.
Grasp the prefilled pen so the window is visible.
Create a firm surface at the injection site by stretching or pinching the skin until the injection is finished. Pinching is required for children under 12 years of age.
Place the pen on the skin and firmly push down on the pen. The yellow safety guard will disappear and a click may be heard. Hold the prefilled pen down until the injection is complete, which may take 15 seconds. The window will turn fully yellow once the injection is complete and a second click may be heard.
Confirm that the window is fully yellow. This indicates that all medicine was injected.
Dispose the used prefilled pen and cap in an appropriate sharps container.
Storage: Store in the refrigerator at 2 to 8 degrees C (36 to 46 degrees F) in the original carton to protect from light. Do not freeze. If needed, may keep at room temperature at 20 to 25 degrees C (68 to 77 degrees F) for 30 days. If not used within 30 days, discard evolocumab.
Single-use SureClick autoinjector administration
Do not remove the orange cap until you are ready to inject. Do not leave the orange cap off for more than 5 minutes; this can dry out the medication.
When ready for injection, pull the orange cap straight off. Do not twist, bend, or wiggle the orange cap. Do not put fingers into or on the yellow safety guard. It is normal to see a drop of medication come out at the end of the needle or yellow safety guard.
Stretch or pinch the cleansed injection site skin to create a firm surface. Hold the stretch or pinch and place the yellow end of the autoinjector on the skin at a 90 degree angle.
Firmly push the autoinjector down onto the skin until the yellow safety guard stops moving, this will unlock the grey start button.
Press the gray button to start the injection. A click may be heard or felt, and the window will begin to turn yellow. It is okay to let go of the gray start button.
Continue to push the autoinjector down onto the skin until the injection is complete, which may take up to 15 seconds. The window will turn from clear to yellow; this means the injection is complete. A second click may be heard or felt.
Remove the autoinjector from the skin; the needle will be automatically covered.
After removing the autoinjector from the skin, if the window has not turned yellow or if it looks like the medicine is still injecting, this means you have not received a full dose. Do not try to reuse the autoinjector.
Dispose the used autoinjector in an appropriate sharps container.
Storage: Store in the refrigerator at 2 to 8 degrees C (36 to 46 degrees F) in the original carton to protect from light. Do not freeze. If needed, may keep at room temperature at 20 to 25 degrees C (68 to 77 degrees F) for 30 days. If not used within 30 days, discard evolocumab.
    Name
    Injectable Administration
    Description
    The evolocumab prefilled single-dose autoinjector and prefilled single-dose syringe are available as formulations that contain dry natural rubber (a derivative of latex) in the needle cover, which may cause an allergic reaction in individuals with latex hypersensitivity. If an individual is sensitive to latex, consider prescribing a formulation of evolocumab that does not contain dry natural rubber, such as the prefilled, single-dose pen.
Visually inspect for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration. Solution is clear to opalescent, colorless to pale yellow. Air bubbles may be present and that is okay. Do not use if the solution is cloudy, discolored or contains particles.
Do not use if packaging is open or damaged or needle cap is missing or not securely attached.
Do not use if any part of the prefilled auto-injector, syringe, or pen appears cracked or broken or if it has been dropped on a hard surface, even if you cannot see a break. Use a new prefilled auto-injector, syringe, or pen and call 1-844-REPATHA.
Missed dose: If a dose is missed, and it is within 7 days from the missed dose, administer evolocumab and resume the original dosing schedule. If it has been more than 7 days from the missed dose, for every 2 week dosing, wait until the next dose on the original schedule and, for once monthly dosing, administer the dose and start a new schedule based on this date.
