Drug Class: Deferiprone is an iron chelator approved for the treatment of transfusional iron overload due to thalassemia syndromes, sickle cell disease, or other anemias in adults and pediatric patients 3 years and older.

Routes: oral; Common adult doses: 99 mg/kg/day PO.

Common Side Effects (excluding routine GI upset): enterocolitis, gi bleeding, pancreatitis, peptic ulcer, acute cerebellar syndrome, depression, drowsiness, headache, increased intracranial pressure, psychomotor impairment, seizures, teeth grinding (bruxism), agranulocytosis, infection, neutropenia, pancytopenia, thrombocytosis, anaphylactic shock, hyperhidrosis, ocular inflammation, photosensitivity, purpura, rash, urticaria, elevated hepatic enzymes, hepatitis, hepatomegaly, hyperbilirubinemia, jaundice, arthralgia

In an Overdose: No overdose/antidote information is listed in this file.

Key Nursing Considerations: Contraindications/precautions: Deferiprone may cause agranulocytosis which can lead to serious infection and death. Neutropenia may precede the development of agranulocytosis. Hazardous drug: NIOSH 2024 List: Table 2

  Contraindications And Precautions
    Headline
    Drug Interactions
    General Information
    The coadministration of certain medications may lead to harm and require avoidance or therapy modification; review all drug interactions prior to concomitant use of other medications.
    Headline
    agranulocytosis, neutropenia
    General Information
    Deferiprone may cause agranulocytosis which can lead to serious infection and death. Neutropenia may precede the development of agranulocytosis. Neutropenia and agranulocytosis usually resolve after discontinuation of therapy; however, there have been fatal cases of agranulocytosis associated with deferiprone. The mechanism for agranulocytosis due to the drug is not known. Monitor the absolute neutrophil count (ANC) prior to initiation of deferiprone and regularly during therapy according to the following schedule: monitor ANC weekly during the first 6 months of therapy, monitor ANC every 2 weeks during the next 6 months of therapy, and monitor ANC every 2 to 4 weeks (or at the patient's blood transfusion interval in patients that have not experienced an interruption due to any decrease in ANC) after 1 year of therapy. Consider a reduction in the frequency of ANC monitoring on an individual patient basis, according to the assessment of the patient's understanding of the risk minimization measures required during therapy. If agranulocytosis or severe neutropenia develops (i.e., ANC less than 0.5 x 109/L), immediately interrupt therapy and consider hospitalization and other management as clinically appropriate. Do not resume deferiprone therapy in patients who develop agranulocytosis unless potential benefits outweigh potential risks. Do not rechallenge patients who have developed neutropenia unless potential benefits outweigh potential risks. For neutropenia with an ANC of 0.5 to less than 1.5 x 109/L, immediately interrupt therapy and all other medications with the potential to cause neutropenia and obtain a complete blood cell (CBC) count, an ANC, and a platelet count daily until the white blood cell (WBC) count recovers. Interrupt deferiprone therapy if an infection develops and monitor the ANC frequently. Advise patients to immediately interrupt therapy and talk to their health care provider if they experience any symptoms of infection (e.g., fever, chills, sore throat, mouth sores, or flu-like symptoms).
    Headline
    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Deferiprone may cause fetal harm when administered during pregnancy, based on data from animal studies. There is insufficient data on the use of deferiprone during human pregnancy to inform a drug-associated risk of major birth defects or miscarriage. Postmarketing data from 39 pregnancies in deferiprone-treated individuals reported 23 healthy newborns, 6 spontaneous abortions, 9 with unknown outcomes, and 1 infant born with anal atresia, nephroptosis, ventricular septal defect, hemivertebral, and urethral fistula. Data from 10 pregnancies in partners of drug-exposed individuals reported 5 healthy newborns, 1 healthy newborn with slight hypospadias, 1 was elective termination, 1 with intrauterine death of twins, and 2 with unknown outcomes. In animal studies, pregnant rabbits and rats given oral deferiprone during organogenesis at doses 33% and 49%, respectively, of the maximum recommended human dose (MRHD) resulted in embryo-fetal death, structural malformations, and alterations to growth. Advise pregnant individuals of the potential fetal risk.
    Headline
    people who can cause pregnancy in others, people who may become pregnant, reproductive risk
    General Information
    Counsel people who may become pregnant and people who can cause pregnancy in others about the reproductive risk associated with deferiprone. Deferiprone may cause fetal harm. Verify pregnancy status prior to treatment initiation. Advise people who may become pregnant to use effective contraception while taking deferiprone and for at least 6 months after the last dose. For those that can cause pregnancy in others, contraception should be used during deferiprone treatment and for at least 3 months after the last dose.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Advise patients to avoid breast-feeding while taking deferiprone and for at least 2 weeks after the last dose. There are no data on the presence of deferiprone in human milk, its effects on the breastfed child, or its effects on milk production. However, deferiprone has the potential to cause serious side effects in the breastfed child, including the potential tumorigenicity based on data from animal studies.

Aluminum Hydroxide: (Moderate) Concurrent use of deferiprone with food, mineral supplements, and antacids that contain polyvalent (trivalent) cations has not been studied. However, since deferiprone has the potential to bind polyvalent cations (e.g., iron, aluminum, and zinc), allow at least a 4-hour interval between deferiprone and other medications or dietary supplements containing these polyvalent cations. Such medications can include antacids, iron salts, aluminum hydroxide, dietary supplements containing polyvalent minerals, and zinc salts.

Dichlorphenamide: (Moderate) Use dichlorphenamide and deferiprone together with caution. Metabolic acidosis is associated with the use of dichlorphenamide and has been reported with the postmarketing use of deferiprone. Concurrent use may increase the severity of metabolic acidosis. Measure sodium bicarbonate concentrations at baseline and periodically during dichlorphenamide treatment. If metabolic acidosis occurs or persists, consider reducing the dose or discontinuing dichlorphenamide therapy.

    General Information
    Inform patients that their urine might show a reddish/brown urine discoloration (chromaturia) due to the excretion of the iron-deferiprone complex.     abdominal pain, diarrhea, dyspepsia, enterocolitis, GI bleeding, nausea, pancreatitis, peptic ulcer, vomiting
    General Information
    Gastrointestinal (GI) symptoms were the most frequent adverse reactions reported by patients receiving deferiprone in clinical trials and led to the discontinuation of therapy in 1.6% of patients during thalassemia trials (n = 642). Nausea (7% to 13%), abdominal pain or discomfort (10% to 26%), vomiting (10% to 19%), diarrhea (3% to 5%), and dyspepsia (2%) were the most commonly reported GI adverse events reported during controlled clinical trials (n = 794). Abdominal pain was reported more frequently in pediatric patients compared to adults in clinical trials (n = 86). Rectal GI bleeding, peptic ulcer, enterocolitis, pancreatitis, and parotid gland enlargement have been reported during postmarketing use of deferiprone.

    Headline
    agranulocytosis, infection, neutropenia, pancytopenia, thrombocytosis
    General Information
    Decreased neutrophil count and/or neutropenia was reported in 7% to 8% of adult patients receiving deferiprone in clinical trials. Decreased neutrophil count was reported more frequently in pediatric patients compared to adults in clinical trials (n = 86). Neutropenia may precede agranulocytosis. Agranulocytosis (absolute neutrophil count (ANC) less than 0.2 x 109/L) was reported in 0.5% to 1% of patients taking deferiprone during pooled clinical trials. Although agranulocytosis and neutropenia usually resolve after discontinuation of therapy, fatal cases of agranulocytosis have been reported. The mechanism of deferiprone-induced agranulocytosis is not known. Monitor the ANC prior to initiation of deferiprone and regularly during therapy according to the following schedule: monitor ANC weekly during the first 6 months of therapy, monitor ANC every 2 weeks during the next 6 months of therapy, and monitor ANC every 2 to 4 weeks (or at the patient's blood transfusion interval in patients that have not experienced an interruption due to any decrease in ANC) after 1 year of therapy. Consider a reduction in the frequency of ANC monitoring on an individual patient basis, according to the assessment of the patient's understanding of the risk minimization measures required during therapy. Implement a plan to monitor for and manage agranulocytosis and neutropenia prior to beginning deferiprone therapy and interrupt treatment if neutropenia develops. Advise patients to discontinue deferiprone therapy and immediately contact their physician if they experience any symptoms of infection. If an infection develops, interrupt therapy and monitor the ANC frequently. Thrombocytosis and pancytopenia have also been reported during postmarketing use of deferiprone.

    elevated hepatic enzymes, hepatitis, hepatomegaly, hyperbilirubinemia, jaundice
    General Information
    Elevated hepatic enzymes, reported as increased ALT or AST values, were reported in approximately 8% of patients receiving deferiprone in pooled clinical trials. Four (0.62%) treated subjects discontinued the drug due to increased serum ALT concentrations and 1 (0.16%) due to an increase in both ALT and AST. Jaundice, hyperbilirubinemia, hepatomegaly, and infectious hepatitis were also reported during postmarketing use of deferiprone. Monitor serum transaminase values prior to initiation of deferiprone and monthly during therapy, and consider interruption of therapy if there is a persistent increase in the serum transaminase concentrations.

Description
  Text
  Deferiprone is an iron chelator approved for the treatment of transfusional iron overload due to thalassemia syndromes, sickle cell disease, or other anemias in adults and pediatric patients 3 years and older. Iron overload is a serious complication caused by frequent blood transfusions in patients with blood transfusion-dependent anemias, such as beta thalassemia, sickle cell disease, and other anemias. In patients with chronic iron toxicity, chelation treatments are effective for rapid iron removal, maintenance of low iron stores, prevention of hepatic, cardiac, and other organ damage caused by iron overload, and ultimately improved survival. Deferoxamine, a common treatment, is administered parenterally and compliance with a regimen of prolonged subcutaneous infusions can be challenging. Deferiprone, like deferasirox, is administered orally, and will likely be a more acceptable regimen for chronic use, thus enhancing the management of chronic iron toxicity. Deferiprone has been associated with agranulocytosis, and absolute neutrophil counts (ANC) must be assessed during treatment.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Deferiprone is an orally active chelating agent with an affinity for Fe3+. It binds with ferric ions to form neutral complexes (3:1 deferiprone to iron) that are stable in a wide range of pH values. Deferiprone has a high affinity for iron with a lower affinity for other trivalent metals such as copper, aluminum, and zinc.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Oral Route
    Description
    Three times a day formulations: Deferiprone is rapidly absorbed from the upper gastrointestinal tract, with drug concentrations appearing in the blood within 5 to 10 minutes post-dose. Following a single oral dose, peak serum concentrations (Cmax) are achieved in approximately 1 to 2 hours. In healthy subjects, the mean Cmax and AUC of deferiprone were 20 mcg/mL and 50 mcg x hour/mL, respectively. Administration with food produces no clinically significant differences in the pharmacokinetics of deferiprone.
Twice a day formulation: In fasted subjects, peak serum concentrations (Cmax) are achieved approximately 2 hours after a single dose. Compared with fasted conditions, administration with a high-fat meal does not alter the Cmax and AUC of deferiprone. In healthy subjects receiving a 1,000 mg (twice a day) tablet dose with food, the mean Cmax and AUC of deferiprone were 6 +/- 2 mcg/mL and 28 +/- 7 mcg x hour/mL, respectively. In vitro dissolution studies found that alcohol at concentrations of 40% (V/V) caused an 88% release of deferiprone from the twice a day tablet formulation within 2 hours; there was only a 4% release in the absence of alcohol.

  Text
  Deferiprone is administered orally. Once in the systemic circulation, the apparent mean volume of distribution is 97 +/- 28 L. Deferiprone is primarily metabolized by UGT1A6. The major metabolite is 3-O-glucuronide, which lacks iron-binding capacity. The half-life of deferiprone is approximately 2 hours. The majority (more than 90%) of deferiprone is eliminated from the plasma within 5 to 6 hours, and 75% to 90% is recovered in the urine in the first 24 hours (primarily as the metabolite).
