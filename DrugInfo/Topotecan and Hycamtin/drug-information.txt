Drug Class: Topotecan is a semi-synthetic, water-soluble derivative of camptothecin, a cytotoxic plant alkaloid isolated from the Chinese tree Camptotheca acuminata.

Routes: oral, injection (IV/IM/SC); Common adult doses: Intravenous: 4 mg IV

        Dosage Description
        1.5 mg/m2 IV (maximum dose, 4 mg) once daily over 30 minutes on days 1, 2, 3, 4, and 5, every 21 days until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity. In a multicenter, randomized, phase 3 clinical trial, women with ovarian cancer that progressed during or after platinum-based chemotherapy were randomized to receive either topotecan or paclitaxel 175 mg/m2 IV over 3 hours on day 1 every 21 days. The overall response rate (ORR) was 21% vs. 14% (not significant) in patients treated with topotecan (n = 112) compared with paclitaxel (n = 114), including complete responses in 5% versus 3%, respectively; the median time to response was 7.6 weeks vs. 6 weeks, respectively. Patients who received topotecan also had nonsignificant improvements in duration of response (6 months vs. 5 months), time to progression (4.4 months vs. 3.4 months), and overall survival (14.5 months vs. 12.2 months) compared with paclitaxel. Studies concluded topotecan is active in ovarian patients who had developed resistance to platinum-containing therapy, defined as tumor progression while on or tumor relapse within 6 months after completion of, a platinum-containing regimen.

Following the completion of 4 cycles of cisplatin/etoposide, topotecan was compared to observation in a phase 3 trial in chemotherapy-naive extensive-stage SCLC patients. Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  General Information
  Topotecan is contraindicated for use by patients who have a history of severe hypersensitivity reactions such as anaphylactoid reactions to topotecan or to any of its ingredients. The capsules contain gelatin and may be inappropriate for use by patients with gelatin hypersensitivity. The injection contains mannitol and may be inappropriate for use by patients with mannitol hypersensitivity.
 
Topotecan should be used only by clinicians experienced in myelosuppressive cancer chemotherapy. Patients receiving topotecan should be under close medical supervision in facilities with laboratory and supportive resources sufficient to monitor and treat patients compromised by drug toxicity. Topotecan should not be given to patients with severe bone marrow suppression, including thrombocytopenia or neutropenia. Blood counts should be monitored frequently during treatment. Prior to initiating topotecan therapy, patients should have a baseline neutrophil count > 1500/mm3 and platelet count > 100,000/mm3. Frequent monitoring of peripheral blood counts should be instituted during topotecan treatment. Dosage adjustments are recommended in patients who experience severe neutropenia (absolute neutrophil count (ANC) = 1000/mm3, platelets >= 100,000/mm3, and hemoglobin >= 9 g/dl (with transfusion if necessary). Due to bone marrow suppression, topotecan should be used cautiously in patients who have had previous myelosuppressive therapy such as chemotherapy or radiation therapy. Patients with active infection should be treated prior to receiving topotecan. Patients with a history of varicella zoster, other herpes infection (e.g., herpes simplex), or other viral infection are at risk for reactivation of the infection when treated with chemotherapy.
 
The safety and effectiveness of oral and intravenous topotecan in children have not been established. Severe topotecan-induced myelotoxicity may occur at lower doses in children than in adults.
 
Thrombocytopenia associated with topotecan may be severe; avoid intramuscular injections (IM) in patients with a platelet count 1.5 mg/dl. There are insufficient data available regarding the efficacy of continuing topotecan monotherapy after cisplatin discontinuation in cervical cancer patients. Elderly patients are more likely to have decreased renal function; therefore, care should be taken in dose selection, and it may be useful to monitor renal function during treatment. Also, the elderly may be more likely than younger patients to have oral topotecan-related diarrhea (see Adverse Reactions). A survival benefit was noted among patients at least 65 years of age with thoracic cancer who got oral topotecan and best supportive care as compared with recipients of only best supportive care; Of the 682 patients, 33% were 65 years of age and older, and 4.8% were 75 years of age and older.
 
Cases of interstitial lung disease (ILD), including some fatalities, have been reported with the use of topotecan. Patients with a history of ILD, pulmonary fibrosis, lung cancer, exposure to thoracic radiation therapy, and the use of pneumotoxic drugs and/or colony stimulating factors may have an increased risk. Monitor patients for pulmonary symptomatology suggestive of ILD including cough, fever, dyspnea, and/or hypoxia. Discontinue topotecan in any patients that have a confirmed new diagnosis of ILD.
 
Topotecan is an irritant; avoid extravasation. Should extravasation occur, flushing the site with sterile water and application of cold packs are recommended.
 
Use care to avoid accidental exposure to topotecan during preparation, handling, and administration. The use of protective gowns, gloves, and goggles is recommended. Avoid ocular exposure of topotecan solutions. If exposure occurs, the eye should be rinsed immediately and thoroughly.
 
Vaccination during chemotherapy or radiation therapy should be avoided because the antibody response is suboptimal. When chemotherapy is being planned, vaccination should precede the initiation of chemotherapy by >= 2 weeks. The administration of live vaccines to immunocompromised patients should be avoided. Those undergoing chemotherapy should not be exposed to others who have recently received the oral poliovirus vaccine (OPV). Measles-mumps-rubella (MMR) vaccination is not contraindicated for the close contacts, including health care professionals, of immunocompromised patients. Passive immunoprophylaxis with immune globulins may be indicated for immunocompromised persons instead of, or in addition to, vaccination. When exposed to a vaccine-preventable disease such as measles, severely immunocompromised children should be considered susceptible regardless of their vaccination history.

    Headline
    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components.
    Headline
    bone marrow suppression, infection, neutropenia, thrombocytopenia
    General Information
    Topotecan can cause severe bone marrow suppression. Do not administer the first cycle of topotecan (injection or capsule) to patients with baseline neutropenia (ANC less than 1,500 cells/mm3) or thrombocytopenia (platelet counts less than 100,000 cells/mm3). Frequently monitor blood counts during treatment. An interruption of therapy, dose reduction, or discontinuation of therapy may be necessary if myelosuppression occurs. Due to the risk of myelosuppression, use topotecan with caution in patients with an active infection; myelosuppression which may increase the severity of an infection and impede recovery.
    Headline
    pulmonary disease, pulmonary fibrosis, thoracic radiation therapy
    General Information
    Use topotecan with caution in patients who have a history of pre-existing pulmonary disease; severe pneumonitis/interstitial lung disease has been reported in patients treated with topotecan. Additional risk factors for topotecan-induced pulmonary toxicity include the use of a history of pulmonary fibrosis, pneumotoxic drugs, thoracic radiation therapy, and the use of colony stimulating factors. Monitor patients for new or worsening respiratory symptoms including fever, cough, or dyspnea. Immediately interrupt topotecan therapy in patients with new or worsening respiratory symptoms and permanently discontinue therapy if no other potential causes are identified.
    Headline
    renal failure, renal impairment
    General Information
    Reduce the dose of topotecan for patients with renal impairment (CrCl 20 to 39 mL/min for patients receiving IV topotecan and less than 49 mL/min for patients receiving oral topotecan). Insufficient data are available for patients with renal failure or patients with a CrCl less than 20 mL/min who are receiving IV topotecan.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Topotecan may cause fetal harm when administered during pregnancy, based on its mechanism of action and animal studies. There are no available data on topotecan use during human pregnancy; however, topotecan caused embryolethality, fetotoxicity, and teratogenicity in animal studies when administered at doses similar to the clinical doses during organogenesis. Maternal toxicity, embryolethality, and reduced fetal body weight occurred when topotecan was given to rabbits at doses roughly equivalent to the 1.5 mg/m2 clinical IV dose based on BSA on days 6 to 20 of gestation. In rats, similar exposure for 14 days prior to mating through day 6 of gestation resulted in fetal resorption, microphthalmia, pre-implant loss, and mild maternal toxicity. Increased post-implantation mortality in rats occurred on days 6 to 17 of gestation at about half of the 1.5 mg/m2 IV dose based on BSA, along with increased fetal malformations including microphthalmia, anophthalmia, rosette formation of the retina, coloboma of the retina, ectopic orbit, dilated lateral and third ventricles, and skull and vertebrae malformations.
    Headline
    people who can cause pregnancy in others, people who may become pregnant, reproductive risk
    General Information
    Counsel people who may become pregnant and people who can cause pregnancy in others about the reproductive risk associated with topotecan. Topotecan may cause fetal harm. Verify pregnancy status prior to treatment initiation. Advise people who may become pregnant to use effective contraception while taking topotecan and for 6 months after the last dose. For those that can cause pregnancy in others, contraception should be used during topotecan treatment and for 3 months after the last dose. In addition, based on animal data, topotecan may affect female and male fertility.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Advise patients to avoid breast-feeding while taking topotecan and for 1 week after the last dose. There are no data on the presence of topotecan in human milk, its effects on the breast-fed child, or its effects on milk production. Lactating rats excrete high concentrations of topotecan in milk.

Abrocitinib: (Major) Avoid coadministration of abrocitinib with oral topotecan due to increased topotecan exposure; abrocitinib may be administered with intravenous topotecan. Oral topotecan is a substrate of the P-gp and abrocitinib is a P-gp inhibitor. Coadministration increases the risk of topotecan-related adverse reactions. Oral administration within 4 hours of another P-gp inhibitor increased the dose-normalized AUC of topotecan lactone and total topotecan 2-fold to 3-fold compared to oral topotecan alone.

Adverse Reactions
  Headline
  Adverse Reactions
  General Information
  Bone marrow suppression, consisting of leukopenia, thrombocytopenia, neutropenia, and anemia, is the dose limiting toxicity of topotecan; pancytopenia has been reported. Bone marrow suppression is not cumulative with topotecan treatment. The white blood cell count decreases with increasing topotecan dose or topotecan AUC; a correlation exists between topotecan lactone AUC day 1 and percent decrease of leukocytes. Neutrophil, red blood cell, and platelet nadirs occurred on day 15 (median) for patients with thoracic cancer who got oral topotecan 2.3 mg/m2/day for 5 consecutive days. A neutrophil count <500 cells/mm3 occurred in 32% of patients and was most common during the first treatment course (20% of patients). The median duration of neutropenia was 7 days. Infection, sepsis, and febrile neutropenia occurred in 17%, 2%, and 4% of patients, respectively. Death due to sepsis occurred in 1% of patients. A platelet count <10,000 cells/mm3 occurred in 6% of patients with a median duration of 3 days, and a hemoglobin concentration <8 g/dl occurred in 25% of patients. Leukopenia, thrombocytopenia, and anemia are also common with intravenous topotecan. During single-agent IV topotecan clinical trials for ovarian cancer, neutropenia (< 1500 cells/mm3) occurred in 97% of patients and severe neutropenia (< 500 cells/mm3) occurred in 78%. Grade 4 neutropenia (< 500 cells/mm3) was most common during the first course of treatment (60% of ovarian and lung cancer patients) and occurred in 39% of all courses. The median duration of neutropenia is 7 days and the nadir occurred at a median of 12 days. Sepsis or fever/infection (any severity) occurred in 43% of ovarian cancer patients; grade 4 topotecan-related sepsis or febrile neutropenia occurred in 23% of ovarian and lung cancer patients, and sepsis was fatal in 1% of these patients. In cervical cancer patients treated with topotecan and cisplatin, neutropenia of any grade occurred in 89% of patients; grade 3 and grade 4 neutropenia occurred in 26% and 48% of patients in clinical trials, respectively. Platelet counts < 75,000/mm3 occurred in 69% of ovarian cancer patients treated with topotecan; grade 4 thrombocytopenia (< 25,000/mm3) occurred 27% of lung and ovarian cancer patients. Median duration of grade 4 thrombocytopenia is 5 days and the platelet nadir occurred at a median of 15 days. Platelet transfusions were given to 15% of ovarian and lung cancer patients in 4% of courses during topotecan clinical trials. During cervical cancer clinical trials, grade 3 and 4 thrombocytopenia was reported in 26% and 7% of patients, respectively. Hemoglobin concentrations < 8 g/dl were reported in 37% of lung and ovarian cancer patients and in 14% of courses during topotecan clinical trials. Red blood cell transfusions were required in 52% of ovarian and lung cancer patients. During cervical cancer trials, anemia was reported in 94% of patients; grade 3 and 4 anemia was reported in 34% and 6% of patients treated with topotecan and cisplatin, respectively. Rarely, severe bleeding has been reported in association with thrombocytopenia. During cervical cancer trials, coagulation abnormalities were reported in 6% and hemorrhage was reported in 15% of patients; there was one death due to hemorrhage aggravated by topotecan-induced thrombocytopenia. A phase III study evaluated the use of amifostine in combination with topotecan in 30 patients with advanced gynecologic malignancies. Patients were randomized to receive either topotecan alone or in combination with amifostine. The rate of grade 3/4 neutropenia was 67% in those patients who received topotecan alone and 38% in those who received topotecan in combination with amifostine (p=0.003). Dosage adjustments are recommended for patients who experience severe and/or prolonged bone marrow suppression (see Dosage). Inform patients about blood count reductions and the importance of frequent blood tests, and ask patients to promptly report the development of fever or other signs of infection such as chills, cough, or burning pain on urination.
 
Gastrointestinal complaints in ovarian and lung cancer patients treated with IV topotecan include nausea/vomiting (64%/45%), diarrhea (32%), constipation (29%), abdominal pain (22%), anorexia (19%), and stomatitis (18%). Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, and anorexia are also side effects of oral topotecan; do not give an oral replacement dose if the patient vomits. Nausea and vomiting are usually mild to moderate and do not require routine prophylactic therapy. Diarrhea that may require hospitalization because of its severity has been reported during treatment with oral topotecan. The median time to onset of Grade 2 or worse diarrhea was 9 days. Diarrhea can occur at the same time as drug-related neutropenia and its sequelae; the incidence of Grade 3 or 4 diarrhea within 5 days of Grade 3 or 4 neutropenia events was 5%. Inform patients about the possibility of diarrhea, and ask patients to promptly report the development of diarrhea. Aggressive management of diarrhea is recommended; oral topotecan dosage reduction may be needed (see Dosage). The overall incidence of drug-related diarrhea was 22% among the 682 patients with thoracic cancer who received oral topotecan; 4% of patients had Grade 3 diarrhea, and 0.4% had Grade 4 diarrhea. Drug-related diarrhea was more frequent in patients 65 years of age or older (28%) as compared with younger patients (19%). Transient elevated hepatic enzymes were observed in 8% of lung and ovarian cancer patients; grade 3/4 elevations occurred in 4%. Grade 3/4 hyperbilirubinemia, including jaundice, occurred in < 2% of patients. In cervical cancer trials of topotecan and cisplatin, nausea/vomiting (55%/40%) and stomatitis-pharyngitis (6%) were reported. Topotecan-induced neutropenia can lead to neutropenic colitis, which may be fatal. Consider the possibility of neutropenic colitis if a patient has fever, neutropenia, and a compatible pattern of abdominal pain.
 
Additional non-hematologic complaints in ovarian and lung cancer patients treated with IV topotecan include alopecia (49%), fatigue (29%), fever (28%), asthenia or weakness (25%), pain including back pain and musculoskeletal pain (23%), dyspnea (22%), headache (18%), cough (15%), paresthesias usually grade 1 (9%), GI obstruction (4.5%), arthralgia (1%), and malaise (1%). Constitutional symptoms including fatigue (lethargy, malaise, asthenia), fever (in the absence of neutropenia), rigors, chills, weight loss, and weight gain were reported in 69% of cervical cancer patients during clinical trials. Of cervical cancer patients treated topotecan and cisplatin, 59% reported pain symptoms including abdominal pain or cramping, arthralgia, bone pain, chest pain (unspecified), dysmenorrhea, dyspareunia, earache, headache, hepatic pain, myalgia, neuropathic pain, pain due to radiation, pelvic pain, pleuritic pain, rectal or perirectal pain, and tumor pain. Total alopecia occurred in 31% of ovarian and lung cancer patients. Skin rash (unspecified) occurred in 16% of patients participating in ovarian and lung cancer trials. Rash symptoms include pruritus, urticaria, dermatitis, bullous rash/eruption, and maculopapular rash. Allergic reactions to topotecan have been reported infrequently, and rarely, anaphylactoid reactions, angioedema, severe dermatitis, and severe pruritus have been observed in clinical practice. Inadvertent extravasation of topotecan has been associated with mild local reactions including erythema and ecchymosis (bruising). The most common grade 3/4 non-hematologic adverse events associated with topotecan administration include the following: nausea/vomiting (8%), fatigue (5%), and dyspnea (8%). Alopecia, fatigue, and asthenia are also side effects of oral topotecan.
 
Cases of interstitial lung disease (ILD), including some fatalities, have been reported with the use of topotecan. Underlying risk factors include a history of ILD, pulmonary fibrosis, lung cancer, thoracic exposure to radiation, and the use of pneumotoxic drugs and/or colony stimulating factors. Monitor patients for symptomatology suggestive of ILD including cough, fever, dyspnea, and/or hypoxia. Discontinue topotecan if a new diagnosis of ILD is confirmed.
 
Topotecan may cause infertility in both men and women. In animal studies, doses about three-fifths of a human clinical dose on a mg/m2 basis caused superovulation possibly related to inhibition of follicular atresia. Also, in animal studies, doses about one-sixth of a human clinical dose on a mg/m2 basis may have caused an increase in the incidence of multinucleated spermatogonial giant cells in the testes.

Description
  Text
  Topotecan is a semi-synthetic, water-soluble derivative of camptothecin, a cytotoxic plant alkaloid isolated from the Chinese tree Camptotheca acuminata. It is a topoisomerase I inhibitor that is FDA approved in capsule form as monotherapy for the treatment of small cell lung cancer (SCLC), and as an intravenous therapy for the treatment of ovarian cancer, cervical cancer, and SCLC. Topotecan can cause severe myelosuppression; closely monitor complete blood counts (CBC). An interruption of therapy or dose adjustment may be necessary for patients with neutropenia, thrombocytopenia, or anemia.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  All camptothecin analogs, including topotecan, work by inhibiting topoisomerase I, a cellular enzyme involved in maintaining the topographic structure of DNA during translation, transcription, and mitosis. Topoisomerase I relieves the torsional strain in the DNA helix during replication and RNA transcription by inducing single-strand breaks. By binding with the topoisomerase I/DNA complex, topotecan prevents the relegation of the single-strand breaks. However, this activity is not sufficient to cause cytotoxicity because the complexes are easily reversible once the drug is removed. The cytotoxicity of topotecan is thought to be due to double-strand DNA damage produced during DNA synthesis when replication enzymes interact with the ternary complex formed by topotecan, topoisomerase I, and DNA. As a result of this complex formation, both the initial cleavage reaction and relegation steps are inhibited. Irreversible DNA damage occurs when a DNA replication fork encounters the topotecan-topoisomerase I complex resulting in double-strand DNA breaks (Replication Collision Model). Camptothecins are highly S-phase specific in their activity due to the requirement of DNA synthesis. Inhibitors of DNA synthesis, such as hydroxyurea, can protect cells from camptothecin-induced cytotoxicity. As with other agents that damage DNA, camptothecins cause cycle arrest in the G2-phase; however, the actual mechanisms of cell death following camptothecin administration has not been determined.
 
The 'Transcription Collision Model' has been proposed to explain S-phase-independent cellular activity of camptothecins. In this model, the collision between topoisomerase I-cleavage complexes found on the template strand and elongating RNA polymerase results in transcription arrest and conversion of topoisomerase I cleavage complexes into irreversible single strand breaks. Ultimately, this cascade results in the termination of RNA transcription at the arrested site, causes double-strand breakage, and cell death. Additional cellular effects exerted by camptothecins include inhibition of RNA synthesis, multi-ubiquitination and degradation of topoisomerase I, chromatin reorganization, and activation of signal transduction molecules. Other effects of camptothecins include radiation sensitization and antiviral effects. Topoisomerase I inhibitors appear to inhibit initial DNA repairs including sealing of broken strands and repair of base damage following radiation therapy. Camptothecins have been shown to decrease HIV-1 replication but the mechanism has not been established. Camptothecins can cause damage at the chromosomal level. However, little is known about the possible long-term toxicities of camptothecins. Other DNA-damaging agents such as alkylating agents and topoisomerase II inhibitors have been associated with mutagenicity and secondary malignancies.
 
Exposure to topoisomerase I and II inhibitors results in synergistic antitumor effects when administered sequentially. This is secondary to the upregulation of topoisomerase levels and by increasing the topoisomerase II mRNA expression after 24 to 48 hours. These effects have been exemplified utilizing mice tumor-xenografts.
 
Mechanisms of resistance to topotecan include inadequate accumulation of drug in the tumor secondary to overexpression of MDR-1, P-glycoprotein, the breast cancer resistance protein (BCRP), and/or other transporters which result in increased activity of efflux mechanisms. Point mutations of topoisomerase I confer resistance to topoisomerase inhibitors via alternations of the minor and major grooves of DNA which results in instability of the topotecan-topoisomerase I-DNA ternary complex. Loss of topoisomerase I nucleolar localization may result in a decrease in the overall amount of topoisomerase I associated with DNA. Overexpression of the X-ray repair cross-complementing gene I protein (XRCC) leads to topotecan resistance in cells. XRCC protein is involved with base-excision repair mechanisms. Overexpression bcl-2 has been associated with relative resistance to topotecan. The clinical implications of these resistance mechanisms are under investigation.

  Text
  Topotecan is administered orally and intravenously, with approximately 35% bound to plasma proteins. The volume of distribution is approximately 75 liters/m2, indicating that the lactone form is extensively bound to tissue components; topotecan does penetrate the blood-brain barrier and has been measured in the cerebrospinal fluid. Plasma protein binding is about 7% to 35%. Based on a small study (n = 4), the presence or absence of pleural or ascitic fluids does not affect topotecan clearance, terminal disposition half-lives, or AUC ratios of lactone to total drug in plasma. It undergoes reversible pH-dependent hydrolysis of its lactone moiety, with an equilibrium existing between the lactone and ring-opened hydroxy-acid (carboxylate) forms. At pH of 4 or less, the pharmacologically active lactone form is exclusively present, whereas the ring-opened hydroxy-acid form predominates at physiologic pH. In vitro studies indicate topotecan is metabolized to an N-demethylated metabolite. The mean metabolite: parent AUC ratio was less than 10% for total topotecan and topotecan lactone following oral administration and approximately 3% after IV administration.
 
Overall recovery of drug-related material after 5 daily doses of topotecan was 57% after oral administration and 73.4% (+/- 2.3%) after IV administration. A mean value of 20% of the oral dose and 51% (+/- 3%) of the IV dose was recovered in the urine as total topotecan; 2% and 3% (+/- 1%), respectively, were recovered in the urine as N-desmethyl topotecan. Fecal elimination of total topotecan accounted for 33% of the dose after oral administration, and 18% (+/- 4%) after IV administration; 1.5% and 1.7% (+/- 0.6%), respectively, were excreted in the feces as N-desmethyl topotecan. An O-glucuronide metabolite of both topotecan and N-desmethyl topotecan have been identified in the urine. Overall, the N-desmethyl metabolite contributed a mean of less than 6% (range, 4% to 8%) of the total drug-related material accounted for in the urine and feces.
 
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes and drug transporters: BCRP, P-glycoprotein (P-gp)
Topotecan is a substrate of BCRP and P-gp. In vitro inhibition studies indicate that topotecan is not affected by the activity of CYP1A2, CYP2A6, CYP2C8/9, CYP2C19, CYP2D6, CYP2E, CYP3A, CYP4A, or dihydropyrimidine dehydrogenase.

Dosage Adjustment Guidelines
  Hepatic Impairment
  Specific guidelines for dosage adjustments in hepatic impairment are not available; it appears that no dosage adjustments are needed.
  Renal Impairment
  The threshold creatinine clearance rate for reduced oral or intravenous doses is different.
For the oral formulation:
CrCL 50 to 80 mL/min: No dosage adjustment needed.
CrCL 30 to 49 mL/min: Reduce the dose of topotecan to 1.5 mg/m2 PO once daily.
CrCL less than 30 mL/min: Reduce the dose of topotecan to 0.6 mg/m2 PO once daily.
 
For the intravenous formulation:
Monotherapy, CrCL 40 to 60 mL/min: No dosage adjustment needed.
Monotherapy, CrCL 20 to 39 mL/min: Reduce the dose of topotecan to 0.75 mg/m2 IV once daily.
Monotherapy, CrCL less than 20 mL/min: Insufficient data are available to provide a dosage recommendation.
 
Intermittent hemodialysis
In a case report, a patient underwent a 4-hour hemodialysis (HD) procedure using a Fresenius HD machine which utilized a Terumo 220 cuperammonium Rayon H dialysis filter. Approximately 60% of the total drug amount was removed by HD.
