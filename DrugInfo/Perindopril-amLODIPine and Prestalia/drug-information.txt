Drug Class: Amlodipine and perindopril are used together in an oral preparation to treat hypertension.

Routes: oral; Common adult doses: 14 mg/day PO perindopril; 10 mg/day PO amlodipine.

    General Information
    Worsening angina and acute myocardial infarction can develop after starting or increasing the dose of amlodipine, particularly in individuals with severe obstructive coronary artery disease.
    Headline
    ACE inhibitor induced cough, angioedema, Black patients, females, Hispanic patients, tobacco smoking
    General Information
    Perindopril; amlodipine is contraindicated in individuals with hereditary or idiopathic angioedema or a history of angioedema related to previous angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE) inhibitor treatment. Individuals with a history of angioedema unrelated to ACE inhibitors may be at increased risk for angioedema while receiving an ACE inhibitor. Other risk factors for ACE inhibitor-induced angioedema may include a history of ACE inhibitor induced cough, females, tobacco smoking, and those older than 65 years. ACE inhibitors have also been associated with a higher rate of angioedema in Black patients and Hispanic patients. In addition, ACE inhibitors are less effective in lowering blood pressure in Black patients. Hence, a higher initial dose for the ACE inhibitor or alternative initial therapy with a thiazide diuretic or long-acting calcium channel blocker may be considered in Black patients with hypertension.
    Headline
    hymenoptera venom (insect sting) allergy desensitization
    General Information
    Treatment with angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE) inhibitors may increase the risk of anaphylactoid reactions in individuals undergoing hymenoptera venom (insect sting) allergy desensitization. Life-threatening anaphylactoid reactions occurred in 2 individuals undergoing desensitizing treatment with hymenoptera venom while receiving ACE inhibitors. These reactions abated when ACE inhibitors were temporarily withheld, but reappeared upon rechallenge. However, a retrospective analysis of 79 subjects who underwent hymenoptera venom (insect sting) allergy desensitization did not show an association between ACE inhibitor therapy and increased frequency of systemic reactions to venom immunotherapy.
    Headline
    acute myocardial infarction, cerebrovascular disease, coronary artery disease, hypertrophic obstructive cardiomyopathy, hyponatremia, hypovolemia, severe aortic stenosis
    General Information
    Angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE) inhibitors can cause symptomatic hypotension that may be complicated by oliguria, progressive azotemia, and rarely acute renal failure, or death. Risk factors for the development of excessive hypotension include heart failure with systolic blood pressure below 100 mmHg, coronary artery disease, cerebrovascular disease, hyponatremia, high dose diuretic therapy, dialysis, or severe hypovolemia and/or salt depletion of any etiology. Avoid use of ACE inhibitors in individuals who are hemodynamically unstable after an acute myocardial infarction. Symptomatic hypotension is also possible with amlodipine, particularly in individuals with severe aortic stenosis. Hypotension may worsen symptoms or cause complications in individuals with severe aortic stenosis or hypertrophic obstructive cardiomyopathy. Monitor individuals closely during the first 2 weeks of ACE inhibitor therapy and whenever the dose of ACE inhibitor (and/or diuretic) is increased.
    Headline
    autoimmune disease, collagen-vascular disease, diabetes mellitus, low-density lipoprotein apheresis, renal disease
    General Information
    Angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE) inhibitors can cause hyperkalemia. Risk factors for the development of hyperkalemia include renal insufficiency, diabetes mellitus, and concomitant use of potassium-sparing diuretics, potassium supplements, and/or potassium-containing salt substitutes. Individuals whose renal function may depend on the activity of the renin-angiotensin system (e.g., individuals with renal artery stenosis, chronic renal disease, severe heart failure, post-myocardial infarction, or hypovolemia) may be at risk for developing acute renal failure on ACE inhibitor therapy. Consider withholding or discontinuing ACE inhibitor therapy in individuals who develop a clinically significant decrease in renal function while receiving an ACE inhibitor. Monitor renal function and potassium periodically in individuals receiving ACE inhibitor therapy. Dosage adjustment of perindopril; amlodipine may be necessary in individuals with renal impairment. Anaphylactoid reactions have been reported in individuals taking angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE) inhibitors who are receiving dialysis with high-flux membranes (e.g., AN69). Consider an alternative dialysis membrane or an alternative medication in individuals with a history of reaction. Dialysis is also a risk factor for excessive hypotension with ACE inhibitor therapy. Anaphylactoid reactions have also been reported in individuals receiving ACE inhibitor therapy who are undergoing low-density lipoprotein apheresis with dextran sulfate adsorption. Additionally, ACE inhibitors may cause agranulocytosis and/or neutropenia, especially in individuals with renal failure or autoimmune disease, including collagen-vascular disease. Frequent hematologic monitoring is recommended in individuals with renal insufficiency or autoimmune disease who are receiving ACE inhibitors.

    Perindopril; amlodipine may block angiotensin II formation secondary to compensatory renin release in individuals undergoing major surgery or during anesthesia with agents that produce hypotension. If hypotension occurs, correct by volume expansion.

    hepatic failure
    General Information
    Titrate the perindopril; amlodipine dose slowly in individuals with severe hepatic failure. Amlodipine is extensively metabolized by the liver and the elimination half-life is prolonged in individuals with impaired hepatic function.
    Headline
    children, infants, neonates
    General Information
    It is not known whether post-natal use of angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors in neonates, infants, and children younger than 2 years, before maturation of kidney function is complete, has a long-term deleterious effect on the kidney. Nephrogenesis is thought to be complete around birth; however, maturation of other aspects of kidney function, such as glomerular filtration and tubular function, may continue until approximately 2 years of age.
    Headline
    geriatric
    General Information
    Monitor blood pressure for up to 2 weeks after titrations at doses above 7 mg perindopril; 5 mg amlodipine in geriatric adults; experience with doses exceeding 7 mg perindopril; 5 mg amlodipine is limited in geriatric adults. The mean blood pressure effect of perindopril was somewhat smaller in adults older than 60 years compared to younger adults, although the difference was not significant. Dizziness and rash have occurred with perindopril at increased rates in older adults compared to younger adults. Plasma concentrations of both perindopril and perindoprilat were increased in older adults compared to concentrations in younger subjects. Older adults have decreased clearance of amlodipine with a resulting increase in overall amlodipine exposure.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Once pregnancy is detected, discontinue perindopril; amlodipine as soon as possible. Use of medications that affect the renin-angiotensin system (RAS), such as ACE inhibitors and angiotensin II receptor antagonists, are generally not recommended for use during pregnancy. Use of RAS inhibitors during the second and third trimesters of pregnancy is associated with reduced fetal renal function and increased risks of fetal and neonatal morbidity and mortality. Exposure to these agents in pregnancy may result in adverse outcomes including fetal death, hypotension, neonatal skull hypoplasia, craniofacial abnormalities, fetal limb contractures, hypoplastic lung development, anuria, fetal growth restriction, and both reversible and irreversible renal failure. Additionally, cases of oligohydramnios and anhydramnios have been reported. Development of oligohydramnios may be associated with fetal lung hypoplasia and skeletal deformations. Use of RAS inhibitors during the first trimester of pregnancy may increase the risk of fetal harm, such as congenital malformations; however, available data are conflicting. Data with amlodipine use in pregnancy are insufficient to inform a drug-associated risk for major birth defects and miscarriage. Data from animal reproductive studies indicate no evidence of adverse developmental effects when pregnant rats and rabbits received oral amlodipine during organogenesis at doses approximately 10- and 20-times the maximum recommended human dose, respectively. Litter size for rats was decreased by about 50%, and the number of intrauterine deaths was increased by approximately 5-fold. Amlodipine has been shown to prolong the gestation period and duration of labor in rats at this dose. In rare cases when another antihypertensive agent cannot be used to treat a pregnant patient, serial ultrasound examinations should be performed to assess the intra-amniotic environment. If oligohydramnios is observed, discontinue perindopril; amlodipine unless it is considered life-saving for the pregnant individual. Oligohydramnios may not appear until after the fetus has sustained irreversible injury.

    neonates and infants exposed to this medication in utero
    General Information
    Closely observe neonates and infants exposed to this medication in utero for hypotension, oliguria, and hyperkalemia. If oliguria or hypotension occurs, blood pressure and renal perfusion support may be required, as well as exchange transfusion or dialysis to reverse hypotension and/or support decreased renal function.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Use perindopril; amlodipine with caution in breast-feeding patients. Perindopril and amlodipine are present in breast milk in small amounts. Due to limited infant exposure, neither agent is expected to cause adverse effects in the breastfed child. However, alternatives such as benazepril, captopril, enalapril, or nifedipine may be preferred. In a study of 31 lactating patients with pregnancy-induced hypertension taking amlodipine, the median relative infant dose (RID) was 4.2% (interquartile range, 3.12% to 7.25%) and the maximum RID was 15.2%. No adverse effects of amlodipine on breast-fed children have been observed. In another study that enrolled 8 lactating patients, the average RID for amlodipine was 3.4% (range 1.56% to 4.32%). In a study of 10 lactating patients taking perindopril the RID was 0.005% to 0.2% for perindopril and 0.03% to 4.6% for the active metabolite perindoprilat. Ten infants were also breast-fed with no effects on growth or development.

    teratogenesis
    General Information
    Due to the potential for teratogenesis, perindopril; amlodipine should not be used during pregnancy. ACE inhibitors have been associated with fetal and neonatal abnormalities when administered to women during the second or third trimesters of pregnancy. Adverse fetal and neonatal effects have included hypotension, neonatal skull hypoplasia, anuria, renal failure, oligohydramnios, and death. Oligohydramnios has been associated with fetal lung hypoplasia and skeletal deformations. If oliguria or hypotension occurs in a neonate with a history of in utero exposure to perindopril; amlodipine, blood pressure and renal perfusion support may be required, as well as exchange transfusion or dialysis to reverse hypotension and/or support decreased renal function. Perindopril, which crosses the placenta, can theoretically be removed from the neonatal circulation by these means, but limited experience has not shown that such removal is central to the treatment of these infants.

    Headline
    anaphylactoid reactions, angioedema
    General Information
    Angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE) inhibitors have been associated with anaphylactoid reactions, likely due to their inhibitory effect on eicosanoid and polypeptide metabolism, including bradykinin metabolism. Angioedema of the face, extremities, lips, tongue, glottis, and larynx has been reported in individuals treated with ACE inhibitors; angioedema was reported in 0.1% of perindopril-treated subjects in clinical trials. If angioedema occurs, discontinue perindopril immediately. Appropriate monitoring and therapy should be provided until signs and symptoms have resolved completely. Involvement of the upper respiratory tract can induce acute respiratory distress and be fatal. If there is involvement of the tongue, glottis or larynx, appropriate therapy (e.g., 0.3 to 0.5 mL subcutaneous epinephrine 1 mg/mL) and/or measures to ensure a patent airway should be promptly provided. Even in those instances where swelling of only the tongue is involved, without respiratory distress, individuals may require prolonged observation since treatment with antihistamines and corticosteroids may not be sufficient. The onset of angioedema usually occurs within hours or at most 1 week after starting ACE inhibitor therapy, but may occur at any time during therapy. The mechanism is unknown but may involve drug-induced auto-antibodies, bradykinin accumulation, dysregulation of the complement system, or histamine. Intestinal angioedema has been reported with ACE inhibitors. Individuals with intestinal angioedema presented with abdominal pain (with or without nausea or vomiting). In some cases, there was no prior history of facial angioedema and C1 esterase concentrations were normal. Intestinal angioedema was diagnosed by procedures including abdominal CT scan or ultrasound, or at surgery. Symptoms resolved after stopping the ACE inhibitor. Treatment with ACE inhibitors may increase the risk of anaphylactoid reactions in individuals undergoing hymenoptera venom (insect sting) allergy desensitization. Life-threatening anaphylactoid reactions occurred in 2 individuals undergoing desensitizing treatment with hymenoptera venom while receiving ACE inhibitor therapy. These reactions abated when ACE inhibitors were temporarily withheld, but reappeared upon rechallenge. However, a retrospective analysis of 79 subjects who underwent hymenoptera venom (insect sting) allergy desensitization did not show an association between ACE inhibitor therapy and increased frequency of systemic reactions to venom immunotherapy. Of 17 subjects taking an ACE inhibitor while undergoing desensitization, none experienced a systemic reaction to venom immunotherapy; comparatively, 13 of 62 subjects not taking an ACE inhibitor experienced a systemic reaction during venom immunotherapy.

Description
  Text
  Amlodipine and perindopril are used together in an oral preparation to treat hypertension. Amlodipine is a dihydropyridine calcium channel blocker and is a potent peripheral vasodilator, similar to nifedipine and other members of the dihydropyridine class. Amlodipine has the longest half-life of the group, thus allowing for once-daily dosing. Perindopril is an angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE) inhibitor. Perindopril is a prodrug that is hydrolyzed to perindoprilat. Perindopril lacks a sulfhydryl group, which has implications for the safety profile. Amlodipine; perindopril was approved by the FDA in January 2015

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Perindopril; amlodipine combines two antihypertensive agents with different mechanisms to lower blood pressure.
 
Perindopril: Perindoprilat, the active moiety of perindopril, competes with angiotensin I for its binding site on the angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE). As a result, the drug blocks the conversion of angiotensin I to angiotensin II. Angiotensin II is a potent vasoconstrictor and a negative feedback mediator for renin activity. Thus, when perindoprilat lowers angiotensin II plasma levels, blood pressure decreases and plasma renin activity increases. In addition, baroreceptor reflex mechanisms are stimulated in response to the fall in blood pressure. Perindoprilat inhibits both ACE and kininase II. Kininase II, identical to ACE, is an enzyme that degrades bradykinin, a potent vasodilator, to inactive peptides. Whether increased bradykinin levels play a part in the therapeutic effects of ACE inhibitors is presently unclear. Bradykinin-induced vasodilation is thought to be of secondary importance in the blood-pressure lowering effect of ACE inhibitors. A bradykinin mechanism may, however, contribute to ACE-inhibitor-induced angioneurotic edema and cough. ACE-inhibiting drugs can act locally to reduce vascular tone by decreasing local angiotensin II-induced sympathetic and/or vasoconstrictive activity. ACE inhibitors also can inhibit presynaptic norepinephrine release and postsynaptic adrenergic receptor activity, thus decreasing vascular sensitivity to vasopressor activity; however, this action may not be clinically evident at usual doses. Decreases in plasma angiotensin II levels also result in a reduction in aldosterone secretion, with a subsequent decrease in sodium and water retention. ACE inhibitors reduce LVH. Perindoprilat causes arterial dilation, thereby lowering total peripheral vascular resistance. In hypertensive patients, blood pressure is decreased with little or no change in heart rate, stroke volume, or cardiac output.
 
Amlodipine: Amlodipine inhibits the influx of extracellular calcium across the myocardial and vascular smooth muscle cell membranes. The resultant decrease in intracellular calcium (serum calcium levels remain unchanged) inhibits the contractile processes of the myocardial smooth muscle cells, resulting in dilation of the coronary and systemic arteries. As with other calcium-channel blockers of the dihydropyridine class, amlodipine exerts its effects mainly on arteriolar vasculature. It has no significant effect on sinus node function or cardiac conduction, nor does it possess negative inotropic effects at clinical doses. Because it has a gradual onset, reflex tachycardia does not occur, a side effect that is common with other peripheral vasodilators. Amlodipine therapy usually does not affect hemodynamic parameters in patients with normal ventricular function.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Oral Route
    Description
    Perindopril: After oral administration, perindopril is rapidly absorbed with a bioavailability of 75%. Peak perindopril plasma concentrations occur in approximately 1 hour. Perindoprilat has a mean overall systemic bioavailability of about 25%. Peak perindoprilat concentrations occur 3 to 7 hours after oral perindopril administration. Administration with food does not alter absorption.
 
Amlodipine: Amlodipine is slowly but almost completely absorbed. Oral bioavailability ranges from 64% to 90%. Peak plasma concentrations are achieved between 6 to 12 hours post-dose, and maximum hypotensive effects are correspondingly delayed. Food does influence these parameters.
  Special Populations
    Name
    Hepatic Impairment
    Description
    Perindopril: Perindopril plasma concentrations are increased in patients with hepatic disease. In patients with hepatic impairment, the perindoprilat plasma concentrations are about 50% higher than that seen in healthy subjects or hypertensive patients with normal liver function. Perindopril; amlodipine is not recommended for use in patients with hepatic impairment.
 
Amlodipine: Amlodipine is extensively metabolized by the liver; clearance is decreased in patients with hepatic impairment. In these patients, the plasma elimination half-life is prolonged (56 hours) and the AUC is increased by 40 to 60%.  Perindopril; amlodipine is not recommended for use in patients with hepatic impairment.
    Name
    Renal Impairment
    Description
    Perindopril: Perindopril plasma concentrations are increased in patients with renal impairment. In renal impairment, the AUC of perindoprilat at creatinine clearances of 30 to 80 mL/min is two-fold that observed at a creatinine clearance of approximately 100 mL/min. The AUC of perindoprilat increases markedly when the creatinine clearance drops below 30 mL/min. Perindopril and perindoprilat are significantly removed by hemodialysis. Perindopril; amlodipine is not recommended for use in patients with CrCl < 30 ml/min.
 
Amlodipine: The pharmacokinetics of amlodipine are not affected by renal impairment. Perindopril; amlodipine is not recommended for use in patients with CrCl < 30 ml/min.
    Name
    Geriatric
    Description
    Perindopril: Perindopril and perindoprilat plasma concentrations in elderly patients are approximately twice that observed in younger patients. Elderly patients have decreased renal perindoprilat excretion and increased conversion of perindopril to perindoprilat.
 
Amlodipine: The elimination half-life of single 5 mg oral doses is significantly prolonged (48 vs. 30 to 50 hours) in the elderly. Amlodipine clearance is reduced and AUC is increased by approximately 40 to 60%.
  Text
  Perindopril; amlodipine is administered orally.
Perindopril: Perindopril is a prodrug that is extensively metabolized. Perindopril is 60% bound to plasma proteins; perindoprilat is 10% to 20% protein-bound. Approximately 30% to 50% of systemically available perindopril is hydrolyzed to the active agent perindoprilat by hepatic esterase. Five other inactive metabolites of perindopril have been identified (perindopril and perindoprilat glucuronides, dehydrated perindopril, and the diastereoisomers of dehydrated perindoprilat). Perindoprilat and its metabolites are eliminated primarily by the renal route. Only 4% to 12% of the dose recovered unchanged in the urine. The ranges for elimination half-lives of perindopril and perindoprilat are 0.8 to 1.3 and 3 to 10 hours, respectively.
Amlodipine: Amlodipine is approximately 93% bound to plasma proteins. Like other calcium-channel blockers, amlodipine is primarily metabolized by CYP3A4 isoenzymes. It is extensively metabolized to inactive compounds, and 10% of the parent compound and 60% of the inactive metabolites are excreted in the urine. The terminal half-life is about 30 to 50 hours, which is significantly longer than the other currently available dihydropyridines. Steady-state plasma levels are reached after 7 to 8 days of consecutive daily dosing.
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes and drug transporters: CYP3A4
Amlodipine is a CYP3A4 substrate, and its metabolism may theoretically be affected by CYP3A4 inhibitors or inducers. Coadministration with diltiazem, a moderate CYP3A inhibitor, increases exposure to amlodipine by 60%. However, coadministration of erythromycin, also a moderate CYP3A inhibitor, does not significantly change the systemic exposure to amlodipine.
