    General Information
    Safety data from clinical trials found 16% of patients experienced ocular pain following instillation of the cenegermin ophthalmic drops. Ocular pain may also arise as corneal healing occurs. Other reactions occurring in 1% to 10% of drug recipients, included corneal deposits, foreign body sensation, ocular hyperemia, ocular inflammation, photophobia, and tearing. Description
  Text
  Cenegermin is a recombinant human nerve growth factor approved for the treatment of neurotrophic keratitis (a rare degenerative corneal condition resulting from damage to the trigeminal nerve). The drug is formulated as an ophthalmic solution; and must be dispensed weekly as a kit containing: a weekly carton with 7 multiple-dose vials in an insulated pack; 8 vial adapters; 45 pipettes; 45 sterile disinfectant wipes; a dosing card. Cenegermin is administered 6 times day (at 2-hour intervals). Contact lenses should be removed before each administration, and reinserted no sooner than 15 minutes after the dose. At least 15 minutes should separate administration of cenegermin and other topically applied ophthalmic products; cenegermin should be given first if the concurrently administered ophthalmic product is an ointment, gel, or viscous drop. The most frequently reported adverse event associated with the drugs use is ocular pain.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Cenegermin is a recombinant nerve growth factor that binds to specific high-affinity (i.e., TrkA) and low-affinity (i.e., p75NTR) receptors in the anterior segment of the eye to support corneal innervation and integrity in patients with neurotrophic keratitis. Neurotrophic keratitis is a rare degenerative corneal condition that develops in patients who have sustained damage to the trigeminal nerve (via herpetic infection, ocular or neurologic surgeries, chemical burns, abuse of anesthetics, diabetes mellitus, or multiple sclerosis). Once damaged, the nerve's ability to maintain corneal homeostasis is impaired; thereby resulting in decreased corneal sensitivity, impaired tearing reflexes, alterations in the metabolism and vitality of the corneal epithelium, and reduced epithelial healing. Treatment with recombinant growth factor restores the function of the injured neurons, and subsequently, corneal homeostasis.

The extent of systemic exposure to cenegermin following ophthalmic administration is unknown. During clinical trials, nerve growth hormone (NGF) concentrations were undetectable in most drug recipients; only 5 out of 52 treated patients had NGF concentrations that were higher than the lower limit of quantification (i.e., 32,000 pg/mL). Administration
  General Information

The product is to be dispensed weekly as a kit containing: a weekly carton with 7 multiple-dose vials in an insulated pack; 8 vial adapters; 45 pipettes; 45 sterile disinfectant wipes; a dosing card.
  Route Specific Administrations
    Name
    Ophthalmic Administration
    Description
    Preparation for administration:
Within 5 hours of receipt, remove the frozen weekly carton from the insulated container and store the carton in the refrigerator between 2 to 8 degrees C (36 to 46 degrees F) for up to 14 days. If treatment is started immediately after receiving the frozen weekly carton, remove 1 vial from the carton and allow it to thaw at room temperature before use (this could take up to 30 minutes). Do not refreeze or shake vials.
Each morning, remove a new unopened vial (2 vials if treating both eyes) from the refrigerated weekly carton and prepare in the following way:
Wash your hands.
If wearing contact lenses, removed them before drug administration. Lenses may be reinserted 15 minutes after administration.
Removed the plastic flip-off cap from the vial.
Peel off the back of the vial adapter blister pack.
Without removing the vial adapter from its blister pack, connect it to the vial by firmly pushing it down until it snaps into place over the neck of the vial. The spike of the adapter should pierce through the vial's rubber stopper. Once connected, do not remove the adapter from the vial.
Once the vial adapter has been connected, the vial may be stored for up to 12 hours in the refrigerator between 2 to 8 degrees C (36 to 46 degrees F) or at room temperature up to 25 degrees C (77 degrees F). Discard open vials after 12 hours even if there is solution left in it.
 
Administration of the dose:
Using a single sterile disinfectant wipe, gently clean the surface of the valve on the connector part of the vial adapter.
After cleaning, wait approximately 1 minute to allow the valve to dry.
Remove a pipette from its protective packaging.
Screw the pipette clockwise into the connector part of the vial adapter. Make sure the pipette plunger is pushed all the way down.
Turn the vial upside-down with the pipette still connected. Gently pull the plunger until it stops. Make sure the plunger has reached the stop point and that the pipette contains the eye drop solution.
If the pipette is empty, keep the vial with the connected pipette upside-down, push the plunger all the way in and pull it out again.
Once correctly filled, unscrew the pipette counter-clockwise from the vial adapter. Pull the pipette straight up to remove it.
Sit or lie down to steady yourself.
Holding the pipette pointing down, between your middle finger and thumb, tilt your head back and position the pipette above the affected eye. Do not touch your eye with the pipette.
With your other hand, pull down the lower eyelid to create a conjunctival pocket.
Gently push the plunger down until at least 1 drop is released into the conjunctival pocket. If you miss your eye and there is no longer solution in the pipette, try again using a new pipette and wipe.
With your head still tilted back, blink a few times so the medicine covers the surface of your eye.
Throw away the used pipette, even if there is still solution left in it.
A new pipette and sterile wipe must be used for each dose. If both eyes require treatment, a new pipette and sterile wipe are required for each eye.
Document each dose on the weekly dose recording card provided with the delivery kit. Consider use of an alarm as a reminder to instill dose every 2 hours. If a dose is missed, treatment should be continued as normal at the next scheduled administration.
