          Dosage Description
          800 mg/m2 IV on cycle 1, day 1, followed by 600 mg/m2 IV every 3 weeks OR 400 mg/m2 IV every 2 weeks until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity in combination with fluoropyrimidine- and platinum-containing chemotherapy. Administer zolbetuximab prior to chemotherapy when given on the same day. In 1 multicenter, randomized, double-blind phase 3 trial (SPOTLIGHT), zolbetuximab was administered in combination with mFOLFOX6 (oxaliplatin 85 mg/m2, leucovorin (or equivalent) 400 mg/m2 IV, and fluorouracil 400 mg/m2 IV bolus followed by 2,400 mg/m2 IV as a continuous infusion over 48 hours [1,200 mg/m2 per day], every 2 weeks for up to 12 treatments); after 12 treatments, patients could continue treatment with zolbetuximab, fluorouracil and leucovorin at the provider's discretion until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity. In the SPOTLIGHT trial, treatment with zolbetuximab plus mFOLFOX6 significantly improved median progression-free survival (PFS) (10.6 months vs. 8.7 months) and median overall survival (OS) (18.2 months vs. 15.5 months) compared with placebo plus mFOLFOX6 in patients with locally advanced unresectable or metastatic HER2-negative gastric or GEJ adenocarcinoma with CLDN18.2-positive tumors. The objective response rate (ORR) was 40.3% versus 39.7% (complete response [CR], 4.9% vs. 2.8%), respectively, for a median duration of 10.3 and 10.5 months. A similar proportion of patients in each arm received subsequent chemotherapy after progression (48% vs. 53%). In another multicenter, randomized, double-blind, phase 3 trial (GLOW), zolbetuximab was administered with CAPOX (oxaliplatin 130 mg/m2 IV on day 2 plus capecitabine 1,000 mg/m2 PO twice daily on days 1 to 14 followed by 7 days off, every 21 days for up to 8 cycles). In this trial, treatment with zolbetuximab plus CAPOX significantly improved median PFS compared with placebo plus CAPOX (8.2 months vs. 6.8 months) and median OS (14.4 months vs. 12.2 months) in the same patient population as the SPOTLIGHT trial; patients could continue zolbetuximab and capecitabine after 8 cycles of treatment at the provider's discretion until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity. The ORR was 32.3% versus 31.2% (CR, 2.4% vs. 0.8%), respectively, for a median duration of 8.3 and 6.2 months.

    General Information
    There are no data on the use of zolbetuximab during human pregnancy.   Adverse Reactions
    Headline
    anaphylactoid reactions, cough, dysphonia, infusion-related reactions, throat irritation, urticaria
    General Information
    Hypersensitivity reactions including anaphylaxis or anaphylactoid reactions occurred in 18% of patients treated with zolbetuximab in combination with mFOLFOX6 or CAPEOX in 2 randomized clinical trials (grade 3 or 4, 2%) including 2 patients who experienced anaphylactic reactions. Infusion-related reactions occurred in 3.2% of patients who received zolbetuximab in these trials (grade 3, 0.4%). Monitor patients for signs and symptoms of anaphylaxis (e.g., urticaria, repetitive cough, wheezing, throat irritation/tightness, and dysphonia/change in voice) during the infusion and for 2 hours after completion (or longer if clinically indicated); additional signs and symptoms of infusion reactions include nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, salivary hypersecretion, fever, chest discomfort, chills, back pain, cough, and hypertension. Interrupt the infusion for any grade 2 hypersensitivity reactions or grade 3 nausea/vomiting and resume at a reduced rate when symptoms are grade 1 or less. Premedicate with antihistamines prior to subsequent infusions and follow standard infusion rates. Discontinue zolbetuximab for severe or life-threatening hypersensitivity reactions; treat symptoms according to standard medical care.

Description
  Text
  Zolbetuximab-clzb is a claudin 18.2 (CLDN18.2)-directed cytolytic antibody approved for the first-line treatment of adults with locally advanced unresectable or metastatic human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2)-negative gastric or gastroesophageal junction (GEJ) adenocarcinoma whose tumors are CLDN182 positive, in combination with fluoropyrimidine- and platinum-containing chemotherapy. Hypersensitivity reactions including anaphylaxis and serious and fatal infusion-related reactions have occurred. Monitor patients during and for at least 2 hours after infusion with zolbetuximab. Zolbetuximab was approved in October 2024.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Zolbetuximab is a chimeric claudin 18.2 (CLDN18.2)-directed cytolytic immunoglobulin G1 antibody that depletes CLDN18.2-positive cells via antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC) and complement-dependent cytotoxicity (CDC). Claudins (CLDNs) are important cellular proteins that help maintain tissue homeostasis by interacting with CLDNs from adjacent cells to form tight junctions; they are also able to recruit signaling proteins. Tight junctions are responsible for cell-to-cell adhesion in epithelial cell sheets as well as functioning as gatekeepers to regulate the passage of ions, water, and macromolecules (including tumor cells) through paracellular spaces. Tight junctions also maintain cellular polarity and prevent molecules from mixing between apical and lateral membranes. A loss of CLDN function may be associated with an increased potential for metastatic disease, as a loss of the binding characteristics of tight junctions is the first step of the epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) where there is a progressive loss of cellular polarity and adhesion, allowing for the migration of malignant cells.
There are several different CLDNs, of which CLDN18.2 is almost exclusively found in the tight junctions of normal gastric mucosa cells. While it may be downregulated in gastric cancer cells, CLDN18.2 expression is maintained in up to 80% of gastric cancer tumors. In normal tissue, it is located deep within a supramolecular complex and is inaccessible to antibodies. In gastric tumors, changes in cellular polarity due to the EMT process lead to it being exposed on the cell surface where it is able to be recognized by anti-claudin18.2 antibodies such as zolbetuximab. Zolbetuximab had increased antitumor activity in CLDN18.2-expressing mouse tumor models in combination with chemotherapy compared to either zolbetuximab or chemotherapy alone.

    Description
    The geometric mean Cmax of zolbetuximab at steady-state was 415 mcg/mL (CV, 22%) and the AUCtau was 3,149 mcg*day/mL (CV, 37%). Steady-state was achieved by 18 weeks after a first dose of 800 mg/m2 IV followed by 600 mg/m2 IV every 3 weeks. Reconstitution
100 mg vial: Slowly add 5 mL of Sterile Water for Injection to each vial, resulting in a concentration of 20 mg/mL. Direct the stream along the walls of the vial and not directly onto the lyophilized powder.
300 mg vial: Slowly add 15 mL of Sterile Water for Injection to each vial, resulting in a concentration of 20 mg/mL. Direct the stream along the walls of the vial and not directly onto the lyophilized powder.
Slowly swirl each vial until the contents are dissolved; do not shake. Allow the vial to settle until any bubbles are gone.
Storage after reconstitution: Reconstituted vials may be stored at room temperature (15 to 30 degrees C or 59 to 86 degrees F) for up to 5 hours if not used immediately. The product does not contain a preservative.
 
Dilution
Add the appropriate volume of reconstituted zolbetuximab to an infusion bag containing an amount of 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection necessary to obtain a final concentration of 5 mg/mL; discard any unused portion of the vials.
Mix by gentle inversion; do not shake.
Storage after dilution: After dilution, zolbetuximab may be stored at room temperature (15 to 30 degrees C or 59 to 86 degrees F) for no longer than 6 hours from the end of dilution to completion of the infusion. It may also be stored refrigerated (2 to 8 degrees C or 36 to 46 degrees F) for no longer than 16 hours from end of dilution to completion of the infusion. Do not freeze.
 
Intravenous Infusion
Use of a 0.2 micron in-line filter is recommended.
First dose (800 mg/m2): Begin at an initial infusion rate of 100 mg/m2 per hour. If there are no adverse reactions after 30 to 60 minutes, the rate may be increased to 200 to 265 mg/m2 per hour.

If the infusion time exceeds the recommended storage time after dilution, discard the infusion and prepare a new infusion bag to continue the infusion.
