Drug Class: Ethiodized oil is an injectable poppy seed oil-based, iodinated radiopaque contrast agent indicated for hysterosalpingography, lymphography, and selective hepatic intra-arterial use for imaging tumors in patients with known hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC).

Routes: injection (IV/IM/SC); Common adult doses: 15 mL intrauterine for hysterosalpingography; 4 mL intralymphatic for unilateral lymphography of the upper extremities; 8 mL intralymphatic for unilateral lymphography of the lower extremities; 3 mL intralymphatic for penile lymphography; 2 mL intralymphatic...

Common Side Effects (excluding routine GI upset): acute respiratory distress syndrome (ards), cough, dyspnea, pulmonary embolism, hepatic decompensation, thrombosis, edema, headache, impaired wound healing, phlebitis, rash

In an Overdose: No overdose/antidote information is listed in this file.

Key Nursing Considerations: Contraindications/precautions: This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components. Ethiodized oil use is contraindicated in areas of the liver where the bile ducts are dilated unless external biliary drainage was performed before hepatic intraarterial injection.

Contraindications And Precautions

  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  Contraindications And Precautions
    Headline
    Drug Interactions
    General Information
    The coadministration of certain medications may lead to harm and require avoidance or therapy modification; review all drug interactions prior to concomitant use of other medications.
    Headline
    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components.
    Headline
    Treatment-Related Restrictions of Use
    General Information
    Ethiodized oil use is contraindicated in areas of the liver where the bile ducts are dilated unless external biliary drainage was performed before hepatic intraarterial injection. Hepatic intraarterial injection of ethiodized oil can exacerbate the following hepatic disease: portal hypertension and cause variceal bleeds due to obstruction of intrahepatic portal channels by opening a pre-sinusoidal anastomosis; hepatic ischemia and cause liver enzyme elevations, fever, and abdominal pain; and hepatic failure and cause ascites and encephalopathy.
Ethiodized oil hysterosalpingography is contraindicated in individuals with known or suspected reproductive tract neoplasia due to the risk of peritoneal spread of neoplasm, acute pelvic inflammatory disease, marked cervical erosion, endocervicitis and intrauterine bleeding, in the immediate pre- or postmenstrual phase, pregnancy, and within 30 days of curettage or conization. Perform hysterosalpingography during the follicular phase of the menstrual cycle.
Ethiodized oil lymphography is contraindicated in individuals with a right to left cardiac shunt, advanced pulmonary disease, tissue trauma or hemorrhage, advanced neoplastic disease with expected lymphatic obstruction, previous surgery interrupting the lymphatic system, and radiation therapy to the examined area.
    Headline
    allergic disorder, asthma
    General Information
    Avoid ethiodized oil use in individuals with a history of bronchial asthma or allergic disorder because of an increased risk of a hypersensitivity reaction to ethiodized oil. Administer ethiodized oil only in situations where trained personnel and therapies are promptly available for the treatment of hypersensitivity reactions, including personnel trained in resuscitation; ensure continuous medical monitoring and maintain an intravenous access line. Monitor for signs and symptoms of hypersensitivity reactions during and for at least 30 minutes after ethiodized oil administration.
    Headline
    heart failure, improper use, respiratory failure, respiratory insufficiency
    General Information
    Pulmonary and cerebral embolism can result from improper use by inadvertent intravascular injection or extravasation of ethiodized oil. Avoid ethiodized oil use in individuals with severe respiratory insufficiency, respiratory failure, heart failure, or right-sided cardiac overload. Perform radiological monitoring during the ethiodized oil injection. Do not exceed the recommended maximum dose and rate of injection of ethiodized oil. To minimize the risk of pulmonary embolism during lymphography, obtain radiographic confirmation of intralymphatic injection (rather than intravenous administration), and terminate the procedure when ethiodized oil becomes visible in the thoracic duct or lymphatic obstruction is observed.
    Headline
    renal failure, renal impairment
    General Information
    Screen for renal dysfunction by obtaining a medical history and/or laboratory tests before administering ethiodized oil intra-arterially. Consider follow-up renal function assessments for individuals with a history of renal impairment or renal failure.
    Headline
    bleeding, trauma
    General Information
    Ethiodized oil is contraindicated in individuals with trauma injuries or recent bleeding.
    Headline
    hyperthyroidism
    General Information
    Ethiodized oil is contraindicated in individuals with hyperthyroidism. Iodinated contrast media, such as ethiodized oil, can affect thyroid function and may cause hyperthyroidism or hypothyroidism. Ethiodized oil may remain in the body for several months, depending on the dose administered and route of administration. Keep the ethiodized oil dose as low as possible and consider monitoring thyroid function closely for several months after administration of ethiodized oil.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Ethiodized oil for hysterosalpingography is contraindicated during pregnancy due to potential fetal risk from an intrauterine procedure. Verify pregnancy status within 24 hours before administration for hysterosalpingography, due to the potential risk to the fetus from an intrauterine procedure. Ethiodized oil is also contraindicated within 30 days after conization or curettage. Available postmarketing data in pregnant patients are limited and insufficient to determine a drug-associated risk of major birth defects or miscarriage. Ethiodized oil may disrupt thyroid function in both the pregnant patient and the fetus, which can adversely affect fetal development. Ethiodized oil is retained for several weeks after exposure; if exposure occurs prior to or during pregnancy, consider thyroid function testing for the pregnant person. In a small prospective study (n = 22 euthyroid infertility patients undergoing hysterosalpingography), urinary iodine excretion peaked at 4 weeks post-procedure and remained elevated through 24 weeks. Transient elevations in thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH) with normal T4 were observed in some patients 4 to 8 weeks after hysterosalpingography. Animal reproductive studies using the indicated routes have not been conducted; oral studies did not demonstrate embryotoxicity or teratogenicity.
    Headline
    neonates and infants exposed to this medication in utero
    General Information
    Consider thyroid testing in neonates and infants exposed to this medication in utero and in those who were born to patients exposed to ethiodized oil prior to pregnancy. The use of ethiodized oil before or during pregnancy results in placental transfer of iodide, which may disrupt fetal thyroid function. Postmarketing reports have described goiter and hypothyroidism in fetuses and infants exposed to ethiodized oil in utero and in those born to patients exposed to the drug prior to pregnancy.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Use ethiodized oil with caution during breast-feeding. Ethiodized oil significantly increases iodine concentrations in human milk, which may impair thyroid function in the breastfed child. The duration of elevated milk iodine concentrations is unknown but may persist for more than 2 weeks after administration. Consider evaluating thyroid function in breastfed children exposed via human milk if clinically indicated.

Description
  Text
  Ethiodized oil is an injectable poppy seed oil-based, iodinated radiopaque contrast agent indicated for hysterosalpingography, lymphography, and selective hepatic intra-arterial use for imaging tumors in patients with known hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC). Ethiodized oil is for intrauterine, intralymphatic, and selective hepatic intra-arterial use only. Pulmonary embolism may occur immediately or after a few hours to days from inadvertent intravascular administration or intravasation of ethiodized oil and cause decreased pulmonary diffusing capacity and pulmonary blood flow, pulmonary infarction, acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS), and fatalities. To minimize the risk of pulmonary embolism, slow injection with radiologic monitoring is necessary during ethiodized oil administration. Also, iodinated contrast media, such as ethiodized oil, can affect thyroid function and may cause hyperthyroidism or hypothyroidism. Ethiodized oil may remain in the body for several months, depending on dose administered and route of administration; thyroid function monitoring for several months after ethiodized oil administration may be considered.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Ethiodized oil is an iodinated contrast media used to visualize anatomical structures of the body. Iodine is the radiopaque component of ethiodized oil, allowing for the opacification of vessels and body structures in the path of the flow of the contrast media. The degree of enhancement is directly related to the local iodine content in the body structures to be imaged.

Pharmacokinetics

    Hepatic Intraarterial Route
After hepatic intraarterial injection, ethiodized oil that is retained in normal hepatic parenchyma is phagocytized by the Kupffer cells of the liver and washed out via the hepatic lymphatic system in about 2 to 4 weeks. In hepatocellular carcinoma, contrast retention in the liver is prolonged, allowing reimaging of the tumor for 4 weeks or longer.

Inject into a lymphatic vessel under radiologic guidance to prevent inadvertent venous administration or intravasation.
For lymphography of the upper and lower extremities: Start the injection into a lymphatic channel at a rate not to exceed 0.2 mL/minute. Inject the total dose in no less than 1.25 hours. Use frequent radiologic monitoring to determine the appropriate injection rate and to follow the contrast within the lymphatics. Interrupt the injection if the patient experiences pain. Terminate the injection if a lymphatic blockage is present or as soon as the contrast is visualized in the thoracic duct. Obtain immediate post-injection images. Re-image at 24 or 48 hours to evaluate nodal architecture.
 
Intrauterine Administration
Perform the procedure during the follicular phase of the menstrual cycle.
Exclude the following conditions before performing the procedure: pregnancy, uterine bleeding, endocervicitis, acute pelvic inflammatory disease, immediate pre- or postmenstrual phase, or within 30 days of curettage or conization.
Using aseptic technique, inject into the endometrial cavity with fluoroscopic control.
Inject 2 mL increments until tubal patency is determined.
Stop the injection if the patient experiences excessive discomfort.
A 24-hour image can be obtained if, after slow peritoneal spillage, the evaluation of adhesions is needed.
