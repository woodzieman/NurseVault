Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  General Information
  Because digoxin immune Fab is a foreign (sheep-derived) protein, both DigiFab® and Digibind® should be used cautiously in patients with known ovine protein hypersensitivity. Each vial of DigiFab® contains approximately 75 mg of mannitol and should be used cautiously in patients with mannitol hypersensitivity. Also, both DigiFab® and Digibind® products are prepared by isolating the immunoglobulin fraction of the ovine serum, digesting it with papain, and isolating the digoxin-specific Fab fragments by affinity chromatography. Patients with papain hypersensitivity or allergies to chymopapain, other papaya extracts, or the pineapple extract bromelain may have an allergic response to the product. Do not administer DigiFab® to a patient with a known history of hypersensitivity to papaya or papain unless the benefits outweigh the risks and appropriate management for anaphylactic reactions is readily available. Patients with a history of an allergy to antibiotics, latex, or dust mites may also be at risk for an allergic reaction to digoxin immune Fab (see Adverse Reactions). Some dust mite allergens and some latex allergens share antigenic structures with papain.Digoxin immune Fab should be used with caution in patients with renal impairment or renal failure. Elderly patients often have reduced renal function. Elimination of digoxin immune Fab-digoxin complexes may be delayed in patients with reduced renal function. The risk of digoxin release from the Fab fragment-digoxin complex is potentially increased when excretion of the complex is slowed. Free digoxin could cause toxicity to recur. For example, a functionally anephric patient had recurrence of atrioventricular block due to digoxin 10 days after reversal by digoxin immune Fab. However, in a study, toxicity recurrence was only associated with an inadequate initial dose. No dose reduction or escalation is needed for patients with impaired kidney function (see Dosage). In patients functionally anephric, digoxin immune Fab is not excreted and may be eliminated by the reticuloendothelial system. It is not clear if detoxification or reintoxification will occur; therefore, prolonged monitoring for digoxin toxicity is recommended. Also, do not begin treatment with digoxin until all Fab fragments have been eliminated by the body, which may take a week or longer in patients with impaired renal function. Monitoring of free or unbound digoxin concentrations may be beneficial.Digoxin immune Fab is classified in FDA pregnancy risk category C. Limited data are available on the use of the product in pregnant women. Give to a pregnant woman only when clearly needed. The maternal benefits may outweigh the unknown fetal risks, as digoxin immune Fab should only be used for life-threatening or potentially life-threatening digoxin toxicity (see Dosage).Caution should be exercised when administering Digoxin immune Fab to a woman who is breast-feeding her infant. It is not known whether this drug is excreted in human milk. However, ingested breast milk that contained digoxin immune Fab would be digested in the infant's stomach. Any risk to a nursing infant appears to be very small.Patients with digoxin toxicity and hypokalemia may need potassium supplementation. Acute digoxin overdose causes hyperkalemia and a compensatory hyperkaliuresis. Reversal of digoxin effects on sodium-potassium ATPase by digoxin immune Fab can lead to hypokalemia. Closely monitor serum potassium concentrations before, during, and after therapy with digoxin immune Fab.

    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components. Do not use digoxin immune Fab in people who have experienced a hypersensitivity reaction to papaya or papain unless the benefits outweigh the risks and appropriate management for anaphylactic reactions is readily available. Digoxin immune Fab is a foreign (sheep-derived) protein. Individuals with a known allergy to sheep protein are at risk for an anaphylactic reaction, as are individuals who have previously received intact ovine antibodies or ovine Fab.
    Headline
    renal failure, renal impairment
    General Information
    Use digoxin immune Fab with caution in patients with renal failure. The elimination half-life of digoxin immune Fab in renal failure has not been clearly defined. The elimination half-life appears to be increased up to 10-fold in patients with renal impairment. Monitor patients with severe renal failure who have received digoxin immune Fab for digitalis toxicity for a prolonged period for possible recurrence of toxicity. Monitoring of free (unbound) digoxin concentrations after the administration of digoxin immune Fab may be appropriate in order to establish recurrent toxicity in renal failure patients. Postpone redigitalization, if possible, until the Fab fragments have been eliminated from the body, which may require several days and, in patients with impaired renal function, may require a week or longer.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    It is not known whether digoxin immune Fab can cause fetal harm if administered during pregnancy. Animal studies have not been conducted. Administer the drug to pregnant patients only if clinically needed.
    Headline
    heart failure
    General Information
    Individuals with poor heart function or heart failure may deteriorate secondary to the withdrawal of the inotropic action of digoxin by digoxin immune Fab. Provide additional support if needed by using other intravenous inotropes such as dopamine, dobutamine, or vasodilators. Take additional care not to aggravate the digitalis induced rhythm disturbances. Postpone redigitalization, if possible, until the Fab fragments have been eliminated from the body, which may require days to weeks in individuals with impaired renal function.
    Headline

    General Information
    Use digoxin immune Fab with caution during breast-feeding. It is not known whether digoxin immune Fab is excreted in human milk. The risk of exposure through milk is likely to be low, based on the drug's large molecular weight and poor oral absorption.

Adverse Reactions
  Headline
  Adverse Reactions
  General Information
  As digoxin immune Fab is an ovine (i.e., sheep) product, allergic reactions are possible. Allergic reactions to Digibind® have been reported rarely. Rash (unspecified) occurred in 3 of 717 patients who received Digibind®, as determined from analysis of observational data. All 3 patients reported a history of an antibiotic allergy (see Contraindications). The first patient had an allergy to penicillin and tetracycline and developed a generalized rash, facial flushing, and eyelid swelling during the Digibind® infusion. The second patient had an allergy to cefaclor and developed pruritus and a rash 30 minutes after the infusion. The third patient had an allergy to penicillin and also developed a pruritic rash on the day of the infusion. All 3 patients' reactions were successfully treated with either diphenhydramine, hydrocortisone, or an unknown therapy. The Fab fragment of the antibody lacks the the antigenic determinants of the Fc fragment, and DigiFab® is monovalent and thus, formation of extended immune complexes with the antigen are unlikely. The absence of the antigenic determinants of the Fc fragment should render digoxin immune Fab less immunogenic than intact immunoglobulin molecules. Skin testing for allergy was not useful in predicting an allergic response to Digibind®, and allergy testing can delay urgently needed therapy. One patient developed erythema but no accompanying wheal reaction at the site of skin testing with Digibind®, but the patient had no adverse reaction to systemic treatment with the product. Skin testing was not performed during the clinical studies of DigiFab® and is not suggested before administration of this product. Skin testing may be appropriate for high risk individuals such as patients with known allergies to sheep protein or patients with a previous exposure to intact ovine antibodies or ovine Fab (see Contraindications). The intradermal skin test can be performed by diluting 0.1 ml of reconstituted Digibind® (9.5 mg/ml) in 9.9 ml of sterile, isotonic saline (1:100 dilution, 95 mcg/ml). Inject 0.1 ml of the 1:100 dilution (9.5 mcg) intradermally or place one drop of the 1:100 dilution on the skin and make a 1/4-inch scratch through the drop with a sterile needle. Look for an urticarial wheal surrounded by a zone of erythema 20 minutes after applying the solution. If skin testing causes a systemic reaction, apply a tourniquet above the site of testing and institute measures to treat anaphylaxis. The patient should not be treated with future doses of Digibind® unless absolutely essential. If Digibind® is used in a patient with a positive skin test, pretreat the patient with corticosteroids and diphenhydramine and be prepared to treat anaphylaxis. If an anaphylactic reaction occurs, stop the digoxin immune Fab infusion and institute appropriate therapy. Balance the need for epinephrine with the potential risk of its administration in the setting of digitalis toxicity. Inform all patients of the possibility of an anaphylactic reaction, and carefully monitor patients.Due to the high efficiency of digoxin immune Fab in removing digoxin, precipitation of heart failure may occur due to the loss of the positive inotropic effects of digoxin. Also, the loss of effects of digoxin on the atrioventricular node may lead to a rapid ventricular response in patients with atrial fibrillation.Acute digoxin overdose causes hyperkalemia and a compensatory hyperkaliuresis. Reversal of digoxin effects on sodium-potassium ATPase by digoxin immune Fab can lead to hypokalemia. Serum potassium should be monitored closely before, during, and after therapy with digoxin immune Fab.Antibody formation is possible, as exposure to digoxin-specific ovine immune Fab may cause sensitization to ovine serum protein. Human antibodies to ovine Fab have been reported in some patients who received Digibind®, but no clinical reports of decreased efficacy due to antibody presence have been reported to date. Hypothetically, the presence of human antibodies against ovine Fab could lead to diminution of the drug's efficacy. The consequences of repeated exposures are unknown. Digoxin immune Fab should only be administered in specific conditions (see Dosage).An injection site reaction may occur during the intravenous infusion of digoxin immune Fab. One of 8 patients who received DigiFab® had phlebitis of the infusion vein. Also, one patient had moderate postural hypotension that became mild in severity before resolving. Stop the infusion if infusion rate-related reactions occur. If clinically appropriate, the infusion may be started at a slower rate. If digoxin immune Fab is given by bolus injection because of imminent cardiac arrest, an increased incidence of infusion-related reactions may occur.
  Adverse Reactions
    Headline
    anaphylactoid reactions, angioedema, bronchospasm, cough, erythema, hypotension, infusion-related reactions, laryngeal edema, orthostatic hypotension, pruritus, rash, sinus tachycardia, urticaria, wheezing
    General Information
    Allergic reactions including anaphylaxis or anaphylactoid reactions are possible during administration of digoxin immune Fab. Carefully monitor all patients for signs and symptoms of an acute allergic reaction such as urticaria, pruritus, erythema, angioedema, bronchospasm with wheezing or cough, stridor, laryngeal edema, hypotension, rash, and sinus tachycardia. During clinical trials, 1 out of the 8 healthy volunteers who received digoxin immune Fab developed transient orthostatic hypotension. Administration of digoxin immune Fab has also been associated with the development of infusion-related reactions. These reactions are more likely to occur when the drug is administered via a bolus injection; therefore, bolus injections should be used only if cardiac arrest is imminent. If an infusion rate-related reaction or anaphylactoid reaction occurs (such as hypotension, wheezing, or urticaria), stop the infusion at once and administer appropriate treatment. Balance the need for epinephrine with the potential risk of its administration in the setting of digitalis toxicity. If appropriate, the infusion can be re-started at a slower rate.

    Headline
    hypokalemia
    General Information
    Hypokalemia (13%) was a common adverse reaction related to administration of digoxin immune Fab. During severe digoxin intoxication, life-threatening elevation of serum potassium can occur due to digoxin causing a shift of potassium from inside to outside the cell. This may lead to increased urinary excretion of potassium so that a patient may have hyperkalemia but a whole body potassium deficiency. When digoxin immune Fab is administered, potassium shifts back into the cell with a resulting decline in serum potassium concentration. This digoxin immune Fab induced hypokalemia can develop rapidly, and may require cautious treatment with potassium supplementation. Monitor serum potassium closely during and after therapy with digoxin immune Fab.

    Headline
    laboratory test interference
    General Information
    Digoxin immune Fab may cause laboratory test interference with digitalis immunoassay measurements. Standard serum digoxin concentration measurements may be misleading until the Fab fragments are eliminated from the body. In patients with renal impairment, this may take several days to a week or more. Obtain serum digoxin concentrations prior to digoxin immune Fab administration, if possible. At least 6 to 8 hours are required for equilibration between serum and tissue, so absorption of the last dose may continue from the intestine. Serum digoxin measurements may be difficult to interpret if samples are drawn soon after the last digitalis dose. The concentration of digoxin in the serum may rise after administration of digoxin immune Fab. This will be almost entirely bound to the Fab fragment and therefore not able to react with receptors in the body.

Description
  Text
  Digoxin immune Fab is a protein that consists of antibody fragments, which are used as an antidote for life-threatening or potentially life-threatening digoxin toxicity or overdose. The antibody fragments are obtained by immunizing sheep with a digoxin derivative, isolating immunoglobulins from the sheep serum, digesting the immunoglobulins with papain, and obtaining the specific antibody fragments through affinity chromatography. Digoxin immune Fab is available in 40 mg vials, with each vial binding approximately 0.5 mg of digoxin. Monitor drug recipients during and after administration for infusion-related and acute allergic reactions. Additionally, closely monitor patients' temperature, blood pressure, electrocardiogram, and serum potassium concentration during and after treatment.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Digoxin immune Fab treats digoxin toxicity or overdose. The drug is an immunoglobulin fragment that has a high affinity for digoxin molecules, with each vial binding approximately 0.5 mg of digoxin. The affinity between the antidote and the cardiac glycoside is greater than the affinity between the cardiac glycoside and tissue binding sites. By binding molecules of digoxin, digoxin immune Fab reduces the free digoxin concentration and shifts the equilibrium away from the receptors; thereby reversing the cardiotoxic effects of digoxin. Minutes after administration of digoxin immune Fab, free digoxin concentrations decrease from roughly 80% to 5% or less and total (i.e., bound and free) digoxin serum concentrations increase by 10- to 30-fold. Subsequently, the digoxin immune Fab-digoxin complexes are cleared from the body by the kidneys and reticuloendothelial system.

Administration
  General Information
  For storage information, see the specific product information within the How Supplied section.
If possible, determine the serum digoxin or digitoxin concentration before administration of digoxin immune Fab. This measurement establishes the concentration of serum digoxin or digitoxin at the time of diagnosis of digitalis intoxication. At least 6 to 8 hours are required for digoxin equilibration between tissue and serum. Consider the time for equilibration in the interpretation of serum digoxin concentrations.
After administration of digoxin immune Fab, standard serum digoxin concentration measurements may be clinically misleading until the Fab fragments are eliminated from the body. Measure free (unbound) serum digitalis concentration to determine an accurate concentration.

Infuse IV slowly over at least 30 minutes. If infusion rate-related reactions occur, stop the infusion and restart at a slower rate. If cardiac arrest is imminent, bolus administration is acceptable. An increased incidence of infusion-related reactions may be expected with bolus injection.

Maximum Dosage Limits
  Text
  The amount of digoxin immune Fab needed depends on patient-specific factors such as age, amount of digoxin ingested, and patient response.

Dosage Adjustment Guidelines
  Hepatic Impairment
  Specific guidelines for dosage adjustments in hepatic impairment are not available; it appears that no dosage adjustments are needed.
  Renal Impairment
  Elimination of digoxin immune Fab-digoxin complexes may be delayed in patients with renal impairment. For patients with severe renal impairment, monitor for recurrence of digoxin toxicity for a prolonged period.
