Drug Class: Hyaluronidase is a parenteral enzyme that hydrolyzes hyaluronic acid in connective tissue, allowing greater permeation of local tissues.

Routes: injection (IV/IM/SC); Common adult doses: Maximum dosage information is not available.

Common Side Effects (excluding routine GI upset): ecchymosis, edema, erythema, injection site reaction, maculopapular rash, anaphylactoid reactions, angioedema, urticaria

In an Overdose: No overdose/antidote information is listed in this file.

Key Nursing Considerations: Contraindications/precautions: Hyaluronidase hypersensitivity or hypersensitivity to any other ingredient in the formulation is a contraindication to the use of hyaluronidase. Depending upon the product origin, avoid in patients with ovine protein hypersensitivity (e.g., Vitrase) or bovine protein hypersensitivity (Amphadase).

        Dosage Description
        Total doses of 150 to 900 units have been used; divide the total dose and inject subcutaneously or intradermally between 5 to 10 sites along the leading edge of the extravasation. Dilute with normal saline to a final concentration of 15 to 150 units/mL depending on the size of the extravasation; larger areas of extravasation require a greater total number of injections to circumvent the site, and therefore, more dilute injections must be used to administer the total dose. Adjunctive medical therapy for extravasation should also be considered, including hot or cold compresses (extravasating agent dependent), elevation of the affected limb, surgical debridement, and wound care. Examples of drugs where hyaluronidase may be effective in treating extravasation include: antineoplastic agents (e.g., vinca alkaloids), osmotic fluids, (e.g., total parenteral nutrition, 10% or greater dextrose injection, calcium salts, mannitol, potassium salts, sodium bicarbonate, aminophylline, radiocontrast media, or hypertonic saline), or irritants (e.g., diazepam, nafcillin, phenytoin, thiopental, or vasopressin).

Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  General Information
  Hyaluronidase hypersensitivity or hypersensitivity to any other ingredient in the formulation is a contraindication to the use of hyaluronidase. Depending upon the product origin, avoid in patients with ovine protein hypersensitivity (e.g., Vitrase) or bovine protein hypersensitivity (Amphadase). Also, each vial of Amphadase contains 0.1 mg of thimerosal; avoid Amphadase use in patients with thimerosal hypersensitivity. Venom from insect stings (e.g., wasps, bees) may include hyaluronidase allergens, so individuals with such allergies may be more likely to exhibit hyaluronidase sensitivity. A preliminary test for sensitivity should be conducted before using hyaluronidase (see Administration and Dosage).
 
Patients and clinicians should be encouraged to use hyaluronidase from legal commercial sources only. Because hyaluronidase is usually derived from bovine or ovine tissues, there is the potential for contamination with bovine or ovine pathogens if the raw material is illegally imported from outside the US. The FDA does monitor animal-derived drug and food products; those animal ingredients originating in prohibited countries are prohibited entry into the US. However, these safeguards cover legal importations only.
 
Hyaluronidase should not be used for intravenous administration because the enzyme is rapidly inactivated and the effects relative to absorption and dispersion of other drugs are not produced when it is administered intravenously. When considering the administration of any other drug with hyaluronidase, clinicians should be familiar with the precautions pertaining to the other drug and observe them. For example, when epinephrine is injected along with hyaluronidase, precautions related to epinephrine (e.g., cardiovascular disease, thyroid disease, diabetes, digital nerve block, ischemia of the fingers and toes, etc.) should be observed. While commonly used to treat drug extravasation, care must be taken to choose hyaluronidase for the proper clinical scenarios in which it has been proven to be helpful, and to avoid clinical scenarios in which the drug may cause further tissue injury. Hyaluronidase should not be used to enhance the absorption and dispersion of dopamine and/or alpha-agonist drugs. Furosemide, benzodiazepines and phenytoin have been found to be incompatible with hyaluronidase.
 
Hyaluronidase should not be administered by direct application to the cornea during ocular surgery. Avoid unintended (accidental) ocular exposure.
 
Hyaluronidase should not be injected into acutely inflamed or infected areas to avoid a potential risk of spreading a localized infection. Do not use hyaluronidase to reduce swelling from bites or stings to avoid a potential risk of spreading a toxin and/or infectious agent. Venom from insect stings (e.g., wasps, bees) may include hyaluronidase allergens, so the administration of hyaluronidase may actually worsen the reaction to the sting in allergic individuals.
 
During hypodermoclysis (usually performed in children and infants), special care must be taken to avoid overhydration (hypervolemia) in the pediatric patient by controlling the rate and total volume of the clysis.
 
Hyaluronidase is classified in FDA pregnancy risk category C. It is not known whether it can cause fetal harm when administered during pregnancy or can affect reproduction capacity. No adequate and well controlled animal studies have been conducted with hyaluronidase to determine reproductive effects. No adequate and well controlled studies have been conducted in pregnant women. Hyaluronidase should be used during pregnancy only if clearly needed.
 
It is not known whether hyaluronidase is excreted in human milk. Because many drugs are excreted in human milk, caution should be exercised when hyaluronidase is administered to a breast-feeding woman.

    Headline
    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components. Because insect venoms (e.g., wasp and bee stings) may contain hyaluronidase allergens, individuals with a history of such insect sting allergies may be at increased risk of hypersensitivity reactions to hyaluronidase.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Studies of hyaluronidase use during human pregnancy as an aid to delivery have been performed without reports of maternal or fetal harm. Administration of hyaluronidase during labor and obstetric delivery was reported to cause no increase in blood loss or differences in cervical trauma. Additionally, studies of hyaluronidase as an aid to conception (assist the in vitro fertilization of human eggs) have not reported maternal or fetal harm.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    There are no data available on the presence of hyaluronidase in human milk, the effects on breastfed infants, or the effects on milk production to inform about the risk of hyaluronidase to an infant during breast-feeding. Consider the developmental and health benefits of breast-feeding along with the mother's clinical need for hyaluronidase and any potential adverse effects on the breastfed infant from hyaluronidase.

Adverse Reactions
  Headline
  Adverse Reactions
  General Information
  Hyaluronidase is generally well tolerated and is associated with few adverse events. The most frequently reported adverse experiences has been a local injection site reaction (e.g., erythema, irritation). Hyaluronidase has been reported to enhance the adverse events associated with coadministered drug products. Edema has been reported most frequently in association with hypodermoclysis. Studies have demonstrated that hyaluronidase is antigenic; repeated injections of relatively large amounts of this enzyme may result in antibody formation of neutralizing antibodies. Testicular atrophy (degeneration) may occur as a result of organ-specific antibodies against purified ovine (Vitrase) or bovine (Amphadase) testicular hyaluronidase. Allergic reactions (e.g., urticaria, angioedema) have been reported in less than 0.1% of patients receiving hyaluronidase. Anaphylactoid reactions following retrobulbar block or intravenous injections have occurred rarely. Symptoms of hyaluronidase sensitivity or toxicity consist of local or macular edema; urticaria; erythema; chills; nausea/vomiting; dizziness; sinus tachycardia; and hypotension. The enzyme should be discontinued and supportive measures initiated immediately if symptoms of sensitivity or toxicity occur.
 
Results from an experimental study in humans on the influence of hyaluronidase in bone repair indicate that this enzyme alone in the usual clinical dosage does not deter bone healing.
 
When solutions devoid of inorganic electrolytes are given by hypodermoclysis, hypovolemia may occur. This may be prevented by using solutions containing adequate amounts of inorganic electrolytes and/or controlling the volume and speed of administration. This is not an adverse reaction of hyaluronidase per say, more an adverse event that may occur during the procedure where hyaluronidase is administered.
 
Intravitreal injection of hyaluronidase has produced the formation of strand or sheets between the lens and the optic nerve head (vitreal stranding). Avoid use in patients with a history of vitreal stranding. Phase III studies on the use of Vitrase for the treatment of vitreous hemorrhage revealed an increased risk of dose-dependent sterile hypopyon and increased risk of dose-dependent iritis with a 75 unit dose compared to a lower incidence in patients receiving 55 units intravitreally. While the incidence of death (primarily cardiac in nature) was 5% in all treatment groups, this was comparable with the incidence in the saline and nontreatment groups. The FDA stated that this is a relatively high death rate for an ophthalmic study, however the percentage incidence is most likely due to the patient populations' risk factors (e.g., diabetic for many years, elderly, chronic disease) and not the use of hyaluronidase. The FDA has requested additional efficacy and safety data from the manufacturer and another review of Vitrase for the treatment of vitreous hemorrhage.

Description
  Text
  Hyaluronidase is a parenteral enzyme that hydrolyzes hyaluronic acid in connective tissue, allowing greater permeation of local tissues. It is approved for use as adjunct therapy to increase the absorption and dispersion of other injected drugs, particularly local anesthetics; for use in hypodermoclysis rehydration, a method of subcutaneous fluid replacement; and for use as an adjunct in subcutaneous urography for improving resorption of radiopaque agents. Beyond FDA-approved indications, hyaluronidase has been used as an adjunct to non-pharmacologic management of extravasation of certain drugs. Hyaluronidase was FDA-approved in 1950.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Hyaluronidase is a dispersion agent that breaks down hyaluronic acid in connective tissue resulting in increased permeability and enhanced diffusion of concurrently administered parenteral agents. Hyaluronidase hydrolyzes hyaluronic acid by splitting the glucosaminidic bond between C1 of the glucosamine moiety and C4 of glucuronic acid. This temporarily decreases the viscosity of the cellular cement and promotes diffusion of injected fluids or of localized transudates or exudates, thus facilitating their absorption. When no spreading factor is present, material injected subcutaneously spreads very slowly, but hyaluronidase causes rapid spreading of other materials, provided local interstitial pressure is adequate to furnish the necessary mechanical impulse. Such an impulse is normally initiated by injected solutions. The rate of diffusion is proportionate to the amount of enzyme administered, and the extent is proportionate to the volume of solution.
During local anesthesia, hyaluronidase is used to increase the anesthetized area and decrease the time to anesthetic onset of action. During intraocular surgery, hyaluronidase is given to decrease the time to onset of local anesthetics and reduce the risk of surgery-related increased intraocular pressure (IOP). In cases of extravasation, hyaluronidase is used to increase the dispersion and absorption of select agents after unintentional drug infiltration.

  Text
  Hyaluronidase is administered through multiple routes including interstitial, intramuscular, intradermal, intraocular, retrobulbar, and subcutaneous injection. The rate of hyaluronidase-induced diffusion of concurrently administered drugs is proportionate to the amount of enzyme administered, while the extent of diffusion is proportionate to the volume of solution. Knowledge of the mechanisms involved in the clearance of injected hyaluronidase is limited. It is known, however, that the blood of a number of mammalian species brings about the inactivation of hyaluronidase; hyaluronidase-induced absorption and dispersion of other drugs is not achieved after intravenous administration.

    Description
    Visually inspect parenteral products for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration whenever solution and container permit. The solutions should be clear and colorless.
Hyaluronidase should NOT be administered intravenously due to rapid inactivation. Hyaluronidase may be administered for infiltration use, interstitial use, subcutaneous use, intramuscular use, intraocular use, retrobulbar use, or soft tissue use.
Do not inject hyaluronidase into or around acutely inflamed or infected areas to avoid a potential risk of spreading a localized infection. Do not use hyaluronidase to reduce swelling from bites or stings.
Furosemide, benzodiazepines, 2% lidocaine with 10 mcg/mL or 5 mcg/mL epinephrine (due to presence of sodium metabisulfite), and phenytoin have been found to be incompatible with hyaluronidase.
 
Administration for Absorption and Dispersion of other Drugs
Absorption and dispersion of other injected drugs may be enhanced by adding 50 to 300 units, most typically 150 units of hyaluronidase, to the injection solution prior to infiltration use, interstitial use, intramuscular use, intraocular use, retrobulbar use, soft tissue use, or subcutaneous use.
Do not use hyaluronidase to enhance the dispersion and absorption of dopamine or alpha agonist drugs. Consult appropriate references regarding physical or chemical incompatibilities before adding hyaluronidase to a solution containing another drug.
