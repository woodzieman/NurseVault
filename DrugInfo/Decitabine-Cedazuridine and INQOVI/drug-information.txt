Drug Class: Decitabine; cedazuridine is an oral combination product that includes a nucleoside metabolic inhibitor (decitabine) and a cytidine deaminase inhibitor (cedazuridine).

        Route Age Groups
          Heading
          Adults
          Dosage Description
          35 mg decitabine and 100 mg cedazuridine PO once daily for 5 days (days 1, 2, 3, 4, 5) of each 28-day cycle. Complete at least 4 cycles of therapy and continue until disease progression. Therapy interruption and/or a dose reduction may be necessary in patients who develop toxicity. Patients with intermediate-1 or -2 or high-risk MDS received decitabine; cedazuridine or IV decitabine (20 mg/m2 per day) for 5 days per 28-day cycle for the first 2 cycles followed by long-term oral decitabine; cedazuridine thereafter in a multicenter, randomized, crossover, phase 2 study (n = 80; Study ASTX727-01-B). The crossover schedule was either oral therapy in cycle 1 and IV decitabine in cycle 2 (sequence A) or IV decitabine in cycle 1 and oral therapy in cycle 2 (sequence B). At a median follow-up time of 24.3 months, the overall response rate in the overall study population was 60% (CR, 21%); the median duration of CR was 13.3 months. The median overall survival time was 18.3 months for all patients. Patients received a median of 7 treatment cycles. The CR rate was 25% in a subset of patients with MDS (n = 117) in the ASCERTAIN trial which had the same crossover design. In these patients, the median duration of CR was approximately 14.1 months at a median follow-up time of approximately 31.8 months. The median overall survival time was 31.8 months for patients with MDS and 16 additional CMML patients who received oral decitabine; cedazuridine in this study.

          35 mg decitabine and 100 mg cedazuridine PO once daily for 5 days (days 1, 2, 3, 4, 5) of each 28-day cycle in combination with venetoclax until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity.     Headline
    renal failure, renal impairment
    General Information
    Use decitabine; cedazuridine with caution in patients with renal impairment. Nephrotoxicity including renal failure has been reported with decitabine; cedazuridine therapy. Patients with moderate renal impairment (CrCl 30 to 59 mL/min) may be at an increased risk for adverse reactions. A delay in the next cycle of therapy and a dosage reduction may be necessary in patients who develop kidney toxicity. Decitabine; cedazuridine has not been evaluated in patients with severe renal impairment (CrCl 15 to 29 mL/min) or end-stage renal disease (CrCl less than 15 mL/min).
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Decitabine; cedazuridine may cause fetal harm when administered during pregnancy, based on human and animal data and its mechanism of action. Advise pregnant people of the potential fetal risk. Limited published data of decitabine use throughout the first trimester describe adverse developmental fetal outcomes, including major birth defects. A published case report describes multiple fetal structural abnormalities, including holoprosencephaly, absence of nasal bone, mid-facial deformity, cleft lip and palate, polydactyly, and rocker-bottom feet, in an exposed fetus at the 18th week of gestation after 6 cycles of maternal decitabine therapy. In animal studies, adverse developmental outcomes, including embryo-fetal mortality, alterations to growth, and structural abnormalities, were observed in pregnant mice and rats starting at doses approximately 7% of the recommended human dose.
    Headline
    people who can cause pregnancy in others, people who may become pregnant, reproductive risk
    General Information
    Counsel people who may become pregnant and people who can cause pregnancy in others about the reproductive risk associated with decitabine; cedazuridine. Decitabine; cedazuridine can cause fetal harm. Verify pregnancy status prior to treatment initiation. Advise people who may become pregnant to use effective contraception while taking decitabine; cedazuridine and for 6 months after the last dose. For those that can cause pregnancy in others, contraception should be used during decitabine; cedazuridine treatment and for 3 months after the last dose. Based on animal data, decitabine; cedazuridine may cause male infertility. It is not known if these effects are reversible.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Advise patients to avoid breast-feeding while taking decitabine; cedazuridine and for 2 weeks after the last dose, due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in the breastfed child. There are no data on the presence of decitabine; cedazuridine or their metabolites in human milk, their effects on the breastfed child, or their effects on milk production.

Interactions
azaCITIDine: (Major) Avoid the concomitant use of decitabine; cedazuridine with drugs that are metabolized by the enzyme cytidine deaminase (CDA), such as azacitidine; the cytotoxicity of azacitidine may be increased. Cedazuridine is a CDA inhibitor. CDA is one of the enzymes responsible for the metabolism of azacitidine to form inactive metabolites.
Capecitabine: (Major) Avoid the concomitant use of decitabine; cedazuridine with drugs that are metabolized by the enzyme cytidine deaminase (CDA), such as capecitabine; the effectiveness of capecitabine may be reduced. Cedazuridine is a CDA inhibitor. Capecitabine is a prodrug that depends on CDA for get converted to the active therapeutic drug, 5-fluorouracil.
Cholera Vaccine: (Moderate) Patients receiving immunosuppressant medications may have a diminished response to the live cholera vaccine. When feasible, administer indicated vaccines prior to initiating immunosuppressant medications. Counsel patients receiving immunosuppressant medications about the possibility of a diminished vaccine response and to continue to follow precautions to avoid exposure to cholera bacteria after receiving the vaccine.
Cytarabine, ARA-C: (Major) Avoid the concomitant use of decitabine; cedazuridine with drugs that are metabolized by the enzyme cytidine deaminase (CDA), such as cytarabine; the cytotoxicity of cytarabine may be increased. Cedazuridine is a CDA inhibitor. CDA is one of the enzymes responsible for the metabolism of cytarabine to form inactive metabolites.
Dengue Tetravalent Vaccine, Live: (Contraindicated) Avoid administration of the live dengue virus vaccine with immunosuppressive drug therapy and prior to immune recovery following treatment with immunosuppressive drug therapy. When feasible, administer indicated live virus vaccines at least 4 weeks before planned immunosuppression or wait until at least 3 months after discontinuation. The time to restoration of immune competence may be longer in some patients. Patients with altered immunocompetence may be at increased risk for severe adverse reactions due to uninhibited growth of the attenuated live virus. Additionally, vaccines may be less effective if administered during a period of altered immunocompetence.
Dichlorphenamide: (Moderate) Use dichlorphenamide and decitabine together with caution as both drugs can cause metabolic acidosis. Concurrent use may increase the severity of metabolic acidosis. Measure sodium bicarbonate concentrations at baseline and periodically during dichlorphenamide treatment. If metabolic acidosis occurs or persists, consider reducing the dose or discontinuing dichlorphenamide therapy.
Gemcitabine: (Major) Avoid the concomitant use of decitabine; cedazuridine with drugs that are metabolized by the enzyme cytidine deaminase (CDA), such as gemcitabine; the cytotoxicity of gemcitabine may be increased. Cedazuridine is a CDA inhibitor. CDA is one of the enzymes responsible for the metabolism of gemcitabine to form inactive metabolites.
SARS-CoV-2 (COVID-19) vaccines: (Moderate) Patients receiving immunosuppressant medications may have a diminished response to the SARS-CoV-2 virus vaccine. When feasible, administer indicated vaccines prior to initiating immunosuppressant medications. Counsel patients receiving immunosuppressant medications about the possibility of a diminished vaccine response and to continue to follow precautions to avoid exposure to SARS-CoV-2 virus after receiving the vaccine.
SARS-CoV-2 Virus (COVID-19) mRNA Vaccine: (Moderate) Patients receiving immunosuppressant medications may have a diminished response to the SARS-CoV-2 virus vaccine. When feasible, administer indicated vaccines prior to initiating immunosuppressant medications. Counsel patients receiving immunosuppressant medications about the possibility of a diminished vaccine response and to continue to follow precautions to avoid exposure to SARS-CoV-2 virus after receiving the vaccine.
SARS-CoV-2 Virus (COVID-19) Recombinant Spike Protein Nanoparticle Vaccine: (Moderate) Patients receiving immunosuppressant medications may have a diminished response to the SARS-CoV-2 virus vaccine. When feasible, administer indicated vaccines prior to initiating immunosuppressant medications. Counsel patients receiving immunosuppressant medications about the possibility of a diminished vaccine response and to continue to follow precautions to avoid exposure to SARS-CoV-2 virus after receiving the vaccine.
Sodium Iodide I-131: (Moderate) Monitor for bone marrow suppression during concomitant use of sodium iodide I-131 and antineoplastic agents. Concomitant use of bone marrow depressants may enhance the depression of the hematopoietic system caused by the use of large doses of sodium iodide I-131.
Tuberculin Purified Protein Derivative, PPD: (Moderate) Immunosuppressives may decrease the immunological response to tuberculin purified protein derivative, PPD. This suppressed reactivity can persist for up to 6 weeks after treatment discontinuation. Consider deferring the skin test until completion of the immunosuppressive therapy.

    Headline
    anemia, hemolytic anemia, leukopenia, neutropenia, thrombocytopenia
    General Information
    Severe myelosuppression, including fatal adverse reactions, can occur in patients treated with decitabine; cedazuridine. Myelosuppression may occur more frequently in the first or second treatment cycles in patients with myelodysplastic syndrome (MDS) or chronic myelomonocytic leukemia (CMML). In a pooled analysis from 2 clinical studies, worsening hematologic parameters from baseline were reported in patients with myelodysplastic syndrome or chronic myelomonocytic leukemia with cycle 1 (n = 103 to 107) and all cycles (n = 203 to 208) of oral decitabine; cedazuridine therapy. Worsening hematologic toxicity included decreased leukocyte count/leukopenia (cycle 1: 79%; grade 3 or 4, 65%; all cycles: 87%; grade 3 or 4, 81%), decreased platelet count/thrombocytopenia (cycle 1: 79%; grade 3 or 4, 65%; all cycles: 82%; grade 3 or 4, 76%), decreased neutrophil count/neutropenia (cycle 1: 70%; grade 3 or 4, 65%; all cycles: 73%; grade 3 or 4, 71%), decreased hemoglobin level/anemia (cycle 1: 58%; grade 3 or 4, 41%; all cycles: 71%; grade 3 or 4, 55%), and febrile neutropenia (cycle 1: grade 3 or 4, 10%; all cycles: 33%; grade 3 or 4, 32%). Myelosuppression was also reported in patients with acute myeloid leukemia (AML) treated with decitabine; cedazuridine plus venetoclax including decreases from baseline in leukocytes (grade 3 or 4, 91%), platelets (70%; grade 3 or 4, 69%), neutrophils (grade 3 or 4, 48%), hemoglobin (54%; grade 3 or 4, 50%), and lymphocytes (97%; grade 3 or 4, 81%) in 1 single-arm trial. Febrile neutropenia (grade 3 or 4, 52%) and fatal hemolytic anemia (1%) also occurred in patients in the same population. If myelosuppression occurs, an interruption of therapy or dose reduction may be required based on severity; administer growth factors and anti-infective therapies as appropriate.

    Headline
    infection, pharyngitis, pneumonitis, sinusitis
    General Information
    Fatal and serious infectious events have been reported with decitabine; cedazuridine therapy. In a pooled analysis from 2 clinical studies, infection including upper respiratory tract infection (23%; grade 3 or 4, 1%), pneumonia (21%; grade 3 or 4, 15%), sepsis (14%; grade 3 or 4, 11%), and cellulitis (12%; grade 3 or 4, 5%) occurred in patients with myelodysplastic syndrome or chronic myelomonocytic leukemia who received oral decitabine; cedazuridine therapy (n = 208). Additionally, fatal cases of sepsis (1%), septic shock (1%), and pneumonia (1%) were reported in patients in the same population. The term pneumonia included pneumonitis; the term sepsis included septic shock and endocarditis; and the upper respiratory tract infection term included naso-pharyngitis and sinusitis. Infection, excluding fungal infections (40%; grade 3 or 4, 13%), sepsis (28%; grade 3 or 4, 18%), and pneumonia (25%; grade 3 or 4, 20%) also occurred in patients with acute myeloid leukemia treated with decitabine; cedazuridine plus venetoclax in a separate single-arm trial with fatal pneumonia occurring in 2% and fatal sepsis occurring in 8% of patients in the same population. Administer anti-infective therapies or prophylaxis as appropriate.

Description
  Text
  Decitabine; cedazuridine is an oral combination product that includes a nucleoside metabolic inhibitor (decitabine) and a cytidine deaminase inhibitor (cedazuridine). Decitabine; cedazuridine is indicated for the treatment of adult patients with myelodysplastic syndromes (MDS), including previously treated and untreated, de novo and secondary MDS with the following French-American-British subtypes (refractory anemia, refractory anemia with ringed sideroblasts, refractory anemia with excess blasts, and chronic myelomonocytic leukemia) and intermediate-1, intermediate-2, and high-risk International Prognostic Scoring System groups. It is also indicated for the treatment of newly diagnosed acute myeloid leukemia (AML) in adults 75 years or older, or who have comorbidities that preclude use of intensive induction chemotherapy. Severe myelosuppression, including fatal adverse reactions and infectious complications may occur; monitor complete blood cell counts. Decitabine; cedazuridine was approved by the FDA in July 2020.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Decitabine; cedazuridine is a combination product.
Decitabine is a pyrimidine nucleoside analog that gets incorporated into DNA during the S-phase of the cell cycle. It also inhibits DNA methyltransferase causing DNA hypomethylation which results in cellular differentiation or apoptosis. Decitabine-induced hypomethylation in cancer cells may restore normal function to genes that are critical for the control of cellular differentiation and proliferation. Decitabine has poor oral bioavailability due to high levels of an enzyme, cytidine deaminase (CDA), found in the gastrointestinal tract and liver that rapidly inactivates cytidine, including the cytidine analog decitabine.
Cedazuridine is a novel CDA inhibitor designed to remain stable in acidic environments. It improves oral bioavailability of decitabine by increasing its systemic exposure.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Oral Route
    Description
    The geometric mean ratio of the decitabine AUC was 99% (90% CI, 93% to 106%) following the administration of 5 consecutive days of oral 35 mg decitabine and 100 mg cedazuridine compared with 5 consecutive days of IV decitabine in patients with myelodysplastic syndrome, chronic myelomonocytic leukemia, and acute myeloid leukemia. The oral bioavailability of cedazuridine was 20% (CV, 23%). The steady-state AUC values were 178 nanogram/mL X hour (CV, 53%) for decitabine and 3,291 nanogram/mL X hour (CV, 45%) for cedazuridine; the time to steady-state was 2 days. The Cmax values were 145 nanogram/mL (CV, 55%) for decitabine and 371 nanogram/mL (CV, 52%) for cedazuridine. The median Tmax values were 1 hour (range, 0.3 to 3 hours) for decitabine and 3 hours (range, 1.5 to 6.1 hours) for cedazuridine.
Following administration with a high-fat meal (approximately 800 to 1,000 calories; 50% of calories from fat), the AUC and Cmax of decitabine decreased to 50% and 25%, respectively; the AUC and Cmax of cedazuridine decreased to 92% and 99%, respectively.

  Text
  Decitabine; cedazuridine is administered orally. In vitro, decitabine is minimally bound to plasma proteins (fraction unbound, 94% to 96%); the fraction unbound for cedazuridine is 62% to 66%. Following administration of the recommended dosage (35 mg decitabine and 100 mg cedazuridine PO daily for 5 consecutive days/28-day treatment cycle) in patients with myelodysplastic syndromes, chronic myelomonocytic leukemia, and acute myeloid leukemia, the apparent volume of distribution values were 417 L (coefficient of variation (CV), 54%) for decitabine and 296 L (CV, 51%) for cedazuridine, the steady-state apparent clearance values were 197 L/hour (CV, 53%) for decitabine and 30.3 L/hour (CV, 46%) for cedazuridine, and the mean steady state half-life values were 1.5 hours for decitabine and 6.7 hours for cedazuridine. Following an oral dose in healthy subjects, 46% and 51% of the total cedazuridine dose was recovered in the urine and feces, respectively; 21% and 27% was recovered as unchanged drug in the urine and feces, respectively. Decitabine is primarily metabolized by cytidine deaminase and by physicochemical degradation; cedazuridine is converted to its epimer by physicochemical degradation.

Dosage Adjustment Guidelines
  Hepatic Impairment
  Specific guidelines for dosage adjustments in patients with baseline hepatic impairment are not available; it appears that no dosage adjustments are needed.
Treatment-Related Hepatotoxicity
A serum bilirubin level of 2 times the upper limit of normal (ULN) or greater OR an AST or ALT level of 2 times the ULN or greater: Delay starting the next cycle of therapy. Resume decitabine; cedazuridine therapy at the same or at a reduced dosage when the toxicity resolves.
  Renal Impairment
  No dosage adjustment is necessary in patients with mild (creatinine clearance (CrCl), 60 to 89 mL/min) or moderate (CrCl, 30 to 59 mL/min) renal impairment at baseline. Decitabine; cedazuridine has not been evaluated in patients with severe renal impairment (CrCl, 15 to 29 mL/min) or end-stage renal disease.
Treatment-Related Nephrotoxicity
Serum creatinine level of 2 mg/dL or greater: Delay starting the next cycle of therapy. Resume decitabine; cedazuridine at the same or at a reduced dosage when the toxicity resolves.
