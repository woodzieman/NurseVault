    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Available human data are insufficient to establish tick-borne encephalitis vaccine-associated risk during pregnancy. Additionally, developmental and reproductive toxicity studies have not been conducted in animals.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Use the tick-borne encephalitis vaccine with caution during breast-feeding. There is no information regarding the presence of the vaccine in human milk, its effects on the breast-fed child, or its effects on milk production. Consider the benefits of breast-feeding, the risk of potential drug exposure in the child, and the risk of not vaccinating the lactating individual.
    Headline
    immunosuppression
    General Information
    Patients with significant immunosuppression may not have an adequate antibody response to the tick-borne encephalitis vaccine. Immunosuppressed persons may include patients with human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) infection; severe combined immunodeficiency (SCID); hypogammaglobulinemia; agammaglobulinemia; altered immune states due to generalized neoplastic disease; or an immune system compromised by radiation therapy or drug therapy (e.g., chemotherapy or corticosteroid therapy with greater than physiologic doses). Short-term (less than 2 weeks) corticosteroid therapy or intra-articular, bursal, or tendon injections with corticosteroids should not be immunosuppressive.

Description
  Text
  Tick-borne encephalitis (TBE) vaccine is a vaccine approved for tick-borne encephalitis prophylaxis in adult and pediatric patients 1 year of age and older. TBE is a viral infection that involves the central nervous system. It is caused by the tick-borne encephalitis virus (TBEV), a member of the family Flaviviridae. Symptoms are nonspecific and may include fever, malaise, anorexia, muscle aches, headache, nausea, and/or vomiting. After about 8 days of remission, a second phase of disease occurs in 20% to 30% of patients. This phase involves the central nervous system and patients may experience symptoms of meningitis (e.g., fever, headache, stiff neck), encephalitis (e.g., drowsiness, confusion, sensory disturbances, motor abnormalities such as paralysis), or meningoencephalitis. Long-term or permanent neurologic symptoms are possible. The primary tick-borne encephalitis vaccine series should be completed at least 1 week prior to potential exposure to TBEV.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  The tick-borne encephalitis vaccine induces tick-borne encephalitis virus (TBEV) neutralizing antibodies, which are thought to confer protection. A protective antibody concentration has not been defined.

    A clinical trial found 90.6% of vaccine recipients 16 years and older were seropositive 7 days after the third vaccination. Administration
  General Information
  For storage information, see the specific product information within the How Supplied section.
Verify immunization status in the state or local immunization information system in addition to the health care electronic record. If this vaccine has been previously given, question the patient, parent, or guardian about any symptoms or signs of an adverse reaction.
Advise individuals on the benefits and risks of the vaccine. Provide a copy of the Vaccine Information Statement (VIS).
Record manufacturer, lot number, administration date, and the name and address of the person administering the vaccine.
Ensure supportive care, including epinephrine 1 mg/mL injection, is readily accessible in the event of a clinically significant hypersensitivity reaction.
Complete a Vaccine Adverse Event Reporting System (VAERS) report if adverse events have been identified. The VAERS toll-free information line is 1-800-822-7967 and email address is info@vaers.org.
