Drug Class: Terbutaline is an oral and parenteral bronchodilator and is a systemic short-acting beta-2 adrenergic agonist (SABA).

Routes: injection (IV/IM/SC), oral; Common adult doses: Dosage Description

Common Side Effects (excluding routine GI upset): arrhythmia exacerbation, chest pain (unspecified), dizziness, hypokalemia, palpitations, premature ventricular contractions (pvcs), pulmonary edema, qt prolongation, sinus tachycardia, st-t wave changes, tremor, anxiety, asthenia, drowsiness, hallucinations, headache, hypertonia, insomnia, muscle cramps, paresthesias, weakness, elevated hepatic enzymes, hyperhidrosis, injection site reaction, rash (unspecified), vasculitis

In an Overdose: No overdose/antidote information is listed in this file.

Key Nursing Considerations: Contraindications/precautions: To minimize the development of tolerance, terbutaline aerosol should not be used continuously. It has been shown that continuous use of beta-2 agonists for 12 months accelerates the decline in lung function.

Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  General Information
  To minimize the development of tolerance, terbutaline aerosol should not be used continuously. It has been shown that continuous use of beta-2 agonists for 12 months accelerates the decline in lung function. Patients should be reevaluated at regular intervals and should not increase either the dose or frequency of administration.Terbutaline can elevate blood glucose levels and should be used with caution in patients with diabetes mellitus.Terbutaline possesses positive inotropic and chronotropic effects; the effects are dose-related. Terbutaline should be used with caution in patients with cardiovascular disorders including ischemic cardiac disease (coronary artery disease), hypertension, cardiac arrhythmias (e.g., tachycardia), or QT prolongation; rarely, such conditions can be aggravated by the use of terbutaline. The risk is higher if higher dosages of terbutaline are used. If such patients do not respond adequately to terbutaline; choose alternative therapy. Beta-agonists should be avoided in patients with congenital long QT syndrome due to the risk of torsade de pointes. Significant changes in systolic and diastolic blood pressures could be expected to occur in some patients after use of any beta-adrenergic bronchodilator. Elderly patients may be more sensitive to the side effects of beta-agonists, especially tremor and tachycardia; this risk is higher in patients with preexisting coronary artery disease. Although not clearly established, airway responsiveness to beta-agonist medications may also change with age.Patients with hyperthyroidism (thyrotoxicosis, thyroid disease) can be more susceptible to the effects of the sympathomimetic drugs. Terbutaline should be prescribed cautiously in these patients.Terbutaline should be used with caution in patients with a history of seizures (seizure disorder).Terbutaline should be used with caution in patients with a known sensitivity to sympathomimetic agents (e.g., pheochromocytoma). Other ingredients in the aerosol product should also be considered in this context.Terbutaline is classified as a pregnancy risk category B drug. It crosses the placenta, but studies have not indicated any teratogenic effects in humans. Fetal tachycardia has followed parenteral use of terbutaline during pregnancy. Terbutaline can inhibit uterine contractions and inhibit labor; it should be used cautiously during the second and third trimesters. Despite these precautions, the 2004 guidelines of the National Asthma Education and Prevention Program (NAEPP) Asthma and Pregnancy Working Group include short-acting inhaled beta2-agonists as first line therapy for mild intermittent asthma during pregnancy, if treatment is required. Albuterol is preferred over other short-acting beta2-agonists due to extensive safety-related information during pregnancy. However, there is no evidence of fetal injury with the use of other short-acting inhaled beta2-agonists, and maintaining a previously established treatment regimen may be more beneficial to the patient. Selection of any pharmacological treatment for asthma control during pregnancy should include the specific needs of the patient, based on an individual evaluation, and consideration of the potential benefits or risks to the fetus.Terbutaline is excreted into breast milk in small amounts and should be used cautiously during breast-feeding. The American Academy of Pediatrics considers terbutaline to be compatible with breast-feeding.Beta2-agonists should be administered with extreme caution to patients being treated with MAOI therapy (see Drug Interactions).

    Headline
    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components.
    Headline
    Treatment-Related Restrictions of Use
    General Information
    Be alert for deterioration of asthma in individuals taking terbutaline in the treatment of asthma, regardless of dosage form used. Increased terbutaline use may indicate asthma destabilization. Asthma may deteriorate acutely over a period of hours or chronically over several days or weeks. If deterioration of asthma occurs during therapy with terbutaline, appropriate evaluation of the individual and the treatment strategy is warranted, giving special consideration to corticosteroid therapy. Terbutaline has no anti-inflammatory activity and is not a substitute for corticosteroid therapy. The use of beta-agonists alone may not be adequate to control asthma in many individuals. Early consideration should be given to adding anti-inflammatory agents (e.g., corticosteroids) to the therapeutic regimen. Corticosteroids should not be stopped or reduced when terbutaline therapy is instituted.
    Headline
    cardiac arrhythmias, coronary artery disease, hypertension
    General Information
    Monitor heart rate and blood pressure in patients receiving high doses of terbutaline; cardiovascular adverse effects are more likely to occur when aggressive doses are used. Terbutaline, like other beta2-agonists and sympathomimetic amines, should be used with caution in individuals with cardiovascular disorders, especially coronary insufficiency (coronary artery disease), cardiac arrhythmias, and hypertension. Terbutaline can produce a clinically significant cardiovascular effect in some patients as measured by increases in pulse rate, systolic or diastolic blood pressure, and cardiac arrhythmias, such as supraventricular tachycardia and extrasystoles. If such effects occur, terbutaline may need to be discontinued.
    Headline
    hyperthyroidism
    General Information
    Terbutaline, as with all sympathomimetic amines, should be used with caution in individuals with hyperthyroidism. Individuals with hyperthyroidism can be more susceptible to the effects of sympathomimetic drugs.
    Headline
    seizure disorder
    General Information
    Terbutaline should be used with caution in individuals with a history of seizure disorder. There have been rare reports of seizures in individuals receiving terbutaline; seizures did not recur in these individuals after the drug was discontinued.
    Headline
    diabetes mellitus, diabetic ketoacidosis
    General Information
    Use systemic terbutaline with caution in individuals with diabetes mellitus. Large doses of intravenous terbutaline have been reported to aggravate pre-existing diabetes mellitus and diabetic ketoacidosis.
    Headline
    geriatric
    General Information
    Geriatric adults may be more sensitive to the side effects of systemic beta-agonists such as terbutaline, especially tremor, restlessness, and tachycardia. Inhaled bronchodilators are generally preferred over systemic agents for the older adult. In general, dose selection for geriatric adults should be cautious, reflecting the greater frequency of decreased hepatic, renal, and cardiac function.
    Headline
    labor, pregnancy, tocolysis
    General Information
    There are no adequate and well-controlled studies on the use of terbutaline during human pregnancy. Terbutaline crosses the placenta, with umbilical blood concentrations 11% to 48% that of the pregnant person's blood concentration. In animal studies, rat offspring have demonstrated alterations in behavior and brain development when dams were given subcutaneous terbutaline at doses resulting in an exposure approximately 6.5 times the human subcutaneous dose of 15 mg/day, on a mg/m2 basis, during late gestation and lactation. No teratogenic effects were observed in offspring of pregnant rats and rabbits given oral terbutaline doses 32 and 65 times, respectively, the maximum recommended human dose. Terbutaline is not FDA-approved for tocolysis. Use of injectable terbutaline for prolonged tocolysis beyond 48 to 72 hours, or for maintenance of tocolysis in the outpatient setting, is contraindicated. Additionally, the use of oral terbutaline for acute or maintenance tocolysis is contraindicated. Serious side effects, including increased heart rate, transient hyperglycemia, hypokalemia, cardiac arrhythmias, pulmonary edema, myocardial ischemia, and death have been reported after administration of terbutaline to pregnant individuals. Increased fetal heart rate and neonatal hypoglycemia may also occur. Restrict the use of terbutaline for the treatment of bronchospasm during labor and obstetric delivery to patients in whom the benefits clearly outweigh the risks, due to the potential for interference with uterine contractility.  In general, systemic therapies, such as terbutaline, are not preferred for the treatment of asthma during pregnancy. Short-acting inhaled beta-2 agonists, such as albuterol, are recommended as first-line therapy for mild intermittent asthma during pregnancy, if treatment is required.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Use terbutaline with caution during breast-feeding. Terbutaline is excreted into human milk in small amounts; based on concentrations in human milk after oral doses of 7.5 to 15 mg/day, it was estimated that an exclusively breast-fed child would receive 0.2% to 0.7% of the lactating individual's dose. Use of oral terbutaline is unlikely to affect a breastfed child. Expert panels consider inhaled beta-agonists to be first line over systemic therapy in a lactating individual, due to low bioavailability and low systemic concentrations. Alternatives to systemic terbutaline would include inhaled albuterol or levalbuterol, for example.

  General Information

Description
  Text
  Terbutaline is an oral and parenteral bronchodilator and is a systemic short-acting beta-2 adrenergic agonist (SABA). Clinically, its main use historically has been for the treatment of bronchospasm. Inhaled short-acting beta-2 agonists (SABAs) are considered the preferred pharmacologic treatment to relieve acute bronchospasm; there is no clear or proven advantage to systemic therapy such as with terbutaline, even in acute care situations, Terbutaline is typically not used unless inhalational therapy is not possible.  However, systemic use can cause cardiovascular side effects and hemodynamic instability; closely monitor treated individuals. Terbutaline has tocolytic properties and has been used off-label for delaying premature labor; however, injectable terbutaline should not be used for the prevention or prolonged treatment (beyond 48 to 72 hours) of preterm labor due to the potential for serious maternal cardiovascular events and death. Furthermore, oral terbutaline should not be used to treat preterm labor or prevent recurrent preterm labor.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Mechanism of Action: Terbutaline stimulates beta-adrenergic receptors. Unlike isoproterenol, terbutaline is more selective for beta-2 receptors than beta-1 receptors. It has insignificant effects on alpha-receptors. Since the lung contains large quantities of beta-2 receptors (located on bronchiolar smooth muscle), terbutaline causes bronchodilation. Stimulation of beta-2 receptors causes relaxation of bronchial smooth muscle, which, in turn, increases bronchial airflow. Terbutaline probably stimulates activation of the enzyme adenyl cyclase, increasing production of cyclic adenosine monophosphate. The increase in cAMP produces vasodilation and muscle relaxation in vascular and smooth muscle. Increased cAMP may lower intracellular calcium by enhancing the efflux of calcium from vascular smooth muscle cells and preventing calcium transmembrane influx. Cyclic AMP may inactivate myosin kinase, reducing phosphorylation of myosin, resulting in smooth muscle relaxation. Intracellular calcium concentrations and cyclic adenosine monophosphate (cAMP) are interdependent. Tolerance to terbutaline can occur just as it does to other drugs that exert their effects via receptor stimulation. In addition, it has been shown that continuous use of beta-2 agonists for a period of 12 months can accelerate the decline in lung function in asthmatic patients.Beta-receptors, including the beta-2 subtype, also are found in the heart. As a result, all beta-adrenergic bronchodilators are also cardiostimulatory, but terbutaline is less so than isoproterenol. Other effects of beta-2 stimulation include hyperglycemia, uterine relaxation, and vasodilation of systemic arterioles, although beta-adrenergic agonists are poor antihypertensive agents. Terbutaline is specifically used to treat recurrent attacks of bronchial asthma and acute bronchospasm.

  Text
  Terbutaline can be administered orally, parenterally, or via inhalation. Small concentrations are distributed into breast milk and cross the placenta, but studies have not indicated any teratogenic effects in humans. Systemic terbutaline undergoes partial metabolism in the liver and is excreted in the urine, about 60% as unchanged drug and the rest as metabolites, with a small amount excreted via the bile in the feces. The possibility of some GI or first-pass metabolism is indicated by a smaller percentage of unchanged drug excreted following oral administration.
