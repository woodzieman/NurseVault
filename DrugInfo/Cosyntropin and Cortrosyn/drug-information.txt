    Drug Interactions
    General Information
    The coadministration of certain medications may lead to harm and require avoidance or therapy modification; review all drug interactions prior to concomitant use of other medications.
    Headline
    hepatic cirrhosis, nephrotic syndrome
    General Information
    Any condition that elevates or lowers cortisol binding globulin concentrations may increase or decrease plasma total cortisol concentrations, respectively. Cortisol binding globulin concentrations can be low in hepatic cirrhosis or nephrotic syndrome. Measure cortisol binding globulin concentrations as necessary to ensure accuracy of interpretation of plasma total cortisol concentrations.
    Headline
    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Available data from case reports over decades of use with cosyntropin during pregnancy have not identified an increased risk of major birth defects, miscarriage, or other adverse outcomes. Unidentified adrenal insufficiency can result in adverse outcomes for the pregnant individual and fetus; therefore, the diagnosis of suspected adrenal insufficiency during pregnancy should not be delayed. Adrenocortical disease during pregnancy is relatively rare as most cases are diagnosed before an individual becomes pregnant, but ACTH stimulated normal cortisol values have been established for each trimester. The use of cosyntropin to confirm the diagnosis of adrenal insufficiency during pregnancy, when suspected, is described in the literature.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Cosyntropin is compatible with breast-feeding. There are no data on the presence of cosyntropin in human milk, its effects on the breastfed child, or its effects on milk production. However, cosyntropin has a large molecular weight (2933.44) and a short half-life, which greatly limits the potential for exposure of the breastfed infant through human milk.

Description
  Text
  Cosyntropin is a parenteral synthetic peptide containing the same first 24 amino acid sequence as natural corticotropin (ACTH). Cosyntropin is less potent and less allergenic than other exogenous ACTH preparations (i.e., corticotropin). It is used for diagnostic testing of adrenal function.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Cosyntropin exhibits the same corticosteroidogenic activity as natural adrenocorticotropic hormone (ACTH) and the pharmacologic profile of the 2 compounds are similar. Various studies have shown that the biologic activity of ACTH resides in the N-terminal portion of the molecule and that the 1 to 20 amino acid residue is the minimal sequence retaining full activity. An injection of 0.25 mg cosyntropin will stimulate the adrenal cortex maximally and to the same extent as 25 units of natural ACTH. This dose of cosyntropin will produce maximal secretion of 17-OH corticosteroids, 17-ketosteroids, and/or 17-ketogenic steroids.

Pharmacokinetics
  Text
  Cosyntropin is administered intramuscularly and intravenously. Peak plasma cortisol concentrations occur at approximately 2.5 +/- 0.5 hours after intravenous injection of cosyntropin and return to baseline values by 6 hours. The exposure-response relationship and time course of pharmacodynamic response for the safety and efficacy of cosyntropin injection have not been fully characterized.

Standard doses (i.e., 125 or 250 mcg doses)
Reconstitute 0.25 mg vial with 1 mL of 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection for a concentration of 250 mcg/mL.
Discard any unused portion of the vial.
 
Low doses (i.e., 1 mcg doses or doses less than 125 mcg)
Reconstitute 0.25 mg vial with 1 mL of 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection for a concentration of 250 mcg/mL.
Withdraw 1 mL and further dilute in 10 to 250 mL (depending on desired dose/concentration) of 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection for a resultant concentration of 1 to 25 mcg/mL.
Storage: Dilutions of 1 mcg/mL in 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection have been reported to be stable refrigerated (2 to 8 degrees C) for up to 60 days. Dilutions of 5 mcg/mL in 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection have been reported to be stable for at least 4 months when refrigerated.
 
IV Push
Withdraw the appropriate dose and inject IV over 1 to 2 minutes.
