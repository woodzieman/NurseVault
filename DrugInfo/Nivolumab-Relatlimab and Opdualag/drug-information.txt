    Headline
    autoimmune disease, inflammatory bowel disease
    General Information
    Use nivolumab; relatlimab with caution in patients with a history of autoimmune disease, including inflammatory bowel disease. Immune-mediated reactions, which may be severe or fatal, can occur in patients treated with drugs that bind to either the programmed death-receptor 1 (PD-1) or the PD-ligand 1 (PD-L1), thus removing inhibition of the immune response, such as nivolumab; relatlimab. These immune-mediated reactions can occur in any organ system or tissue and can also affect more than one body system simultaneously. Although usually occurring during treatment with PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors, immune-mediated reactions can occur at any time after starting therapy including after discontinuation of therapy. Because early identification and management is critical to safe usage of PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors, closely monitor patients for signs and symptoms that may be clinical manifestations of underlying immune-mediated reactions. Initiate an appropriate workup to exclude alternative etiologies including infection in cases of suspected immune-mediated reaction. Begin medical management promptly, including specialty consultation as appropriate. An interruption or discontinuation of nivolumab; relatlimab therapy may be necessary, depending on the severity of the reaction. In general, if an interruption or discontinuation of therapy is necessary, administer systemic corticosteroids (1 to 2 mg/kg per day of prednisone or equivalent); upon improvement to grade 1 or less, begin a steroid taper over at least 1 month. Consider additional systemic immunosuppression in patients whose symptoms are not controlled with corticosteroid therapy.

    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components.
    Headline
    pulmonary disease, thoracic radiation therapy
    General Information
    Use nivolumab; relatlimab with caution in patients who have a history of pre-existing pulmonary disease; immune-mediated pneumonitis/interstitial lung disease has been reported in patients treated with nivolumab; relatlimab. The incidence is higher in patients who have received prior thoracic radiation therapy, except in patients with classical Hodgkin lymphoma, where pneumonitis rates were similar regardless of prior thoracic radiation. Monitor patients for signs and symptoms of pneumonitis (e.g., new or worsening cough, chest pain, shortness of breath). If confirmed, nivolumab; relatlimab therapy may need to be interrupted or discontinued depending on severity; treatment with systemic corticosteroids may be necessary.
    Headline
    corneal transplant, organ transplant
    General Information
    Use nivolumab; relatlimab with caution in patients who have previously had a solid organ transplant or other transplant (including corneal transplant). Immune-mediated organ transplant rejection has been reported with PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitor therapy.
    Headline
    cardiac disease
    General Information
    Use nivolumab; relatlimab with caution in patients with cardiovascular risk factors such as hypertension, heart failure, cardiac disease, and myocardial infarction as they are at increased risk of cardiac immune-related adverse reactions such as myocarditis, vasculitis, and pericarditis.  Immune-mediated myocarditis has been reported with nivolumab; relatlimab therapy. Assess patients who develop cardiac or cardiopulmonary symptoms for the presence of myocarditis. If myocarditis is suspected, hold therapy and begin high-dose steroids (i.e., prednisone or methylprednisolone 1 to 2 mg/kg per day) and get a cardiology consultation to start a diagnostic work-up. Permanently discontinue nivolumab; relatlimab in patients who develop grade 2 or higher myocarditis.
    Headline
    neurological disease
    General Information
    Use nivolumab; relatlimab with caution in patients who have a history of neurological disease such as myasthenia gravis or Guillain-Barre syndrome. Immune-mediated neurotoxicity has been reported with nivolumab; relatlimab or other PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors. Nivolumab; relatlimab may need to be interrupted or discontinued depending on the severity of the neurotoxicity; treatment with systemic corticosteroids may also be necessary.
    Headline
    allogeneic stem cell transplant
    General Information
    Perform a risk/benefit analysis prior to starting treatment with a PD-1/PD-L1 blocking antibody, such as nivolumab; relatlimab, in patients who may receive or who have a history of undergoing an allogeneic stem cell transplant (SCT). Fatal and other serious complications including hyperacute, acute, and chronic graft-versus-host-disease, sinusoidal obstruction syndrome (SOS) previously termed veno-occlusive disease (VOD) after reduced intensity conditioning, and a steroid-requiring febrile syndrome (without an identified infectious cause) have been reported in patients who receive an allogeneic SCT before or after being treated with a PD-1/PD-L1 blocking antibody. Monitor patients closely for evidence of transplant-related complications and intervene promptly. Complications may occur despite intervening therapy between PD-1/PD-L1 blockade and allogeneic SCT.
    Headline
    obesity
    General Information
    Monitor patients with obesity as well as those who are considered overweight for an increased risk of infusion-related reactions when treated with nivolumab; relatlimab. Patients with a high BMI have a greater risk even if they have a low metabolic risk (i.e., fewer than 2 of diabetes, dyslipidemia, hypertension).
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Nivolumab; relatlimab may cause fetal harm if administered during pregnancy, based on its mechanism of action and data from animal studies. There are no available data on nivolumab; relatlimab use during human pregnancy to evaluate a drug-associated risk. Human immunoglobulin G4 is known to cross the placenta; therefore, there may be drug exposure to the developing fetus. The risk of exposure is likely to be greater during the second and third trimesters of pregnancy. The PD-1 pathway is associated with decidual CD8-positive T-cell function and the PD1/PD-L1 interaction may be important in maintaining fetal tolerance during pregnancy. PD1/PDL1 Inhibitors, such as nivolumab, may impair maternal immune tolerance resulting in spontaneous abortion, intrauterine growth restriction, or early-onset preeclampsia. In animal reproduction studies in pregnant cynomolgus monkeys, increased rates of abortion and premature infant death were observed with twice weekly nivolumab administration at doses resulting in AUC values of 9 to 42 times higher than the clinical human dose of 3 mg/kg. There are no available animal data evaluating relatlimab.
    Headline
    people who may become pregnant, reproductive risk
    General Information
    Counsel people who may become pregnant about the reproductive risk associated with nivolumab; relatlimab. Nivolumab; relatlimab may cause fetal harm. Verify pregnancy status prior to treatment initiation. Advise people who may become pregnant to use effective contraception while taking nivolumab; relatlimab and for at least 5 months after the last dose.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Advise patients to avoid breast-feeding while taking nivolumab; relatlimab and for 5 months after the last dose. There are no data on the presence of nivolumab; relatlimab in human milk, its effects on the breast-fed child, or its effects on milk production. Consider the benefits of breast-feeding, the patient's clinical need for treatment, and any potential adverse effects on the breast-fed child from the medication or from the patient's underlying medical condition.

Interactions
Sodium Iodide I-131: (Moderate) Monitor for bone marrow suppression during concomitant use of sodium iodide I-131 and antineoplastic agents. Concomitant use of bone marrow depressants may enhance the depression of the hematopoietic system caused by the use of large doses of sodium iodide I-131.
Tuberculin Purified Protein Derivative, PPD: (Moderate) Immunosuppressives may decrease the immunological response to tuberculin purified protein derivative, PPD. This suppressed reactivity can persist for up to 6 weeks after treatment discontinuation. Consider deferring the skin test until completion of the immunosuppressive therapy.

    anorexia, colitis, diarrhea, gastritis, nausea
    General Information
    Immune-mediated diarrhea or colitis (7%; grade 3, 1.1%), diarrhea (24%; grade 3 or 4, 2%), nausea (17%; grade 3 or 4, 0.6%), and anorexia/decreased appetite (15%; grade 3 or 4, 0.6%) occurred in adult patients with advanced melanoma who received nivolumab; relatlimab (n = 355) in a randomized trial. All patients who developed immune-mediated colitis received systemic corticosteroids; colitis resolved in 83% of these patients. Additionally, immune-mediated gastritis and duodenitis were each reported in less than 1% of patients who received nivolumab; relatlimab or other PD-1/PD-L1 blocking antibodies. Monitor patients for symptoms of colitis (e.g., diarrhea, severe abdominal pain). Nivolumab; relatlimab therapy may need to be interrupted or discontinued depending on severity; treatment with systemic corticosteroids may be necessary. In patients with steroid-refractory colitis, consider repeating infectious workup to exclude alternative etiologies.

Description
  Text
  Nivolumab; relatlimab is a fixed-dose combination of 2 immunoglobulin G4-kappa monoclonal antibodies. Nivolumab is a programmed death receptor-1 (PD-1) blocking antibody and relatlimab is a lymphocyte activation gene-3 (LAG-3) blocking antibody. It is indicated for the treatment of adults and pediatric patients 12 years of age or older with unresectable or metastatic melanoma. Immune-mediated adverse reactions including colitis, pneumonitis, hepatitis, endocrinopathies, skin reactions, and nephritis have been reported in clinical trials; treatment with high-dose corticosteroids may be necessary for patients who develop immune-mediated toxicity.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Nivolumab is a fully human IgG4 monoclonal antibody that inhibits the programmed death receptor-1 (PD-1) immune checkpoint protein, one of the key checkpoint molecules that mediates tumor-induced immune suppression. PD-1 receptors are expressed on T cells, B cells, monocytes, and natural killer T cells, following their activation in response to inflammatory signals. PD-1 has two known ligands, programmed death-ligand-1 (PD-L1) and programmed death-ligand-2 (PD-L2), which are expressed on antigen-presenting cells, including dendritic cells. PD-L1, which is also expressed on some nonhematopoietic cells, is believed to be the primary mediator of PD-1-dependent immunosuppression. The PD-1 pathway regulates the balance between T-cell activation and the protection of healthy tissues from immune-mediated damage. In tumor cells, the PD-1 pathway is thought to play an important role in the interaction of tumor cells with the host immune response, and PD-L1 expression in a tumor cell may provide adaptive immune resistance and lead to a poor outcome. Nivolumab selectively binds to the PD-1 receptor and blocks its interaction with PD-L1 and PD-L2, which causes a release of the PD-1 pathway-mediated inhibition of the immune response. This prevents the negative regulatory signal mediated by the receptor (PD-1)-ligand interaction and thus promotes the host immune response in which the tumor cells are recognized as foreign and eliminated. Syngeneic mouse tumor models have shown that blocking the PD-1 pathway results in decreased growth of tumors.
Relatlimab is a human IgG4 monoclonal antibody that inhibits the lymphocyte activation gene-3 (LAG-3) immune checkpoint protein. LAG-3 is expressed on the surface of lymphocytes including CD4+ and CD8+ T-cells, natural killer T-cells, and regulatory T (Treg) cells; it is also stored in the lysosomes and may surface faster when T-cells are activated. Binding to the LAG-3 receptor prevents the interaction with its ligands (e.g., Major histocompatibility complex [MHC] II, fibrinogen-like protein [FGL] 1) leading to reduced LAG-3 pathway-mediated inhibition of the immune response and increased T cell proliferation and cytokine secretion.
The combination of nivolumab and relatlimab appears to have additive inhibitory effects on T-cell function and result in improved tumor response in melanoma. LAG-3 inhibition potentiated the anti-tumor activity of PD-1 inhibition in murine syngeneic tumor models resulting in less tumor growth and more tumor regression.

      Description
      Nivolumab; relatlimab is available as a clear to opalescent and colorless to slightly yellow solution single-dose vial containing 240 mg of nivolumab and 80 mg of relatlimab; do not freeze or shake.
Administer as a diluted or undiluted solution; the maximum infusion volume depends on the patient population.
Preparation:
Withdraw the required volume of drug and transfer into an intravenous container (i.e., di 2-ethylhexyl phthalate [DEHP]-plasticized polyvinyl chloride [PVC], ethyl vinyl acetate [EVA], or polyolefin [PO] IV bag).
If diluting, add either 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection or 5% Dextrose Injection to prepare an infusion admixture.
The final concentration is 3 to 12 mg/mL of nivolumab and 1 to 4 mg/mL relatlimab; the maximum infusion volume is 160 mL in adult or pediatric patients 12 years of age or older who weigh 40 kg or more and 4 mL/kg in adult patients who weigh less than 40 kg.
Mix the diluted solution by gentle inversion; do not shake.
Discard partially used or empty vials.
Storage of prepared solution: When stored at room temperature, use within 8 hours from the time of preparation to end of the infusion. When stored under refrigeration (2 to 8 degrees C; 36 to 46 degrees F), use within 24 hours from the time of preparation to end of infusion (includes time to equilibration to room temperature); protect from light and do not freeze.
Intravenous (IV) Infusion:
Administer as an IV infusion over 30 minutes through a line containing a sterile, non-pyrogenic, low protein binding in-line polyethersulfone (PES), nylon, or polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) filter (pore size of 0.2 to 1.2 micrometer).
Do not coadminister with other drugs through the same IV line.
Flush the IV line at the end of infusion.

Dosage Adjustment Guidelines
  Hepatic Impairment
  Treatment-Related Immune-Mediated Hepatitis
AST or ALT more than 3 to 8 times the upper limit of normal (ULN) or total bilirubin more than 1.5 to 3 times ULN: Hold nivolumab; relatlimab and administer corticosteroids (i.e., prednisone 1 to 2 mg/kg per day or equivalent, followed by at least a 1-month taper). Resume therapy when the adverse event recovers to grade 1 or less after the corticosteroid taper.
AST or ALT more than 8 times ULN (regardless of baseline level) or total bilirubin more than 3 times ULN: Permanently discontinue nivolumab; relatlimab and administer corticosteroids.
  Renal Impairment
  Treatment-Related Immune-Mediated Nephritis with Renal Dysfunction
Grade 2 or 3 increased SCr: Hold nivolumab; relatlimab and administer corticosteroids (i.e., prednisone 1 to 2 mg/kg per day or equivalent, followed by at least a 1-month taper). Resume therapy when the adverse event recovers to grade 1 or less after the corticosteroid taper.
Grade 4 increased SCr: Permanently discontinue nivolumab; relatlimab and administer corticosteroids.
