Drug Class: Docetaxel is an antimicrotubule chemotherapy agent belonging to the taxoid family and is similar to paclitaxel in structure, mechanism of action, and spectrum of antitumor activity.

Routes: injection (IV/IM/SC); Common adult doses: 100 mg/m2 IV over 1 hour every 3 weeks as a single agent; doses of 100 mg/m2 IV have been associated with toxic deaths in patients with breast and lung cancer. Doses up to 40 mg/m2 have been given on a weekly basis.

Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  General Information
  The incidence of treatment-related mortality associated with docetaxel therapy is increased in patients with abnormal liver function, in patients receiving higher docetaxel doses, and in patients with non-small cell lung cancer, and a history of prior treatment with platinum-based chemotherapy who receive docetaxel as a single agent at a dose of 100 mg/m2.Docetaxel should be used cautiously in patients who have had previous myelosuppressive therapy such as chemotherapy or radiation therapy. Frequent monitoring of peripheral blood cell counts is necessary in patients receiving docetaxel in order to monitor for bone marrow suppression, including neutropenia and thrombocytopenia; docetaxel should not be administered to patients with severe neutropenia ( 1500/mm3 and platelets recover to a level > 100,000/mm3. A 25% reduction in the docetaxel dose is recommended during subsequent cycles following severe neutropenia (= 7 days, febrile neutropenia, or a grade 4 infection. Addition of colony-stimulating factors has also been recommended in patients who experience severe neutropenia, especially when docetaxel is given in combination with other agents such as cisplatin and 5-fluorouracil (see Dosage). Docetaxel is a radiation sensitizer. Concurrent administration of docetaxel and radiation therapy may increase both the efficacy and toxicity of the radiation treatment. Docetaxel should be used only by clinicians experienced in the use of chemotherapy and/or radiation therapy. Patients with an active infection should receive appropriate therapy prior to being treated with docetaxel. Patients with a history of varicella zoster, other herpes infection (e.g., herpes simplex), or other viral infection are at risk for reactivation of the infection when treated with chemotherapy.Docetaxel should not be used in patients with hepatic disease, including biliary tract disease or jaundice, as severe life-threatening toxicities may occur due to decreased metabolism of docetaxel, despite decreased doses; an increased incidence of treatment related mortality may occur with docetaxel therapy in patients with abnormal liver function. Severe adverse reactions include including severe stomatitis, dermatological reactions, fluid retention, infections, and thrombocytopenia as well as severe neutropenia, febrile neutropenia, and toxic death (see Adverse Reactions). Bilirubin, SGOT, SGPT, and alkaline phosphatase values should be obtained and reviewed by the treating healthcare professional prior to each cycle of docetaxel therapy. In general, docetaxel should not be given to patients with bilirubin concentrations greater than the upper limit of normal (ULN) or to patients with SGOT and/or SGPT greater than 1.5 times the ULN concomitant with alkaline phosphatase > 2.5 times the ULN. Patients with elevations of bilirubin or abnormalities of transaminase concentrations with concurrent with alkaline phosphatase are at increased risk for the development of grade 4 neutropenia, febrile neutropenia, infections, severe thrombocytopenia, severe stomatitis, severe skin toxicity, and toxic death. Patients with isolated elevations of transaminase > 1.5 x ULN also had a higher rate of febrile neutropenia grade 4 but did not have an increased incidence of toxic death. Determine and review bilirubin, SGOT or SGPT, and alkaline phosphatase concentrations before each cycle of docetaxel therapy.Docetaxel is contraindicated in patients who have a history of severe docetaxel hypersensitivity, polysorbate 80 hypersensitivity; patients with a history of other taxane hypersensitivity should be given docetaxel with caution. Patients should be observed closely for hypersensitivity reactions, especially during the first and second infusions. Severe hypersensitivity reactions (e.g., acute bronchospasm, generalized rash/erythema, hypotension, or very rarely fatal anaphylaxis) have occurred despite 3-day corticosteroid premedication (see Adverse Reactions). If a women becomes pregnant while receiving this drug, she should be counseled of the potential harm to the fetus and the possibility of loss of pregnancy.Adverse effects to the nursing infant are unknown. Patients should be instructed to either discontinue breast-feeding or discontinue docetaxel therapy.Use care to avoid accidental exposure to docetaxel during preparation, handling and administration. The administration of live vaccines to immunocompromised patients should be avoided. Those undergoing chemotherapy should not be exposed to others who have recently received the oral poliovirus vaccine (OPV). Measles-mumps-rubella (MMR) vaccination is not contraindicated for the close contacts, including health care professionals, of immunocompromised patients.   Contraindications And Precautions
    Headline
    Drug Interactions
    General Information
    The coadministration of certain medications may lead to harm and require avoidance or therapy modification; review all drug interactions prior to concomitant use of other medications.
    Headline
    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of severe hypersensitivity to it or any of its components. Some formulations are also contraindicated in patients with a history of severe hypersensitivity to other medications formulated with polysorbate 80. Closely monitor patients with a previous history of hypersensitivity to paclitaxel; cross-hypersensitivity including anaphylaxis has been reported.
    Headline
    serious hypersensitivity reactions or anaphylaxis
    General Information
    Serious hypersensitivity reactions or anaphylaxis have been reported in patients treated with docetaxel. Observe patients closely, especially during the first and second infusions; reactions may occur within a few minutes following initiation of an infusion. Immediately discontinue the docetaxel infusion if a hypersensitivity reaction occurs; interruption of therapy is not required for minor reactions such as flushing or localized skin reactions occur. Premedicate all patients with an oral corticosteroid prior to initiation of a docetaxel infusion; despite premedication, severe hypersensitivity reactions have still been reported despite 3 days of dexamethasone premedication.
    Headline
    infection, neutropenia
    General Information
    Docetaxel is contraindicated in patients with neutropenia (ANC less than 1,500 cells/mm3). Use docetaxel with caution in patients with an active infection; treatment can result in myelosuppression which may increase the severity of an infection and impede recovery. Neutropenic patients may also be at increased risk for developing gastrointestinal complications including enterocolitis and neutropenic enterocolitis. Monitor complete blood counts at baseline and prior to each cycle of therapy. An interruption of therapy, dose reduction, or discontinuation of therapy may be necessary for patients with severe and prolonged neutropenia, febrile neutropenia, or severe infection.
    Headline
    hepatic failure, hepatotoxicity, hyperbilirubinemia
    General Information
    Hepatotoxicity associated with docetaxel may cause an increase in treatment-related mortality. Avoid the use of docetaxel in patients with hyperbilirubinemia (bilirubin greater than the upper limit of normal [ULN]) and in patients with an AST and/or ALT greater than 1.5 times ULN with a concomitant alkaline phosphatase greater than 2.5 times the upper limit of normal; patients with isolated transaminitis (AST/ALT greater than 1.5 times ULN) may be considered for a dose reduction. Patients with elevated bilirubin levels or increased transaminases with concurrent increases in alkaline phosphatase are at increased risk for the development of severe neutropenia, febrile neutropenia, infections, severe thrombocytopenia, severe stomatitis, severe skin toxicity, and toxic death. Patients with severe hepatic impairment or hepatic failure have not been studied. Consider the alcohol content of some docetaxel formulations when given to patients with hepatic impairment. Measure liver function tests at baseline and prior to each cycle of docetaxel.
    Headline
    fluid retention, pericardial effusion, pleural effusion
    General Information
    Closely monitor patients with a pre-existing pericardial effusion or pleural effusion from the first dose of docetaxel. Severe fluid retention (e.g., poorly tolerated peripheral edema, generalized edema, pleural effusion requiring urgent drainage, dyspnea at rest, cardiac tamponade, and/or pronounced abdominal distention due to ascites) has been reported with docetaxel therapy and effusions may be exacerbated.
    Headline
    activities requiring coordination and concentration, driving or operating machinery
    General Information
    Most docetaxel injection formulations contain ethanol to dissolve the active ingredients; take this into account for patients in whom ethanol ingestion should be avoided or minimized. The amount of alcohol varies between products; consult individual product labeling for alcohol content. Dosing has resulted in reports of ethanol intoxication or feeling drunk during and after treatment. Slowing the rate of infusion may help to resolve symptoms of alcohol intoxication. Patients should give consideration to the effect of the alcohol content in docetaxel on driving or operating machinery immediately after the infusion. Advise patients to use caution when driving or operating machinery or performing other dangerous duties or activities requiring coordination and concentration immediately after a docetaxel infusion due to some formulations containing alcohol and the potential for altered or decreased consciousness or coordination.   An alcohol-free docetaxel formulation is available.
    Headline
    mortality
    General Information
    Increased treatment-related mortality has been reported with docetaxel therapy at doses of 75 mg/m2 and 100 mg/m2 in patients with locally advanced or metastatic non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) and a history of prior platinum-based chemotherapy. Doses of 60 mg/m2 and 100 mg/m2 were also associated with deaths possibly or probably related to treatment of metastatic breast cancer patients with normal baseline liver function, both previously treated and untreated, and in patients with various tumor types who had abnormal baseline liver function. Approximately half of these deaths occurred during the first cycle; sepsis accounted for the majority of deaths.
    Headline
    children, infants
    General Information
    The alcohol content of docetaxel injection should be taken into account when administered to infants and children; an alcohol-free formulation of docetaxel is available.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Docetaxel may cause fetal harm when administered during pregnancy, based on its mechanism of action and findings from animal studies. An increased incidence of intrauterine mortality, resorptions, reduced fetal weights, and fetal ossification delays occurred when docetaxel was given to pregnant rats and rabbits during organogenesis at doses 0.02 and 0.003 times, respectively, the maximum recommended human dose based on body surface area. Additionally, most docetaxel injection formulations contain alcohol, which has been independently associated with fetal harm, including central nervous system abnormalities, behavioral disorders, and impaired intellectual development. The American Society of Clinical Oncology provides additional information on the use of certain medications for the management of cancer during pregnancy.
    Headline
    people who can cause pregnancy in others, people who may become pregnant, reproductive risk
    General Information
    Counsel people who may become pregnant and people who can cause pregnancy in others about the reproductive risk associated with docetaxel. Docetaxel can cause fetal harm. Verify pregnancy status prior to treatment initiation. Advise people who may become pregnant to use effective contraception while taking docetaxel and for at least 2 months after the last dose. For people who can cause pregnancy in others, contraception should be used while taking docetaxel and for 4 months after the last dose. Based on animal data, docetaxel may cause male infertility.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Advise patients to avoid breast-feeding while taking docetaxel and for 1 week after the last dose, due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in the breastfed child. There are no data on the presence of docetaxel in human milk, its effects on the breastfed child, or its effects on milk production.

Erythromycin: (Minor) Docetaxel is metabolized by cytochrome P450 3A (CYP3A4 and CYP3A5) enzymes. Erythromycin is a CYP3A4 inhibitor. In vitro studies have shown drugs that inhibit, induce, or are also metabolized by CYP3A enzymes can significantly affect the metabolism of docetaxel. In a small pharmacokinetic study, 7 patients received 2 courses of docetaxel, one with concurrent ketoconazole (docetaxel 10 mg/m2) and one without ketoconazole (docetaxel 100mg/m2). The ketoconazole dosage was 200 mg once daily for 3 days. Concurrent administration of ketoconazole decreased the clearance of docetaxel by 49% as compared to giving docetaxel alone. However, there was large interpatient variability in the reduction in clearance. Use docetaxel cautiously when administered concurrently with inducers or inhibitors of CYP3A enzymes.

  General Information
  Reversible bone marrow suppression, specifically neutropenia, is the major dose-limiting toxicity of docetaxel. Other hematological toxicities associated with docetaxel therapy include leukopenia, anemia, and thrombocytopenia. Docetaxel-induced neutropenia is not cumulative. Almost all patients developed neutropenia ( 7 days, febrile neutropenia, or grade 4 infection in a docetaxel cycle. Anemia and thrombocytopenia occur much less frequently; severe anemia (hemoglobin 1.7-times the upper limit of normal). Disseminated intravascular coagulation often in association with sepsis or multiorgan failure has been reported post-marketing. In patients receiving docetaxel, doxorubicin, and cyclophosphamide (TAC) during the clinical trial (n=744), neutropenia was noted in 71.4% of patients and grade 3/4 neutropenia occurred in 65.5% of patients. Anemia (91.5%) and thrombocytopenia (39.4%) were also reported in patients receiving TAC; although grade 3/4 reactions were less common (4.3% and 2%, respectively). Fever in the absence of infection was reported in more patients receiving TAC (46.5%) vs. fluorouracil, doxorubicin, and cyclophosphamide (FAC) (17.1%). Infection was reported at similar incidence in both treatment arms, TAC 39.4% and FAC 36.3%. No septic deaths were reported in either arm of this study.Cutaneous reactions occur in almost 50% of patients receiving docetaxel, but are severe in only 5% of patients with normal liver function. Reversible reactions are characterized by a vesicular rash with localized eruptions and pruritus, mainly on the feet and/or hands but also on the arms, face, or thorax. These reactions usually occur within 1 week of docetaxel administration and resolve prior to the next cycle. An erythematous injection site reaction is common. Erythema is often noted at sites of bandages, pads, or tape. Other injection site reactions include skin hyperpigmentation, inflammation, redness or dryness of the skin (xerosis), phlebitis, and swelling. There are reports of severe reactions including local erythema with swelling and exfoliative dermatitis. Rare cases of bullous rash such as erythema multiforme or Stevens-Johnson syndrome have also occurred. Multiple factors may have contributed to the development of these effects. In patients with hepatic dysfunction, severe cutaneous reactions occur in about 10% of patients. With severe reactions, adjustment of the docetaxel dose is required. Among 92 breast cancer patients who were premedicated with corticosteroids for 3 days, there were no cases of severe skin toxicity reported. Nail discoloration consisting of hypo- or hyperpigmentation or occasional onycholysis may be reported and may be associated with pain. Subungual hemorrhage has been reported. Alopecia occurs in 80% of patients. Radiation recall reaction has been reported in clinical trials and/or post-marketing surveillance.Hypersensitivity reactions (21%) may occur within minutes of beginning a docetaxel infusion; however, a reduced incidence of these reactions occurs with appropriate premedication with corticosteroids. All patients should be premedicated with an oral corticosteroid before the initiation of the docetaxel infusion (see Administration). Minor hypersensitivity reactions include flushing, rash with or without pruritus, chest tightness, back pain, dyspnea, fever, and chills. Despite appropriate premedication, severe hypersensitivity reactions characterized by hypotension and/or bronchospasm, or generalized rash (unspecified) with erythema occurred in 5—15% of patients during clinical trials. Fatal anaphylaxis (anaphylactic shock or anaphylactoid reactions) occurred very rarely. Severe hypersensitivity reactions have been reported in patients premedicated with 3 days of corticosteroids. Observe patients closely for hypersensitivity reactions, especially during the first or second infusions. If minor reactions such as flushing or localized skin reactions occur, interruption of therapy is not required. Immediately discontinue the infusion for severe reactions. Do not give a docetaxel rechallenge to a patient who has a severe hypersensitivity reaction.Severe fluid retention (edema) occurs in 6.5% of patients receiving docetaxel despite corticosteroid premedication, and may worsen existing peripheral edema, generalized edema, pleural effusion, dyspnea at rest, cardiac tamponade, or ascites. Patients with fluid retention may be treated with standard measures (e.g., salt restriction, oral diuretics). Docetaxel appears to induce enhanced fluid filtration followed by a capillary leak syndrome leading to edema. Fluid retention usually starts as peripheral edema in the lower extremities or postmastectomy-side arm and may become generalized. The median weight gain is roughly 2 kg. Severe fluid retention is characterized by poorly tolerated peripheral edema, generalized edema, pleural effusion requiring urgent drainage, dyspnea at rest, cardiac tamponade, or pronounced abdominal distention due to increased ascites. The use of corticosteroid premedication reduces the severity and delays the onset of moderate to severe fluid retention. In breast cancer patients receiving premedication with dexamethasone for 3 days, moderate fluid retention occurred in 27% of patients and severe retention in 6.5% of patients. The median cumulative dose to onset of moderate to severe fluid retention was 819 mg/m2. The cumulative docetaxel dose associated with treatment discontinuation due to edema is 1021 mg/m2. Resolution of fluid retention may take up to 16 weeks following completion of docetaxel therapy.Neurologic toxicity occurs about 50% of patients receiving single agent docetaxel, but is severe in only 5%. A moderate peripheral neuropathy, asthenia, dysesthesia, pain, burning sensations, and paresthesias characterize docetaxel-induced neurotoxicity. Adjust the dose of docetaxel for severe reactions or if symptoms persist, discontinue treatment. Symptoms usually resolve within 9 weeks from onset. Asthenia occurs in 62% of patients and is severe in about 14.9% of patients with normal liver function. It has lead to discontinuation of treatment in 1.8% of patients. In patients with liver dysfunction, severe asthenia occurs in roughly 25% of patients. Symptoms of fatigue and weakness may last from a few days to several weeks and may be associated with deterioration of performance status in patients with progressive disease. The neuromotor toxicity consisting mainly of distal extremity weakness occurs in about 4% of patients. Rare cases of seizures or transient loss of consciousness have been observed, sometimes appearing during the infusion of the drug. Arthralgia occurs in about 9% of patients. Myalgia occurs in 18—21% of patients and is severe in upper limits of normal (ULN) occurred in 9% of patients receiving docetaxel. Elevated hepatic enzymes defined as SGOT or SGPT > 1.5 times the ULN or alkaline phosphatase > 2.5 times ULN were observed in 18.9% and 7.3% of patients. Whether these changes are due to docetaxel or the underlying disease has not been established. Rare cases of hepatitis have been reported. In metastatic breast cancer trials including previously treated and untreated patients, docetaxel 100 mg/m2 was associated with deaths considered possibly or probably related to treatment in 2% of patients with normal baseline liver function and 11.5% of patients with abnormal liver function. Approximately half of these deaths occurred during the first cycle. Sepsis accounted for the majority of deaths. In previously-treated patients with locally advanced or metastatic non-small cell lung cancer, docetaxel 100 mg/m2 was associated with increased treatment-related mortality (14% and 5% in two trials vs. 2.8% for patients receiving 75 mg/m2).As opposed to paclitaxel, cardiac arrhythmias including atrial flutter, sinus tachycardia, or other dysrhythmias occur rarely with docetaxel. Heart failure, unstable angina, pulmonary edema and hypertension are also uncommon. Hypotension occurs in 3.6% of patients treated with single agent docetaxel. Other cardiovascular events reported in clinical trials and/or post-marketing surveillance include atrial fibrillation, chest pain (unspecified), deep venous thrombosis, pulmonary embolism, syncope, myocardial infarction, tachycardia, and ECG abnormalities. In a clinical trial (n=744), patients receiving docetaxel, doxorubicin, and cyclophosphamide (TAC) reported more cardiovascular events than patients treated with fluorouracil, doxorubicin, and cyclophosphamide; dysrhythmias (7.9% vs. 6%), hypotension (2.6% vs 1.1%), and congestive heart failure (2.3% vs 0.9%). One patient in each arm of this study died due to heart failure. Additional cardiovascular reactions reported in patients receiving TAC include syncope (1.6%) and vasodilation (27%).Conjunctivitis (5%), lacrimation (9.9—11.3%), or lacrimation with or without conjunctivitis have been reported during treatment with docetaxel. Excessive tearing, which may be attributable to lacrimal duct obstruction, has been reported. Rare cases of transient visual impairment/disturbances (flashes, flashing lights, scotomata) typically occurring during the drug infusion and associated with hypersensitivity reactions have been reported. These were reversible upon discontinuation of the infusion.Pulmonary reactions reported during clinical trials and/or post-marketing surveillance include dyspnea, acute pulmonary edema, acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS), and interstitial pneumonia. Pulmonary fibrosis has been reported rarely.In patients receiving docetaxel, doxorubicin, and cyclophosphamide (TAC) during the clinical trial for the adjuvant treatment of node-positive breast cancer (n=744), amenorrhea was reported in 61.7% of patients receiving TAC.Secondary malignancy, specifically treatment-induced acute myeloid leukemia (AML) or myelodysplasia, can occur in patients treated with anthracyclines and/or cyclophosphamide. AML was reported in the clinical trial examining the docetaxel, doxorubicin, and cyclophosphamide regimen (TAC) for adjuvant breast cancer therapy. The cumulative risk of developing treatment-related AML at 5 years was 0.4% in TAC-treated patients and 0.1% in patients treated with fluorouracil, doxorubicin, and cyclophosphamide. The risk of AML is comparable to the risk observed for other anthracycline/cyclophosphamide-containing adjuvant regimens for breast cancer. In the docetaxel, doxorubicin, and cyclophosphamide treated patients, the risk of delayed myelodysplasia or myeloid leukemia requires hematological follow-up. Very care cases of AML and myelodysplastic syndrome have been reported in association with docetaxel when used in combination with other chemotherapy agents and/or radiotherapy.

Description
  Text
  Docetaxel is an antimicrotubule chemotherapy agent belonging to the taxoid family and is similar to paclitaxel in structure, mechanism of action, and spectrum of antitumor activity. It is a semi-synthetic taxane prepared with a precursor extracted from yew needles. Nonhematologic toxicities differ between docetaxel and paclitaxel; fluid retention and skin lesions are more severe with docetaxel. Docetaxel is FDA approved for the treatment of breast cancer, non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), castration-resistant prostate cancer, gastric cancer, and squamous cell cancer of the head and neck; it also has several off-label indications. Treatment-related mortality is increased in patients with abnormal liver function, in patients receiving higher doses, and at a monotherapy dose of 100 mg/m2, in patients with NSCLC and a history of prior treatment with platinum-based chemotherapy. It should not be given to patients with increased liver function tests or in patients with neutrophil counts less than 1,500 cells/mm3. All patients should be premedicated with oral corticosteroids prior to each dose of docetaxel to reduce the incidence and severity of hypersensitivity, fluid retention, and skin toxicity.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Docetaxel is an antimicrotubule chemotherapy agent. It is a taxane derivative synthesized from 10-deacetyl baccatin III, a noncytotoxic substance extracted from the needles of the European yew tree (Taxus baccata). Docetaxel promotes the assembly of microtubules and stabilizes their formation by inhibiting depolymerization. These microtubules are extremely stable and nonfunctional. Docetaxel also promotes tubulin stabilization in conditions where polymerization would not normally occur and stabilizes microtubules in the presence of cold temperatures. Cell death results from inhibition of normal microtubule processes. The cytotoxic effect of docetaxel is the prevention of effective cell division. Docetaxel specifically blocks critical components in S-phase that are essential for mitosis. Gross damage is detectable after mitosis, with dysfunction in cytokinesis and accumulation of multinucleated non-viable cells. Docetaxel affects centrosome organization in S-phase, resulting in incomplete mitosis and cell death. It is only partially toxic against cells in mitosis and has minimal toxicity against cells in G1. This leads to an accumulation of cells at the G2/M phase.

    In a population pharmacokinetic analysis, total body clearance of docetaxel was decreased by an average of 27% in patients with mild to moderate liver impairment (AST/ALT greater than 1.5 times upper limit of normal (ULN) and alkaline phosphatase greater than 2.5 times ULN), resulting in a 38% increase in systemic exposure (AUC).   Text
  Docetaxel is administered by intravenous infusion. Docetaxel is 94% to 97% protein bound, primarily to alpha1-acid glycoprotein, albumin, and lipoproteins. A three-compartment model best describes docetaxel elimination, with an initial rapid distribution phase and late (terminal) phase. The mean steady-state volume of distribution (Vd) was 113 liters. The estimated mean total body clearance is 18 liters/hour/m2 and the mean terminal elimination half-life is 116 hours. Within 7 days of administration of radiolabeled docetaxel to 3 cancer patients, urinary excretion accounted for 6% and fecal excretion 75% of the administered radioactivity. Approximately 80% of the radioactivity recovered in feces is excreted during the first 48 hours as one major and 3 minor metabolites; less than 8% was recovered as unchanged drug.
 
Affected cytochrome (CYP) 450 isoenzymes and drug transporters: CYP3A4
Docetaxel is metabolized by CYP3A4 in vitro. All 4 principle metabolites of docetaxel have greatly reduced cytotoxic activity against cancer cell lines in vitro and in vivo.

  Text
  The suggested maximum tolerated dose (MTD) for docetaxel is dependent on performance status, other chemotherapy agents or radiation given in combination, and disease state. The optimal dose or infusion duration of docetaxel has not been determined. Therefore, dosing may vary from protocol to protocol. If questions arise, clinicians should consult the appropriate references to verify the dose.
