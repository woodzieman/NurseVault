    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components.
    Headline
    renal failure
    General Information
    Nerandomilast is not recommended in individuals with renal failure (eGFR less than 15 mL/minute/1.73 m2). The use of nerandomilast has not been investigated in these individuals.
    Headline
    Child-Pugh class C, hepatic failure
    General Information
    Nerandomilast is not recommended for use in individuals with severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh Class C) or hepatic failure. The use of nerandomilast has not been investigated in these individuals. No dosage adjustment is needed in individuals with mild (Child-Pugh Class A) or moderate (Child-Pugh Class B) hepatic impairment.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    There are no available data on the use of nerandomilast in pregnancy to evaluate the drug associated risk of major birth defects, miscarriage or other adverse maternal or fetal outcomes. Based on animal reproductive studies, there may be an increase in the risk for fetal loss. Oral administration of nerandomilast to pregnant rats during organogenesis at an exposure 5 times the maximum recommended human dose (MRHD) of 36 mg/day resulted in an increase in embryo-fetal loss. Maternal toxicity, as evidenced by decreased body weight gains and adverse clinical signs, was observed at exposures approximately 7 times the MRHD. No fetal or maternal toxicities were observed at exposures up to 3 to 5 times the MRHD. Embryo-fetal development studies in pregnant rabbits showed no effects on maternal or fetal development at an exposure that was approximately 4 times the MRHD. Advise pregnant people of the potential risk of fetal loss.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    There are no data on the presence of nerandomilast or its metabolite in human milk, the effects on the breastfed infant, or the effects on milk production. Nerandomilast is present in animal milk. The developmental and health of breast-feeding should be considered along with the mother's clinical need for nerandomilast and any potential adverse effects on the breastfed infant or from the underlying maternal condition.

    anorexia, diarrhea, nausea, vomiting, weight loss
    General Information
    Diarrhea (16% to 42%) was the most common gastrointestinal (GI) adverse reaction reported in individuals receiving nerandomilast during clinical trials. Diarrhea was generally mild to moderate in intensity, typically occurred within the first 3 months of treatment, and was dose related, with a higher incidence observed with the 18 mg twice-daily dose compared with the 9 mg twice-daily dose. Diarrhea was more common with concomitant nintedanib (up to 62%) than with concomitant pirfenidone (up to 24%) and was the most frequent adverse reaction leading to treatment discontinuation (2% to 6%), with lower discontinuation rates observed in individuals not receiving concomitant antifibrotic therapy. Description
  Text
  Nerandomilast is an oral phosphodiesterase 4B (PDE4B) inhibitor indicated for idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) and progressive pulmonary fibrosis (PPF). In clinical trials, nerandomilast significantly reduced the decline in forced vital capacity (FVC) over 52 weeks in individuals with idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) and progressive pulmonary fibrosis (PPF) compared with placebo, with numerically greater effects observed with the 18 mg twice-daily dose. However, nerandomilast did not demonstrate statistically significant improvements in the composite outcome of time to first acute IPF exacerbation, time to first interstitial lung disease exacerbation, hospitalization for respiratory causes, or death. The most common adverse reactions include diarrhea, COVID-19 infection, upper respiratory tract infection, and decrease in weight or appetite. Nerandomilast was FDA-approved in October 2025.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Nerandomilast is an inhibitor of phosphodiesterase 4 (PDE4). It has a nine-fold preferential inhibition of PDE4B isoenzyme over PDE4A, PDE4C, and PDE4D based on in vitro data. This preference limits the adverse events associated with pan-PDE4 inhibitor without compromising efficacy. PDE4 hydrolyzes and inactivates cyclic adenosine monophosphate (cAMP). Nerandomilast exerts both anti-fibrotic and immunomodulatory effects as PDE4B inhibition elevates intracellular cAMP levels and reduces the expression of pro-fibrotic growth factors and inflammatory cytokines, which are overexpressed in idiopathic and progressive pulmonary fibrosis.
Pharmacodynamics
Increases in nerandomilast steady state trough concentrations were associated with better efficacy, as indicated by a smaller reduction in forced vital capacity (FVC) from baseline over 52 weeks.

    Nerandomilast exposure increases in a dose proproptional manner. A high fat meal (1,000 calories, 50% fat) did not significantly alter the pharmacokinetics of nerandomilast.   Nerandomilast is administered orally. In vitro, nerandomilast is 77% protein bound and is not concentration dependent. The blood to plasma ratio is approximately 0.6 to 0.8. The central Vd is 93 L. Nerandomilast is primarily metabolized by CYP3A and to a lesser extent by multiple UGT enzymes. The predominate metabolite is the active R-enantiomer and the minor metabolite is the S-enantiomer of nerandomilast. After a single oral dose of radiolabeled nerandomilast, approximately 58% of the dose was recovered in feces (14% as unchanged) and 36% of the dose was recovered in urine (13% as unchanged). The apparent oral clearance is 15.2 L/hour. The elimination half-life is 17 hours.
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes and drug transporters: CYP3A4, P-gp
Nerandomilast is a CYP3A4 and a P-gp substrate. Nerandomilast is not a substrate of BCRP, OATP1B1, OATP1B3, OAT1, OAT3, and OCT2. Nerandomilast does not inhibit CYP3A4, CYP1A2, CYP2B6, CYP2C8, CYP2C9, CYP2C19, CYP2D6, BCRP, OAT1, OAT3, OCT2, OATP1B1, or OATP1B3. Nerandomilast is an inhibitor of P-gp, MATE1, MATE2-K, but is not expected to cause clinically significant interactions.
