Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  General Information
  Felodipine is structurally related to nifedipine and is contraindicated in patients with known serious dihydropyridine hypersensitivity.
 
Felodipine is an FDA pregnancy risk category C drug. Felodipine's effects during pregnancy have not been adequately studied in humans, but fetal anomalies, labor complications, and enlarged mammary glands have occurred in animal models. If felodipine is used during pregnancy, or if the patient becomes pregnant while taking this drug, she should be apprised of the potential hazard to the fetus, possible digital anomalies of the infant, and the potential effects of felodipine on labor and delivery and on the mammary glands of pregnant females.
 
Use caution when administering felodipine to a woman who is breast-feeding her infant. It is not known whether felodipine administered as monotherapy is secreted in human milk; however, studies of the combination of enalapril and felodipine in rats indicate that felodipine concentrates in milk to a level almost ten-fold that found in plasma. Also, enlarged mammary glands have occurred in animals exposed to felodipine during gestation; it is unclear if this effect would have an effect on lactation.
 
The safety and effectiveness of felodipine in children have not been established.
 
Felodipine decreases peripheral resistance and can worsen hypotension. Felodipine should not be used in patients with systolic blood pressures of less than 90 mm Hg (i.e., severe hypotension). Felodipine should be used with caution in patients with mild to moderate hypotension. Blood pressure should be monitored carefully in all patients receiving felodipine.
 
Risk-benefit should be assessed when considering the use of felodipine in patients with bradycardia, heart failure, left ventricular dysfunction, acute myocardial infarction with pulmonary congestion, or cardiogenic shock because the potential negative inotropic effects of the drug can worsen these conditions.
 
Felodipine may also cause reflex tachycardia which in susceptible individuals (e.g., severe obstructive coronary artery disease) could precipitate or worsen angina pectoris or ischemic heart disease. Patients should be monitored for worsening angina when felodipine therapy is begun.
 
Felodipine should be used with caution in patients with aortic stenosis or hypertrophic cardiomyopathy. Felodipine is relatively contraindicated in patients with advanced aortic stenosis because the drug can worsen the abnormal valve pressure gradient associated with this condition.
 
Hepatic dysfunction can reduce the clearance of felodipine by approximately 60%. Felodipine should be used cautiously or in lower doses in patients with hepatic disease. Renal impairment does not change felodipine concentrations, but accumulation of inactive metabolites may occur. The elderly can experience a delayed clearance of felodipine and can be at greater risk for accumulation and toxicity. Elderly patients may be more sensitive to the usual dosage of felodipine and require dosage reduction (see Dosage section).
 
Calcium channel blockers should be used cautiously in patients with gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD) or hiatal hernia associated with reflux esophagitis. The drugs relax the lower esophageal sphincter.

    Use felodipine with caution in individuals with heart failure or left ventricular dysfunction, particularly in combination with a beta-blocker.     This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components.

    A lower felodipine starting dose is recommended in individuals with hepatic failure; monitor blood pressure closely during dosage adjustment of felodipine in these individuals.     General Information
    Felodipine, like other calcium channel blockers, may occasionally precipitate significant hypotension and, rarely, syncope. It may lead to reflex tachycardia, which in susceptible individuals (e.g., severe obstructive coronary artery disease) may precipitate angina pectoris.
    Headline
    geriatric
    General Information
    Use cautious dose selection for felodipine in geriatric adults, usually starting at the low end of the dosing range, reflecting the greater frequency of decreased hepatic, renal, or cardiac function, and of concomitant disease or other drug therapy.
    Headline
    labor, obstetric delivery, pregnancy
    General Information
    There are no adequate and well-controlled studies on felodipine use during human pregnancy. Advise pregnant individuals of the potential hazard to the fetus, possible digital anomalies of the infant, and the potential effects on labor and obstetric delivery and on the mammary glands of pregnant individuals. Studies in pregnant rabbits given felodipine doses 0.8 to 8 times the maximum recommended human dose (MRHD) on a mg/m2 basis showed digital anomalies consisting of reduction in size and degree of ossification of the terminal phalanges in the fetuses. The frequency and severity of the changes appeared dose-related and were noted even at the lowest dose. These changes have been shown to occur with other members of the dihydropyridine class and are possibly a result of compromised uterine blood flow. Similar fetal anomalies were not observed in rats given felodipine. In a teratology study in cynomolgus monkeys, no reduction in the size of the terminal phalanges was observed, but an abnormal position of the distal phalanges was noted in about 40% of the fetuses. A prolongation of parturition with difficult labor and an increased frequency of fetal and early postnatal deaths were observed in rats administered felodipine doses 8 times or more the MRHD on a mg/m2 basis. Significant enlargement of the mammary glands, in excess of the normal enlargement for pregnant rabbits, was found with felodipine doses 2.1 times the MRHD. This effect occurred only in pregnant rabbits and regressed during lactation. Similar changes in the mammary glands were not observed in rats or monkeys.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Use felodipine with caution during breast-feeding. There are no data on the presence of felodipine in human milk, its effects on the breast-fed child, or its effects on milk production. An alternative drug, such as nifedipine, may be preferred due to the lack of available data on felodipine use during breast-feeding.

Grapefruit juice inhibits the cytochrome P-450 3A4 isozyme in the gut wall. Grapefruit juice contains furanocoumarins that are metabolized by CYP3A4 to reactive intermediates. The pharmacokinetics are affected by the interval between the ingestion of grapefruit and the adminstration of felodipine. For example, the closer the time in between the ingestion of felodipine and the grapefruit juice, the greater the effect of the interaction. In addition, repeated ingestion of grapefruit (200 ml of juice, 3 times a day for 7 days) doubled the size of the interaction for 24 hours, consistent with a cumulative inhibitory action. Patient vulnerability to this interaction varies markedly, probably due the patient's levels of CYP3A4 in the small intestine. Elderly patients have the greatest possibility of ingesting grapefruit and interacting medications and are the most vulnerable to the adverse clinical consequences. According to the manufacturer of felodipine, caution should be used when coadministering felodipine with grapefruit juice. Description
  Text
  Felodipine is an oral calcium-channel blocker used in the treatment of hypertension. It is a member of the dihydropyridine class and is a potent peripheral vasodilator similar to nifedipine, isradipine, and amlodipine. Felodipine has greater selectivity for vascular smooth muscle relative to myocardial muscle than does nifedipine. Felodipine was approved by the FDA in July 1991 for the treatment of hypertension.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Similar to other calcium-channel antagonists, felodipine inhibits the influx of extracellular calcium across the myocardial and vascular smooth muscle cell membranes. Serum calcium levels remain unchanged. Felodipine inhibits this influx possibly by deforming the channel, inhibiting ion-control gating mechanisms, and/or interfering with the release of calcium from the sarcoplasmic reticulum. The resultant decrease in intracellular calcium inhibits the contractile processes of the smooth muscle cells, resulting in dilation of coronary and systemic arteries. These effects elicit an increased oxygen delivery to the myocardial tissue, a decreased total peripheral resistance, a decreased systemic blood pressure, and a decreased afterload.
 
Felodipine has no effect on atrioventricular (AV) or sinoatrial nodal conduction. Negative inotropic effects rarely are noted clinically, presumably due to a reflex increase in heart rate in response to felodipine's vasodilatory activity. Felodipine therapy usually does not affect cardiovascular parameters in patients with normal ventricular function, but patients with decreased left ventricular function can experience an increase in ejection fraction and a decrease in left ventricular filling pressures. Reduced afterload and reduced myocardial wall tension lead to reduced myocardial oxygen demand, which primarily accounts for the benefit of felodipine and other dihydropyridines in the treatment of angina. Felodipine causes vasodilation in coronary, skeletal, and cerebral vasculature.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Oral Route
    Description
    Felodipine is rapidly absorbed after an oral dose but undergoes extensive first-pass metabolism, resulting in a bioavailability of approximately 13% to 16%. Peak serum concentrations occur 2.5 to 5 hours after administration. Food increases the bioavailability of felodipine. When administered either with a high fat or carbohydrate diet, Cmax is increased by approximately 60%; however, the AUC is unchanged. Administration after a light meal has no effect on the pharmacokinetics. The bioavailability is increased approximately 2-fold when taken with grapefruit juice, with no change in the elimination half-life. Onset of action for felodipine occurs within 2 to 5 hours.

    Description
    Felodipine elimination is not significantly altered by renal impairment or hemodialysis. Renal impairment does not change felodipine concentrations. Felodipine is highly protein bound and is not likely to be removed significantly by hemodialysis.

    Pediatrics
    Description
    A pharmacokinetics study in 6 pediatric patients aged 9 to 16 years reported a median time to peak serum concentrations of 3.8 hours and a terminal half-life of 21.2 hours. Of note, cyclosporine was being taken concurrently in 5 of 6 patients at the time of the study; the 1 patient not receiving cyclosporine had a significantly shorter terminal half-life of 7.2 hours. Plasma concentrations of felodipine, after a single dose and at steady state, increase with age.
    Name
    Geriatric
    Description
    Plasma concentrations of felodipine, after a single dose and at steady state, increase with age. Mean clearance in elderly hypertensives (mean age 74 years) was about 45% of that for young volunteers (mean age 26 years). At steady state, the mean AUC for young patients was 39% of that for the elderly. Felodipine elimination is significantly altered by aging.
  Text
  Felodipine is administered orally. It is highly protein-bound (99%). It is not known whether the drug is distributed into human breast milk. Hepatic metabolism is complete and results in inactive compounds that are excreted in the urine (70%) and feces (10%). Less than 1% is eliminated renally. Elimination half-life is roughly 11 to 16 hours, and duration of action allows for once-daily dosing.
 
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes and drug transporters: CYP3A4
Felodipine is a substrate of CYP3A4. Coadministration with strong CYP3A4 inhibitors may lead to several-fold increases in felodipine plasma concentrations, either due to an increase in bioavailability and/or due to a decrease in drug metabolism; increased antihypertensive effects may result. Felodipine has been demonstrated to inhibit P-gp; however, clinically significant drug interactions are not expected based on an in vivo study.

Dosage Adjustment Guidelines
  Hepatic Impairment
  Reduce initial dosage to 2.5 mg PO once daily; dosage may be increased cautiously based on clinical response.
  Renal Impairment
  Specific guidelines for dosage adjustments in renal impairment are not available; it appears that no dosage adjustments are needed.
 
Intermittent hemodialysis
Felodipine is highly protein bound and is not likely to be significantly removed by hemodialysis.
