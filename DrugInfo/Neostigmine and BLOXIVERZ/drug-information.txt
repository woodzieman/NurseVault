Drug Class: Neostigmine is an oral and parenteral parasympathomimetic agent (cholinesterase inhibitor).

Routes: injection (IV/IM/SC); Common adult doses: 0.07 mg/kg or 5 mg total dose, whichever is less, IV; 1.5 mg/dose IM and 1.5 mg/day subcutaneously have been used off-label.

Common Side Effects (excluding routine GI upset): bronchial secretions, bronchospasm, cholinergic crisis, dyspnea, respiratory arrest, respiratory depression, lacrimation, miosis, visual impairment, hypersalivation, coma, dizziness, drowsiness, dysarthria, headache, insomnia, seizures, shivering, arrhythmia exacerbation, av block, bradycardia, cardiac arrest, hypotension, sinus tachycardia, syncope, arthralgia, muscle cramps, weakness, angioedema, erythema multiforme

In an Overdose: Severe adverse reactions, including those listed above, as well as respiratory paralysis (may result in respiratory depression or respiratory arrest) and cardiac arrest, can be seen in the cholinergic crisis caused by overdosage. Cholinergic crisis may result from neostigmine overdosage; it is characterized by increasing muscle weakness, and through involvement of the respiratory muscles, may result in death.

Key Nursing Considerations: Contraindications/precautions: Neostigmine is contraindicated in patients with known hypersensitivity to the drug. Because of the presence of the bromide ion, oral neostigmine bromide tablets are contraindicated in patients with a known bromide hypersensitivity.

Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  General Information
  Neostigmine is contraindicated in patients with known hypersensitivity to the drug. Because of the presence of the bromide ion, oral neostigmine bromide tablets are contraindicated in patients with a known bromide hypersensitivity.
 
Neostigmine is contraindicated in patients with GI obstruction/ileus or urinary tract obstruction because it increases contractions of smooth muscle. Neostigmine can exacerbate peritonitis by increasing GI motility and its use in this condition is contraindicated. Neostigmine also stimulates gastric acid secretion and should be used with caution in peptic ulcer disease. Neostigmine should be used with caution in patients with vagotonia. Large doses of neostigmine should be avoided in situations where there might be an increased absorption rate from the intestinal tract. Neostigmine should be used with caution when co-administered with anticholinergic drugs, in order to avoid reduction of intestinal motility.
 
Neostigmine should not be used for cholinesterase inhibitor toxicity. It is important to differentiate between myasthenic crisis and cholinergic crisis since both conditions present with similar symptoms. Myasthenic crisis requires treatment with anticholinesterase therapy while cholinergic crisis (indicative of anticholinesterase overdose, with excess acetylcholine available through cholinesterase inhibition) requires discontinuation of cholinesterase inhibitor therapy.
 
Neostigmine must be used cautiously in patients with hypotension and bradycardia because it can further decrease the blood pressure and heart rate by increasing vagal tone. Neostigmine also has direct stimulatory effects on the myocardium, which can increase oxygen demand. This can be dangerous for patients with cardiac disease, particularly coronary artery disease and/or cardiac arrhythmias.
 
Neostigmine should also be used cautiously in patients with hyperthyroidism or seizure disorder because it can exacerbate these conditions by stimulating the CNS.
 
Neostigmine can cause bronchoconstriction and should therefore be used with caution in patients with asthma.
 
Neostigmine is metabolized by microsomal liver enzymes and hydrolyzed by cholinesterases. Theoretically, hepatic disease associated with significant hepatic impairment could hinder metabolism, although the clinical significance of this effect is not known. Initiate dosage cautiously; monitor for potential drug toxicity.
 
Following neostigmine administration, about 50—67% of a dose is excreted in the urine as unchanged drug, and the drug half-life is prolonged in patients with end-stage renal disease. Significant renal impairment or renal failure may lead to accumulation of neostigmine. Adjust dosage for patients with CrCl <= 50 ml/min (see Dosage, Patients with renal impairment).
 
Neostigmine is classified as FDA pregnancy risk category C: it is not known whether or not neostigmine is teratogenic. Anticholinesterase drugs can cause uterine irritation which may lead to premature labor if given intravenously near term. Use during pregnancy should be avoided unless the potential benefits of therapy outweigh the possible risks involved.
 
It is not known whether neostigmine is excreted in human milk. Because many drugs are excreted in human milk, and because of the potential for serious adverse reactions from neostigmine, a decision should be made whether to discontinue breast-feeding or to discontinue the drug, taking into account the importance of the drug to the mother.

    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components.
    Headline
    cardiac arrhythmias, coronary artery disease, myasthenia gravis
    General Information
    Use neostigmine with caution in individuals with coronary artery disease, including recent acute coronary syndrome, cardiac arrhythmias, or myasthenia gravis. In these individuals, the risk of blood pressure and heart rate complications (e.g., bradycardia, hypotension, dysrhythmia) may be increased. Administer an anticholinergic agent (e.g., atropine or glycopyrrolate) prior to or concurrently with neostigmine to mitigate the risk of cardiovascular complications.
    Headline
    mechanical GI obstruction, peritonitis, urinary tract obstruction
    General Information
    Neostigmine is contraindicated in individuals with peritonitis, mechanical GI obstruction, or urinary tract obstruction.
    Headline
    hepatic failure
    General Information
    Carefully monitor individuals with hepatic impairment or hepatic failure if a hepatically cleared neuromuscular blocking agent was used during their surgical procedure as the duration of action may be prolonged by hepatic insufficiency, whereas neostigmine, which undergoes renal elimination, will not likely be affected. This could result in the effects of the neuromuscular blocking agent outlasting those of neostigmine. This same situation may arise if the neuromuscular blocking agent has active metabolites. The interval for redosing the neuromuscular blocking agent during the surgical procedure may be useful in determining whether, and to what extent, post-operative monitoring needs to be extended.
    Headline
    renal failure, renal impairment
    General Information
    Closely monitor individuals with renal impairment or renal failure to assure the effects of the neuromuscular blocking agent, particularly a neuromuscular blocking agent that is cleared by the kidneys, do not persist beyond those of neostigmine. The interval for redosing the neuromuscular blocking agent during the surgical procedure may be useful in determining whether, and to what extent, post-operative monitoring needs to be extended.
    Headline
    geriatric
    General Information
    Use neostigmine with caution and monitor geriatric adults for a longer period due to the increased likelihood of decreased renal function. The duration of action of neostigmine is prolonged in geriatric adults; however, geriatric adults may also experience slower spontaneous recovery from neuromuscular blocking agents. Monitor geriatric adults longer to ensure that additional doses of neostigmine are not needed. Determine the duration of monitoring based on the anticipated duration of action for the neuromuscular blocking agent used.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Use neostigmine during pregnancy only if clearly needed. There are no adequate or well-controlled studies of the use of neostigmine in pregnant women. It is not known if neostigmine can cause fetal harm or affect reproductive ability when administered during pregnancy. No adverse effects or teratogenic effects were seen in animal studies in which rats or rabbits were treated with neostigmine during organogenesis, although doses utilized were well below predicted exposures in humans. The effect of neostigmine on the mother and fetus, with regard to labor and delivery, including need for intervention during delivery (e.g., forceps or resuscitation of the newborn), is not known. Anticholinesterase drugs, including neostigmine, may cause uterine irritability and induce premature labor when administered to pregnant women near term.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Consider the developmental and health benefits of breast-feeding along with the mother's need for neostigmine and any potential adverse effects on the breast-fed child from neostigmine or the underlying maternal condition. Neostigmine has not been studied in lactating women. It is not known whether neostigmine is present in human milk, or if neostigmine has effects on milk production or the breast-fed child.

Patients receiving concurrent NSAIDs should be monitored closely for symptoms of active or occult gastrointestinal bleeding. Adverse Reactions
  Headline
  Adverse Reactions
  General Information
  The most common unwanted effects of neostigmine's cholinergic stimulation of end organs are nausea/vomiting, diarrhea, abdominal pain, miosis, hypersalivation, diaphoresis, sinus bradycardia, bronchospasm, and increased bronchial secretions. Myasthenia, muscle cramps, fasciculations, and hypotension also can occur. Severe adverse reactions, including those listed above, as well as respiratory paralysis (may result in respiratory depression or respiratory arrest) and cardiac arrest, can be seen in the cholinergic crisis caused by overdosage. However, all of the above symptoms can occur to varying degrees and do not necessarily indicate a cholinergic crisis. Atropine can be used concomitantly to reduce adverse muscarinic effects, but this can also mask the early signs of true crisis.
  Adverse Reactions
    Headline
    bronchial secretions, bronchospasm, cholinergic crisis, dyspnea, respiratory arrest, respiratory depression
    General Information
    Dyspnea and oxygen desaturation (less than 90%) have been reported with neostigmine in 1% or more of adults in clinical trials. Bronchospasm, increased oral, pharyngeal, and bronchial secretions, respiratory depression, and respiratory arrest have been reported with neostigmine in postmarketing. Cholinergic crisis may result from neostigmine overdosage; it is characterized by increasing muscle weakness, and through involvement of the respiratory muscles, may result in death. It is important to differentiate cholinergic crisis from myasthenic crisis, as both conditions result in extreme muscle weakness but require radically different treatment. Myasthenic crisis requires treatment with anticholinesterase therapy, while cholinergic crisis requires prompt discontinuation of cholinesterase inhibitor therapy and administration of atropine. Administer an anticholinergic agent (e.g., atropine or glycopyrrolate) prior to or concurrently with neostigmine to counteract muscarinic-type adverse reactions.

Due to the voluntary nature of post-marketing reports, neither a frequency nor a definitive causal relationship can be established.

Description
  Text
  Neostigmine is an oral and parenteral parasympathomimetic agent (cholinesterase inhibitor). It is similar to, but shorter-acting than, pyridostigmine. Neostigmine bromide is the oral form of the drug, while neostigmine methylsulfate is the intravenous (IV), intramuscular (IM), and subcutaneous (SC) form. Both formulations (bromide and methylsulfate) are approved for the symptomatic treatment of myasthenia gravis when given via oral, SC, and IM routes. Neostigmine methylsulfate is also indicated for reversal of the effects of non-depolarizing neuromuscular blocking agents when given IV and for the prevention and treatment of post-operative distention and urinary retention (after exclusion of a mechanical obstruction) when given as an IM or SC injection. Neostigmine was approved by the FDA in 1939.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Mechanism of Action: Neostigmine exerts its effects by competing with acetylcholine for its binding site on acetylcholinesterase. By interfering with acetylcholine enzymatic destruction, neostigmine potentiates the action of acetylcholine on both the skeletal muscle (nicotinic receptor) and the GI tract (muscarinic receptor). Neostigmine also can stimulate cholinergic responses in the eyes (causing miosis) if directly applied. Different muscle groups exhibit different levels of response to anticholinesterase agents, and doses that produce stimulation of one muscle group can cause weakness, through overdose, in another.Specific responses to cholinesterase inhibitors include: increased skeletal muscle tone (nicotinic); increased gastric motility and GI tone (muscarinic); bradycardia (muscarinic); ureteral constriction (muscarinic); stimulation of the sweat and salivary glands (muscarinic); and constriction of the bronchi (muscarinic). There is also some evidence that they have a direct action on skeletal muscle.Neostigmine methylsulfate also can be used to antagonize the effects of nondepolarizing neuromuscular blocking agents such as pancuronium and tubocurarine. Other uses include treating postoperative, nonobstructive urinary retention and postoperative, nonobstructive abdominal distension, for which bethanechol is used more commonly.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Oral Route
    Description
    Only 1 to 2% of neostigmine bromide is absorbed from the GI tract. As a general rule, 15 mg of neostigmine bromide orally is equivalent to 0.5 mg of neostigmine methylsulfate parenterally due to poor absorption of the tablet from the intestinal tract. Peak plasma concentrations occur in 1 to 2 hours after oral drug administration, with considerable individual variability. The onset of action occurs from 2 to 4 hours when taken orally.
    Name
    Intravenous Route
    Description
    When used for neuromuscular blocker reversal, doses of 0.03 to 0.07 mg/kg IV will generally achieve a train-of-four twitch ratio of 90% within 10 to 20 minutes of IV administration of neostigmine methylsulfate.     Name
    Intramuscular Route
    Description
    Neostigmine methylsulfate is rapidly absorbed after intramuscular administration, with peak plasma concentrations observed at 30 minutes. Half-life ranges from 51—90 minutes. The onset of action occurs within 20 to 30 minutes after administration and last for a duration of 2.5 to 4 hours.
  Special Populations
    Name
    Renal Impairment
    Description
    Approximately 80% of a neostigmine dose is excreted in the urine as parent drug and metabolites, with approximately 50% of the dose excreted unchanged.The elimination half-life of neostigmine is prolonged in patients with end-stage renal disease. In a pharmacokinetic study of 18 adult patients, anephric patients had a plasma serum clearance of 7.8 ml/kg/min and an elimination half-life of 181.1 minutes compared to 16.7 ml/kg/min and 79.8 minutes in patients with normal renal function. Another study found that neostigmine methylsulfate's half-life increased from 79.8 (+/- 48.6) minutes in patients with normal renal function to 104.7 (+/- 64) minutes in transplant patients and 181 (+/- 54) minutes in anephric patients.
    Name
    Pediatrics
    Description
    Infants and Children
When compared to adults, infants and children require half the dose of neostigmine to produce comparable neuromuscular blockade antagonism. The time to produce 50% antagonism against a neuromuscular blocking agent (ED50) for infants (13.1 mcg/kg) and children (15.5 mcg/kg) is less than the value for adults (22.9 mcg/kg). Vd is similar for infants (0.54 L/kg), children (0.49 L/kg), and adults (0.52 L/kg); however, the elimination half-life for infants and children is significantly shorter. Elimination half-life is 39 +/- 5 minutes in infants, 48 +/- 16 minutes in children, and 67 +/- 8 minutes in adults. These differences are most likely due to higher plasma clearance values of infants (13.6 mL/kg/minute) and children (11.1 mL/kg/minute) compared to adults (9.6 mL/kg/minute). In addition, local differences at the neuromuscular junction (e.g., decreased enzyme activity, fewer acetylcholine reserves, limited receptor quantity) may explain lower dosage requirements in pediatric patients. Time to peak antagonism and duration of action is similar among age groups.
  Text
  Neostigmine is administered intravenously, intramuscularly, subcutaneously, and orally. The drug has a volume of distribution between 0.12 and 1.4 L/kg, and does not readily cross the blood-brain barrier. Approximately 15 to 25% of neostigmine is bound to serum albumin. Neostigmine is metabolized by microsomal liver enzymes and hydrolyzed by cholinesterases. In adults, the half-life ranges from 42 to 60 minutes (mean of 52 minutes) for the oral formulation, 51 to 90 minutes for the intramuscular formulation, and 24 to 113 minutes for the intravenous formulation. Duration of effect varies considerably among patients. About 80% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours as parent drug and metabolites, with approximately 50% of the dose excreted unchanged.
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes: none

    Administration Details
      Name
      Intravenous Administration
      Description
      Neostigmine for neuromuscular blocking agent (NMBA) reversal requires a trained health care provider familiar with the use, actions, characteristics, and complications of NMBAs and NMBA reversal agents.
Ensure individuals are well ventilated and a patent airway maintained prior to neostigmine administration and until complete recovery of normal ventilation. Assess satisfactory recovery by adequacy of skeletal muscle tone and respiratory measurements in addition to response to peripheral nerve stimulation.
Peripheral nerve stimulation devices capable of delivering train-of-four (TOF) stimulus are essential to effective use of neostigmine.
Use a peripheral nerve stimulator to determine the time of initiation of neostigmine. The twitch response to the first stimulus in the TOF must be at least 10% of its baseline level (i.e., the response prior to NMBA administration) prior to the administration of neostigmine.
Continue TOF monitoring to evaluate the extent of recovery of neuromuscular function and the possible need for additional doses of neostigmine.
Do not rely on TOF monitoring alone to determine the adequacy of reversal of neuromuscular blockade as related to an individual's ability to adequately ventilate and maintain a patent airway after tracheal extubation.
Administer an anticholinergic agent (e.g., atropine or glycopyrrolate) prior to or concurrently with neostigmine.
Continue to monitor for adequacy of reversal from NMBAs for a time period that would assure full recovery based on the individual's medical condition and the pharmacokinetics of neostigmine and the NMBA used.

Dosage Adjustment Guidelines
  Hepatic Impairment
  Specific guidelines for dosage adjustments in hepatic impairment are not available. Neostigmine is metabolized by microsomal liver enzymes and hydrolyzed by cholinesterases. Theoretically hepatic impairment could hinder metabolism, although the clinical significance of this effect is not known. Initiate dosage cautiously; monitor for potential drug toxicity.
  Renal Impairment
  The FDA-approved product label for IV neostigmine methylsulfate states that dosage adjustments are not necessary in patients with renal impairment ; however, other sources suggest the following dosage adjustments:
CrCl > 50 ml/min: No dosage adjustment is needed.
CrCl 10—50 ml/min: Administer 50% of the dose at suggested dosing intervals for clinical indication.
CrCl < 10 ml/min: Administer 25% of the dose at suggested dosing intervals for clinical indication.
