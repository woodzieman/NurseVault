Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  General Information
  BCG is capable of dissemination when administered by the intravesical route and serious infection, including fatal infection, has been reported in patients receiving intravesical BCG. Clinicians should exercise caution to not cause trauma to the urinary tract or introduce contaminants into the urinary system. Seven to 14 days should elapse before BCG is administered following TUR, biopsy, or traumatic catheterization.
 
Patients with an infection including active mycobacterial infection (i.e., tuberculosis, TB) should not receive BCG Live intravesical therapy. Tuberculin skin testing should be performed prior to intravesical treatment. A positive Mantoux test is a contraindication for BCG Live only if there is evidence of an active tuberculosis infection. Use of BCG Live or vaccination with BCG (See BCG Vaccine monograph) may cause tuberculin sensitivity.
 
Treatment with BCG Live intravesical therapy should be delayed until resolution of fever, urinary tract infection (UTI), or gross hematuria. Systemic BCG infection may be promoted if BCG Live comes into contact with an actively bleeding mucosa. Intravesical BCG Live administration should be given no sooner than 7—14 days following bladder biopsy, transurethral resection, or catheterization that induced local trauma. Treatment with antibiotics, especially anti-tuberculin agents, should be completed prior to BCG Live treatment because antimicrobial therapy may interfere with the effectiveness of therapy. Patients with an aneurysm or prosthetic device such as prosthetic heart valves may develop an ectopic BCG infection. The risk of infection development is unknown but is considered to be small. Consideration of the benefit and risk of treatment with BCG Live is needed. Patients should be monitored for fever >= 101.3 degrees F (38.5 degrees C) for longer than 48—72 hours or increasing liver function tests that may indicate systemic BCG infection. Local symptoms and inflammation (i.e., epididymitis, prostatitis, or orchitis) lasting more than 2—3 days may also suggest active BCG infection. In patients who develop fever or experience an acute febrile illness consistent with BCG infection, 2 or more antimycobacterial agents should be administered while diagnostic evaluation is being conducted. BCG Live intravesical therapy should be discontinued. Negative culture results do not necessarily rule out infection. BCG Live is sensitive to the most commonly used anti-TB agents with the exception of pyrazinamide.
 
Elderly patients; patients with diabetes mellitus, renal failure, leukemia, or lymphoma; and patients who are receiving radiation therapy, chemotherapy, or corticosteroid therapy may be immunosuppressed. Use of the BCG Live is contraindicated in patients who are immunosuppressed, as immunosuppression may lead to clinical disease (see Adverse Reactions) and prevent an appropriate immune response to BCG. Patients with bone marrow suppression, human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) infection, or acquired immunodeficiency syndrome (AIDS) should not receive BCG Live due to the risk of systemic mycobacterium sepsis. Also, patients with immunosuppression may not be able to mount an appropriate immune response to BCG Live intravesical treatment. Carefully consider the use of BCG Live in patients who may become immunosuppressed such as patients with myasthenia gravis and patients on an organ transplant list. 
 
Precautions should be taken to avoid accidental exposure to BCG solutions during preparation and administration, as these solutions contain live, attenuated mycobacterium. After usage, all equipment and materials used for preparation and instillation of the BCG solution should be placed into plastic bags labeled 'Infectious Waste' and disposed of accordingly as biohazardous waste. Patients should be informed that any urine voided within 6 hours of instillation of BCG should be disinfected with an equal volume of 5% hypochlorite solution (undiluted household bleach) and allow to stand for 15 minutes before flushing. Patients should be advised to sit while urinating during the 24 hours following BCG instillation.
 
People with latex hypersensitivity may not be appropriate candidates for TheraCys® administration. The vial stopper contains latex. Although patients do not handle the drug vial, some latex particles could contaminate the drug suspension. The Tice® BCG product does not contain latex.
 
Intravenous administration and subcutaneous administration of BCG Live should be avoided. Bacillus Calmette-Guerin (BCG) Live may be administered via an intravesical bladder infusion (see Administration).
 
Bacillus Calmette-Guerin (BCG) Live intravesical therapy should be used cautiously in patients with irritable bladder symptoms, including patients with neurogenic bladder. An increased incidence of bladder spasm and spontaneous discharge of the intravesical instillate may occur. Administer cautiously to patients with a small bladder capacity; small bladder capacity has been associated with an increased risk of severe local reactions.
 
Bacillus Calmette-Guerin (BCG) products are considered FDA pregnancy risk category C agents. It is not known whether BCG Live intravesical causes fetal harm if given to a pregnant woman. Therapy with BCG Live should be given to a pregnant woman only if clearly needed. Females of reproductive age should take precautions against becoming pregnant while on BCG Live therapy.
 
It is not known whether BCG is excreted in breast milk. Because many drugs are excreted in breast milk and because of the potential for harm in nursing infants, a decision should be made whether to discontinue breast-feeding or to discontinue BCG therapy taking into account the importance of therapy to the mother.
 
The safety and effectiveness of BCG Live intravesical therapy for children with superficial bladder cancer has not been established.

Adverse Reactions
  Headline
  Adverse Reactions
  General Information
  BCG infections have been reported in health care workers primarily from exposures from accidental needle sticks and skin lacerations during the preparation of BCG Live for administration. Nosocomial infections have been reported in patients receiving parenteral drugs prepared in areas in which BCG was reconstituted (see Administration). Recipients of BCG Live may develop a localized (e.g., urinary tract or epididymitis) or systemic BCG infection. Mycobacterium bovis infections have occurred in the lung, liver, bone, bone marrow, kidney, regional lymph nodes, and prostate. Several deaths have been reported as a result of systemic BCG infection (e.g., hepatitis or pneumonitis) and sepsis. Of 674 patients who received Tice® BCG, = 101.3 degrees F (38.5 degrees C) or acute, localized inflammation such as epididymitis, prostatitis, or orchitis lasting > 48—72 hours suggest active infection. Progressive cough, increasing intensity of systemic manifestations with repeated instillations, or persistent elevated hepatic enzymes may also suggest systemic BCG infection. In patients who develop fever or experience an acute febrile illness consistent with BCG infection, 2 or more antimycobacterial agents should be administered while diagnostic evaluation is being conducted. BCG Live intravesical therapy should be discontinued. Negative culture results do not necessarily rule out infection. BCG Live is sensitive to the most commonly used anti-tuberculosis agents; however, all BCG strains are resistant to pyrazinamide, and this antibiotic should not be used. Monitor patients for signs and symptoms of toxicity after each instillation of BCG. For a systemic infection, an infectious disease specialist should be consulted, and the patient should be treated with appropriate anti-tuberculosis therapy.Following intravesical administration of BCG Live solutions, irritable bladder symptoms related to the inflammatory response induced by BCG are common. Localized adverse effects that occurred in at least 10% of 112 patients who received TheraCys® included dysuria, urinary frequency, hematuria, cystitis, urinary tract infection, urinary urgency, and genital pain. In another study, nocturia, bladder spasm, bladder discomfort or pain, urinary incontinence, and urinary debris also occurred in at least 10% of 674 patients who received Tice® BCG. Of another 222 patients who received Tice® BCG, 9% had hemorrhagic cystitis. Dysuria, urinary frequency, hematuria, and cystitis can be severe (at least grade 3). Symptoms usually begin 2—6 hours after instillation and persist for 24—72 hours. Local reactions are usually seen after the third treatment and increase in frequency and severity with progressive treatments. Small bladder capacity has been associated with an increased risk of severe, local reactions; consideration of bladder capacity may be advisable. Delay of treatment may reduce the severity of a reaction caused by a subsequent treatment. Irritative bladder symptoms associated with BCG Live administration can be managed with pyridium, oxybutynin chloride, diphenhydramine, phenazopyridine, propantheline, acetaminophen, or ibuprofen. There is no evidence that BCG Live dose reduction or antituberculosis drug therapy can prevent or lessen the irritative effects of BCG Live. Also, there is no evidence that acute, local urinary tract toxicity is due to mycobacterial infection.Systemic adverse effects that occurred in at least 10% of 112 patients who received TheraCys® included malaise, fever, chills, anemia, nausea/vomiting, and anorexia. Arthralgia, myalgia, diarrhea, leukopenia, coagulopathy, abdominal pain, headache, or skin rash (unspecified) occurred in 2—10% of patients. Uveitis, conjunctivitis, iritis, keratitis, and granulomatous choreoretinitis in isolation and in concert with arthralgia, urinary symptoms, or skin rash (unspecified) have been reported. In another study, fatigue, rigors, dizziness, headache, weight loss, or a flu-like syndrome also occurred in at least 10% of 674 patients who received Tice® BCG. The flu-like syndrome may last 1—2 days and may reflect hypersensitivity reactions. Thrombocytopenia and urinary obstruction occurred in <1% of patients. The risk of these adverse events appears to be elevated among patients who are positive for HLA-B27. Discontinue BCG and immediately evaluate the patient for systemic infection if a patient has a persistent fever or an acute febrile illness consistent with BCG infection.Bladder contracture is a long-term complication of BCG Live intravesical therapy that occurs in < 1% of patients. Management includes stopping BCG Live instillations, hydrodistention, and bladder augmentation. Antituberculosis therapy is not necessary unless there are signs of infection or inflammation.Cases of nephrogenic adenoma (adenomatous metaplasia of the bladder) have been reported following BCG Live instillation. This disorder is usually benign and believed to result from chronic irritation or trauma to the bladder.Granulomatous prostatitis and granulomatous hepatitis have been reported in patients who receive intravesical BCG Live. A biopsy of the prostate or liver is needed for diagnosis, as tissue and urine cultures may be negative. Most of these patients remain asymptomatic and may not require treatment. However, some patients may experience acute or severe symptoms; treatment with antituberculosis agents (i.e., isoniazid and rifampin) for 3—6 months may be indicated. Elevated levels of prostate specific antigen (PSA) occur transiently in about 40% of patients during BCG Live treatment. Firm prostatic nodules with increased PSA following BCG Live treatment appears to be part of the clinical picture of BCG-induced granulomatous prostatitis. Some authors recommend postponing prostate biopsy to see if the PSA levels return to normal after completion of BCG Live therapy.Ureteral obstruction may occur secondary to cystitis, ureteritis, or prostatitis. However, ureteral obstruction is usually self-limiting and resolves once BCG Live therapy is stopped. Ureteral obstruction occurs more frequently in patients with carcinoma in situ or ureteral reflux due to granulomatous reactions around the ureteral orifice. Prophylactic isoniazid has been suggested to decrease urinary frequency and urgency, but a randomized trial showed no benefit, and this therapy is not recommended.

    bladder discomfort, cystitis, dysuria, hematuria, hemorrhagic cystitis, increased urinary frequency, nocturia, urinary incontinence, urinary tract obstruction, urinary urgency
    General Information
    Bladder and urinary system adverse events have been reported in patients with carcinoma in situ of the urinary bladder (CIS) or primary or recurrent superficial bladder cancer who received intravesical bacillus calmette-guerin (BCG) live (TICE BCG) in clinical studies. Bladder irritation occurs in approximately 60% of TICE BCG-treated patients and are likely caused by an immune-related inflammatory response to treatment. Typically, symptoms start 4 to 6 hours after the instillation and last for 24 to 72 hours; irritation symptoms are usually observed following the third instillation and the severity tends to increase with each instillation. Symptoms can usually be managed symptomatically; there is no evidence that dose reduction or antituberculous drug therapy can prevent or lessen bladder irritation due to treatment. Bladder and urinary system adverse events reported in a review of 674 patients who received intravesical TICE BCG include dysuria (60%; grade 3 or higher, 11%), increased urinary frequency (40%; grade 3 or higher, 7%), hematuria (26%; grade 3 or higher, 7%), cystitis (6%; grade 3 or higher, 2%), urinary urgency (6%; grade 3 or higher, 1%), nocturia (5%; grade 3 or higher, 1%), urinary incontinence (2%), and urinary debris (2%; grade 3 or higher, less than 1%). Urethritis and urinary tract obstruction were reported in 1% or less TICE BCG-treated patients in this evaluation. Direct bladder toxicity including dysuria (52% vs. 35%; grade 3 or higher, 3% vs. 2%), frequency (50% vs. 29%; grade 3 or higher, 2% vs. 3%), hematuria (38% vs. 25%; grade 3 or higher, 3% vs. 2%), hemorrhagic cystitis (9% vs. 5%; grade 3 or higher, 1% vs. 0%), bladder discomfort/cramps (8% vs. 4%), and incontinence (4% vs. 1%) was seen more often in patients treated with TICE BCG compared with those who received mitomycin C in a randomized clinical trial.

    General Information
    Infection, in some cases serious or fatal, has been reported in patients with carcinoma in situ of the urinary bladder (CIS) or primary or recurrent superficial bladder cancer who received intravesical bacillus calmette-guerin (BCG) live (TICE BCG). Description
  Text
  The TICE strain of Bacillus Calmette-Guerin (BCG) Live is a live, attenuated culture preparation of the BCG strain of Mycobacterium bovis; it acts as an immunostimulant and is immunologically similar to M. tuberculosis. It is indicated for the treatment and prophylaxis of carcinoma in situ (CIS) of the urinary bladder and for the prophylaxis of primary or recurrent stage Ta and/or T1 papillary tumors following transurethral resection (TUR); it is not indicated for papillary tumors of stages higher than T1, and is not recommended for stage TaG1 papillary tumors unless they are a high risk for recurrence. TICE BCG Live is not a vaccine against cancer and should not be used as an immunizing agent for tuberculosis prevention. TICE BCG Live carries a black box warning regarding the risk of infection in both patients and healthcare professionals due to its containing live, attenuated mycobacteria; use caution and treat TICE BCG as biohazardous material during preparation, handling, and disposal.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  The TICE strain of BCG is a live, attenuated culture preparation of the Bacillus of Calmette and Guerin (BCG) strain of Mycobacterim bovis; it acts as an immunostimulant and is immunologically similar to M. tuberculosis. Intravesical administration of BCG produces a nonspecific, localized immune reaction consisting of granulomatous and inflammation reactions with histocyte and leukocyte infiltration; the mechanism of action is not fully understood. Based on preclinical mouse studies, it appears that BCG attaches to the urothelium after instillation, including bladder cancer cells, via an interaction between mycobacterial fibronectin attachment proteins (FAPs) in the bacterial cell wall and fibronectin in the urothelium. This stimulates an immune response as the bacterial cell surface glycoproteins acting as antigens upregulate cytokine production including interleukin (IL)-1, IL-2, IL-6, IL-8, IL-12, granulocyte macrophage colony-stimulating factor (GM-CSF), interferon gamma, and tumor necrosis factor (TNF)-alpha. Macrophages, monocytes, natural killer (NK) cells, neutrophils, polymorphonuclear cells (PMNs), B cells, and T cells have been detected in the urine of patients treated with BCG; the strength of the immune response increases with repeated treatments. Increased CD4 cell count and CD4:CD8 ratio have been significantly associated with improved response to BCG.

In one clinical trial, nonselected patients with bladder cancer were treated with 6 weekly instillations of Connaught strain BCG, containing at least 1.8 x 10^8 cfu. BCG was present in 96.4% of urine samples taken 2 hours after instillation, 67.9% of urine samples taken 24 hours after instillations, and 15.5% of urine samples after 6 days. Administration
  General Information
  For storage information, see the specific product information within the How Supplied section.
CAUTION: Bacillus Calmette-Guerin (BCG) Live, USP contains live bacteria and should be prepared in a biological safety cabinet and handled using aseptic technique. All equipment, supplies, and receptacles in contact with these products should be disposed of as biohazards. Do not prepare parenteral drugs in areas where BCG has been prepared. Nosocomial infections have been reported in patients receiving parenteral drugs prepared in areas in which BCG was reconstituted.
  Route Specific Administrations
    Name
    Other Administration Route(s)
    Description
    Intravesical Administration
Ensure correct formulation selection prior to administration of BCG Live; there are no data to support the interchangeability of BCG products. BCG Live (TICE BCG) is not a vaccine. BCG Vaccine, not BCG Live, should be used for the prevention of tuberculosis.
Wait 7 to 14 days after bladder biopsy before administering BCG.
For intravesical administration only; do not administer IV or subcutaneously.
Do not prepare parenteral medications in areas where BCG has been prepared in order to avoid cross-contamination.
Patients should not drink liquids for 4 hours prior to treatment and should empty their bladder prior to BCG administration.
 
Reconstitution:
Add 1 mL of preservative-free 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection to 1 vial of BCG. Avoid the use of bacteriostatic solutions for reconstitution and administration.
Gently swirl until a homogenous, cloudy suspension is attained; avoid foaming or forceful agitation as this may cause clumping of the mycobacteria.
Dilute the suspension in preservative-free Sodium Chloride Injection to a final volume of 50 mL. Do not filter the contents of the BCG vial.
Storage after reconstitution: Store refrigerated (2 to 8 degrees C or 36 to 46 degrees F) and use within 2 hours or reconstitution. Discard any suspension remaining after administration. Protect from direct sunlight.
 
Bladder instillation:
Mix gently prior to instillation.
Instill BCG into the bladder by gravity flow via catheter. Remove the catheter when the instillation is complete.
Retain BCG in the bladder for 2 hours, then instruct the patient to void. During this time, reposition the patient from left side to right side, back and abdomen, every 15 minutes, in order to maximize bladder surface exposure to BCG.
If the patient is unable to retain the suspension for 2 hours, they may void sooner.
 
After Instillation:
Instruct patients to void while seated to avoid splashing of urine.
Disinfect voided urine with an equal volume of household bleach for 15 minutes before flushing for 6 hours after each treatment.
Instruct patients to increase fluid intake to flush the bladder in the hours after treatment.
