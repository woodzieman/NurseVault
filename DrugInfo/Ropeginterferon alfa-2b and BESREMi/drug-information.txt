        Route Age Groups
          Heading

          Dosage Description
          initial, 100 mcg subcutaneously every 2 weeks. Increase the dose by 50 mcg every 2 weeks to a maximum dose of 500 mcg until hematologic parameters are stabilized (hematologic response: hematocrit value less than 45%, platelet count less than 400 x 10^9 cells/L, and leukocyte count less than 10 x 10^9 cells/L). Consider dose re-escalation in case of prior dose reduction, recovery, and lack of hematologic response. Continue therapy for at least 1 year at the dosage that achieves hematologic stability, then the dosing interval may be extended to every 4 weeks. Therapy interruption, dose reduction, or discontinuation may be necessary in patients who develop toxicity or adverse reactions. Phlebotomy as rescue treatment to normalize blood hyperviscosity may be necessary during the titration phase. The complete hematologic response rate was 61% in patients with JAK2V617F mutation-positive polycythemia vera who received ropeginterferon alfa-2b in a multicenter, single-arm trial (n = 51; PEGINVERA). The median time to response was 7.8 months and the median duration of response was 14.3 months. Patients in this study received ropeginterferon alfa-2b for a median of 61 months at a mean dose of 237 +/- 110 mcg; 16% of patients had newly diagnosed disease at study entry.

        Heading
        Adults
        Dosage Description
        Recommended starting dose is 250 mcg subcutaneously. At 2 weeks, increase dose to 350 mcg; then at 4 weeks, increase to a maintenance dose of 500 mcg every 2 weeks throughout treatment unless dose modification is required for safety or tolerability. Consider dose re-escalation in case of prior dose reduction, recovery, and lack of hematologic response (response defined as platelet count less than 400 x 10^9 cells/L and leukocyte count less than 10 x 10^9 cells/L). Therapy interruption, dose reduction, or discontinuation may be necessary in patients who develop toxicity or adverse reactions. In a randomized, open-label study (SURPASS ET), patients with essential thrombocythemia and documented resistance or intolerance to hydroxyurea who received ropeginterferon alfa-2b achieved a significantly higher response rate compared with anagrelide at both months 9 and 12 (37.4% vs. 3.6%). Response rate using modified European Leukemia Net (ELN) criteria included blood count remission (platelet count 400 x 10^9 cells/L or less and white blood count less than 9.5 x 10^9 cells/L) and improvement or non-progression of splenomegaly and absence of hemorrhagic or thrombotic events. Patients in both arms received low-dose aspirin during the 12 months of study treatment, unless contraindicated.

    General Information
    Ropeginterferon alfa-2b can cause or aggravate serious adverse effects including fatal or life-threatening neuropsychiatric, autoimmune, ischemic, and infectious disorders.
    Headline
    major psychiatric disorder, suicidal ideation
    General Information
    Ropeginterferon alfa-2b is contraindicated in patients with a history of severe major psychiatric disorder, particularly severe depression, suicidal ideation, or suicide attempt. Alpha interferons, including ropeginterferon alfa-2b, may cause or aggravate life-threatening or fatal neuropsychiatric disorders. Monitor patients closely for symptoms of psychiatric disorders with periodic clinical evaluations; consider psychiatric consultation and treatment as appropriate. An interruption of therapy, dose reduction, or discontinuation of therapy may be necessary based on severity. Treatment-related psychiatric disorders may resolve after therapy is stopped.
    Headline
    autoimmune disease, immunosuppression
    General Information
    Ropeginterferon alfa-2b is contraindicated in patients with a history or presence of active serious or untreated autoimmune disease, patients with active serious or untreated endocrine disease associated with autoimmune disease, and transplant recipients who are receiving immunosuppression. Alpha interferons, including ropeginterferon alfa-2b, may cause or aggravate autoimmune disorders. Monitor patients closely for signs or symptoms of autoimmune disorders with periodic clinical and laboratory evaluations; obtain thyroid function tests in patients who develop symptoms suggestive of thyroid disease. Discontinuation of therapy may be necessary based on severity. Treatment-related autoimmune disorders may resolve after therapy is stopped.
    Headline
    Child-Pugh class B, Child-Pugh class C, hepatic failure
    General Information
    Ropeginterferon alfa-2b is contraindicated in patients with moderate (Child-Pugh class B) or severe (Child-Pugh class C) hepatic impairment including hepatic failure at baseline. Monitor liver function tests (e.g., AST, ALT, gamma-glutamyltransferase (GGT), bilirubin levels) and observe patients for signs or symptoms of hepatic dysfunction at baseline and during therapy. Therapy interruption, a dosage reduction, or discontinuation may be necessary in patients who develop hepatotoxicity. Discontinue ropeginterferon alfa-2b in patients who develop evidence of hepatic decompensation (e.g., jaundice, ascites, hepatic encephalopathy, hepatorenal syndrome, variceal hemorrhage) or moderate or severe hepatic impairment during therapy.
    Headline
    cardiac arrhythmias, coronary artery disease, hypertension, New York Heart Association Class II heart failure, New York Heart Association Class III heart failure, New York Heart Association Class IV heart failure, stroke, unstable angina
    General Information
    Avoid the use of ropeginterferon alfa-2b in patients with severe or unstable cardiovascular disease (e.g., including uncontrolled hypertension, New York Heart Association Class II heart failure, New York Heart Association Class III heart failure, New York Heart Association Class IV heart failure, serious cardiac arrhythmias, significant coronary artery disease or stenosis, unstable angina) or recent stroke or myocardial infarction. Monitor patients who have a history of cardiovascular disorders and/or thrombotic events closely for evidence of cardiovascular toxicity during therapy. Patients with hypertension may also be at an increased risk of developing retinopathy-associated disease; perform an ophthalmologic exam at baseline and periodically during therapy. Alpha interferons, including ropeginterferon alfa-2b, may cause or aggravate life-threatening or fatal ischemic disorders. Monitor patients closely for signs or symptoms of ischemic disorders with periodic clinical evaluations. Discontinuation of therapy may be necessary based on severity. Treatment-related ischemic disorders may resolve after therapy is stopped.
    Headline
    activities requiring coordination and concentration, driving or operating machinery
    General Information
    Patients should avoid driving or operating machinery until they know how ropeginterferon alfa-2b therapy will affect them. Patients who experience dizziness, somnolence, or hallucination during therapy should continue to avoid driving or operating machinery or performing activities requiring coordination and concentration. There is a risk of additive central nervous system toxicity in patients who are also receiving narcotic, hypnotic, or sedative agents; avoid concomitant use if possible, otherwise, monitor these patients closely.
    Headline
    diabetes mellitus
    General Information
    Use ropeginterferon alfa-2b with caution in patients with diabetes mellitus; these patients may be at an increased risk of developing retinopathy-associated disease. Serious ocular toxicity has been reported with ropeginterferon alfa-2b therapy, including retinopathy, retinal detachment, and retinal artery or vein occlusion resulting in blindness. Perform an ophthalmologic exam at baseline and periodically during therapy. Promptly evaluate patient-reported eye symptoms. Discontinue therapy in patients who develop new or worsening eye disorders.
    Headline
    renal failure, renal impairment
    General Information
    Avoid use of ropeginterferon alfa-2b therapy in patients with severe renal impairment (eGFR less than 30 mL/minute), including renal failure. Renal toxicity has been reported with ropeginterferon alfa-2b therapy. Monitor renal function tests (e.g., serum creatinine) at baseline and during therapy. Therapy discontinuation may be necessary based on severity.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Based on its mechanism of action and animal findings, ropeginterferon alfa-2b may cause fetal harm and should be considered to have abortifacient potential. Human data on ropeginterferon alfa-2b use during pregnancy are insufficient to determine a drug-associated risk of major birth defects, miscarriage, or adverse outcomes. However, maternal toxicity, including decreased food intake and transient weight loss, occurred in pregnant cynomolgus monkeys given ropeginterferon alfa-2b twice weekly during organogenesis at all dose levels. Embryonic death occurred at exposures 275 times (Cmax) and 64 times (AUC) the human exposure at the maximum recommended human dose (MRHD). In surviving fetuses, no effects on development or structural abnormalities were observed, despite first-trimester exposures up to 961 times (Cmax) and 224 times (AUC) the MRHD. Untreated polycythemia vera during pregnancy is associated with increased risks, including thrombosis, hemorrhage, and miscarriage. Pregnant patients should be counseled on the potential fetal risks of treatment as well as the risks of untreated disease.
    Headline
    people who may become pregnant, reproductive risk
    General Information
    Counsel people who may become pregnant about the reproductive risk associated with ropeginterferon alfa-2b. Ropeginterferon alfa-2b may cause fetal harm. Verify pregnancy status prior to treatment initiation. Advise people who may become pregnant to use effective contraception while taking ropeginterferon alfa-2b and for 8 weeks after the last dose. Based on its mechanism of action, ropeginterferon alfa-2b may cause disruption of the menstrual cycle. Fertility studies have not been conducted.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Advise patients to avoid breast-feeding while taking ropeginterferon alfa-2b and for 8 weeks after the last dose due to the potential for adverse reactions in the breastfed child. There are no data on the presence of ropeginterferon alfa-2b in human or animal milk, its effects on the breastfed child, or its effects on milk production.

    chills, fever, infection, influenza, pharyngitis, rhinitis
    General Information
    Life-threatening or fatal infections have been reported in patients who received interferon alfa products, including ropeginterferon alfa-2b. In pooled safety populations across clinical trials, infection (45% to 48%; grade 3 or higher, 4% to 8%) and influenza-like illness (11%) were reported in patients treated with ropeginterferon alfa-2b. Bacterial infection (24%; grade 3 or higher, 5%) and nasopharyngitis (13%) were reported in patients with essential thrombocythemia treated with ropeginterferon alfa-2b in a clinical trial; fever (26%) also occurred.

    Headline
    ascites, elevated hepatic enzymes, hepatic decompensation, hepatic encephalopathy, hepatitis, jaundice
    General Information
    Hepatotoxicity has been reported in patients who received interferon alfa products, including ropeginterferon alfa-2b. In the pooled safety population for essential thrombocythemia, elevated hepatic enzymes including AST (44%; grade 3 or higher, 1.1%) and ALT (43%; grade 3 or higher, 2.7%), increased gamma-glutamyl transferase levels (16%), and increased blood bilirubin (grade 3 or higher, 0.5%) were reported in patients treated with ropeginterferon alfa-2b; ALT levels 3 times the ULN or higher (20%) and AST levels 3 times the ULN or higher (9%) occurred. Grade 4 hepatitis was reported in 1 patient, with a time to recovery of 11 days. In a pooled safety population from clinical trials for polycythemia vera, elevated hepatic enzymes (20%) were reported in patients who received ropeginterferon alfa-2b; some cases were reported after long-term use. Most of these patients had increased hepatic enzyme levels of 1.25 to 5 times the ULN and were able to resume therapy upon toxicity resolution. Therapy interruption, a dosage reduction, or discontinuation may be necessary in patients who develop hepatotoxicity. Discontinue ropeginterferon alfa-2b in patients who develop evidence of hepatic decompensation (e.g., jaundice, ascites, hepatic encephalopathy, hepatorenal syndrome, or variceal hemorrhage) or evidence of Child-Pugh class B or C hepatic impairment during therapy.

Description
  Text
  Ropeginterferon alfa-2b-njft is an interferon agent administered as a subcutaneous injection. It is indicated for the treatment of adult patients with polycythemia vera, and it is also indicated for adults with essential thrombocythemia. Ropeginterferon alfa-2b has a boxed warning for fatal or life-threatening neuropsychiatric, autoimmune, ischemic, and infectious disorders. It was approved by the FDA in November 2021.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Ropeginterferon alfa-2b is a N-terminal monopegylated covalent conjugate of proline interferon alfa-2b with a methoxy polyethylene glycol (mPEG) moiety. It is produced via recombinant DNA technology using Escherichia coli. Ropeginterferon alfa-2b works by binding to a transmembrane receptor termed interferon alfa receptor (IFNAR) in the bone marrow, initiating a downstream signaling cascade through the activation of kinases (i.e., Janus kinase (JAK)-1 and tyrosine kinase (TYK)-2), and activator of transcription (STAT) proteins. Nuclear translocation of STAT proteins controls specific gene-expression and various other cellular effects. The mechanism of action of ropeginterferon alfa-2b for the treatment of polycythemia vera and essential thrombocythemia is not wholly known.

Prefilled Syringe
Preparation
Allow the refrigerated prefilled syringe to warm to room temperature (15 to 25 degrees C; 59 to 77 degrees F) for 15 to 30 minutes; keep the syringe in the carton and lay it flat on a clean work surface.
Remove the prefilled syringe cap and attach the covered needle.
Uncap the needle and move air bubbles to the top.
If applicable, discard some of the medication to achieve the prescribed dose; push the syringe plunger to align the top of the gray stopper to the correct syringe dose line/numbered markings.
Subcutaneous injection
After cleaning the injection site with an alcohol swab and letting it air dry, inject the prescribed dose subcutaneously into the lower stomach/abdomen (at least 2 inches away from the belly button) or the top of thighs.
Rotate the injection site; do not inject into skin that is irritated, red, bruised, infected, or scarred.
Do not recap the needle; dispose of the used syringe with the needle still attached into an FDA-cleared sharps disposal container.
 
Prefilled Pen Injector
Preparation
Allow the refrigerated prefilled pen injector to warm to room temperature (15 to 25 degrees C; 59 to 77 degrees F) for 15 to 30 minutes; keep the pen injector in the carton and lay it flat on a clean work surface
Remove the prefilled pen injector cap by pulling it straight off.
Clean the rubber stopper on the tip of the prefilled pen injector with an alcohol swab; peel off the protective seal from the needle.
Attach the capped needle by pushing it straight onto the prefilled pen injector until it snaps into place; turn or rotate the needle until it clicks into place and will no longer turn or rotate freely. Remove the needle cover.
Turn the dose selector knob until the drop symbol is centered in the dose window. Hold the prefilled pen injector with the needle pointing up and press the injection button all the way in until it stops and the dose window returns to zero ("0"). Repeat these steps 3 more times for a total of 4 cycles.
If liquid medicine is not at the tip of the needle, repeat the above steps up to 3 more times for a total of 7 cycles or until there is liquid medicine at the tip of the needle.
Subcutaneous injection
Set the dose by turning the dose selector clockwise until the prescribed dose is centered in the dose window. If the prescribed dose is passed, the dose selector may be dialed back to the correct dose.
Hold the prefilled pen injector straight (90 degrees) over the cleaned injection site and push the prefilled pen injector down to fully insert the needle without touching the injection button.
Press and hold the injection button all the way until it stops; the number in the dose window will go back to zero ("0").
Keep holding the injection button down for 5 seconds to make sure the full dose is delivered. There may still be liquid remaining in the pen injector after the injection.
Remove the needle from the skin by lifting straight up.
Do not recap the needle; dispose of the used pen injector with the needle still attached into an FDA-cleared sharps disposal container.

  Hepatic Impairment
  Baseline Hepatic Impairment

Treatment-Related Hepatotoxicity (Polycythemia Vera)
NOTE: For treatment-related toxicity, reduce the dosage to the next lower level or interrupt therapy per guidance below. If there is insufficient efficacy at a reduced dosage, consider increasing the ropeginterferon alfa-2b dosage (e.g., increase dosage by 50 mcg every 2 weeks) when the toxicity has recovered to grade 1.
Elevated hepatic enzymes with concomitant increased bilirubin level OR other evidence of hepatic decompensation: Hold therapy until toxicity recovery. Restart dose at 50 mcg lower than the interrupted dose. If the interrupted dose is 50 mcg, hold therapy until toxicity recovery. Consider permanently discontinuing ropeginterferon alfa-2b if toxicity persists after 4 dosage reductions.
Elevated hepatic enzymes greater than 5 to 20 times the ULN: Reduce dose by 50 mcg every 2 weeks until toxicity recovers as follows: ALT and AST levels recover to less than 3 times the ULN and gamma-glutamyltransferase (GGT) level recovers to less than 2.5 times the ULN if transaminase and GGT levels were normal at baseline. In patients with abnormal transaminase or GGT levels at baseline, reduce the ropeginterferon alfa-2b dosage when ALT and AST levels recover to 3 times baseline levels and the GGT level recovers to 2.5 times the baseline level. If the interrupted dose is 50 mcg every 2 weeks, hold therapy until toxicity recovery.
Elevated hepatic enzymes greater than 20 times the ULN: Hold therapy until toxicity recovery as follows: ALT and AST levels recover to less than 3 times the ULN and GGT level recovers to less than 2.5 times the ULN if transaminase and GGT levels were normal at baseline. In patients with abnormal transaminase or GGT levels at baseline, hold therapy until ALT and AST levels recover to 1.5 times baseline levels and the GGT level recovers to 2 times the baseline level. Consider re-escalation to the previous dose level if toxicity recovers to grade 1 or less and if patient is a nonresponder. Consider permanently discontinuing ropeginterferon alfa-2b if toxicity persists after 4 dosage reductions.
Evidence of Child-Pugh class B or C hepatic impairment: Discontinue ropeginterferon alfa-2b therapy.
