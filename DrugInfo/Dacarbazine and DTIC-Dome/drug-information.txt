Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  General Information
  Dacarbazine therapy is contraindicated in patients with a history of dacarbazine (DTIC) hypersensitivity.Dacarbazine therapy is commonly associated with bone marrow suppression, especially neutropenia and thrombocytopenia, but anemia may also occur. Patients should be allowed to recover their blood cell counts to normal prior to dacarbazine administration. Dacarbazine should be used cautiously in patients who have had previous myelosuppressive therapy such as chemotherapy or radiation therapy. Close monitoring of hematologic parameters is necessary during dacarbazine therapy. Dacarbazine should be administered under the supervision of a qualified physician experienced in the use of cancer chemotherapeutic agents. Patients with an active infection should be treated prior to receiving dacarbazine. Patients with a history of varicella zoster, other herpes infection (e.g., herpes simplex), or other viral infection are at risk for reactivation of the infection when treated with chemotherapy.Intramuscular injections should not be administered to patients with platelet counts = 2 weeks. The administration of live vaccines to immunocompromised patients should be avoided. Those undergoing chemotherapy should not be exposed to others who have recently received the oral poliovirus vaccine (OPV). Measles-mumps-rubella (MMR) vaccination is not contraindicated for the close contacts, including health care professionals, of immunocompromised patients. Passive immunoprophylaxis with immune globulins may be indicated for immunocompromised persons instead of, or in addition to, vaccination. When exposed to a vaccine-preventable disease such as measles, severely immunocompromised children should be considered susceptible regardless of their vaccination history.Dacarbazine is classified as FDA pregnancy category C. Although adequate studies in humans have not been conducted, administration of antineoplastic agents, particularly combination treatment, during the first trimester of pregnancy should be avoided, if possible. Animal studies have indicated teratogenic effects in doses higher than human doses. In addition, adverse reactions that are observed in adults receiving dacarbazine potentially can occur in the fetus.Although it is unknown whether dacarbazine is excreted into breast milk, its use during breast-feeding is generally not recommended due to its carcinogenic and mutagenic effects.Dacarbazine has been shown to be carcinogenic in animal studies. The development of a secondary malignancy is a possibility following dacarbazine therapy.

    Headline
    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components.
    Headline
    serious adverse effects
    General Information
    Dacarbazine may cause serious adverse effects including potentially fatal hemopoietic depression/bone marrow suppression and hepatic necrosis. Monitor complete blood cell counts (e.g., white blood cells, red blood cells, platelets) and liver function tests during treatment.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Dacarbazine may cause fetal harm when used during pregnancy. In animal studies, dacarbazine was teratogenic in rats at doses 20 times the human daily dose on day 12 of gestation. In rabbits, fetal skeletal anomalies were observed in rabbits given doses 7 times the human daily dose on days 6 to 15 of gestation. There are no adequate and well-controlled studies of dacarbazine use during human pregnancy. The American Society of Clinical Oncology provides additional information on the use of certain medications for the management of cancer during pregnancy.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Advise patients to avoid breast-feeding during the use of dacarbazine. There are no data on the presence of dacarbazine in human milk, its effects on the breastfed child, or its effects on milk production. However, dacarbazine has the potential to cause serious side effects in the breastfed child; tumorigenicity was seen in animal trials. Consider the benefits of breast-feeding, the patient's clinical need for treatment, and any potential adverse effects on the breastfed child from the medication or from the patient's underlying medical condition.

Adverse Reactions
  Headline
  Adverse Reactions
  General Information
  Adverse GI effects occur in as many as 90% of patients receiving dacarbazine; nausea/vomiting and anorexia will usually occur within 1 hour following IV administration and can last for up to 12 hours. Tolerance to these effects usually occurs within 1 to 2 days and is less with subsequent cycles.The hematological status of patients undergoing chemotherapy should be closely monitored for signs of hematologic toxicity such as pancytopenia, leukopenia, neutropenia, anemia, and thrombocytopenia. Anemia usually is not severe. Neutropenia and thrombocytopenia reach a nadir around days 21—25.Signs of hepatotoxicity have been reported in patients receiving dacarbazine alone or in combination with other chemotherapeutic agents. These effects are not common but can be severe including hepatic failure, thrombosis, or an allergic hepatic vasculitis.Tissue irritation, phlebitis, and pain at the injection site can occur if dacarbazine extravasates. An injection site reaction consisting of urticaria may also occur. Cold packs above the injection site may relieve local pain, burning, or irritation. Dacarbazine is classified as an irritant not a vesicant.Patients can develop malaise, myalgia, and fever approximately 1 week after dacarbazine therapy. The effects may not dissipate for 3 weeks.With high-dose dacarbazine therapy, hypotension has been noted and may require close observation.Photosensitivity involving the head and hands has been noted during therapy with dacarbazine. This effect is not common but can be extremely uncomfortable.

Description
  Text
  Dacarbazine is a purine analog that acts like an alkylating agent. It is approved for the treatment of metastatic melanoma and Hodgkin lymphoma. Dacarbazine may cause serious adverse effects including potentially fatal myelosuppression and hepatic necrosis. Dacarbazine was approved by the FDA in 1975.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Dacarbazine is a cell cycle nonspecific antineoplastic agent with similarities to purine analogs that functions as an alkylating agent after activation. It requires metabolic activation to exert its cytotoxic effects. Although the exact mechanism of action is not known, dacarbazine is hypothesized to inhibit DNA synthesis by purine analog effects, alkylating activity, and interaction with sulfhydryl groups.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics

    Intravenous Route
    Description
    After intravenous administration, the volume of distribution exceeds total body water content, which suggests localization in tissues such as the liver. Excretion of unchanged dacarbazine in the urine is 40% of the injected dose in 6 hours, primarily by renal tubular secretion.

  Text
  Dacarbazine is administered intravenously. At therapeutic concentrations, dacarbazine is not appreciably bound to human plasma protein. Dacarbazine is extensively degraded. Besides unchanged dacarbazine, 5-aminoimidazole -4 carboxamide (AIC) is a major metabolite excreted in the urine. The disappearance of dacarbazine from the plasma is biphasic with an initial half-life of 19 minutes and a terminal half-life of 5 hours.
