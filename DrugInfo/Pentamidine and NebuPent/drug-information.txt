Drug Class: Pentamidine isethionate is an antifungal and antiprotozoal agent of the aromatic diamidine-derivative class.

Routes: inhalation, injection (IV/IM/SC); Common adult doses: 4 mg/kg/day IM/IV; 300 mg nebulized once every 4 weeks.

Common Side Effects (excluding routine GI upset): azotemia, flank pain, hematuria, nephrotoxicity, renal failure, urinary incontinence, hyperchloremic acidosis, hyperkalemia, hypocalcemia, hypomagnesemia, siadh, hypertension, hypotension, palpitations, peripheral vasodilation, sinus tachycardia, stroke, syncope, torsade de pointes, vasculitis, ventricular tachycardia, injection site reaction, phlebitis, skin ulcer, tissue necrosis, anaphylactoid reactions, erythema, pruritus, rash, stevens-johnson syndrome

In an Overdose: No overdose/antidote information is listed in this file.

Key Nursing Considerations: Contraindications/precautions: Intramuscular or rapid intravenous administration of pentamidine is associated with hypotension, which can occur following the initial dose. Patients should be in a supine position.

Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  General Information
  Intramuscular or rapid intravenous administration of pentamidine is associated with hypotension, which can occur following the initial dose. Patients should be in a supine position. Blood pressure should be monitored frequently until it stabilizes. Pentamidine for inhalation can also cause hypotension. Prolonged therapy with pentamidine has been associated with dysrhythmias (see Adverse Reactions). Equipment for airway maintenance and other supportive measures should be readily available.
 
Pentamidine can cause cardiac arrhythmias, ventricular tachycardia, QT prolongation, torsades de pointes, as well as other cardiac side effects (see Adverse Reactions). Pentamidine is considered to be associated with a well-established risk for QT prolongation and torsades de pointes (TdP). Pentamidine should be avoided where possible in patients patients with congenital or acquired QT prolongation syndromes or a history of torsade de pointes. Patients receiving this medication who have cardiac disease or arrhythmias should be monitored closely, and ECGs should be given during and after parenteral pentamidine therapy.
 
Pentamidine is toxic to pancreatic cells (see Adverse Reactions). Patients receiving intravenous pentamidine can become acutely hypoglycemic immediately following the infusion. Patients with diabetes mellitus, patients with preexisting pancreatitis, or those with hypoglycemia/hyperglycemia should receive pentamidine with caution. Patients should be monitored for hypoglycemia for 4—6 hours following administration of pentamidine via any route.
 
Patients with a history of bleeding disorders, anemia, or bone marrow suppression should receive intravenous pentamidine with caution, since it can cause thrombocytopenia, leukopenia, and/or anemia. Risk-benefit should be considered in patients who have had previous radiation therapy or cytotoxic therapy.
 
Although pentamidine can be administered via intramuscular injection, caution should be used when administering intramuscular injections to patients receiving pentamidine. IM injections may cause bleeding, bruising, or hematomas due to thrombocytopenia secondary to pentamidine-induced bone marrow suppression
 
Pentamidine should be used with caution in patients with dental disease. Pentamidine can cause myelosuppression and there may be an increased risk of infection. Dental work should be performed prior to initiating pentamidine therapy or deferred until blood counts return to normal. Patients should be instructed on proper oral hygiene.
 
Parenteral pentamidine can cause azotemia or acute renal insufficiency (see Adverse Reactions). It should be used with caution in patients with preexisting renal disease. Nephrotoxicity can be worsened by dehydration, so patients should be well hydrated before therapy.
 
Pentamidine can cause elevations in AST, ALT, bilirubin, and/or alkaline phosphatase. Patients with preexisting hepatic disease may be especially vulnerable to the effects of this medication.
 
Asthmatic patients can experience bronchospasm when taking aerosolized pentamidine. Caution should be used in patients with asthma. Pretreatment with a bronchodilator may reduce the risk of bronchospasm.
 
Pentamidine is classified as a pregnancy category C drug. Adequate and well-controlled studies in humans have not been done, although pentamidine is known to cross the placenta in rats and has been shown to be mildly embryotoxic in rabbits at doses of 1, 3, and 8 mg/kg body weight.
 
It is not known if pentamidine is excreted in breast milk. Breast-feeding is not recommended during pentamidine therapy due to the potentially serious adverse reactions, unless the benefits outweigh the unknown risk.

    Drug Interactions
    General Information
    The coadministration of certain medications may lead to harm and require avoidance or therapy modification; review all drug interactions prior to concomitant use of other medications.
    Headline
    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components.
    Headline
    bradycardia, cardiomyopathy, congenital long QT syndrome, coronary artery disease, females, geriatric, hypocalcemia, hypokalemia, hypomagnesemia, QT prolongation, ventricular tachycardia
    General Information
    Avoid use of pentamidine in people with baseline QT prolongation or who have conditions that may increase the risk of QT prolongation or torsade de pointes, including bradycardia, congenital long QT syndrome, hypocalcemia, hypokalemia, hypomagnesemia, geriatric adults, females, structural abnormalities that interfere with electrical conduction (e.g., cardiomyopathy, coronary artery disease, ischemic heart disease), or in those who have other additional risk factors for QT prolongation or torsade de pointes. The use of other medications that have been associated with QT prolongation or torsade de pointes may further increase risk. Additionally, use pentamidine with caution in individuals with ventricular tachycardia. Monitor serum calcium before, during, and after pentamidine therapy.
    Headline
    hypertension, hypotension
    General Information
    Use pentamidine with caution in individuals with hypotension or hypertension. Fatalities due to severe hypotension have been reported in individuals treated with parenteral pentamidine. Monitor blood pressure closely during pentamidine administration and several times thereafter until the blood pressure is stable. Ensure equipment for emergency resuscitation is readily available.
    Headline
    asthma, tobacco smoking
    General Information
    Individuals with asthma or a history of tobacco smoking may be at higher risk for bronchospasm or cough when receiving aerosolized pentamidine. Pretreatment with a bronchodilator may reduce the risk of these symptoms.
    Headline
    hyperglycemia, hypoglycemia, pancreatitis
    General Information
    Use pentamidine with caution in individuals with hyperglycemia, hypoglycemia, or pancreatitis. Fatalities due to hypoglycemia and acute pancreatitis have been reported with parenteral pentamidine. Monitor blood glucose concentrations at baseline, daily during pentamidine therapy, and periodically after treatment discontinuation.
    Headline
    anemia, leukopenia, thrombocytopenia
    General Information
    Use pentamidine with caution in individuals with anemia, leukopenia, or thrombocytopenia. Monitor complete blood count before, during, and after pentamidine therapy.
    Headline
    hepatic failure
    General Information
    Use pentamidine with caution in individuals with hepatic dysfunction or hepatic failure. The efficacy or safety of alternative pentamidine dosing protocols have not been established for individuals with impaired hepatic function. Monitor liver function tests before, during, and after pentamidine therapy.
    Headline
    renal failure, renal impairment
    General Information
    Use pentamidine with caution in individuals with renal impairment or renal failure. The efficacy or safety of alternative pentamidine dosing protocols have not been established for individuals with impaired renal function. Monitor blood urea nitrogen and serum creatinine at baseline, daily during pentamidine therapy, and after treatment discontinuation.
    Headline
    Stevens-Johnson syndrome
    General Information
    Use pentamidine with caution in individuals with Stevens-Johnson syndrome.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    It is not known whether pentamidine can cause fetal harm when administered during pregnancy. Animal reproduction studies have not been conducted. A literature report described embryo-lethality in pregnant rats given intravenous pentamidine at 4 mg/kg/day; teratogenicity was not observed in that study. It is unknown whether aerosolized pentamidine crosses the placenta at clinically significant concentrations. Administer pentamidine during pregnancy only when the potential benefits outweigh the potential fetal risks.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Use pentamidine with caution during breast-feeding. There are no data on the presence of pentamidine in human milk, its effects on the breast-fed child, or its effects on milk production. However, pentamidine has the potential to cause serious side effects in the breast-fed child. Consider the benefits of breast-feeding, the patient's clinical need for treatment, and any potential adverse effects on the breast-fed child from the medication or from the patient's underlying medical condition.

Since pentamidine may cause QT prolongation independently of electrolyte imbalances, the risk for cardiac arrhythmias is potentiated by the concomitant use of agents associated with electrolyte loss. Closely monitor serum electrolytes during pentamidine therapy.
Vancomycin: (Major) Concomitant use of parenteral vancomycin with other nephrotoxic drugs, such as systemic pentamidine, can lead to additive nephrotoxicity. Renal function should be monitored closely and vancomycin doses should be adjusted according to vancomycin serum concentrations.
Vandetanib: (Major) Avoid coadministration of vandetanib with pentamidine due to an increased risk of QT prolongation and torsade de pointes (TdP). If concomitant use is unavoidable, monitor ECGs for QT prolongation and monitor electrolytes; correct hypocalcemia, hypomagnesemia, and/or hypomagnesemia prior to vandetanib administration. An interruption of vandetanib therapy or dose reduction may be necessary for QT prolongation. Avoid concomitant use if possible, especially in patients with additional risk factors for TdP. Consider taking steps to minimize the risk for QT/QTc interval prolongation and TdP, such as electrolyte monitoring and repletion and ECG monitoring, if concomitant use is necessary.
Vasopressin, ADH: (Moderate) Monitor hemodynamics and adjust the dose of vasopressin as needed when used concomitantly with drugs suspected of causing syndrome of inappropriate antidiuretic hormone (SIADH), such as pentamidine. Use together may increase the pressor and antidiuretic effects of vasopressin.
Vemurafenib: (Major) Vemurafenib has been associated with QT prolongation. If vemurafenib and another drug that is associated with a possible risk for QT prolongation and torsade de pointes (TdP) must be coadministered, ECG monitoring is recommended; closely monitor the patient for QT interval prolongation. Pentamidine has been associated with QT prolongation. Pentamidine is a CYP2D6 substrate and vemurafenib is a weak CYP2D6 inhibitor; therefore, increased pentamidine concentrations may occur.

Voriconazole: (Major) Voriconazole has been associated with QT prolongation and rare cases of torsades de pointes. Drugs with a possible risk for QT prolongation and TdP that should be used cautiously and with close monitoring with voriconazole include pentamidine. Also, voriconazole is an inhibitor of the CYP 2C19 isoenzyme and interactions are possible with agents that are substrates of this enzyme, such as pentamidine; increased plasma levels of pentamidine may occur.

  Headline
  Adverse Reactions
  General Information
  Adverse reactions to pentamidine are most common after parenteral administration, however, many are possible after administration of the nebulized form of the drug.Nephrotoxicity occurs in at least 25% of patients receiving parenteral pentamidine for Pneumocystis pneumonia. Azotemia and renal tubular necrosis can occur. Renal insufficiency is usually only mild to moderate and returns to normal following pentamidine discontinuation. Dehydration or the use of other nephrotoxic drugs can increase the potential for nephrotoxicity. If renal function worsens, pentamidine dose adjustment is usually not necessary, since only small quantities of the drug are eliminated renally.Several electrolyte disorders have been reported during pentamidine therapy. These include hypocalcemia and hypomagnesemia. Hyperkalemia is reported in up to 100% of patients with AIDS receiving pentamidine for 6 or more days. Hyperkalemia is often associated with hyperchloremic acidosis. The degree of hyperkalemia is usually out of proportion to the degree of coexisting renal impairment. Luminal pentamidine inhibits reabsorption of sodium in the distal nephron by blocking apical sodium channels, an action that is similar to potassium-sparing diuretics. Pentamidine decreases the electrochemical gradients that drive secretion of distal nephron potassium, thus leading to increased serum potassium concentrations.Hypotension can occur following IV or IM administration of pentamidine. Ventricular tachycardia also can occur. Rapid injection or infusion can increase the incidence of hypotension and/or arrhythmias. ECG changes associated with pentamidine therapy include QT prolongation. Parenteral pentamidine has been associated with torsade de pointes independently of electrolyte imbalances in an immunosuppressed renal transplant patient and in patients with AIDS. Intravenous pentamidine should always be given over a period of 60 minutes, and the appropriate equipment should be on hand should severe hypotension or arrhythmias occur.Intramuscular administration of pentamidine can lead to abscess, pain, erythema, and induration. Phlebitis can occur following IV administration. Patients should be observed for any injection site reaction after IM injection.Pruritus, erythema, xerosis, urticaria, and, rarely, Stevens-Johnson syndrome have been reported in patients receiving pentamidine.Pentamidine is toxic to pancreatic cells and can cause pancreatitis. Pancreatitis has been reported after both parenteral and aerosolized pentamidine. In 5—10% of patients, hypoglycemia as low as 25 mg/dl occurred shortly after administration of parenteral pentamidine. This effect is believed to result from excessive insulin release. After prolonged therapy, pancreatic toxicity progresses and a relative insulin deficit ensues. Hyperglycemia and insulin-dependent diabetes mellitus that can be permanent have also been documented in patients after either parenteral or nebulized pentamidine. Blood sugar should be monitored for several hours after parenteral doses of pentamidine, especially in patients who are NPO. Administration just prior to bedtime should be discouraged.Leukopenia and thrombocytopenia can occur in patients receiving parenteral pentamidine. Anemia also can occur and has been associated with both parenteral and nebulized forms of the medication.Nausea/vomiting, anorexia, and occasionally dysgeusia, manifested as a metallic taste, can occur from pentamidine. Sialadenitis also has been associated with pentamidine therapy.Some of the most common central nervous system side effects reported in patients receiving pentamidine theapy include confusion, dizziness, and neuralgia. Fatigue has also been reported in patients receiving pentamidine by inhalation.Cough and bronchospasm have been reported from inhaled pentamidine. These side effects are more common in patients with asthma and can be alleviated by using a bronchodilator before inhaling the pentamidine.

Description
  Text
  Pentamidine isethionate is an antifungal and antiprotozoal agent of the aromatic diamidine-derivative class. It is structurally and pharmacologically similar to stilbamidine. Pentamidine is indicated for the treatment and prophylaxis of Pneumocystis pneumonia (PCP). It is also used off-label for trypanosomiasis (African sleeping sickness) and leishmaniasis. Pentamidine is available as an injection and for oral inhalation, although parenteral administration is associated with several major adverse reactions.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Pentamidine's mechanism of action is not clearly known and can vary, depending on the organism being treated. Pentamidine may interfere with the incorporation of nucleotides into both DNA and RNA. It also may inhibit oxidative phosphorylation and the biosynthesis of DNA, RNA, proteins, and phospholipids. Finally, pentamidine may have folate-antagonist actions, since other agents that are effective against Pneumocystis (e.g., TMP-SMX, pyrimethamine, and trimetrexate) are also folate antagonists.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Intravenous Route
    Description
    After a single 4 mg/kg IV dose of pentamidine, peak plasma pentamidine concentrations are approximately 0.6 mcg/mL after completion of the IV infusion. Following multiple IV doses for 7 days, compared to the mean AUC on day 1, the mean AUC on day 4 and 7 were about 2- and 3-fold higher, respectively, suggesting steady state was not achieved by day 7 of dosing. The half-life of intravenous pentamidine is approximately 6.5 hours.
    Name
    Intramuscular Route
    Description
    After a single 4 mg/kg IM dose of pentamidine, peak plasma pentamidine concentrations are approximately 0.2 mcg/mL. The Tmax after IM injection is approximately 1 hour. Peak steady-state concentrations after repeated doses of 4 mg/kg/day reach 0.5 mcg/mL. The half-life of intramuscular pentamidine is approximately 9.5 hours.
    Name
    Inhalation Route
    Description
    Systemic absorption of inhaled pentamidine is minimal. After a nebulized dose of 4 mg/kg, serum pentamidine concentrations average less than 20 ng/mL. The extent of pentamidine accumulation and distribution following chronic inhalation therapy is unknown. There is no information available on the elimination of nebulized pentamidine.
  Special Populations
    Name
    Hepatic Impairment
    Description
    Pharmacokinetic data are unavailable for pentamidine use in patients with hepatic impairment.
    Name
    Renal Impairment
    Description
    Elimination of systemic pentamidine is primarily renal, and half-life may be prolonged in patients with renal impairment. However, no correlation between renal function and plasma clearance of pentamidine has been found. Plasma pentamidine concentrations are not reduced by hemodialysis or peritoneal dialysis. Pharmacokinetic data are unavailable following aerosol administration of pentamidine in patients with renal impairment.
  Text
  Pentamidine is poorly absorbed from the gastrointestinal tract and must be given parenterally or by inhalation to be effective. Pentamidine has a large Vd and parenteral doses of pentamidine are widely distributed into body tissues and fluids. The highest concentrations are found in the liver, followed by the kidneys, adrenals, spleen, lungs, and pancreas. Pentamidine is thought to have poor CNS penetration. The protein binding of pentamidine is approximately 70%. Biotransformation of pentamidine in humans is unknown. Pentamidine was extensively metabolized in rats, with at least six metabolites identified. Elimination of systemic drug is primarily renal. Only a small amount of drug (2.5% to 5%) is excreted unchanged in the urine over 24 hours. Urinary excretion may persist for up to 6 to 8 weeks after discontinuation. Fecal excretion appears to be minimal. Elimination half-lives vary depending on the route of administration. Pentamidine appears to be eliminated very slowly from tissues in which the drug principally accumulates (e.g., liver, lungs). The terminal half-life is approximately 10 to 12 days, although a large variability has been documented. Multiple dosing may result in drug accumulation, even in patients with normal renal function.
 
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes and drug transporters: CYP3A5, CYP2D6, CYP2C19, CYP1A1, CYP4A11
Previous data suggest that pentamidine was metabolized by the CYP2C19 isoenzyme; however, this has not been confirmed. In vitro data suggest that the CYP1A1, 3A5, and 4A11 isoenzymes may contribute to the metabolism of pentamidine, but data from healthy volunteers show that the CYP1A1 and 2D6 are the mediating enzymes in humans.
