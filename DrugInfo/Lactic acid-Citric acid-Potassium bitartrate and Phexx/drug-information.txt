    General Information
    The majority of adverse reactions associated with the use of lactic acid; citric acid; potassium bitartrate vaginal gel were mild and few reported events led to discontinuation. The most common adverse reactions associated with the use of the gel in the U.S. population in Studies 1 and 2 (n = 2,480) were vulvovaginal burning sensation (18%) and vulvovaginal pruritis (14.5%). The following adverse reactions were reported in 2% or more of patients who used lactic acid; citric acid; potassium bitartrate vaginal gel: vulvovaginal mycotic infection (including vulvovaginal candidiasis) (9.1%), urinary tract infection (9%), vulvo-vaginal irritation or discomfort (9%), bacterial vaginosis (8.4%), vaginal discharge (5.5%), genital discomfort (4.1%), dysuria (3.1%), and vulvo-vaginal pain (2.1%). Among 2,804 subjects who received the gel in the clinical trials, 0.36% (n = 10) reported adverse reactions of cystitis, pyelonephritis, or other upper urinary tract infection (UTI). Of these, 1 case of pyelonephritis was considered serious and required hospitalization. The discontinuation rate in the trials was 1.6%. The most common adverse reactions leading to study discontinuation were vulvovaginal burning sensation (0.7%); and vulvovaginal pruritus or discomfort (0.1% each). In clinical studies, 1 subject out of 2,804 subjects reported a suspected hypersensitivity. Male partners also reported adverse reactions; 9.8% (131 of 1,330) reported symptoms of local discomfort (burning, itching, pain, and "other", e.g., penile irritation). Of these local discomfort symptoms, 74.7% were mild, 21.4% were moderate, and 3.9% were severe. Two subjects discontinued participation in the study due to male partner symptoms.

Description
  Text
  Lactic acid, citric acid, and potassium bitartrate are used in combination as a non-hormonal vaginal contraceptive gel that is a vaginal pH modulator. The gel is indicated for the prevention of pregnancy in people of reproductive potential for use as an 'on-demand' method of contraception, prior to sexual intercourse. This gel is not effective for the prevention of pregnancy when administered after intercourse. The estmated efficacy rate is 86% with "typical-use", defined as cycles with inconsistent or incorrect use and sexual intercourse occurring at least once per month. In people who use the gel as "perfect-use", defined as following the directions exactly and consistently with no additional methods of contraception, and sexual intercourse reported at least once per month, the efficacy rate is 98.7%. The gel does not protect against the transmission of human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) infection or the transmission of any sexually-transmitted disease, including gonorrhea or chlamydia. The Centers for Disease Control's U.S. Medical Eligibility Criteria describe considerations for risk vs. benefits, including medical conditions or attributes that contraindicate use; these criteria can help prescribing practitioners in product selection for individual patients; vaginal pH modulators have essentially no restrictions for use and are compatible with breast-feeding. The FDA approved Lactic acid, citric acid, and potassium bitartrate contraceptive gel in May 2020.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  In vitro studies showed that lactic acid; citric acid; potassium bitartrate vaginal gel produced a normal vaginal pH range (pH 3.5 to 4.5) in the prescience of semen. In clinical studies, post-coital testing demonstrated a pH less than 5 in the majority of subjects, and sperm motility reduction. The gel is designed to maintain an acidic environment in the vagina that is generally inhospitable to sperm.
 
Contraceptive effectiveness is an important parameter to consider in determining contraceptive choice. Most 'on-demand' contraceptives have lower overall efficacy than routine hormonal contraceptives. During clinical trials of the contraceptive vaginal gel, the primary efficacy endpoint was the 7-cycle 'typical use' cumulative pregnancy rate as derived by Kaplan-Meier life-table analysis. A total of 101 on-treatment pregnancies occurred in 1,183 subjects, contributing 4,769 evaluable natural cycles. The 7-cycle cumulative pregnancy rate was 13.7% (95% CI: 10.0%, 17.5%), excluding cycles with back-up contraception, cycles less than 21 days or more than 35 days in length, and cycles in which no intercourse was reported. The Pearl Index, typically defined as the number of contraceptive failures per 100 women-years of exposure, was also estimated from the data. The estimated Pearl Index, calculated based on data from the 7-cycle "typical use' study, was 27.5 (95% CI: 22.4%, 33.5%).

Pharmacokinetics
  Text
  Lactic acid; citric acid; potassium bitartrate gel is administered vaginally. Pharmacokinetic studies in humans have not been performed. Systemic exposures of lactic acid, citric acid, and potassium bitartrate following vaginal administration are not expected to lead to safety concerns. In vitro studies with commonly used vaginal preparations (miconazole, metronidazole, tioconazole, and a product for maintaining normal vaginal pH) showed no significant effect on the pH or buffering capacity of lactic acid; citric acid; potassium bitartrate gel.

    Description
    Vaginal Gel (Phexx) General Instructions
Insert 1 applicatorful of the gel into the vagina just before or up to 1 hour before each act of vaginal intercourse. A new applicatorful of the gel must be used if you have vaginal sex more than 1 time within 1 hour.
The gel may be used during any part of the menstrual cycle. It may be used as soon as it is safe to resume vaginal intercourse after childbirth, abortion, or miscarriage.
The gel may be used concomitantly with hormonal contraceptives; latex, polyurethane, and polyisoprene condoms; and vaginal diaphragms. Avoid use with vaginal rings.
Other products for vaginal infections including miconazole, metronidazole, and tioconazole may be used concomitantly with the vaginal gel.
 
Vaginal Gel Administration
Wash your hands with soap and water before opening the foil pouch.
Remove the pre-filled applicator and plunger rod from the foil pouch; do NOT remove the pink cap.
Gently and slowly insert the plunger rod into the pre-filled applicator. Push until you feel the tip of the plunger rod connect to the inside of the pre-filled applicator. Do not push hard or continue to push after the tip of the plunger rod connects to the inside of the pre-filled applicator; this could cause the gel to go into the pink cap. Use a new pre-filled applicator if the gel goes into the pink cap.
After the plunger rod is connected to the pre-filled applicator, remove the pink cap from the pre-filled applicator. The extra space between the gel and the end of the pre-filled applicator is normal. The pre-filled applicator is now ready for use.
Hold the pre-filled applicator at the grooved area closest to the plunger rod. Gently insert the pre-filled applicator into the vagina as far as it will comfortably go while you continue to hold it by the grooved area firmly. This can be done sitting with your knees apart, lying on your back with your knees bent, or while standing with your feet apart or knees bent.
While the pre-filled applicator is inserted in your vagina, use your index finger to push the plunger rod down until it stops; this is to make sure you receive the entire dose. It is normal for a small amount of gel to be left in the applicator; you will still get the right dose.
Gently remove the plunger rod and pre-filled applicator from the vagina and throw away the used pre-filled applicator and caps should be disposed of in the trash.
