    serious hypersensitivity reactions or anaphylaxis
    General Information
    There is a risk of serious hypersensitivity reactions or anaphylaxis with the use of obiltoxaximab. Diphenydramine premedication is recommended prior to the administration of obiltoxaximab; however, diphenhydramine does not prevent anaphylaxis and may mask or delay the onset of symptoms of hypersensitivity. Administer obiltoxaximab in a monitored setting by personnel trained and equipped to manage anaphylaxis. Monitor throughout the infusion period and for a period of time after administration.
    Headline

    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    There are no data on the use of obiltoxaximab during pregnancy to inform on drug-associated risk. There are adverse effects on maternal and fetal outcomes associated with anthrax in pregnancy, including a high risk of maternal, fetal, and neonatal deaths, particularly in the absence of treatment. In pregnant rabbits, intravenous administration of obiltoxaximab during organogenesis was not associated with adverse developmental outcomes at up to 0.62 times the human systemic exposure at the maximum recommended adult dose.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Use obiltoxaximab with caution during breast-feeding. There are no data on the presence of obiltoxaximab in human milk, its effects on the breast-fed child, or its effects on milk production. Maternal IgG is known to be present in human milk. Consider the benefits of breast-feeding, the patient's clinical need for treatment, and any potential adverse effects on the breast-fed child from the medication or from the patient's underlying medical condition.

  Adverse Reactions
    Headline
    chest pain (unspecified), cough, cyanosis, dizziness, dysphonia, dyspnea, pruritus, rash, serious hypersensitivity reactions or anaphylaxis, urticaria
    General Information
    Serious hypersensitivity reactions or anaphylaxis has been reported with obiltoxaximab administration. Hypersensitivity reactions were the most common adverse events reported in obiltoxaximab clinical trials, occurring in 10.6% of patients. Anaphylaxis occurred during or immediately after the infusion in 0.9% of patients. Manifestations included rash (unspecified), urticaria, cough, dyspnea, cyanosis, postural dizziness, and chest discomfort. The obiltoxaximab infusion was discontinued in 2.5% of patients due to hypersensitivity or anaphylaxis. Adverse events reported in these patients included urticaria, rash, cough, pruritus, dizziness, throat irritation, dysphonia, dyspnea, and chest discomfort. Patients who received diphenhydramine premedication were less likely to experience adverse reactions compared to those who did not, including cough (1% vs. 8%), rash (2% vs. 7%), pruritus (3% vs. 4%), and throat irritation (0% vs. 3%). Overall, adverse events that could be related to hypersensitivity included pruritus (4%), cough (3%), urticaria (2%), chest pain (unspecified)/discomfort (less than 1.5%), dysphonia (less than 1.5%), dyspnea (less than 1.5%), cyanosis (less than 1.5%), and dizziness (less than 1.5%). Monitor closely throughout the infusion period and for a period of time after administration. If anaphylaxis or hypersensitivity reactions occur, stop the infusion immediately and treat appropriately.

    antibody formation
    General Information
    As with all therapeutic proteins, there is potential of immunogenicity with obiltoxaximab. Antibody formation was evaluated in clinical trials, and 2.5% of patients who received at least 1 dose of obiltoxaximab were positive for a treatment-emergent anti-therapeutic antibody (ATA) response. Quantitative titers were low and ranged from 1:20 to 1:320. There was no evidence of altered pharmacokinetics or toxicity profile in these patients. The incidence of antibody formation is highly dependent on assay sensitivity and specificity, assay methodology, sample handling, timing of sample collection, concomitant medications, and underlying disease; therefore the comparison of incidence of antibodies to obiltoxaximab with the incidence to other products may be misleading.

Description
  Text
  Obiltoxaximab is a monoclonal antibody that neutralizes toxins produced by Bacillus anthracis for the treatment of inhalational anthrax in combination with appropriate antibacterial drugs or when alternative therapies are not available or not appropriate in adult and pediatric patients. Obiltoxaximab was approved under the FDA’s Animal Efficacy Rule, which allows efficacy findings from adequate and well-controlled animal studies to support FDA approval when it is not feasible or ethical to conduct trials in humans. In this case, because inhalational anthrax is a rare and lethal disease, it is not possible to conduct adequate efficacy trials in humans. Safety and pharmacokinetics were studied in healthy adult volunteers. Results were extrapolated from adult trials to determine pediatric dosing. Obiltoxaximab was FDA-approved in March 2016.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Obiltoxaximab is a monoclonal antibody that binds the protective antigen (PA) of Bacillus anthracis toxin. This inhibits the binding of PA to its cellular receptors, preventing the intracellular entry of the anthrax lethal factor and edema factor, the enzymatic toxin components responsible for the pathogenic effects of anthrax toxin.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Intravenous Route
    Description
    The pharmacokinetics of obiltoxaximab are linear across single IV doses ranging from 4 to 16 mg/kg. In healthy volunteers, the mean Cmax was 400 +/- 91.2 mcg/mL, and the mean AUC was 5170 +/- 1360 mcg x day/mL following a single IV dose of 16 mg/kg. Because efficacy cannot be tested in humans, a comparison to exposures in animals is necessary. Humans achieve similar or greater systemic exposure after a single 16 mg/kg IV dose compared to animal models. Although obiltoxaximab is intended for single dose administration, the pharmacokinetics after a second dose were studied (n = 65). When a second 16 mg/kg IV dose was administered 2 weeks after the first dose, the AUC was approximately twice that of a single dose on day 1 or day 120. No significant differences were noted in the mean Cmax, AUC, CL, or half-life of obiltoxaximab when the 2 doses were administered 4 months or more apart.

      Dilution
Keep vials in their cartons before preparation of the infusion solution to protect obiltoxaximab from light.
Do not shake the vials.
Using aseptic technique, withdraw the appropriate volume from the obiltoxaximab vial to deliver the prescribed dose.
Using 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection, dilute the obiltoxaximab dose to the recommended final total volume based on weight as detailed below. Depending on the final volume, choose either a syringe or infusion bag to deliver the dose.
1 kg or less: final total volume for infusion = 7 mL.
1.1 to 2 kg: final total volume for infusion = 15 mL.
2.1 to 3 kg:  final total volume for infusion = 20 mL.
3.1 to 4.9 kg: final total volume for infusion = 25 mL.
5 to 10 kg: final total volume for infusion = 50 mL.
11 to 30 kg: final total volume for infusion = 100 mL.
31 kg or more: final total volume for infusion = 250 mL.
Gently mix the solution. Do not shake.
Discard any unused portion remaining in the obiltoxaximab vial(s).
Storage: Once diluted, do not store the obiltoxaximab solution in a syringe; administer immediately. The diluted solution prepared in an IV infusion bag is stable for 8 hours at room temperature (20 to 25 degree C or 68 to 77 degrees F) or refrigerated (2 to 8 degrees C or 36 to 46 degrees F).
 
IV Infusion
Premedicate with diphenhydramine prior to the obiltoxaximab infusion.
Use a 0.22 micron inline filter.
Infuse IV over 90 minutes. Rate is based on weight as follows:
1 kg or less: 4.7 mL/hour.
1.1 to 2 kg: 10 mL/hour.
2.1 to 3 kg: 13.3 mL/hour.
3.1 to 4.9 kg: 17 mL/hour.
5 to 10 kg: 33.3 mL/hour.
11 to 30 kg: 67 mL/hour.
31 kg or more: 167 mL/hour.
Flush the line with 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection a the end of the IV infusion.
