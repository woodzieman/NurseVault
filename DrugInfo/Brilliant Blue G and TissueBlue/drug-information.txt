Description
  Text
  Brilliant Blue G is an intravitreally administered dye used during ophthalmic procedures to visualize the internal limiting membrane (ILM). When administered into the vitreous cavity, the dye selectively stains the ILM, but not the epiretinal membrane nor the retina. This selectivity in staining allows for easier visualization and removal of the ILM. Adverse reactions reported in procedures in which brilliant blue G has been used are often associated with the procedure itself, and include retinal events (break, tear, detachment, hemorrhage) and cataracts.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Brilliant blue G is a dye. When administered intravitreally during ophthalmic procedures, brilliant blue G selectively stains the internal limiting membrane (ILM), but not the epiretinal membrane nor the retina. This selectivity in staining allows for easier visualization and removal of the ILM. The exact mechanism of this selectivity has not been determined.

Pharmacokinetics

      Name
      Other Injectable Administration
      Description
      Intravitreal Administration
The dye is supplied in a single-patient-use, pre-filled syringe to which a blunt cannula can be attached for administration during ophthalmic surgery.
The intravitreal injection procedure must be carried out under aseptic conditions, which include the use of surgical hand disinfection, sterile gloves, a sterile drape, and sterile eyelid speculum (or equivalent).
Prior to administration, ensure the plunger moves smoothly. Do not use the syringe if the plunger does not move smoothly to prime the cannula.
Carefully inject the dye into the balanced salt solution-filled vitreous cavity. Ensure that the cannula does not contact the retina and that the dye does not get under the retina.
Once stained, immediately remove all excess dye from the vitreous cavity.
