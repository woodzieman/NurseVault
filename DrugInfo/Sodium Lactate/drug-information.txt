Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  General Information
  The acid-base status of a patient should be assessed periodically during sodium lactate administration to minimize the possibility of overdosage or metabolic alkalosis.
 
Because of the high sodium content of sodium lactate, the drug should be used with extreme caution in patients receiving corticotropin or corticosteroids, in patients with renal disease or insufficiency, or in patients with congestive heart failure or other sodium-retaining conditions. The drug also should be used cautiously in patients with conditions of impaired utilization of lactic acid such as beriberi, hepatic disease or insufficiency, shock, congestive heart failure, or hypoxemia. Sodium lactate is substantially excreted by the kidney, and the risk of toxic reactions may be greater in patients with renal impairment or renal failure. Because elderly patients are more likely to have decreased renal function, it is prudent to initiate the dosage cautiously and to monitor renal function, acid-base balance, and electrolytes.
 
Sodium lactate therapy should be used with extreme caution in patients with potassium depletion because alkalosis can increase the risk of developing hypokalemia and arrhythmias in these patients.
 
Sodium lactate should not be used for the treatment of lactic acidosis.
 
Sodium lactate should not be used in patients with hypernatremia or other conditions in which the administration of sodium would be hazardous.
 
No adequate or well-controlled reproductive studies have been done in laboratory animals or humans. It is unknown whether sodium lactate is harmful to the fetus. Sodium lactate should be used during pregnancy only when the potential benefits justify the unknown risks to the fetus.
 
It is not known whether sodium lactate is excreted in human milk. Because many drugs are excreted in human milk, caution should be exercised when sodium lactate is administered to a woman who is breast-feeding an infant.

    General Information
    Use sodium lactate with particular caution, if at all, in individuals with metabolic alkalosis or who are at risk for alkalosis. Clinical evaluation and periodic laboratory determinations may be necessary to monitor changes in acid base balance during prolonged sodium lactate therapy or whenever the condition or the rate of administration warrants such evaluation.
    Headline
    hepatic failure, hyperlactatemia
    General Information
    Use sodium lactate with particular caution, if at all, in individuals with conditions associated with hyperlactatemia or impaired lactate utilization, such as severe hepatic insufficiency or hepatic failure. Hyperlactatemia can develop in individuals with severe hepatic insufficiency, since lactate metabolism may be impaired. In addition, sodium lactate may not produce its alkalinizing action in individuals with severe hepatic insufficiency, since lactate metabolism may be impaired.

Description
  Text
  Description: Sodium lactate is a parenteral alkalizing agent used in the management of mild to moderate metabolic acidosis. Sodium lactate and other bicarbonate precursors are used to overcome the chemical and physical incompatibilities associated with administration of parenteral bicarbonate. Sodium lactate was approved by the FDA in March 1971.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Mechanism of Action: Lactate is a naturally produced substance that is metabolized by the liver to either bicarbonate or glycogen. The formation of glycogen from lactate is stimulated by the body's need for extra energy following utilization of available glucose, which usually occurs with exercise. Conversely, lactate is converted to bicarbonate when the body's acid-base balance is altered as a result of bicarbonate deficiency (metabolic acidosis). To form bicarbonate, lactate is slowly hydrolyzed to carbon dioxide and water, which are then converted to bicarbonate by the addition of a hydrogen ion. Thus, the actions of sodium lactate mimic those of sodium bicarbonate (see separate monograph on Sodium Bicarbonate for complete discussion).

Pharmacokinetics
  Text
  Sodium lactate is administered intravenously. Excess bicarbonate ions that result from the administration of sodium lactate are excreted in the urine, causing alkalization of the urine. This alkalization decreases renal absorption and increases the clearance of certain drugs, intoxicants, weak acids, and blood pigments. There is no excretion of unchanged lactate.
