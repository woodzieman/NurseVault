    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    The available data on the use of topical difamilast during pregnancy are insufficient to evaluate for a drug-associated risk of major birth defects, miscarriage, or other adverse outcomes. In animal studies, difamilast given subcutaneously to pregnant rabbits and rats during the period of organogenesis at doses 3 and 30 times the maximum recommended human dose (MRHD) produced no fetal structural abnormalities. However, increased skeletal variations were observed at 14 times the MRHD in rabbits. At 263 times the MRHD, subcutaneous difamilast resulted in increased post-implantation loss, decreased fetal weight, delayed ossification, and increased visceral abnormalities in rats.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Use difamilast with caution during breast-feeding. There are no data on the presence of difamilast or its metabolites in human milk, its effects on the breastfed child, or its effects on milk production. Difamilast and/or its metabolites are excreted into the milk of lactating rats. To minimize potential exposure to the breastfed child, advise individuals not to apply difamilast directly to the nipple or areola and avoid direct contact of treated areas with the child's skin. Consider the benefits of breast-feeding, the patient's clinical need for treatment, and any potential adverse effects on the breastfed child from the medication or from the patient's underlying condition.

Description
  Text
  Difamilast is a topical phosphodiesterase-4 (PDE-4) inhibitor approved for the treatment of mild to moderate atopic dermatitis. During clinical trials, 21% to 47% of individuals treated with difamilast achieved Investigator's Global Assessment (IGA) success (clear or almost clear with a 2-grade or greater improvement from baseline) compared to 3% to 18% of individuals treated with a vehicle ointment across all trials. The most common reported side effect is nasopharyngitis. Difamilast is not for ophthalmic, oral, or intravaginal use. Difamilast was FDA-approved in February 2026.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Difamilast is a phosphodiesterase-4 (PDE-4) inhibitor. The inhibition of PDE-4 activity leads to accumulation of intracellular cyclic adenosine monophosphate (AMP) and decreased production of various inflammatory cytokines and chemokines. The specific mechanism by which difamilast exerts its therapeutic action is not well defined.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Topical Route
    Description
    A pharmacokinetic study was conducted in 31 adult individuals with mild to moderate atopic dermatitis. The mean body surface area involvement was 6% +/- 3% (range 3% to 19%). Individuals applied an average of approximately 0.9 grams (range 0.1 to 3.8 grams per application) of difamilast 1% ointment twice daily for 4 weeks. On Day 29, the mean Cmax and AUC from 0 to 12 hours post dose were 0.76 +/- 1.16 nanograms/mL and 6.10 +/- 8.85 nanograms x hour/mL, respectively.

    Description
    In a 2-week pharmacokinetic study of pediatric patients (n = 31, aged 2 years and older) with moderate to severe atopic dermatitis and a mean body surface area involvement of 44% +/- 13% (range 25% to 80%), the mean Cmax and AUC from 0 to 8 hours post dose were 16.9 +/- 21.9 nanograms/mL and 86.2 +/-79.6 nanograms x hour/mL, respectively, on Day 15. The average dose of difamilast 1% ointment was approximately 4.3 grams (1.7 to 11.7 grams per application) twice daily. Systemic concentrations of difamilast were at steady-state by Day 15, with no evidence of accumulation. The plasma difamilast trough concentration corrected by dose in pediatric patients was 1.3 to 1.9 times higher than in adults on Day 1 and Day 15, respectively.

  Text
  Difamilast is administered topically. In vitro, difamilast serum protein binding is 99% and is not concentration-dependent. Difamilast is metabolized to form 3 major metabolites in the plasma via CYP3A4-mediated O-deethylation (metabolite 1), CYP1A2-mediated hydroxylation (metabolite 2), and enzymatic hydrolysis (metabolite 3). In healthy Japanese individuals, after both single dosing and twice daily administration of difamilast for 2 weeks, metabolite 1 and difamilast were undetectable in the urine. The urinary excretion ratios of the other metabolites were less than 0.1% of the administered dose.
 
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes and drug transporters: CYP3A4, CYP1A2, BCRP
In vitro, difamilast is a substrate of CYP3A4, CYP1A2, and BCRP, but not a substrate of P-gp, OATP1B1, or OATP1B3. Difamilast is not expected to inhibit CYP1A2, CYP2A6, CYP2B6, CYP2C8, CYP2C9, CYP2C19, CYP2D6, CYP2E1, CYP3A4, P-gp, BCRP, OAT1, OAT3, OCT1, OCT2, OATP1B1, OATP1B3, MATE1, or MATE2-K at clinically relevant concentrations. Induction of CYP1A2, CYP2B6, CYP2C9, and CYP3A4 by difamilast is expected to be low at clinically relevant concentrations.
