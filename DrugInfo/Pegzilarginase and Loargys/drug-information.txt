    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components.
    Headline
    serious hypersensitivity reactions or anaphylaxis
    General Information
    Serious hypersensitivity reactions or anaphylaxis may occur with pegzilarginase administration. Hypersensitivity reactions have included facial swelling, rash, flushing, and dyspnea. Reactions typically occur during the first few doses, but may also occur later in treatment. Individuals who develop anti-drug antibodies (ADA) generally have a higher incidence of hypersensitivity reactions compared to those who do not develop ADA. Initiate pegzilarginase by a health care provider knowledgeable in the management of hypersensitivity reactions and in a health care setting with appropriate medical monitoring and support measures, including access to cardiopulmonary resuscitation equipment. Consider pre-medication with an antihistamine. Corticosteroids can be considered in individuals who have previously developed a hypersensitivity reaction. Advise the individual about the signs and symptoms of life-threatening hypersensitivity reactions, including anaphylaxis. If a severe hypersensitivity reaction occurs, discontinue pegzilarginase and initiate appropriate medical treatment, including epinephrine. Consider the risks and benefits of readministering pegzilarginase following a severe reaction. If a mild or moderate reaction occurs, consider treatment with antihistamines and/or corticosteroids. For intravenous administration, consider temporarily holding the infusion or slowing the infusion rate.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    There are no data on the use of pegzilarginase during pregnancy to evaluate for a drug-associated risk of major birth defects, miscarriage, or other adverse outcomes. In animal studies, IV pegzilarginase administration during organogenesis produced maternal toxicity at exposures approximately 3 and 8 times higher than the human exposure (based on AUC at the maximum recommended human dose [MRHD]). Maternal effects included reduced body weight, reduced body weight gain, decreased food consumption, and reduced mean gravid uterine weights. These findings were associated with decreased fetal body weights and an increased incidence of fetal developmental malformations and variations.
    Headline

Consider the benefits of breast-feeding, the patient's clinical need for treatment, and any potential adverse effects on the breastfed child from the medication or from the patient's underlying medical condition.

    antibody formation
    General Information
    Anti-drug antibody formation may occur during pegzilarginase therapy. During clinical trials, anti-pegzilarginase-nbln antibodies alone were reported in 6% to 9% of individuals, anti-PEG antibodies alone in 0% to 13% of individuals, and both anti-pegzilarginase-nbln and anti-PEG antibodies occurred in 3% to 31% of individuals. Anti-PEG antibodies peaked at Week 3 in one clinical trial and at Week 7 in another clinical trial. Anti-pegzilarginase-nbln antibodies showed similar titer profiles as anti-PEG antibodies in a trial, while no clear peak was observed in the other trial. Antibody titers decreased with continued treatment, and the median duration of anti-drug antibody (ADA) formation positivity was approximately 2 weeks. Hypersensitivity reactions occurred at a higher incidence in individuals with ADAs compared to those who were ADA negative. No clinically significant effect of ADAs on the incidence of injection site reactions was identified. The effect of ADAs on the effectiveness of pegzilarginase in the treatment of arginase 1 deficiency is unknown.

Description
  Text
  Pegzilarginase is an arginine specific enzyme indicated for the treatment of hyperargininemia in individuals with arginase 1 deficiency (ARG1-D) in combination with dietary protein restriction. ARG1-D is an inherited autosomal recessive genetic disorder caused by mutations in the ARG1 gene. These mutations result in the production of an abnormal arginase enzyme leading to excessive arginine in the body. Affected individuals may experience a lag in growth, develop muscle stiffness and lack of control of the legs, and experience slowed cognitive development. In a clinical trial, once-weekly intravenous pegzilarginase significantly reduced plasma arginine concentrations compared with placebo, with 90% of treated patients achieving arginine concentrations below the targeted concentration (200 micromolar). During the open-label extension, reductions in plasma arginine were maintained, and individuals who switched from placebo to pegzilarginase achieved similar reductions. During administration, monitor individuals closely for hypersensitivity reactions, including anaphylaxis. Consider pre-medication with antihistamines and initiate treatment in a healthcare setting with appropriate medical monitoring and support measures, including access to cardiopulmonary resuscitation equipment. Pegzilarginase was FDA-approved in February 2026.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Arginine 1 deficiency (ARG1-D) is an inherited metabolic disorder caused by reduced activity of the arginase 1 enzyme, resulting in persistently elevated plasma arginine levels. Pegzilarginase provides an external source of arginase 1 enzyme activity in individuals with ARG1-D and lowers plasma arginine by converting it to urea and ornithine.
 
Pharmacodynamics
Treatment with IV pegzilarginase led to a dose-dependent reduction in plasma arginine levels, with a median time to nadir of 2 to 5 hours. After once weekly IV treatment, plasma arginine levels reached steady state on or before Week 8. Treatment with IV or subcutaneous pegzilarginase led to increases in plasma ornithine levels and decreases in plasma levels of the guanidino compounds argininic acid, guanidinoacetic acid, alpha-keto-delta-guanidinovaleric acid, and N-alpha-acetyl-L-arginine. Predose plasma arginine levels were similar after switching from IV to subcutaneous pegzilarginase at the same dose. At a given dose level, the nadir following subcutaneous dosing was higher and led to shorter durations of pegzilarginase-induced hypoargininemia.

      Name

      Intravenous infusion
Calculate the dose and determine the number of vials needed based on actual body weight. Round the calculated volume to the nearest 0.1 mL.
Remove the vial(s) from the refrigerator and allow the vials to reach room temperature.
Use a syringe to withdraw the calculated volume from the vial(s). Discard any unused portion from the vial.
Dilute the withdrawn volume of pegzilarginase solution in 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection to a maximum concentration of 0.5 mg/mL.
Gently invert the infusion bag to mix the solution. Avoid vigorous shaking or agitation.
Administer the infusion over at least 30 minutes.
After the infusion, flush the line with 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection.
Do not mix other medications with pegzilarginase or administer other medications through the same intravenous line.
Storage: May store at room temperature at 20 to 25 degrees C (68 to 77 degrees F) for 2 hours or in a refrigerator for 4 hours. Discard if not administered within these time frames, including total infusion time.
Missed dose: Administer pegzilarginase as soon as possible. Do not administer 2 doses on the same day or within 4 days of another dose to make up for a missed dose. Ensure there is a minimum of 4 days between doses.
