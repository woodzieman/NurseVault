Routes: injection (IV/IM/SC); Common adult doses: 120 mg/dose subcutaneously.

Common Side Effects (excluding routine GI upset): injection site reaction, pruritus, weight gain

Indication Specific Dosing
  General Information
  General dosing information:
Perform hepatitis B virus (HBV) infection screening in all patients before satralizumab initiation. Satralizumab is contraindicated in patients with active HBV infection, confirmed by positive results for surface antigen [HBsAg] and anti-HBV tests. Consult liver disease experts before starting and during treatment with satralizumab for patients who are negative for HBsAg and positive for HB core antibody [HBcAb+] or are carriers of HBV [HBsAg+].
Evaluate for active tuberculosis and test for latent infection before initiating treatment with satralizumab. Consult infectious disease experts before initiating treatment with satralizumab for patients with active tuberculosis or positive tuberculosis screening without a history of appropriate treatment.
Assess liver transaminases and serum bilirubin before starting satralizumab treatment. Use caution when considering initiation of satralizumab treatment in patients whose aspartate aminotransferase (AST) or alanine aminotransferase (ALT) concentrations are greater than 1.5 times the upper limit of normal (ULN).
Delay satralizumab administration in patients with an active infection until the infection has resolved.

    Headline
    hepatitis B infection, tuberculosis
    General Information
    Satralizumab is contraindicated in individuals with active hepatitis B infection, confirmed by positive results for surface antigen [HBsAg] and anti-hepatitis B virus (HBV) tests, or active or untreated latent tuberculosis. Perform HBV screening in all individuals before initiation of satralizumab treatment. Consult liver disease experts before starting and during treatment with satralizumab for individuals who are negative for HBsAg and positive for HB core antibody [HBcAb+] or are carriers of HBV [HBsAg+]. Evaluate for active tuberculosis and test for latent infection before initiating treatment with satralizumab. Consider anti-tuberculosis therapy before treatment initiation for individuals with a history of latent active tuberculosis in whom an adequate course of treatment cannot be confirmed, and for individuals with a negative test for latent tuberculosis but with risk factors for tuberculosis infection. Consult infectious disease experts before initiating treatment with satralizumab for individuals with active tuberculosis or positive tuberculosis screening without a history of appropriate treatment. Monitor individuals for the development of symptoms and signs of tuberculosis with satralizumab, even if initial tuberculosis testing is negative. Delay satralizumab administration in individuals with an active infection until the infection is resolved.

    Headline
    hepatic disease
    General Information
    Use caution when considering initiation of satralizumab treatment in individuals with hepatic disease and aspartate aminotransferase (AST) or alanine aminotransferase (ALT) concentrations more than 1.5 times the upper limit of normal (ULN). Assess liver transaminases and serum bilirubin before starting satralizumab treatment. Monitor ALT and AST concentrations every 4 weeks for the first 3 months of treatment, followed by every 3 months for 1 year, and thereafter as clinically indicated.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Available data on the use of satralizumab during pregnancy are insufficient to evaluate a drug-associated risk of major birth defects, miscarriage, or other adverse outcomes. Monoclonal antibodies are increasingly transported across the placenta as pregnancy progresses, with the largest amount transferred during the third trimester. In animal studies, no adverse effects on fetal development were observed in the offspring of monkeys given satralizumab at doses that resulted in plasma exposures 3 and 100 times greater than that in humans at the recommended monthly maintenance dose. However, immune function was impaired in offspring at both doses. There is a pregnancy exposure registry that monitors outcomes in people exposed to satralizumab during pregnancy. Care teams are encouraged to register pregnant patients, or pregnant individuals may enroll themselves in the registry by calling 1-833-277-9338.

    neonates and infants exposed to this medication in utero
    General Information
    It is unclear how the immune system of neonates and infants exposed to this medication in utero will react to subsequent administration of a live vaccine. Based on its mechanism of action and animal data, satralizumab may impair immune function. Monoclonal antibodies are increasingly transported across the placenta as pregnancy progresses, with the largest amount transferred during the third trimester. Consider the risks and benefits prior to administering live or live-attenuated vaccines in neonates and infants exposed to satralizumab in utero.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Use satralizumab with caution during breast-feeding. There are no data on the presence of satralizumab in human milk, its effects on the breast-fed child, or its effect on milk production. Satralizumab is present in animal milk. Satralizumab is a recombinant humanized monoclonal antibody based on a IgG2 framework; IgG is excreted in human milk. The potential for absorption in the breast-fed child is unknown. Consider the benefits of breast-feeding, the patient's clinical need for treatment, and any potential adverse effects on the breast-fed child from the medication or from the patient's underlying medical condition.

    Headline
    infection, pharyngitis
    General Information
    An increased risk of infection, including serious and potentially fatal infections, has been observed in patients treated with satralizumab. In a randomized, placebo-controlled trial of patients with neuromyelitis optica spectrum disorder (NMOSD) who were anti-AQP4 seropositive and not receiving concurrent immunosuppressive therapy, the rate of infections was 51 patients/100 patient-years (95% CI: 32, 78) in satralizumab-treated patients (n = 41) compared with 108 patients/100 patient-years (95% CI: 52, 199) in patients given placebo (n = 23). The rate of serious infections was 5 patients/100 patient-years (95% CI: 1, 14) in satralizumab-treated patients compared with 4 patients/100 patient-years (95% CI: 0, 21) in patients given placebo. In a second randomized trial of patients with NMOSD who were anti-AQP4 seropositive and receiving concurrent immunosuppressive therapy, the rate of infections was 168 patients/100 patient-years (95% CI: 100, 265) in satralizumab-treated patients (n = 26) compared with 143 patients/100 patient-years (95% CI: 83, 229) in patients given placebo (n = 26). The rate of serious infections was 4 patients/100 patient-years (95% CI: 1, 15) in satralizumab-treated patients compared with 10 patients/100 patient-years (95% CI: 2, 28) in patients given placebo. Nasopharyngitis was reported in 12% to 31% of satralizumab-treated patients compared to 4% to 15% of patients given placebo, and pharyngitis was reported in 12% of satralizumab-treated patients compared to 8% of patients given placebo. Upper respiratory tract infection was reported in 19% of satralizumab-treated patients compared to 12% of patients given placebo. Cellulitis was reported in 10% of satralizumab-treated patients compared to 0% of patients given placebo.

    neutropenia, thrombocytopenia
    General Information
    In a randomized, placebo-controlled trial of patients with neuromyelitis optica spectrum disorder (NMOSD ) who were anti-AQP4 seropositive and not receiving concurrent immunosuppressive therapy, neutropenia was reported in 10% of satralizumab-treated patients (n = 41) compared to 4% of patients given placebo (n = 23). Among satralizumab-treated patients, 10% had neutrophils below 1 x 109/L compared to 9% given placebo. There was 1 patient treated with satralizumab with neutrophil counts less than 0.5 x 109/L. In another study, a single patient discontinued satralizumab because of neutropenia. Monitor neutrophils 4 to 8 weeks after initiation of therapy, and thereafter at regular clinically determined intervals. Interrupt satralizumab treatment if the neutrophil count is below 1 x 109/L and confirmed by repeat testing; do not restart treatment until the neutrophil count is more than 1 x 109/L. Median percent reductions in the C3 and C4 components of complement were 20% to 23% and 50% to 53%, respectively, in satralizumab-treated patients (n = 67) compared to 0% and 1%, respectively, of patients given placebo (n = 49). A shift in platelet count decreases from normal at baseline to below the lower limit of normal (LLN) (thrombocytopenia) occurred in 26% of satralizumab-treated patients (n = 41) compared to 5% of patients given placebo (n = 23). In a second randomized, placebo-controlled trial of patients with NMOSD who were anti-AQP4 seropositive and receiving concurrent immunosuppressive therapy, decreases in platelet counts from normal at baseline to below the LLN occurred in 35% of satralizumab-treated patients (n = 26) compared to 17% of patients given placebo (n = 26). None of the patients had a decrease in platelet count to less than 50 x 109/L. Median percent reductions in fibrinogen were 38% and 33% in satralizumab-treated patients compared to 5% and 0% of patients given placebo in each study, respectively. Increased blood creatine phosphokinase was reported in 10% of satralizumab-treated patients (n = 41) compared to 4% of patients given placebo (n = 23).

    antibody formation
    General Information
    As with all therapeutic proteins, there is the potential for immunogenicity and antibody formation. During 2 randomized, placebo-controlled trials, anti-drug-antibodies (ADAs) were observed in 38% and 73% of satralizumab-treated patients. The ability of these ADAs to neutralize satralizumab binding is unknown. Patients with higher body weight and lower exposure were more likely to develop ADAs (irrespective of treatment with other immunosuppressive therapy). Exposure was lower in ADA positive patients. Although anti-satralizumab antibody development was not found to affect the efficacy of satralizumab in these patients, the available data are too limited to make definitive conclusions. Immunogenicity does not have a clinically-relevant impact on safety. Based on the available information, neither dose interruption nor modification is warranted in those patients who develop ADAs.

    elevated hepatic enzymes
    General Information
    In a randomized, placebo-controlled trial of patients with neuromyelitis optica spectrum disorder (NMOSD) who were anti-AQP4 seropositive, elevated hepatic enzymes from normal at baseline to above the upper limit of normal (ULN) in ALT or AST occurred in 43% and 25% of satralizumab-treated patients (n = 41), respectively, compared to 13% and 9% of patients given placebo (n = 23). In a second study of similar design, increases from normal at baseline to above the ULN in ALT or AST occurred in 8% and 8% of satralizumab-treated patients (n = 26), respectively, compared to 12% and 19% of patients given placebo (n = 26). In both trials combined, elevations of ALT or AST more than 3 times the ULN occurred in 3% of satralizumab-treated patients compared to 0% of patients given placebo. The elevations were not associated with increases in total bilirubin. A single patient receiving satralizumab had an elevation of ALT more than 5 times the ULN, which was observed 4 weeks after initiation of therapy, normalizing 78 days after treatment discontinuation. Assess liver transaminases and serum bilirubin before starting satralizumab treatment. Use caution when considering initiation of satralizumab treatment in patients whose aspartate aminotransferase (AST) or alanine aminotransferase (ALT) concentrations are greater than 1.5 times the upper limit of normal (ULN). Monitor ALT and AST concentrations every 4 weeks for the first 3 months of treatment, followed by every 3 months for 1 year, and thereafter as clinically indicated. If an ALT or AST elevation of greater than 5 times the ULN occurs and is associated with a bilirubin elevation, discontinue satralizumab; reinitiation is not recommended. If an ALT or AST elevation of greater than 5 times the ULN occurs and is not associated with a bilirubin elevation, discontinue satralizumab; when the ALT or AST concentration has returned to normal, treatment with satralizumab can be restarted after a benefit-risk assessment of the patient. Closely monitor liver function parameters if treatment is restarted. If any subsequent increase in ALT/AST and/or bilirubin above the ULN is observed, discontinue satralizumab; another reinitiation is not recommended.

  Text
  Satralizumab is a subcutaneous interleukin 6 (IL-6) receptor antagonist humanized monoclonal antibody, based on a human IgG2 framework, indicated for the treatment of neuromyelitis optica spectrum disorder (NMOSD) in adults who are anti-aquaporin-4 (AQP4) antibody positive. In a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial of patients with NMOSD who were anti-AQP4 antibody-positive and not receiving concurrent immunosuppressive therapy, the time to first adjudicated relapse was significantly longer in patients treated with satralizumab (n = 41) compared to placebo (n = 23) [risk reduction 74%; HR 0.26 (0.11, 0.63); p = 0.0014]. In a second randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial of patients with NMOSD who were anti-AQP4 antibody-positive and receiving concurrent immunosuppressive therapy, the time to first adjudicated relapse was significantly longer in patients treated with satralizumab (n = 26) compared to placebo (n = 26) [risk reduction 78%; HR 0.22 (0.06, 0.82); p = 0.0143]. There was no evidence of benefit in anti-AQP4 antibody-negative patients. Satralizumab is intended for patient self-administration under the guidance of a health care professional. After proper training, a patient may self-inject, or the patient's caregiver may administer, if the health care professional deems appropriate. Satralizumab is associated with increased risks of infection, mild and moderate hepatic enzyme elevations, and decreases in neutrophil counts; laboratory monitoring is necessary.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Satralizumab is an interleukin 6 (IL-6) receptor antagonist humanized monoclonal antibody, based on a human IgG2 framework. The exact mechanism of action by which satralizumab exerts its therapeutic effects in neuromyelitis optica spectrum disorder (NMOSD) is not known but is presumed to involve inhibition of IL-6 mediated signaling through binding to soluble and membrane-bound IL-6 receptors.

  Text
  Satralizumab is administered subcutaneously. Satralizumab undergoes biphasic distribution. The central and peripheral Vd of satralizumab were 3.46 L and 2.07 L, respectively. The concentration-time course of satralizumab in subjects with neuromyelitis optica spectrum disorder (NMOSD) was accurately described by a 2-compartment population pharmacokinetic model with parallel linear and target-mediated (Michaelis-Menten) elimination and first-order subcutaneous absorption. Inter-compartmental clearance is 0.336 L/day. The metabolism of satrilzumab has not been directly studied, as antibodies are primarily cleared by catabolism. The total clearance of satralizumab is concentration-dependent. Linear clearance (accounting for approximately half of the total clearance at steady-state using the recommended dose in NMOSD subjects) is estimated to be 0.0601 L/day. The terminal half-life of satralizumab is approximately 30 days (range, 22 to 37 days).

Administration
  General Information
  For storage information, see the specific product information within the How Supplied section.
  Route Specific Administrations
    Administration Details
      Name
      Subcutaneous Administration
      Description
      Satralizumab is intended for patient self-administration under the guidance of a health care team. After proper training, an individual may self-inject, or the individual's caregiver may administer, if the health care team deems appropriate.
Before use, remove satralizumab from the refrigerator and allow it to sit at room temperature outside the carton for 30 minutes. Do not warm using any other method. Instruct individuals to use the syringe or the autoinjector within 5 minutes of removing the cap. Do not remove the cap until ready to inject.
Do not shake.
Choose the injection site in the abdomen or thigh. Do not inject into the 2 inch (5 cm) area around the navel. Rotate injection sites with each administration. Each injection must be at least 1 inch (2.5 cm) away from the most recent injection site. Do not inject into moles, scars, or areas where the skin is tender, bruised, red, hard, or not intact.
Instruct individuals/caregivers on proper injection techniques. They should review the "Instructions for Use" for the prefilled syringe or autoinjector, as appropriate.
Storage: Satralizumab can be stored outside of refrigeration for a maximum of 8 days at a temperature that does not exceed 30 degrees C (86 degrees F). If removed from and returned to the refrigerator, the total combined time out of refrigeration should not exceed 8 days.
 
Single-Dose, Prefilled Autoinjector
Clean the injection site with an alcohol pad and allow it to dry. Do not touch the injection site again before you inject.
Hold the autoinjector with the cap pointing upwards. Pull the cap straight off. The needle will remain hidden. Use the autoinjector within 5 minutes of removing the cap. Advise individuals not to touch the green needle shield of the autoinjector or put the cap back on after it has been removed, as this may unintentionally activate the autoinjector.
Hold the autoinjector so that the viewing window is visible. Place the autoinjector flat against the skin with the green needle shield facing down. Do not change the angle of the injection. You may see drops of liquid, which is normal.
Push firmly and hold down the autoinjector against the skin. The first click indicates the injection has started. The green plunger moves down in the viewing window. It might take up to 15 seconds for the green plunger to move all the way down and fill the viewing window. Do not lift the autoinjector if the green plunger is moving. This may cause a loss of medication.
Keep holding down the autoinjector firmly and wait for the second click. This means the injection is completed. Watch the green plunger as it moves down or check if the green plunger has already filled the entire viewing window.
If the green plunger does not fill the viewing window, you may not have received the full dose. Dispose of the autoinjector in an FDA-cleared sharps disposal container and contact the individual's care team.
Remove the autoinjector by lifting it straight up from the skin. The green needle shield will lock into place, covering the needle when the injection is complete.
Do not rub the injection site. A small bandage may be placed over the injection site if needed.
Put the used autoinjector and the cap in an FDA-cleared sharps disposal container right away after use. Do not dispose of the used autoinjector in household trash.
 
Single-Dose, Prefilled Syringe
Clean the injection site with an alcohol pad and allow it to dry. Do not touch the injection site again before you inject.
Hold the barrel of the syringe between thumb and index finger. Pull the needle cap straight off. Use the syringe within 5 minutes of removing the cap.
Pinch the area of cleaned skin, and insert the needle at a 45 to 90-degree angle.
After the needle is inserted, let go of the pinched skin. Slowly inject all the medicine in the syringe (1 mL) by gently pushing the plunger all the way down until it touches the activation guards.
Gently release the plunger and allow the needle to come out of the skin at the same angle it was inserted.
Do not rub the injection site. A small bandage may be placed over the injection site if needed.
Put the used syringe and needle cap in an FDA-cleared sharps disposal container right away after use. Do not dispose of the used syringe in household trash.

    Injectable Administration
    Description
    Visually inspect parenteral products for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration whenever solution and container permit. Satralizumab is a clear, colorless to slightly yellow solution. Do not use if the solution is cloudy, discolored, has particles, or any part of the prefilled syringe is damaged.
Delay satralizumab administration in patients with an active infection until the infection is resolved.
Missed dose: If a dose is missed for any reason other than increases in liver enzymes, administer the missed dose as soon as possible; do not wait until the next planned dose.
Delayed or missed second loading dose: 120 mg subcutaneously as soon as possible, and administer the third loading dose 2 weeks later.
Delayed or missed third loading dose: 120 mg subcutaneously as soon as possible, and administer the first maintenance dose 4 weeks later.
Last dose administered less than 8 weeks: 120 mg subcutaneously as soon as possible, and reset the dose schedule to every 4 weeks.
Last dose administered 8 weeks to 12 weeks: 120 mg subcutaneously every 2 weeks for 2 doses, then 120 mg subcutaneously every 4 weeks.
Last dose administered 12 weeks or more: 120 mg subcutaneously every 2 weeks for 3 doses, then 120 mg subcutaneously every 4 weeks.

Dosage Adjustment Guidelines
  Hepatic Impairment
  Specific guidelines for dosage adjustments in hepatic impairment are not available; it appears that no dosage adjustments are needed.
Monitor ALT and AST concentrations every 4 weeks for the first 3 months of treatment, followed by every 3 months for 1 year, and thereafter as clinically indicated.
If an ALT or AST elevation of greater than 5 times the ULN occurs and is associated with a bilirubin elevation, discontinue satralizumab; reinitiation is not recommended.
If an ALT or AST elevation of greater than 5 times the ULN occurs and is not associated with a bilirubin elevation, discontinue satralizumab; when the ALT or AST concentration has returned to normal, treatment with satralizumab can be restarted after a benefit-risk assessment of the patient. Closely monitor liver function parameters if treatment is restarted. If any subsequent increase in ALT/AST and/or bilirubin above the ULN is observed, discontinue satralizumab; another reinitiation is not recommended.
 
Recommended dosage for restart of satralizumab treatment after liver transaminase elevation:
Last dose administered less than 12 weeks: Restart 120 mg subcutaneously every 4 weeks.
Last dose administered 12 weeks or more: Restart 120 mg subcutaneously every 2 weeks for 3 doses, then 120 mg subcutaneously every 4 weeks.
  Renal Impairment
  Specific guidelines for dosage adjustments in renal impairment are not available; it appears that no dosage adjustments are needed.
