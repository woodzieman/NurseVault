    General Information
    Use belantamab mafodotin with caution in patients with a history of ocular disease. Ocular toxicity, defined as changes in the corneal epithelium and changes in best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA) based on ophthalmic exam findings, has been reported with belantamab mafodotin therapy. All patients should use preservative-free artifical tears during therapy starting with the first dose and avoid the use of contact lenses for the duration of therapy. Perform an ophthalmic exam (including slit lamp exam and BCVA assessment) at baseline (within 4 weeks prior to the first dose), before each dose (within 10 days prior to the next planned dose), promptly for new or worsening symptoms, and as clinically indicated. Therapy interruption, a dose reduction, or discontinuation may be necessary based on ophthalmic exam findings and toxicities. Advise patients to use caution with driving or operating machinery or performing activities requiring coordination and concentration due to the risk of reduced visual acuity.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Belantamab mafodotin may cause fetal harm if administered during pregnancy, based on its mechanism of action. The cytotoxic component of belantamab mafodotin, mcMMAF, disrupts microtubule function, is genotoxic, and can be toxic to rapidly dividing cells. Advise pregnant people of the potential fetal risk. Human immunoglobulin G (IgG) is known to cross into the placenta; therefore, belantamab mafodotin may be transmitted from the pregnant person to the developing fetus.
    Headline

    General Information
    Counsel people who may become pregnant and people who can cause pregnancy in others about the reproductive risk associated with belantamab mafodotin. Verify pregnancy status prior to treatment initiation. Advise people who may become pregnant to use effective contraception while taking belantamab mafodotin and for 4 months after the last dose. For those that can cause pregnancy in others, contraception should be used during belantamab mafodotin treatment and for 6 months after the last dose. Based on animal data, belantamab mafodotin may cause male and female infertility; these effects were not reversible in male rats.

    Headline
    blurred vision, cataracts, corneal deposits, diplopia, foreign body sensation, keratopathy, lacrimation, ocular irritation, ocular pain, ocular pruritus, ocular toxicity, photophobia, visual impairment, xerophthalmia
    General Information
    Belantamab mafodotin can cause ocular toxicity, defined as changes in the corneal epithelium resulting in changes in vision, including severe visual impairment, and symptoms such as blurred vision and dry eye; ocular toxicity may be associated with difficulty reading or driving. Ocular toxicity occurred in 92% (grade 2, 9%; grade 3, 56%; grade 4, 21%) of patients treated with belantamab mafodotin plus bortezomib and dexamethasone in the DREAMM-7 trial, including a reduction in visual acuity (89%; grade 3 or 4, 57%) and corneal exam findings (86%; grade 3 or 4, 72%) based on ophthalmic exam findings, blurred vision (66%; grade 3 or 4, 22%), xerophthalmia (51%; grade 3 or 4, 7%), photophobia (47%; grade 3 or 4, 2%), foreign body sensation in eyes (44%; grade 3 or 4, 3%), ocular irritation (43%; grade 3 or 4, 5%), and ocular pain (33%; grade 3 or 4, 0.8%). The median time to onset of the first grade 2 to 4 ophthalmic exam findings was 43 days (range, 15 to 611 days) with a median duration of all grade 2 to 4 ophthalmic exam findings of 85 days (range, 5 to 813 days); patients experienced a median of 3 episodes (range, 1 to 11) of ocular toxicity. Of patients with grade 2 to 4 ophthalmic exam findings, 42% had improvement of the last event to grade 1 or better, 22% had resolution of the last event based on return to baseline or normal ophthalmic exam findings. The most commonly reported corneal exam findings in patients treated with belantamab mafodotin included superficial punctate keratopathy, microcyst-like deposits (corneal deposits), epithelial changes, and haze. Cases of corneal ulcer, including cases with infection, have been reported and should be promptly managed by an eye care professional. Reductions in visual acuity to 20/50 or worse in at least one eye occurred in 69% of patients, including 29% who experienced a change in visual acuity to 20/100 or worse, and 12% who experienced a change to 20/200 or worse. Of patients with reduced visual acuity to 20/50 or worse in at least one eye, 61% had resolution of the last event to baseline or better; of those patients with reduced visual acuity to 20/100 or worse, 57% had resolution of the last event; of those with reduced visual acuity to 20/200 or worse, 48% had resolution of the last event. Other ocular adverse events reported in patients treated with belantamab mafodotin in the DREAMM-7 trial included cataracts (24%; grade 3 or 4, 8%), visual impairment not otherwise specified (11%; grade 3 or 4, 5%), and increased lacrimation, diplopia, ocular pruritus, and corneal ulcer (including cases with infection) each occurring in less than 10% of patients. All patients should use preservative-free lubricant eye drops at least 4 times daily during therapy starting with the first dose; avoid use of contact lenses during treatment. Ophthalmic exams (i.e., visual acuity and slit lamp) should be performed by an eye care professional (i.e., ophthalmologist or optometrist) at baseline (within 4 weeks prior to the first dose), prior to each dose (within 10 days prior to the next planned dose; as close to the next dose as possible), and promptly for worsening symptoms. Therapy interruption, a dose reduction, or discontinuation may be necessary in patients who develop corneal toxicity or changes in visual acuity.

Description
  Text
  Belantamab mafodotin-blmf is an antibody-drug conjugate that targets B-cell maturation antigen (BCMA), a protein expressed on normal B lymphocytes and multiple myeloma cells. It is indicated in combination with bortezomib and dexamethasone for the treatment of adults with relapsed or refractory multiple myeloma who have received at least 2 prior lines of therapy, including a proteasome inhibitor and an immunomodulatory agent. Belantamab mafodotin carries a black box warning for ocular toxicity; changes to the corneal epithelium resulting in changes in vision, including severe visual impairment, and symptoms such as blurred vision, dry eyes, and corneal ulcers with or without infection have occurred. Ophthalmic exams are required at baseline, prior to each dose, and for new or worsening ocular symptoms. The FDA approved belantamab mafodotin in 2020 for the treatment of adult patients with relapsed or refractory multiple myeloma who had received at least 4 prior therapies, including an anti-CD38 monoclonal antibody, a proteasome inhibitor, and an immunomodulatory agent. Belantamab mafodotin was voluntarily removed from the US market by the manufacturer in November 2022 due to failure to meet the primary endpoint of superior progression-free survival in the confirmatory randomized (DREAMM-3) trial. The current indication was approved by the FDA in October 2025 and includes a new line of therapy and a new treatment combination.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Belantamab mafodotin is a B-cell maturation antigen (BCMA)-directed antibody and microtubule inhibitor conjugate. It has 3 components consisting of an afucosylated, humanized immunoglobulin G1 (IgG1) monoclonal antibody covalently linked to a microtubule inhibitor (monomethyl auristatin F (mcMMAF)) via a protease-resistant maleimidocaproyl linker. BCMA is a protein that is expressed on normal B lymphocytes and multiple myeloma cells. Belantamab mafodotin is internalized after binding to BCMA, cys-mcMMAF, the cytotoxic agent, is released via proteolytic cleavage which causes disruption in the microtubule network, cell cycle arrest, and apoptosis. In vitro, cys-mcMMAF-induced apoptosis, tumor lysis via antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity, and antibody-dependent cellular phagocytosis have been demonstrated in multiple myeloma cells.

    Belantamab mafodotin exhibits dose-proportional pharmacokinetics; clearance gradually decreases over time. Following an IV dose of belantamab mafodotin 2.5 mg/kg, the mean Cmax value was 43.7 mcg/mL (CV, 22.1%), the mean Cmin value was 2.03 mcg/mL (CV, 62.5%), and the mean AUC value was 3,950 mcg/mL x hour (CV, 30.6%). Accumulation was minimal to moderate for belantamab mafodotin (Cycle 3 to Cycle 1 ratio was 1.13 for Cmax and 1.58 for AUC) as observed with a dosing regimen of every 3 weeks.

  Text
  Belantamab mafodotin is administered intravenously. It has a geometric mean steady-state volume of distribution of 10.8 L (coefficient of variation (CV), 22.2%), a steady-state plasma clearance of 0.605 L/day (CV, 43%), and a steady-state elimination half-life of 17 days (CV, 31%). In vitro, cys-mcMMAF exhibited low protein binding (70% unbound at a concentration of 5 ng/mL) in human plasma. The fraction of intact cys-mcMMAF excreted in the urine was approximately 18% of the dose in Cycle 1, with no evidence of other mcMMAF-related metabolites. The monoclonal antibody portion of belantamab mafodotin is metabolized into small peptides and individual amino acids by catabolic pathways. Cys-mcMMAF had limited metabolic clearance in human hepatic incubation studies.
Affected drug transporters: OATP1B1, OATP1B3, MRP1, MRP2, MRP3, BSEP
In vitro, cys-mcMMAF is a substrate of organic anion transporting polypeptide (OATP)1B1 and OATP1B3; multidrug resistance-associated protein (MRP)1, MRP2, MRP3; bile salt export pump (BSEP); it is also a possible substrate of P-glycoprotein.

      Reconstitution:
Calculate the required dose (mg) and remove the correct number of vials from the refrigerator; let vial(s) stand for approximately 10 minutes to allow them to reach room temperature (20 to 25 degrees C or 68 to 77 degrees F).
Reconstitute each 70 mg vial with 1.4 mL of Sterile Water for Injection for a final vial concentration of 50 mg/mL.
Gently swirl the vial to aid in dissolution; do not shake.
The vial solution should be clear to opalescent, colorless to yellow to brown liquid, and free of extraneous particulate matter.
Storage following reconstitution: Store in the original container for up to 4 hours in the refrigerator (2 to 8 degrees C or 36 to 46 degrees F) or at room temperature (20 to 25 degrees C or 68 to 77 degrees F) prior to dilution; do not freeze. Discard if not diluted within 4 hours.
Dilution:
Withdraw the calculated dose/volume from the reconstituted vial(s) and dilute in 250 mL of 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection to achieve a final concentration of 0.2 mg/mL to 2 mg/mL.
Infusion bags must be made of polyvinylchloride (PVC) or polyolefin (PO).
Discard any unused reconstituted solution left in the vial.
Mix by gently inverting the infusion bag; do not shake.
The diluted solution should be clear and colorless and free of particulate matter.
Storage following dilution: Store the diluted admixture for up to 24 hours in the refrigerator (2 to 8 degrees C or 36 to 46 degrees F); do not freeze. Once removed from the refrigerator, administer within 6 hours (includes administration time).
Intravenous (IV) infusion:
Allow the diluted admixture to acclimate to room temperature (if applicable) prior to administration. Diluted solution may be kept at room temperature for no more than 6 hours, including infusion time.
Administer belantamab mafodotin as an IV infusion over approximately 30 minutes using a PVC or PO infusion-set.
The diluted admixture does not require a filter for administration; if the solution is filtered, use a polyethersulfone (PES)-based 0.2-micron filter.
Do not mix or administer with other products.

Dosage Adjustment Guidelines
  Hepatic Impairment
  No dosage adjustment is necessary in patients with mild hepatic impairment (total bilirubin level ULN or less and an AST level greater than the ULN OR total bilirubin level greater than ULN to 1.5 times the ULN or less and any AST level).
The recommended dose has not been established in patients with moderate or severe hepatic impairment.
  Renal Impairment
  Specific guidelines for dosage adjustments in renal impairment are not available; it appears that no dosage adjustments are needed.
