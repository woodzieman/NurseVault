Drug Class: Bivalirudin is a direct thrombin inhibitor indicated for use as an anticoagulant in patients undergoing percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI), including patients with heparin-induced thrombocytopenia (HIT) or heparin-induced thrombocytopenia and thrombosis syndrome (HITTS).

Routes: injection (IV/IM/SC); Common adult doses: 1.75 mg/kg/hour continuous IV infusion.

Common Side Effects (excluding routine GI upset): bleeding, gi bleeding, hematoma, hematuria, intracranial bleeding, retroperitoneal bleeding

In an Overdose: No specific antidote for bivalirudin is available; however, it is hemodialyzable.

Key Nursing Considerations: Contraindications/precautions: Bivalirudin is contraindicated in patients with a known hypersensitivity reaction to the drug (hirudin hypersensitivity) or hypersensitivity any of the product components. Bivalirudin is contraindicated in patients with active major bleeding.

Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  General Information
  Bivalirudin is contraindicated in patients with a known hypersensitivity reaction to the drug (hirudin hypersensitivity) or hypersensitivity any of the product components.
 
Bivalirudin is contraindicated in patients with active major bleeding. Coagulopathy should be ruled out before initiation of therapy. Blood coagulation tests should be performed before and regularly during therapy. Bivalirudin should not be initiated in patients with a baseline aPTT ratio of 2.5 or more. For patients at increased risk of bleeding, a careful assessment must be made weighing the risk of bivalirudin administration versus its anticipated benefit. Conditions which increase the risk of bleeding include: a recent stroke; severe uncontrolled hypertension; infective endocarditis; advanced renal disease; dissecting aortic aneurysm; peptic ulcer disease; diverticulitis; inflammatory bowel disease; hemophilia; menstruation; hepatic disease (due to a reduced generation of vitamin K-dependent clotting factors or vitamin K deficiency); recent major surgery or trauma, including eye, brain, or spinal cord surgery; lumbar puncture; spinal anesthesia; recent puncture of large vessels or organ biopsy; an anomaly of vessels or organs; or recent major bleeding (including intracranial bleeding, GI bleeding, intraocular bleeding, retroperitoneal bleeding, or pulmonary bleeding). During clinical trials of bivalirudin, 44% of patients were >= 65 years of age and 12% were >= 75 years of age. In general, elderly patients experienced more bleeding than younger patients; however, patients treated with bivalirudin in each age group did experience fewer bleeding events when compared to heparin. Bivalirudin should not be used in patients with uncontrollable bleeding. Patients should be monitored closely for signs and/or symptoms of bleeding.
 
Advanced renal disease can be a risk factor for bleeding events. Since approximately 20% of an administered bivalirudin dose is excreted unchanged in the urine, the manufacturer recommends a reduced dose in patients with severe renal impairment (i.e., CrCl < 30 ml/min) or renal failure (see Dosage). There is no known antidote to bivalirudin, however, bivalirudin is hemodialyzable.
 
Bivalirudin is not intended for intramuscular administration. Although most bleeding associated with the use of bivalirudin in PTCA occurs at the site of arterial puncture, hemorrhage can occur at any site. An unexplained fall in blood pressure or hematocrit, or any unexplained symptom, should lead to serious consideration of a hemorrhagic event and cessation of bivalirudin administration. If possible, intramuscular injections of other drugs should be avoided in patients receiving bivalirudin. Caution is also advised when bivalirudin is used as the anti-thrombin agent during brachytherapy procedures. Operators are advised to maintain meticulous catheter technique, with frequent aspiration and flushing, paying special attention to minimizing conditions of stasis within the catheter or vessels.
 
Bivalirudin is approved for use during PCI in patients with or at risk of developing heparin-induced thrombocytopenia (HIT) and heparin-induced thrombocytopenia and thrombosis syndrome (HITTS). In a single group, open-label study of patients with HIT undergoing PCI, 28 patients received high-dose bivalirudin (1 mg/kg IV bolus followed by 2.5 mg/kg/hr IV infusion for up to 4 hours) and 25 patients received low-dose bivalirudin (0.75 mg/kg IV bolus followed by 1.75 mg/kg/hr for up to 4 hours). Activated clotting time (ACT) values at the time of device activation were 379 seconds and 317 seconds for the high dose and low dose groups, respectively. Ninety-eight percent of patients received aspirin, 86% received clopidogrel, and 19% received glycoprotein IIb/IIIa inhibitors. Following the procedure, 94% of patients had TIMI grade 3 flow and stenosis < 50%; major bleeding occurred in 1 patient in the high-dose group. Two patients developed thrombocytopenia after receiving bivalirudin in combination with glycoprotein IIb/IIIa inhibitors.
 
Bivalirudin is classified as FDA pregnancy risk category B. However, bivalirudin is intended for use with aspirin when administered for FDA-approved use in patients undergoing percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI). Aspirin is classified as pregnancy category C during the first and second trimesters and as pregnancy category D during the third trimester. Regular use of full-dose aspirin late in pregnancy may result in constriction or premature closure of the fetal ductus arteriosus. Animal data suggest bivalirudin causes no teratogenic effects when administered in the early stages of pregnancy or during the period of organogenesis. It is not known if the bivalirudin crosses the placental barrier in humans. There are no adequate and well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Bivalirudin with aspirin should only be used during pregnancy if clearly needed.
 
It is not known if bivalirudin is excreted in human milk. Because many drugs are excreted in human milk and because of the potential for serious adverse reactions in nursing infants, a decision should be made to discontinue breast-feeding or discontinue bivalirudin, taking into account the importance of the drug to the mother.
 
Safe and effective use of bivalirudin has not been established in children.

    General Information
    An increased risk of thromboembolism has been associated with the use of bivalirudin in gamma brachytherapy. If using bivalirudin during brachytherapy procedures, maintain meticulous catheter technique, with frequent aspiration and flushing, paying special attention to minimizing conditions of stasis within the catheter or vessels.
Bivalirudin affects the International Normalized Ratio (INR). INR measurements made in individuals being treated with bivalirudin may not be useful for determining the appropriate warfarin dose.

    bleeding disorder, geriatric, major bleeding
    General Information
    Bivalirudin is contraindicated in individuals with active major bleeding. Monitor individuals with a bleeding disorder more frequently for signs and symptoms of bleeding. Geriatric adults have experienced more bleeding events compared to younger adults.
    Headline
    renal failure, renal impairment
    General Information
    Reduce the bivalirudin dose and monitor anticoagulant status more frequently in individuals with renal impairment or renal failure (CrCl less than 30 mL/minute). Bivalirudin clearance is decreased in individuals with moderate or severe renal impairment.
    Headline
    labor, obstetric delivery, pregnancy
    General Information
    There are no adequate data on the use of bivalirudin during pregnancy to assess the risk of adverse developmental outcomes. In animal reproduction studies, no evidence of fetal harm was observed in rats or rabbits at doses up to 1.6 and 3.2 times, respectively, the maximum recommended human dose. At maternally toxic doses in rats, reductions in litter size and the number of live fetuses were observed, along with fetal skeletal variations. No studies have evaluated the use of bivalirudin during labor or obstetric delivery due to the potential risk of drug-induced hemorrhage.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Bivalirudin is compatible with breast-feeding. There are no data on the presence of bivalirudin in human milk, its effects of bivalirudin on the breastfed child, or its effects on milk production. However, due to its short half-life and large molecular weight, it is unlikely to enter human milk. Waiting to breast-feed for 3 hours after the dose would greatly reduce any potential risk to the breastfed child.  While bivalirudin is not specifically addressed, guidelines suggest r-hirudin, a similar medication, may be continued while breast-feeding.

    General Information
    Acute stent thrombosis (less than 4 hours) has been reported at a greater incidence in bivalirudin-treated patients (1.2%) compared to heparin-treated patients (0.2%) with ST segment elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI) undergoing primary percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI). If using bivalirudin during brachytherapy, maintain meticulous catheter technique, with frequent aspiration and flushing, paying special attention to minimizing conditions of stasis within the catheter or vessels.

Description
  Text
  Bivalirudin is a direct thrombin inhibitor indicated for use as an anticoagulant in patients undergoing percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI), including patients with heparin-induced thrombocytopenia (HIT) or heparin-induced thrombocytopenia and thrombosis syndrome (HITTS). It is a parenteral bivalent analog of hirudin. Hirudin is a natural anticoagulant secreted in the saliva of the medicinal leech, Hirudo medicinalis. In comparison to heparin which binds only circulating thrombin, direct thrombin inhibitors block circulating thrombin and clot-bound thrombin. Bivalirudin with or without prior treatment with heparin is recommended for patients with ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI) undergoing PCI. Guidelines recommend bivalirudin (or argatroban) over other nonheparin anticoagulants for patients with acute or subacute HIT who require PCI. The use of bivalirudin is also recommended over other nonheparin anticoagulants and heparin plus antiplatelet agents for patients with acute or subacute HIT who require urgent cardiac surgery. Additionally, bivalirudin is recommended as initial anticoagulation before PCI or angiography in patients with non-ST-elevation acute coronary syndrome (NSTE-ACS). In patients undergoing PCI who are at high risk of bleeding, it is reasonable to use bivalirudin monotherapy in preference to the combination of heparin and a GP IIb/IIa receptor antagonist. Acute stent thrombosis has been reported at a higher incidence in patients treated with bivalirudin compared to heparin for STEMI undergoing PCI. Patients must be carefully monitored for at least 24 hours after primary PCI for signs and symptoms consistent with myocardial ischemia in a facility capable of managing ischemic complications.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Bivalirudin is a specific and reversible direct thrombin inhibitor. Bivalirudin directly inhibits thrombin by binding to both the catalytic site and to the anion-binding exosite of circulating and clot-bound thrombin. Thrombin, a serine proteinase, cleaves fibrinogen into fibrin monomers and activates factor XIII to factor XIIIa, allowing fibrin to develop a covalently cross-linked framework which stabilizes the thrombus; thrombin also actives factor V and factor VIII, which promotes further thrombin generation and activates platelets, stimulating aggregation and granule release. The binding of bivalirudin to thrombin is reversible. There is no known antidote to bivalirudin.

  Special Populations
    Name
    Renal Impairment
    Description
    Bivalirudin clearance is reduced by 21% in patients with moderate and severe renal impairment and 70% in patients receiving hemodialysis. The elimination half-life of bivalirudin is 34 minutes in patients with moderate renal impairment, 57 minutes in patients with severe renal impairment, and 3.5 hours in dialysis-dependent patients. Approximately 25% of bivalirudin is cleared by hemodialysis.
    Name
    Pediatrics
    Description
    The pharmacokinetic/pharmacodynamic response of bivalirudin was similar in pediatric patients compared to adults in a study of 106 neonates, infants, children, and adolescents undergoing cardiac catheterization. However, mean Cmax, AUC, and Cavg increased with increasing age. This correlated with a higher observed clearance rate in neonates (11.2 mL/kg/minute) compared to older children (5.98 mL/kg/minute). Overall, mean Cmax and AUC were approximately 1.5-fold higher in older children compared to neonates. Mean half-life across pediatric age groups ranged from 15 to 18 minutes, compared to 25 minutes in adults. Observed half-life was 0.25 hours (15 minutes) in neonates, 0.27 hours (16.2 minutes) in infants 1 month to 1 year, 0.3 hours (18 minutes) in young children 2 to 5 years, and 0.28 hours (16.8 minutes) in older children 6 to 15 years.
  Text
  Bivalirudin is administered intravenously. Bivalirudin exhibits linear pharmacokinetics. It does not bind to plasma proteins (other than thrombin) or to red blood cells. Metabolism and excretion occur via proteolytic cleavage and glomerular filtration, respectively. Tubular secretion and tubular reabsorption are also implicated in excretion, although the extent is unknown. Bivalirudin elimination half-life is 25 minutes in patients with normal renal function. The total body clearance of bivalirudin is 3.4 mL/kg/minute.
