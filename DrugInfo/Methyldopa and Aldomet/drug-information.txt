Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  General Information
  Because of the risk of hemolytic anemia, patients receiving methyldopa should have a hemoglobin, hematocrit, or red blood cell count assessed before and during therapy. Positive Coombs' tests occur in 10—20% of patients receiving methyldopa therapy, and this usually reverses within weeks to months following discontinuance of the drug. If there is evidence of anemia or a positive Coombs' test during therapy, hemolysis should be ruled out. Methyldopa should be used with caution in patients with a history of hemolytic anemia or autoimmune disease. If hemolytic anemia is present, the drug should be discontinued.
 
Hepatic function also should be determined periodically to detect methyldopa-induced hepatic changes. Methyldopa is contraindicated in patients who have a history of hepatic disease resulting from methyldopa therapy and in patients with active hepatic disease such as active cirrhosis or acute hepatitis.
 
Methyldopa is contraindicated for concomitant use in patients receiving MAOI therapy (see Drug Interactions).
 
Methyldopa is relatively contraindicated in patients with pheochromocytoma. Methyldopa may interfere with laboratory tests for catecholamines. Methyldopa may also cause pressor responses in patients with a pheochromocytoma.
 
Methyldopa should be used cautiously in patients with renal impairment or renal failure. Methyldopa and active metabolites with a long half-life may accumulate in patients with renal impairment; dosage adjustment is recommended (see Dosage). Methyldopa is significantly removed (about 60% of dose) is removed during a six hour hemodialysis session; a supplemental dose is recommended following dialysis.
 
Methyldopa should be used cautiously in patients receiving other antihypertensive agents because additive effects could occur (see Drug Interactions).
 
Methyldopa should be used cautiously in patients with severe coronary insufficiency, cardiac disease, or cerebrovascular disease because drug-induced hypotension can be hazardous to patients with these conditions.
Methyldopa can cause sedation, so patients should be warned that impaired physical coordination and/or reduced mental alertness can affect their ability to operate machinery or drive a vehicle. Methyldopa should be used cautiously in patients with preexisting mental status changes. Methyldopa should be used with caution in patients with a history of major depression. Parkinson's disease may be exacerbated with in patients receiving methyldopa. Methyldopa has rarely been associated with causing choreoathetotic movements and therefore it should be used with caution in patients with severe bilateral cerebrovascular disease.
 
Some liquid preparations of methyldopa or methyldopate hydrochloride contain sulfites and should be used with caution in patients with a known sulfite hypersensitivity. Use in these patients could precipitate severe allergic reactions including life-threatening asthmatic attacks or anaphylaxis.
 
There are no well-controlled clinical trials of methyldopa in children. Information from the drug manufacturer in regard to dosing methyldopa in pediatric patients is supported by published data in the medical literature regarding the treatment of hypertension in children (see Dosage).
 
During clinical trials of methyldopa, no overall differences in safety or effectiveness were observed between elderly subjects and younger subjects. However, lower doses of methyldopa may be necessary in this population of patients. Elderly patients are more likely to have an increased sensitivity to methyldopa and advanced arteriosclerotic disease, which may lead to syncope. In addition, many elderly patients have decreased renal function and methyldopa is substantially excreted by the kidney. Therefore, the risk of toxic reactions to this drug may be greater in elderly patients and those patients with decreased renal function. It may be useful to monitor renal function in this population.
 
Methyldopa oral dosage forms are classified in FDA pregnancy risk category B. Methyldopa has long been the antihypertensive medication of choice in pregnancy due to a long record of safety and efficacy in this population. Published reports of the use of methyldopa during all trimesters indicate that the possibility of fetal harm appears remote. In five studies, three of which were controlled, involving 332 pregnant hypertensive women, treatment with methyldopa (Aldomet®) was associated with an improved fetal outcome. The majority of these women were in the third trimester when methyldopa therapy was begun. There are no well-controlled studies of the use of methyldopa in pregnant women in the first trimester of pregnancy. In one study, maternal receipt of methyldopa beginning at 16 to 20 weeks of pregnancy resulted in infants whose average head circumference was slightly reduced at birth compared to infants of untreated mothers; however follow up of 195 (97.5%) of the children born to these methyldopa-treated pregnant women failed to uncover any significant adverse developmental effect on the children at 4 years or 7 years of age versus those who were not exposed during gestation.
 
Methyldopa distributes minimally into breast milk; the quantity is not considered to be clinically significant. The American Academy of Pediatrics considers methyldopa to be generally compatible with breast-feeding.

    General Information
    Methyldopa is contraindicated in individuals with a history of significant adverse event(s) associated with previous use, specifically hepatic disease resulting from previous methyldopa therapy, and in individuals with active hepatic disease, such as active hepatic cirrhosis or acute hepatitis. Use methyldopa with caution in individuals with a history of previous hepatic disease or impairment. Monitor hepatic function (i.e. liver function tests) prior to initiation and periodically during therapy, particularly in the first 6 to 12 weeks of treatment or whenever an unexplained fever occurs. Discontinue therapy if abnormalities in liver function tests, fever, or jaundice occur.
    Headline
    renal failure, renal impairment
    General Information
    Use methyldopa cautiously in individuals with renal impairment or renal failure. Methyldopa is largely excreted by the kidney and individuals with impaired renal function may respond to smaller doses. Methyldopa is cleared by dialysis and hypertension may occur after dialysis.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Methyldopa is a commonly used oral agent for the treatment of chronic hypertension during pregnancy. However, methyldopa is generally not as effective as other agents (e.g., calcium channel blockers, beta-blockers), especially for the control of severe hypertension. There are no well-controlled studies on the use of methyldopa in the first trimester of pregnancy. Published reports indicate that methyldopa use during all trimesters of pregnancy poses a low risk of fetal harm. In 5 studies involving 332 pregnant people with hypertension, methyldopa was associated with improved fetal outcomes. Most people began treatment in the third trimester. One study found a small reduction in average head circumference at birth (34.2 cm vs. 34.6 cm) when treatment started between weeks 16 to 20 of pregnancy. Long-term follow-up of 195 children (97.5%) born to methyldopa-treated patients showed no significant adverse effects on the children. At 4 years of age, children whose mothers took methyldopa showed less developmental delay and higher scores on motor and intellectual tests compared to children born to untreated patients. By 7.5 years, developmental and intelligence scores showed no significant differences between children of treated and untreated patients.
    Headline
    cerebrovascular disease
    General Information
    Individuals with severe bilateral cerebrovascular disease may be at risk for the development of involuntary choreoathetotic movements with methyldopa therapy. If choreoathetotic movements develop, discontinue methyldopa.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Methyldopa is compatible with breast-feeding. Methyldopa is present in human milk in clinically insignificant amounts, with a relative infant dose of 0.1% to 0.4%. Due to limited exposure via human milk, no side effects are expected in the breast-fed child.

  Adverse Reactions
  General Information
  The most frequently reported adverse effect of methyldopa therapy is drowsiness. This usually occurs within the first 48—72 hours of administration and may subside with continued therapy. Larger doses produce increased sedation, decreased mental acuity, lapses of memory, and impaired ability to concentrate. Nightmares, vertigo, headache, weakness, and asthenia also have been reported, generally occurring during early treatment.Orthostatic hypotension can occur with methyldopa administration. Sinus bradycardia, worsening angina, and myocarditis have been reported, as well as congestive heart failure and carotid sinus hypersensitivity. Sodium and fluid retention can occur, producing edema, and can often be alleviated with concomitant thiazide diuretic therapy.Patients receiving methyldopa can develop a positive Coombs' test. This is usually not clinically important, but hemolysis with anemia has occurred on rare occasions, causing death in two patients. If hemolysis is present, methyldopa therapy should be discontinued. Methyldopa has also been associated with thrombocytopenia.Methyldopa-induced fever can occur within 3 weeks of initiating therapy. This may be associated with eosinophilia and/or elevated hepatic enzymes. Hepatocellular injury, cirrhosis, hepatitis, and cholestasis have been reported. If methyldopa is the source of these abnormalities, temperature and liver function will usually return to baseline a few months after discontinuance of the drug.Adverse GI effects can occur with methyldopa therapy and include diarrhea, nausea/vomiting, xerostomia (dry mouth), distension, constipation, 'black' tongue, and pancreatitis.Adverse dermatological effects of methyldopa therapy include rash, urticaria, and hyperkeratosis. Photosensitivity has been observed, but appears to be rare.Hypothalamic control of prolactin secretion is exerted by dopamine. Methyldopa produces a net decrease in dopamine levels which can cause hyperprolactinemia. Increased prolactin levels can produce gynecomastia, galactorrhea, and/or amenorrhea. Impotence (erectile dysfunction), libido decrease, ejaculation dysfunction (delayed or no ejaculation), orgasm dysfunction may also occur.Nasal congestion, interstitial nephritis, blurred vision, nocturia, and azotemia also have been reported during methyldopa therapy.

Description
  Text
  Methyldopa is an oral and parenteral antihypertensive agent. An analog of 3,4-dihydroxyphenylalanine (DOPA), methyldopa originally was developed to inhibit DOPA carboxylase, an enzyme critical for the biosynthesis of catecholamines. Methyldopa was later discovered to have antihypertensive activity, but its use as an antihypertensive agent has declined due to the development of other antihypertensive agents with a more favorable adverse side effect profile. Methyldopa is the drug used most often in the treatment of chronic hypertension in pregnant women. Methyldopa received FDA approval in December 1962.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Mechanism of Action: After crossing the blood-brain barrier, methyldopa is decarboxylated to produce alpha-methylnorepinephrine. This metabolite stimulates central inhibitory alpha-adrenergic receptors, thereby reducing peripheral resistance and lowering blood pressure. Although cardiac output and heart rate are not believed to be affected, conflicting data from studies of long-term therapy and the use of methyldopa in patients with congestive heart failure suggest that cardiac output may be decreased and peripheral resistance unaffected. Methyldopa also may act as a false transmitter, thereby exerting a direct effect on peripheral sympathetic nerves. Blood pressure decreases are greatest when the patient is standing but are also significant when the patient is supine. Postural hypotension has been reported in patients receiving methyldopa.Methyldopa can cause sodium and fluid retention, and tolerance can develop during long-term therapy. As an antihypertensive, reduces LVH and does not worsen glucose tolerance, but does exert detrimental effects on serum lipids. Serum prolactin levels can increase during methyldopa administration.

    Roughly 50% of an oral dose of methyldopa is absorbed across the GI tract, with maximal antihypertensive effects occurring 4—6 hours after administration. Plasma levels of methyldopa have no correlation with hypotensive effect.

    Plasma levels of methyldopa have no correlation with hypotensive effect; onset of hypotensive effect occurs no sooner after parenteral administration of methyldopa due to delayed hydrolysis of the intravenous formulation.

    Renal Impairment
    Description
    Renal impairment slows excretion and increases the half-life of methyldopa to approximately 4—6 hours.
  Text
  Methyldopa is administered orally and intravenously.  Methyldopa appears to cross the blood-brain barrier as well as the placenta. The drug distributes in breast milk, but the quantity is probably not clinically significant. Antihypertensive effects last 12—24 hours. Methyldopa is eliminated biphasically, with 95% excreted during the initial phase (plasma half-life of 2 hours) and the rest excreted much more slowly. Unabsorbed drug is excreted in the feces.

Administration
  General Information
  NOTE: When administering methyldopa concomitantly with other antihypertensive agents, the initial oral dosage should not exceed 500 mg/day PO, administered in divided doses. Tolerance to methyldopa may develop during the second or third month of therapy, and may require a dosage increase, or the addition of a thiazide diuretic in order to control blood pressure effectively.

Dosage Adjustment Guidelines
  Hepatic Impairment
  Methyldopa is contraindicated in patients with active hepatic disease, such as acute hepatitis and decompensated cirrhosis.
  Renal Impairment
  Methyldopa is largely excreted by the kidney and individuals with impaired renal function may respond to smaller doses.
Dialysis
Methyldopa is cleared by dialysis and hypertension may occur after dialysis.
