Transitioning to concizumab from other hemostatic products
Discontinue treatment with recombinant factor VIIa at least 12 hours prior to initiating concizumab.

When transitioning from other products to concizumab, consider the half-life of the previous product.

    immune tolerance induction associated with hemophilia treatment
    General Information
    The safety and efficacy of concomitant use of concizumab in individuals receiving ongoing immune tolerance induction associated with hemophilia treatment, a desensitization strategy for the eradication of inhibitors, have not been established, and no data are available. Carefully assess the potential benefits and risks if continuation or initiation of concizumab during immune tolerance induction is considered.
    Headline
    major surgery, planned procedure
    General Information
    There is limited experience with concizumab use in the perioperative setting. It is generally recommended to pause concizumab at least 4 days prior to a planned procedure (i.e., major surgery) and resume 10 to 14 days after the major surgery at the same maintenance dose without a new loading dose, considering the overall clinical picture of the individual. No dose adjustment of concizumab is required for minor surgery. Consult a health care provider experienced in surgery of individuals with bleeding disorders if needed.
    Headline
    atherosclerosis, crush injury, disseminated intravascular coagulation, malignancy, sepsis, thrombotic microangiopathy
    General Information
    Venous and arterial thromboembolic events have been reported with concizumab. These events occurred in individuals with multiple risk factors for thromboembolism, including the use of high and/or frequent doses of breakthrough bleed treatments (i.e., factor products or bypassing agents) or conditions in which tissue factor is overexpressed (e.g., atherosclerosis, crush injury, malignancy, disseminated intravascular coagulation, thrombotic microangiopathy, or sepsis). Inform individuals receiving concizumab treatment of the signs and symptoms of thromboembolic events.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Available data on the use of concizumab during human pregnancy are insufficient to evaluate a drug-associated risk of major birth defects, miscarriage, or other adverse outcomes. Based on its mechanism of action, concizumab may cause fetal harm. Monoclonal antibodies can be actively transported across the placenta. Use concizumab during pregnancy only if the potential benefit for the pregnant person outweighs the potential risk to the fetus. Animal reproduction studies have not been conducted.
    Headline
    people who may become pregnant, reproductive risk
    General Information
    Counsel people who may become pregnant about the reproductive risk associated with concizumab. Concizumab may cause fetal harm. Verify pregnancy status prior to treatment initiation. Advise people who may become pregnant to use effective contraception while taking concizumab and for 7 weeks after the last dose. Evaluate the benefits and thromboembolic risks of the type of contraceptive used.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Use concizumab with caution during breast-feeding. There are no data regarding the presence of concizumab in human milk, its effect on the breast-fed child, or its effect on milk production. Human immunoglobulin G (IgG) is excreted in breast milk during the first few days after birth and decreases to low concentrations soon after. Therefore, a risk to the breast-fed child cannot be excluded during this short time frame. Afterward, concizumab could be used during breast-feeding if clinically needed.

  Adverse Reactions
    Headline
    thromboembolism
    General Information
    Concizumab may cause thromboembolic events. Venous and arterial thromboembolism were reported in 1.9% (6/320) of concizumab-treated subjects in clinical trials. These cases occurred in subjects with multiple risk factors for thromboembolism, including the use of high doses or prolonged treatment with blood factor or bypassing agent (2 of 6 subjects). Monitor for thromboembolic events and inform individuals receiving concizumab of signs and symptoms of thromboembolic events. Discontinue concizumab if a thromboembolic event is suspected; further investigate and implement management strategies. Serious adverse reactions were reported in 6.1% of concizumab-treated subjects in clinical trials; these serious adverse reactions included renal infarct. Concizumab was permanently discontinued in a single subject due to a renal infarct.

    Headline
    antibody formation
    General Information
    During the treatment period in 5 concizumab clinical trials with durations of 11 weeks to 76 weeks or more, anti-concizumab antibody formation was reported in 22.2% (71/320) of concizumab-treated subjects. Of the 71 subjects who tested positive for anti-drug antibodies (ADAs), 18 subjects (25.4%) developed neutralizing antibodies against concizumab. In 2 subjects (2.8%) who developed neutralizing antibodies against concizumab, free tissue factor pathway inhibitor (TFPI) concentrations were restored to baseline, indicating the effectiveness of concizumab was likely compromised. In the remaining 69 subjects (97.2%), there was no identified clinically significant effect of the antibodies on the pharmacokinetics, pharmacodynamics, safety, or effectiveness of concizumab. The observed incidence of ADAs is highly dependent on the sensitivity and specificity of the assay. Differences in assay methods preclude meaningful comparisons of the incidence of ADAs in other studies or of other concizumab products.

    General Information
    Increased concentrations of fibrin D-dimer and prothrombin fragment 1.2 were reported in 29 (9.1%) and 26 (8.1%), respectively, of concizumab-treated subjects in clinical trials. Description
  Text
  Concizumab is a tissue factor pathway inhibitor antagonist indicated for routine prophylaxis to prevent or reduce the frequency of bleeding episodes in people with hemophilia A (congenital factor VIII deficiency) with or without factor VIII inhibitors or hemophilia B (congenital factor IX deficiency) with or without factor IX inhibitors. There is no structural relationship or sequence similarity between concizumab and factor VIII or factor IX; therefore, treatment with concizumab does not induce or enhance the development of direct inhibitors to factor VIII or factor IX. Venous and arterial thromboembolic events have been reported with concizumab use in individuals with multiple risk factors for thromboembolism. Fibrin D-dimer and prothrombin fragment 1.2 concentrations increase with concizumab administration, limiting the reliability of these coagulation biomarkers for clinical decision-making in the setting of suspected thrombosis. Concizumab was FDA-approved in December 2024.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Concizumab is a humanized immunoglobulin G4 (IgG4) monoclonal antibody antagonist of endogenous tissue factor pathway inhibitor (TFPI). By inhibiting TFPI, concizumab enhances factor Xa production during the initiation phase of coagulation, which leads to improved thrombin generation and clot formation with the goal of achieving hemostasis in individuals with hemophilia A or B, regardless of inhibitor status. The effect of concizumab is not influenced by the presence of inhibitory antibodies to factor VIII or factor IX. There is no structural relationship or sequence similarity between concizumab and factor VIII or factor IX; treatment with concizumab does not induce or enhance the development of direct inhibitors to factor VIII or factor IX.
 
Pharmacodynamics
Increasing concizumab dose concentrations resulted in decreased concentrations of free TFPI (plasma TFPI not bound to concizumab) and increased duration of free TFPI suppression. In individuals with hemophilia with inhibitors, free TFPI plasma concentrations pre-dose decreased from 88.3 nanograms/mL at baseline to 10.7 nanograms/mL at week 24 in subjects receiving concizumab prophylaxis compared to 76 nanograms/mL at week 24 in subjects treated on-demand. In individuals with hemophilia without inhibitors, the TFPI plasma concentrations pre-dose decreased from 84.2 nanograms/mL at baseline to 11.1 nanograms/mL at week 24 in subjects receiving concizumab prophylaxis compared to 80.6 nanograms/mL at week 24 for subjects treated on-demand. Mean thrombin peak within the range of normal plasma reflected that concizumab re-established thrombin generation capacity. Additionally, additive and sometimes synergistic increases in thrombin peak as quantified in the thrombin generation assay have been observed in plasma from individuals with hemophilia receiving concizumab prophylaxis with concomitant recombinant factor VIII or recombinant factor IX, or bypassing agents including, recombinant factor VIIa and activated prothrombin complex concentrate (aPCC). Depending on concentrations of concizumab and hemostatic agents, the impact on thrombin peak ranged from being additive with all hemostatic agents to an extra 40% effect with recombinant factor VIIa, an extra 33% effect with aPCC, an extra 22% effect with recombinant factor VIII, and less than 13% effect with recombinant factor IX.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Subcutaneous Route
    Description
    Concizumab Tmax ranges from 8 to 99 hours (4.1 days) after a single subcutaneous concizumab dose of 0.05 to 3 mg/kg in healthy and hemophilia subjects. After a single concizumab loading dose of 1 mg/kg, the steady-state concentrations were reached around Day 4 and remained within a stable exposure range with daily maintenance doses. Steady-state concizumab concentrations during 24-hour dosing interval at week 24 in individuals with hemophilia A or B with inhibitors are 1,167.1 nanograms/mL, 665.4 nanograms/mL, and 2.2 for Cmax,SS, Ctrough,SS, and Cmax/Ctrough ratio, respectively. In comparison, steady-state concizumab concentrations during 24-hour dosing interval at week 24 in individuals with hemophilia A or B without inhibitors are 893.4 nanograms/mL, 647.3 nanograms/mL, and 1.5 for Cmax,SS, Ctrough,SS, and Cmax/Ctrough ratio, respectively. Concizumab AUC and Cmax increase with increasing dose in a greater than dose-proportional manner after subcutaneous administration.

  Text
  Concizumab is administered subcutaneously. The Vd of concizumab for a typical 70 kg individual is approximately 3 L. Concizumab is expected to be metabolized into small peptides by catabolic pathways. It is cleared by linear and non-linear mechanisms and exhibits non-linear pharmacokinetics due to target-mediated drug disposition, which occurs when it forms concizumab/tissue factor pathway inhibitor complex. Once the target becomes saturated, the linear pathway (i.e., catabolism) dominates. Based on population pharmacokinetic analysis, 90% of concizumab is expected to be eliminated by the end of approximately 4 days after the last dose (time for 50% of drug to be eliminated is approximately 1 day).
 
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes and drug transporters: none

    Administration Details
      Name
      Subcutaneous Administration
      Description
      After proper training in subcutaneous injection technique, an individual may self-inject or the individual's caregiver may administer concizumab if a health care provider determines that it is appropriate.
Concizumab may be injected right from the refrigerator or allowed to reach room temperature prior to injection. The pen can be warmed in the palms of the hands. Do not use any other heating sources.
To attach a new needle to the pen, tear off the paper tab and push the needle straight onto the pen. Turn the needle clockwise until it is tight. Pull off the outer and inner needle cap.
Needles with a gauge of 32 and length of 4 mm are recommended for use with the concizumab pen.
Prime the concizumab pen before each dose. Turn the dose selector 1 marking and press the dose button. A stream of concizumab leaving the needle tip should appear.
If no stream appears, repeat the priming process up to 6 times until a stream appears. If still no stream appears, prepare a new needle and test again. If still no stream appears after attaching a new needle, do not use the pen. Use a new pen or call the manufacturer at 1-844-668-6732 for assistance.
Turn the dose selector to select the prescribed dose. The dose can be adjusted by turning the dose selector in either direction (forwards or backwards).
If a larger dose is needed than can be dialed, multiple injections must be administered to obtain the full dose.
Use a new needle and prime the concizumab pen before each injection.
Choose an injection site on the abdomen (at least 2 inches away from the belly button) or thigh.
Do not administer in areas where the skin is tender, bruised, red, or hard, or areas where there are moles, scars, or stretch marks.
Rotate the injection site with each injection every day.
Clean the injection site with an alcohol swab, and allow the site to dry. Do not touch the injection site after cleaning.
To administer subcutaneously, insert the needle at the injection site at a 90-degree angle and press and hold the dose button down until the dose counter returns to 0. After the dose counter has returned to 0, count slowly to 6 while the needle is still in the skin and then remove the needle from the skin.
In children and lean individuals or if using a needle longer than 4 mm, use injection techniques that minimize risk of intramuscular injection (e.g., injecting into a pinched fold of skin).
The pen clicks during the injection; a click may also be heard or felt when the dose counter returns to 0.
Carefully remove the needle from the pen by turning the needle counterclockwise. Do not touch the needle on either side to avoid a needle stick.
Dispose the needle in an appropriate sharps disposal container. Do not put the needle cap back on.
Place the pen cap back on the pen to protect concizumab from light.
Storage: After first use, store the concizumab pen with the cap on in the original carton to protect from light in the refrigerator between 36 and 46 degrees F (2 and 8 degrees C) or at room temperature no warmer than 86 degrees F (30 degrees C) for up to 28 days. Write the date of first use in the space provided on the carton. Discard the concizumab pen 28 days after first opening, even if medication is left in the pen.
Do not store in direct sunlight and keep away from direct heat.
Do not freeze. Do not use concizumab if it has been frozen or stored above 86 degrees F (30 degrees C).

    Description
    Visually inspect parenteral products for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration whenever solution and container permit. Concizumab is a clear to slightly opalescent and colorless to slightly yellow solution that may contain translucent to white particles. Do not use if the solution is discolored.
Initiate treatment in a non-bleeding state.
Missed dose: Adherence to daily dosing of concizumab is important to maintain protection against bleeding. This is especially important during the initial 4 weeks of treatment to ensure a correct maintenance dose is established.
Initial 4-weeks of treatment: Resume once-daily treatment at the initial dose.
Established maintenance dose:
1 missed dose: Resume once-daily treatment at the previously established maintenance dose.
2 to 6 missed doses: Resume treatment with a double dose (twice the previously established maintenance dose) followed by once-daily treatment at the previously established maintenance dose.
7 or more missed doses: Consider a new loading dose followed by once-daily treatment at the previously established maintenance dose.
