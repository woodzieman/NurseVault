    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    There are no data on the use of linerixibat during pregnancy to establish a drug-associated risk of major birth defects, miscarriage, or adverse developmental outcomes. Minimal fetal exposure is expected due to low systemic absorption following oral administration. Linerixibat may inhibit absorption of fat-soluble vitamins (FSV), which are essential for normal fetal growth and development. Monitor pregnant individuals for FSV deficiency and supplement as needed; increased supplementation of FSVs may be needed during pregnancy. Animal reproduction studies observed no malformations or effects on embryo-fetal survival at exposures 470 and 1,100 times the recommended human dose, respectively, in pregnant rabbits and rats. A pregnancy safety study is available that monitors pregnancy exposures and outcomes in individuals who have taken linerixibat during pregnancy. Pregnant individuals exposed to linerixibat or their health care providers, should report linerixibat exposure by contacting Intercept Pharmaceuticals at 1-844-782-4278.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Use linerixibat with caution during breast-feeding. Linerixibat is minimally absorbed after oral administration; no exposure to the breastfed child is expected. There are no data on the presence of linerixibat in human milk, its effects on the breastfed child, or its effects on milk production. Linerixibat may reduce the absorption of fat-soluble vitamins. Monitor fat-soluble vitamin concentrations during treatment and increase intake if deficiency is observed during breast-feeding. Consider the benefits of breast-feeding, the individuals individual's clinical need for treatment, and any potential adverse effects on the breastfed child from the medication or from the individual's underlying medical condition.

  Adverse Reactions
    Headline
    abdominal pain, diarrhea, dyspepsia, gastroesophageal reflux, nausea
    General Information
    Diarrhea (62%) was the most common adverse reaction in individuals treated with linerixibat compared to placebo (18%). Of those who experienced diarrhea, 56/74 (76%) of linerixibat-treated individuals and 10/21 (48%) of placebo-treated individuals did so within the first 20 days of treatment initiation. Among individuals with diarrhea, most events in the linerixibat group were mild (46/74, 62%) or moderate (25/74, 34%) with 4% (3/74) of cases being severe. In the placebo group, 62% (13/21) were mild, 38% (8/21) moderate, and 0% were severe. Discontinuation of treatment due to diarrhea occurred in 4% of linerixibat-treated individuals compared to less than 1% in the placebo group. If diarrhea occurs, advise individuals to monitor for dehydration and consider interrupting or discontinuing linerixibat if diarrhea persists. Other gastrointestinal adverse reactions reported during clinical trials were abdominal pain (including upper and lower abdominal pain and abdominal discomfort, 26%), nausea (10%), dyspepsia (8%), gastroesophageal reflux disease (7%), and abdominal distension (7%).

    General Information
    Elevated hepatic enzymes were observed more commonly in linerixibat-treated individuals (14%) compared to placebo (6%). Increased alanine aminotransferase (ALT) (9%) and increased aspartate aminotransferase (AST) (8%) were reported during clinical trials. Elevations in total bilirubin (TB) to more than 2 times baseline levels occurred in 9 (8%) of linerixibat -treated individuals compared to 3 (3%) of placebo-treated individuals. Obtain baseline liver tests (ALT, AST, TB, direct bilirubin [DB], and alkaline phosphatase [ALP]) prior to initiating treatment. Monitor levels per standard clinical practice for individuals with primary biliary cholangitis during treatment with linerixibat. If new elevated hepatic enzymes occur, monitor ALT, AST, TB, DB, and ALP more frequently. If liver test elevations persist, discontinue treatment.

    Linerixibat may adversely affect absorption of fat-soluble vitamins (FSVs; vitamins A, D, E, and K) resulting in vitamin A deficiency, vitamin D deficiency, vitamin E deficiency, and vitamin K deficiency. Description
  Text
  Linerixibat is an ileal bile acid transporter (IBAT) inhibitor indicated for the treatment of cholestatic pruritus associated with primary biliary cholangitis (PBC) in adults. Linerixibat inhibits the IBAT, reducing reabsorption of bile acids from the terminal ileum and increasing fecal excretion of bile acids. The efficacy of linerixibat was evaluated in a 24-week, randomized, double-blind placebo-controlled trial (n=238). Individuals treated with linerixibat demonstrated a greater reduction from baseline in Monthly Itch Score compared to placebo (LS mean change: -2.86 vs -2.15), with an LS mean difference of -0.72 (95% CI:-1.15, -0.28, p = 0.001). Improvement in pruritus was observed as early as Week 2 (LS mean difference: -0.71, p less than 0.001). Additionally, linerixibat also improved pruritus-related sleep interference with greater reductions in Monthly Sleep Score compared to placebo (LS mean change: -2.77 vs. -2.24), with an LS mean difference of -0.53 (95% CI: -0.98, -0.07, p = 0.024). Avoid use in individuals with decompensated cirrhosis or those with prior or active hepatic decompensation events (e.g. variceal bleeding, ascites, hepatic encephalopathy). Monitor for elevated hepatic enzymes, diarrhea/dehydration, and fat-soluble vitamin deficiency during treatment. Linerixibat was FDA-approved in March 2026.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Linerixibat is a reversible inhibitor of the ileal bile acid transporter (IBAT). It decreases the reabsorption of bile acids in the terminal ileum, leading to their increased fecal elimination. Cholestatic pruritus is a common symptom in people with PBC, and the pathophysiology of pruritus in people with PBC is not completely understood. Although the complete mechanism by which linerixibat improves pruritus in PBC individuals is unknown, it may involve inhibition of the IBAT as observed by a decrease in mediators of pruritus including serum bile acids.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Oral Route
    Description
    Linerixibat is minimally absorbed following oral administration and the absolute oral bioavailability is 0.05%. After a single 40 mg dose, under fasted conditions in healthy adults, the median Tmax was 4.5 hours and the mean Cmax and AUC was 31.1 pg/mL and 105 hour x pg/mL, respectively. After multiple 40 mg twice-daily doses in individuals with PBC and cholestatic pruritus, the mean Cmax was 922 pg/mL (95% CI: 347 to 1,500). Concomitant administration of a high-fat meal delayed median Tmax from 4.5 to 8 hours and decreased the AUC and Cmax by 21.9% and 33.7%, respectively; however, these changes were not considered clinically significant due to highly variable pharmacokinetic parameters in the food effect study. Despite this, dosing at least 30 minutes before food or beverage other than water is recommended to allow drug entry into the gastrointestinal lumen before bile acid release upon eating.
  Special Populations
    Name
    Hepatic Impairment
    Description
    Of 238 individuals enrolled, 25 (11%) individuals were diagnosed with cirrhosis (F4 and Child-Pugh Class A), out of which 13 (5%) individuals received linerixibat treatment. A general trend of higher exposure of linerixibat was observed in individuals with cirrhosis compared to individuals without cirrhosis, although the number of individuals was very small. In a pharmacokinetic study in individuals with moderate hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh Class B), the mean AUC and Cmax values were approximately 13- and 9.4-fold higher than matched healthy individuals. The clinical significance of this increase is unknown. Pharmacokinetics of linerixibat have not been evaluated in people with severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh Class C).

  Text
  Linerixibat is administered orally. It is 71% to 76.4% protein bound. After oral radiolabeled linerixibat, about 97% of the dose was recovered in feces and about 0.04% in urine; more than 99% of fecal radioactivity was unchanged, unabsorbed linerixibat. After a single 90 mg dose (2.25 times the approved recommended dose) in healthy adults, the mean half-life was 6.76 hours.
 
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes and drug transporters: CYP3A4, OATP1B1, OATP1B3
In vitro, linerixibat inhibited CYP3A4 and may inhibit OATP1B1 and OATP1B3, but a clinically meaningful interaction is not likely due to the low fraction absorbed. It did not induce CYP1A2, CYP2B6, or CYP3A4 and did not inhibit P-gp, BCRP, OAT1, OAT3, OCT2, MATE1, or MATE2K in vitro.
