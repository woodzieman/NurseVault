Drug Class: Brimonidine, a selective alpha-agonist, and brinzolamide, a carbonic anhydrase inhibitor, are combined together to decrease intraocular pressure (IOP) in patients with ocular hypertension or open-angle glaucoma.

Routes: ophthalmic; Common adult doses: 3 drops/day per affected eye.

Common Side Effects (excluding routine GI upset): blurred vision, dysgeusia, ocular irritation, xerophthalmia, anxiety, asthenia, depression, dizziness, drowsiness, fatigue, headache, insomnia, syncope, alopecia, contact dermatitis, urticaria, dyspnea, nasal dryness, pharyngitis, rhinitis, arrhythmia exacerbation, bradycardia, chest pain (unspecified), hypertension, palpitations, sinus tachycardia

In an Overdose: No overdose/antidote information is listed in this file.

Key Nursing Considerations: Contraindications/precautions: Carbonic anhydrase activity has been observed in both the cytoplasm and around the plasma membranes of the corneal endothelium. This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components.

Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  Contraindications And Precautions
    Headline
    Drug Interactions
    General Information
    The coadministration of certain medications may lead to harm and require avoidance or therapy modification; review all drug interactions prior to concomitant use of other medications.
    Headline
    reduced corneal endothelial cell density
    General Information
    Use brimonidine; brinzolamide with caution in patients with reduced corneal endothelial cell density (low endothelial cell counts); the potential for corneal edema is increased in these patients. Carbonic anhydrase activity has been observed in both the cytoplasm and around the plasma membranes of the corneal endothelium.
    Headline
    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components. Brinzolamide is a chemical sulfonamide possessing a structure and pharmacologic activity distinct from the bacteriostatic sulfonamides. Rarely, sulfonamides of any type can produce allergic reactions, some of which may be severe. Use brinzolamide with caution, if at all, in patients with a history of sulfonamide hypersensitivity.
    Headline
    children, infants, neonates
    General Information
    Brimonidine; brinzolamide is contraindicated in neonates, infants, and children under the age of 2 years. Additionally, when the individual component brimonidine was studied in children 2 to 7 years of age, somnolence (50 to 83%) and decreased alertness were seen in children 2 to 6 years of age.
    Headline
    cardiovascular disease
    General Information
    Brimonidine has minimal effects on blood pressure and other cardiopulmonary hemodynamics; however, brimonidine, brinzolamide should be used with caution in patients with severe cardiovascular disease.
    Headline
    General Information
    General Information
    Brimonidine; brinzolamide has not been studied in patients with acute closed-angle glaucoma. Management of patients with acute closed-angle glaucoma requires therapeutic interventions in addition to ocular hypotensive agents.
    Headline
    depression, orthostatic hypotension, Raynaud's phenomenon, thromboangiitis obliterans
    General Information
    Brimonidine; brinzolamide should be used with caution in individuals with Raynaud phenomenon, thromboangiitis obliterans (Buerger's disease), depression, cerebral or coronary insufficiency and orthostatic hypotension. Brimonidine may potentiate syndromes associated with vascular insufficiency.
    Headline
    contact lenses
    General Information
    Brimonidine; brinzolamide contains the preservative benzalkonium chloride, which may be absorbed by soft contact lenses. Contact lenses should be removed during instillation of brimonidine; brinzolamide but may be reinserted after 15 minutes.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    There are no adequate and well-controlled studies of brimonidine; brinzolamide use during human pregnancy. In animal studies, brimonidine crossed the placenta and entered into the fetal circulation to a limited extent. Developmental toxicity studies performed in rats with oral doses of 0.66 mg brimonidine base/kg revealed no evidence of harm to the fetus. Dosing at this level resulted in a plasma drug concentration approximately 100 times higher than that seen in humans at the recommended human ophthalmic dose. Developmental toxicity studies with brinzolamide at oral doses up to 120 times the recommended human ophthalmic dose increased the number of fetal variations.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Use brimonidine; brinzolamide with caution during breast-feeding. There are no data on the presence of brimonidine in human milk after ophthalmic use; however, limited data have not identified adverse reactions in the breastfed child. It is not known whether brinzolamide is excreted in human milk after topical ophthalmic administration. It is excreted in the milk of lactating rats. If use is necessary, reduce systemic absorption by applying nasolacrimal occlusion for at least 1 minute after instillation and wiping away excess solution. Consider the benefits of breast-feeding, the patient's clinical need for treatment, and any potential adverse effects on the breastfed child from the medication or from the patient's underlying condition.

    blurred vision, dysgeusia, ocular irritation, xerophthalmia
    General Information
    The most frequently reported adverse reactions in patients treated with brimonidine; brinzolamide occurring at an incidence of approximately 3 to 5% include blurred vision, ocular irritation, dysgeusia, xerophthalmia, and ocular allergy. Adverse reactions led to discontinuation of therapy in approximately 11% of patients.

    General Information
    There have been rare reports of bacterial keratitis associated with the use of multiple dose containers of topical ophthalmic products. These containers had been inadvertently contaminated by patients who, in most cases, had a concurrent corneal disease or a disruption of the ocular epithelial surface. Contamination of ophthalmic preparations, like brimonidine; brinzolamide, can lead to severe ocular damage and possible blindness.

Description
  Text
  Brimonidine, a selective alpha-agonist, and brinzolamide, a carbonic anhydrase inhibitor, are combined together to decrease intraocular pressure (IOP) in patients with ocular hypertension or open-angle glaucoma. Brimonidine; brinzolamide is available as fixed-dose ophthalmic drop that is administered as 1 drop three times daily, with the intent of reducing medication burden. The fixed dose combination resulted in a statistically significantly greater reduction in IOP (16.3—19.8 mm Hg) after 3 months of treatment than that of either brimonidine 0.2% (17.9—22.5 mm Hg; p < 0.001) or brinzolamide 1% (19.3—20.9 mm Hg; p < 0.002) administered as monotherapy in a clinical trial of 660 patients with either open-angle glaucoma or ocular hypertension. Brimonidine; brinzolamide (Simbrinza) was FDA-approved in April 2013.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Brimonidine and brinzolamide both decrease elevated intraocular pressure (IOP). Reduction of elevated or normal IOP occurs irrespective of the presence of glaucoma.
Brimonidine: Brimonidine is a potent, selective alpha-2-agonist that is 1000-fold more selective for the alpha-2 versus the alpha-1 receptor. When brimonidine is compared to clonidine and apraclonidine, it is 7 to 12 and 23 to 32 times, respectively, more selective for the alpha-2 receptor. Brimonidine decreases IOP, without causing mydriasis, by reducing aqueous humor production and increasing uveoscleral aqueous humor outflow. Brimonidine has been shown to be neuroprotective to the optic nerve in animal studies.
Brinzolamide: Brinzolamide inhibits carbonic anhydrase II (CA-II). Carbonic anhydrase is an enzyme found in many tissues of the body, including the eye. It catalyzes the reversible reaction involving the hydration of carbon dioxide and the dehydration of carbonic acid. Carbonic anhydrase exists as a number of isoenzymes, the most active being carbonic anhydrase II, found primarily in red blood cells (RBCs), but also in other tissues. Brinzolamide inhibits carbonic anhydrase in the ciliary processes of the eye to decrease aqueous humor secretion, presumably by slowing the formation of bicarbonate ions with subsequent reduction in sodium and fluid transport. This results in decreased aqueous humor secretion and subsequently a reduction in IOP. Brinzolamide appears to have no clinically significant biochemical or hematologic effects when administered topically to the eye.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Other Route(s)

    Ophthalmic Route
The systemic plasma exposure (AUC and Cmax) to brimonidine and brinzolamide is similar after dosing with the fixed combination to that observed following dosing with the individual components.
Brimonidine: Following ophthalmic administration of brimonidine 0.2%, the Tmax occurred in 1—4 hours with a systemic half-life of 3 hours. The time to peak ocular hypotensive effect on IOP is about 2 hours.
Brinzolamide: Following ophthalmic administration, brinzolamide is absorbed into the systemic circulation. The time to peak ocular hypotensive effect is 2—3 hours.
  Text
  Brimonidine; brinzolamide ophthalmic suspension is administered topically to the eye.
Brimonidine: Brimonidine is extensively metabolized by the liver and is eliminated in the urine.
Brinzolamide: In the systemic circulation, brinzolamide distributes extensively into erythrocytes and exhibits a long half-life in whole blood (about 111 days) due to its affinity for carbonic anhydrase type II (CA-II). Approximately 60% of brinzolamide in plasma is protein bound. Brinzolamide is metabolized to N-desethyl brinzolamide which also binds to carbonic anhydrase and accumulates in erythrocytes. In the presence of brinzolamide, this metabolite mainly binds to CA-I. Plasma concentrations of both brinzolamide and the metabolite are low and generally below the level of assay detection (< 10 ng/mL). Brinzolamide is eliminated primarily in the urine as unchanged drug. N-Desethyl brinzolamide is also found in the urine along with lower concentrations of the N-desmethoxypropyl and O-desmethyl metabolites.
