Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  Contraindications And Precautions
    Headline
    Drug Interactions
    General Information
    The coadministration of certain medications may lead to harm and require avoidance or therapy modification; review all drug interactions prior to concomitant use of other medications.
    Headline
    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components. Dexbrompheniramine is a first-generation antihistamine of the propylamine class. Allergies to one antihistamine can sometimes lead to cross-reactivity with other chemically-related antihistamines.
    Headline
    chronic obstructive pulmonary disease
    General Information
    Use dexbrompheniramine; pseudoephedrine with caution in people with chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), including emphysema or chronic bronchitis. The anticholinergic activity of first-generation H1-antagonists, such as dexbrompheniramine, may result in thickened bronchial secretions in the respiratory tract, thereby aggravating COPD.
    Headline
    cardiac disease, hypertension
    General Information
    Sympathomimetics such as pseudoephedrine may raise heart rate and/or blood pressure in susceptible individuals. Use dexbrompheniramine; pseudoephedrine with caution in individuals with cardiac disease or hypertension. People with heart disease or high blood pressure should check with their care team before nonprescription (OTC) use.
    Headline
    prostatic hypertrophy
    General Information
    Use dexbrompheniramine; pseudoephedrine with caution in people with difficulty in urination due to prostatic hypertrophy, as sympathomimetic agents such as pseudoephedrine and anticholinergics such as dexbrompheniramine may aggravate urinary retention.
    Headline
    glaucoma
    General Information
    Dexbrompheniramine; pseudoephedrine should be used with caution in people with glaucoma, as an increase in intraocular pressure may occur from the anticholinergic actions of the medication. Older adults are more susceptible to the anticholinergic effects of dexbrompheniramine, including possible precipitation of undiagnosed glaucoma.
    Headline
    renal failure, renal impairment
    General Information
    Dexbrompheniramine; pseudoephedrine should be used with caution in people with severe renal impairment (CrCl less than 30 mL/minute) as a pseudoephedrine dosage reduction has been suggested for these individuals. Due to decreased elimination and potential pseudoephedrine drug accumulation, patients with renal failure may be at increased risk for drug-related toxicity. Many experts recommend against pseudoephedrine use in severe acute or chronic kidney disease (CKD) or renal failure.
    Headline
    children, infants, neonates
    General Information
    Due to the risk for serious adverse reactions, the FDA recommends against administration of over the counter (OTC) cough and cold products to neonates, infants and children younger than 2 years of age. When administering OTC medications to older pediatric patients, they advise caregivers to read product labels carefully, use caution when administering multiple products to avoid duplication of ingredients, and use only measuring devices specifically designed for use with medications. Care teams should thoroughly assess the use of similar products, both prescription and nonprescription, to avoid duplication of therapy and the potential for inadvertent overdose.
    Headline
    diabetes mellitus, thyroid disease
    General Information
    Use dexbrompheniramine; pseudoephedrine with caution in individuals with diabetes mellitus or thyroid disease. Sympathomimetic agents such as pseudoephedrine may aggravate these conditions.
    Headline
    geriatric
    General Information
    The Beers Criteria identifies first-generation antihistamines as potentially inappropriate medications (PIMs) for geriatric adults, recommending their avoidance due to their high anticholinergic properties, decreased clearance in older age, the development of tolerance when used as sleep aids, and an increased risk of anticholinergic effects and toxicity compared to younger individuals. These medications should particularly be avoided in patients with dementia or cognitive impairment (due to adverse CNS effects), those at high risk for delirium (which can worsen or trigger new-onset delirium), and men with lower urinary tract symptoms or benign prostatic hyperplasia (due to risks of urinary retention or hesitancy).
    Headline
    activities requiring coordination and concentration, driving or operating machinery
    General Information
    Dexbrompheniramine can cause drowsiness. Advise individuals receiving dexbrompheniramine; pseudoephedrine to avoid driving or operating machinery and activities requiring coordination and concentration until the effects of the drug are known. Additive drowsiness may be seen during coadministration with alcohol and other CNS depressants.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Avoidance of combination products containing dexbrompheniramine; pseudoephedrine is generally recommended during pregnancy. Non-pharmacologic methods (e.g., fluids, rest) are recommended to be tried first for symptomatic relief of colds or allergies during pregnancy. Pseudoephedrine may reduce blood flow to the placenta and the fetus, and there is some evidence use may be associated with birth defects if used during early pregnancy. Evidence from case-control studies in human pregnancy indicate there may be an increased risk of gastroschisis, small intestinal atresia, and hemifacial microsomia in babies exposed in utero to pseudoephedrine, particularly in the first trimester. No well controlled trials of dexbrompheniramine use in human gestation are available. Loratadine and cetirizine are acceptable antihistamine alternatives based on their excellent safety data and recommendation in multiple guidelines for use during pregnancy.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Combination products containing dexbrompheniramine; pseudoephedrine should be used with caution during breast-feeding. Pseudoephedrine is excreted into breast milk. Peak milk concentrations occur 1 to 1.5 hours after a maternal oral dosage, and peak milk concentrations usually exceed those of maternal plasma. The total amount of pseudoephedrine (measured by AUC) in milk is 2 to 3 times that of plasma. However, only 0.5% of a maternal dose would probably be ingested by an infant during breast-feeding within any 24 hours. Pseudoephedrine may reduce milk production. Milk production over a 24-hour period was reduced by an average of 24% compared to placebo after a single 60 mg dose of pseudoephedrine. Based on concentrations in breast milk and assuming a maternal dose of 240 mg pseudoephedrine daily, it was estimated that an infant would receive 4.3% of the maternal weight-adjusted dose. It is suggested to avoid pseudoephedrine use if lactation is not yet well established or if there are difficulties producing sufficient milk.. Sympathomimetic adverse effects (irritability, excessive crying, and altered sleeping patterns) have been reported in a breastfed infant following maternal administration of pseudoephedrine; symptoms resolved within 12 hours of drug discontinuation. Small, occasional doses of dexbrompheniramine would not be expected to cause adverse effects in breastfed infants; insignificant amounts of dexbrompheniramine are secreted in the breast milk. Larger doses or more prolonged use may cause effects in the infant or decrease the milk supply. Side effects in breast-feeding infants may manifest as irritability, disturbed sleeping patterns, drowsiness, hyperexcitability, or excessive crying. Antihistamines can lower basal prolactin secretion and may interfere with the establishment of lactation. Consider treatment alternatives to dexbrompheniramine; pseudoephedrine. Guidelines recommend loratadine or cetirizine at the lowest dose as preferred antihistamines because they are safer with only low levels found in breast milk.

    General Information
    Possible central nervous system (CNS) side effects of first-generation antihistamines are drowsiness and dizziness. Alternatively, antihistamines can cause paradoxical CNS stimulation manifested as nervousness/anxiety, restlessness, excitability, irritability, insomnia, hallucinations, or seizures. These reactions may occur more frequently in children and with the use of high doses.  CNS effects such as restlessness, headache, lightheadedness, dizziness, tremor, and insomnia have been infrequently reported during therapy with pseudoephedrine at usual doses. If nervousness, dizziness, or sleeplessness occur, discontinue pseudoephedrine. Seizures or psychosis may occur, but have more commonly occurred with excessive dosage, overdosage, or in patients with renal failure receiving the maximum dosage (without dosage adjustment).

Description
  Text
  Dexbrompheniramine; pseudoephedrine is an oral combination of an antihistamine and nasal decongestant indicated for the temporary relief of symptoms, such as runny nose, itchy, watery eyes, nasal congestion, sneezing, or itching of the nose and throat, due to allergic rhinitis or other respiratory allergies or the common cold. Dexbrompheniramine is an antihistamine with anticholinergic and sedative effects; excitability may occur, especially in children. Sympathomimetics, such as pseudoephedrine, may cause increased blood pressure. However, well-controlled hypertensive adults receiving pseudoephedrine at recommended doses (240 mg/day PO) generally do not appear at risk for significant elevations in blood pressure, although small increases in blood pressure and heart rate may occur.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Dexbrompheniramine is an H1-antagonist belonging to the alkylamine class of antihistamines. Antihistamines appear to compete with histamine for receptor sites on effector cells. Dexbrompheniramine also has anticholinergic (drying) and sedative effects. Among the antihistaminic effects, it antagonizes the allergic response (vasodilatation, increased vascular permeability, increased mucus secretion) of nasal tissue. Pseudoephedrine is a sympathomimetic amine that exerts a decongestant action on the nasal mucosa via alpha adrenergic activity. The vasoconstrictor effects of pseudoephedrine reduce nasal blood flow. Pseudoephedrine produces peripheral effects similar to those of ephedrine and central effects similar to, but less intense than, amphetamines. While its vasoconstrictor action is similar to that of ephedrine, pseudoephedrine has less pressor effect in normotensive adults. It has the potential for excitatory side effects.

    Description
    After a single dexbrompheniramine 2 mg dose, the initial histamine-antagonizing response is produced within 1 hour and a maximum response in about 3 hours.     Name
    Renal Impairment
    Description
    Pseudoephedrine is primarily excreted unchanged in the urine. A decrease in renal function is likely to decrease the clearance of pseudoephedrine, prolonging the half-life and resulting in accumulation. Therefore, pseudoephedrine may accumulate in patients with renal impairment.
  Text
  Dexbrompheniramine; pseudoephedrine is administered orally. Dexbrompheniramine is about twice as potent as the racemic compound. Pseudoephedrine is rapidly and almost completely absorbed from the gastrointestinal tract. Pseudoephedrine is extensively distributed into extravascular sites. The apparent Vd of pseudoephedrine is 2.6 to 3.5 L/kg. About 43% to 96% of an administered dose of pseudoephedrine is excreted unchanged in the urine. The remainder is apparently metabolized in the liver to inactive compounds by N-demethylation, parahydroxylation, and oxidative deamination. Pseudoephedrine has a mean elimination half-life of 4 to 6 hours which is dependent on urine pH; the elimination half-life is decreased at urine pH lower than 6 and may be increased at urine pH higher than 8.
