Contraindications And Precautions

  Contraindications And Precautions
    Headline
    Drug Interactions
    General Information
    The coadministration of certain medications may lead to harm and require avoidance or therapy modification; review all drug interactions prior to concomitant use of other medications.
    Headline
    depression, suicidal ideation
    General Information
    Prescribers should weigh the potential risks and benefits before using bimekizumab in patients with a history of severe depression or suicidal ideation or behavior. Advise patients, their caregivers, and families to monitor for the emergence or worsening of depression, suicidal ideation, or other mood changes. If such changes occur, advise them to promptly seek medical attention or call the National Suicide and Crisis Lifeline. Patients treated with bimekizumab with new or worsening symptoms of depression or suicidal ideation and/or behavior should be referred to a mental health professional, as appropriate. Prescribers should also re-evaluate the risks and benefits of continuing treatment with bimekizumab if such events occur. Patients with active suicidal ideation, suicidal ideation within the month prior to screening, a history of suicide attempt within the past 5 years prior to screening, or moderately severe to severe major depression were excluded from clinical studies.
    Headline
    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components.
    Headline
    infection
    General Information
    Bimekizumab may increase the risk of infections and may increase the risk for reactivation of latent infections, due to its effects on the immune system. Do not initiate bimekizumab until any clinically important active infection in a patient resolves or is adequately treated. Instruct patients treated with bimekizumab to seek medical advice if signs or symptoms of clinically important chronic or acute infection occur. If a patient develops a serious infection or is not responding to standard therapy, monitor the patient closely and discontinue bimekizumab until the infection resolves. Evaluate all patients for tuberculosis before starting bimekizumab. Avoid use in patients with active tuberculosis infection. Treatment of latent tuberculosis should be initiated prior to treatment with bimekizumab. In patients with a history of latent or active tuberculosis without a confirmed adequate treatment course, consider anti-tuberculosis therapy before starting bimekizumab. Monitor patients closely for signs and symptoms of active tuberculosis infection during and after treatment with bimekizumab.
    Headline
    inflammatory bowel disease
    General Information
    Avoid us of bimekizumab in patients with active inflammatory bowel disease. During treatment, monitor patients for signs and symptoms of inflammatory bowel disease (including ulcerative colitis and Crohn disease) and discontinue treatment if new onset or worsening of signs and symptoms occurs.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Available data regarding the use of bimekizumab during pregnancy are insufficient to evaluate for a drug-associated risk of major birth defects, miscarriages, or other adverse maternal or fetal outcomes. Human IgG does cross the placental barrier; therefore, bimekizumab may be transmitted from mother to fetus. No effects on neonatal development were observed when monkeys were given bimekizumab at dose exposures up to 38-times the maximum recommended human dose (MRHD). The clinical significance of these findings is unknown. There is a pregnancy exposure registry that monitors outcomes in pregnant patients exposed to bimekizumab; pregnant patients are encouraged to enroll themselves at https://mothertobaby.org/pregnancy-studies/ or call 1-877-311-8972.
    Headline
    hepatic disease
    General Information
    Patients with acute hepatic disease or hepatic cirrhosis may be at increased risk for severe hepatic injury; avoid use of bimekizumab in these patients. Treatment with bimekizumab was associated with increased incidence of liver enzyme elevations compared to treatment with placebo in randomized clinical trials. Test liver enzymes, alkaline phosphatase, and bilirubin at baseline, periodically during treatment with bimekizumab and according to routine patient management. If treatment-related increases in liver enzymes occur and drug-induced liver injury is suspected, interrupt bimekizumab until a diagnosis of liver injury is excluded. Permanently discontinue bimekizumab in patients with causally associated combined elevations of transaminases and bilirubin.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    There are no data on the presence of bimekizumab in human or animal milk, the effects on the breastfed infant, or the effects on milk production. Endogenous IgG and monoclonal antibodies are transferred in human milk. The effects of local gastrointestinal exposure and limited systemic exposure in the breastfed infant to bimekizumab are unknown. The developmental and health benefits of breast-feeding should be considered along with the mother's clinical need for bimekizumab and any potential adverse effects on the breastfed infant from bimekizumab or from the underlying maternal condition. If a breast-feeding infant experiences an adverse effect related to a maternally administered drug, health care providers are encouraged to report the adverse effect to the FDA.

Monitor for toxicity and adjust the dosage of CYP substrates as appropriate, especially during initiation and discontinuation of bimekizumab therapy. Bimekizumab may alter CYP enzyme expression.

Adverse Reactions

  Adverse Reactions
    Headline
    suicidal ideation
    General Information
    During the 16-week clinical trials, passive suicidal ideation was reported in up to 1.8% of patients receiving bimekizumab and up to 0.6% of patients in the placebo groups. Further, during an open-label extension trial, 1 completed suicide was reported in a bimekizumab treated patient after 718 days of treatment (1/2,480; 0.01/100 patient years). The completed suicide occurred in a plaque psoriasis patient without a past reported psychiatric history. Additionally, suicide attempts were noted in 3 plaque psoriasis patients (0.04/100 patient years), 1 psoriatic arthritis patient (0.04/100 patient years), 1 non-radiographic axial spondyloarthritis patient (0.25/100 patient years), no ankylosing spondylitis patients, and 2 hidradenitis suppurativa patients (0.15/100 patient years). A causal association between treatment with bimekizumab and an increased risk of suicidal behavior has not been established. Bimekizumab was not studied in patients with active suicidal ideation, suicidal ideation within the month prior to screening, a history of suicide attempt within the past 5 years prior to screening, or moderately severe to severe major depression. Monitor for the emergence or worsening of depression, suicidal ideation, or other mood changes.

Description
  Text
  Bimekizumab is a recombinant humanized immunoglobulin (IgG1) monoclonal antibody that antagonizes the effects of interleukin 17A (IL-17A) and interleukin 17F (IL-17F). It is approved for use in adults to treat moderate to severe plaque psoriasis, active psoriatic arthritis, active non-radiographic axial spondyloarthritis, active ankylosing spondylitis, and moderate to severe hidradenitis suppurativa. Adverse effects associated with bimekizumab therapy include worsening symptoms of depression or suicidal ideation, increased risk of infection, hepatic enzyme abnormalities, and exacerbation of inflammatory bowel disease. Prior to initiating therapy, obtain liver function tests, evaluate for active or latent tuberculosis infection, and ensure the patient is current on all age-appropriate vaccinations. Bimekizumab was initially FDA approved in 2023.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Bimekizumab is a humanized immunoglobulin IgG1-kappa monoclonal antibody with two identical antigen binding regions that selectively bind to human interleukin 17A (IL-17A), interleukin 17F (IL-17F), and interleukin 17-AF cytokines, and inhibits their interaction with the IL-17 receptor complex. IL- 17A and IL-17F are naturally occurring cytokines that are involved in normal inflammatory and immune responses. Bimekizumab inhibits the release of proinflammatory cytokines and chemokines.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Subcutaneous Route
    Description
    Following subcutaneous administration, the absolute bioavailability of bimekizumab is 70%. The median peak plasma concentration is 25 mcg/mL (range: 12 to 50 mcg/mL) and is reached in 3 to 4 days. Bimekizumab exhibited dose-proportional pharmacokinetics in patients with plaque psoriasis over a dose range of 64 mg to 480 mg (0.2- to 1.5-times the approved recommended dosage) following subcutaneous administration. The pharmacokinetics of bimekizumab are comparable in patients with plaque psoriasis, psoriatic arthritis, non-radiographic axial spondyloarthritis, and ankylosing spondylitis; however, the median steady state trough concentration in patients with hidradenitis suppurative is approximately 40% lower than patients with plaque psoriasis.

  Text
  Bimekizumab is administered subcutaneously. For most patients, the median volume of distribution at steady state is 11.2 L; however, the volume of distribution in patients with hidradenitis suppurative is estimated to be approximately 18% higher. Bimekizumab is expected to be degraded into small peptides by catabolic pathways. Drug clearance is independent of dose, with the median (coefficient of variation %) clearance (CL/F) of bimekizumab being 0.337 L/day (32.7%) and the mean terminal elimination half-life being 23 days. For patients with hidradenitis suppurative, the apparent clearance is estimated to be approximately 31% higher.
 
Affected cytochrome P450 (CYP450) isoenzymes and drug transporters: None
The formation of CYP450 enzymes can be altered by increased levels of certain cytokines (e.g., IL-1, IL-6, IL-10, TNFalpha, IFN) during chronic inflammation. Treatment with bimekizumab may modulate serum levels of some cytokines.

Administration
  General Information
  For storage information, see the specific product information within the How Supplied section.
  Route Specific Administrations
    Administration Details
      Name
      Subcutaneous Administration
      Description
      Bimekizumab is available as a prefilled syringe and as an autoinjector.
The product is intended for use under the guidance and supervision of a physician.
Patients may self-inject or caregivers may give subcutaneous injections after proper training.
If 2 separate 160 mg injections are used to achieve the recommended dose, administer each injection subcutaneously at a different anatomical site.
Administration sites for patient administration include the upper arms, thighs, and any quadrant of the abdomen. Health care professionals or caregivers may inject in the upper, outer arm.
Do not administer where the skin is tender, bruised, red, hard, thick, scaly, or affected by psoriasis.
Rotate sites of injection with each dose. Injections should be administered at a different location than was used for the previous injection.
Wash hands with soap and water before drug administration. Clean the injection site with an alcohol wipe and let dry.
Bimekizumab does not contain preservatives; therefore, discard any unused product remaining in the prefilled syringe or autoinjector. Discard the single-dose autoinjector or syringe after use in a proper puncture-resistant container.
Missed doses: If a dose or administration time is missed, administer the missed dose as soon as possible. Thereafter, resume dosing at the regularly scheduled time.
 
Preparation for the use of the prefilled syringes or autoinjector:
Remove the carton containing bimekizumab from the refrigerator. To protect from light, do not remove the prefilled syringes or autoinjectors from the carton. Allow 30 to 45 minutes to reach room temperature. DO NOT use any methods to speed the warming process, such as a microwave, hot water, or sunlight.
Inspect syringe or autoinjector for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration.
Storage of unopened prefilled-syringes and autoinjectors: Protect from light and store refrigerated at 2 to 8 degrees C (36 to 46 degrees F) until time of use. Do not freeze and do not use the injection if it has been frozen. Do not shake.
If needed, a prefilled-syringe or autoinjector may be stored at room temperature up to 25 degrees C (77 degrees F) for up to 30 days in the original carton to protect from light. Once the product has been stored at room temperature, do not return to the refrigerator; discard, if unused, within 30 days.
Record the date when first removed from the refrigerator in the spaces provided on the carton.
 
Administration
Autoinjector
Hold autoinjector at a 90 degree angle to the cleaned injection site.
With the autoinjector flat against the skin, firmly press the autoinjector down against your skin.
The autoinjector will give a 'click' sound to indicate the injection has started. It will take approximately 20 seconds to receive your dose. Do not lift the autoinjector away from the skin.
A yellow color indicator should fill the viewing window. If the window has not turned yellow, the full dose may not have been administered.
A second 'click' will indicate the injection is complete. Continue to hold the autoinjector down against the skin for 5 seconds after the second click to ensure you have received the full dose.
Remove from skin and dispose of autoinjector.
Do not rub injection site.
Prefilled syringe
Clean skin and gently pinch and hold a fold of skin.
Insert the needle into your skin at a 45-degree angle.
Release skin and fully push the plunger head all the way down until all the medication is delivered.
Remove from skin and dispose of syringe.
Do not rub the injection site.
