        Dosage Description
        10 mg intrathecally twice weekly (days 1 and 4) for 8 weeks has been studied. Intrathecal thiotepa was compared with intrathecal methotrexate in a randomized trial in 52 patients with previously untreated neoplastic meningitis. Patients in this study had a solid tumor (breast cancer, n = 25; lung cancer, n = 12; other, n = 4) or lymphoma (n = 10) as the primary tumor type. The median overall survival times were 14.1 weeks in the thiotepa arm and 15.9 weeks in the methotrexate arm. After 8 weeks of therapy, no patient experienced a complete response or improvement. Eight patients in each treatment arm converted from a positive to a negative cytology after therapy. Patients in the thiotepa arm experienced significantly less neurologic (p < 0.008) and skin/mucous membrane (p = 0.042) toxicity compared with the methotrexate arm. Serious toxicities reported with thiotepa included bacterial meningitis resulting in death (n = 1), grade 4 hematologic toxicity (n = 2), and respiratory arrest (n = 1).

Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  General Information
  Thiotepa is contraindicated in patients with severe bone marrow suppression. Thiotepa should be used cautiously in patients with who have had previous myelosuppressive therapy such as chemotherapy or radiation therapy. Since these adverse effects can be fatal, the patient must be warned to immediately report any signs such as fever, sore throat, or abnormal bleeding. Patients with preexisting marrow suppression, including neutropenia and/or thrombocytopenia, should be allowed to recover their counts prior to thiotepa administration. Hematological status (hematocrit, hemoglobin concentration, leukocyte and platelet counts) should be monitored closely during thiotepa therapy (weekly) and for at least 3 weeks after discontinuance of therapy. Thiotepa dosages should be reduced or discontinued if leukocyte counts fall below 3000/mm3 or if platelet counts fall below 150,000/mm3. Patients with an active infection should be treated prior to receiving thiotepa. Patients with a history of varicella zoster, other herpes infection (e.g., herpes simplex), or other viral infection are at risk for reactivation of the infection when treated with chemotherapy.
 
Myelosuppressive effects of thiotepa can increase the risk of infection or bleeding; therefore, dental work should be delayed until blood counts have returned to normal. Patients, especially those with dental disease, should be instructed in proper oral hygiene, including caution in use of regular toothbrushes, dental floss, and toothpicks.
 
Intramuscular injections should not be administered to patients with platelet count = 2 weeks. The administration of live vaccines to immunocompromised patients should be avoided. Those undergoing chemotherapy should not be exposed to others who have recently received the oral poliovirus vaccine (OPV). Measles-mumps-rubella (MMR) vaccination is not contraindicated for the close contacts, including health care professionals, of immunocompromised patients. Passive immunoprophylaxis with immune globulins may be indicated for immunocompromised persons instead of, or in addition to, vaccination. When exposed to a vaccine-preventable disease such as measles, severely immunocompromised children should be considered susceptible regardless of their vaccination history.

    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components.
    Headline
    bone marrow suppression, radiation therapy
    General Information
    Thiotepa may cause severe bone marrow suppression; the risk of severe myelosuppression is increased for patients who have received prior radiation therapy or chemotherapy. Periodically monitor complete blood counts during treatment with thiotepa; provide supportive care for infections, bleeding, and symptomatic anemia. High doses of thiotepa may cause bone marrow ablation with resultant infection or bleeding, requiring hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (HSCT) to prevent potentially fatal complications; do not begin a preparative regimen for HSCT containing thiotepa if a stem cell donor is not available.
    Headline
    renal failure, renal impairment
    General Information
    Monitor patients with moderate to severe renal impairment (CrCl less than 30 mL/min) for signs and symptoms of toxicity for an extended period of time following treatment with thiotepa. Moderate renal impairment decreases renal excretion of thiotepa which may increase the incidence and severity of adverse reactions; the effects of severe renal impairment or renal failure on thiotepa exposure are unknown.
    Headline
    Child-Pugh class B, Child-Pugh class C, hepatic failure
    General Information
    Monitor patients with moderate to severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh class B; Child-Pugh class C; bilirubin levels greater than 1.5 to 3 times the upper limit of normal [ULN] and any AST) for treatment-related adverse reactions during treatment and for an extended period of time after treatment. Patients with moderate hepatic impairment may have increased thiotepa exposure due to decreased hepatic metabolism which may increase the incidence and severity of adverse reactions; the effect of severe hepatic impairment or hepatic failure on thiotepa exposure is unknown.
    Headline
    new primary malignancy
    General Information
    Thiotepa, like most other alkylating agents, is carcinogenic when administered to laboratory animals. There is some evidence that thiotepa is carcinogenic in humans; therefore, patients who receive thiotepa may have an increased risk of developing a new primary malignancy.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Thiotepa may cause fetal harm when administered during pregnancy, based on its mechanism of action and findings from animal data. Advise pregnant people of the potential fetal risk. Embryo-fetal toxicities, including neural tube defects and malformations of the skeletal system, were observed in animal reproduction studies in mice and rats at doses approximately 0.125 times and equivalent to, respectively, the maximum recommended human daily dose based on body-surface area. Additionally, thiotepa was lethal to rabbit fetuses at approximately 2 times the maximum recommended human therapeutic dose.
    Headline
    people who can cause pregnancy in others, people who may become pregnant, reproductive risk
    General Information
    Counsel people who may become pregnant and people who can cause pregnancy in others about the reproductive risk associated with thiotepa. Thiotepa can cause fetal harm. Verify pregnancy status prior to treatment initiation. Advise people who may become pregnant to use effective contraception while taking thiotepa and for 6 months after the last dose. For those that can cause pregnancy in others, contraception should be used during thiotepa treatment and for 1 year after the last dose. Based on animal data, thiotepa may cause male or female infertility. Because thiotepa may damage spermatozoa and testicular tissue, advise people who can cause pregnancy in others to consider sperm banking prior to treatment initiation.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Advise patients to avoid breast-feeding while taking thiotepa, due to the risk of serious adverse reactions in the breast-fed child. There are no data on the presence of thiotepa or its metabolites in human milk, their effects on the breast-fed child, or their effects on milk production.

Adverse Reactions
  Headline
  Adverse Reactions
  General Information
  Hematologic toxicity is the major adverse effect associated with thiotepa therapy and is usually dose-related and cumulative. Manifestations of hematologic toxicity include thrombocytopenia, leukopenia, neutropenia, anemia, and sometimes pancytopenia, which can be fatal. Thrombocytopenia can be prolonged. Hematological status (hematocrit, hemoglobin concentration, leukocyte and platelet counts) should be monitored closely during thiopeta therapy (weekly) and for at least 3 weeks after discontinuance of therapy. Thiotepa dosages should be reduced or discontinued if leukocyte counts fall below 3000/mm3 or if platelet counts fall below 150,000/mm3. Bone marrow function should be allowed to recover fully between treatments. Severe hematological toxicity may be treated with supportive therapy, antibiotics for infections, and blood transfusions.Thiotepa-induced cellular destruction can result in tumor lysis syndrome (TLS) which includes hyperkalemia, hyperphosphatemia, hyperuricemia, and hypocalcemia. Uric acid nephropathy, acute renal failure due renal tubular obstruction, metabolic acidosis, and nephrolithiasis can also occur. These problems are more severe when a highly chemosensitive disease is treated (i.e., leukemia or lymphoma). Aggressive alkalinization of the urine and use of allopurinol can prevent urate nephropathy; close monitoring of serum electrolytes and renal function is recommended.Hypersensitivity reactions, manifested as bronchospasm or urticaria, have been noted with thiotepa therapy. Laryngeal edema, wheezing, and anaphylactic shock may occur.Intravesical thiotepa therapy may cause local toxicity. Irritative symptoms such as dysuria, urinary urgency and related bladder symptoms are common. Urinary retention may occur. Rare occurrences of chemical or hemorrhagic cystitis may follow intravesical, but not intravenous thiotepa therapy. Adverse intravesical effects rarely lead to discontinuation of therapy.Injection site reaction, specifically pain or contact dermatitis, has been reported with intravenous thiotepa administration. Instillation of thiotepa may also be associated with pain.Thiotepa can cause dermatitis and alopecia. There have been reports of skin depigmentation following topical administration. Instances of skin hyperpigmentation have been reported following high IV doses of thiotepa. In some cases this was thought to be a result of local skin concentration, possibly due to occlusive materials. Pigmentation became evident within 1—4 days, darkened over a week, then faded over the next month.Gastrointestinal effects are infrequent with recommended doses of thiotepa. They include abdominal pain, anorexia, and nausea/vomiting. At doses >= 700—800 mg/m2 esophagitis, stomatitis, and enterocolitis may occur.Adverse effects of thiotepa on the CNS are dose limiting. At recommended doses dizziness, headache, and blurred vision may occur.There have been reports of amenorrhea with thiotepa therapy. Fatigue and weakness are generalized reactions.Secondary malignancy, specifically acute nonlymphocytic leukemia, has been observed after intravenous thiotepa use.
  Adverse Reactions
    Headline
    anemia, bone marrow suppression, neutropenia, thrombocytopenia
    General Information
    Thiotepa may cause severe bone marrow suppression; the risk of severe myelosuppression is increased for patients who have received prior radiation therapy or chemotherapy. High doses of thiotepa may cause bone marrow ablation with resultant infection or bleeding, requiring hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (HSCT) to prevent potentially fatal complications; do not begin a preparative regimen for HSCT containing thiotepa if a stem cell donor is not available. Severe cytopenias including neutropenia, anemia, and thrombocytopenia occurred in all regardless of the treatment received in a retrospective analysis of pediatric patients with class 3 beta-thalassemia who underwent allogenic hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (HSCT) with busulfan and cyclophosphamide, with (n = 25) or without (n = 51) thiotepa. Febrile bone marrow aplasia, decreased hemoglobin, and refractoriness to platelet transfusion were reported with the use of thiotepa in postmarketing experience.

Description
  Text
  Thiotepa is an alkylating agent indicated in adults for the treatment of breast cancer, malignant effusions, ovarian cancer, and superficial papillary carcinoma of the urinary bladder. It is indicated in pediatric patients with class 3 beta-thalassemia to reduce the risk of graft rejection when used in conjunction with high-dose busulfan and cyclophosphamide as a preparative regimen for allogeneic hematopoietic progenitor cell transplantation (HSCT). Thiotepa carries a black box warning for severe myelosuppression, with high doses potentially causing bone marrow ablation. Thiotepa was FDA approved in 1959.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Thiotepa is a polyfunctional alkylating agent, related chemically and pharmacologically to a nitrogen mustard compound. It disrupts the bonds of DNA by releasing ethylenimine radicals, mimicking the effects of irradiation. A main bond disruption is caused by the alkylation of guanine at the N-7 position, severing the link between the purine base and the sugar and releasing alkylated guanines.

  Text
  Thiotepa is administered intravenously or via intravesical or intracavitary instillation. It is approximately 10% to 20% bound to plasma proteins. Following the administration of thiotepa 20 mg to 250 mg/m2 as an IV bolus or up to a 4-hour IV infusion, the mean volume of distribution ranged from 1 to 1.9 L/kg, the mean clearance ranged from 14.6 to 27.9 L/hr/m2, and the mean terminal elimination half-life ranged from 1.4 to 3.7 hours for thiotepa and from 4.9 to 17.6 hours for its major active metabolite, N,N',N''triethylenephosphoramide (TEPA). Thiotepa undergoes hepatic metabolism. Urinary excretion of thiotepa accounts for less than 2% of a dose, with TEPA accounting for 11% or less of the dose. Thiotepa and/or its active metabolites may also be excreted in part via skin in patients receiving high-dose therapy.
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes: CYP3A4 and CYP2B6
In vitro data suggests that thiotepa is metabolized to TEPA via CYP3A4 and CYP2B6 and that it is a CYP2B6 inhibitor. In vitro, inhibitors of CYP3A4 and CYP2B6 decrease the metabolism of thiotepa. In vitro, thiotepa is also a CYP2B6 inhibitor.

Change occlusive dressing and clean the covered skin at least twice daily through 48 hours after administration of thiotepa.
Change bed sheets daily during treatment.

Reconstitution (Tepadina brand only)
15 mg vial: Add 1.5 mL of Sterile Water for Injection for a final concentration of 10 mg/mL.
100 mg vial: Add 10 mL of Sterile Water for Injection for a final concentration of 10 mg/mL.
Mix by repeated inversions until completely dissolved; the reconstituted solution may occasionally show opalescence.
Storage: The reconstituted solution may be stored for up to 8 hours refrigerated (2 to 8 degrees C or 36 to 46 degrees F).
Dilution (Tepadina and Tepylute brands)
If using the Tepylute brand of thiotepa, remove the vial from refrigeration 1 hour prior to dilution.
Dilute the appropriate volume of thiotepa solution in 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection to obtain a final concentration between 0.5 mg/mL and 1 mg/mL. Gently invert to mix.
Storage: Diluted thiotepa may be stored under refrigeration (2 to 8 degrees C or 36 to 46 degrees F) for up to 24 hours, or at room temperature (25 degrees C or 77 degrees F) for up to 4 hours.
 
Activation of Multichamber Flexible Bag (Tepadina brand)
Remove the multichamber flexible bag from the aluminum overwrap, with the label facing upwards.
Ensure that there are no leakages from the connection ports or from the chambers.
Check the integrity of the peel seal, verifying the absence of liquid in the smaller chamber.
Overlap your hands on the lower portion of the larger chamber and press firmly with uniform pressure until the peel seal is broken; this may take up to 5 seconds.

The full bag contains 200 mg of thiotepa. If a dose less than 200 mg is necessary, withdraw the appropriate volume and transfer to an empty, sterile bag via Luer Port 3.
Storage: An activated multichamber flexible bag may be stored refrigerated (2 to 8 degrees C or 36 to 46 degrees F) for up to 168 hours or at room temperature (20 to 25 degrees C or 68 to 77 degrees F) for up to 56 hours.
 
Intravenous Infusion
Administer using an infusion set equipped with a 0.2 micron in-line filter.
Flush the catheter with approximately 5 mL of 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection prior to each infusion.
High doses of thiotepa for allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (HSCT): Infuse intravenously via central venous catheter over 3 hours.
Flush the catheter with approximately 5 mL of 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection following each infusion.

Intravesicular Administration
Instill into bladder via catheter.
Retain solution in the bladder for 2 hours. If the patient is unable to retain 60 mL for 2 hours, the dose may be given in a volume of 30 mL.
Reposition the patient every 15 minutes for maximum tumor contact.
