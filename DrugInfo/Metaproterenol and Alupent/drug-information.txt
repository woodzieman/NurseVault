Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  General Information
  Metaproterenol is relatively contraindicated in patients with hyperthyroidism (thyrotoxicosis, thyroid disease), diabetes mellitus, seizures, or hypersensitivity to sympathomimetics (pheochromocytoma). Metaproterenol should be used cautiously in any patient with cardiac disease because it has cardiostimulatory actions. Metaproterenol should be used with caution in patients with ischemic cardiac disease (coronary artery disease), hypertension, cardiac arrhythmias (including tachycardia), or QT prolongation. In addition, beta-agonists should be avoided in patients with congenital long QT syndrome due to the risk of torsade de pointes. Elderly patients may be more sensitive to the side effects of beta-agonists, especially tremor and tachycardia; this risk is higher in patients with preexisting coronary artery disease. Although not clearly established, airway responsiveness to beta-agonist medications may also change with age.Metaproterenol is absolutely contraindicated in patients with a known hypersensitivity to the drug, to propellant fluorocarbons, or to any component of the specific dosage formulation.Metaproterenol is classified as a category C drug during pregnancy because its safe use during pregnancy has not been established. Furthermore, metaproterenol can interfere with uterine contractility as a result of its beta-adrenergic-mediated relaxant effects on smooth muscle (oral > inhalation). Despite this precaution, the 2004 guidelines of the National Asthma Education and Prevention Program (NAEPP) Asthma and Pregnancy Working Group include short-acting inhaled beta2-agonists as first line therapy for mild intermittent asthma during pregnancy, if treatment is required. Albuterol is preferred over other short-acting beta2-agonists due to extensive safety-related information during pregnancy. However, there is no evidence of fetal injury with the use of other short-acting inhaled beta2-agonists, and maintaining a previously established treatment regimen may be more beneficial to the patient. Selection of any pharmacologic treatment for asthma control during pregnancy should include the specific needs of the patient, based on an individual evaluation, and consideration of the potential benefits or risks to the fetus.It is not known if metaproterenol is excreted into breast milk, so caution must be exercised in breast-feeding mothers who may require metaproterenol.The safety and effectiveness of regular-release metaproterenol tablets have not been established for children < 6 years. The safety and effectiveness of metaproterenol inhalation aerosol have not been established for children < 12 years; the manufacturer does not recommend its use in this population.Metaproterenol is relatively contraindicated in patients with hyperthyroidism because they can be more sensitive to sympathomimetics and be predisposed to thyrotoxic effects.Metaproterenol is relatively contraindicated in patients with seizures and seizure disorder because stimulation of the sympathetic nervous system via beta-adrenergic stimulation can lower the seizure threshold.Metaproterenol is relatively contraindicated in patients with diabetes mellitus because of the potential for decreased glycemic control.Beta2-agonists should be administered with extreme caution to patients being treated with MAOI therapy (see Drug Interactions).

    Headline
    cardiac arrhythmias, coronary artery disease, hypertension, hypokalemia, QT prolongation
    General Information
    Monitor heart rate and blood pressure in patients receiving high doses of metaproterenol; cardiovascular adverse effects are more likely to occur when aggressive doses are used. Metaproterenol, like other beta2-agonists and sympathomimetic amines, should be used with caution in patients with cardiovascular disorders, especially coronary insufficiency (coronary artery disease), cardiac arrhythmias, and hypertension. Metaproterenol can produce a clinically significant cardiovascular effect in some patients as measured by increases in pulse rate, systolic or diastolic blood pressure, and cardiac arrhythmias, such as supraventricular tachycardia and extrasystoles. If such effects occur, metaproterenol may need to be discontinued. In addition, beta-agonists have been reported to produce electrocardiographic (ECG) changes, such as flattening of the T wave, QT prolongation, and ST segment depression, although the clinical significance of these findings is unknown. Fatalities have been reported in association with excessive use of inhaled sympathomimetic drugs. Beta-adrenergic agonist therapies like metaproterenol may produce significant hypokalemia in some patients, possibly through intracellular shunting, which has the potential to produce adverse cardiovascular effects. The decrease is usually transient, not requiring supplementation.

    Headline
    labor, pregnancy
    General Information
    Metaproterenol is classified as a category C drug during pregnancy because its safe use during pregnancy has not been established. Furthermore, metaproterenol can interfere with uterine contractility as a result of its beta-adrenergic-mediated relaxant effects on smooth muscle (oral > inhalation). Despite this precaution, the 2004 guidelines of the National Asthma Education and Prevention Program (NAEPP) Asthma and Pregnancy Working Group include short-acting inhaled beta2-agonists as first line therapy for mild intermittent asthma during pregnancy, if treatment is required. Albuterol is preferred over other short-acting beta2-agonists due to extensive safety-related information during pregnancy. However, there is no evidence of fetal injury with the use of other short-acting inhaled beta2-agonists, and maintaining a previously established treatment regimen may be more beneficial to the patient. Because of the potential for beta-agonist interference with uterine contractility, use of metaproterenol for management of asthma during labor should be restricted to those patients in whom the benefits clearly outweigh the risks. Selection of any pharmacologic treatment for asthma control during pregnancy should include the specific needs of the patient, based on an individual evaluation, and consideration of the potential benefits or risks to the fetus.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    It is not known if metaproterenol is excreted into breast milk; according to the manufacturer, caution must be exercised in breast-feeding mothers who may require metaproterenol. Metaproterenol via inhaled administration typically results in low systemic concentrations; therefore, it is unlikely that nursing infants would be exposed to clinically significant amounts via breast milk. According to the 2004 recommendations of the National Asthma Education and Prevention Program for managing asthma during pregnancy, there is currently no contraindication for use of short-acting inhaled beta2-agonists during lactation. Consider the benefits of breast-feeding, the risk of potential infant drug exposure, and the risk of an untreated or inadequately treated condition. If a breast-feeding infant experiences an adverse effect related to a maternally administered drug, healthcare providers are encouraged to report the adverse effect to the FDA.
    Headline
    General Information
    General Information
    Do not exceed recommended dosages of beta-agonists; fatalities have been reported in association with excessive use of inhaled sympathomimetic drugs in patients with asthma. The exact cause of death is unknown, but cardiac arrest after an unexpected development of a severe acute asthmatic crisis and subsequent hypoxia is suspected.
    Headline
    children, infants, neonates
    General Information
    The safety and effectiveness of regular-release metaproterenol tablets have not been established for neonates, infants and children < 6 years. The safety and effectiveness of metaproterenol inhalation aerosol have not been established for neonates, infants and children < 12 years; the manufacturer does not recommend its use in this population.
    Headline
    seizure disorder
    General Information
    Metaproterenol is relatively contraindicated in patients with seizures and seizure disorder because stimulation of the sympathetic nervous system via beta-adrenergic stimulation can lower the seizure threshold.
    Headline
    hyperthyroidism, thyroid disease, thyrotoxicosis
    General Information
    Metaproterenol is relatively contraindicated in patients with hyperthyroidism (thyrotoxicosis, thyroid disease) because they can be more sensitive to sympathomimetics and be predisposed to thyrotoxic effects.
    Headline
    diabetes mellitus
    General Information
    Metaproterenol is relatively contraindicated in patients with diabetes mellitus because of the potential for decreased glycemic control.
    Headline
    deterioration of asthma, paradoxical bronchospasm
    General Information
    Paradoxical bronchospasm can occur after treatment with inhaled metaproterenol and can be life-threatening. If this occurs, metaproterenol should be discontinued immediately and supportive care provided as necessary. Additionally, increased metaproterenol use may indicate asthma destabilization. Asthma may deteriorate acutely over a period of hours or chronically over several days or weeks. If deterioration of asthma occurs during therapy with metaproterenol, appropriate evaluation of the patient and the treatment strategy is warranted, giving special consideration to corticosteroid therapy. Metaproterenol has no anti-inflammatory activity and is not a substitute for inhaled or oral corticosteroid therapy. The use of beta-agonists alone may not be adequate to control asthma in many patients. Early consideration should be given to adding anti-inflammatory agents (e.g., corticosteroids) to the therapeutic regimen. Corticosteroids should not be stopped or reduced when metaproterenol therapy is instituted.
    Headline
    geriatric
    General Information
    Geriatric adults may be more sensitive to the side effects of non-selective beta agonists such as metaproterenol, especially tremor and tachycardia, especially when given systemically; systemic use should generally be avoided due to the increased risk for side effects vs. inhaled use.

Adverse Reactions
  Headline
  Adverse Reactions
  General Information
  As with the administration of other sympathomimetics, use of metaproterenol can cause CNS effects including weakness, drowsiness, tremor, nervousness, restlessness, headache, fatigue, malaise, and dizziness.Cardiac arrhythmias (or arrhythmia exacerbation) associated with metaproterenol administration include sinus tachycardia, palpitations, premature ventricular contractions (PVCs), and other ECG changes secondary to metaproterenol's beta1-receptor agonism. Description
  Text
  NOTE: All products associated with this monograph are off-market. The content is retained for historical reference only and is no longer actively maintained.
Metaproterenol is an oral and inhaled synthetic beta-2 and beta-1 agonist that is structurally and pharmacologically similar to isoproterenol. It is more selective for beta-2 receptors than isoproterenol; however, unlike isoproterenol, metaproterenol is used exclusively as a bronchodilator. Metaproterenol use for asthma and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) has declined due to the availability of more selective short-acting beta-2 agonists (SABAs) such as albuterol and levalbuterol. Guidelines recommend against the use of oral short-acting beta-agonists, such as metaproterenol, due to lower efficacy and increased risk for side effects. Use inhaled SABAs for acute bronchospasm, do not use oral agents. Additionally, selective SABAs (e.g., albuterol) are preferred over less beta-2 selective agents such as metaproterenol to minimize potential for adverse cardiac effects. In general, if a SABA is required more than 2 days a week or significant symptoms recur, the Panel recommends initiating treatment for persistent asthma (e.g., inhaled corticosteroids). Quick-relief medication such as SABAs should be made available to all patients with persistent asthma for acute symptom control. According to guidelines, inhaled SABAs may be used for symptom relief in individuals with mild COPD (Group A) and as rescue therapy in combination with maintenance treatments in those with more symptomatic or exacerbation-prone disease (Groups B and E). During COPD exacerbations, short-acting bronchodilators, particularly a SABA with or without a short-acting muscarinic antagonist (SAMA), are recommended as initial bronchodilator therapy. Oral beta-agonists, such as metaproterenol, are generally not preferred because inhaled bronchodilators provide more favorable efficacy and safety profiles.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  NOTE: All products associated with this monograph are off-market. The content is retained for historical reference only and is no longer actively maintained.
Metaproterenol is a moderately selective beta- adrenergic agonist that stimulates receptors of the smooth muscle in the lungs, uterus, and vasculature supplying skeletal muscle, with minimal or no effect on alpha-adrenergic receptors. Intracellularly, the actions of metaproterenol are mediated by cAMP, the production of which is augmented by beta stimulation. The drug is believed to work by activating adenylate cyclase, the enzyme responsible for producing the cellular mediator cAMP. Binding of metaproterenol to beta-2 receptors in the lungs causes relaxation of bronchial smooth muscles, which in turn relieves bronchospasm, reduces airway resistance, facilitates mucous drainage, and increases vital capacity. Metaproterenol can inhibit degranulation and release of inflammatory autocoids from mast cells. Actions on peripheral vascular smooth muscle cause vasodilation and a modest decrease in diastolic blood pressure. Beta-2 adrenergic stimulation also results in intracellular accumulation of serum potassium, possibly due to stimulation of the Na/K ATPase pump, and can cause moderate decreases in serum potassium concentrations. Metaproterenol is believed to produce a less pronounced decrease in arterial oxygen tension than isoproterenol.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Oral Route
    Description
    Although metaproterenol is well absorbed from the GI tract, the drug is extensively metabolized by the liver on first pass, resulting in an oral bioavailability of 40%. The onset of action is within 15 to 30 minutes following oral administration. The peak effects are seen within about 1 hour after oral administration. Effects can last up to 4 hours after a single dose given orally, but the duration of action can decrease with prolonged use secondary to tachyphylaxis.

    Inhalation Route
    Description
    The onset of action is within 1 minute after oral inhalation and 5 to 30 minutes after nebulization of metaproterenol. The peak effects are seen within about 1 hour after inhalation. Effects can last up to 4 hours after a single dose given by inhalation, but the duration of action can decrease with prolonged use secondary to tachyphylaxis.

Solution for nebulization
Do not use metaproterenol solutions that contain a precipitate or have a brown, pinkish, or darker than slightly yellow color.
Hand-bulb nebulizer: Administer desired dose using the undiluted 5% inhalation solution.
Intermittent positive pressure breathing (IPPB) apparatus or nebulizer: Dilute each dose of the 5% inhalation solution in 2.5 ml of 0.9% Sodium Chloride (NS). More dilute solutions (e.g., 0.4% of 0.6%) may be used without dilution. Each 0.4% vial (2.5 ml) is equal to 0.2 mL of the 5% solution (10 mg) which is diluted to 2.5 mL with 0.9% Sodium Chloride (NS).
