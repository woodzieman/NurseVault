Adverse Reactions
  Headline
  Adverse Reactions
  Adverse Reactions
    Headline
    blurred vision, headache, thrombosis
    General Information
    Administration of zopapogene imadenovec may increase the risk of thrombotic events, as adenoviral vector-based therapies have the potential to induce prothrombotic antibody development. Monitor patients for signs and symptoms of thrombosis, such as shortness of breath, chest pain, leg swelling, persistent abdominal pain, or neurological symptoms (including severe or persistent headaches or blurred vision). Treat thrombotic events according to clinical practice guidelines. During a clinical trial involving 38 patients who received treatment with zopapogene imadenovec, no cases of thrombosis were reported; however, 4 patients (11%) developed headache and 1 patient (3%) reported increased blurred vision.

Description
  Text
  Zopapogene imadenovec is a nonreplicating adenoviral vector-based immunotherapy indicated for the treatment of individuals with recurrent respiratory papillomatosis (RRP). It works by expressing a fusion antigen derived from selected regions of human papillomavirus (HPV) types 6 and 11 proteins, thereby generating an immune response targeted against HPV 6 and 11 infected cells. The most common adverse reactions include injection site reactions, occurring in most patients. Important precautions include monitoring for local injection site reactions for at least 30 minutes after the initial dose and for signs and symptoms of thrombotic events, as adenoviral vector-based therapies may induce prothrombotic antibody development. The drug is administered as 4 separate subcutaneous injections given over a 12-week interval. Surgical debulking of visible papillomas to establish minimal residual disease is recommended prior to the initial dose, and visible papillomas should be removed prior to the third and fourth doses to maintain minimal residual disease during treatment. Use universal biohazard precautions for handling and disposal. Zopapogene imadenovec was FDA-approved in August 2025.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Zopapogene imadenovec is a nonreplicating adenoviral vector-based immunotherapy indicated for the treatment of recurrent respiratory papillomatosis (RRP). Pharmacodynamics
In a clinical study, induction of HPV 6- and HPV 11-specific T cell responses was higher in RRP patients who demonstrated a clinical response to treatment (i.e., reduction in or elimination of the requirement for surgical debulking during the 12 months after completion of treatment). At 6 weeks post-treatment, the mean fold-change from baseline was 164.9 in responders vs. 5.1 in nonresponders (p less than 0.018). This difference persisted at 12 weeks post-treatment (mean fold-change 61.5 in responders vs. 11.5 in nonresponders).

      Subcutaneous Administration
      Description
      Preparation
Obtain the following recommended supplies and materials:
The single dose vial stored in a freezer at a temperature no warmer than -60 degrees C (-76 degrees F)
Water bath or dry bead bath set to 37 degrees C (98.6 degrees F)
3 mL sterile syringe
Sterile needle (18 to 22 gauge without a filter)
Sterile needle (23 to 25 gauge, 1/2 to 5/8 inch long)
70% isopropyl alcohol pads
Remove the carton containing the single dose vial from the freezer when ready for administration.
Immediately thaw by placing the vial (in an upright position) in a 37 degree C (98.6 degree F) water bath or dry bead bath until there are no visible ice crystals in the vial. Exposure of the thawed vial to the water bath or dry bead bath should be less than 5 minutes.
Once thawed, immediately wipe the vial with 70% isopropyl alcohol.
Swirl gently and visually inspect the vial and contents. The suspension should appear slightly opalescent to opalescent, colorless, and free of visible particulates. Do not use if particulates or discoloration are visible in the suspension.
Aseptically withdraw 1 mL of suspension from the thawed vial using a 3 mL syringe with an 18 to 22 gauge needle. DO NOT use a filter needle.
Replace the needle with a 23 to 25 gauge subcutaneous injection needle.
Discard the empty vial and used needle in a biohazard container.
Do not hold the product at room temperature for more than 60 minutes after thawing. Do not store the thawed vial or filled syringe in a refrigerator, freezer, or on dry ice. Protect from light and do not shake the vial or syringe.
 
Subcutaneous Injection
Appropriate injection sites include the lateral regions of the upper arm and thigh. Avoid areas of edema, potential infection, wounds, scars, or the site of a recent subcutaneous injection.
Clean the injection site thoroughly with an alcohol swab and allow at least 30 seconds for the site to dry.
Inject the dose subcutaneously.
Without massaging the injection site, clean again with an alcohol swab.
Place potentially contaminated materials from the injection site in a sealable bag and dispose of in regular trash. Follow these precautions for 1 to 2 weeks after the injection. Practice proper hand hygiene when coming into direct contact with patient body waste.
Avoid direct contact with the injection site (e.g., touching or scratching) and dressings for approximately 24 hours after treatment.
Monitor the patient for local injection site reactions for at least 30 minutes after the initial treatment. Additionally, monitor patients for signs and symptoms of thrombotic events. Treat these events according to clinical practice.
