Drug Class: Triprolidine is a first generation oral sedating antihistamine (H1-blocker) belonging to the alkylamine class.

Routes: oral; Common adult doses: 10 mg/day PO.

Common Side Effects (excluding routine GI upset): agitation, anxiety, confusion, dizziness, drowsiness, dyskinesia, hallucinations, headache, insomnia, irritability, lethargy, seizures, blurred vision, mydriasis, xerophthalmia, pyrosis (heartburn), dysuria, impotence (erectile dysfunction), polyuria, urinary retention, arrhythmia exacerbation, orthostatic hypotension, peripheral vasodilation, sinus tachycardia

In an Overdose: No overdose/antidote information is listed in this file.

Key Nursing Considerations: Contraindications/precautions: The anticholinergic activity of first-generation H1-antagonists may result in thickened bronchial secretions in the respiratory tract, thereby aggravating COPD. This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components.

Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  Contraindications And Precautions
    Headline
    Drug Interactions
    General Information
    The coadministration of certain medications may lead to harm and require avoidance or therapy modification; review all drug interactions prior to concomitant use of other medications.
    Headline
    chronic obstructive pulmonary disease
    General Information
    Use sedating antihistamines such as triprolidine with caution in people with chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), including emphysema or chronic bronchitis. The anticholinergic activity of first-generation H1-antagonists may result in thickened bronchial secretions in the respiratory tract, thereby aggravating COPD.
    Headline
    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components. Triprolidine is a first-generation antihistamine of the alkylamine class. Allergies to one antihistamine can sometimes lead to cross-reactivity with other chemically-related antihistamines.
    Headline
    glaucoma
    General Information
    Triprolidine should be used with caution in people with glaucoma, as an increase in intraocular pressure may occur from the anticholinergic actions of the medication. Older adults are more susceptible to the anticholinergic effects of triprolidine, including possible precipitation of undiagnosed glaucoma.
    Headline
    prostatic hypertrophy
    General Information
    Due to the anticholinergic effects which may aggravate urinary retention, first-generation antihistamines, such as triprolidine, should be used with caution in people with trouble urinating due to prostatic hypertrophy.
    Headline
    activities requiring coordination and concentration, driving or operating machinery
    General Information
    Triprolidine can cause drowsiness. Advise individuals receiving triprolidine to avoid driving or operating machinery and activities requiring coordination and concentration until the effects of the drug are known. Additive drowsiness may be seen during coadministration with other CNS depressants, including ethanol ingestion.
    Headline
    children, infants, neonates
    General Information
    Due to the risk for serious adverse reactions, the FDA recommends against administration of over the counter (OTC) cough and cold products to neonates, infants and children younger than 2 years of age. When administering OTC medications to older pediatric patients, they advise caregivers to read product labels carefully, use caution when administering multiple products to avoid duplication of ingredients, and use only measuring devices specifically designed for use with medications. Care teams should thoroughly assess the use of similar products, both prescription and nonprescription, to avoid duplication of therapy and the potential for inadvertent overdose.
    Headline
    geriatric
    General Information
    The Beers Criteria identifies first-generation antihistamines as potentially inappropriate medications (PIMs) for geriatric adults, recommending their avoidance due to their high anticholinergic properties, decreased clearance in older age, the development of tolerance when used as sleep aids, and an increased risk of anticholinergic effects and toxicity compared to younger individuals. These medications should particularly be avoided in patients with dementia or cognitive impairment (due to adverse CNS effects), those at high risk for delirium (which can worsen or trigger new-onset delirium), and men with lower urinary tract symptoms or benign prostatic hyperplasia (due to risks of urinary retention or hesitancy).
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    No well-controlled studies have assessed triprolidine use during pregnancy. A retrospective study examining the relationship between drug consumption and congenital abnormalities found that women who took antihistamines during the first trimester had fewer abnormalities than those in a control group. Two retrospective analyses of congential disorders in live infants born to people enrolled in the Group Health Cooperative of Puget Sound health plan and of the 628 women who ingested a combination of triprolidine and pseudoephedrine (Actifed) during the first trimeter, 9 infants (1.4%) had congenital disorders (types not listed). Baseline congenital disorder rates were 1.2% and 1.6% for study populations of 6,837 and 6,509 women whose pregnancies resulted in live births, respectively. Loratadine and oral cetirizine are acceptable antihistamine alternatives based on their excellent safety data and recommendation in multiple guidelines for use during pregnancy. Non-pharmacologic methods (e.g., fluids, rest) are recommended to be tried first for symptomatic relief of colds or allergies during pregnancy. Pregnant patients should see their health care professional for a proper diagnosis and for treatment recommendations.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Use triprolidine with caution during breast-feeding, as there are alternative choices with more data regarding use during lactation. Excretion of triprolidine in breast milk appears to be low and was studied among 3 lactating women following ingestion of a combination of triprolidine 2.5 mg/pseudoephedrine 60 mg tablet. Triprolidine was present in the breast milk of all 3 women at concentrations representing 0.06% to 0.2% of the ingested dose and no adverse effects were noted in the breastfed infants. There are published reports of drowsiness, disturbed sleeping patterns, hyperexcitability, excessive crying, and irritability have been reported in infants exposed to some antihistamines via breast milk. Antihistamines can lower basal prolactin secretion and may interfere with the establishment of lactation. Alternatives to triprolidine may be considered. Guidelines recommend loratadine or cetirizine at the lowest dose as preferred antihistamines in breast-feeding individuals because they are safer with only low levels found in breast milk.

    General Information
    Triprolidine has been associated with CNS depression manifested as drowsiness and mental impairment. A double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover trial conducted in 12 subjects demonstrated that these effects may occur as early as 1.5 hours following triprolidine administration. If dizziness, insomnia, or anxiety occur, triprolidine should be discontinued.

Description
  Text
  Triprolidine is a first generation oral sedating antihistamine (H1-blocker) belonging to the alkylamine class. It is used for the treatment of allergic and vasomotor rhinitis, allergic conjunctivitis, and uncomplicated skin manifestations of angioedema and urticaria. Triprolidine was approved by the FDA in 1958.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Triprolidine is a first generation H1-antagonist that competes with histamine for binding at H1-receptor sites to lessen symptoms caused by histamine release. H1-antagonists also produce anticholinergic effects through antagonism of neuronal and neuromuscular muscarinic receptors. First generation antihistamines do cross the blood-brain barrier to bind to receptors in the central nervous system.

Pharmacokinetics

    Description
    Triprolidine is rapidly absorbed following oral administration; however, absolute bioavailability is unknown. A dose of 0.04 mg/kg in 7 healthy adults (mean dose 2.7 +/- 0.4 mg) produced a mean peak serum level of 15.6 +/- 8.3 ng/ml in a mean time of 2 hours.
  Text
  Triprolidine is administered orally. Therapeutic concentrations typically last 4—8 hours. Triprolidine is excreted in breast milk and it is not known if the drug crosses the placenta. A molecular weight of 279 suggests that placental transfer could occur. Triprolidine undergoes hepatic metabolism and approximately 1% of a dose is excreted unchanged in urine. Serum half-life is between 3—3.3 hours.
