Drug Class: Midodrine, a prodrug, is an oral alpha1-agonist indicated for the treatment of symptomatic orthostatic hypotension.

Routes: oral; Common adult doses: 30 mg/day PO; while higher doses have been tolerated by some adults, the safety and efficacy of these higher doses have not been systematically studied or established.

Common Side Effects (excluding routine GI upset): bradycardia, hypertension, supine hypertension, chills, dysuria, gooseflesh, hyperesthesia, increased urinary frequency, paresthesias, pruritus, urinary retention, urinary urgency, anxiety, asthenia, back pain, confusion, dizziness, drowsiness, erythema multiforme, flushing, headache, insomnia, pyrosis (heartburn), rash (unspecified), visual impairment, xerosis

In an Overdose: No overdose/antidote information is listed in this file.

Key Nursing Considerations: Contraindications/precautions: Midodrine should not be administered to patients treated with MAOI therapy (see Drug Interactions). Midodrine can cause marked elevation of supine blood pressure.

Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  General Information
  Midodrine should not be administered to patients treated with MAOI therapy (see Drug Interactions).
 
Midodrine can cause marked elevation of supine blood pressure. Due to the effect of midodrine on the cardiovascular system, use of midodrine in patients with severe organic cardiac disease or persistent and excessive supine hypertension is contraindicated. Midodrine should be used with caution in patients with heart failure. Due to its sympathomimetic effects, midodrine should be used with caution in patients with hyperthyroidism (thyroid disease). Midodrine is absolutely contraindicated in patients with pheochromocytoma or thyrotoxicosis because of the potential to exacerbate cardiotoxicity in these disease states. Midodrine should only be used when necessary, with the expected benefits outweighing the risks. The indication for use of midodrine in the treatment of symptomatic orthostatic hypotension is based primarily on a change in the surrogate marker of effectiveness, an increase in systolic blood pressure measured one minute after standing, a surrogate marker considered likely to correspond to a clinical benefit. However, the clinical benefits of midodrine impacting quality of life (e.g., improved ability to carry out activities of daily living) have not been verified.
 
Because the active metabolite of midodrine, desglymidodrine, is excreted primarily in the urine, midodrine is contraindicated in patients with acute renal disease, acute renal failure, or urinary retention. Although not systematically studied in patients with renal dysfunction, midodrine should be used with caution at lower dosage in patients with renal impairment. The active metabolite of midodrine is removed by hemodialysis (dialysis).
 
Midodrine has not been studied in patients with hepatic disease. Midodrine is partially metabolized by the liver; use with caution in patients with hepatic disease.
 
Midodrine is classified in FDA pregnancy risk category C. There are no adequate and well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Use midodrine during pregnancy only if the potential benefits to the mother outweigh the potential risk to the fetus. No teratogenicity has been demonstrated during animal studies.
 
It is unknown if midodrine is excreted in human breast milk. Because many drugs are excreted in breast milk, use of midodrine in breast-feeding mothers should be undertaken with caution.
 
The safety and efficacy of midodrine in children have not been established.
 
Midodrine may be used concomitantly with fludrocortisone. Fludrocortisone is known to cause an increase in intraocular pressure and glaucoma. Use this combination cautiously in patients with orthostatic hypotension who also have diabetes mellitus or a history of visual disturbance.

    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components.
    Headline
    cardiac disease, pheochromocytoma, supine hypertension
    General Information
    Due to the effect of midodrine on the cardiovascular system, use of midodrine is contraindicated in individuals with severe organic cardiac disease, pheochromocytoma, or persistent and excessive supine hypertension. Midodrine can cause marked elevation of supine blood pressure. The use of midodrine is not recommended in individuals with initial supine systolic pressure more than 180 mmHg as these individuals were excluded from the clinical trials. Supine hypertension can be controlled by preventing the individual from becoming fully supine (i.e., sleeping with the head of the bed elevated). Elevations in sitting blood pressure may also occur with midodrine therapy. Regularly monitor supine and sitting blood pressures during midodrine therapy, particularly when coadministered with other medications that cause vasoconstriction. Discontinue midodrine if supine hypertension persists or there is an excessive increase in supine blood pressure.
    Headline
    renal failure, renal impairment, urinary retention
    General Information
    Midodrine is contraindicated in acute renal disease (i.e., acute renal impairment, acute renal failure) and urinary retention. Since desglymidodrine, the active metabolite of midodrine, is primarily excreted in the urine, elevated concentrations are expected in individuals with renal impairment; thus, a lower initial dose of midodrine is recommended. Assess renal function prior to starting midodrine therapy. Use midodrine with caution in individuals with urinary retention problems, as desglymidodrine may cause urinary retention through stimulation of alpha-receptors on the bladder neck.
    Headline
    hepatic failure
    General Information
    Midodrine is partially metabolized by the liver; use with caution in individuals with hepatic failure or impairment.
    Headline
    diabetes mellitus, visual disturbance
    General Information
    Use midodrine with caution in individuals with orthostatic hypotension who also have diabetes mellitus or a history of visual disturbance who are also taking fludrocortisone acetate. Fludrocortisone is known to cause an increase in intraocular pressure and glaucoma.
    Headline
    hyperthyroidism
    General Information
    Midodrine is contraindicated in individuals with hyperthyroidism (i.e., thyrotoxicosis).
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    There are no adequate and well-controlled studies on the use of midodrine during human pregnancy. In animal studies, an increased rate of embryo resorption, reduced fetal body weight (rats and rabbits), and decreased fetal survival (rabbits) were observed after administration of midodrine at doses that were 13 (rats) and 7 (rabbits) times the maximum recommended human dose based on body surface area. No teratogenicity was observed. Use midodrine during pregnancy only if the potential benefits outweigh the potential fetal risks.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Use midodrine with caution during breast-feeding. There are no data on the presence of midodrine in human milk. Consider the benefits of breast-feeding, the patient's clinical need for treatment, and any potential adverse effects on the breastfed child from the medication or from the patient's underlying medical condition.

Description
  Text
  Midodrine, a prodrug, is an oral alpha1-agonist indicated for the treatment of symptomatic orthostatic hypotension. It is chemically related to the older drug methoxamine. Midodrine was granted accelerated approval status based on increases in 1-minute standing systolic blood pressure (SBP) in patients with orthostatic hypotension, regardless of the etiology. Clinical benefits of midodrine (e.g., improved ability to perform life activities) have not been established, however, standing SBP is considered a surrogate marker which is likely to correspond to a clinical benefit. In one study of patients with autonomic insufficiency, efficacy of midodrine was superior to that of ephedrine; in another study, midodrine caused less side effects than use of fludrocortisone. Midodrine is one of the few medications officially approved in the treatment of orthostatic hypotension, including neurogenic orthostatic hypotension, such as may occur in patients with diabetic cardiovascular autonomic neuropathy and other conditions.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Midodrine is a prodrug that is enzymatically hydrolyzed to the pharmacologically active metabolite, desglymidodrine. Desglymidodrine is a selective agonist at peripheral alpha1-receptors. By binding to alpha1-receptors on arteries and veins, desglymidodrine produces an increase in vascular tone and elevates blood pressure. Midodrine is devoid of any beta-adrenergic activity and there is no stimulation of norepinephrine release. No central mechanism of action exists because both midodrine and desglymidodrine diffuse poorly across the blood-brain barrier. The clinical effects of midodrine include increased supine and standing blood pressure, decreased venous capacity secondary to increased venous tone, increased peripheral vascular resistance, and decreased supine and standing heart rate.

    Midodrine is rapidly and almost completely absorbed after oral administration. Following absorption, midodrine is deglycinated to form the active metabolite desglymidodrine. The distribution of midodrine in humans is unknown. Protein binding of either agent is insignificant. Deglycination of midodrine appears to take place in many tissues and both midodrine and desglymidodrine are partly metabolized by the liver. Neither midodrine nor desglymidodrine is metabolized by monoamine oxidase. No other metabolites of midodrine have been identified, however, only 2—4% of a single oral dose is excreted as unchanged drug and 30—40% as desglymidodrine over 24 hours. The renal excretion of midodrine is insignificant while approximately 80% of desglymidodrine is excreted in the urine by active renal secretion. The exact mechanism of renal excretion is not known, but is proposed to be by the base-secreting pathway responsible for secretion of other basic drugs. Elimination half-life is about 25 minutes for the prodrug midodrine and roughly 3—4 hours for the active metabolite desglymidodrine.

    May be administered without regard to meals. Food does not affect bioavailability. Doses are often given around daytime and evening meals to accomplish the usual every 4-hour dose schedule.
To reduce the potential for supine hypertension while sleeping, do not administer doses after the evening meal or within 3 to 4 hours of bedtime.
Supine and standing blood pressure should be monitored regularly during treatment.

Dosage Adjustment Guidelines
  Hepatic Impairment
  Specific guidelines for dosage adjustments in hepatic impairment are not available as midodrine has not been studied in patients with hepatic disease. Midodrine is partially metabolized by the liver and caution is recommended with use in patients with hepatic disease.
  Renal Impairment
  The manufacturer recommends initiating treatment with 2.5 mg/dose (given 3 times daily) in patients with renal impairment because the active metabolite, desglymidodrine, is excreted renally. Titrate cautiously within the usual dosage range.
 
Intermittent hemodialysis
The active metabolite of midodrine, desglymidodrine, is removed by dialysis. A maintenance dosage of 5 mg PO 3 times daily has been recommended for patients receiving hemodialysis.
