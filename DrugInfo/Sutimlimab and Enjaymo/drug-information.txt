Indication Specific Dosing
  General Information
  General Dosing Information
Vaccinate individuals against encapsulated bacteria, including S. pneumoniae and N. meningitidis (serogroups A, C, W, Y, and B), at least 2 weeks prior to initiation of sutimlimab and according to the most current Advisory Committee on Immunization Practices (ACIP) recommendations.
If urgent sutimlimab treatment is indicated in individuals not up to date with vaccines for S. pneumoniae and N. meningitidis according to ACIP recommendations, administer these vaccines as soon as possible.
If treatment is interrupted, closely monitor for signs and symptoms of recurrent hemolysis such as elevated concentrations of total bilirubin or lactate dehydrogenase along with a decrease in hemoglobin, or reappearance of symptoms such as fatigue, dyspnea, palpitations, or hemoglobinuria. Consider restarting sutimlimab if these symptoms occur after drug discontinuation.

    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    There are no available data on sutimlimab use during human pregnancy to evaluate for a drug-associated risk of major birth defects, miscarriage, or adverse maternal or fetal outcomes. Human immunoglobulin G (IgG) antibodies are known to cross the placental barrier and, therefore, sutimlimab may be transferred from the mother to the developing fetus. In animal reproduction studies, administration of sutimlimab to pregnant monkeys during organogenesis at doses 2- to 3-times the maximum recommended human dose (MRHD) did not result in adverse effects on pregnancy or offspring development.
    Headline
    systemic infection
    General Information
    Sutimlimab increases susceptibility to serious infections, including those caused by encapsulated bacteria, such as N. meningitidis (any serogroup, including non-groupable strains), S. pneumoniae, and H. influenzae type B. Life-threatening and fatal meningococcal infections have occurred in both vaccinated and unvaccinated individuals treated with complement inhibitors. Complete or update vaccination for encapsulated bacteria at least 2 weeks prior to administration of the first dose of sutimlimab, according to the most current Advisory Committee on Immunization Practices (ACIP) recommendations for individuals receiving a complement inhibitor. Revaccinate considering the chronic duration of therapy with sutimlimab. Note that ACIP recommends an administration schedule for individuals receiving complement inhibitors that differs from the administration schedule in the vaccine prescribing information. If urgent sutimlimab treatment is required in individuals who are not up to date with vaccines, administer vaccine(s) as soon as possible. Vaccination does not eliminate the risk for infections due to these organisms, despite the development of antibodies after vaccination. Closely monitor for early signs and symptoms of serious infection and evaluate immediately if an infection is suspected. If sutimlimab is administered to individuals with active systemic infection, monitor closely for signs and symptoms of worsening infection. Some infections may become rapidly life-threatening or fatal if not recognized and treated promptly. Instruct individuals to seek immediate medical care if these signs and symptoms occur. Promptly treat known infections. Consider interruption of sutimlimab treatment in individuals receiving treatment for a serious infection. Consider the individual's immune status when initiating treatment. Sutimlimab has not been studied in individuals with chronic systemic infections such as hepatitis B, hepatitis C, or HIV.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    It is uncertain whether sutimlimab is safe for use during breast-feeding. Consider the developmental and health benefits of breast-feeding along with the mother's clinical need for sutimlimab and the potential adverse effects on the breast-fed child from sutimlimab or the underlying maternal condition. There are no data on the presence of sutimlimab in human milk, effects on the breast-fed child, or the effects on milk production. Maternal immunoglobulin G (IgG) is known to be present in human milk. The effects of local gastrointestinal exposure and limited systemic exposure in the breast-fed child to sutimlimab are unknown.
    Headline
    autoimmune disease
    General Information
    Individuals with autoimmune disease experienced relapse or worsening of preexisting autoimmune disease with sutimlimab in clinical trials. Monitor individuals treated with sutimlimab for signs and symptoms of autoimmune disease and manage medically. Sutimlimab may potentially increase the risk for developing autoimmune diseases such as systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE), based on its mechanism of action. Development of SLE has been associated with inherited classical complement deficiency. Subjects with SLE or autoimmune disease with positive anti-nuclear antibody were excluded from sutimlimab clinical trials.

  Adverse Reactions
    Headline
    cystitis, fever, infection, pharyngitis, sepsis
    General Information
    Sutimlimab use increases susceptibility to serious infections, including encapsulated bacteria infection (e.g., N. meningitidis (any serogroup, including non-groupable strains), S. pneumoniae, and H. influenzae). Ensure persons are appropriately vaccinated. Closely monitor for early signs and symptoms of serious infection and evaluate immediately if infection is suspected. Instruct persons to seek immediate medical care if these signs and symptoms occur. Promptly treat known infections. Serious infection may become rapidly life-threatening or fatal if not recognized and treated early. Consider interruption of sutimlimab treatment in persons receiving treatment for a serious infection. If sutimlimab is administered to persons with active systemic infections, monitor closely for signs and symptoms of worsening infection. Serious infections were reported in 15% of subjects receiving sutimlimab in clinical trials. These infections included urinary tract infection with sepsis, respiratory tract infection, pneumonia, otomastoiditis, and skin infections. One subject (1.5%) died due to klebsiella pneumonia. Specific types of infections reported with sutimlimab during clinical trials included urinary tract infection (38%), respiratory tract infection (25%), bacterial infection (25%), naso-pharyngitis (21%), and viral infections (21%). The categories of infections were defined as follows: urinary tract infections (cystitis and urosepsis); respiratory tract infections (upper and lower respiratory tract infection, bronchitis, and COVID-19 pneumonia); bacterial infections (Escherichia urinary tract infection, bacterial urinary tract infection, bacterial cystitis, Escherichia sepsis, pneumococcal sepsis, streptococcal sepsis, klebsiella pneumonia, and staphylococcal wound infection); viral infections (oral herpes, herpes zoster, viral respiratory tract infection, and herpes simplex viremia). Fever was reported in 21% of subjects treated with sutimlimab in clinical trials.

Description
  Text
  Sutimlimab is an intravenous classical complement inhibitor indicated for the treatment of hemolysis in adults with cold agglutinin disease (CAD). Sutimlimab increases the risk of serious infections, including encapsulated bacteria infections caused by N. meningitidis (any serogroup), S. pneumoniae, and H. influenzae. Life-threatening and fatal infections with encapsulated bacteria have occurred in both vaccinated and unvaccinated individuals treated with complement inhibitors; vaccination against encapsulated bacteria is recommended to be completed at least 2 weeks prior to starting sutimlimab to reduce the risk of serious infections. Sutimlimab was FDA-approved in February 2022.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Sutimlimab is an immunoglobulin G subclass 4 (IgG4) monoclonal antibody that inhibits hemolysis in patients with cold agglutinin disease by preventing complement opsonin deposition on the surface of red blood cells. This occurs via inhibition of the classical complement pathway at complement protein component 1, s subcomponent (C1s), a serine protease that cleaves C4.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Intravenous Route
    Description
    More than 90% inhibition of the complement pathway was observed after a single sutimlimab infusion when serum sutimlimab concentrations were 100 mcg/mL or more. C4 concentrations returned to normal (0.2 g/L) within 1 week after the first dose. The exposure of sutimlimab increases proportionally over a dosage range of 60 to 100 mg/kg (0.3- to 1.5-times the maximum approved dosage). Steady-state is achieved by week 7 of treatment with an accumulation ration of less than 2.

  Text
  Sutimlimab is administered intravenously. It binds to complement protein component 1, s subcomponent (C1s) in the serum. Vd at steady state is approximately 5.8 L. Antibodies such as sutimlimab are metabolized by degradation into small peptides and individual amino acids. The terminal elimination half-life is 21 days with a clearance of approximately 0.14 L/day at the recommended dosage. Terminal elimination half-life and clearance vary at different doses because of target-mediated drug disposition at lower drug concentrations.

Preparation of Undiluted Sutimlimab
Remove vials from the refrigerator.
To minimize foaming, do not shake sutimlimab.
Withdraw the calculated volume of sutimlimab from the appropriate number of vials and add to an empty infusion bag.
Storage: If not used immediately, store infusion solution under refrigeration at 2 to 8 degrees C (36 to 46 degrees F). Once removed from refrigeration, administer within 8 hours. Do not exceed a total time of 36 hours from time of preparation to infusion completion; this includes refrigeration, adjustment to room temperature, and infusion time.
 
Preparation of Diluted Sutimlimab
Remove vials from the refrigerator.
To minimize foaming, do not shake sutimlimab.
Withdraw the calculated volume of sutimlimab from the appropriate number of vials.
Dilute the calculated volume with 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection to a total volume of 500 mL.
Storage: If not used immediately, store infusion solution under refrigeration at 2 to 8 degrees C (36 to 46 degrees F). Once removed from refrigeration, administer within 8 hours. Do not exceed a total time of 36 hours from time of preparation to infusion completion; this includes refrigeration, adjustment to room temperature, and infusion time.
 
IV Infusion (Undiluted or Diluted Sutimlimab)
If prepared solution has been refrigerated, allow it to adjust to room temperature (15 to 25 degrees C [59 to 77 degrees F]) prior to infusion. In-line infusion warmers may be used; do not exceed a temperature of 40 degrees C (104 degrees F).
Administer through a 0.2 micron in-line filter with a polyethersulfone (PES) membrane.
Prime the infusion tubing with the solution immediately before infusion.
Administer over 1 to 2 hours depending on the patient's body weight and cardiopulmonary status:
For undiluted solutions:
Weight 75 kg or more without cardiopulmonary disease: 150 mL/hour
Weight 75 kg or more with cardiopulmonary disease: 75 mL/hour
Weight 39 to 74 kg without cardiopulmonary disease: 130 mL/hour
Weight 39 to 74 kg with cardiopulmonary disease: 65 mL/hour
For diluted solutions:
Weight 70 kg or more without cardiopulmonary disease: 500 mL/hour
Weight 70 kg or more with cardiopulmonary disease: 250 mL/hour
Weight 39 to 69 kg: 250 mL/hour
Slow or stop the infusion if an infusion reaction occurs.
Flush the tubing immediately after the infusion with a sufficient quantity (approximately 20 mL) of 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection.
Monitor the patient for hypersensitivity and/or infusion-related reactions for at least 2 hours after completion of the initial infusion and for 1 hour after completion of subsequent infusions.
