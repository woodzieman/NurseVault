        6 mg subcutaneously once per chemotherapy cycle, at least 24 hours after administration of cytotoxic chemotherapy. Pegfilgrastim should not be administered within 14 days prior to the next cycle of cytotoxic chemotherapy.   Febrile neutropenia (defined as a body temperature of 38.2 degrees C or higher and an absolute neutrophil count (ANC) less than 500 cells/mm3 on the same day of the fever or the day after) occurred in significantly fewer breast cancer patients who received a single subcutaneous injection of pegfilgrastim 6 mg (n = 463) compared with placebo (n = 465) starting on day 2 of the first cycle of docetaxel 100 mg/m2 IV every 3 weeks (1% vs. 17%). Additionally, the incidence of febrile neutropenia-related hospitalization (1% vs. 14%) and IV anti-infective agent use (2% vs. 10%) was significantly lower with pegfilgrastim compared with placebo in this multicenter, double-blind, randomized trial. Treatment with a single subcutaneous dose of pegfilgrastim 6 mg followed by daily placebo injections (n = 75) was noninferior to subcutaneous filgrastim 5 mcg/kg/day (n = 77) in patients with breast cancer who received doxorubicin 60 mg/m2 and docetaxel 75 mg/m2 IV on day 1 repeated every 3 weeks in a multicenter, double-blind, randomized study. Treatment began starting on day 2 or approximately 24 hours after chemotherapy and continued until the ANC was 10,000 cells/mm3 or greater. The mean duration of grade 4 neutropenia in cycle 1 was 1.8 days in the pegfilgrastim arm and 1.6 days in the filgrastim arm. In a multicenter, randomized, open-label clinical trial of pediatric and young adult patients with sarcoma (age 21 or younger), the recovery of neutrophil counts following myelosuppressive chemotherapy was similar in patients treated with pegfilgrastim 100 mcg/kg (n = 37) or filgrastim 5 mcg/kg/day subcutaneously (n = 6). The American Society of Clinical Oncology clinical guidelines recommend the use of colony-stimulating factors (CSFs) for primary prophylaxis in patients who have a febrile neutropenia risk of 20% or more based on patient-, disease-, and treatment-related factors, including patients receiving a dose-dense chemotherapy regimen with the intention of a cure.

Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  General Information
  The safe and effective use of filgrastim concurrently with cytotoxic chemotherapy has not been established. Rapidly dividing myeloid cells are potentially more sensitive to cytotoxic drugs. Pegfilgrastim should not be used for 14 days before or 24 hours after administration of cytotoxic chemotherapy. Concurrent use of pegfilgrastim with radiation therapy, mitomycin C, antimetabolites (e.g., 5-fluorouracil or cytosine arabinoside), or chemotherapeutic agents that have a delayed myelosuppressive effect (e.g., nitrosoureas), has not been evaluated and should be avoided.Pegfilgrastim has not been evaluated for peripheral blood stem cell (PBSC) mobilization and should not be used in this setting. The administration of filgrastim, the parent compound of pegfilgrastim, for PBSC mobilization has been associated with rare cases of splenic rupture, some of which were fatal. Patients receiving pegfilgrastim who report left upper abdominal pain or shoulder tip pain should be evaluated for an enlarged spleen or splenic rupture (see Adverse Reactions).Adult respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS) has been reported in neutropenic patients with sepsis receiving filgrastim, the parent compound of pegfilgrastim, and is postulated to be secondary to an influx of neutrophils at sites of inflammation in the lungs. Neutropenic patients who develop fever, pulmonary infiltrates, or respiratory insufficiency should be evaluated for the possibility of ARDS. In the event ARDS occurs, pegfilgrastim therapy should be discontinued and/or withheld until resolution of ARDS.Severe sickle cell crises have been reported in patients with sickle cell disease (specifically homozygous sickle cell anemia, sickle/hemoglobin C disease, and sickle/beta+ thalassemia) who receive filgrastim, the parent compound of pegfilgrastim, for peripheral stem cell mobilization or following chemotherapy. Some cases were fatal. Severe sickle cell crises have also been associated with pegfilgrastim use in patients with sickle cell disease. Pegfilgrastim should be used with caution in patients with sickle cell disease and only after careful consideration of the potential risks and benefits. Sickle cell patients who have received pegfilgrastim should be kept well hydrated and monitored for the occurrence of sickle cell crisis.Pegfilgrastim is classified as FDA pregnancy risk category C. Although no adequate human studies have been conducted on the effects of this drug upon the fetus, animal reproduction studies have shown adverse fetal effects. Therefore, in making the decision to administer this drug during pregnancy, the potential risks to the fetus must be weighed against the potential benefits to the mother.It is unknown whether pegfilgrastim is excreted into breast milk. Because many drugs and proteins are excreted in breast milk, caution is recommended if pegfilgrastim is administered to a woman who is breast-feeding.Because pegfilgrastim (Neulasta®) is only available in a fixed dose preparation, this formulation should not be used in infants, children, or small adolescents weighing < 45 kg.The safety and efficacy of pegfilgrastim in the treatment of neutropenia due to myeloid hematological diseases (e.g., myelodysplastic syndrome, acute or chronic myeloid leukemia) have not been established. The possibility that filgrastim, the parent compound of pegfilgrastim, can act as a growth factor for any type of neoplastic disease, including T-cell, lung, head and neck, and bladder tumor cell lines, cannot be excluded. However, in studies of patients receiving filgrastim for acute myeloid leukemia remission induction, there was no significant difference in remission rate, disease-free survival, or overall survival as compared to placebo.Pegfilgrastim is contraindicated for use in patients who have E. coli protein hypersensitivity. Patients who may have reacted to E. coli asparaginase should not receive pegfilgrastim. Although hypersensitivity reactions have not been reported with pegfilgrastim, allergic-type reactions including anaphylactoid reactions, skin rash, and urticaria occurring on initial or subsequent treatment have been reported with the parent compound of pegfilgrastim (i.e., filgrastim).Increased hematopoietic activity of the bone marrow in response to growth factor therapy (e.g., pegfilgrastim) has been associated with transient positive bone imaging changes. This should be considered when interpreting bone-imaging results.

    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components.     Headline
    sickle cell disease
    General Information
    Use pegfilgrastim with caution in patients with sickle cell disease or disorder because severe sickle cell crises have been reported in this patient population; some cases were fatal. Discontinue pegfilgrastim if sickle cell crisis occurs.
    Headline
    activities requiring coordination and concentration, driving or operating machinery
    General Information
    Patients should avoid driving or operating machinery or performing activities requiring coordination and concentration during hours 26 through 29 following the application of an on-body injector (OBI). This time frame includes the dose delivery period and 1 hour after. Patients should have a caregiver nearby for the first dose.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Available human data on pegfilgrastim use during pregnancy are limited and do not establish a clear risk of major birth defects, miscarriage, or adverse outcomes. Retrospective studies suggest pegfilgrastim exposure during pregnancy does not significantly affect fetal outcomes or cause neutropenia. However, preterm deliveries have been reported in some patients. Published studies in pregnant people exposed to filgrastim products have not shown an association with harmful pregnancy outcomes. In animal studies, no reproductive or developmental toxicity was observed in the offspring of pregnant rats given cumulative doses about 10 times the human dose based on body surface area. In pregnant rabbits, increased embryo-lethality and spontaneous abortions occurred at 4 times the maximum recommended human dose, accompanied by maternal toxicity. Dosing during organogenesis resulted in decreased maternal food intake, weight loss, lower fetal weights, and delayed skull ossification, but no structural anomalies were observed. Higher doses (approximately 4 times the human dose) led to more than half of the pregnancies ending in spontaneous abortion, which was not seen at the recommended dose. In pregnant rats, doses up to 10 times the human dose during various gestation periods showed no fetal loss or malformations.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Use pegfilgrastim with caution during breast-feeding. There are no data on the presence of pegfilgrastim in human milk, its effects on the breast-fed child, or its effects on milk production. Other filgrastim products are poorly secreted into human milk. Filgrastim products are also not orally absorbed, further limiting exposure to the breast-fed child. Consider the benefits of breast-feeding, the patient's clinical need for treatment, and any potential adverse effects on the breast-fed child from the medication or from the patient's underlying medical condition.

Adverse Reactions
  Headline
  Adverse Reactions
  General Information
  The most frequent adverse effect reported during pegfilgrastim therapy is deep, throbbing medullary bone pain. Of 467 adult patients who received pegfilgrastim, 31% reported bone pain, 21% reported myalgia, and 16% reported arthralgia. In a trial of 37 pediatric patients with sarcoma, bone pain was also the most common adverse reaction. The pain occurs most often in the lower back (back pain), pelvis, ribs, sternum, or long bones (i.e., sites containing bone marrow). Sternal pain can imitate the chest pain (unspecified) of an acute myocardial infarction. Bone pain is usually mild to moderate in severity and can be controlled in most patients with non-opiate analgesics. Infrequently, bone pain is severe enough to require opiate analgesics.
 
Reversible elevations in LDH, alkaline phosphatase, and uric acid (hyperuricemia), which did not require treatment, were observed. The incidence of these laboratory abnormalities with pegfilgrastim was less than that seen with filgrastim during clinical trials. In clinical studies, leukocytosis (WBC counts > 100,000 cells/mm3) was observed in < 1% of 932 pegfilgrastim-treated patients in clinical studies. White blood cell counts above 100,000 cells/mm3 represent a medical emergency because of the risk of serious adverse effects such as brain infarction, respiratory failure, intracranial hemorrhage, retinal hemorrhage, myocardial infarction, and acute limb ischemia. As leukocytosis may be caused by immature precursors (blasts) or expansion of mature leukocyte types (granulocytes, lymphocytes, monocytes), a WBC differential and peripheral blood smear will help determine the etiology of the WBC excess.
 
Allergic reactions including anaphylactic shock, angioedema, anaphylactoid reactions, rash (unspecified), and urticaria have been associated with pegfilgrastim. Most reactions occurred after the initial drug exposure. In rare cases, symptoms including anaphylactic reactions reappeared within days after treatment cessation for the initial event. In some cases, allergic symptoms recurred upon reexposure. Permanently discontinue pegfilgrastim usage for patients who have a serious allergic reaction.
 
Acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS), postulated to be secondary to an influx of neutrophils to inflammation sites in the lungs, has been reported in patients with neutropenia and sepsis taking filgrastim. Evaluation for ARDS is recommended for patients who take pegfilgrastim and have neutropenia and fever, lung infiltrates, or respiratory distress (dyspnea). If ARDS occurs, discontinue pegfilgrastim and withhold the drug until ARDS resolution.
 
Rare reports of splenic rupture after pegfilgrastim administration have been received. Splenic rupture has been associated with the parent compound, filgrastim; some cases were fatal. Evaluate patients who present with left upper abdominal pain and/or shoulder tip pain for splenomegaly or splenic rupture.
 
Binding antibodies to pegfilgrastim were detected using a BIAcore assay that had an approximate limit of detection of 500 ng/ml. Specifically, pre-existing binding antibodies were detected in 51 of 849 patients with metastatic breast cancer. Also, 4 of 521 patients who were negative for binding antibodies at baseline developed binding antibodies to pegfilgrastim after treatment. None of these 4 patients had evidence of neutralizing antibodies. The clinical significance of antibody formation against pegfilgrastim is unknown. Theoretically, an antibody directed against pegfilgrastim may cross-react with endogenous G-CSF and cause immune-mediated neutropenia. Cytopenias resulting from a neutralizing antibody response to exogenous growth factors have been reported on rare occasions in patients treated with other recombinant growth factors.

Description
  Text
  Pegfilgrastim is a leukocyte growth factor that binds to cell surface receptors on hematopoietic cells stimulating proliferation, differentiation, commitment, and end cell functional activation. It is indicated to increase survival in patients acutely exposed to myelosuppressive doses of radiation; efficacy for this indication was based on animal data in monkeys. Pegfilgrastim is indicated to decrease the incidence of infection, as manifested by febrile neutropenia, in patients with nonmyeloid malignancies receiving myelosuppressive anticancer drugs associated with a clinically significant incidence of febrile neutropenia. Pegfilgrastim is not indicated for the mobilization of peripheral blood progenitor cells for hematopoietic stem-cell transplantation. Acute respiratory distress syndrome, fatal splenic rupture, and glomerulonephritis have occurred with pegfilgrastim products.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Granulocyte colony-stimulating factor (G-CSF) is a glycoprotein involved in the regulation and production of neutrophils in response to host defense needs. Filgrastim, and thus pegfilgrastim, has the same biologic activity as native G-CSF. Pegfilgrastim is a covalent conjugate of recombinant human G-CSF and a polyethylene glycol. Pegfilgrastim acts on hematopoietic cells by binding to specific cell surface receptors, thereby stimulating proliferation, differentiation, commitment, and end cell functional activation.

    Description
    The mean Cmax was 123 +/- 113 nanograms (ng)/mL, the median Tmax was 24 hours (range, 16 to 120 hours), and the mean AUCinf value was 10,000 +/- 11,600 ng x hour/mL following the subcutaneous administration of pegfilgrastim 6 mg given no sooner than 24 hours after the completion of chemotherapy (for 4 cycles) in 73 patients with high-risk stage II or stage III/IV breast cancer. Based on pharmacokinetic data, the AUC of pegfilgrastim 300 mcg/kg in irradiated nonhuman primates is greater than the AUC in humans who received a single dose of pegfilgrastim 6 mg. Based on population modeling and simulation in adults, two 6 mg doses of pegfilgrastim, administered one week apart, produced a clinically relevant effect on the duration of grade 3 and 4 neutropenia; animal data and clinical data in humans suggest a correlation between pegfilgrastim exposure and the duration of severe neutropenia as a predictor of efficacy.

  Route Specific Administrations
    Administration Details
      Name
      Subcutaneous Administration
      Description
      Pegfilgrastim is available as a single-dose prefilled syringe for manual use, a single-dose vial (Neulasta), a kit containing an on-body injector (OBI) with a single-dose prefilled syringe (Neulasta, Udenyca), and a single-dose prefilled autoinjector (Udenyca).
Read and become familiar with the manufacturer's "Instructions for Use" prior to preparing or injecting the prefilled syringe or autoinjector or prior to preparing and applying the OBI.
Do not use the single-use prefilled syringe intended for manual injection with the OBI or the patient will receive less than the recommended dose; additionally, do not fill the OBI with any other drug.
Do not use the single-use prefilled syringe contained in the OBI kit as a manual injection or the patient will receive more than the recommended dose.
Do not administer pegfilgrastim between 14 days before and 24 hours after administration of cytotoxic chemotherapy.
Manual Use
Allow the prefilled syringe to reach room temperature (for a minimum of 30 minutes; or a minimum of 15 to 30 minutes for pegfilgrastim-bmez) before administering; refer to the manufacturer's package insert for recommendations on how long syringes may be kept at room temperature before they need to be discarded.
Do not freeze the pegfilgrastim prefilled syringe.
If the pegfilgrastim prefilled syringe is frozen, allow the syringe to thaw in the refrigerator prior to injection; do not use the syringe if it has been frozen more than once.
Subcutaneous injection using prefilled syringe for manual use:
Do not activate the needle guard prior to injection; pull the needle cover straight off.
Do not directly administer doses less than 6 mg (0.6 mL) from the prefilled syringe due to the potential for dosing errors. The prefilled syringe does not have graduation marks necessary for the accurate measurement of doses.
Inject pegfilgrastim in a recommended injection site (i.e., the outer area of the upper arm, the abdomen excluding the 2-inch area around the navel, the front of the middle thigh, and the upper outer areas of the buttocks).
 
Single-Dose Vial
Use the single-dose vial for pediatric patients weighing less than 45 kg.
Allow the vial to reach room temperature (for a minimum of 30 minutes) before administering; discard vials kept at room temperature longer than 48 hours.
Do not freeze the pegfilgrastim single-dose vial.
Subcutaneous injection using single-dose vials:
Withdraw the required volume from the 4 mg/0.4 mL vial. Discard unused portion.
Administer subcutaneously.
 
Prefilled Autoinjector
Do not use the prefilled autoinjector in pediatric patients weighing less than 45 kg; the autoinjector delivers the entire 6-mg dose and is not adjustable.
Allow the prefilled syringe to reach room temperature (for a minimum of 30 minutes) before administering; discard syringes kept at room temperature longer than 48 hours.
Do not freeze the pegfilgrastim prefilled syringe.
If the pegfilgrastim prefilled syringe is frozen, allow the syringe to thaw in the refrigerator prior to injection; do not use the syringe if it has been frozen more than once.
Subcutaneous injection using prefilled autoinjector:
Do not remove the cap from the prefilled autoinjector until you are ready to inject.
Inject pegfilgrastim in a recommended injection site (i.e., the back of the upper arm, the abdomen excluding the 2-inch area around the navel, the front of the middle thigh, and the upper outer areas of the buttocks).
 
On-Body Injector (OBI)
Apply the OBI on the patient after chemotherapy completes; report problems to Amgen at 1-800-772-6436 for Neulasta Onpro or Coherus at 1-800-483-3692 for Udenyca Onbody.
The OBI is not recommended for use in patients acutely exposed to radiation; additionally, the OBI has not been studied in pediatric patients.
Do not expose the OBI to medical imaging studies (e.g., X-ray scan, MRI, CT scan and ultrasound), radiation treatment, and oxygen rich environments such as hyperbaric chambers.
Allow kits to warm up to room temperature 30 minutes before application; discard any kits kept at room temperature for more than 12 hours.
Preparation:
Locate the medicine port; insert the single-use, prefilled syringe (provided in the kit) needle at 90 degrees and slowly fill the OBI with pegfilgrastim.
After filling, the Neulasta OBI needs to be placed on the patient within 3 minutes after the activation light starts to flash.
Application:
Clean the application site with alcohol and apply the OBI to dry, intact, and oil-free skin on the abdomen (horizontally with light facing up and visible to the patient) or back or the arm (vertically with light facing down toward the elbow).
Ensure the adhesive does not bend or curl while applying the OBI to the skin.
Do not use other materials to hold the OBI in place that could cover visual indicators or compress the OBI against the patient's skin.
Once applied, the OBI should be kept at temperatures between 41 and 104 degrees F (5 and 40 degrees C).
The OBI should remain dry starting at 24 hours after placement (approximately 3 hours before drug delivery begins).
Subcutaneous injection using OBI:
The OBI will deliver the pegfilgrastim dose approximately 27 hours after placement. The Neulasta OBI delivers the dose over 45 minutes; the Udenyca OBI delivers the dose over 5 minutes.
The patient or a caregiver should monitor the site during the dose administration period and for 1 hour after delivery to ensure the full dose is received and to monitor for symptoms of an allergic reaction.
For a missed dose due to OBI failure or leakage, a new dose using the single prefilled syringe for manual use should be administered as soon as possible.
