    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components. The oral iodoquinol product contains 64% organically bound iodine, therefore, iodine hypersensitivity may also be of concern.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    It is not known whether hydrocortisone; iodoquinol can cause fetal harm if used during pregnancy. Animal reproduction studies have not been conducted. The more potent corticosteroids have been shown to be teratogenic after dermal application in laboratory animals. Do not use topical corticosteroids in large amounts, on large areas, or for prolonged periods in pregnant individuals. Intermittent use of mild to moderate potency topical agents has not been associated with fetal harm. However, the use of high potency topical corticosteroids has been associated with fetal growth restriction, particularly when the cumulative dose throughout pregnancy exceeds 300 grams.
    Headline
    children, infants, neonates
    General Information
    The Key Potentially Inappropriate Drugs in Pediatrics (KIDs) list includes topical hydrocortisone as a potentially inappropriate medication for use in neonates, infants, and children younger than 2 years and use should be avoided. If avoidance is not possible, use hydrocortisone; iodoquinol with caution and appropriate monitoring.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Use hydrocortisone; iodoquinol with caution during breast-feeding. There are no data on the presence of topically applied iodoquinol in breast milk. Topical corticosteroids, such as hydrocortisone, can be used safely during lactation because exposure through human milk is minimal. To minimize potential risk, ensure the child's skin does not come into direct contact with the treated area. Do not apply the medication to the nipple or areola, as the child could ingest medication from the skin. Before breast-feeding, thoroughly wipe off the treated area to prevent transfer of medication to the child.

    Headline
    hyperthyroidism
    General Information
    Although adverse reactions are expected to be minimal with topical use of hydrocortisone; iodoquinol due to low systemic iodoquinol concentrations, hyperthyroidism has been reported with oral use of iodoquinol. Iodoquinol contains 64% organically bound iodine. Significant systemic concentrations of iodine-containing drugs like iodoquinol may result in overt thyroid disease. It is unknown if topical iodoquinol preparations could result in thyroid disease; however, iodoquinol may be absorbed through the skin and interfere with thyroid function tests.

    laboratory test interference
    General Information
    Iodoquinol can cause laboratory test interference. Iodoquinol may be absorbed through the skin and interfere with thyroid function tests. If such tests are to be conducted, wait at least 1 month after discontinuation of hydrocortisone; iodoquinol to perform these tests. Additionally, the ferric chloride test for phenylketonuria can yield a false positive result if iodoquinol is present in the diaper or urine.

Description
  Text
  NOTE: This monograph discusses the use of the hydrocortisone; iodoquinol topical combination product. Clinicians may wish to consult the individual monographs of hydrocortisone or iodoquinol for more information about each specific agent.
 
Hydrocortisone and iodoquinol are used together in a topical preparation to treat skin infections. Hydrocortisone is a glucocorticoid that has antiinflammatory, antipruritic, and vasoconstrictive properties. Iodoquinol is used for its antibacterial and antifungal activity.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Hydrocortisone: Hydrocortisone has anti-inflammatory, antipruritic, and vasoconstrictive properties. The exact mechanism of anti-inflammatory activity is unclear for topical steroids.  However, there is some evidence that a correlation exists between vasoconstrictor potency and therapeutic efficacy. Corticosteroids are thought to act by the induction of phospholipase A2 inhibitory proteins (lipocortins). These proteins may control the biosynthesis of potent mediators of inflammation such as prostaglandins and leukotrienes by inhibiting the release of their common precursor, arachidonic acid, which is released from membrane phospholipids by phospholipase A2.
Iodoquinol: Iodoquinol has antifungal and antibacterial activity.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Topical Route
    Description
    Hydrocortisone: The extent of percutaneous absorption of topical steroids is determined by many factors including the vehicle, the integrity of the epidermal barrier, and the use of occlusive dressings. Hydrocortisone is absorbed through normal intact skin. Inflammation or inflammatory disease processes in the skin can increase percutaneous absorption. Occlusive dressings substantially increase percutaneous absorption of steroids.
Iodoquinol: There is no data regarding the percutaneous absorption of iodoquinol.
  Text
  Hydrocortisone; iodoquinol is administered topically. Once absorbed through the skin, hydrocortisone is metabolized in the liver and most body tissues to hydrogenated and degraded forms, such as tetrahydrocortisone and tetrahydrocortisol. These metabolites are excreted in the urine, mainly as conjugated glucuronides. A small portion is excreted as unchanged hydrocortisone. Based on oral administration, an absorbed iodoquinol would be recovered in the urine as a glucuronide.
