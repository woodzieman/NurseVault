    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Available data on the use of sepiapterin during pregnancy are insufficient to determine a drug-associated risk of major birth defects, miscarriage, or other adverse maternal or fetal outcomes. However, uncontrolled blood Phe concentrations before and during pregnancy are associated with an increased risk of adverse pregnancy outcomes and fetal adverse effects. To minimize the risk of hyperphenylalaninemia-related fetal harm, maintain blood Phe concentrations between 120 and 360 micromol/L during pregnancy and during the 3 months before conception. In animal reproduction studies, oral administration of sepiapterin to pregnant rats and rabbits during organogenesis at dose exposures up to 9- and 6- times the human exposure at the maximum recommended human dose (MRHD) of 60 mg/kg, respectively, resulted in no adverse developmental effects.
    Headline

Consider the developmental and health benefits of breast-feeding along with the individual's clinical need for sepiapterin and any potential adverse effects on the breastfed infant from sepiapterin or from the underlying maternal condition.

Adverse Reactions
  Headline
  Adverse Reactions
  Adverse Reactions
    Headline
    bleeding, hematoma, menorrhagia
    General Information
    Sepiapterin may increase the risk of bleeding. Bleeding events, including superficial hematoma, prolonged bleeding, and heavy menstrual bleeding (menorrhagia) have occurred in less than 2% of patients treated with sepiapterin. One patient with non-traumatic superficial hematomas and prolonged bleeding was rechallenged at a lower dose of sepiapterin with recurrence of symptoms, which led to treatment discontinuation. The patient experienced symptoms 15 days after initial exposure and 2 days after rechallenge. The patient had normal blood counts and coagulation studies at the time of the bleeding. Advise the patient about the increased risk of bleeding associated with sepiapterin and to contact their health care provider if they experience any signs of increased bleeding. Consider treatment interruption with sepiapterin in patients with active bleeding.

Description
  Text
  Sepiapterin is a phenylalanine hydroxylase (PAH) activator indicated for the treatment of hyperphenylalaninemia (HPA) in individuals with sepiapterin-responsive phenylketonuria (PKU). It is a natural precursor of the enzymatic cofactor tetrahydrobiopterin (BH4), a critical cofactor for PAH. By enhancing BH4 availability, sepiapterin facilitates PAH activity and effectively reduces blood phenylalanine (Phe) concentrations and has the potential to treat a broad range of PKU patients. PKU is a rare, inherited metabolic disorder caused by a deficiency in the enzyme phenylalanine hydroxylase, leading to the accumulation of Phe to toxic concentrations. Untreated or poorly controlled PKU can result in irreversible neurological damage, including intellectual disability, seizures, and developmental delays. While newborns appear asymptomatic at birth, symptoms progress if not managed early. Diagnosis is typically made through routine newborn screening. An increased risk of bleeding was observed with sepiapterin in clinical trials. Sepiapterin was FDA-approved in July 2025.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Sepiapterin is a precursor of tetrahydrobiopterin (BH4), an essential enzymatic cofactor required for the activity of phenylalanine hydroxylase (PAH). In patients with phenylketonuria (PKU), PAH activity is reduced due to genetic variants in the PAH gene. This increased enzymatic function promotes the conversion of phenylalanine (Phe) to tyrosine, thereby reducing elevated blood Phe concentrations associated with PKU.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Oral Route
    Description
    After oral administration, sepiapterin is rapidly absorbed, with a Tmax of approximately 1 to 3 hours. It is rapidly metabolized to its active metabolite, tetrahydrobiopterin (BH4), with peak BH4 concentrations occurring around 4 hours post-dose. The exposure of sepiapterin (Cmax 2.9 nanogram/mL; AUC 19.9 nanogram x hour/mL) was generally less than 2% of that of BH4 (Cmax 432 nanogram/mL; AUC 3,618 nanogram x hour/mL). No accumulation of BH4 was observed after repeated once-daily administration of sepiapterin at doses up to 60 mg/kg in healthy adult volunteers.
 
Effects of food
Administration of sepiapterin with food results in increased exposure to sepiapterin and BH4. When sepiapterin was administered at 20 or 60 mg/kg daily with a low-fat meal in healthy adult subjects, BH4 exposures were 169% to 172% higher for Cmax and 162% to 173% higher for AUC compared to administration under fasted conditions. When sepiapterin at 20 or 60 mg/kg was administered with a high-fat, high-calorie meal, BH4 exposures were 221% to 226% higher for Cmax and 251% to 284% higher for AUC compared to administration under fasted conditions.

  Text
  Sepiapterin is administered orally. Sepiapterin exhibits low plasma protein binding (mean, 15.4%), while the active metabolite, tetrahydrobiopterin (BH4), shows higher binding (24.1% to 41.3%). The apparent Vd for BH4 is approximately 11,433 L in adults with phenylketonuria; the Vd for sepiapterin could not be reliably determined due to its rapid conversion to BH4. Increases in cerebrospinal fluid BH4 concentrations have been observed after administration of sepiapterin 60 mg/kg once daily for 7 days. Sepiapterin is extensively metabolized to its active form, BH4 via a 2-step process involving sepiapterin reductase or carbonyl reductase and dihydrofolate reductase. BH4 undergoes further metabolism through both enzymatic and nonenzymatic pathways, including by phenylalanine hydroxylase, tyrosine hydroxylase, tryptophan hydroxylase, pterin-4-alpha-carbinolamine dehydratase, dihydropteridine reductase, xanthine oxidase, and nitric oxide synthase. After a single oral dose of radiolabeled sepiapterin (4,000 mg), mean recovery was 6.7% in urine and 26.2% in feces, with a combined recovery of 32.9% by 240 hours; the low recovery is likely due to the formation of volatile intestinal metabolites. The terminal half-life of BH4 is approximately 5 hours, and its apparent clearance is 1,498 L/hour in adults with phenylketonuria. Plasma concentrations of sepiapterin decline rapidly and fall below the lower limit of quantitation by 12 hours post-dose.
 
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes and drug transporters: BCRP
Both sepiapterin and BH4 were a substrate and inhibitor of efflux transporter BCRP in vitro.

Storage of Liquid and Soft Food Mixtures
If the sepiapterin liquid or soft food mixture is not administered immediately, cover and store the mixture at controlled room temperature between 20 to 25 degrees C (68 to 77 degrees F) for up to 6 hours or refrigerate between 2 to 8 degrees C (36 to 46 degrees F) for up to 24 hours.
If the liquid or soft food mixture is stored, stir for at least 30 or 60 seconds, respectively, prior to administration of the prescribed dose.
Discard unused sepiapterin mixture after 6 hours at controlled room temperature or after 24 hours if refrigerated.
