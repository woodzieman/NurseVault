Drug Class: Ublituximab is an intravenously (IV) administered monoclonal antibody that binds to CD20 on the surface of immune cells called B cells and depletes them from the circulation.

Routes: intravenous; Common adult doses: 450 mg/dose IV.

Indication Specific Dosing
  General Information
  Assessments needed before the first dose of ublituximab:
Perform hepatitis B virus (HBV) screening. Consult liver disease experts before initiating treatment in people who are negative for HBsAg and positive for hepatitis B core antibody (HBcAb+) or are carriers of HBV (HBsAg+). Ublituximab is contraindicated in people with active HBV confirmed by positive hepatitis B surface antigen (HBsAg) and anti-HBV tests.
Obtain serum aminotransferases (alanine aminotransferase [ALT] and aspartate aminotransferase [AST]), alkaline phosphatase, and bilirubin concentrations before ublituximab initiation.
Perform testing for quantitative serum immunoglobulins. Consult immunology experts before initiating treatment in people with low serum immunoglobulins.
Administer all live or live-attenuated immunizations according to guidelines at least 4 weeks prior to ublituximab initiation, and administer all non-live immunizations at least 2 weeks prior to initiation. Additionally, the history of use of immunosuppressive medications and their additive effects should be evaluated.
 
Before each dose of ublituximab:
Determine whether the individual has an active infection. In case of active infection, delay the infusion of ublituximab until the infection resolves.

    Headline
    General Information
    General Information
    Administer all immunizations according to immunization guidelines at least 4 weeks before initiation of ublituximab for live or live-attenuated vaccines and, whenever possible, at least 2 weeks prior to initiation of ublituximab for non-live vaccines. Ublituximab may interfere with the effectiveness of non-live vaccines. The safety of immunization with live or live-attenuated vaccines during or following administration of ublituximab has not been studied. Vaccination with live virus vaccines is not recommended during treatment with ublituxiamb and until B-cell repletion.
    Headline
    history of significant adverse event(s) associated with previous use
    General Information
    Ublituximab is associated with a variety of infusion-related reactions, with severities ranging from mild to life-threatening. Ublituximab is contraindicated for use in patients with a history of significant adverse event(s) associated with previous use, including a history of a life-threatening infusion reaction to ublituximab. Premedicate the patient with corticosteroids and antihistamines, and possibly other premedication (an antipyretic) to reduce the risk of infusion-related reactions. After completion of the first 2 infusions, monitor for at least 1 hour for signs or symptoms of an infusion reaction. Post-infusion monitoring of subsequent infusions is at prescriber discretion unless an infusion reaction and/or hypersensitivity has been observed with the current or any prior infusion. Infusion reactions may occur up to 24 hours after the infusion. Infusion rate modifications are necessary for infusion reactions, and adjustments are dependent on the severity of the reaction. For life-threatening reactions, immediately and permanently stop treatment. For less severe reactions, management may include temporarily stopping the infusion and/or reducing the infusion rate. In all cases, institute appropriate supportive treatment. While no fatal infusion reactions occurred during clinical trials, 0.6% of patients did experience serious reactions, some requiring hospitalization. The highest incidence of infusion reactions is associated with the first infusion.
    Headline
    hepatitis B infection
    General Information
    Ublituximab is contraindicated for use in patients with an active hepatitis B infection. Perform hepatitis B virus (HBV) screening for all patients prior to treatment, and do not administer ublituximab to patients with active HBV confirmed by positive results for hepatitis B surface antigen (HBsAg) and hepatitis B surface antibody (anti-HB) tests. If patients are negative for HBsAg and positive for HB core antibody (HBcAb+) or are carriers of HBV (HBsAg+), consult liver disease experts before initiation and during treatment. Hepatitis B reactivation occurred in a patient treated with ublituximab during clinical trials. Fulminant hepatitis, hepatic failure, and death caused by HBV reactivation have occurred in patients treated with other anti-CD20 antibodies.
    Headline
    hypogammaglobulinemia, infection
    General Information
    Delay ublituximab administration in patients with an active infection until the infection is resolved. Perform testing for quantitative serum immunoglobulins before initiating ublituximab treatment. Serious, including life-threatening or fatal, bacterial and viral infections have been reported in patients receiving ublituximab. An increased risk of infections, including serious and fatal bacterial, fungal, and new or reactivated viral infections, has been observed during and following completion of treatment with other anti-CD20 B-cell depleting therapies. Consult immunology experts before initiating treatment with ublituximab in patients with low serum immunoglobulins (hypogammaglobulinemia). Monitor quantitative serum immunoglobulin concentrations during treatment and after treatment discontinuation, until B-cell repletion, especially in the setting of recurrent serious infections. Ublituximab decreased immunoglobulin M (IgM), and no decline of immunoglobulin G (IgG) was observed at the end of clinical trials. Decreases in IgM and IgG concentrations less than the lower limit of normal have shown an association with an increased rate of serious infections during clinical trials of other anti-CD20 monoclonal antibody therapies. When initiating ublituximab after an immunosuppressive therapy or initiating an immunosuppressive therapy after ublituximab treatment, consider the potential for increased immunosuppression. Ublituximab has not been studied in combination with other multiple sclerosis therapies. Consider ublituximab discontinuation in patients with serious opportunistic or recurrent serious infections, or if prolonged hypogammaglobulinemia requires treatment with intravenous immunoglobulins.
    Headline
    hepatic disease
    General Information
    Use ublituximab with caution in individuals with hepatic disease. Clinically significant liver injury, without findings of viral hepatitis, has been reported during postmarketing experience. Signs of liver injury have occurred weeks to months after administration of ublituximab. Individuals treated with ublituximab with an alanine aminotransaminase (ALT) or aspartate aminotransferase (AST) greater than 3 times the upper limit of normal (ULN) and serum bilirubin greater than 2 times the ULN are potentially at risk for severe drug-induced liver injury. Obtain liver function tests prior to ublituximab treatment and with any signs of hepatic injury during treatment. If ublituximab liver injury is suspected, discontinue the medication.
    Headline
    people who may become pregnant, reproductive risk
    General Information
    Counsel people who may become pregnant about the reproductive risk associated with ublituximab. There is potential for fetal harm. Advise people who may become pregnant to use effective contraception while taking ublituximab and for 6 months after the last dose.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    There are no data on the developmental risk associated with the use of ublituximab during pregnancy. Data from case reports of pregnancies occurring during clinical trials with ublituximab are insufficient to identify a drug-associated risk of major birth defects, miscarriage, or adverse outcomes. It is known that monoclonal antibodies can be actively transported across the placenta. In pregnant transgenic huCD20 mice, a pharmacologically relevant animal model, weekly intravenous administration of ublituximab (0, 5, 20, or 50 mg/kg) during organogenesis resulted in no adverse pregnancy or embryo-fetal outcomes. However, there was a marked reduction in B-cells in fetal blood at all but the lowest dose tested. Weekly intravenous administration of ublitiximab to pregnant monkeys during all trimesters of pregnancy produced a severe immunogenic response in dams, which resulted in embryo-fetal los and maternal morbidity and mortality. External, visceral, and skeletal abnormalities occurred in 2 offspring from dams exposed during the second trimester of pregnancy. Findings included contractures and abnormal flexion of multiple limbs and tail, shortened mandible, elongate calvarium, enlargement of ears, and/or craniomandibular abnormalities which were attributable to brain necrosis. The findings were considered secondary to an immunogenic reaction and are not considered relevant to humans. There is a pregnancy exposure registry that monitors outcomes in pregnant people exposed to ublituximab. Individuals who become pregnant while taking ublituximab or within 6 months following their last dose are eligible for enrollment. Information about the registry can be obtained at https://www.briumvipregnancyregistry.com or by calling 1-877-411-4546.
    Headline
    neonates and infants exposed to this medication in utero
    General Information
    Avoid administration of live vaccines to neonates and infants exposed to this medication in utero until B-cell recovery occurs. There are no data on B-cell levels in human neonates after in utero exposure. However, transient peripheral B-cell depletion and lymphocytopenia have been reported in infants with in utero exposure to other anti-CD20 antibodies during pregnancy.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Use ublituximab with caution during breast-feeding. There are no data on the presence of ublituximab in human milk, its effects on the breastfed child, or its effects on milk production. Human IgG antibodies are excreted in human milk. The potential for absorption of ublituximab and subsequent B-cell depletion in the infant are unknown. Weigh the benefits of breast-feeding along with the patient's clinical need for ublituximab and any potential adverse effects on the breastfed infant or from the patient's underlying condition.

    Infusion-related reactions are common among patients treated with ublituximab. Description
  Text
  Ublituximab is an intravenously (IV) administered monoclonal antibody that binds to CD20 on the surface of immune cells called B cells and depletes them from the circulation. This drug is similar to ocrelizumab and ofatumumab. Ublituximab is approved for the treatment of relapsing forms of multiple sclerosis (MS), to include clinically isolated syndrome, relapsing-remitting disease, and active secondary progressive disease in adults. An IV infusion is given every 24 weeks after a loading regimen of 2 infusions given 2 weeks apart. Evidence suggests that B cells play a role in immune-system mediated damage to the brain and spinal cord in those affected by MS. The efficacy of ublituximab for the treatment of relapsing forms of MS was demonstrated in 2, randomized, double-blind, active comparator trials, in which ublituximab was compared to teriflunomide over 96 weeks. Across both trials, ublituximab significantly reduced annualized relapse rates compared to teriflunomide (relative reductions of 49% to 59%). There was no significant difference between the treatments in the proportion of patients with 12-week confirmed disability progression (risk reduction of 16%; 5.2% ublituximab vs. 5.9% teriflunomide, p = 0.51). Serious infusion reactions and infections, including life-threatening and fatal infections, have occurred with use.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  The exact mechanism by which ublituximab exerts its therapeutic effect in multiple sclerosis is unknown. Ublituximab likely binds to CD20, a cell surface antigen present on pre-B and mature B lymphocytes. Following binding, antibody-dependent cellular cytolysis and complement-mediated lysis occur.

IV infusion administration:
Administer under the close supervision of an experienced healthcare professional with access to appropriate medical support to manage severe reactions.
Administration can cause serious infusion-related reactions. Carefully monitor the patient during each infusion, and for at least 1 hour following the completion of the first 2 infusions. Post-infusion monitoring of subsequent infusions is at prescriber discretion unless infusion reaction and/or hypersensitivity has been observed in association with the current or any prior infusion.
Premedication: Premedicate 30 minutes prior to infusion with methylprednisolone 100 mg IV (or an equivalent oral or IV corticosteroid) and 30 to 60 minutes prior to infusion with an antihistamine (e.g., diphenhydramine) to reduce the frequency and severity of infusion reactions. An antipyretic (e.g., acetaminophen) may also be considered.
Prior to the start of the IV infusion, the contents of the infusion bag should be at room temperature.
Administer through a dedicated infusion line. No incompatibilities between ublituximab and polyvinyl chloride (PVC) or polyolefin (PO) IV administration sets have been observed.
First infusion (150 mg): Administer at an initial rate of 10 mL/hour for 30 minutes with subsequent rate increases to 20 mL/hour for the next 30 minutes, 35 mL/hour for the next 60 minutes, and 100 mL/hour for the remaining 2 hours for a total infusion duration of 4 hours.
Subsequent infusions (450 mg): Administer at an initial rate of 100 mL/hour for 30 minutes. Increase to 400 mL/hour for the remaining 30 minutes for a total infusion duration of 1 hour.
Rate of infusion modifications in response to infusion reactions depend on reaction severity:
Life-threatening or disabling infusion reactions: Immediately discontinue the infusion and provide appropriate supportive treatment. Permanently discontinue ublituximab.
Severe infusion reactions: Immediately interrupt the infusion and provide appropriate supportive treatment. Wait until all symptoms have resolved before resuming infusion. When restarting, begin at one-half of the infusion rate at the time of infusion reaction onset. If this rate is tolerated, it may be increased according to recommended infusion rate titration guidelines. The rate modifications do not change the dose administered but do increase the duration of infusion time.
Mild or moderate infusion reactions: Reduce the infusion rate to one-half of the rate at the onset of the infusion reaction and maintain the reduced rate for a minimum of 30 minutes. If this rate is tolerated, it may be increased according to recommended infusion rate titration guidelines. The rate modifications do not change the dose administered but do increase the duration of infusion time.
Missed doses: If a planned infusion is missed, administer as soon as possible; do not wait until the next scheduled dose. Reset the dose schedule to administer the next sequential dose 24 weeks after the missed dose is administered. Infusions must be separated by at least 5 months.
