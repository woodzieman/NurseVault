Drug Class: Somatrogon is a once-weekly, subcutaneous, long acting human growth hormone analog approved for the treatment of pediatric patients 3 years and older who have growth failure due to inadequate secretion of endogenous growth hormone.

    Headline
    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components.
    Headline
    critical illness, respiratory failure, trauma
    General Information
    Somatrogon is contraindicated in individuals with acute critical illness due to complications following open-heart surgery such as coronary artery bypass graft surgery (CABG), abdominal surgery, multiple accidental trauma, or acute severe respiratory insufficiency (acute respiratory failure) because of the risk of increased mortality. The safety of continuing somatrogon replacement treatment for approved indications in individuals who develop acute critical illnesses has not been established.
    Headline
    malignancy
    General Information
    Somatrogon is contraindicated in individuals with active malignancy. There is an increased risk of malignancy progression with growth hormone treatment in individuals with active malignancy. Any pre-existing neoplastic disease must be inactive, and treatment complete, prior to beginning somatrogon therapy. Discontinue somatrogon treatment in individuals who have recurrent cancer. In childhood cancer survivors who were treated with radiation therapy to the brain/head for their first neoplasm and who developed subsequent growth hormone deficiency and were treated with somatropin, an increased risk of a new primary malignancy has been reported. Intracranial tumors, in particular meningiomas, were the most common of these second neoplasms. Monitor all individuals with a history of growth hormone deficiency secondary to an intracranial neoplasm routinely while on somatrogon therapy for progression or recurrence of the tumor. Because children with certain rare genetic causes of short stature have an increased risk of developing malignancies, consider the risks and benefits of starting somatrogon in these individuals. If treatment with somatrogon is initiated, monitor these individuals carefully for development of neoplasms. There is risk of malignant changes of preexisting nevi (i.e., skin cancer) in individuals treated with somatropin. Monitor individuals on somatrogon therapy carefully for increased growth, or potential malignant changes, of preexisting nevi. Advise individuals and caregivers to report significant changes in behavior, onset of headaches, vision disturbances, and/or changes in skin pigmentation or the appearance of preexisting nevi. Discontinue somatrogon therapy if evidence of neoplasia develops.
    Headline
    diabetes mellitus, proliferative diabetic retinopathy, severe non-proliferative diabetic retinopathy
    General Information
    Somatrogon is contraindicated in individuals with active proliferative diabetic retinopathy and severe non-proliferative diabetic retinopathy as growth hormone (GH) appears to influence the progression of this disease. Use somatrogon cautiously in individuals with diabetes mellitus or risk factors for glucose intolerance as somatrogon may decrease insulin sensitivity, especially at higher doses. New onset type 2 diabetes mellitus has been reported in individuals taking GH products. Individuals with pre-diabetes or undiagnosed diabetes may experience worsening glycemic control and develop symptoms. Monitor glucose concentrations in all individuals receiving somatrogon, especially those with risk factors for diabetes mellitus (e.g., obesity, Turner syndrome, family history). Monitor individuals with type 1 or type 2 diabetes or pre-diabetes closely; dose adjustments of antidiabetic medications may be necessary.
    Headline
    obesity, obstructive sleep apnea, respiratory insufficiency
    General Information
    Somatrogon is contraindicated in individuals with Prader-Willi syndrome who are severely obese or have severe respiratory impairment. Unless individuals with Prader-Willi syndrome also have a diagnosis of growth hormone deficiency, somatrogon is not indicated for long-term treatment of pediatric patients who have growth failure due to genetically confirmed Prader-Willi syndrome. There have been reports of fatalities with the use of growth hormone in pediatric patients with Prader-Willi syndrome who had one or more of the following risk factors: severe obesity, history of respiratory insufficiency or sleep apnea, or unidentified respiratory infection. Male individuals with one or more of these factors may be at increased risk. Individuals with Prader-Willi syndrome should be evaluated for upper airway obstruction before initiation of treatment with growth hormone. If during treatment with growth hormone, individuals show signs of upper airway obstruction (including onset of or increased snoring), treatment should be interrupted. All individuals with Prader-Willi syndrome should be evaluated for obstructive sleep apnea and monitored if sleep apnea is suspected.
    Headline
    adrenal insufficiency, hypothyroidism, pituitary insufficiency
    General Information
    Individuals who have or at risk for pituitary insufficiency, and are receiving somatrogon, may be at risk for reduced serum cortisol concentrations and/or unmasking of central (secondary) adrenal insufficiency. Individuals treated with glucocorticoid replacement for previously diagnosed adrenal insufficiency may require an increase in their maintenance or stress doses following initiation of somatrogon treatment. In addition, individuals with untreated hypothyroidism will have an inadequate response to somatrogon therapy. Periodic thyroid function tests should be performed and treatment with thyroid hormone initiated when indicated.
    Headline
    scoliosis
    General Information
    Individuals with a history of scoliosis should receive somatrogon with caution. Because growth hormone increases growth rate, individuals with scoliosis can experience progression of scoliosis. Individuals should be monitored for progression of scoliosis.
    Headline
    epiphyseal closure
    General Information
    Somatrogon is contraindicated in individuals with epiphyseal closure. Slipped capital femoral epiphysis may occur more frequently during periods of rapid growth and can lead to osteonecrosis. Cases of slipped capital femoral epiphysis, with or without osteonecrosis, have been reported in pediatric patients with short stature receiving somatrogon. Evaluate pediatric patients presenting with a new limp or complaints of hip or knee pain.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Available data on the use of somatrogon during human pregnancy are insufficient to evaluate a drug-associated risk of major birth defects, miscarriage, or other adverse outcomes. In animal studies, there was no evidence of embryo-fetal toxicity at doses up to 45 times the maximum recommended human dose based on average plasma concentration.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    There are no data on the presence of somatrogon in human milk, its effects on the breast-fed child, or its effect on milk production. Consider the benefits of breast-feeding, the patient's clinical need for treatment, and any potential adverse effects on the breast-fed child from the medication or from the patient's underlying medical condition.

    antibody formation
    General Information
    Antibody formation may occur with somatrogon use. During a 12-month clinical trial, 84/109 (77.1%) of somatrogon-treated patients tested positive for antidrug antibodies, with most showing specificity to human growth hormone. The antidrug antibodies persisted in most of the subjects during the study. Neutralizing antibodies developed in 8/217 (3.7%) of somatrogon-treated patients during the study for up to 42 months of exposure to somatrogon. The neutralizing antibodies were transient in all subjects. Antidrug antibodies, including neutralizing antibodies, did not appear to have a clinically significant impact on the safety or efficacy of somatrogon during the 12-month randomized treatment period. Additionally, no apparent effect of antidrug antibodies on growth was observed for an additional 30 months of exposure to somatrogon in the uncontrolled extension period of another study.

    Laboratory test interference may occur with somatrogon. Description
  Text
  Somatrogon is a once-weekly, subcutaneous, long acting human growth hormone analog approved for the treatment of pediatric patients 3 years and older who have growth failure due to inadequate secretion of endogenous growth hormone. A clinical trial evaluated the safety and efficacy of somatrogon administered once-weekly compared to once-daily somatropin; the primary endpoint was height velocity. Mean annualized height velocity at 52 weeks was 10.1 cm/year in patients receiving somatrogon compared to 9.78 cm/year in patients receiving somatropin. Prior to starting somatrogon therapy, perform a fundoscopic eye examination to exclude papilledema and reassess periodically. Papilledema may be a symptom of intracranial hypertension and growth hormones may induce or worsen intracranial hypertension.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Somatrogon binds to growth hormone (GH) receptors in the cell membrane of target cells, which results in intracellular signal transduction and a number of other pharmacodynamic effects. Somatrogon binding leads to activation of the STAT5b signaling pathway and increases the serum concentration of insulin-like growth factor-1 (IGF-1). GH and IGF-1 stimulate metabolic changes, linear growth, and enhance growth velocity in pediatric patients with growth hormone deficiency (GHD).

  Text
  Somatrogon is administered by subcutaneous injection. The estimated apparent central volume of distribution of somatrogon was approximately 0.342 L/kg and apparent peripheral volume of distribution was 0.671 L/kg. Somatrogon metabolism is thought to be classical protein catabolism, with recovery of the amino acids and return to the systemic circulation. Excretion was not evaluated in clinical studies. The mean somatrogon estimated apparent clearance was 0.0398 L/hour/kg and mean observed half-life was 37.7 hours, allowing for weekly dosing. Somatrogon will be present in the circulation for about 8 days after the last dose.
After single dose administration, somatrogon produces dose-dependent increases in insulin-like growth factor-1 (IGF-1). After multiple dosing, IGF-1 concentrations were in the normal range for growth hormone deficiency (GHD) patients, similar to daily somatropin. Maximum IGF-1 concentrations were observed approximately 2 days (48 hours) post-dose, with the average weekly IGF-1 occurring approximately 4 days post-dose.
 
Affected cytochrome P450 (CYP450) isoenzymes and drug transporters: Unknown
Limited published data indicate that growth hormone treatment increases CYP450-mediated antipyrine clearance. Thus, somatrogon may alter the clearance of compounds known to be metabolized by CYP450 enzymes. No specific data are available.

The 60 mg/1.2 mL (50 mg/mL) prefilled pen dials in 0.5 mg increments and delivers doses from 0.5 to 30 mg.
Missed doses: Administer a missed dose as soon as possible and not more than 3 days after the missed dose. If more than 3 days have passed, skip the missed dose and administer the next dose on the regular dosing day.
When changing the regular dosing day to a different day of the week: Ensure that at least 3 days have elapsed between the last dose and the newly-established regular dosing day.
Storage: Store an in-use pen in a refrigerator at 36 to 46 degrees F (2 to 8 degrees C) with the cap on for up to 28 days. Do not store the pen with a needle attached. Write the date of first use on the pen label. Do not freeze or shake. Do not expose to heat and do not use the pen if it has been frozen. Store away from direct sunlight.
 
Subcutaneous Injection
Patients and/or caregivers may inject the dose after proper training by their health care provider.
This pen should never be shared among patients due to the risk for bloodborne infections. Reserve the use of any pen for 1 patient only.
Administer subcutaneously once weekly in the upper arms, thigh, abdomen, or buttocks; rotate injection sites weekly to avoid lipohypertrophy.
The pen needle should remain in the skin for at least 10 seconds to ensure complete delivery of the dose (slowly count to 10).
If 2 cartridges are necessary for the prescribed dose, prepare and inject separately using a new needle. If more than 1 injection is needed to complete the full dose, each injection should be given in a different injection site.
Always remove and discard the needle in an FDA-cleared sharps disposal container after each injection; store prefilled pen without an injection needle attached. Always use a new needle for each injection to prevent contamination.
Refer to Instructions for Use that come with the pen device or https://www.pfizer.com/products/product-detail/ngenla-injection for complete administration instructions with illustrations.
