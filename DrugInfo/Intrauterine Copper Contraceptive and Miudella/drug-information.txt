    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components. For example, the T-frame of the Paragard T device contains polyethylene with barium sulfate in addition to the copper component. Miudella contains polypropylene, copper, nitinol, an alloy of nickel and titanium, or other trace elements present in the copper component of the device.

    General Information
    The copper intrauterine device (IUD) is contraindicated in people with a previously placed IUD that has not been removed.
The Miudella brand copper intrauterine device (IUD) is contraindicated for use as post-coital contraception (emergency contraception).

    General Information
    General Information
    Like other intrauterine system contraceptives, the copper intrauterine device (IUD) does not protect against the transmission of human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) infection or other sexually transmitted infection (STI). The use of a barrier method as a partial protection against acquiring STIs should be strongly recommended.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    The copper intrauterine device (IUD) is contraindicated during pregnancy or suspected pregnancy because there is no need for pregnancy prevention in an individual who is already pregnant and the IUD may cause adverse pregnancy outcomes. If intrauterine pregnancy occurs with a copper IUD in place and the strings are visible or can be retrieved from the cervical canal, remove the copper IUD because leaving it in place may increase the risk of spontaneous abortion and preterm labor. Removal of the copper IUD may also result in spontaneous abortion. If an individual becomes pregnant with a copper IUD in place and if the copper IUD cannot be removed or the individual chooses not to have it removed, advise the individual that failure to remove the copper IUD increases the risk of miscarriage, sepsis, premature labor, and premature delivery. Prenatal care should include counseling about these risks and to report immediately any flu-like symptoms, fever, chills, cramping, pain, bleeding, vaginal discharge or leakage of fluid, or any other symptom that suggests complications of the pregnancy. Evaluate for possible ectopic pregnancy in any individual who becomes pregnant while using a copper IUD because a pregnancy that occurs with a copper IUD in place is more likely to be ectopic than a pregnancy in the general population. However, because a copper IUD prevents most pregnancies, individuals who use a copper IUD have a lower risk of an ectopic pregnancy than sexually active individuals who do not use any contraception.
    Headline
    requires an experienced clinician
    General Information
    Improper insertion of copper intrauterine devices (IUDs) increases the risk of complications. Proper training prior to first use of a copper IUD can minimize the risk of improper insertion. Thus, insertion of this IUD requires an experienced clinician who is trained in IUD insertion using aseptic technique. Clinicians should be aware of potential complications of use [including infectious risks, device embedment, uterine perforation, device expulsion, abnormal bleeding, or pregnancy loss (if pregnancy occurs with IUD in situ)] and their proper management should they occur. The Miudella brand copper IUD is available only through a risk evaluation and mitigation strategy (REMS) called the Miudella REMS program to ensure healthcare providers are trained on the proper insertion technique.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Experts consider use of the copper IUD compatible with breast-feeding. No difference has been detected in concentration of copper in human milk before and after insertion of the copper intrauterine device (IUD). There is no information on the effect of copper in a breastfed child or the effect on milk production. The developmental and health benefits of breast-feeding should be considered along with the mother's clinical need for the copper IUD and any potential adverse effects on the breastfed child from the copper IUD. A postmarketing safety study with IUDs, including the copper IUD, demonstrated an increased risk of uterine perforation in lactating women. The risk of perforation may be increased if the copper IUD is inserted when the uterus is fixed, retroverted, or not completely involuted during the postpartum period. If perforation does occur, locate and remove the copper IUD promptly. Surgery may be required. Preoperative imaging followed by laparoscopy or laparotomy is often required to remove the copper IUD from the peritoneal cavity.

    Headline
    abnormal uterine bleeding, cervical cancer, distortion of the uterine cavity, uterine cancer
    General Information
    Insertion of a copper intrauterine device (IUD) is contraindicated in people with abnormalities of the uterus that result in distortion of the uterine cavity, including uterine leiomyoma or fibroids, as these conditions may interfere with the placement and retention of the IUD. Sonography may be indicated to ensure appropriate anatomical conditions for IUD placement. Insertion into a uterine cavity measuring less than 6 cm may increase the incidence of expulsion, bleeding, pain, and perforation. The copper IUD is also contraindicated in people with known uterine or cervical malignancy (cervical cancer or uterine cancer). The copper IUD is contraindicated in individuals with undiagnosed abnormal uterine bleeding. Because irregular bleeding/spotting is common during the first year of copper IUD use, exclude endometrial pathology (polyps or cancer) prior to the insertion of the copper IUD in those with persistent or uncharacteristic vaginal bleeding.

    acquired immunodeficiency syndrome (AIDS), acute pelvic inflammatory disease, endometritis, increased risk for pelvic inflammatory disease, leukemia, lower genital tract infection, people who inject drugs
    General Information
    The insertion of a copper intrauterine device (IUD) may increase the risk for pelvic infection, endometritis, and sepsis. Copper IUD insertion is contraindicated in individuals with known or suspected acute pelvic inflammatory disease (PID), a history of postpartum endometritis, postabortal endometritis, or infected abortion (septic abortion) in the past 3 months, untreated acute cervicitis or acute vaginitis (including bacterial vaginosis) or other lower genital tract infections until the infection is controlled, and in individuals with conditions associated with an increased risk for pelvic inflammatory disease. PID is often associated with sexually transmitted disease. The risk of PID is greater for individuals who have multiple sexual partners, and also for those whose sexual partner(s) have multiple sexual partners. People who have had PID are at increased risk for PID recurrence or re-infection. In particular, ascertain whether the individual is at increased risk of infection (e.g., leukemia, acquired immunodeficiency syndrome (AIDS), people who inject drugs).

    Individuals using the copper intrauterine device (IUD) can be safely scanned with magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) only under certain MRI system conditions. Should an individual with a copper IUD need to have an examination that involves an MRI, the individual should inform her healthcare provider and scan technician that a copper IUD is in place before the MRI procedure. Adverse Reactions

  Adverse Reactions
  Adverse Reactions
    Headline
    abdominal pain, chills, dyspareunia, endometritis, fever, infection, pelvic pain
    General Information
    Intrauterine devices (IUDs), including the copper IUD, increase the risk of pelvic inflammatory disease (PID), other pelvic infection, and sepsis, most likely due to organisms being introduced into the uterus during insertion. Copper IUDs are contraindicated in individuals with known or suspected PID or endometritis, including postpartum endometritis or postabortal endometritis in the past 3 months. PID can have serious consequences, such as tubal damage leading to ectopic pregnancy or infertility, hysterectomy, sepsis, and death. In clinical trials with Paragard, the incidence of PID that resulted in removal was approximately 0.1%. Remove the copper IUD in cases of recurrent PID or endometritis, or if an acute pelvic infection is severe or does not respond to treatment. Severe infection or sepsis, including Group A streptococcal sepsis (GAS), have been reported following copper IUD insertion, sometimes causing severe pain within hours and/or sepsis within days. Because death from GAS is more likely if treatment is delayed, prompt recognition and management are essential. Use aseptic technique during insertion to minimize the risk of serious infections such as GAS. Actinomycosis has also been associated with IUD use; symptomatic individuals with known actinomycosis infection should have the device removed and receive antibiotics. Actinomycetes can be found in genital tract cultures in healthy individuals without IUDs. The significance of actinomyces-like organisms on a Pap smear in an asymptomatic IUD user is unknown, and this finding alone does not always require IUD removal and treatment. When possible, confirm a Pap smear diagnosis with cultures. Individuals should be advised to recognize symptoms of pelvic infection, such as prolonged or heavy menstrual bleeding, unexplained vaginal bleeding, odorous vaginal discharge, lower abdominal pain, pelvic pain or tenderness, dyspareunia, chills, fever, and genital lesions or sores. Bleeding and/or pain were common reasons for discontinuation.

    General Information
    Bleeding pattern alterations may occur with copper IUD use and may result in menstrual irregularity, including heavier and longer menstrual cycles with intermenstrual spotting. For Miudella, bleeding/pain included the preferred terms abdominal pain, abdominal pain lower, post procedural discomfort, procedural pain, coital bleeding, dysmenorrhea, dyspareunia, heavy menstrual bleeding, intermenstrual bleeding, menometrorrhagia, pelvic discomfort, pelvic pain, polymenorrhea, uterine hemorrhage, and uterine spasm.     General Information
    Published studies on pregnancy outcomes exposed to copper intrauterine device (IUD) report up to 27% spontaneous fetal abortion when the IUD was removed compared to 77% spontaneous fetal abortion when the IUD remained in the uterus.     uterine perforation
    General Information
    One of the more common device-related events with copper intrauterine devices (IUDs) leading to discontinuation of use was device expulsion. The incidence of IUD expulsion in the clinical trials was 2.3% with Paragard and 3.6% through 3 years of use with Miudella. The rate diminished after the first year. IUD expulsion caused discontinuation that ranged from 0 to 5.7 per 100 subjects treated with Paragard and from 0.9 to 1.9 per 100 subjects treated with Miudella. Malposition of the IUD was reported in 1.7% of subjects treated with Paragard during clinical trials. Consider further diagnostic imaging, such as x-ray, to confirm expulsion if the IUD is not found in the uterus. Remove a partially expelled copper IUD. Do not attempt to push a partially expelled IUD into the uterus. Partial or complete expulsion of the copper IUD may reduce contraceptive efficacy and result in pregnancy. Serious, but rare, adverse events associated with the copper IUD include uterine embedment and cervical or uterine perforation. IUD embedment was reported in 0.9% of subjects treated with Paragard during clinical trials. Partial penetration or embedment of the copper IUD in the myometrium can make removal difficult. In some cases, surgical removal may be necessary. Breakage of an embedded IUD during non-surgical removal has been reported with Paragard. Partial or total perforation of the uterine wall or cervix may occur during insertions, although the perforation may not be detected until sometime later. Perforation may reduce contraceptive efficacy and result in pregnancy. The incidence of perforation during or following the copper IUD insertion in clinical trials was 0.2% with Paragard and 0.1% with Miudella. Additionally, partial uterine perforation was reported in 0.9% of subjects treated with Paragard during clinical trials. Delayed detection or removal of the copper IUD in cases of uterine perforation may result in migration outside the uterine cavity, which may cause adhesions, peritonitis, intestinal penetration, GI obstruction, abscesses and/or damage to adjacent organs. A postmarketing safety study demonstrated an increased risk of uterine perforation in lactating IUD recipients for Paragard and in postpartum and lactating individuals for Miudella. The risk of perforation may be increased if an IUD is inserted when the uterus is fixed, retroverted or not completely involuted during the postpartum period. If perforation is suspected or occurs, locate and remove the copper IUD promptly or as soon as possible. Surgery may be required. Preoperative imaging followed by laparoscopy or laparotomy may be required to remove the copper IUD from the peritoneal cavity.

Description
  Text
  Intrauterine copper contraceptives are nonhormonal intrauterine devices (IUDs). Paragard is a T-shaped copper IUD product and is indicated for prevention of pregnancy for up to 10 years in people who can become pregnant. Another copper IUD, Miudella, is indicated for prevention of pregnancy for up to 3 years. A copper IUD is a highly effective form of contraception, and may be used in appropriately selected nulliparous and parous patients, including individuals who are breast-feeding. Copper IUDs were initially FDA-approved in 1984.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Copper continuously released into the uterine cavity contributes to the contraceptive effectiveness of intrauterine copper. Mechanism(s) by which copper enhances contraceptive efficacy include interference with sperm transport and fertilization of an egg.

    General Administration Information
The intrauterine copper device (IUD) (Paragard and Miudella) is for insertion into the uterus and should only be administered by health care providers specifically trained in the applicable insertion procedures.
Miudella is available only through a restricted program under a Risk Evaluation and Mitigation Strategy (REMS).
Perform a bi-manual exam to establish the size, shape, and position of the uterus, rule out abnormalities that would preclude safe placement, and exclude pregnancy (consider the possibility of ovulation and conception) prior to use.
Follow all instructions for the IUD device regarding preparation of the device and insertion technique.
Counsel recipient on risks and warnings before IUD placement; advise the patient to read the IUD patient labeling.
 
Preparation
General Preparation Information
Consider administration of analgesics to recipient prior to insertion. Expert guidance is available regarding options for reducing pain during IUD insertion, including the use of lidocaine.
Insertion of IUD may be associated with pain, bleeding, vasovagal reactions (syncope, bradycardia), or seizures, especially in people with a predisposition to these conditions.
If cervical stenosis is encountered, avoid undue force. Dilators and analgesia/local anesthesia may be helpful.
 
Paragard
Verify the expiration date of the IUD device prior to insertion.
Do not remove the inserter from the tray before the arms are loaded into the insertion tube. The tray will be used to fold the IUD arms down and load the IUD into the inserter.
Use strict aseptic technique throughout preparation
Prepare placement tools, such as speculum, cotton swab, tenaculum, uterine sound, scissors, and forceps.
Insert a speculum and, using a cotton swab, cleanse the cervix and vagina with an antiseptic solution.
Apply a tenaculum to the cervix and use gentle traction to align the cervical canal with the uterine cavity.
Gently insert a sterile uterine sound to measure the depth of the uterine cavity.
The uterus should sound a depth of 6 to 9 cm, except when inserting Paragard immediately postabortion or immediately postpartum. Insertion into the uterine cavity measuring less than 6 cm may increase the incidence of expulsion, bleeding, pain, and perforation.
 
Miudella
Verify the expiration date of the IUD device prior to insertion. Do not open the package until required for insertion; do not use if the seal of the sterile package is broken or appears compromised.
The IUD is provided preloaded in a sterile single-use inserter for placement.
Ensure use of clean technique during the procedure.
Gently insert a speculum to visualize the cervix.
Thoroughly cleanse the cervix with chlorhexidine or povidone-iodine.
Apply a sterile tenaculum to the cervix. Apply traction with the tenaculum. Gently advance a sterile uterine sound to the fundus. Note any uterine anomaly and the depth in centimeters.
 
Administration
General Administration Information
Follow IUD insertion instructions exactly as described to ensure proper placement.
Timing of IUD insertion based on clinical status:
Starting the IUD in those persons not currently using hormonal or intrauterine contraception: The IUD can be inserted any time during the menstrual cycle.
Switching to the IUD from an oral, transdermal, vaginal hormonal contraceptive, or injectable progestin contraceptive: The IUD may be inserted at any time during the menstrual cycle; discontinue the previous method.
Switching to the IUD from a contraceptive implant or use of another IUD: Insert the IUD on the same day the implant or IUD is removed (insert at any time during the menstrual cycle).
Inserting the IUD after abortion or miscarriage (Paragard): Immediately after abortion, although immediate placement has a slightly higher risk of expulsion than placement at other times. Insertion after second trimester abortion is associated with a higher risk of expulsion than insertion after a first trimester abortion.
Inserting the IUD after childbirth (Paragard): May insert immediately postpartum. Insertion before uterine involution is complete, which may not occur until the second postpartum month, has been associated with increased risk of expulsion. There appears to be an increased risk of uterine perforation in lactating individuals.
Inserting the IUD after childbirth (Miudella): The IUD should be placed no earlier than 4 weeks post-pregnancy to mitigate the risk of expulsion that may be increased when the uterus is not completely involuted at the time of insertion.
The retrieval threads should be visible after insertion.
Placement can be checked with ultrasound.
Examine the IUD recipient after the first menses following insertion to confirm the IUD is still in place.
 
Removal
General Removal Information
Remove the IUD by applying gentle traction on the exposed threads with forceps.
The arms of the system will fold upward as it is withdrawn from the uterus.
Make sure the IUD is intact upon removal.
Never reinsert and/or reuse a removed IUD.
Removal may be associated with pain and/or bleeding, vasovagal reactions, such as syncope or bradycardia, or seizures, especially in patients with a predisposition to these conditions.
 
Paragard
May be removed at any time prior to 10 years after insertion.
Remove no later than 10 years after insertion. A new Paragard may be inserted at the time of removal if continued contraceptive protection is desired.
If removal cannot be accomplished by gentle pulling, consider checking Paragard location and assess for embedment and perforation, with imaging if necessary.
Breakage or embedment of Paragard in the myometrium can make removal difficult.
Analgesia, paracervical anesthesia, cervical dilation, alligator forceps or other grasping instrument, or hysteroscopy may assist in removing an embedded Paragard.
 
Miudella
May be removed at any time prior to 3 years after insertion.
Remove no later than 3 years after insertion.
After removal, a new Miudella may be inserted if continued contraceptive protection is desired.
If pregnancy is not desired and the patient is not having a new IUD or implant placed that day, remove Miudella during the first 7 days of the menstrual cycle if the patient is experiencing regular menses.
If removal will occur at other times during the cycle, or the patient does not experience regular menses, start a new contraceptive method 1 week prior to removal.
If the threads are not visible, determine the location of Miudella by ultrasound.
If Miudella is found in the uterine cavity on ultrasound, it may be removed using a narrow forceps, such as alligator forceps.
Consider use of local anesthetic for a paracervical block if cervical dilation is required.
After removal, examine the system to ensure that it is intact.

Maximum Dosage Limits
  Age Groups
    Name
    Adults
    Max Dosage
    Paragard: 1 copper intrauterine device (IUD) every 10 years.
Miudella: 1 copper intrauterine device (IUD) every 3 years.
    Name
    Geriatric
    Max Dosage
    Safety and efficacy have not been established.
    Name
    Adolescents
    Max Dosage
    Paragard: 1 copper intrauterine device (IUD) every 10 years. While not specifically studied in those younger than 18 years, safety and efficacy are expected to be the same for postpubertal adolescents as for adults.
Miudella: 1 copper intrauterine device (IUD) every 3 years. While not specifically studied in those younger than 18 years, safety and efficacy are expected to be the same for postpubertal adolescents as for adults.
    Name
    Children
    Max Dosage
    Not indicated in prepubescent individuals.

Dosage Adjustment Guidelines
  Hepatic Impairment
  Specific guidelines for dosage adjustments in hepatic impairment are not available; it appears that no dosage adjustments are needed.
  Renal Impairment
  Specific guidelines for dosage adjustments in renal impairment are not available; it appears that no dosage adjustments are needed.
