    General Information
    Calamine; pramoxine is compatible with breast-feeding. There are no data on the presence of calamine or pramoxine in human milk. No adverse reactions have been reported in breast-feeding individuals using topically applied pramoxine. Direct ingestion of pramoxine has caused nausea and vomiting, but no serious toxicity. If calamine; pramoxine is applied to the chest area, advise breast-feeding individuals to wipe it off thoroughly prior to breast-feeding.

    Some patients may rarely develop hypersensitivity reactions to pramoxine, such as allergic contact dermatitis; however, since this anesthetic is chemically distinct from ester and amide local anesthetics, it appears less likely to cause cross-sensitivity reactions than some other agents.  Discontinue calamine; pramoxine use if symptoms worsen or do not improve within 7 days.

Description
  Text
  Calamine; pramoxine is a topical combination of a skin protectant and analgesic indicated for the temporary relief of pain or itching associated with rashes due to poison ivy, oak, or sumac, insect stings, or minor skin irritation or cuts. It also dries the oozing and weeping of poison ivy, oak, or sumac. Pramoxine is not structurally related to ester or amide local anesthetics; its unique chemical structure is likely to minimize the risk of cross-sensitivity reactions in patients allergic to other anesthetics.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Calamine lotion contains zinc oxide or zinc carbonate and is used frequently for symptomatic relief in pruritus because of its soothing properties. Zinc also inhibits mast cell degranulation and thereby reduces the secretion of histamine, an important mediator of inflammatory response and an inducer of itch. Pramoxine is a topical anesthetic and acts by stabilizing the neuronal membrane of nerve endings with which it comes into contact. Pramoxine is not structurally related to ester or amide local anesthetics; its unique chemical structure is likely to minimize the risk of cross-sensitivity reactions in patients allergic to other anesthetics.
