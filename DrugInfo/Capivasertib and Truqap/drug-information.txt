          Heading
          Adults
          Dosage Description
          400 mg PO twice daily on days 1, 2, 3, and 4 followed by 3 days off, every 7 days, in combination with fulvestrant 500 mg IM on days 1, 15, 29 and every 28 days thereafter. Continue treatment until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity. Consider adding a luteinizing hormone-releasing hormone (LHRH) agonist for men and for pre- and perimenopausal women according to current clinical practice standards. Coadministration of certain drugs may need to be avoided or dosage adjustments may be necessary; review drug interactions. In a multicenter, randomized, double-blind, phase 3 clinical trial (CAPItello-291), treatment with capivasertib plus fulvestrant significantly improved the median progression-free survival (PFS) compared with placebo plus fulvestrant in patients with HR-positive, HER2-negative, locally advanced or metastatic breast cancer with eligible PIK3CA/AKT1/PTEN alterations and progression on an aromatase inhibitor (AI) based treatment in the metastatic setting or recurrence on or within 12 months of completing neoadjuvant or adjuvant therapy with an AI (7.3 months vs. 3.1 months). The median objective response rate was 26% versus 8%, respectively (complete response, 2.3% vs. 0%), with a median duration of response of 10.2 months in the fulvestrant arm and 8.6 months in the placebo arm. Overall survival results were immature.

  Contraindications And Precautions
    Headline
    Drug Interactions
    General Information
    The coadministration of certain medications may lead to harm and require avoidance or therapy modification; review all drug interactions prior to concomitant use of other medications.
    Headline
    Child-Pugh class B, Child-Pugh class C, hepatic failure
    General Information
    Monitor patients with moderate hepatic impairment at baseline (Child-Pugh class B; or bilirubin greater than 1.5 to 3 times the upper limit of normal [ULN] and any AST) for adverse reactions related to potentially increased capivasertib exposure. Capivasertib has not been studied in patients with severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh class C; or bilirubin greater than 3 times ULN and any AST) or hepatic failure.
    Headline
    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components.
    Headline
    diabetes mellitus, hyperglycemia
    General Information
    Use capivasertib cautiously in patients with diabetes mellitus; severe hyperglycemia associated with ketoacidosis occurred in patients treated with capivasertib in clinical trials. The safety of capivasertib treatment has not been established in patients with Type 1 diabetes or Type 2 diabetes that is not controlled or requiring insulin as these patients were excluded from clinical trials. Patients with well-controlled Type 2 diabetes may require intensified antihyperglycemic treatment and closer monitoring of fasting blood glucose (FG). Consider consultation with a healthcare provider specializing in hyperglycemia management for patients with risk factors for hyperglycemia or for those who experience hyperglycemia. Evaluate FG and A1C at baseline and optimize blood glucose prior to treatment. Monitor (or have patients self-monitor) FG on days 3 or 4 of the dosing week during weeks 1, 2, 4, 6, 8, and then monthly during treatment and as clinically indicated; monitor A1C every 3 months during treatment and as clinically indicated. If hyperglycemia occurs, increase FG monitoring to at least twice weekly, on days on and off capivasertib, until it returns to baseline; if antihyperglycemic medications are needed, monitor FG at least once weekly for 2 months, followed by once every 2 weeks or as clinically indicated. Educate patients about the signs and symptoms of hyperglycemia and lifestyle changes. An interruption of therapy, dose reduction, or discontinuation of therapy may be necessary if hyperglycemia occurs, based on the severity.
    Headline
    infection
    General Information
    Use capivasertib with caution in patients with an active infection; treatment can result in myelosuppression, which may increase the severity of an infection and impede recovery.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Pregnancy should be avoided during capivasertib treatment and for at least 1 month after the last dose. Although there are no adequately controlled studies in people who may become pregnant, capivasertib can cause fetal harm or death when administered during pregnancy based on its mechanism of action and animal studies. In an animal reproduction study, oral administration of capivasertib to pregnant rats during organogenesis resulted in adverse developmental outcomes including embryofetal death (postimplantation loss), reduced fetal weights, and minor fetal visceral variations at a dose equivalent to approximately 0.7 times the human exposure at the recommended dose based on AUC. Individuals who are pregnant or who may become pregnant while receiving capivasertib should be apprised of the potential hazard to the fetus.
    Headline
    people who can cause pregnancy in others, people who may become pregnant, reproductive risk
    General Information
    Counsel patients about the reproductive risk associated with capivasertib. People who may become pregnant should avoid pregnancy and use effective contraception during and for at least 1 month after the last dose of capivasertib. People who can cause pregnancy in others should use effective contraception during treatment and for 4 months after the last dose. People who may become pregnant should undergo pregnancy testing prior to initiation of capivasertib. Individuals who become pregnant while receiving capivasertib should be apprised of the potential hazard to the fetus.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in nursing infants from capivasertib, advise individuals to discontinue breast-feeding during treatment. It is not known whether capivasertib is present in human milk, although many drugs are excreted in human milk.

Interactions
Abacavir; Dolutegravir; lamiVUDine: (Moderate) Monitor for increased toxicity of dolutegravir if coadministered with capivasertib. Concurrent use may increase the plasma concentrations of dolutegravir. Dolutegravir is a substrate of UGT1A1; capivasertib is an UGT1A1 inhibitor.
Acetaminophen; Caffeine; Dihydrocodeine: (Moderate) Concomitant use of dihydrocodeine with capivasertib may alter dihydrocodeine plasma concentrations, resulting in an unpredictable effect such as reduced efficacy or symptoms of opioid withdrawal or prolonged opioid adverse reactions, including hypotension, respiratory depression, profound sedation, coma, and death. If coadministration is necessary, monitor patients closely at frequent intervals and consider a dosage adjustment of dihydrocodeine until stable drug effects are achieved. Discontinuation of capivasertib could alter dihydrocodeine plasma concentrations, resulting in an unpredictable effect such as prolonged opioid adverse reactions or decreased opioid efficacy, and potentially lead to a withdrawal syndrome in those with physical dependence to codeine. If capivasertib is discontinued, monitor the patient carefully and consider adjusting the opioid dosage if appropriate. Dihydrocodeine is primarily metabolized by CYP2D6 to dihydromorphine, and by CYP3A; capivasertib is a dual moderate 2D6 and weak 3A inhibitor. CYP3A inhibitors may increase codeine-related adverse effects while CYP2D6 inhibitors may reduce efficacy.
Acetaminophen; Codeine: (Moderate) Concomitant use of codeine with capivasertib may alter codeine plasma concentrations, resulting in an unpredictable effect such as reduced efficacy or symptoms of opioid withdrawal or prolonged opioid adverse reactions, including hypotension, respiratory depression, profound sedation, coma, and death. It is recommended to avoid this combination when codeine is being used for cough. If coadministration is necessary, monitor patients closely at frequent intervals and consider a dosage adjustment of codeine until stable drug effects are achieved. Discontinuation of capivasertib could alter codeine plasma concentrations, resulting in an unpredictable effect such as prolonged opioid adverse reactions or decreased opioid efficacy, and potentially lead to a withdrawal syndrome in those with physical dependence to codeine. If capivasertib is discontinued, monitor the patient carefully and consider adjusting the opioid dosage if appropriate. Codeine is primarily metabolized by CYP2D6 to morphine, and by CYP3A to norcodeine; norcodeine does not have analgesic properties. Capivasertib is a dual moderate CYP2D6 and weak CYP3A inhibitor. CYP3A inhibitors may increase codeine-related adverse effects while CYP2D6 inhibitors may reduce efficacy.

    General Information
    Patients aged 65 years and older may have reduced tolerance to capivasertib therapy and should be monitored closely for adverse reactions. In 2 clinical trials, older patients treated with capivasertib plus either fulvestrant or abiraterone plus prednisone experienced higher rates of grade 3 to 5 adverse reactions (57% to 77% vs. 36% to 60%), dose reductions (30% to 36% vs. 15% to 24%), treatment interruptions (57% to 72% vs. 30% to 53%), and permanent discontinuations (23% to 27% vs. 7% to 8%) compared with patients younger than 65 years.
    Headline
    diabetic ketoacidosis, hyperglycemia
    General Information
    Hyperglycemia has been reported in patients who received capivasertib, with an increase in random glucose levels from baseline reported in 58% of patients treated with capivasertib plus fulvestrant compared with 17% of those who received placebo plus fulvestrant (grade 3 or 4, 9% vs. 0%) in a randomized clinical trial; an increase in fasting glucose occurred in 37% versus 29% of patients, respectively (grade 3 or 4, 0.6% vs. 0%). Increases in non-fasting glucose also occurred more often in patients treated with capivasertib plus abiraterone and prednisone compared with those who received placebo plus abiraterone and prednisone in another clinical trial (49% vs. 31%; grade 3 or 4, 23% vs. 2.3%); increases in fasting glucose occurred in 70% versus 50% of patients, respectively (grade 3 or 4, 14% vs. 3%). Diabetic ketoacidosis occurred in 0.3% to 1.2% of patients who received capivasertib plus either fulvestrant or abiraterone/prednisone. The median time to onset of hyperglycemia was 15 days with capivasertib and fulvestrant; it was 72 days with capivasertib and abiraterone/prednisone. Diabetic metabolic decompensation was reported 0.6% of patients who received capivasertib plus prednisone. An exposure-response relationship was observed for grade 3 or 4 hyperglycemia at doses ranging from 0.2 to 2 times the recommended dose. The safety of capivasertib treatment has not been established in patients with Type 1 diabetes mellitus or Type 2 diabetes that is uncontrolled or requiring insulin. An interruption of therapy, dose reduction, or discontinuation of therapy may be necessary depending on the severity of hyperglycemia; permanently discontinue capivasertib if ketoacidosis occurs.

Description
  Text
  Capivasertib inhibits AKT1, AKT2, and AKT3 and phosphorylation of downstream AKT substrates. It is indicated, in combination with fulvestrant, for the treatment of hormone receptor (HR)-positive, HER2-negative, locally advanced or metastatic breast cancer with one or more PIK3CA/AKT1/PTEN-alterations following progression on at least one endocrine-based regimen in the metastatic setting or recurrence on or within 12 months of completing adjuvant therapy. It is also indicated for the treatment of metastatic PTEN-deficient, androgen pathway modulation-naive or -sensitive (mAPMN/S) prostate cancer, in combination with abiraterone and prednisone. Severe hyperglycemia and diabetic ketoacidosis occurred in patients treated with capivasertib in clinical trials. The safety of capivasertib treatment has not been established in patients with Type 1 diabetes mellitus or Type 2 diabetes that is uncontrolled or requiring insulin; patients with insulin-dependent diabetes mellitus were excluded from clinical studies. Capivasertib was approved by the FDA in November 2023.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Capivasertib inhibits all 3 isoforms of serine/threonine kinase AKT (AKT1, AKT2, and AKT3) and inhibits phosphorylation of downstream AKT substrates. AKT is a kinase in the phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase (PIK3)/AKT/phosphatase and tensin homolog (PTEN) signaling pathway.
Breast Cancer
Overactivation of the AKT pathway occurs in approximately half of hormone receptor (HR)-positive, HER2-negative breast cancers as a result of the activation of upstream signaling pathways, mutations in AKT1, loss of PTEN function, and mutations in the catalytic subunit alpha of PIK3CA. In vitro, capivasertib reduced the growth of breast cancer cell lines including those with relevant PIK3CA or AKT1 mutations or PTEN alteration. In vivo, capivasertib, alone and in combination with fulvestrant, inhibited tumor growth of mouse xenograft models including estrogen receptor positive breast cancer models with alterations in PIK3CA, AKT1, and PTEN.
Prostate Cancer
In vitro, capivasertib reduced growth of androgen receptor-positive PTEN-deficient prostate cancer cell lines. In vivo, capivasertib inhibited tumor growth in mouse xenograft models bearing PTEN-deficient prostate cancer cells and demonstrated increased antitumor activity when used in combination with abiraterone.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Oral Route
    Description
    The geometric mean Cmax of capivasertib at steady-state is 1,247 nanog/mL (CV, 28%); the geometric mean AUC at steady-state is 7,356 hours x nanog/mL (CV, 38%). Plasma concentrations are approximately 0.2% to 5.1% of the steady-state Cmax during off-dosing days. Steady-state concentrations are predicted to be attained on the third and fourth dosing day of each week, beginning on week 2. The time to maximum concentration (Tmax) is approximately 1 to 2 hours and the absolute bioavailability is 29%. The Cmax and AUC of capivasertib are dose-proportional over a range of 80 to 800 mg (0.2 to 2 times the approved recommended dosage).
Neither a high-fat meal (approximately 1,000 kcal, 60% fat) nor a low-fat meal (approximately 400 kcal, 26% fat) had a clinically meaningful effect on the pharmacokinetics of capivasertib.

  Text
  Capivasertib is administered orally. It is 78% bound to plasma proteins; the plasma-to-blood ratio is 0.71. The steady-state oral volume of distribution is 1,847 liters (CV, 36%). The half-life of capivasertib is 8.3 hours and the steady-state oral clearance is 50 liters/hour (CV, 37%); renal clearance was 21% of the total clearance. After a single radiolabeled 400-mg oral dose, the mean total recovery was 45% from the urine and 50% from the feces.
Pharmacodynamics
Increased capivasertib exposure is associated with increased incidence of grade 2 to 4 diarrhea, rash, and hyperglycemia at 0.2 to 2 times the approved recommended dosage.
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes and drug transporters: CYP3A4, UGT2B7, CYP2D6, UGT1A1, BCRP, OATP1B1, OATP1B3, OAT3, MATE1, MATE2-K, and OCT2
Capivasertib is primarily metabolized by CYP3A4 and UGT2B7. In vitro, it is also a weak CYP3A inhibitor, a moderate inhibitor of CYP2D6, and a UGT1A1 inhibitor. In vitro, capivasertib induces CYP1A2 and inhibits BCRP, OATP1B1, OATP1B3, OAT3, MATE1, MATE2-K, and OCT2.

  Route Specific Administrations

    Oral Administration
    Description
    Swallow tablets whole. Do not chew, crush, or split tablets; do not take tablets that are broken, cracked, or otherwise not intact.
Capivasertib tablets may be dispensed in the original bottle or in a USP equivalent tight container. If not dispensed in the original bottle, advise patients to discard the medication after 45 days.
If a patient misses a dose within 4 hours of the scheduled time, they can take the missed dose. If it is more than 4 hours after the scheduled time, the patient should skip the missed dose and take the next dose at its usually scheduled time.
If a patient vomits a dose, they should not take an additional dose. They may take the next dose at its usual scheduled time.
