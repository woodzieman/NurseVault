Drug Class: Diphtheria toxoid, tetanus toxoid, acellular pertussis vaccine, DTaP; hepatitis B vaccine, recombinant; inactivated poliovirus vaccine, IPV (Pediarix) is a combination vaccine that protects against 5 diseases: diphtheria, tetanus, pertussis, hepatitis B, and poliomyelitis.

Routes: intramuscular; Common adult doses: Do not use.

Common Side Effects (excluding routine GI upset): apnea, cough, dyspnea, infection, fever, hypotonia, inconsolable crying, insomnia, irritability, restlessness, alopecia, anaphylactoid reactions, angioedema, erythema multiforme, headache, pruritus, rash, serum sickness, stevens-johnson syndrome, urticaria, elevated hepatic enzymes, intussusception, drowsiness, fatigue, lethargy

Indication Specific Dosing
  General Information
  General dosing information:
Pediarix is not recommended for completion of the first 3 doses of the DTaP vaccination series if the series was initiated with a DTaP vaccine from a different manufacturer. May use Pediarix if a patient has received Infanrix.
Pediarix may be used to complete the hepatitis B vaccination series in infants and children regardless of the manufacturer of the previous vaccine.
Pediarix may be used to complete the first 3 doses of the IPV series in infants who have received an IPV dose(s) from a different manufacturer.
Pediarix is not recommended as a booster vaccination.
Interruption of the recommended schedule should not interfere with the final immunity achieved by Pediarix. Regardless of the time elapsed between doses, there is no need to start the series over again.

        Dosage Description
        0.5 mL IM at intervals of 6 to 8 weeks (preferably 8 weeks) for 3 doses, ideally given at 2, 4, and 6 months of age. Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  General Information
  Prior to administration, health care personnel should inform the patient, parent, guardian, or responsible adult of the vaccine's benefits and risks. This should include the provision of the vaccine information statement from the manufacturer. The responsible adult should report any adverse reaction following vaccine administration to the health care provider. The U.S. Department of Health and Human Services has established a Vaccine Adverse Event Reporting System (VAERS) to accept all reports of suspected adverse events after the administration of any vaccine. This includes, but is not limited to, the reporting of events required by the National Childhood Vaccine Injury Act of 1986. The toll-free number for VAERS is 800—822—7967.
 
For known exposures to hepatitis B virus (HBV), the hepatitis B immune globulin (HBIG) should be given in combination with the appropriate vaccine in accordance with the ACIP recommendations. Diphtheria toxoid, tetanus toxoid, acellular pertussis vaccine, DTaP; Hepatitis B vaccine, recombinant; inactivated poliovirus vaccine, IPV may not prevent HBV infection in those with an unrecognized illness at the time of vaccination. Additionally, it may not prevent HBV infection if adequate antibody titers are not achieved.
 
Do not give Diphtheria toxoid, tetanus toxoid, acellular pertussis vaccine, DTaP; Hepatitis B vaccine, recombinant; inactivated poliovirus vaccine, IPV via intravenous administration, intraarterial administration, subcutaneous administration, or intradermal administration. Patients with thrombocytopenia, vitamin K deficiency, a coagulopathy (e.g., hemophilia), thrombocytopenia, or those receiving anticoagulant therapy should be monitored closely when given Pediarix because bleeding can occur at the IM injection site.
 
Pediarix vaccine is not recommended for use in the elderly or any other adult populations. This vaccine is not recommended for any adults, adolescents, or children >= 7 years of age. Safety and efficacy in neonates or infants =40.5 degrees C (105 degrees F) within 48 hours not due to another identifiable cause; collapse or shock like state (hypotonic-hyporesponsive episode) within 48 hours; persistent, inconsolable crying lasting >=3 hours, occurring within 48 hours; and convulsions with or without fever occurring within 3 days.
 
Pediarix is contraindicated in patients who have experienced encephalopathy (coma, decreased level of consciousness, prolonged seizures) within 7 days of administration of a previous dose of a pertussis-containing vaccine that is not attributable to another cause. Pediarix is also contraindicated in patients with a progressive neurological disease including infantile spasms, an uncontrolled seizure disorder, or progressive encephalopathy; do not administer the pertussis vaccine to patients with these conditions until a treatment regimen has been established and the condition has stabilized. The ACIP recognizes that children with a personal or family history of seizure disorder may have an increased seizure risk following immunization with a vaccine containing DTP (e.g. Pediarix) versus children without such histories. The ACIP recognizes that vaccination of infants or children with stable neurological disease, including well-controlled seizures, may not contraindicate immunization with a vaccine containing DTP. Consideration of deferral of vaccination is recommended in unstable, deteriorating, or progressive neurologic disorders, and ACIP and AAP guidelines should be reviewed. If the decision to administer Pediarix is made, the parent or guardian should be informed of the potential risks (see Adverse Reactions). For children at higher risk of seizures than the general population, the ACIP recommends that acetaminophen or ibuprofen be administered at the time of vaccination and for 24 hours after to reduce the possibility of postvaccination fever.
 
If Guillain-Barre syndrome occurs within 6 weeks of receipt of prior vaccine containing tetanus toxoid, the decision to give subsequent doses of Pediarix or any vaccine containing tetanus toxoid should be based on careful consideration of the potential benefits and possible risks.
 
Immunosuppression can cause a decreased response to tetanus toxoid. Patients on immunosuppressive therapy including chemotherapy, who are receiving radiation therapy, or who have other immunodeficiency may need to delay therapy with Pediarix because of decreased response. Patients with HIV infection should receive immunization.
 
Pediarix is classified as FDA pregnancy risk category C. Administration of Pediarix during pregnancy is not recommended. Animal reproduction studies have not been conducted with this vaccine; the risk of Pediarix vaccination to a human fetus or reproductive capacity is unknown. Pregnant patients who require tetanus-diphtheria immunization or boosters, should refer to the tetanus/diphtheria (DT) toxoids (adult) product recommendations for use in pregnancy. There is no information on the excretion of Pediarix in breast milk during breast-feeding.

    General Information

Adverse Reactions
  Headline
  Adverse Reactions
  General Information
  The most common adverse reactions to Pediarix™ include injection site reaction (usually erythema, swelling and tenderness), fussiness, and somnolence (drowsiness). As with other vaccines that use aluminum as an adjuvant, a small nodule may be palpable at the vaccination site for several weeks. Other common adverse reactions to Pediarix™ include restlessness (up to 40%), unusual crying (up to 25%), and anorexia (up to 18%). These reactions did not occur more frequently with Pediarix™ than with separately administered vaccines. Injection site reactions (including cellulitis, induration, itching, nodule/lump, pain, redness, swelling, and warmth at the site of injection), limb pain, limb swelling, anaphylactic reaction, anaphylactoid reaction, hypersensitivity, apnea, dyspnea, angioedema, erythema, rash, urticaria, pallor, and petechiae have been reported during the post-marketing surveillance program. The events have a suspected causal connection to components of Pediarix™ or other vaccines or drugs, but a reliable estimate of their frequency or establishment a causal relationship to vaccine exposure is not possible because these events are reported voluntarily from a population of uncertain size.When compared to separately administered vaccines, Pediarix™ was found to have a higher incidence of fever (28% vs 20%). The prevalence of fever appears to be highest on the day of vaccination and the following day. The majority of episodes of fever (> 98%) resolved within the 4-day period following vaccination.Adverse events reported during the post-marketing surveillance program for Pediarix™ include cyanosis, diarrhea, vomiting, Sudden Infant Death Syndrome, upper respiratory tract infection, abnormal liver function tests, bulging fontanelle, insomnia, irritability, and nervousness. Because Pediarix™ is a combination vaccine, it is possible for patients to experience adverse effects that have previously been reported following administration of its separate components. Worldwide voluntary reports of adverse effects received for Infanrix® and Engerix-B® in children younger than 7 year of age for which 20 or more reports were received include asthenia, lethargy, malaise, somnolence, Sudden Infant Death Syndrome (SIDS), cyanosis, edema, pallor, abdominal pain, diarrhea, nausea/vomiting, lymphadenopathy, thrombocytopenia, jaundice, elevated hepatic enzymes, cellulitis, arthralgia, limb swelling, headache, hypotonia, respiratory tract infection, erythema multiforme, petechiae, pruritus, rash (unspecified), urticaria and otalgia. Adverse effects for which fewer than 20 reports were received include intussusception, idiopathic thrombocytopenia purpura, anaphylactic shock, angioedema, encephalopathy, hypotonic-hyporesponsive episode, and alopecia. This list includes serious adverse events or events that have a suspected causal connection to components of Pediarix™ or other vaccines or drugs. Description
  Text
  Diphtheria toxoid, tetanus toxoid, acellular pertussis vaccine, DTaP; hepatitis B vaccine, recombinant; inactivated poliovirus vaccine, IPV (Pediarix) is a combination vaccine that protects against 5 diseases: diphtheria, tetanus, pertussis, hepatitis B, and poliomyelitis. It is approved as a 3-dose primary series in infants born of hepatitis B surface antigen (HBsAg)-negative mothers. Pediarix may be used to complete the first 3 doses of the DTaP series in children who have received 1 or 2 doses of Infanrix, to complete the hepatitis B series after 1 or 2 prior hepatitis B vaccine doses, and to complete the first 3 doses of the IPV series in children who previously received 1 or 2 doses of IPV from another manufacturer. Pediarix is contraindicated in individuals with severe allergic reaction after a previous dose of any diphtheria toxoid-, tetanus toxoid-, pertussis-, hepatitis B-, or poliovirus-containing vaccine or to any vaccine component, encephalopathy within 7 days after a previous pertussis-containing vaccine, or progressive neurologic disorders until neurologic status is clarified and stabilized. In clinical studies, immune responses to diphtheria, tetanus, pertussis, and poliovirus were non-inferior to those achieved with separately administered vaccines. Common adverse reactions included local injection-site reactions such as pain, redness, and swelling, and systemic reactions including fever, drowsiness, irritability or fussiness, and loss of appetite; rates of fever were higher with the Pediarix vaccine than with separately administered vaccines. The administration of combination vaccines, such as Pediarix, can reduce the number of injections individuals receive and may improve vaccination coverage, timeliness, and may reduce cost for extra health care visits. The Pediarix vaccine was FDA-approved on December 13, 2002.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Diphtheria toxoid, tetanus toxoid, acellular pertussis vaccine, DTaP; hepatitis B vaccine, recombinant; inactivated poliovirus vaccine, IPV (Pediarix) provides protection against diphtheria, tetanus, pertussis (whooping cough), hepatitis B, and poliomyelitis.
Diphtheria: Corynebacterium diphtheriae infections are mediated by diphtheria exotoxin, an extracellular protein metabolite of toxigenic strains of C. diphtheriae. Diphtheria toxoid induces the production of neutralizing antibodies against the exotoxin. A serum diphtheria antitoxin concentration of at least 0.1 International Units/mL is considered protective.
Tetanus: Exotoxin released by Clostridium tetani causes the neuromuscular dysfunction associated with tetanus. The tetanus toxoid contains antigens that induce the production of antibodies against the exotoxin. A serum tetanus antitoxin concentration of 0.01 International Units/mL is considered the minimum protective concentration, and 0.1 International Units/mL is generally regarded as protective.
Pertussis: Pertussis, or whooping cough, is a disease of the respiratory tract caused by Bordetella pertussis. The acellular pertussis vaccine contains pertussis antigens, including pertussis toxin, filamentous hemagglutinin, and pertactin, which induce antibody responses against these antigens; a serological correlate of protection against pertussis has not been established.
Hepatitis B: The hepatitis B component induces antibody to hepatitis B surface antigen, and antibody concentrations of 10 milli-International Units/mL or greater are recognized as conferring protection against hepatitis B virus infection.
Poliomyelitis: Immunization with inactivated poliovirus vaccine (IPV) stimulates the immune system to produce neutralizing anti-poliovirus antibodies against each of the 3 poliovirus serotypes (Types 1, 2, and 3).

Pharmacokinetics
  Special Populations
    Name
    Pediatrics
    Description
    Infants 2 to 6 months
The immunogenicity of Pediarix was evaluated in pediatric patients who received the vaccine at 2, 4, and 6 months of age (n = 154 to 168). Infants received either Pediarix concomitantly with Hib conjugate vaccine and PCV7 (n = 154 to 168) or separately administered Infanrix, Engerix-B, and IPV concomitantly with the same Hib and PCV7 vaccines (n = 141 to 155). Immune responses were evaluated 1 month after the third dose with a sampling range of 20 to 60 days. Adjusted geometric mean antibody concentrations for pertussis antigens (including pertussis toxin (PT), pertactin, and filamentous hemagglutinin (FHA)), and seroprotection rates for diphtheria, tetanus, and poliovirus were non-inferior to those of the individual vaccine components. The following antibody responses were observed for Pediarix versus separate vaccine administration: anti-diphtheria toxoid 0.1 International Units/mL or greater (99.4 % Pediarix vs. 98.7% individual vaccines), anti-tetanus toxoid 0.1 International Units/mL or greater (100% vs. 98.1%), anti-pertussis response of 5 ELISA units/mL or greater in seronegative infants or at least maintenance of pre-vaccination concentration in seropositive infants, with anti-PT (98.7% vs. 95.1%), anti-FHA (98.7% vs. 96.5%), anti-pertactin (91.7% vs. 95.1%), and anti-polio types 1, 2, and 3 neutralizing antibody titer 1:8 or greater (100% vs. 100% for all). Anti-hepatitis B surface antigen concentrations of 10 milli-international units/mL or greater occurred in 97.7% and 99.2% of those immunized with Pediarix and the separate vaccine group, respectively. Because of differences in the hepatitis B vaccination schedule among individuals in the study, no clinical limit for non-inferiority was pre-defined for the hepatitis B immune response; however, in a previous U.S. study, non-inferiority of Pediarix relative to separately administered Infanrix, Engerix-B, and an oral poliovirus vaccine with respect to the hepatitis B immune response was demonstrated.

Administration
  General Information
  For storage information, see the specific product information within the How Supplied section.
Verify immunization status in the state or local immunization information system in addition to the health care electronic record. If this vaccine has been previously given, question the patient, parent, or guardian about any symptoms or signs of an adverse reaction.
Advise individuals on the benefits and risks of the vaccine. Provide a copy of the Vaccine Information Statement (VIS).
Record manufacturer, lot number, administration date, and the name and address of the person administering the vaccine.
Ensure supportive care, including epinephrine 1 mg/mL injection, is readily accessible in the event of a clinically significant hypersensitivity reaction.
Complete a Vaccine Adverse Event Reporting System (VAERS) report if adverse events have been identified. The VAERS toll-free information line is 1-800-822-7967 and email address is info@vaers.org.
