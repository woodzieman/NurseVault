        Heading
        Adults
        Dosage Description
        Infuse the cultured fraction (minimum dose of 8 X 108 total viable cells containing a minimum of 9.2 X 107 CD34+ cells) followed by the noncultured fraction (minimum dose of 4 X 108 total viable cells containing a minimum of 2.4 X 107 CD3+ cells); this is considered a single dose. Premedicate patients with acetaminophen 650 mg PO, an antihistamine (e.g., diphenhydramine 50 mg IV), and hydrocortisone 50 mg IV starting 30 to 60 minutes prior to the infusion. Ensure the patient is well hydrated. Hold the infusion for hypersensitivity reactions or moderate to severe infusion reactions. The time to neutrophil engraftment (primary endpoint) was significantly improved in patients with hematologic malignancies who received an allogeneic stem cell transplant with omidubicel compared with standard umbilical cord blood (UCB) (12 days vs. 22 days; p less than 0.001) in a multicenter, randomized, phase 3 trial (n = 125; Study P0501). Additionally, the rate of platelet engraftment by 42 days was significantly higher in omidubicel-treated patients (55% vs. 35%; p = 0.028). The incidence of grade 2 or 3 bacterial or invasive fungal infection within the first 100 days after transplant was 37% in the omidubicel arm and 57% in the standard UCB arm (p = 0.03). Graft-versus-host disease (GVHD) rates, treatment failure rates, and mortality rates were not significantly different between treatment arms. Of note, patients treated with omidubicel received a transplant at a median of 41 days after random assignment compared with 26 days for patients treated with standard UCB. Eligible patients (median age, 41 years; range, 13 to 65 years) had at least a 4 of 6 human leukocyte antigen (HLA) donor match. Conditioning regimens included total body irradiation and fludarabine plus thiotepa or cyclophosphamide or chemotherapy only with thiotepa, busulfan, and fludarabine. All patients received GVHD prophylaxis with a calcineurin inhibitor and mycophenolate mofetil; antibacterial prophylaxis and granulocyte-colony stimulating factors were given post transplant.

    General Information
    The coadministration of certain medications may lead to harm and require avoidance or therapy modification; review all drug interactions prior to concomitant use of other medications.
    Headline
    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components. Omidubicel is also contraindicated in individuals with known hypersensitivity to dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO), Dextran 40, gentamicin, human serum albumin, or bovine products. Monitor individuals with a history of hypersensitivity to antibiotics for allergic reactions after omidubicel administration; omidubicel may contain residual antibiotics if the cord blood donor was exposed to antibiotics in utero.
    Headline
    infusion-related reactions
    General Information
    Infusion-related reactions have occurred with omidubicel and may be fatal. Infusion-related reactions may begin within minutes of starting the omidubicel infusion and intensify and peak several hours after the completion of the infusion. Premedication is required prior to omidubicel administration. Monitor closely for signs and symptoms of infusion-related reactions; if a reaction occurs, pause the omidubicel infusion and institute supportive care as needed.
    Headline
    graft-versus-host disease
    General Information
    Acute and chronic graft-versus-host disease (GVHD) have occurred after omidubicel therapy and may be fatal. Administer immunosuppressive drugs to individuals treated with omidubicel to decrease the risk of GVHD. Monitor for signs and symptoms of GVHD and treat if GVHD develops.
    Headline
    graft failure
    General Information
    Primary and secondary graft failure has been reported after omidubicel administration. Primary graft failure, which may be fatal, is usually a result of immunologic rejection. Monitor laboratory values for evidence of hematopoietic recovery (e.g., CBC with differential).
    Headline
    engraftment syndrome
    General Information
    Because omidubicel is derived from umbilical cord blood, engraftment syndrome may occur with omidubicel in individuals who receive an allogenic umbilical cord stem cell transplant; this syndrome may progress to multiorgan failure and death. Monitor for signs and symptoms of engraftment syndrome, such as unexplained fever, rash, hypoxemia, weight gain, and pulmonary infiltrates in the peri-engraftment period. Treat with corticosteroids as soon as engraftment syndrome is recognized to ameliorate symptoms.
    Headline
    autoimmune cytopenia
    General Information
    Autoimmune cytopenia has occurred with omidubicel after treatment for severe aplastic anemia. Risk factors for post-transplant autoimmune cytopenia include younger age, antithymocyte globulin (ATG)-containing conditioning, underlying severe aplastic anemia, and delayed T-cell chimerism. Monitor complete blood count (CBC) prior to and after omidubicel infusion. Manage cytopenias according to local institutional guidelines.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    There are no available data on the use of omidubicel during pregnancy to assess the risk of major birth defects, miscarriage, or other adverse outcomes. No animal reproductive or developmental studies have been conducted. In clinical trials, one individual reported 2 pregnancies occurring 9 months and 3.5 years after transplant; no birth complications or neonatal concerns were reported. Use omidubicel during pregnancy only if the potential benefit justifies the potential risk to the fetus.
    Headline
    people who may become pregnant, reproductive risk
    General Information
    Counsel people who may become pregnant about the reproductive risk associated with omidubicel. Verify pregnancy status prior to starting the conditioning regimen for omidubicel. Follow contraception recommendations for the medications used for conditioning. There are insufficient exposure data to provide a recommendation on the duration of contraception after omidubicel treatment.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Use omidubicel with caution during breast-feeding. There is no data on the presence of omidubicel in human milk, its effects on the breastfed child, or its effects on milk production. Consider the benefits of breast-feeding, the patient's clinical need for treatment, and any potential adverse effects on the breastfed child from the medication or from the patient's underlying medical condition.

Adverse Reactions
  Headline
  Adverse Reactions
  Adverse Reactions
    Headline
    General Information
    General Information
    Omidubicel may transmit rare genetic diseases involving the hematopoietic system because it is derived from umbilical cord blood. Cord blood donors have been screened to exclude donors with sickle cell anemia and anemias due to abnormalities in hemoglobins C, D, and E. Because of the age of the donor at the time cord blood collection takes place, the ability to exclude rare genetic diseases is severely limited. Report the occurrence of transmitted rare genetic disease from omidubicel to the manufacturer at 1-844-477-7478.
    Headline
    post-transplant lymphoproliferative disorder, secondary malignancy
    General Information
    Malignancy of donor origin including post-transplant lymphoproliferative disorder (PTLD) and donor cell transmitted myelodysplastic syndrome have been reported in patients who received an allogenic umbilical cord stem-cell transplant with omidubicel. In patients who received omidubicel for severe aplastic anemia (n = 17), PTLD occurred in 12% of patients. PTLD development may be attributed to donor lymphoid cells transformed by the Epstein-Barr virus (EBV); consider serial monitoring of blood for EBV DNA in patients with persistent cytopenias. Monitor lifelong for secondary malignancies. In the event that a secondary malignancy occurs, contact the manufacturer at 1-844-477-7478.

    Headline
    infection, sepsis
    General Information
    Viral (75%; grade 3, 8%), bacterial (65%; grade 3, 8%), and fungal (21%; grade 3, 6%) infection occurred in patients with hematologic malignancies who received omidubicel (n = 52) in a randomized trial; 6% of patients died from infection in this trial. Infections including human herpesvirus 6 (94%), BK virus (76%), bacterial (59%; grade 3 or higher, 41%), Epstein-Barr virus (53%; grade 3 or higher, 29%), cytomegalovirus (47%; grade 3 or higher, 18%), pneumonia (35%, grade 3 or higher, 24%), adenovirus (24%; grade 3 or higher, 6%), rhinovirus (24%; grade 3 or higher, 6%), sepsis (grade 3 or higher, 18%), and upper respiratory tract (18%; grade 3 or higher, 6%) occurred in patients with severe aplastic anemia who received omidubicel (n = 17) in another clinical trial. Febrile neutropenia (41%; grade 3 or higher 41%) was also reported in this trial. Bacterial infections included skin, clostridium difficile, urinary tract, and device-related infections. Additionally, transmission of infectious disease may occur because omidubicel is derived from umbilical cord blood. Disease may be caused by known or unknown infectious agents. Donors are screened for increased risk of infection with human immunodeficiency virus (HIV), human T-cell lymphotropic virus (HTLV), hepatitis B virus (HBV), hepatitis C virus (HCV), T. pallidum, West Nile Virus (WNV), transmissible spongiform encephalopathy (TSE) agents, vaccinia, and Zika virus (for umbilical cord blood collected since March 2016). Donors are also screened for clinical evidence of sepsis and communicable disease risks associated with xenotransplantation. Maternal blood samples are tested for HIV types 1 and 2, HTLV types I and II, HBV, HCV, T. pallidum, and WNV. Testing of maternal and infant donor blood is also performed for evidence of donor infection due to cytomegalovirus (CMV). Omidubicel is tested for sterility, endotoxin, and mycoplasma. There may be an effect on the reliability of the sterility test results if the cord blood donor was exposed to antibiotics in utero. Final sterility test results may not be available at the time of use, but Quality Assurance (QA) will communicate any positive results from sterility testing to the physician. Test results may be found on the container label and/or in accompanying records. Product manufacturing also includes bovine-derived reagents; all animal-derived reagents are tested for animal viruses, bacteria, fungi, and mycoplasma before use. These measures do not totally eliminate the risk of transmitting these or other transmissible infectious diseases and disease agents. Report the occurrence of transmitted infection from omidubicel to the manufacturer 1-844-477-7478.

    Headline
    infusion-related reactions
    General Information
    Hypersensitivity and infusion-related reactions have occurred with omidubicel administration. Signs and symptoms of hypersensitivity reactions may include bronchospasm, wheezing, angioedema, pruritus, hives, fever, and hypotension during or after omidubicel infusion. Infusion-related reactions may begin within minutes of starting the omidubicel infusion and intensify and peak several hours after the completion of the infusion. Infusion-related reactions occurred in 24% (grade 3 or higher, 12%) of patients with severe aplastic anemia who received omidubicel (n = 17) in a clinical trial. The term infusion-related reactions included hypertension, headache, and hypoxia. Premedicate individuals with antipyretics, histamine antagonists, and corticosteroids, and monitor closely for signs and symptoms of hypersensitivity and infusion-related reactions. When a reaction occurs, pause the omidubicel infusion and institute supportive care as needed.

Description
  Text
  Omidubicel is a nicotinamide modified allogeneic hematopoietic progenitor cell therapy indicated for the treatment of hematologic malignancies in individuals who are planned for umbilical cord blood transplantation after myeloablative conditioning to reduce the time to neutrophil recovery and the incidence of infection and for the treatment of severe aplastic anemia after reduced intensity conditioning. Omidubicel is derived from umbilical cord blood. Autoimmune cytopenias, engraftment syndrome, graft failure, graft-versus-host disease, infusion reactions, and malignancy of donor origin have been reported with omidubicel. Transmission of infectious or rare genetic diseases may occur with omidubicel because it is derived from umbilical cord blood. Omidubicel was FDA-approved in April 2023.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Omidubicel is a cryopreserved nicotinamide (NAM)-modified allogeneic hematopoietic progenitor cell therapy derived from the same patient-specific cord blood unit. It consists of 2 components: 1) a Cultured Fraction (CF) of allogeneic, CD34+ hematopoietic progenitor cells (HPCs) and differentiated myelomonocytic cells, dendritic cells, and granulocytes and 2) a Noncultured Fraction of allogeneic, hematopoietic mature myeloid and lymphoid cells and other lineage committed hematopoietic cells. Ex-vivo culturing of cord blood derived HPCs in the presence of NAM helps to preserve their stemness and prevent the accelerated proliferation, differentiation, cellular stress, and signaling pathways that typically happens when HPCs are removed from the cord. NAM-modified HPCs migrate to the bone marrow and result in rapid neutrophil engraftment and multi lineage immune reconstitution.

Pharmacokinetics
  Text
  Omidubicel is administered intravenously. Conventional pharmacokinetic parameters of absorption, distribution, metabolism, and elimination are not applicable to omidubicel as this medication is a cryopreserved nicotinamide modified allogeneic hematopoietic progenitor cell therapy derived from cord blood.
Pharmacodynamics
Rapid and broad immune reconstitution of dendritic cells, monocytes, natural killer (NK) cells, CD4+ T cells, and CD8+ T cells occur as soon as 1 week post transplantation. B-cell reconstitution occurs as early as 28 days post transplantation and all lineage cell reconstitution occurs throughout the 1-year follow-up period. There is a linear correlation between the CD34+ cell content in the Culture Fraction and the reconstitution of T-cells and NK cells. Additionally, days to neutrophil recovery decreased with an increase in the omidubicel CD34+ cell dose in dose-response modeling.
 
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes and drug transporters: none

Premedication
Hematologic Malignancies
Premedicate approximately 30 to 60 minutes prior to omidubicel infusion with acetaminophen (650 mg PO or 10 mg/kg up to a maximum of 650 mg), diphenhydramine (50 mg IV or 0.5 mg/kg up to a maximum of 50 mg) or dexchlorpheniramine (10 mg IV), and hydrocortisone (50 mg IV or 0.5 mg/kg up to a maximum of 50 mg).
Avoid prophylactic use of methylprednisolone in conjunction with omidubicel for treatment of hematologic malignancies.
Ensure adequate hydration.
Severe Aplastic Anemia
Premedicate approximately 30 minutes prior to omidubicel infusion with acetaminophen 650 mg PO and diphenhydramine 25 to 50 mg PO or IV or weight-based dosing for pediatric populations as per institutional guidelines.
For individuals receiving anti-thymocyte globulin (ATG): administer prednisone 1 mg/kg/day PO (or IV methylprednisolone if clinically indicated) 1 day prior to the first dose and continue according to institutional guidelines.
Ensure adequate hydration.
Prophylactic and Supportive Therapies
Administer prophylactic and supportive therapies, including granulocyte-colony stimulating factor, for prevention or treatment of transplant complications (e.g., graft-verus-host disease, infections) according to institutional guidelines.
Confirm emergency medications are available prior to omidubicel infusion and during the recovery period as per institutional guidelines.

      Name
      Intravenous Administration
      Description
      For comprehensive preparation, storage, and administration information, refer to the Administration section of the Full Prescribing Information for omidubicel.
A single dose of omidubicel consists of 2 fractions: a cultured fraction (CF) and a non-cultured fraction (NF).
Dilution of each cryopreserved bag is required prior to infusion; use the provided infusion solution that is specific to each fraction.
Do not wash, spin down, and/or resuspend CF or NF in new media prior to infusion; additionally, do not irradiate.
Verify the individual's identity upon receipt of omidubicel, prior to thaw, and prior to the infusion.
Infuse the diluted CF bag first, followed by the diluted NF bag.
Preparation
NOTE: Follow the preparation steps below for both the CF and NF cryopreserved bags. Thaw the CF first; do not thaw the NF cryopreserved bag until the diluted CF bag has been safely administered.
Remove from storage and place the Infusion Solution in the Biological Safety Cabinet (BSC) (if available) for 20 minutes to up to 24 hours at room temperature.
Remove the metal cassette from the liquid nitrogen storage; leave the cryopreserved bag in the overwrap bag during thawing and dilution.
Once the cryopreserved bag is removed from the metal cassette, thawing and dilution must be carried to completion and the cells administered within 2 hours post-dilution for the CF and within 1 hour post-dilution for the NF.
After incubating the cryopreserved bag for 5 minutes at room temperature, place it in an approximately 37 degrees C water bath until the product reaches a liquid consistency (usually takes 3 to 8 minutes).
Do not massage, knead, or apply pressure; keep the bag fully submerged until thawed.
Remove the thawed bag from the water bath when the cells have completely thawed; do not remove the overwrap bag.
Dilution
Remove the Infusion Solution from its sterile bag; ensure that the pinch clamp is closed.
While it remains in the overwrap bag, insert the spike adapter attached to the Infusion Solution bag into 1 of the ports of the thawed bag.
CF: Open the pinch clamp on the Infusion Solution tubing and add approximately 20 mL to the CF bag; gently swirl the bag until mixed well. Add the remaining Infusion Solution (approximately 60 mL) to the CF bag; close the valve and swirl gently.
NF: Open the pinch clamp on the Infusion Solution tubing and add approximately 10 mL to the NF bag; gently swirl the bag until mixed well. Add the remaining Infusion Solution (approximately 30 mL) to the NF bag; close the valve and swirl gently.
Remove the overwrap of the diluted bag and check its integrity.
Heat seal and detach the emptied Infusion Solution.
Connect the Transfusion Infusion Set to the free port on the diluted bag or connect the set in accordance with internal policies.
Place the diluted bag in a new sterile bag.
Intravenous (IV) Infusion
Administer the diluted CF bag first; begin the NF infusion within 1 hour after the end of the CF infusion.
Prime the infusion set tubing with normal saline prior to spiking the CF or NF bag.
Administer IV via gravity infusion through a central venous catheter; do not use a leukodepleting filter.
Infuse the entire contents within 2 hours post-dilution for the CF and within 1 hour post-dilution for the NF; do not exceed an infusion rate of 10 mL/kg per hour. Reduce the infusion rate if fluid load is not tolerated.
Monitor for signs and symptoms of hypersensitivity or other infusion-related reactions during and after the infusion. If a reaction occurs, pause the infusion and institute supportive care as needed; appropriately manage any reaction to the CF before thawing the NF.
Following the infusion, wash the tubing with normal saline at the same infusion rate to ensure as many cells as possible are delivered to the individual.
