    Drug Interactions
    General Information
    The coadministration of certain medications may lead to harm and require avoidance or therapy modification; review all drug interactions prior to concomitant use of other medications.
    Headline
    immunosuppression
    General Information
    Patients with significant immunosuppression, including those receiving immunosuppressive therapy, may have a diminished immune response to the chikungunya vaccine, recombinant. Immunosuppressed persons may include patients with severe combined immunodeficiency (SCID); hypogammaglobulinemia; agammaglobulinemia; altered immune states due to generalized neoplastic disease or human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) infection; complement deficiencies; an immune system compromised by radiation therapy or drug therapy (e.g., chemotherapy or corticosteroid therapy with greater than physiologic doses); or patients receiving treatment that inhibits terminal complement activation (e.g. eculizumab). Short-term (less than 2 weeks) corticosteroid therapy or intra-articular, bursal, or tendon injections with corticosteroids should not be immunosuppressive.
    Headline
    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    There are no clinical studies regarding the use of the chikungunya vaccine, recombinant during pregnancy. A developmental toxicity study evaluated the effects of the chikungunya vaccine, recombinant on female rabbits. The vaccine was given at a dose equivalent to a human dose on 5 different occasions: twice before mating, twice during pregnancy, and once during lactation. The results showed a lower survival rate of the baby kits. There were no negative effects on other postnatal development parameters. There was no evidence of harm to the fetus due to the vaccine. Another study was done with female rats at an equivalent human vaccine dose and the same timing as the previous study. In this case, there were no negative effects on the survival of the baby rats or on other postnatal development parameters. Individuals who are exposed to the chikungunya vaccine, recombinant around the time of conception or during pregnancy are encouraged to enroll in the pregnancy registry by calling 1-888-230-2491 or by visiting bnpregnancyregistry.com.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    There are no data available to assess the impact of the chikungunya vaccine, recombinant on milk production, its presence in breast milk, or its effects on the breastfed child. Consider the developmental and health benefits of breast-feeding along with the mother's clinical need for the vaccine and any potential adverse effects on the breastfed child from the vaccine or from the underlying maternal condition.

Description
  Text
  Chikungunya vaccine, recombinant is a vaccine indicated for the prevention of disease caused by chikungunya virus. The vaccine utilizes virus-like particles designed to mimic the chikungunya virus without the ability to infect cells, replicate, or cause disease. Chikungunya virus is transmitted through the bite of an infected mosquito and typically presents with symptoms such as fever, rash, fatigue, headache, and often severe and incapacitating joint pain. Clinical trials showed the chikungunya vaccine, recombinant induced neutralizing antibodies in up to 97.8% of vaccinated individuals 21 days after vaccination. A rapid immune response started to develop within 1 week of the vaccination. The most common adverse reactions were fatigue, headache, and myalgia. The chikungunya vaccine, recombinant was FDA-approved in February 2025.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  The exact mechanism of protection has not been determined for the chikungunya vaccine, recombinant. The chikungunya vaccine, recombinant elicits chikungunya-specific immune responses.
