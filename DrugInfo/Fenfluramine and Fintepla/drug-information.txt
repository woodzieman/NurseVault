Drug Class: Fenfluramine is an oral 5HT-2 receptor agonist indicated for the treatment of seizures associated with Dravet syndrome and Lennox-Gastaut syndrome.

Routes: oral; Common adult doses: 26 mg/day PO.

Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  General Information
  NOTE: This drug is discontinued in the US.Fenfluramine is contraindicated in patients with advanced arteriosclerosis, moderate to severe hypertension, glaucoma, hyperthyroidism, or symptomatic cardiovascular disease including cardiac arrhythmias. Fenfluramine should be used cautiously in patients with cardiac disease including valvular heart disease. Valvular heart disease has been reported in women receiving the combination of fenfluramine and phentermine (FDA Public Health Advisory, July 8, 1997).Do not combine fenfluramine with MAOI therapy (see Drug Interactions). Concurrent use can result in hypertensive crisis. Simultaneous use of these agents is contraindicated. MAOIs should be discontinued 2—4 weeks before initiation of fenfluramine therapy.Fenfluramine should be used cautiously in patients with a history of major depression. Further depression of mood has occurred during fenfluramine therapy and/or following withdrawal of fenfluramine. Additionally, fenfluramine should not be used in patients with psychotic disorders. Agitation, delusions, and combativeness have been reported in some patients with schizophrenia who were receiving fenfluramine. Fenfluramine is contraindicated in patients with agitated states or with a history of substance abuse including alcoholism. Adverse psychiatric effects (e.g., psychosis) may occur in patients with a history of alcoholism.Fenfluramine should not be used in patients with pulmonary hypertension. Pulmonary hypertension has occurred rarely in patients receiving fenfluramine and may be reversible following discontinuance of fenfluramine therapy. Rechallenge may result in recurrence of pulmonary hypertension. One fatality resulting from irreversible pulmonary hypertension has been reported in an adult patient receiving seven 1-month courses of fenfluramine.The safety and efficacy of fenfluramine in children have not been established.No information is available concerning the safe use of fenfluramine during pregnancy and/or in breast-feeding women. Avoid use in these circumstances.

    Headline
    Drug Interactions
    General Information
    The coadministration of certain medications may lead to harm and require avoidance or therapy modification; review all drug interactions prior to concomitant use of other medications.
    Headline
    pulmonary hypertension, valvular heart disease
    General Information
    Obtain an echocardiogram before treatment with fenfluramine, every 6 months during treatment, and 3 to 6 months after treatment to evaluate for valvular heart disease and pulmonary hypertension. Consider the benefits vs. risks of fenfluramine treatment if any of the following signs are observed on echocardiogram: valvular abnormality or new abnormality; valvular heart disease (VHD) as indicated by mild or greater aortic regurgitation or moderate or greater mitral regurgitation, with additional characteristics of VHD (e.g., valve thickening or restrictive valve motion); or pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH) as indicated by elevated right heart/pulmonary artery pressure (pulmonary artery systolic pressure more than 35 mm Hg). There is a known association between serotonergic drugs with 5-HT2B receptor agonist activity, including fenfluramine, and valvular heart disease and pulmonary arterial hypertension.
    Headline
    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components.
    Headline
    mood disorder, suicidal ideation
    General Information
    Monitor for worsening of depression, suicidal thoughts or behavior, or any unusual changes in behavior in individuals with mood disorder or suicidal ideation; antiepileptic drugs (AEDs), including fenfluramine, increase the risk of suicidal thoughts or behavior in individuals taking these medications for any indication. When considering AED use, balance the risk of suicidal thoughts or behaviors with the risk of untreated illness. The relative risk for suicidal thoughts or behavior was higher in clinical trials in subjects with epilepsy than in clinical trials in subjects with psychiatric or other conditions, but the absolute risk differences were similar for the epilepsy and psychiatric indications. Epilepsy and many other illnesses for which AEDs are prescribed are themselves associated with morbidity and mortality and an increased risk of suicidal thoughts and behavior. If suicidal thoughts and behavior emerge during treatment, consider whether the emergence of these symptoms may be related to the illness being treated.
    Headline
    activities requiring coordination and concentration, driving or operating machinery
    General Information
    Advise individuals against driving or operating machinery or doing activities requiring coordination and concentration until they have gained sufficient experience on fenfluramine to gauge whether it adversely affects their ability to do so. Fenfluramine can cause somnolence, sedation, and lethargy.
    Headline
    renal failure, renal impairment
    General Information
    A lower maximum fenfluramine dose is recommended in individuals with severe renal impairment (eGFR 15 to 29 mL/minute/1.73 m2). Fenfluramine has not been studied in individuals with renal failure (eGFR less than 15 mL/minute/1.73 m2).
    Headline
    Child-Pugh class A, Child-Pugh class B, Child-Pugh class C, hepatic failure
    General Information
    A lower maximum fenfluramine dose is recommended in individuals with Child-Pugh class A, Child-Pugh class B, or Child-Pugh class C hepatic impairment or hepatic failure.
    Headline
    adolescents, children
    General Information
    Carefully monitor the growth of children and adolescents treated with fenfluramine. Monitor weight regularly during treatment with fenfluramine and consider dose modifications if a decrease in weight occurs. Fenfluramine can cause decreases in appetite and weight.
    Headline
    geriatric
    General Information
    In general, use cautious dose selection for fenfluramine in geriatric adults, usually starting at the low end of the dosing range, reflecting the greater frequency of decreased hepatic, renal, or cardiac function, and of concomitant disease or other drug therapy.
    Headline
    people who can cause pregnancy in others, people who may become pregnant, reproductive risk
    General Information
    Counsel people who may become pregnant and people who can cause pregnancy in others about the reproductive risk associated with fenfluramine. In animal studies, oral administration of fenfluramine resulted in adverse reproductive effects, including infertility in both males and females at clinically relevant doses in the presence of parental toxicity. When males and female rats were given fenfluramine doses of 3, 6.9, or 17.3 mg/kg/day prior to and throughout mating, and in females continuing until day 7 of gestation, decreased fertility, increased abnormal sperm production, and epithelial vacuolation of the epididymis occurred at the highest dose tested. At medium and high doses, altered estrous cyclicity, a reduction in corpora lutea and implantations, and an increase in embryolethality occurred.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Avoid the use of fenfluramine during breast-feeding. There are no data on the presence of fenfluramine or its metabolites in human milk, its effects on the breast-fed child, or its effects on milk production. Consider the benefits of breast-feeding, the patient's clinical need for treatment, and any potential adverse effects on the breast-fed child from the medication or from the underlying maternal medical condition.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Available data on the use of fenfluramine during pregnancy are insufficient to evaluate a drug-associated risk of major birth defects, miscarriage, or other adverse outcomes. Fenfluramine may cause decreased appetite and decreased weight; monitor patients closely for adequate weight gain during pregnancy. Based on animal data, fenfluramine may cause fetal harm. In animal studies, fenfluramine administration throughout organogenesis (rat and rabbit) or throughout gestation and lactation (rat) resulted in adverse effects on development (fetal malformations, embryofetal and offspring mortality, and growth impairment). Oral fenfluramine administration to pregnant rats during organogenesis resulted in decreased fetal body weights and increases in fetal malformations at the highest dose tested (34.6 mg/kg/day), which was associated with maternal toxicity. At the no-effect dose (8.6 mg/kg/day) for adverse effects on embryo-fetal development in rats, maternal plasma exposures of fenfluramine and norfenfluramine (its major metabolite) were approximately 2 and 5 times, respectively, those in humans at the maximum recommended human dose (MRHD) of 26 mg/day. Oral fenfluramine administration to pregnant rabbits throughout organogenesis resulted in increased embryofetal mortality at all doses (0, 4.3, 8.6, 13 mg/kg/day) and increases in fetal malformations at 13 mg/kg/day, which was associated with maternal toxicity. At the lowest dose tested (4.3 mg/kg/day), maternal plasma exposures of fenfluramine and norfenfluramine were lower than those in humans at the MRHD. Oral fenfluramine administration to female rats throughout gestation and lactation resulted in increases in stillborn pups and neonatal offspring at 34.6 mg/kg/day and delayed growth and reflex development during the pre-weaning period at all doses. Maternal weight gain was decreased at all doses during pregnancy, and the 8.6 and 34.6 mg/kg/day doses during lactation. At the lowest dose tested in rats (4.3 mg/kg/day), maternal plasma exposures of fenfluramine and norfenfluramine were approximately 0.5 and 3 times, respectively, those in humans at the MRHD. Seizures, with or without the use of antiseizure medications, have been associated with several adverse outcomes during pregnancy. The risk of maternal or fetal injury may be greatest for individuals with untreated or poorly controlled seizures. Individuals with seizures who become pregnant should not abruptly discontinue antiseizure medications due to the risk of status epilepticus or severe seizures, which may be life-threatening. Encourage pregnant patients taking fenfluramine to enroll in the North American Antiepileptic Drug (NAAED) Pregnancy Registry. Patients must enroll themselves by calling the registry at 1-888-233-2334 or visiting http://www.aedpregnancyregistry.org.

Adverse Reactions
  Headline
  Adverse Reactions
  General Information
  NOTE: This drug is discontinued in the US.The most common adverse reactions associated with fenfluramine therapy are drowsiness, diarrhea, and xerostomia. Diarrhea usually occurs within the first week of therapy and may require a dose reduction or termination of therapy. Abdominal pain, constipation, and nausea/vomiting have also been reported with fenfluramine therapy. Dysuria, increased urinary frequency, libido decrease, libido increase, and impotence (erectile dysfunction) have also been reported. Hemolytic anemia has occurred in one patient. Other reported adverse effects include agitation, anxiety, bruxism, chest pain, confusion, dizziness, dysgeusia, dysarthria (slurred speech), fatigue or weakness, fever, headache, incoordination, insomnia, lethargy, elevated mood, mental depression, myalgia, ocular irritation, polydipsia, restlessness, vivid and disturbing dreams, and nightmares. Sudden withdrawal following prolonged therapy may cause temporary mental depression. Some mentally depressed patients who have received fenfluramine have become more depressed. In patients with alcoholism, paranoia, mental depression, and psychosis have occurred after receiving fenfluramine.Cardiovascular and autonomic adverse reactions are infrequent with fenfluramine therapy. Adverse reactions such as blurred vision, chills, diaphoresis, fainting, hypertension, and hypotension have occurred rarely. In one patient who was receiving fenfluramine prior to surgery, cardiac arrest and death occurred following induction of general anesthesia. Thirty-three cases of cardiac valvulopathy in women receiving fenfluramine and phentermine for obesity have been reported to the FDA as of July 8, 1997. The women were between the ages of 30 and 72 and had been taking the drug combination for 1 month to 28 months. The cardiac valvulopathy was described as unusual valvular morphology and regurgitation involving the mitral, aortic, and tricuspid valves. Six women required surgical intervention. The histopathology of the valves in these 6 women were not unlike that seen in carcinoid syndrome or ergotamine toxicity. The course of the valvular disease when the drugs are discontinued is unknown at this time (FDA Public Health Advisory, July 8, 1997). Since cardiac valvulopathy has also been reported with ergot alkaloids and these drugs also affect serotonin balance, a serotonin mechanism may explain this adverse reaction for fenfluramine.Pulmonary hypertension has occurred rarely in patients receiving fenfluramine and may be reversible following discontinuance of fenfluramine therapy. Rechallenge may result in recurrence of pulmonary hypertension. One fatality resulting from irreversible pulmonary hypertension has been reported in an adult patient receiving seven 1-month courses of fenfluramine. A case-control study assessed 95 patients with primary pulmonary hypertension and 355 controls from 35 centers in Europe. When the previous year was assessed, patients with primary pulmonary hypertension were 10 times as likely to have received anorectic agents (mainly derivatives of fenfluramine) and were 23 times as likely to have received anorectic agents for greater than 3 months when compared to controls. Although this does not prove causality, the manufacturer recommends that treatment be discontinued if symptoms of pulmonary hypertension (dry cough, dyspnea upon exertion, syncope, chest pain, palpitations, or cardiac insufficiency) occur.Fenfluramine stimulates the release of serotonin (5-HT), enhancing serotonin activity in the CNS. While not frequently reported, fenfluramine may cause symptoms consistent with the serotonin syndrome when used with other medications (e.g., SSRIs) that enhance serotonin activity within the CNS. In animals, fenfluramine has been shown to cause dose-related, long-lasting (> 2 weeks) brain serotonin neurotoxicity. In a review of 71 articles concerning fenfluramine neurotoxicity, animal data supporting this issue were examined. According to the review, fenfluramine caused depletion of brain serotonin which was associated with long-term decreases in brain levels of 5-hydroxyindoleacetic acid (the major metabolite of serotonin), the activity of tryptophan hydroxylase (the rate-limiting enzyme in serotonin synthesis), and the density of serotonin uptake sites. The review also stated that doses of fenfluramine that produce signs of brain serotonin neurotoxicity in animals were of the same magnitude as those used to treat humans for weight loss when known relationships of body mass and drug clearance between primates and humans were taken into account. However, no clinical studies have been performed in humans and the pathological and the clinical potential for fenfluramine-induced brain serotonin neurotoxicity is unknown.

    General Information
    Fenfluramine can cause valvular heart disease or pulmonary arterial hypertension. Serotonergic drugs with 5-HT2B receptor agonist activity, including fenfluramine, are associated with cardiac valvulopathy and pulmonary hypertension. In clinical trials of up to 3 years in duration, no subject receiving fenfluramine developed valvular heart disease or pulmonary arterial hypertension; however, cases of valvular heart disease and pulmonary arterial hypertension have been reported in the postmarketing setting. Abnormal echocardiogram (consisting of trace and mild mitral regurgitation and trace aortic regurgitation) occurred in 18% of subjects receiving fenfluramine 0.2 mg/kg/day, 9% receiving 0.4 mg/kg/day, 23% receiving 0.7 mg/kg/day, and 6% given placebo. Neither subject had valvular heart disease. The rates of mild aortic regurgitation were consistent with those seen during the screening period prior to treatment (3 subjects with Lennox-Gastaut syndrome and 1 subject with Dravet syndrome). Obtain an echocardiogram before treatment with fenfluramine, every 6 months during treatment, and 3 to 6 months after treatment to evaluate for valvular heart disease and pulmonary hypertension. Consider the benefits versus the risks of treatment with fenfluramine if valvular abnormality, valvular heart disease, or pulmonary hypertension is observed on an echocardiogram.

    General Information
    Antiepileptic drugs (AEDs) such as fenfluramine increase the risk of suicidal ideation and behavior. Monitor all patients taking fenfluramine closely for emerging or worsening depression or suicidal ideation. Description
  Text
  Fenfluramine is an oral 5HT-2 receptor agonist indicated for the treatment of seizures associated with Dravet syndrome and Lennox-Gastaut syndrome. Fenfluramine significantly reduced the monthly convulsive seizure frequency compared to placebo in 2 randomized, controlled trials in pediatric patients (2 to 18 years) with Dravet syndrome. In a randomized, double-blind trial of patients with Dravet syndrome whose seizures were not adequately controlled on 1 or more antiepileptic drugs (n = 119), the median reduction in seizure frequency was 42.3% and 74.9% in patients treated with adjunctive fenfluramine 0.2 mg/kg/day and 0.7 mg/kg/day, respectively, compared to 19.2% in patients given placebo. Use of fenfluramine 0.2 mg/kg/day resulted in a 32.4% greater reduction in mean monthly convulsive seizure frequency when compared to placebo (95% CI 6.2% to 52.3%, p = 0.0209); fenfluramine 0.7 mg/kg/day was associated with a 62.3% greater reduction in mean monthly convulsive seizure frequency vs. placebo (95% CI 47.7% to 72.8%; p less than 0.0001). Patients treated with fenfluramine 0.4 mg/kg/day as part of a stable antiepileptic drug regimen containing stiripentol (n = 87) experienced a 54% greater reduction in mean monthly convulsive seizure frequency compared to placebo (95% CI 35.6% to 67.2%; p less than 0.001). Most patients responded to treatment with fenfluramine within 3 to 4 weeks, and the effect generally remained consistent over the 14- or 15-week treatment period. In a randomized, controlled trial in patients (2 to 35 years) with Lennox-Gastaut syndrome whose seizures were not adequately controlled on 1 or more antiepileptic drugs (n = 263), fenfluramine 0.7 mg/kg/day significantly reduced the monthly seizure frequency compared to placebo. Fenfluramine was previously FDA-approved for the treatment of obesity; however, due to cases of valvular heart disease attributed to fenfluramine, it was voluntarily withdrawn from the market for this use. In patients initiating fenfluramine therapy, echocardiogram monitoring before treatment, every 6 months during treatment, and 3 to 6 months after treatment is required to evaluate for valvular heart disease and pulmonary hypertension.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  The precise mechanisms of action by which fenfluramine exerts its anticonvulsant effect are not known. Fenfluramine and the metabolite, norfenfluramine, increase extracellular concentrations of serotonin through interaction with serotonin transporter proteins and exhibit agonist activity at serotonin 5HT-2 receptors. There is an association between serotonergic drugs with 5-HT2B receptor agonist activity, including fenfluramine and norfenfluramine, and valvular heart disease and pulmonary arterial hypertension.

  Text
  Fenfluramine is administered orally. After oral administration in healthy subjects, the geometric mean (CV%) apparent Vd of fenfluramine is 11.9 (16.5%) L/kg. Plasma protein binding is 50% in vitro and is independent of drug concentration. Over 75% of fenfluramine is metabolized to norfenfluramine prior to elimination, primarily by CYP1A2, CYP2B6, and CYP2D6. Other enzymes involved to a minor extent are CYP2C9, CYP2C19, and CYP3A4/5. Fenfluramine and norfenfluramine are pharmacologically active. Norfenfluramine is then deaminated and oxidized to form inactive metabolites. Most of an orally administered dose of fenfluramine (more than 90%) is excreted in the urine as fenfluramine, norfenfluramine, or other metabolites accounting for less than 25% of the total; less than 5% is found in feces. The elimination half-life of fenfluramine is 20 hours, and the geometric mean (CV%) clearance (CL/F) is 24.8 (29%) L/hour.
 
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes and drug transporters: CYP1A2, CYP2B6, and CYP2D6; also (minor) CYP2C9, CYP2C19, and CYP3A4/5
Coadministration with strong CYP1A2, CYP2B6, or CYP3A inducers will decrease fenfluramine plasma concentrations, which may lower the efficacy of fenfluramine. Coadministration of fenfluramine with strong CYP1A2 or CYP2D6 inhibitors will increase fenfluramine plasma concentrations.
