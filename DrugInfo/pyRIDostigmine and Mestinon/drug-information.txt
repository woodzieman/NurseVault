Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  General Information
  GI obstruction or ileus (i.e., bowel obstruction) contraindicates the use of pyridostigmine. Cholinergic activity of the drug can exacerbate these conditions. Pyridostigmine is also contraindicated in patients with urinary tract obstruction (i.e., bladder obstruction) because it can cause ureteral constriction.
 
Patients with known hyperreactivity to pyridostigmine or related drugs, such as physostigmine, should not receive the drug because they can have a severe cholinergic response. Atropine sulfate injection should be available as an antidote.
 
Patients with bromide hypersensitivity should be treated with caution. Pyridostigmine is used as the bromide salt and has been shown to produce a hypersensitivity reaction manifested as rash in some patients. The effect is reversible upon withdrawal of the drug.
 
Pyridostigmine should not be used for cholinesterase inhibitor toxicity. It is important to differentiate between myasthenic crisis and cholinergic crisis, as both conditions present with similar symptoms. Myasthenic crisis requires treatment with anticholinesterase therapy while cholinergic crisis (indicative of anticholinesterase overdose, with excess acetylcholine available through cholinesterase inhibition) requires discontinuation of cholinesterase inhibitor therapy.
 
Pyridostigmine should be used with caution in patients with peptic ulcer disease, asthma, bradycardia, cardiac arrhythmias, recent coronary occlusion, or vagotonia because cholinergic effects can exacerbate these conditions.
 
Pyridostigmine should be used with caution in patients with hyperthyroidism. The drug can produce bradycardia, aggravating the possibility of arrhythmias.
 
Pyridostigmine should be used with caution in patients with epilepsy or other seizure disorder. Seizures can be precipitated.
 
Pyridostigmine injection contains benzyl alcohol as a preservative and should not be used in patients with known benzyl alcohol hypersensitivity or in neonates. Benzyl alcohol has been associated with 'gasping syndrome' in premature infants.
 
Because the kidneys account for a large portion of the clearance of pyridostigmine, doses may require adjustment in patients with renal impairment. The exact dosage of pyridostigmine in these patients should be titrated to effect.
 
Military personnel must carefully follow instructions and use pyridostigmine only under specific circumstances. For anti-nerve gas use, pyridostigmine must be started several hours prior to exposure to Soman and must be discontinued upon immediate exposure to Soman. At the point that pyridostigmine is discontinued, the antidote atropine and pralidoxime are given. Pyridostigmine is only effective prior to Soman exposure and should not be taken at the time of, or after exposure to Soman. Pyridostigmine is to be used in conjunction with other protective measures including chemical protective masks and battle dress garments.
 
Pyridostigmine is categorized by the FDA as a pregnancy risk category C drug. Use of IV pyridostigmine near term is not recommended because it might induce premature labor. There is a possibility that neonates born to myasthenic mothers can have transient muscle weakness if pyridostigmine is used during pregnancy.
 
It is not known whether pyridostigmine is excreted in human milk. Because many drugs are excreted in human milk, and because of the potential for serious adverse reactions from pyridostigmine, a decision should be made whether to discontinue breast-feeding or to discontinue the drug, taking into account the importance of the drug to the mother. The manufacturer warns that the safe use of pyridostigmine during breast-feeding has not been established. However, the American Academy of Pediatrics considers pyridostigmine to be generally compatible with breast-feeding.
 
The safety and effectiveness of pyridostigmine have not been established in children.
 
Use pyridostigmine cautiously in patients with adrenal insufficiency. Electrolyte imbalances associated with adrenal cortical insufficiency may enhance or inhibit neuromuscular blockade.

    Headline
    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components. Pyridostigmine is also contraindicated in individuals with known hypersensitivity to other anticholinesterase agents. Use pyridostigmine with caution in individuals with known bromide sensitivity. As with any compound containing bromide, a skin rash may be observed in bromide-sensitive individuals; the risks and benefits of pyridostigmine must be weighed against the potential for rash or other adverse reactions in these individuals.
    Headline
    improper use
    General Information
    There are risks associated with improper use of pyridostigmine for pretreatment against the lethal effects of soman nerve agent poisoning (for military medical use only). Incorrect timing or administration may reduce protection or worsen nerve agent toxicity. If pyridostigmine is taken immediately before exposure (e.g., when the gas attack alarm is given) or at the same time as poisoning by soman, it is not expected to be effective and may exacerbate the effects of a sublethal exposure to soman. Do not administer pyridostigmine after exposure to soman. Additionally, pyridostigmine alone will not protect against exposure to soman. The efficacy of pyridostigmine is dependent upon the rapid use of atropine and pralidoxime (2-PAM) after soman exposure. However, individuals must not rely solely upon pretreatment with pyridostigmine and on the 2-PAM antidotes to provide complete protection from poisoning by soman nerve agent; primary protection against exposure to chemical nerve agents is the wearing of protective garments including masks, hoods, and overgarments designed specifically for this use.
    Headline
    asthma, cardiac arrhythmias, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease
    General Information
    Use pyridostigmine with particular caution in individuals at increased risk of anticholinergic adverse reactions, including those with bronchial asthma, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, or cardiac arrhythmias.
    Headline
    mechanical intestinal obstruction, urinary tract obstruction
    General Information
    Pyridostigmine is contraindicated in individuals with mechanical intestinal obstruction or urinary tract obstruction.
    Headline
    renal disease
    General Information
    Use pyridostigmine with caution and select the dosage carefully in individuals with impaired renal function; lower pyridostigmine doses may be necessary in individuals with renal disease. The risk of adverse reactions to pyridostigmine may be increased in individuals with impaired renal function. It may be useful to monitor renal function during pyridostigmine therapy. Pyridostigmine is known to be substantially excreted by the kidney.
    Headline
    adrenal insufficiency, electrolyte imbalance
    General Information
    Electrolyte imbalance and diseases which lead to electrolyte imbalance, such as adrenal insufficiency, have been shown to alter neuromuscular blockade. Depending on the nature of the imbalance, either enhancement or inhibition may be expected. Consider the possibility that such circumstances may interfere with the restoration of neuromuscular function when using parenteral pyridostigmine for neuromuscular blockade reversal.
    Headline
    neonates, premature neonates
    General Information
    Pyridostimine injection contains benzyl alcohol, which has been associated with toxicity (hypotension, metabolic acidosis), particularly in neonates, and an increased incidence of kernicterus, particularly in premature neonates. A "gasping syndrome", characterized by central nervous system depression, metabolic acidosis, gasping respirations, and high concentrations of benzyl alcohol and its metabolites found in the blood and urine, has been associated with benzyl alcohol dosages of more than 99 mg/kg/day in neonates and low-birth-weight neonates. Although normal therapeutic doses of pyridostigmine injection deliver amounts of benzyl alcohol that are substantially lower than those reported in association with the "gasping syndrome", the minimum amount of benzyl alcohol at which toxicity may occur is not known. Premature neonates and low-birth-weight neonates, as well as individuals receiving high dosages, may be more likely to develop toxicity. Consider the combined daily metabolic load of benzyl alcohol from all sources when administering parenteral pyridostigmine and other medications containing benzyl alcohol.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Available human data from case reports and case series over several decades have not identified an association between pyridostigmine use and major birth defects, miscarriage, or other adverse pregnancy outcomes. However, findings from animal studies indicate potential developmental toxicity, including increased embryo-fetal mortality and structural abnormalities at clinically relevant exposures. In the setting of soman nerve agent exposure, treatment with pyridostigmine bromide should not be delayed during pregnancy, as untreated exposure is associated with increased morbidity and mortality in both the pregnant person and fetus. Guidelines consider oral pyridostigmine as first-line therapy for myasthenia gravis during pregnancy. Intravenous administration should be avoided, as it may induce uterine contractions. Pyridostigmine is known to cross the placenta. Neonates born to individuals with myasthenia gravis may exhibit transient muscle weakness following in utero exposure.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Oral pyridostigmine is considered compatible with breast-feeding. Pyridostigmine is present in human milk at low concentrations. Oral bioavailability is poor (approximately 10% to 20%). Infant exposure is therefore minimal, and serum concentrations in the child are very low. The estimated daily dose in a breastfed child is less than 0.1% of the lactating individual's dose. Adverse effects are not expected in breastfed children. There are no specific data evaluating intravenous pyridostigmine during lactation; however, the same general principles as oral use apply. Because intravenous administration can result in higher and more rapid plasma concentrations, some caution may be advised.

  General Information

Description
  Text
  Pyridostigmine is an oral and parenteral cholinesterase inhibitor indicated for the treatment of myasthenia gravis and the reversal of the neuromuscular blocking effects of nondepolarizing muscle relaxants. It is also indicated for military medical use only as a pretreatment against the lethal effects of Soman nerve agent poisoning in conjunction with protective garments. Pyridostigmine facilitates the transmission of impulses across the myoneural junction by inhibiting the destruction of acetylcholine by cholinesterase. Pyridostigmine is an analog of neostigmine but differs from it in certain clinically significant aspects; for example, pyridostigmine has a longer duration of action and is associated with fewer muscarinic effects, such as bradycardia, salivation, and gastrointestinal stimulation. The evidence for the effectiveness of pyridostigmine as pretreatment against Soman-induced toxicity was derived from animal studies alone. Pyridostigmine was approved by the FDA in 1955.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Pyridostigmine is a reversible cholinesterase inhibitor, preventing the destruction of acetylcholine by cholinesterase and thereby allowing freer transmission of nerve impulses across the neuromuscular junction. Pyridostigmine may produce depolarization block when administered at doses above the recommended therapeutic range. The therapeutic index of parenteral pyridostigmine (ratio of reversal dose to blocking dose) is approximately 1:6. The antagonism of neuromuscular blockade by anticholinesterase agents may be influenced by the degree of spontaneous recovery achieved when the reversal agent is administered, by the particular relaxant administered, acid-base balance, body temperature, electrolyte imbalance, and concomitant medications. The effect of pyridostigmine in Soman-induced toxicity is presumed to result from its reversible inhibition of a critical number of acetylcholinesterase active sites in the peripheral nervous system, protecting them from irreversible inhibition by Soman. Pyridostigmine is not thought to enter the brain in significant amounts. When the pyridostigmine-induced inhibition of the enzyme is subsequently reversed, there is a small residual amount of enzyme activity that is adequate to sustain life provided atropine and pralidoxime are administered.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Oral Route
    Description
    Pyridostigmine is poorly absorbed from the gastrointestinal tract with an absolute bioavailability of 10% to 20%. The pharmacokinetics of pyridostigmine are linear over the dose range of 30 to 60 mg. After a single oral dose of pyridostigmine 30 mg in the fasting state, the Tmax was 2.2 +/- 1 hours. After multiple doses of pyridostigmine (30 mg every 8 hours for 21 days), the average steady-state pyridostigmine trough concentration was about one-fourth of the peak concentration after a single dose. The immediate effect of a 180 mg Timespan extended-release tablet is about equal to that of a 60 mg immediate-release tablet; however, duration of effectiveness, although varying in individuals, averages 2.5 times that of a 60 mg dose. The pharmacokinetics of pyridostigmine extended-release tablets are significantly affected by the presence of and composition of food. Oral bioavailability is greater when the tablets are taken with food compared to a fasted state. Peak drug exposure and overall drug exposure were similar when the drug was given with a medium fat, medium calorie meal (MFMC; approximately 600 to 650 Kcal and 40% fat) and a high fat, high calorie meal (HFHC; approximately 800 to 1,000 Kcal and 50% fat). Exposure was greatest when the drug was given with a HFHC meal compared to a low fat, low calorie meal (LFLC; approximately 450 to 500 Kcal and 25% fat). A higher Cmax (55%) and AUC (231%) occurred when the drug was given with a HFHC meal compared to the fasted state, and a higher Cmax (32%) and AUC (89%) occurred when the drug was given with a HFHC meal compared to a LFLC meal. Pyridostigmine exposure was lower during the second half of a 24-hour dosing interval under fed or LFLC meal conditions compared to HFHC and MFMC meal conditions. The following pharmacokinetic parameters (mean [SD]) were reported after single dose administration of 105 mg pyridostigmine extended-release tablets under fasted conditions or with various types of meals: Cmax (ng/mL) 9.3 [4.16] fasted state, 10.91 [3.55] LFLC, 13.76 [3.72] MFMC, 14.43 [3.18] HFHC; AUClast (ng x hours/L) 68.9 [33.31] fasted state, 120.4 [82.38] LFLC, 205.11 [81.09] MFMC, 228.15 [59.99] HFHC; AUCinf (ng x hours/L) 77.22 [40.5] fasted state, 136.45 [88.53] LFLC, 215.81 [81.3] MFMC, 236.18 [61.35] HFHC; half-life (hours) 6.17 [4.15] fasted state, 7.39 [5.14] LFLC, 5.43 [1.4] MFMC, and 5.31 [1.51] HFHC. Reported Tmax (median [minimum, maximum]) was 4 hours [1 to 5] fasted state, 5 hours [4 to 20] LFLC, 10 hours [5 to 18] MFMC, and 16 hours [5 to 20] HFHC.

  Text
  Pyridostigmine is administered orally and intravenously. The pyridostigmine volume of distribution is about 19 +/- 12 L, indicating that it distributes into tissues. Pyridostigmine is a quaternary ammonium compound and does not readily cross the blood-brain barrier.

Administer several hours prior to exposure to soman.
Take with a medium fat, medium calorie meal (e.g., meal with 650 calories, 40% fat) or a high fat, high calorie meal (e.g., meal with 1,000 calories, 50% fat). Do not take with a low-fat, low-calorie meal (e.g., meal with 40 calories, 25% fat). Do not take on an empty stomach.
Do not take pyridostigmine after exposure to Soman. The use of pyridostigmine immediately before exposure to Soman or at the same time as poisoning by Soman is not expected to be efficacious and may exacerbate the effects of a sublethal exposure to Soman.
Discontinue pyridostigmine and immediately administer treatment with atropine and pralidoxime at the first sign of nerve agent poisoning.
Do not take with alcohol.
Missed dose: Take a missed dose as soon as possible and resume dosing at 24-hour intervals from the time the missed dose was taken. Do not take double or extra doses.

      Intravenous Administration
      Description
      Neuromuscular blockade reversal:
The use of pyridostigmine injection for neuromuscular blockade reversal requires an experienced clinician familiar with the use of agents which reverse or antagonize neuromuscular blocking agents.
Administer atropine (0.6 to 1.2 mg) or an equipotent dose of glycopyrrolate immediately before or simultaneously with pyridostigmine to minimize side effects, notably excessive secretions and bradycardia.
Monitor muscle twitch response to peripheral nerve stimulation to obtain maximum clinical benefits and minimize the possibility of overdosage.
Satisfactory reversal can be evident by adequate voluntary respiration, respiratory measurements, and use of a peripheral nerve stimulator device. Ensure the individual is well-ventilated and maintain a patent airway until complete recovery of normal respiration is assured.

Dosage Adjustment Guidelines
  Hepatic Impairment
  Specific guidelines for dosage adjustments in hepatic impairment are not available; it appears that no dosage adjustments are needed.
  Renal Impairment
  Specific recommendations for dosage adjustments in renal impairment are not available. Lower doses may be required in people with renal disease. Titrate the dosage to the desired clinical effect.
