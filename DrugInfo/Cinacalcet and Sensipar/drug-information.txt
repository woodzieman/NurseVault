Drug Class: Cinacalcet is an oral calcimimetic agent, and represents the first approved agent within this drug class.

Routes: oral; Common adult doses: 300 mg/day PO for dialysis patients with secondary hyperparathyroidism; 360 mg/day PO for hypercalcemia associated with parathyroid carcinoma or primary hyperparathyroidism.

Common Side Effects (excluding routine GI upset): dehydration, arrhythmia exacerbation, chest pain (unspecified), heart failure, hypertension, hypotension, qt prolongation, angioedema, rash (unspecified), urticaria, depression, dizziness, headache, paresthesias, asthenia, bone fractures, fatigue, infection

In an Overdose: No overdose/antidote information is listed in this file.

Key Nursing Considerations: Contraindications/precautions: Cinacalcet lowers serum calcium concentrations, and patients should be monitored for hypocalcemia. The manufacturer recommends against initiating cinacalcet in patients with hypocalcemia (e.g., calcium concentrations less than the lower limit of the normal range of 8.4 mg/dl).

          Dosage Description
          Preliminary data from a study in 54 patients with stage 3 or 4 CKD indicate that cinacalcet is effective in decreasing iPTH concentrations. In this study, patients were initiated on 30 mg PO once daily and titrated every 3 weeks to a maximum dose of 180 mg PO once daily for iPTH concentrations that did not decrease by at least 30% as compared to baseline. After 18 weeks of treatment, 56% of patients receiving cinacalcet compared to 19% of patients receiving placebo experienced a decrease in iPTH concentrations of 30% or more (p = 0.006); furthermore, iPTH concentrations decreased by a mean of 32% compared to baseline in patients taking cinacalcet compared to an increase of 6% in patients taking placebo (p less than 0.001). At study end, 52% of patients were receiving 30 mg PO once daily, and 30% were receiving 60 mg PO once daily. Serum calcium concentrations decreased by an average of 7% and the mean calcium concentration remained within the normal range throughout the study. Approximately 28% of patients were receiving vitamin D analogs at study start, and approximately 43% were receiving phosphate binders. Of note, the manufacturer reports of another study in which the majority of patients (80%) not requiring dialysis experienced at least one serum concentration of calcium less than 8.4 mg/dL.

Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  General Information
  Cinacalcet lowers serum calcium concentrations, and patients should be monitored for hypocalcemia. The manufacturer recommends against initiating cinacalcet in patients with hypocalcemia (e.g., calcium concentrations less than the lower limit of the normal range of 8.4 mg/dl). Measure serum calcium concentrations within 1 week after dosage initiation or dosage adjustment and then approximately once every month after establishing a maintenance dose (see Adverse Reactions). Additionally, the long-term safety and efficacy of cinacalcet in non-dialysis patients has not been established. Patients with chronic kidney disease that are not on dialysis who are receiving cinacalcet appear to be at increased risk for hypocalcemia possibly because these patients tend to have lower baseline serum calcium concentrations.Caution should be exercised in patients with a history of seizure disorder and who are receiving cinacalcet. During three clinical trials of chronic kidney disease patients receiving dialysis, 9 of 256 cinacalcet-treated patient and 2 of 470 placebo-treated patients experienced seizures (primarily generalized tonic-clonic). Five of the nine cinacalcet-treated patients and both placebo-treated patients had a history of seizures. Two of the five cinacalcet-treated patients and both placebo-treated patients were receiving antiepileptic drug therapy at the time of the seizure occurrence. The basis for the increased seizure rate in the cinacalcet group is unclear. Since the seizure threshold is lowered by significant reductions in serum calcium concentrations, patients with a history of seizure disorder taking cinacalcet should have serum calcium concentrations monitored closely.During post-marketing surveillance, idiosyncratic hypotension and heart failure have been reported in patients with preexisting impaired heart function (e.g., cardiac disease). A causal relationship to cinacalcet could not be completely excluded and may be mediated by reductions in calcium concentrations. It may be prudent to monitor calcium concentrations closely in patients with a history of cardiac disease.The AUC values for cinacalcet in patients with moderate to severe hepatic disease (as classified by the Child-Pugh method) are 2.4 and 4.2 times greater, respectively, than the values observed for normal patients. Cinacalcet should be used with caution in patients with moderate to severe hepatic disease; serum calcium and intact parathyroid hormone concentrations should be monitored throughout treatment.Cinacalcet has been categorized as pregnancy risk category C by the FDA. In rats or rabbits, there are no data suggesting teratogenicity or adverse fetal or pup effects. However, data from the manufacturer suggest that decreased fetal body weights may occur in conjunction with decreased maternal food consumption and body weight gain in rats receiving 1 to 4 times a comparable human oral dose; similar results are observed in rabbits. Rats receiving doses 2 to 3 times a comparable human oral dose exhibit maternal signs of hypocalcemia (periparturient mortality and early postnatal pup loss), and reductions in postnatal maternal and pup body-weight gain. Cinacalcet has also been shown to cross the placental barrier in rabbits. There are no adequate and well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Cinacalcet should be used during pregnancy only if the potential benefit justifies the potential risk to the fetus.It is not known if cinacalcet is excreted in human milk. Many drugs are excreted in human milk and there is a potential for adverse reactions in nursing infants. If cinacalcet is to be used during breast-feeding, do so with caution and careful monitoring of the nursing infant.The safety and efficacy of cinacalcet in children and adolescents have not been established.

    Headline
    hypocalcemia
    General Information
    Cinacalcet is contraindicated in patients with hypocalcemia, defined as serum calcium concentrations below the lower limit of normal, because it lowers serum calcium concentrations. Life-threatening events and fatalities related to hypocalcemia have been reported in patients treated with cinacalcet. Measure serum calcium concentrations within 1 week after dosage initiation or adjustment and then approximately once every month after establishing a maintenance dose. If serum calcium drops below normal, consider calcium supplementation, increasing the dose of calcium-based phosphate binders or vitamin D sterols, or temporarily withholding cinacalcet. The long-term safety and efficacy of cinacalcet in non-dialysis patients have not been established. Patients with chronic kidney disease not on dialysis who receive cinacalcet may be at increased risk for hypocalcemia, likely due to lower baseline serum calcium concentrations.
    Headline
    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components.
    Headline
    seizure disorder
    General Information
    Monitor serum calcium concentrations in people with a seizure disorder who are receiving cinacalcet. During clinical trials, seizures were reported with a higher incidence in patients treated with cinacalcet compared to placebo. While the basis for the difference in seizure rate is unclear, the seizure threshold is lowered by significant reductions in serum calcium concentrations.
    Headline
    congenital long QT syndrome, family history of sudden cardiac death, QT prolongation
    General Information
    Hypocalcemia induced by cinacalcet can lead to QT interval prolongation, increasing the risk of ventricular arrhythmias. This risk is heightened in patients with congenital long QT syndrome, a history of QT prolongation, a family history of sudden cardiac death or long QT syndrome, and in those with other predisposing conditions. Closely monitor corrected serum calcium concentrations and QT interval in at-risk individuals during cinacalcet treatment.
    Headline
    Child-Pugh class B, Child-Pugh class C, hepatic failure
    General Information
    Monitor serum calcium, serum phosphorus, and intact parathyroid hormone concentrations closely throughout cinacalcet treatment in people with moderate hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh class B) and severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh class C) or hepatic failure. Cinacalcet exposure (AUC) is significantly increased in patients with moderate and severe hepatic impairment.
    Headline
    esophagitis, gastritis, peptic ulcer disease, vomiting
    General Information
    Monitor patients with risk factors for upper GI bleeding (e.g., gastritis, esophagitis, peptic ulcer disease, severe vomiting) for worsening of common GI adverse reactions of nausea and vomiting and for signs and symptoms of GI bleeding and ulcerations associated with cinacalcet. Promptly evaluate and treat any suspected GI bleeding. Cases of GI bleeding, mostly upper GI bleeding, have occurred in patients receiving cinacalcet. The exact cause of GI bleeding in these patients is unknown.
    Headline
    heart failure
    General Information
    Use cinacalcet with caution in patients with heart failure; isolated cases of hypotension, worsening heart failure, and/or arrhythmia exacerbation were reported in patients with impaired cardiac function taking cinacalcet. A causal relationship to cinacalcet could not be excluded and may be due to a reduction in serum calcium concentrations.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Limited case reports of cinacalcet use during pregnancy are insufficient to inform a drug associated risk of adverse developmental outcomes. In animal reproduction studies, when female rats were exposed to cinacalcet during the period of organogenesis through to weaning at 2- to 3-times the systemic drug concentrations (based on AUC) at the maximum recommended human dose (MRHD) of 180 mg/day, peripartum and early postnatal pup loss and reduced pup body weight gain were observed in the presence of maternal hypocalcemia. Cinacalcet has been shown to cross the placental barrier in rabbits. In pregnant female rabbits given oral gavage doses of 2, 12, 25 mg/kg/day cinacalcet during gestation, no adverse fetal effects were observed (exposures less than the human oral dose of 180 mg/day based on AUC comparisons). Reductions in maternal food consumption and body weight gain were seen at doses of 12 and 25 mg/kg/day.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Use cinacalcet with caution during breast-feeding. There are no data on the presence of cinacalcet in human milk, its effects on the breast-fed child, or its effects on milk production. Cinacalcet is excreted into the milk of rats. Due to its large molecular weight and poor oral absorption, the presence cinacalcet in human milk is likely low. Cinacalcet use is not a reason to discontinue breast-feeding; however, the drug should only be used with careful infant monitoring during lactation.

Interactions
Acetaminophen; Caffeine; Dihydrocodeine: (Moderate) Monitor for reduced efficacy of dihydrocodeine and signs of opioid withdrawal if coadministration with cinacalcet is necessary; consider increasing the dose of dihydrocodeine as needed. If cinacalcet is discontinued, consider a dose reduction of dihydrocodeine and frequently monitor for signs of respiratory depression and sedation. Dihydrocodeine is primarily metabolized by CYP2D6 to dihydromorphine, and by CYP3A. Cinacalcet is a moderate inhibitor of CYP2D6. Concomitant use of dihydrocodeine with cinacalcet can increase dihydrocodeine plasma concentrations, but decrease the plasma concentration of the active metabolite, dihydromorphine; this may result in decreased efficacy or onset of a withdrawal syndrome in patients who have developed physical dependence.
Acetaminophen; Codeine: (Moderate) Concomitant use of codeine with cinacalcet may increase codeine plasma concentrations, but decrease the plasma concentration of the active metabolite, morphine, resulting in reduced efficacy or symptoms of opioid withdrawal. It is recommended to avoid this combination when codeine is being used for cough. If coadministration is necessary, monitor patients closely at frequent intervals and consider a dosage increase of codeine until stable drug effects are achieved. Discontinuation of cinacalcet could decrease codeine plasma concentrations and increase morphine plasma concentrations resulting in prolonged opioid adverse reactions, including hypotension, respiratory depression, profound sedation, coma, and death. If cinacalcet is discontinued, monitor the patient carefully and consider reducing the opioid dosage if appropriate. Codeine is primarily metabolized by CYP2D6 to morphine, and by CYP3A to norcodeine; norcodeine does not have analgesic properties. Cinacalcet is a moderate inhibitor of CYP2D6.

Concomitant use with another strong CYP3A inhibitor increased cinacalcet overall exposure by 127%.

Adverse Reactions
  Headline
  Adverse Reactions
  General Information
  Three double-blind placebo-controlled trials were conducted for up to 6 months involving 1126 patients (656 cinacalcet and 470 placebo) with chronic kidney disease on dialysis with secondary hyperparathyroidism. Adverse reactions occurring in > 5% of the cinacalcet treated patients were reported.
 
Gastrointestinal symptoms were the most frequently reported adverse reactions. Nausea/vomiting were reported by 31% and 27% of cinacalcet treated patients, respectively, while diarrhea was reported by 21% of the patients. (NOTE: nausea and vomiting were the most frequently reported adverse events by patients being treated for parathyroid carcinoma in a 10 subject open-label study). The incidence of serious adverse events were similar in both cinacalcet and placebo patients. Less frequent adverse events included myalgia (15% of patients), dizziness (10%), hypertension (7%), asthenia (7%), anorexia (6%), Non-cardiac chest pain (unspecified) (6%) and access infection (5%).
 
During clinical trials, seizures were observed in 1.4% (9/656) of patients receiving cinacalcet versus 0.4% (2/470) of patients receiving placebo. Five of the 9 patients who received cinacalcet had a history of seizure disorder, and 2 of these patients were receiving anti-seizure medications. Both of the patients receiving placebo had a history of seizures and were receiving anti-seizure medications. At baseline, approximately 5% of patients in both study groups had a history of seizures. The reason for the increased rate of seizures in the group of patients receiving cinacalcet is not known; however, significant reductions in serum calcium concentrations do decrease the seizure threshold. In patients with a history of seizures, calcium concentrations should be monitored closely during treatment with cinacalcet.
 
In a short study of chronic kidney disease patients on dialysis, 66% of patients receiving cinacalcet developed at least one serum calcium concentration 7.5 mg/dl, or if symptoms of hypocalcemia occur, calcium-containing phosphate binders and/or vitamin D sterols may be used to increase serum calcium concentrations. If the serum calcium concentration remains below 7.5 mg/dl or if symptoms persist or the dose of vitamin D cannot be increased, hold administration of cinacalcet until serum calcium concentrations reach 8.0 mg/ml, or symptoms of hypocalcemia have resolved. Treatment may then be restarted at the next lowest dose.
 
Adynamic bone disease (renal osteodystrophy) may develop if intact parathyroid hormone levels (iPTH) fall below 100 pg/ml as assessed using the standard Nichols IRMA. Three patients receiving cinacalcet during a clinical evaluation developed adynamic bone disease. Two of the patients had intact parathyroid hormone (iPTH) levels below 100 pg/ml at multiple times during the study. If IPTH levels fall below the National Kidney Foundation Disease Outcomes Quality Initiative (NKF-K/DOQI) recommended range of 150-300 pg/ml, cinacalcet and/or vitamin D sterols dosage should be reduced or discontinued.
 
During clinical trials both hypotension (7% cinacalcet patients vs. 12% placebo patients) and worsening heart failure (2% in both groups) were reported. Additionally, during post-marketing surveillance, isolated cases of hypotension, worsening heart failure and/or arrhythmia exacerbation have been reported in patients with impaired cardiac function taking cinacalcet. A causal relationship to cinacalcet can not be excluded, and may be due to a reduction in serum calcium concentrations.
 
Rash (unspecified) and hypersensitivity reactions have been reported with cinacalcet during post-marketing surveillance.
 
End-stage renal disease patients often have testosterone levels below the normal range. Total and free testosterone levels were decreased in male patients taking cinacalcet during trials. Total testosterone decreased by a median 15.8% and 0.6% in cinacalcet and placebo treated patients, respectively. Median free testosterone decreased 31.3% in cinacalcet treated patients and 16.3% in patients receiving placebo. However, the clinical significance of these reductions is not known.

Description
  Text
  Cinacalcet is an oral calcimimetic agent, and represents the first approved agent within this drug class. It is indicated for the treatment of adults with secondary hyperparathyroidism due to chronic kidney disease, with hypercalcemia associated with parathyroid carcinoma, or with severe hypercalcemia due to primary hyperparathyroidism who are unable to undergo parathyroidectomy. Cinacalcet directly reduces parathyroid hormone (PTH) while lowering the calcium-phosphorus product. This action is consistent with the National Kidney Foundation Disease Outcomes Quality Initiative (K/DOQI) clinical practice guidelines for bone metabolism and disease in chronic kidney disease. In a clinical trial, cinacalcet was shown to reduce high serum calcium levels in adults with parathyroid carcinoma (manufacturer data). Cinacalcet use for the treatment of secondary hyperparathyroidism in pediatric patients (6 to 17 years) with chronic kidney disease on dialysis was evaluated in 2 manufacturer-sponsored studies; however, the studies did not establish a safe and effective cinacalcet dosage in pediatric populations, and severe hypocalcemia with an associated fatality occurred in one pediatric recipient.Cinacalcet was granted orphan designation by the FDA and was originally FDA-approved in 2004.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  The course of chronic kidney disease leads to the development of altered calcium (hypocalcemia) and phosphorus (hyperphosphatemia) metabolism, increased parathyroid hormone (PTH) levels and eventually to subsequent bone disease and vascular and soft tissue calcifications. Hyperphosphatemia results from decreased renal elimination of phosphorus. Increased phosphorus levels directly suppress calcitriol production. Calcium levels decrease as a result of suppressed calcitriol production from the hyperphosphatemia and reduced calcium absorption from the GI tract. Through a feedback mechanism, hypocalcemia, hyperphosphatemia and decreased calcitriol production stimulate the release of parathyroid hormone and proliferation of parathyroid cells leading to secondary hyperparathyroidism. Bone resorption and formation are influenced by many hormonal and non-hormonal factors; hormonal factors affecting bone structure include altered PTH concentrations. When parathyroid hormone levels are too high, osteoclasts are stimulated and result in high bone turnover, leading to decreased cortical bone and bone strength and increased risk of fracture.
 
The calcium-sensing receptor on the surface of the chief cell of the parathyroid gland is the principal regulator of PTH secretion. Cinacalcet, mimicking calcium, increases the sensitivity of this calcium-sensing receptor, and PTH secretion is reduced. The reduction in PTH is associated with a concomitant decrease in serum calcium levels. The feedback loop is hindered and eventually slows the progression of bone disease and the systemic consequences of deranged mineral metabolism.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Oral Route
    Description
    After oral administration of cinacalcet, Cmax is achieved in approximately 2 to 6 hours. Administration with a high-fat meal increased Cmax and AUC by 82% and 68%, respectively, and increased 65% and 50% when given with a low-fat meal compared to a fasting state. The terminal half-life is 30 to 40 hours and steady-state drug concentrations are achieved within 7 days. The Cmax and AUC increase proportionally over the dose range of 30 to 180 mg PO once daily. However, the pharmacokinetic profile does not change over time with this dose range.

  Text
  Cinacalcet is administered orally. It is approximately 93 to 97% bound to plasma protein(s). The volume of distribution is approximately 1,000 L, indicating extensive distribution. The plasma concentrations of major circulating metabolites, including hydrocinnamic and glucuronidated dihydrodiols, markedly exceed parent drug concentrations. The hydrocinnamic metabolite was shown to be inactive while the glucuronide conjugates have minimal or no calcimimetic activity. The primary route of elimination of the metabolites was renal excretion. Approximately 80% of the oral dose was recovered in the urine and 15% in the feces.
 
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes and drug transporters: CYP2D6, CYP3A4, CYP1A2
Cinacalcet is a strong inhibitor of CYP2D6. It is metabolized by multiple enzymes, primarily CYP3A4, CYP2D6, and CYP1A2.
