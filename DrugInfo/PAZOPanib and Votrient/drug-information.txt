        800 mg orally once daily until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity. Coadministration of certain drugs may need to be avoided or dosage adjustments may be necessary; review drug interactions. Treatment with pazopanib significantly improved the median progression-free survival (PFS) time compared with placebo in patients with locally advanced and/or metastatic RCC who had received either no prior therapy or one prior cytokine-based systemic therapy in a randomized, double-blind, phase 3 trial. In the treatment-naive subgroup, the median PFS times were 11.1 months and 2.8 months in the pazopanib and placebo arms, respectively; in patients who previously received cytokine-based therapy, the median PFS times were 7.4 months and 4.2 months, respectively. The response rate was 30% in the pazopanib arm compared with 3% in the placebo arm; the median duration of response for patients treated with pazopanib was 58.7 weeks. Overall survival was 22.9 months for patients randomized to pazopanib and 20.5 months for the placebo arm, including 54% of patients who crossed over from placebo to pazopanib after progression.

    General Information

    Headline
    Child-Pugh class B, Child-Pugh class C, Gilbert syndrome, hepatic failure, hepatotoxicity, HLA-B*5701-positive
    General Information
    Severe and fatal hepatotoxicity has been observed in patients who received pazopanib in clinical trials; patients older than 65 years are at greater risk. Patients who are HLA-B*5701-positive may also be at increased risk. In a pooled pharmacogenetic analysis of data from 31 clinical studies (n = 2,101), grade 2 or higher increases in ALT occurred in 32% of HLA-B*57:01 allele carriers who received pazopanib as monotherapy or in combination with other agents, versus 19% of non-carriers; grade 3 or higher increases in ALT occurred in 19% versus 10% of patients, respectively. An interruption of therapy, dose reduction, or discontinuation of therapy may be necessary if hepatotoxicity occurs. Monitor liver function tests in all patients at baseline, then at weeks 3, 5, 7, and 9, at month 3 (week 12), month 4 (week 16), and then periodically, as clinically indicated; increase monitoring to weekly for patients with transaminitis. Mild, indirect hyperbilirubinemia may occur in patients with Gilbert syndrome; transaminitis in these should be managed as if it is isolated without hyperbilirubinemia. Pazopanib is not recommended for use in patients with severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh class C; or total bilirubin greater than 3 times the upper limit of normal [ULN] and any ALT) or hepatic failure. Consider alternatives to pazopanib in patients with moderate hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh class B; or total bilirubin greater than 1.5 to 3 times ULN and any ALT); a dose reduction is recommended for these patients if treatment is necessary.
    Headline
    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components.
    Headline
    ischemic stroke, myocardial infarction, thromboembolism, thrombophilia
    General Information
    Carefully consider the use of pazopanib in patients with a history of thrombophilia, thromboembolism, myocardial infarction, or ischemic stroke; treatment may increase the risk of arterial and/or venous thromboembolic events. Clinical trials of pazopanib excluded patients with an arterial thromboembolic event in the previous 6 months. Discontinue pazopanib in patients who develop arterial and/or venous thromboembolic events.
    Headline
    heart failure, thoracic radiation therapy
    General Information
    Use pazopanib with caution in patients with a history of heart failure, or in patients with conditions that could impair left ventricular function including coronary artery disease, uncontrolled hypertension, thoracic radiation therapy and previous anthracycline exposure; decreased left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) and congestive heart failure (CHF) have occurred in patients treated with pazopanib. Monitor patients for clinical signs or symptoms of congestive heart failure. Conduct baseline and periodic evaluations of LVEF in patients at risk for cardiac dysfunction, including those with previous anthracycline exposure. An interruption of therapy or discontinuation of therapy may be necessary depending on the severity of cardiac dysfunction.
    Headline
    bradycardia, cardiomyopathy, congenital long QT syndrome, coronary artery disease, females, geriatric, hypocalcemia, hypokalemia, hypomagnesemia, QT prolongation
    General Information
    Avoid use of pazopanib in people with baseline QT prolongation or who have conditions that may increase the risk of QT prolongation or torsade de pointes, including bradycardia, congenital long QT syndrome, hypocalcemia, hypokalemia, hypomagnesemia, geriatric adults, females, structural abnormalities that interfere with electrical conduction (e.g., cardiomyopathy, coronary artery disease, ischemic heart disease), or in those who have other additional risk factors for QT prolongation or torsade de pointes. The use of other medications that have been associated with QT prolongation or torsade de pointes may further increase risk. If pazopanib is used in these populations, monitor ECG and electrolytes (i.e., calcium, magnesium, and potassium) as clinically indicated; correct any hypokalemia, hypomagnesemia, and hypocalcemia.
    Headline
    bleeding disorder, GI bleeding, hemoptysis, intracranial bleeding
    General Information
    Use pazopanib with caution in patients with a bleeding disorder; pazopanib treatment can cause serious cases of bleeding, which may be fatal. Pazopanib has not been studied in patients with hemoptysis, intracranial bleeding, or clinically significant GI bleeding in the previous 6 months. Permanently discontinue pazopanib in patients who experience severe or life-threatening hemorrhage.
    Headline
    major surgery, planned procedure
    General Information
    Do not administer pazopanib for at least 1 week prior to a planned procedure or elective surgery. After major surgery, wait at least 2 weeks and until adequate wound healing prior to restarting pazopanib therapy. Impaired wound healing can occur in patients who receive drugs that inhibit the vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) signaling pathway such as pazopanib. The safety of resuming treatment after resolution of wound healing complications has not been established.
    Headline
    pulmonary disease
    General Information
    Use pazopanib with caution in patients who have a history of pre-existing pulmonary disease; severe pneumonitis/interstitial lung disease has been reported in patients treated with pazopanib. Monitor patients for new or worsening respiratory symptoms including fever, cough, or dyspnea. Immediately interrupt pazopanib therapy in patients with new or worsening respiratory symptoms and permanently discontinue therapy if no other potential causes are identified.
    Headline
    hypertension
    General Information
    Do not begin therapy with pazopanib in patients with uncontrolled hypertension. Monitor blood pressure as clinically indicated and initiate or adjust antihypertensive therapy as appropriate. An interruption of therapy, dose reduction, or discontinuation of therapy may be necessary depending on the severity of hypertension.
    Headline
    fistula, GI perforation
    General Information
    Use pazopanib with caution in patients who have a history of fistula formation or GI perforation; each has been reported with pazopanib therapy. Permanently discontinue pazopanib in patients who develop a GI perforation of any severity or grade 4 fistula.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Pazopanib can cause fetal harm if used during pregnancy, based on its mechanism of action and data from animal studies. In rats and rabbits, pazopanib administration during organogenesis resulted in teratogenicity and abortion at exposures lower than the maximum recommended human dose (MRHD) based on AUC. Teratogenic effects included cardiovascular malformations, incomplete or absent ossification, increases in postimplantation loss, embryo-lethality, and reduced fetal body weight. Advise pregnant individuals of the potential fetal risk.
    Headline
    people who can cause pregnancy in others, people who may become pregnant, reproductive risk
    General Information
    Counsel people who may become pregnant and people who can cause pregnancy in others about the reproductive risk associated with pazopanib. Pazopanib can cause fetal harm. Verify pregnancy status prior to treatment initiation. Advise people who may become pregnant to use effective contraception while taking pazopanib and for at least 2 weeks after the last dose. Counsel people who can cause pregnancy in others (including those who have had a vasectomy) with partners of reproductive potential to use condoms while taking pazopanib and for at least 2 weeks after the last dose. Based on animal data, pazopanib may impair male and female fertility.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Advise patients to avoid breast-feeding while taking pazopanib and for 2 weeks after the last dose. Pazopanib has the potential to cause serious side effects in the breastfed child. There are no data on the presence of pazopanib or its metabolites in human milk, their effects on the breastfed child, or their effects on milk production.

Interactions
Abrocitinib: (Major) Avoid coadministration of pazopanib and abrocitinib due to the potential for increased pazopanib exposure. Pazopanib is a P-gp substrate; abrocitinib is a P-gp inhibitor. Consider selection of an alternative concomitant medication with no or minimal potential to inhibit P-gp.
Acalabrutinib: (Moderate) Coadministration of acalabrutinib and pazopanib may increase pazopanib exposure and increase the risk of pazopanib toxicity. Acalabrutinib is an inhibitor of the breast cancer resistance protein (BCRP) transporter in vitro; it may inhibit intestinal BCRP. Pazopanib is a BCRP substrate.
Acetaminophen; Caffeine; Dihydrocodeine: (Moderate) Concomitant use of dihydrocodeine with pazopanib may alter dihydrocodeine plasma concentrations, resulting in an unpredictable effect such as reduced efficacy or symptoms of opioid withdrawal or prolonged opioid adverse reactions, including hypotension, respiratory depression, profound sedation, coma, and death. If coadministration is necessary, monitor patients closely at frequent intervals and consider a dosage adjustment of dihydrocodeine until stable drug effects are achieved. Discontinuation of pazopanib could alter dihydrocodeine plasma concentrations, resulting in an unpredictable effect such as prolonged opioid adverse reactions or decreased opioid efficacy, and potentially lead to a withdrawal syndrome in those with physical dependence to dihydrocodeine. If pazopanib is discontinued, monitor the patient carefully and consider adjusting the opioid dosage if appropriate. Dihydrocodeine is primarily metabolized by CYP2D6 to dihydromorphine, and by CYP3A4. Pazopanib is a weak inhibitor of CYP3A4 and CYP2D6. CYP3A4 inhibitors may increase dihydrocodeine-related adverse effects while CYP2D6 inhibitors may reduce efficacy.

cloZAPine: (Major) It is unclear if concurrent use of other drugs known to cause neutropenia (e.g., antineoplastic agents) increases the risk or severity of clozapine-induced neutropenia. Because there is no strong rationale for avoiding clozapine in patients treated with these drugs, consider increased absolute neutrophil count (ANC) monitoring and consult the treating oncologist. Additionally, in vitro studies have shown that pazopanib has the potential to prolong cardiac ventricular repolarization (prolong QT interval). Clozapine therapy may lead to S-T segment depression and flattening or inversion of T waves. In theory, concurrent use of pazopanib and clozapine could produce clinically significant prolongation of the QTc interval. Pazopanib is also a weak inhibitor of CYP3A4. Coadministration of pazopanib and clozapine, a CYP3A4 substrate, may cause an increase in systemic concentrations of clozapine. Elevated plasma concentrations of clozapine may increase the risk for QT prolongation, torsade de pointes (TdP), sedation, anticholinergic effects, seizures, orthostasis, or other adverse effects. According to the manufacturer, patients receiving clozapine in combination with an inhibitor of CYP3A4 should be monitored for adverse reactions. Consideration should be given to reducing the clozapine dose if necessary. If the inhibitor is discontinued after dose adjustments are made, monitor for lack of clozapine effectiveness and consider increasing the clozapine dose if necessary.

Cobimetinib is a CYP3A substrate in vitro as well as a P-glycoprotein (P-gp) substrate; pazopanib is a weak inhibitor of CYP3A and a moderate P-gp inhibitor. In healthy subjects (n = 15), coadministration of a single 10 mg dose of cobimetinib with itraconazole (200 mg once daily for 14 days), a strong CYP3A4 inhibitor, increased the mean cobimetinib AUC by 6.7-fold (90% CI, 5.6 to 8) and the mean Cmax by 3.2-fold (90% CI, 2.7 to 3.7). Simulations showed that predicted steady-state concentrations of cobimetinib at a reduced dose of 20 mg administered concurrently with short-term (less than 14 days) treatment of a moderate CYP3A inhibitor were similar to observed steady-state concentrations of cobimetinib 60 mg alone. The manufacturer of cobimetinib recommends avoiding coadministration with moderate to strong CYP3A inhibitors, and significantly reducing the dose of cobimetinib if coadministration with moderate CYP3A inhibitors cannot be avoided. Guidance is not available regarding concomitant use of cobimetinib with weak CYP3A inhibitors.

If concomitant administration with a gastric acid-reducing agent is unavoidable, consider the use of a short-acting antacid in place of an H2-blocker; separate administration of the short-acting antacid and pazopanib by several hours to avoid a reduction in pazopanib exposure. Concomitant use of pazopanib with a proton pump inhibitor decreased pazopanib exposure (AUC and Cmax) by approximately 40%.

    Headline
    elevated hepatic enzymes, hepatic failure, hepatotoxicity, hyperbilirubinemia
    General Information
    Hepatotoxicity, manifested as elevated hepatic enzymes and hyperbilirubinemia, has occurred in patients treated with pazopanib; it may be severe and fatal. Increased ALT (46% to 53%; grade 3 or 4, 10% to 12%), AST (51% to 53%; grade 3 or 4, 8% or less), and total bilirubin (29% to 36%; grade 3 or 4, 1% to less than 4%) occurred in patients treated with pazopanib in 2 randomized, placebo-controlled clinical trials. Most (92%) transaminase elevations occur within the first 18 weeks of treatment. Elevations in ALT greater than 3 times the upper limit of normal (ULN) occurred in 18% of pazopanib-treated patients in clinical trials; ALT elevations greater than 8 times ULN occurred in 4% to 5% of patients. Concurrent elevations in ALT greater than 3 times ULN and bilirubin greater than 2 times ULN (without significant alkaline phosphatase greater than 3 times ULN) occurred in 2% of patients. Three patients died of hepatic failure, two of whom had disease progression. Across STS trials, patients aged 65 or older had a higher incidence of grade 3 or 4 ALT elevations (3% vs. 2%) and AST elevations (4% vs. 1%) compared to patients younger than 65 years. Because pazopanib inhibits UGT1A1 which glucuronidates bilirubin for elimination, mild, indirect (unconjugated) hyperbilirubinemia may occur in patients with Gilbert's syndrome who may have the (TA)7/(TA)7 genotype (UGT1A1*28/*28). Based on the severity of hepatotoxicity, an interruption of therapy, dose reduction, or discontinuation of therapy may be necessary.

Description
  Text
  Pazopanib is an oral multi-tyrosine kinase (e.g., VEGFR, PDGF, FGFR, IL-2) inhibitor. It is approved for use in adults with advanced renal cell carcinoma and advanced soft tissue sarcoma. Pazopanib has a black box warning for hepatotoxicity.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Pazopanib is a multi-tyrosine kinase inhibitor that blocks vascular endothelial growth factor receptor (VEGFR)-1, VEGFR-2, VEGFR-3; platelet-derived growth factor receptor (PDGFR)-alpha and -beta; fibroblast growth factor receptor (FGFR)-1 and -3; cytokine receptor (Kit); interleukin-2 receptor inducible T-cell kinase (Itk); leukocyte-specific protein tyrosine kinase (Lck); and transmembrane glycoprotein receptor tyrosine kinase (c-Fms). In vitro, pazopanib inhibited ligand-induced autophosphorylation of VEGFR-2, Kit, and PDGFR-beta receptors. In mice, pazopanib inhibited VEGF-induced VEGFR-2 phosphorylation in mouse lungs, angiogenesis, and the growth of some human tumor xenografts. In patients with clear cell renal cell cancer (RCC), the most common histology of RCC, inactivation of the von-Hippel Lindau (VHL) gene, is present. Suppression of this gene leads to upregulation of a number of hypoxia-induced genes including VEGF and PDGF. VEGF induces a mitogenic response upon binding to one of the VEGF receptors (VEGFR-1 to -3) on endothelial cells. Transforming growth factor-alpha (TGF-alpha) is also regulated by the VHL gene; TGF-alpha acts as a ligand for EGF receptor and stimulates the growth of epithelial cells of the proximal renal tubule, where most RCCs appear to start.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Oral Route
    Description
    After oral administration, the median time to achieve peak concentrations of pazopanib (Tmax) is 2 to 4 hours after the dose. Doses of 800 mg once daily resulted in a mean AUC value of 1,037 micrograms (mcg) x hour/mL and a Cmax value of 58.1 mcg/mL. Doses above 800 mg did not result in a consistent increase in AUC or Cmax values. Administration of a crushed 400 mg tablet increased the AUC value by 46% and increased the Cmax value by approximately 2-fold compared to administration of a whole tablet; the Tmax decreased by approximately 2 hours.
Effects of food: Systemic exposure to pazopanib is increased when it is administered with food. The AUC and Cmax values were increased by approximately 2-fold when pazopanib was administered with a high-fat (approximately 50% fat) or low-fat (approximately 5% fat) meal.
  Special Populations
    Name
    Hepatic Impairment
    Description
    In patients with mild hepatic impairment (total bilirubin level less than or equal to the ULN and ALT level more than the ULN OR total bilirubin level of more than 1 to 1.5 times the ULN and any ALT level), the median steady-state Cmax value of pazopanib (34 [range, 11 to 104] microgram (mcg)/mL) was similar to patients with no hepatic impairment (52 [range, 17 to 86] mcg/mL) following pazopanib 800 mg once daily; the median AUC value was 774 (range, 215 to 2,034) mcg x hour/mL versus 888 (range, 346 to 1,482) mcg x hour/mL, respectively. Following pazopanib 200 once daily in patients with moderate hepatic impairment, the median Cmax value was 22 (range, 4.2 to 33) mcg/mL and the median AUC value was 257 (range, 66 to 488) mcg x hour/mL.   Text
  Pazopanib is administered orally. It exhibits greater than 99% binding to human plasma protein in vivo, with no concentration dependence over the range of 10 to 100 micrograms (mcg)/mL. It has a mean elimination half-life of 31 hours. Elimination is primarily through the feces with a minor contribution from renal elimination (less than 4%).
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes and drug transporters: CYP3A4, CYP1A2, CYP2C8, P-glycoprotein (P-gp), BCRP
Pazopanib is metabolized primarily by CYP3A4 with a minor contribution from CYP1A2 and CYP2C8; in vitro studies also suggest that it is a substrate for P-gp and BCRP. Pazopanib is a weak inhibitor of CYP3A4, CYP2C8, CYP2D6, UGT1A1, and OATP1B1. In vitro studies with human liver microsomes showed that pazopanib inhibited the activities of CYP1A2, CYP2B6, CYP2C9, CYP2C19, and CYP2E1. In vitro, it inhibits UGT1A1 and OATP1B1 with IC50s of 1.2 and 0.79 micromolar, respectively. Potential induction of CYP3A4 was demonstrated in an in vitro human pregnane X receptor (PXR) assay.

Dosage Adjustment Guidelines
  Hepatic Impairment
  Baseline Hepatic Impairment:
Mild hepatic impairment (total bilirubin level at or below the ULN and ALT level more than the ULN OR total bilirubin more than 1 to 1.5 times ULN with any ALT level): No dosage adjustment necessary.
Moderate hepatic impairment (total bilirubin level more than 1.5 to 3 times the ULN with any ALT level): Consider an alternative agent; otherwise, reduce the dose of pazopanib to 200 mg PO once daily.
Severe hepatic impairment (total bilirubin level more than 3 times the ULN with any ALT level): Use not recommended.
Treatment-Related Hepatotoxicity:
Isolated ALT elevations of 3 to 8 times the ULN: Continue pazopanib therapy. Monitor liver function weekly until ALT returns to grade 1 or baseline.
Isolated ALT elevations of more than 8 times the ULN: Hold pazopanib therapy. If the potential benefit of pazopanib treatment outweighs the risk of hepatotoxicity, treatment may be resumed at a reduced dose of no more than 400 mg PO once daily when the ALT level improves to grade 1 or baseline. Monitor liver function tests weekly for 8 weeks. Permanently discontinue pazopanib treatment if ALT elevations of more than 3 times ULN recur despite dose reductions.
ALT elevations more than 3 times the ULN with concurrent mild, indirect (unconjugated) hyperbilirubinemia (Gilbert's syndrome): Manage per recommendations for isolated ALT elevations.
ALT elevations more than 3 times the ULN with concurrent bilirubin more than 2 times the ULN: Permanently discontinue pazopanib therapy and monitor liver function tests until resolution.
  Renal Impairment
  No dosage adjustments are recommended for renal impairment: however, pazopanib has not been evaluated in patients with severe renal impairment or who are undergoing peritoneal dialysis or hemodialysis.
