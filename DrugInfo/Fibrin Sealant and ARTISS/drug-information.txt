    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components. Fibrin sealant, human is also contraindicated in individuals with known anaphylactic or severe systemic reaction to human blood products. TachoSil fibrin sealant patch is contraindicated in individuals with known anaphylactic or severe systemic reaction to horse proteins. Additionally, Artiss and Tisseel are contraindicated in individuals with known hypersensitivity to aprotinin.
    Headline
    Treatment-Related Restrictions of Use
    General Information
    Fibrin sealant is contraindicated for severe or brisk arterial or venous bleeding, as the sealant will be washed away in the flow of blood before hemostasis can be achieved.
The safety and effectiveness of Tisseel in neurosurgical procedures or other surgeries involving confined spaces have not been evaluated.
    Headline
    intraarterial administration, intravenous administration
    General Information
    The intravascular administration (i.e., intravenous administration or intraarterial administration) of fibrin sealant is contraindicated; life-threatening thromboembolic events may occur, and the likelihood and severity of acute hypersensitivity reactions may increase in susceptible individuals. Do not inject fibrin sealant into highly vascularized tissue, such as nasal mucosa. To minimize the risk of intravascular application, use caution when using fibrin sealant in surgery.
    Headline
    neonates, premature neonates
    General Information
    The use of TachoSil fibrin sealant patch in premature neonates or neonates may be unsafe or ineffective due to small size and limited ability to apply the patch as recommended.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    There are no data on the use of fibrin sealant during pregnancy to evaluate for a drug-associated risk. Animal studies have not been conducted. Because fibrin sealant is made from human plasma, it may carry a risk of transmitting infectious agents. Some viruses, such as parvovirus B19, are difficult to remove or inactivate. Parvovirus B19 infection during pregnancy can result in fetal infection and serious fetal complications. Use fibrin sealant during pregnancy only if clearly needed.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Use fibrin sealant with caution during breast-feeding. There are no data on the presence of fibrin sealant in human milk, its effects on the breastfed child, or its effects on milk production. Consider the benefits of breast-feeding, the patient's clinical need for treatment, and any potential adverse effects on the breastfed child from the medication or from the patient's underlying medical condition.

    General Information
    Hypersensitivity reactions, including fatal anaphylactoid reactions and anaphylactic shock, have been reported with fibrin sealant, human. Such reactions may especially be seen if fibrin sealant is applied repeatedly over time or in the same setting, or for those receiving Artiss or Tisseel, if systemic aprotinin has been previously administered. If a hypersensitivity reaction occurs, discontinue administration of fibrin sealant, and remove the remaining product from the application site and the already polymerized product from the surgical field. Mild reactions may be managed with antihistamines; severe hypotensive reactions require immediate intervention using current principles of shock therapy.     air embolism
    General Information
    Fatal air embolism has been reported with the use of fibrin sealant when applied using a spray device. These reports occurred when the fibrin sealant was sprayed at a pressure higher than indicated for the device in use and when the spray tip was placed closer than the specified distance from the target site. The risk appears to be higher when fibrin sealants are sprayed with air, as compared to CO2. The solubility of compressed CO2 is greater than either compressed N2 or air, thereby reducing the potential effect of embolization. Regardless of the type of gas used, to reduce the incidence of embolization, spray fibrin sealant, human using only the recommended regulator, set within the recommended pressure range, and with the appropriate applicator positioned at the recommended distance. When applying fibrin sealant using a spray device, use pressure within the pressure range and spray from distance specified by the manufacturer. If specific recommendations are not available, avoid using pressure above 20 to 25 psi and spraying closer than 5 to 15 cm from the surface of the tissue. Monitor for changes in blood pressure, pulse, oxygen saturation, and end-tidal carbon dioxide when spraying fibrin sealant.

Description
  Text
  Fibrin sealant, human is a human plasma-derived, fibrinogen and thrombin sealant developed to promote the adherence of autologous skin grafts to burn wounds, to adhere tissue flaps during facial rhytidectomy surgery, for hemostasis of surgical bleeding when control of bleeding by standard surgical techniques is ineffective or impractical, and for colonic anastomoses leakage prophylaxis after temporary colostomy reversal. Fibrin sealant is for topical use only. Intravascular administration of fibrin sealant is contraindicated; life-threatening thromboembolic events may occur and the likelihood and severity of acute hypersensitivity reactions may increase in susceptible patients. Fibrin sealant, human was FDA-approved in 1998.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  The fibrin sealant system initiates the last phase of physiological blood coagulation. Thrombin activates the conversion of fibrinogen into fibrin, which occurs by splitting fibrinogen into fibrinogen monomers and fibrinopeptides. The fibrinogen monomers polymerize and form a fibrin clot. Factor XIIIa, which is activated from Factor XIII by thrombin, crosslinks fibrin. Hemostasis or sealing of tissues is achieved when the formed fibrin clot adheres to the wound surface. Artiss and Tisseel also contain aprotinin, which has been shown to increase resistance of the fibrin sealant clot to degradation in a fibrinolytic environment.

    In rabbit studies of thrombin applied to the cut surface of the liver, slow absorption of biologically inactive peptides resulting from the breakdown of thrombin occurred. TachoSil
Preparation
When in the operating room, the outer aluminum foil pouch may be opened in a non-sterile environment. The inner sterile blister must be opened in a sterile environment.
Remove the patch from the blister. If needed, the blister can be used as a container for pre-moistening of the patch.
Determine the size of patch(es) to be applied to the bleeding surface. Select the appropriate patch so that it extends 1 to 2 cm beyond the margins of the wound. The patch can be cut to the correct size and shape, if desired.
TachoSil contains collagen, which may adhere to surfaces that bleed. To prevent the development of tissue adhesions at undesired sites, adequately cleanse tissue areas outside the desired application area before TachoSil administration.
Cleanse the area to be treated prior to application to remove disinfectants and other fluids. The sealer protein and thrombin solutions of TachoSil can be denatured by alcohol, iodine, or heavy metal ions found in antiseptics. If these substances have been used to clean the wound area, thoroughly rinse and dry prior to application of fibrin sealant.
Cleanse surgical instruments, gloves, and adjacent tissues with saline solution to reduce adherence to the fibrin sealant patch. The white, inactive side of the patch may also adhere to surgical instruments, gloves, and adjacent tissues covered with blood. Failure to adequately clean adjacent tissues may cause adhesions.
Application
To ensure effective adherence, follow the recommended handling, preparation, and application procedures. Incorrect handling may result in non-adherence and reduced efficacy.
Avoid application of TachoSil to contaminated or infected areas of the body, or in the presence of active infection.
Avoid packing when placing TachoSil patches into cavities or closed spaces; this may cause compression of underlying tissue. Use the minimum number of patches necessary to achieve hemostasis.
Apply the yellow, active side of the fibrin sealant patch directly to the bleeding area either wet or dry. If applied wet, pre-moisten the patch in 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection for no more than 1 minute and then apply immediately. In the case of a wet tissue surface, such as oozing bleeding, the patch may be applied without pre-moistening.
Hold patch in place with gentle pressure applied through moistened gloves or a moist pad for at least 3 minutes.
To avoid pulling the patch loose, place a pre-moistened surgical instrument at 1 end of the patch before relieving pressure. Alternatively, gentle irrigation may also be used to remove the pre-moistened pad or gloved hand without removing the patch from the bleeding area.
Leave the patch in place once it adheres to organ tissue. If not satisfied with patch placement, or if bleeding still occurs after the specified duration of compression, repeat application procedure; do not remove already applied fibrin sealant patch.
If more than 1 patch is needed, overlap patches by at least 1 cm.
Remove unattached patches (or part of) and replace with new.
For record-keeping purposes, the patient name and batch number should be recorded with every administration.
Storage: Once removed from inner patch, TachoSil cannot be resterilized; discard unused, opened packages.
