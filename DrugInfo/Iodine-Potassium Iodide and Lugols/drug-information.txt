    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Iodine; potassium iodide, KI has not been evaluated by the FDA for safety and efficacy, and manufacturer labeling has not been approved by the FDA; an FDA pregnancy risk category classification is not available. Iodides cross the placenta and may result in abnormal thyroid function and/or goiter in the neonate. Although prolonged therapy is generally considered contraindicated in pregnant women, short courses have been used in pregnant patients with thyrotoxic crisis that develops during labor or in preparation for thyroidectomy, without evidence of adverse fetal effects.

If a breast-feeding infant experiences an adverse effect related to a maternally ingested drug, healthcare providers are encouraged to report the adverse effect to the FDA.

Description
  Text
  Iodine; potassium iodide, KI is most commonly known as Lugol's solution.   It is a strong iodine solution containing 5% iodine and at least 10% potassium iodide. The oral solution is used as adjunctive therapy for hyperthyroidism, to reduce thyroid gland vascularity, prior to thyroidectomy. Iodides help induce a euthyroid state, facilitating surgery and decreasing postoperative complications. Iodine; potassium iodide can also be used for thyrotoxicosis in conjunction with supportive measures to control fever and adequate fluid intake. It is not intended for long term treatment of hyperthyroidism as, after a variable period of time, the beneficial effects wear off and the condition may return in its initial intensity or become more severe. Optimal control of hyperthyroidism prior to thyroidectomy is usually achieved if therapy is initiated with an antithyroid drug and then iodine; potassium iodide is added on as an adjunct, though iodine; potassium iodide could potentially be used alone. The topical formulation is used as an antiseptic for its' antimicrobial and antifungal properties. Iodine; potassium iodide, KI has been marketed since 1940, prior to when Congress passed the 1962 amended Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act, requiring that the FDA establish both safety and efficacy for all approved drugs.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Systemically, iodides inhibit thyroid hormone release and interrupt of iodotyrosine and iodothyronine synthesis by blocking organification. Large doses increase firmness of the thyroid gland by decreasing its size, vascularity, and friability. Following oral administration, effects on thyroid function are usually observed within 24 hours and are maximal after 10 to 15 days of continuous therapy.
