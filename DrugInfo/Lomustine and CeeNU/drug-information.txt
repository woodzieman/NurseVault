Drug Class: Lomustine is a nitrosourea alkylating agent that is indicated for the treatment of Hodgkin lymphoma and brain cancers.

Routes: oral; Common adult doses: 130 mg/m2 PO (repeated no sooner than every 6 weeks) is FDA approved; 200 mg/m2 PO has been studied off-label as induction therapy in AML patients 60 years and older in combination with other chemotherapy.

Common Side Effects (excluding routine GI upset): bleeding, bone marrow suppression, infection, leukopenia, thrombocytopenia, ataxia, dysarthria, lethargy

In an Overdose: Prescribe, dispense, and administer only 1 lomustine dose at a time to minimize the risk of an overdose; lomustine is administered at a minimum of 6-week intervals.

Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  General Information
  Severe bone marrow suppression, including leukopenia, neutropenia, and thrombocytopenia, is a relative contraindication to systemic lomustine depending upon the underlying disease of the patient. Patients with aggressive Hodgkin's disease may require treatment with lomustine despite severe bone marrow suppression. Bone marrow suppression can occur both during therapy and up to 6 weeks after cessation of therapy. Therefore, this drug should be used only by clinicians experienced in the use of cancer treatments. Complete blood counts should be monitored weekly for at least 6 weeks after a dose. At the recommended dosage, courses of lomustine should not be given more frequently than every 6 weeks. Myelotoxicity of lomustine is cumulative and therefore dosage adjustments should be considered on the basis of nadir blood counts from the prior dose (see Dosage). Lomustine should be used cautiously in patients who have had previous myelosuppressive therapy such as chemotherapy or radiation therapy; a reduced initial dose is suggested in these patients (see Dosage). Concurrent cranial radiation therapy has been associated with increased toxicity (see Adverse Reactions). Patients with an active infection should be treated prior to receiving lomustine. Patients with a history of varicella zoster, other herpes infection (i.e., herpes simplex), or other viral infection are at risk for reactivation of the infection when treated with chemotherapy.
 
Myelosuppressive effects of lomustine can increase the risk of infection or bleeding; therefore, dental work should be delayed until blood counts have returned to normal. Patients, especially those with dental disease, should be instructed in proper oral hygiene, including caution in use of regular toothbrushes, dental floss, and toothpicks.
 
Intramuscular injections should not be administered to patients with platelet counts 1100 mg/m2.
 
Lomustine is classified as FDA pregnancy risk category D. Lomustine can harm the fetus if administered during pregnancy, although definitive testing has not been completed, and the potential benefits of the drug to the mother may outweigh the potentially detrimental effects to the fetus. Lomustine should be given to a pregnant women only in life-threatening situations that will not respond to safer therapy. Females of childbearing potential should use effective contraception during lomustine therapy.
 
Lomustine is excreted into breast milk. Its use during breast-feeding is generally not recommended due to the carcinogenic, teratogenic, and mutagenic effects of this agent.
 
Use care to avoid accidental exposure to lomustine during preparation, handling, and administration. The use of protective gowns, gloves and goggles is recommended. Following skin or ocular exposure, skin and eyes should be thoroughly rinsed.
 
Vaccination during chemotherapy or radiation therapy should be avoided because the antibody response is suboptimal. When chemotherapy is being planned, vaccination should precede the initiation of chemotherapy by >= 2 weeks. The administration of live vaccines to immunocompromised patients should be avoided. Those undergoing chemotherapy should not be exposed to others who have recently received the oral poliovirus vaccine (OPV). Measles-mumps-rubella (MMR) vaccination is not contraindicated for the close contacts, including health care professionals, of immunocompromised patients. Passive immunoprophylaxis with immune globulins may be indicated for immunocompromised persons instead of, or in addition to, vaccination. When exposed to a vaccine-preventable disease such as measles, severely immunocompromised children should be considered susceptible regardless of their vaccination history.

    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components.
    Headline
    bone marrow suppression
    General Information
    Delayed, dose-related, cumulative, and sometimes fatal bone marrow suppression/myelosuppression (e.g., leukopenia, thrombocytopenia) resulting in fatal infection and bleeding has been reported with lomustine therapy. Monitor complete blood counts during therapy and for at least 6 weeks after each dose. Do not administer lomustine more frequently than every 6 weeks. Myelosuppression typically occurs 4 to 6 weeks after drug administration and persists for 1 to 2 weeks. A dose reduction may be necessary based on nadir blood counts and in patients with compromised bone marrow function and/or patients receiving concomitant myelosuppressive chemotherapy.
    Headline
    potential for overdose or poisoning
    General Information
    Fatal toxicity has been reported with lomustine overdose; lomustine has a potential for overdose or poisoning. Do not prescribe, dispense, or administer more than one dose at a time. Physicians and pharmacists should inform patients that only one lomustine dose should be taken no sooner than every 6 weeks.
    Headline
    pulmonary disease
    General Information
    Use lomustine with caution in patients with a history of pulmonary disease. Pulmonary toxicity (e.g., pulmonary infiltrates and pulmonary fibrosis) has been reported with lomustine therapy. Patients with pulmonary disease or a predicted forced vital capacity (FVC) or carbon monoxide diffusing capacity (DLCO) less than 70% at baseline are at increased risk for developing lomustine-induced pulmonary toxicity. The onset of toxicity typically occurs 6 months or more after starting lomustine and with cumulative doses exceeding 1,100 mg/m2. Obtain baseline pulmonary function tests prior to starting lomustine and frequently during treatment; permanently discontinue lomustine in patients diagnosed with pulmonary fibrosis.
    Headline
    renal failure, renal impairment
    General Information
    Use lomustine with caution in patients with renal impairment, including renal failure. Nephrotoxicity including progressive renal failure with a decrease in kidney size has been reported with lomustine therapy. Patients with impaired renal function may be at increased risk of toxic reactions. Monitor renal function and serum electrolytes during therapy.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    Lomustine may cause fetal harm if it is administered during pregnancy, based on animal data and its mechanism of action. Advise pregnant people of the potential fetal risk. Embryo-fetal toxicity and teratogenicity have been observed when lomustine was administered daily in pregnant rats and rabbits at doses approximately 2- to 4-times the total human dose of 130 mg/m2 over 6 weeks (0.18- to 0.27-times the single human dose of 130 mg/m2) based on body surface area. Malformations (i.e., omphalocele, ectopia cordis, scoliosis, syndactyly, hydrocephalus, microphthalmia, anophthalmia, anomalies of aortic arch, dextrocardia, malpositioning of the ovaries and testes, sternoschisis, and shortened/misshapen bone of the fore or hind limbs) and decreased fetal body weight were observed following the administration of daily intraperitoneal lomustine (at dose levels of 0, 2, 4, 6, and 8 mg/kg) in pregnant rats during organogenesis.
    Headline
    people who can cause pregnancy in others, people who may become pregnant, reproductive risk
    General Information
    Counsel people who may become pregnant and people who can cause pregnancy in others about the reproductive risk associated with lomustine. Lomustine can cause fetal harm. Advise people who may become pregnant to use effective contraception during treatment with lomustine and for 2 weeks after the last dose. For those that can cause pregnancy in others, contraception should be used during lomustine treatment and for 3.5 months after the last dose. Based on animal findings and its mechanism of action, there is a risk of infertility with lomustine therapy. In pregnant rats, higher resorption rates and post-implantation loss were observed following the administration of daily intraperitoneal lomustine (at dose levels approximately 0.18 times the clinical dose of 130 mg/m2 based on body surface area (BSA) or approximately 2 times the total clinical dose of lomustine over 6 weeks). Increased abortion rates occurred following lomustine administration (at doses approximately 0.27-times the 130 mg/m2 clinical dose based on BSA or approximately 4-times the total clinical dose of lomustine over 6 weeks) in pregnant rabbits.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Advise patients to avoid breast-feeding while taking lomustine and for 2 weeks after the last dose, due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in the breast-fed child. There are no data on the presence of lomustine in human milk, its effects on the breast-fed child, or its effects on milk production.

Adverse Reactions
  Headline
  Adverse Reactions
  General Information
  Delayed and cumulative myelosuppression is the major adverse effect of lomustine and includes pancytopenia with neutropenia, leukopenia, thrombocytopenia, and anemia. Thrombocytopenia typically occurs 26—34 days after starting therapy and lasts between 1—2 weeks. Neutropenia occurs days 41—46 and lasts about 9—14 days. Approximately 65% of patients receiving lomustine 130 mg/m2 develop leukopenia 1100 mg/m2. There is at least one report of pulmonary toxicity occurring at a cumulative dose of 600 mg/m2. The onset is insidious, and symptoms include cough and dyspnea. A chest X-ray shows an interstitial process. PFT's reveal a restrictive process. Delayed onset pulmonary fibrosis occurring up to 17 years after treatment has been reported in patients who received related nitrosoureas in childhood and early adolescence (1—16 years) combined with cranial radiation therapy for intracranial tumors. There appears to be some late reduction of pulmonary function of all long-term survivors. This form of pulmonary fibrosis may be slowly progressive and has resulted in death in some cases.As with other nitrosoureas, lomustine is associated with the development of secondary malignancy. An increased risk of myelodysplasia has been reported when lomustine is combined with alkylating agents in patients with Hodgkin's disease.

Description
  Text
  Lomustine is a nitrosourea alkylating agent that is indicated for the treatment of Hodgkin lymphoma and brain cancers. Fatal myelosuppression has been reported. Prescribe, dispense, and administer only 1 lomustine dose at a time to minimize the risk of an overdose; lomustine is administered at a minimum of 6-week intervals.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Mechanism of Action: Nitrosourea antineoplastic agents are alkylating agents. This class of drugs work by inhibiting DNA replication, RNA transcription, and nucleic acid function. The drug is believed to inhibit DNA replication, RNA transcription, and nucleic acid function. Lomustine also may modify cellular proteins. Specifically, lomustine possesses a chloroethyl group that alkylate nucleic acids and cell proteins and form DNA-DNA or DNA-protein crosslinks, which lead to the global effects of impaired nucleic acid functioning. As compared to carmustine, lomustine is less likely to form isocyanates leading to diminished carbamoylation effects; although lomustine metabolites exhibit increased alkylating activity. Cells resistant to other alkylating agents are probably resistant to lomustine.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Oral Route
    Description
    The drug is rapidly absorbed across the GI tract following oral administration, or across the skin following topical administration. The exact bioavailability is unknown, but nearly complete oral absorption occurs in 30 minutes. Orally-administered lomustine is rapidly and totally converted to metabolites by first-pass metabolism in the liver. Essentially all of an oral dose is metabolized within 1 hour; some of these metabolites may be active.
  Text
  Lomustine, CCNU is administered orally. The drug distributes throughout the body tissues. Because of the high lipid solubility and the relative lack of ionization at physiologic pH, lomustine crosses the blood-brain barrier quite effectively; however, only lomustine's metabolites can be detected in the CSF. CSF concentrations are >= 50% of plasma concentrations. The half-life of lomustine metabolites ranges from 16 hours to 2 days. Following the administration of radio-labeled lomustine at oral doses of 30—100 mg/m2, about half of the radioactivity was excreted in the urine in the form of metabolites within 24 hours. Most of the metabolites are eliminated slowly in the urine.
