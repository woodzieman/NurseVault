  General Information
  Initiation of therapy for HCV infection:
HCV screening (i.e., HCV-antibody for initial screening, HCV-RNA for confirmation of active infection) recommended for:
All persons 18 years and older (a 1-time, routine, opt-out test)
All persons younger than 18 years with any of the below activities, exposures, or conditions and circumstances associated with an increased risk of HCV infection (a 1-time test)
Each pregnancy (testing as part of routine prenatal care)
All persons with any of the below activities, exposures, or conditions and circumstances associated with an increased risk of HCV infection (periodic repeat testing)
All persons who inject drugs (annual testing)
Men with HIV who have unprotected sex with men, and men who have sex with men taking HIV pre-exposure prophylaxis (annual testing)
HCV risk-associated activities, exposures, or conditions and circumstances include:
Activities: injection drug use (current or ever, including those who injected only once); intranasal illicit drug use; use of glass crack pipes; engagement in chem sex (i.e., combining sex with nonprescription drugs to facilitate or enhance sexual encounter); men who have sex with men.
Exposures: persons on long-term hemodialysis (ever); persons with percutaneous or parenteral exposures in an unregulated setting; healthcare, emergency medical, and public safety workers after needlestick, sharps, or mucosal exposure to HCV-infected blood; children born to mothers with HCV; persons who were ever incarcerated; certain recipients of a prior transfusion or organ transplant (i.e., received blood from a donor who later tested positive for HCV; received a blood transfusion or blood component, or underwent an organ transplant before July 1992; received clotting factor concentrates produced before 1987).
Conditions and Circumstances: HIV infection; sexually active persons about to start pre-exposure prophylaxis for HIV; chronic hepatic disease or chronic hepatitis, including unexplained elevated ALT concentrations; solid organ donors and recipients.
Treatment is recommended for all patients with acute or chronic HCV infection, except those with short life expectancy.
Laboratory tests recommended prior to initiating antiviral therapy: quantitative HCV-RNA test to document baseline viral load; HCV genotype and subtype test (if starting a non-pan-genotypic direct-acting antiviral [DAA]); HBsAg test for active HBV coinfection, and anti-HBs and anti-HBc test for prior HBV coinfection (if starting DAA); test for HIV coinfection (if starting DAA).
 
Place in therapy for HCV infection:
Interferon-based therapies are no longer recommended as part of preferred or alternative treatment regimens.

Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  General Information
  Peginterferon alfa-2a is contraindicated in persons with known hypersensitivity to alfa interferon or any component of the product, including E. coli protein hypersensitivity. Serious acute hypersensitivity reactions (e.g., anaphylaxis, angioedema, acute bronchospasm, and urticaria) have been rarely observed during alfa interferon therapy. If such a reaction develops during treatment with peginterferon alfa-2a, discontinue treatment and begin appropriate medical therapy immediately. Peginterferon alfa-2a solution contains benzyl alcohol as a preservative and is contraindicated in any patient with benzyl alcohol hypersensitivity. The vials and prefilled syringes contains 5 mg of benzyl alcohol per 0.5 ml. Due to the benzyl alcohol content, peginterferon alfa-2a is contraindicated for use in neonates and infants. Permanent neuropsychiatric deficits, multiple system organ failure, and rarely death have been associated with benzyl alcohol exposure in infants and neonates. The exposure threshold for toxicity is unknown.
 
Patients should be warned that depression, homicidal ideation, or suicidal ideation could be adverse effects of peginterferon alfa-2a therapy and to report any of these symptoms to their health care provider. Any patient with a history of substance abuse (e.g., alcoholism), depression or severe psychiatric disorder (e.g., bipolar disorder, mania, psychosis) should receive peginterferon alfa-2a with extreme caution. Former drug addicts may fall back into drug addiction or overdose. All patients should be closely monitored for depressive symptoms and other psychiatric symptoms. Development or exacerbation of neuropsychiatric disorders has been observed in patients receiving alpha interferons. Patients should be monitored closely with baseline and periodic neuropsychiatric monitoring and clinical and laboratory evaluations. Peginterferon alfa-2a should be discontinued in patients with persistently severe or worsening signs or symptoms of these conditions. In many, but not all cases, these disorders resolve after stopping peginterferon alfa-2a. Cessation of therapy should be considered in patients experiencing depression and is recommended for patients with severe depression (see Dosage). Although, dose reduction or cessation of therapy may lead to resolution of the symptoms, depression may persist and suicides have occurred after withdrawing therapy. Institution of psychiatric intervention is recommended. Patients should be warned against driving or operating machinery until they know how peginterferon alfa-2a therapy will affect them. EEG abnormalities can develop during alfa interferon therapy. Thus, peginterferon alfa-2a should be used with caution in patients with a seizure disorder.
 
Peginterferon alfa-2a should be used cautiously in patients with diabetes mellitus, including patients with a history of diabetic ketoacidosis. Patients with diabetes mellitus may be at increased risk for retinal adverse events. Baseline and periodic ocular exams are recommended (see Adverse Reactions). Also, patients with diabetes mellitus may require adjustment of their antidiabetic medications (see Adverse Reactions). If blood glucose concentrations cannot be controlled with medications, peginterferon alfa-2a should not be started or continued in patients already on the drug.
 
Elderly patients may have difficulty tolerating the adverse reactions to peginterferon alfa-2a therapy. Peginterferon alfa-2a is excreted by the kidney. As older patients often have decreased renal function, lower doses and closer monitoring during treatment may be required. Obtundation and coma have been observed in some patients, usually in the elderly, treated with high doses.
 
Patients who develop signs and symptoms of colitis (e.g., abdominal pain, bloody diarrhea, fever) or pancreatitis during peginterferon alfa-2a therapy should discontinue therapy. Ulcerative and hemorrhagic/ischemic colitis, sometimes fatal, have been observed within 12 weeks of starting alpha interferon treatment. Colitis usually resolves within 1—3 weeks of alpha interferon discontinuation. Use cautiously in patients with a history of hypertriglyceridemia, colitis, pancreatitis or ulcerative colitis, as these conditions may be exacerbated by interferon therapy. Elevated triglyceride concentrations have occurred in patients that received peginterferon alfa-2a (see Adverse Reactions). Periodic serum triglyceride concentration determination is recommended during drug receipt.
 
Development or exacerbation of infectious disorders has been observed in patients receiving alpha interferons. Serious and severe bacterial infections, some fatal and some not associated with neutropenia, have been observed in patients treated with alpha interferons, including peginterferon alfa-2a. Caution should be exercised when administering peginterferon alfa-2a to patients with bone marrow suppression or in combination with other myelosuppressive therapy including radiation therapy (see Drug Interactions). Patients should be monitored closely with baseline and periodic monitoring and clinical and laboratory evaluations. Peginterferon alfa-2a should be discontinued in patients with persistently severe or worsening signs or symptoms of infectious processes. Cessation of peginterferon alfa-2a and initiation of an appropriate antibiotic regimen are needed for patients who develop sepsis or another severe infection. In many, but not all cases, these disorders resolve after stopping peginterferon alfa-2a.
 
Development or exacerbation of infectious disorders has been observed in patients receiving alpha interferons. Patients should be monitored closely with periodic clinical and laboratory evaluations. Peginterferon alfa-2a should be discontinued in patients with persistently severe or worsening signs or symptoms of these conditions. In many, but not all cases, these disorders resolve after stopping peginterferon alfa-2a. Peginterferon alfa-2a should be used with caution in patients with baseline leukopenia (neutrophil counts < 1500/mm3), thrombocytopenia (platelets < 90,000/mm3), or anemia (hemoglobin < 10 g/dl). Anemia from peginterferon alfa-2a may be additive to anemia associated with GI bleeding, spherocytosis, or other conditions. Peginterferon alfa-2a is associated with decreases in white cell, neutrophil, and platelet counts during the first 2 weeks of therapy. Complete blood counts (CBC) should be obtained pretreatment and monitored routinely during therapy. Patients who develop neutropenia (neutrophil count < 750/mm3) or thrombocytopenia (platelet count < 50,000/mm3) require dosage adjustment of peginterferon alfa-2a therapy (see Dosage); patients that develop severe decreases in neutrophil (< 0.5 x109/l) or platelet counts (< 25 x109/l) should discontinue peginterferon alfa-2a treatment, at least temporarily. Patients with severe immunosuppression such as organ transplant recipients on immunosuppressives should not be treated with peginterferon alfa-2a (see Dosage). Current treatment guidelines recommend that peginterferon alfa-2a plus ribavirin, the treatment of choice for patients coinfected with HCV and HIV, be used cautiously if the CD4 cell count is < 350 cells/microliter.
 
Peginterferon alfa-2a is contraindicated for use in patients with autoimmune hepatitis and in patients with hepatic disease defined as a Child-Pugh score greater than 6 (class B and C) in cirrhotic patients before or during treatment with peginterferon alfa-2a. Peginterferon alfa-2a is also contraindicated for use in patients with hepatic decompensation with Child-Pugh score greater than or equal to 6 before or during treatment in cirrhotic chronic hepatitis C patients coinfected with HIV. Patients with non-compensated cirrhosis may experience worsening hepatic disease including jaundice, ascites, coagulopathy, hypoalbuminemia, hepatic encephalopathy, hepatic failure, and death after peginterferon alfa-2a treatment. Serum ALT concentrations should be evaluated at baseline, 2 weeks after treatment initiation, and monthly thereafter in order to determine clinical response. Any patient developing liver function abnormalities during peginterferon alfa-2a therapy should be monitored closely because of the possibility of severe hepatotoxicity. If ALT increases are progressive despite dose reduction or are accompanied by hyperbilirubinemia or evidence of hepatic decompensation (Child-Pugh class B or C) or hepatic failure, peginterferon alfa-2a therapy should be immediately discontinued. Patients with hepatitis C who experience progressive ALT increases above baseline concentrations require dosage adjustment (see Dosage). In patients with with chronic hepatitis B and ALT values greater than 5 times the upper limit of normal, dose reduction, temporary drug discontinuation, or permanent drug discontinuation may be appropriate (see Dosage).
 
Peginterferon alfa-2a should be used with caution in patients with a history of autoimmune disease. Peginterferon alfa-2a is contraindicated for use in patients with autoimmune hepatitis. Development or exacerbation of autoimmune diseases (e.g., myositis, hepatitis, thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura (TTP), idiopathic thrombocytopenic purpura (ITP), thyroiditis, thrombocytopenia, rheumatoid arthritis, intersitial nephritis, systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE), or psoriasis) has been observed in patients receiving alpha interferons. Patients should be monitored closely with periodic clinical and laboratory evaluations. Peginterferon alfa-2a should be discontinued in patients with persistently severe or worsening signs or symptoms of these conditions. In many, but not all cases, these disorders resolve after stopping peginterferon alfa-2a.
 
Closely monitor patients with renal disease including renal impairment or renal failure for signs and symptoms of interferon toxicity. Peginterferon alfa-2a is recommended to be used with caution in patients with a creatinine clearance less than 50 ml/minute. Data from patients with reduced renal function are limited. Dose reduction may be needed. In patients with end-stage renal disease requiring hemodialysis (dialysis), dose reduction is recommended (see Dosage). Peginterferon alfa-2a was well tolerated among 25 adults with chronic hepatitis C on hemodialysis; patients were naive to interferon-based therapy. Peginterferon alfa-2a was started at the recommended dose of 135 mcg SC once weekly. During the 24-week treatment period, 3 patients had a dose reduction to 90 mcg once weekly for a platelet count < 50,000/mm3, but no patient had to stop treatment because of an adverse event.
 
Peginterferon alfa-2a is for subcutaneous administration only; do not administer via intravenous administration or intramuscular administration. Intramuscular injections of other medications should be avoided in patients with platelet counts < 50,000/mm3.
 
Peginterferon alfa-2a should be used with caution in patients with dental disease. Myelosuppressive effects of peginterferon alfa-2a increase the risk of infection and bleeding. Dental work should be performed prior to initiating therapy, or deferred if blood counts are abnormal. Patients should be instructed in proper oral hygiene, including caution in use of regular toothbrushes, dental floss, and toothpicks.
 
Patients with thyroid disease (e.g., hyperthyroidism or hypothyroidism) whose thyroid stimulating hormone (TSH) serum concentration cannot be maintained in the normal range by medication should not be treated with peginterferon alfa-2a. If the TSH concentration can be maintained within the normal range, TSH concentration assessment is recommended before treatment and every 3 months following initiation of peginterferon alfa-2a therapy. If the TSH concentration cannot be controlled with medications, peginterferon alfa-2a should be discontinued. Also, the development or exacerbation of autoimmune thyroid diseases (e.g., Graves' disease, thyroiditis) has been observed in patients receiving peginterferon alfa-2a. Patients should be monitored closely with periodic clinical and laboratory evaluations. Peginterferon alfa-2a should be discontinued in patients with persistently severe or worsening signs or symptoms of autoimmune thyroid disease. In many, but not all cases, autoimmune disorders resolve after stopping peginterferon alfa-2a.
 
Safe use of peginterferon alfa-2a during pregnancy has not been established. Peginterferon alfa-2a is classified as FDA pregnancy category C; there are no adequate and well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Peginterferon alfa-2a should only be used during pregnancy if the potential benefit to the mother justifies the potential risk to the fetus. Dose-related menstrual abnormalities and an increased incidence of spontaneous abortions have occurred in primate studies. Peginterferon alfa-2a should be assumed to have abortifacient potential. Decreased serum estradiol and progesterone concentrations have been reported in women treated with human leukocyte interferon; the impact on female fertility has not been fully evaluated. Females of childbearing age should not receive peginterferon alfa-2a unless they are using effective contraception during treatment. Use with ribavirin: Peginterferon alfa-2a may be used with ribavirin, and ribavirin may cause birth defects or fetal death; ribavirin is genotoxic and mutagenic and should be considered a potential carcinogen. Pregnancy must be avoided in female patients and in female partners of male patients. A negative pregnancy test obtained immediately before ribavirin initiation is needed; do not start therapy until the pregnancy test result is obtained. Both men and women of childbearing potential must use 2 forms of effective contraception during treatment and for 6 months after treatment discontinuation, and monthly pregnancy testing during this time is needed.
 
It is not known if peginterferon alfa-2a is excreted into breast milk. Due to the potential of adverse reactions in the infant, a decision as to whether to continue breast-feeding the infant or to discontinue the drug should be made, taking into consideration the importance of the drug to the mother.
 
The safe and effective use of peginterferon alfa-2a in children less than 18 years of age with chronic hepatitis C have not been established.
 
Peginterferon alfa-2a should be used cautiously in patients with pre-existing debilitating conditions, such as a history of cardiac disease (e.g., angina or congestive heart failure), or pulmonary disease, including chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD). Development or exacerbation of ischemic disorders has been observed in patients receiving alpha interferons. Hypertension, supraventricular arrhythmias, chest pain, and myocardial infarction have been observed in patients who received peginterferon alfa-2a. Common adverse effects of peginterferon alfa-2a such as fever and chills may exacerbate preexisting cardiac conditions. Supraventricular tachycardia appears to be correlated with preexisting conditions or previous treatment with cardiotoxic agents. Patients such as those with a history of myocardial infarction and/or previous or current cardiac arrhythmias should be monitored closely with baseline and periodic monitoring and clinical and laboratory evaluations. Patients with preexisting cardiac abnormalities should have electrocardiograms prior to beginning therapy with peginterferon alfa-2a. Patients with hypertension may be at increased risk for retinal adverse events. Baseline and periodic ocular exams are recommended (see Adverse Reactions). Baseline chest X-rays are suggested and should be repeated, if clinically needed. Peginterferon alfa-2a should be discontinued in patients with persistently severe or worsening signs or symptoms of these conditions. In many, but not all cases, these disorders resolve after stopping peginterferon alfa-2a. Patients who develop persistent or unexplained pulmonary infiltrates or pulmonary function impairment should discontinue treatment with peginterferon alfa-2a. Use with ribavirin: Peginterferon alfa-2a may be used with ribavirin, and ribavirin causes hemolytic anemia, which may worsen cardiac disease. Ribavirin is not recommended for patients with a history of significant or unstable cardiac disease. Also, the use of peginterferon alfa-2a and ribavirin is contraindicated for use by patients with hemoglobinopathies such as thalassemia major or sickle-cell anemia.
 
All patients should receive an ophthalmic exam prior to beginning therapy with peginterferon alfa-2a. Patients with ocular disease, including hypertensive or diabetic retinopathy, should receive periodic ophthalmic exams during the use of peginterferon alfa-2a. Decreased vision or loss of vision has been associated with alpha interferon therapy (see Adverse Reactions). Any patient who experiences changes in vision or a visual disturbance during alpha interferon therapy should have a complete eye exam promptly. Peginterferon alfa-2a therapy should be discontinued in any patient who develops new or worsening ophthalmologic disorders.

    General Information
    Alpha interferons, including peginterferon alfa-2a, may cause or exacerbate serious adverse effects, including potentially fatal neuropsychiatric, autoimmune, ischemic, and infectious disorders. Monitor patients closely with periodic clinical and laboratory assessments. Discontinue peginterferon alfa-2a in patients with persistent or worsening signs or symptoms of these conditions. In many, but not all cases, these disorders resolve after stopping peginterferon alfa-2a.
    Headline
    autoimmune hepatitis, decompensated hepatic cirrhosis
    General Information
    Peginterferon alfa-2a is contraindicated for use in patients with autoimmune hepatitis or decompensated hepatic cirrhosis prior to treatment. Patients with chronic hepatitis C (CHC) and cirrhosis, particularly those with HIV coinfection receiving highly active antiretroviral therapy (HAART), are at increased risk for hepatic decompensation and death when treated with alpha interferons, including peginterferon alfa-2a.
    Headline
    activities requiring coordination and concentration, driving or operating machinery, major psychiatric disorder
    General Information
    Use peginterferon alfa-2a with extreme caution in patients with a major psychiatric disorder, particularly depression. Life-threatening or fatal neuropsychiatric reactions may occur during therapy, including suicide, suicidal ideation, homicidal ideation, and depression. These reactions have been reported in patients with and without pre-existing psychiatric illness. Other neuropsychiatric adverse events observed with alpha interferons include aggressive behavior, psychoses, hallucinations, bipolar disorder, and mania. Assess patients for a history of substance abuse disorder prior to initiation, as relapse and drug overdoses have been reported with interferon therapy. Monitor all patients closely for depression and other psychiatric symptoms, and advise them to promptly report depressive symptoms or suicidal thoughts. In severe cases, discontinue peginterferon alfa-2a immediately and institute psychiatric care. Advise patients to avoid driving or operating machinery or engaging in activities requiring coordination and concentration if they experience dizziness, confusion, or drowsiness during therapy.
    Headline
    anemia, neutropenia, thrombocytopenia
    General Information
    Use peginterferon alfa-2a with caution in patients with neutropenia (neutrophils less than 1,500 cells/mm3), thrombocytopenia (platelets less than 90,000 cells/mm3), or anemia (hemoglobin less than 10 g/dL). Peginterferon alfa-2a suppresses bone marrow function and may cause severe cytopenias. Ribavirin may potentiate neutropenia and lymphopenia induced by alpha interferons. Obtain complete blood counts before initiating therapy and monitor routinely during treatment. If neutropenia or thrombocytopenia develop, adjust the dose as needed; discontinue peginterferon alfa-2a temporarily in patients who develop severe neutropenia or thrombocytopenia. Severe neutropenia and thrombocytopenia occur more frequently in HIV-coinfected patients than in monoinfected patients and may result in serious infections or bleeding.
    Headline
    autoimmune disease
    General Information
    Use peginterferon alfa-2a with caution in individuals with a history of autoimmune disease. Development or exacerbation of autoimmune disorders (e.g., myositis, hepatitis, thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura (TTP), immune thrombocytopenic purpura (ITP), thyroiditis, thrombocytopenia, rheumatoid arthritis, interstitial nephritis, systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE), or psoriasis) has been observed in patients receiving alpha interferons. Monitor patients closely with periodic clinical and laboratory evaluations. Discontinue peginterferon alfa-2a in patients with persistently severe or worsening signs or symptoms of these conditions. In many, but not all cases, these disorders resolve after stopping peginterferon alfa-2a.
    Headline
    hyperthyroidism, hypothyroidism
    General Information
    Do not treat individuals with peginterferon alfa-2a who have hyperthyroidism or hypothyroidism at baseline that cannot be effectively managed with medication. Exacerbation of thyroid disorders has been reported during therapy. Discontinue peginterferon alfa-2a in patients who develop thyroid dysfunction that cannot be adequately controlled with medication.
    Headline
    diabetes mellitus, hypoglycemia
    General Information
    Do not treat individuals with peginterferon alfa-2a who have hyperglycemia, hypoglycemia, or diabetes mellitus at baseline that cannot be effectively managed with medication. Exacerbation of blood glucose disorders has been reported during therapy. Discontinue peginterferon alfa-2a in patients who develop blood glucose abnormalities that cannot be adequately controlled with medication.
    Headline
    cardiac disease
    General Information
    Use peginterferon alfa-2a with caution in individuals with cardiac disease. Hypertension, supraventricular arrhythmias, chest pain, and myocardial infarction have been observed in patients treated with peginterferon alfa-2a. Obtain an electrocardiogram (ECG) prior to treatment in those with pre-existing cardiac conditions. Because cardiac disease may be worsened by ribavirin-induced hemolytic anemia, patients with a history of significant or unstable cardiac disease should not receive combination therapy with ribavirin.
    Headline
    pulmonary disease
    General Information
    Use peginterferon alfa-2a with caution in individuals with pulmonary disease. Pulmonary disorders such as dyspnea, pulmonary infiltrates, pneumonia, bronchiolitis obliterans, interstitial pneumonitis, pulmonary hypertension, and sarcoidosis may be induced or aggravated by peginterferon alfa-2a and other alpha interferons, with some cases resulting in respiratory failure and death. Recurrence of respiratory insufficiency and failure has been observed with interferon rechallenge. Suspend treatment in patients who develop pulmonary infiltrates or impaired pulmonary function, and monitor closely if therapy is resumed.
    Headline
    renal failure, renal impairment
    General Information
    Assess renal function in all patients prior to initiation of peginterferon alfa-2a therapy. Dosage adjustment is necessary in individuals with severe renal impairment or renal failure. Closely monitor all patients with any degree of renal impairment for adverse reactions and laboratory abnormalities, especially decreased hemoglobin. If severe adverse reactions or laboratory abnormalities develop, or if adverse reactions persist after dose adjustment, discontinue peginterferon alfa-2a.
    Headline
    ocular disease
    General Information
    Obtain an eye exam at baseline in all individuals receiving peginterferon alfa-2a. Perform periodic ophthalmologic exams during treatment in individuals with pre-existing ocular disease or conditions such as diabetic retinopathy or hypertensive retinopathy. Treatment with peginterferon alfa-2a or other alpha interferons may induce or worsen vision loss, retinopathy including macular edema, retinal artery or vein thrombosis, retinal bleeding, cotton wool spots, optic neuritis, papilledema, and serous retinal detachment. Discontinue treatment if new or worsening ophthalmologic disorders occur.
    Headline
    infants, neonates, premature neonates
    General Information
    Peginterferon alfa-2a is contraindicated in premature neonates, neonates, and infants because it contains benzyl alcohol. Benzyl alcohol is associated with an increased risk of neurologic and other complications, which can sometimes be fatal in this population.
    Headline
    geriatric
    General Information
    Use peginterferon alfa-2a with caution in geriatric patients due to a higher likelihood of decreased renal function and increased risk of adverse reactions. Adverse reactions related to alpha interferons, such as CNS, cardiac, and systemic (e.g., flu-like) effects, may be more severe in elderly patients.
    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    There are no adequate and well-controlled studies evaluating the use of peginterferon alfa-2a in human pregnancy. Based on animal reproduction studies, peginterferon alfa-2a can cause fetal harm and should be assumed to have abortifacient potential. Non-pegylated interferon alfa-2a caused abortion when given to pregnant rhesus monkeys. Too few remaining pregnancies were available to assess teratogenic potential but no developmental abnormalities were observed in the surviving fetuses.
    Headline
    people who may become pregnant, reproductive risk
    General Information
    Counsel people who may become pregnant about the reproductive risk associated with peginterferon alfa-2a. Peginterferon alfa-2a can cause fetal harm. Verify pregnancy status prior to treatment initiation. Advise people who may become pregnant to use effective contraception while taking peginterferon alfa-2a. Female fertility studies have not been performed; however, based on its mechanism of action, peginterferon alfa-2a may disrupt the menstrual cycle.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Advise patients to avoid breast-feeding while taking peginterferon alfa-2a, due to the potential for serious adverse effects in the breastfed child. There are no data on the presence of peginterferon alfa-2a in human milk, its effects on the breastfed child, or its effects on milk production. One case report involving 2 pregnant patients receiving interferon alfa treatment has been published. An analysis immediately postpartum found interferon concentrations in the human milk (1.4 units/mL and 6 units/mL) to be considerably lower than the maternal serum concentrations (20.8 units/mL and 58 units/mL, respectively). Consider the benefits of breast-feeding, the risk of potential infant drug exposure, and the risk of an untreated or inadequately treated condition.

  General Information
  Life-threatening or fatal neuropsychiatric events including suicide, homicidal and suicidal ideation, depression, relapse of drug addiction/overdose, and aggressive behavior occurred in less than 1% of patients with and without previous psychiatric disorders during peginterferon alfa-2a therapy. Depression was reported in 18% of 559 patients that received peginterferon alfa-2a (180 mcg/week SC) during clinical trials. Depression development during peginterferon alfa-2a receipt warrants careful monitoring of the disorder and possible peginterferon alfa-2a dose reduction or discontinuation (see Dosage). Discontinue peginterferon alfa-2a if the condition is persistently severe or if associated signs and symptoms worsen. In many, but not all cases, the disorder resolves after peginterferon alfa-2a withdrawal. Other neuropsychiatric adverse effects reported during clinical trials included insomnia (19%), dizziness (16%), irritability/ anxiety/nervousness (19%), impaired cognition or concentration/confusion (8%), memory impairment (5%), emotional lability (3%), psychosis ( 4 months). Hair loss is temporary and hair growth should return with peginterferon alfa-2a cessation.Severe acute hypersensitivity reactions including urticaria, angioedema, bronchoconstriction (bronchospasm), and anaphylaxis such as anaphylactic shock and anaphylactoid reactions have been rarely observed during alpha interferon and ribavirin therapy. If such reaction occurs, discontinue peginterferon alfa-2a and ribavirin and immediately institute appropriate medical therapy. Serious skin reactions including vesiculobullous eruptions, reactions in the spectrum of Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (erythema muItiforme major) with varying degrees of skin and mucosal involvement and exfoliative dermatitis (erythroderma) have been rarely reported in patients receiving peginterferon alfa-2a with and without ribavirin. Discontinue peginterferon alfa-2a if signs or symptoms of a severe skin reaction develop.Gastrointestinal effects reported by 559 patients that received peginterferon alfa-2a 180 mcg once weekly include nausea/vomiting (24%), diarrhea (16%), xerostomia (dry mouth, 6%), anorexia (17%), weight loss (4%), and abdominal pain (15%). Elevated hepatic enzymes (i.e., ALT) 2- to 5-fold above baseline were observed in some patients receiving peginterferon alfa-2a, including patients with virologic response. Less than 1% of patients had a 5- to 10-fold increase in their ALT serum concentration from the baseline value. The elevations were generally transient and were not associated with deterioration of other liver function tests. However, when the increase in ALT concentrations is progressive despite dose reduction or is accompanied by hyperbilirubinemia, peginterferon alfa-2a therapy should be discontinued. Patients with poorly compensated liver disease may not tolerate adverse effects and could develop ascites, hepatic failure, or death. Severe or fatal GI bleeding, pancreatitis, hemorrhagic/ischemic colitis, fatty liver, cholangitis, and peptic ulcer have been reported in less than 1% of patients treated with peginterferon alfa-2a. Most patients that received peginterferon alfa-2a either alone or with ribavirin developed elevated triglyceride serum concentrations. Random concentrations greater than 400 mg/dl occurred in about 20% of patients. As hypertriglyceridemia can cause pancreatitis, periodic serum triglyceride concentration determination is recommended during drug receipt. Colitis has been observed within 12 weeks of starting alfa interferon therapy. Patients who develop signs and symptoms of colitis (e.g., abdominal pain, bloody diarrhea, fever) or pancreatitis during peginterferon alfa-2a receipt should discontinue the drug. Colitis usually resolves within 1—3 weeks of discontinuation of alpha interferon therapy.Cardiac and pulmonary adverse reactions have been reported during peginterferon alfa-2a therapy. Cardiac arrhythmias (i.e., supraventricular tachycardia (SVT)) or an arrhythmia exacerbation and angina or chest pain (unspecified) have been reported rarely (< 1% of patients). Of 559 patients who received peginterferon alfa-2a 180 mcg per week, 4% each developed dyspnea and/or a cough. Exertional dyspnea and pulmonary embolism each occurred in < 1% of patients.Neutropenia occurs in most patients (95%) who receive peginterferon alfa-2a and may necessitate dosage reduction (see Dosage). Leukopenia and neutropenia generally improve with dosage adjustment. In clinical trials, the median neutrophil count returned to pre-treatment concentrations within 4—8 weeks after stopping therapy. Approximately 5% of patients had decreases in absolute neutrophil counts to below 0.5 x109/L at some time during therapy. Seventeen percent of patients needed dosage adjustment because of neutropenia development. Although permanent dosage reduction was required for 2% of patients, less than 1% of patients had to permanently discontinue the drug.Receipt of peginterferon alfa-2a has been associated with anemia. Although treatment with peginterferon alfa-2a was associated with small gradual decreases in hemoglobin and hematocrit, < 1% of all patients, including those with cirrhosis, required dosage adjustment for anemia. The median drop in the hemoglobin concentration was 2.2 g/dl and 2% developed a hemoglobin concentration below 10 g/dl. Very rarely alpha interferons may be associated with aplastic anemia (< 1% of patients).Although rare, endocrine effects of peginterferon alfa-2a include hyperglycemia, hyperthyroidism and hypothyroidism (3% of 559 patients that received peginterferon alfa-2a 180 mcg per week). Hyperglycemia has been noted in patients with and without diabetes mellitus before peginterferon alfa-2a treatment. Less than 1% of patients developed diabetes mellitus during clinical trials. Patients with diabetes mellitus may require antidiabetic medication dosage adjustment.. The rates of clinically relevant hypothyroidism or hyperthyroidism (requiring treatment, dose modification or discontinuation) were 4% and 1%, respectively. Among patients who developed new onset thyroid abnormalities during peginterferon alfa-2a therapy, approximately half still had abnormalities during the follow-up period. Cross-reactivity with membrane receptors for thyroid stimulating hormone (TSH) and interferon may result in some of these effects. The TSH serum concentration needs to be within the normal range during peginterferon alfa-2a receipt (see Contraindications). If the TSH concentration cannot be maintained within the normal range, peginterferon alfa-2a should be discontinued. A decrease or loss of vision, retinopathy, including macular edema, retinal hemorrhage, cotton-wool spots, and retinal thrombosis, optic neuritis, and papilledema are induced or aggravated by treatment with peginterferon alfa-2a or other alpha interferons. Of 559 patients that received peginterferon 180 mcg weekly, 4% developed blurred vision and < 1% developed a corneal ulcer. The mechanism for ocular adverse effects is not known. The reactions seem to occur after several months of treatment but have also been reported after shorter periods. All patients should receive a baseline eye examination. Patients with preexisting ophthalmologic disorders, such as hypertensive or diabetic retinopathy should receive periodic ophthalmologic exams during therapy. Also, any patient complaining of visual impairment, including changes in visual acuity or visual field, or other ophthalmologic symptoms during peginterferon alfa-2a treatment should have a prompt and complete eye exam. Discontinue peginterferon alfa-2a in patients that develop new or worsening ophthalmologic disorders.Thrombocytopenia is a common adverse effect associated with peginterferon alfa-2a and may necessitate dosage reduction (see Dosage). Of 559 patients that received 180 mcg weekly, 5% had a severe platelet count reduction (< 50,000/mm3) and 52% had a reduction in their platelet count; the median drop from baseline was 45%. Cessation of peginterferon alfa-2a caused a return to median pretreatment platelet counts within 4 weeks. Cerebral hemorrhage occurred in < 1% of patients that received peginterferon alfa-2a during clinical trials.Lymphopenia occurs in most patients (86%) that receive peginterferon alfa-2a. A lymphocyte count below 0.5 x109/l occurred in approximately 5% of patients. Cessation of peginterferon alfa-2a caused a return to median pretreatment lymphocyte counts within 4—12 weeks.Rare cases (< 1% of patients) of autoimmune disease including sarcoidosis, Raynaud's phenomenon, vasculitis, rheumatoid arthritis, lupus-like symptoms or systemic lupus erythematosus, and rhabdomyolysis or myositis have been observed in patients treated with alpha interferons. In very rare cases, the autoimmune event resulted in fatality. Activation and worsening of psoriasis may occur within 2—4 weeks of initiation of alpha interferon therapy. Autoimmune disorder development during peginterferon alfa-2a receipt warrants careful monitoring of the autoimmune disorder and possible peginterferon alfa-2a discontinuation. Discontinue peginterferon alfa-2a if the condition is persistently severe or if associated signs and symptoms worsen. In many, but not all cases, the disorder resolves after peginterferon alfa-2a withdrawal.Low-titer neutralizing antibody formation developed in 3% of 835 patients that received peginterferon alfa-2a with or without ribavirin. Nine percent of patients developed binding antibodies to peginterferon alfa-2a, as assessed by an ELISA assay. The clinical and pathologic significance of the appearance of serum neutralizing antibodies is unknown. No apparent correlation of antibody development to clinical response or adverse events was observed.Hemorrhagic and ischemic cerebrovascular events such as stroke have been observed in patients treated with interferon alfa-based therapies including peginterferon alfa-2a. The cerebrovascular events occurred in patients with few or no reported risk factors for stroke; some events occurred in patients less than 45 years of age. A causal relationship between peginterferon alfa-2a and stroke has not been established. Healthcare providers are encouraged to report to the manufacturer any adverse events thought to be related to peginterferon alfa-2a.

Description
  Text
  Peginterferon alfa-2a is approved for the treatment of chronic hepatitis C virus (HCV) infections in patients 5 years and older, and for the treatment of chronic hepatitis B virus (HBV) infections in patients 3 years and older. The drug is a highly purified protein containing 165 amino acids (recombinant interferon alfa-2a) that is covalently conjugated with bis-monomethoxy polyethylene glycol (PEG). Interferon alfa-2a represents only one specific subtype of alpha interferon (i.e., interferon alpha-2) and is produced by recombinant DNA technology that uses a genetically engineered Escherichia coli bacterium containing DNA that codes for the protein. By increasing the size of the molecule with PEG (average molecular weight of 60,000 daltons), the absorption and half-life are prolonged, and the clearance is decreased. The safety of peginterferon alfa-2a is comparable to interferon alfa-2a; however, the tolerability is increased.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Mechanism of Action: The active component of peginterferon alfa-2a is the interferon alfa-2a moiety. Interferon alfa-2a acts similarly to native interferon alpha. Endogenous alpha-interferons (IFNs) are secreted by leukocytes (e.g., macrophages, B lymphocytes, and non-B non-T lymphocytes) in response to viral infection or various synthetic and biological inducers. All alpha-IFNs share common biologic activities generated by the binding of interferon to the cell-surface receptor. Although the exact mechanism of action is not fully understood, interferon binding to the cell surface receptor is followed by activation of tyrosine kinases, which leads to the production of several IFN-stimulated enzymes such as 2'-5'-oligoadenylate synthetase (2'-5'-OAS) and beta2-microglobulin. These and possibly other IFN-stimulated enzymes are thought to be responsible for the pleiotropic biologic effects of alpha-IFNs, which include antiviral, antiproliferative and immunomodulatory effects, cellular differentiation, regulation of cell surface major histocompatibility antigen expression (HLA class I), and cytokine induction.•Antiviral effects: Interferon exerts antiviral effects by augmenting the production and/or release of specific enzymes. Interferon-induced intracellular enzymes such as 2'5'-OAS and protein kinase contribute to inhibition of viral replication by activating endoribonucleases that cleave single-stranded viral RNA. Thus, translation of viral proteins is inhibited. The activity of IFN-induced enzymes depends on the presence of double-stranded RNA (dsRNA) formed during viral replication. It has been suggested that the antiviral activity of IFNs may be related, in part, to an effect on dsRNA. Interferon-induced enzymes may also inhibit viral penetration and uncoating, and/or viral assembly and release. Expression of major histocompatibility antigens by IFNs may also contribute to antiviral activity by enhancing the lytic effects of cytotoxic T lymphocytes.A wide range of viruses, particularly RNA viruses, are sensitive to the antiviral actions of IFN. Alpha interferons are generally active against the following viruses in vitro: adenovirus; coronavirus; encephalomyocarditis virus; hepatitis B virus; hepatitis C virus (HCV); hepatitis D virus; herpes simplex virus type 1; herpes simplex virus type 2; human immunodeficiency virus (HIV); papillomavirus; poliovirus; rhinovirus; vaccinia virus; varicella-zoster virus; vesicular stomatitis virus; human T-lymphotropic virus type I (HTLV-I). In chronic hepatitis C, INF treatment is associated with normalization of ALT and reduction of serum HCV RNA, as well as improvement in liver histopathology, in responding patients. The HCV genotype 1 is more resistant to interferon alfa treatment than other viral genotypes; 75% of persons in the United States infected with HCV carry this genotype. Many patients with a biochemical and virologic response relapse 1—2 months after stopping IFN therapy.•Effect on hepatic microsomal enzymes: Alpha interferons may inhibit microsomal enzymes involved in the hepatic cytochrome P-450 system. The effect of interferon on the CYP450 system may be related to increased enzyme degradation, suppressed enzyme synthesis, or inhibition of cytochrome P-450. Implications of this effect have not been fully evaluated, but the metabolism of certain drugs may be affected (see Drug Interactions).

    Subcutaneous Route
    Description
    Following a single SC dose, the mean absorption half-life is 4.6 hours. Maximal concentrations occur between 72 and 96 hours post dose and are sustained for 168 hours.     Description
    Similar peginterferon alfa-2a plasma exposures after a 180 mcg once weekly dose were obtained from patients with a CrCl of 30—50 ml/min as compared with values obtained from patients with a CrCl > 80 ml/min.     Description
    The pharmacokinetic parameters of peginterferon alfa-2a (180 mcg/1.73 m2 x BSA given subcutaneously) were evaluated in a study involving 14 children, aged 2—8 years, with chronic hepatitis C. Peginterferon alfa-2a steady-state trough concentrations were similar to those observed in adults receiving 180 mcg fixed dosing; however, the time to reach steady state (Tmax) was longer in the pediatric group (approximately 12 weeks) when compared to the adult population (5—8 weeks). Additionally, the pediatric group experienced a 4-fold lower clearance of peginterferon alfa-2a compared to the clearance reported in adults. Based on this decreased clearance, the mean exposure (AUC) is predicted to be 25—70% higher in children receiving BSA adjusted dosing than in adults receiving fixed dosing. Safety and efficacy of peginterferon alfa-2a have not been established in neonates, infants, or children < 5 years of age.
    Name
    Geriatric
    Description
    The AUC was slightly increased in elderly subjects > 62 years of age taking 180 mcg SC per week of peginterferon alfa-2a, but peak concentrations were similar in these patients as compared with younger adults.
  Text
  Peginterferon alfa-2a is given subcutaneously (SC). In patients with hepatitis C infection, the mean elimination half-life of peginterferon alfa-2a (i.e., Pegasys) is 80 hours (range 50—140 hours) as compared with 5.1 hours (range 3.7—8.5 hours) for interferon alfa-2a (i.e., Roferon-A). When compared to interferon alfa-2a in healthy patients, the mean systemic clearance of peginterferon alfa-2a is approximately 100-fold lower, allowing for a reduced dosing frequency.

Administration
  General Information
  For storage information, see the specific product information within the How Supplied section.
 
NOTE: Variations in dosage and adverse reactions exist among different subtypes of interferon alfa. Therefore, do not use different brands of interferon in a single treatment regimen. Also, the vials and prefilled syringes of peginterferon alfa-2a are not directly interchangeable. The same amount of drug will not be provided by equal volumes.
 
NOTE: Due to possible severe central nervous system reactions and severe hematologic reactions, baseline neuropsychiatric monitoring and complete blood count assessment of all patients are recommended. Pregnancy screening, liver function tests, serum creatinine, and thyroid function tests are also recommended prior to initiating therapy.
 
NOTE: Patients should be well hydrated before drug receipt, if clinically appropriate.

Preparation of subcutaneous dosage immediately prior to injection:
Gently warm the refrigerated medicine to room temperature. Accomplish this by rolling the vial in the palms of your hands for about 1 minute prior to injection. Warm the syringe by placing it on a clean flat surface and waiting until it reaches room temperature. Wait for any condensation on the outside of the syringe to disappear prior to use.
Do not shake the vial or prefilled syringe as foaming may occur.
If using the vial, flip the plastic top off the vial and clean the rubber stopper with an alcohol pad. Obtain a sterile needle and syringe to withdraw and administer the drug solution. If needed, attach the needle to the end of the syringe. Pull the plunger back to the needed dosage mark on the syringe. While keeping the vial upright, insert the needle into the vial and slowly inject all the air into the airspace of the vial; DO NOT inject air into the fluid. Invert the vial and syringe to withdraw the needed amount of drug solution by slowly pulling the plunger back to the needed dosage mark on the syringe. The vials are single-use only; discard any unused portion.
For pediatric patients, prepare dose using ONLY the 180 mcg/mL vial. The weight-based dose should be drawn from the 180 mcg/mL vial using a sterile 1 mL tuberculin syringe.
If using the prefilled syringe, attach the needle by removing the rubber cap from the syringe barrel and placing the needle onto the end of the syringe barrel. Do not remove the clear needle shield until immediately before the injection of the product.
With the needle in the upright position, tap the syringe barrel lightly. Press the plunger slightly to eliminate any air bubbles that may be present. If using the prefilled syringe, you may need to push some medicine out of the syringe to deliver the correct amount. The prefilled syringe is marked for 90 mcg, 135 mcg, and 180 mcg dosage amounts. The edge of the plunger stopper needs to be on the correct dosage line.
 
Subcutaneous (SC) injection technique:
Clean the injection area (upper thigh or abdominal area beside the navel or waistline) with an alcohol swab. Choose a new location for each subsequent injection.
Pinch an area of skin and insert the needle with the bevel up subcutaneously as far as it will go by using a quick dart-like motion.
Release the skin and gently pull back on the syringe before delivering the drug to ensure it is not injected into a blood vessel. If blood appears in the barrel of the syringe, remove the needle and discard the syringe. Do not reuse syringes and needles. If no blood appears, slowly push down on the plunger to deposit the medicine.
After removal of the needle, place an alcohol swab over the injection site and press slightly.
Do not recap the needle. If using a syringe with a needle-stick protection device, place the free end of the orange cap on a flat surface and push down until the cap covers the needle. A click will be heard. Discard the syringe and needle into a sharps container.

    Injectable Administration
    Description
    Administer subcutaneously. Do not inject intradermally, intramuscularly, or intravenously.
Only an individual trained in subcutaneous drug delivery should administer the injection. The initial injection should be given by a trained health care professional. A patient that is properly trained in subcutaneous injection technique may self-inject, if appropriate.
Premedication with acetaminophen or ibuprofen may decrease the incidence of administration-related reactions (i.e., fever). Bedtime administration may increase patient tolerance of therapy.
Visually inspect parenteral products for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration whenever solution and container permit. The solution should be clear and colorless or a light yellow. Do not use if the solution has visible particles, flakes, color, or is cloudy.

Dosage Adjustment Guidelines
  Hepatic Impairment
  Patients with decompensated hepatic disease (e.g., Child-Pugh class B or C) should not be treated with peginterferon alfa-2a. Peginterferon alfa-2a is also contraindicated for use in patients with autoimmune hepatitis. If ALT increases are progressive despite dose reduction or are accompanied by increased bilirubin or evidence of hepatic decompensation, immediately discontinue therapy.
 
Adult patients with chronic hepatitis C: In patients with progressive ALT increases above baseline values, reduce the dose to 135 mcg SC once weekly and perform more frequent liver function monitoring. Peginterferon alfa-2a may be resumed after the ALT flare has subsided.
 
Adult patients with chronic hepatitis B: In patients with ALT values greater than 5 times the upper limit of normal, consider dose reduction to 135 mcg SC once weekly or temporary drug discontinuation and perform more frequent liver function monitoring. Peginterferon alfa-2a may be resumed after ALT flares subside. Therapy discontinuation may be appropriate for patients with persistent ALT elevations more than 10 times the upper limit of normal.
 
Children and Adolescents with chronic hepatitis C:
-For alanine transaminase (ALT) > 5 but 10 x ULN in children and adolescents: Discontinue treatment.
  Renal Impairment
  NOTE: In all patients, estimate the creatinine clearance before peginterferon alfa-2a initiation. Due to a lack of data, dosage recommendations cannot be made for pediatric patients with renal impairment.
CrCl >= 30 ml/min: No dosage adjustment needed.
CrCl < 30 ml/min (Adults): Reduce dose to 135 mcg SC once weekly and closely monitor for signs and symptoms of interferon toxicity. If severe adverse reactions or laboratory abnormalities develop, the dose may be further reduced to 90 mcg SC once weekly until the adverse reactions abate. If intolerance persists after dose adjustment, discontinue peginterferon alfa-2a.
 
Intermittent hemodialysis
Adults: 135 mcg SC once weekly. Monitor patients closely for the potential for reduced efficacy and for signs and symptoms of interferon toxicity. If severe adverse reactions or laboratory abnormalities develop, the dose may be reduced to 90 mcg SC once weekly until the adverse reactions abate. If intolerance persists after dose adjustment, discontinue peginterferon alfa-2a.
