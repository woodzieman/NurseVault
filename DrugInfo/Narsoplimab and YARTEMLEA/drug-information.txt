    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    The available data on the use of narsoplimab during pregnancy are insufficient to evaluate for a drug-associated risk of major birth defects, miscarriage, or other adverse maternal or fetal outcomes. In animal reproduction studies, narsoplimab was administered subcutaneously and intravenously twice weekly to pregnant mice and rabbits during organogenesis at dose exposures up 22 and 91-fold, respectively, the human exposure at the maximum recommended human dose (MRHD) based on area under the concentration-time curve (AUC); there were no adverse effects observed in the absence of maternal toxicity. Transport of endogenous immunoglobulin G (IgG) antibodies across the placenta increases as pregnancy progresses and peaks during the third trimester. Therefore, it is expected that narsoplimab will be present in neonates exposed to this medication in utero during the third trimester. Consider the potential clinical impact of narsoplimab exposure on neonates exposed in utero.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Use narsoplimab with caution during breast-feeding. There are no data on the presence of narsoplimab in human milk, its effects on the breast-fed child, or its effects on milk production. Endogenous maternal immunoglobulin G (IgG) and monoclonal antibodies are transferred into human milk. The effects of local gastrointestinal exposure and the extent of systemic exposure in the breast-fed child to narsoplimab are unknown. In an animal study, narsoplimab was detected in the blood of pre-weaning pups when measured on lactation day 21 after subcutaneous and intravenous administration to lactating mice. Consider the benefits of breast-feeding, the patient's clinical need for treatment, and any potential adverse effects on the breastfed child from the medication or from the patient's underlying medical condition.

Description
  Text
  Narsoplimab is a mannan-binding lectin-associated serine protease 2 (MASP-2) inhibitor indicated for the treatment of hematopoietic stem cell transplantation-associated thrombotic microangiopathy (TA-TMA). Inhibition of MASP-2 blocks lectin-dependent activation of complement component 3 (C3) and C4 without affecting the classical and alternative pathways of complement. MASP-2 inhibition is thought to prevent lectin pathway-mediated cellular injury, including endothelial injury in small blood vessels, in TA-TMA. Narsoplimab is a recombinant human immunoglobulin G4 (IgG4) monoclonal antibody. Serious and life-threatening infections have been reported with narsoplimab. Narsoplimab was FDA-approved in December 2025.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Narsoplimab inhibits mannan-binding lectin-associated serine protease 2 (MASP-2), the effector enzyme of the lectin pathway of the complement system. This inhibition blocks lectin-dependent activation of complement component 3 (C3) and C4 without affecting the classical and alternative pathways of complement. MASP-2 inhibition is thought to prevent lectin pathway-mediated cellular injury, including endothelial injury in small blood vessels, in hematopoietic stem cell transplantation-associated thrombotic microangiopathy (TA-TMA).
 
Pharmacodynamics
The effect of narsoplimab on lectin pathway activity using inhibition of C4d deposition was evaluated in healthy subjects and individuals with TA-TMA. Narsoplimab concentrations achieved in individuals with TA-TMA resulted in more than 80% inhibition of lectin pathway activity. A wash-out period of 6 weeks is sufficient to decrease narsoplimab concentrations to below pharmacologically active concentrations based on pharmacokinetic/pharmacodynamic modeling.

Narsoplimab is expected to be metabolized into small peptides and amino acids by catabolic pathways. Biotransformation or excretion studies have not been conducted with narsoplimab.   Route Specific Administrations
    Administration Details
      Name
      Intravenous Administration
      Description
      Calculate the dose (mg) based on the individual's body weight and the total volume (mL) of narsoplimab solution required.
Remove the narsoplimab vial from the refrigerator and allow the vial to reach room temperature [18 to 25 degrees C (64 to 77 degrees F)] for 30 minutes.
Storage: The vial must be used to prepare the appropriate dosing solution within 4 hours after removal from the refrigerator; discard any unused portion left in the vial.
 
Dilution
After dilution of narsoplimab, the solution may become opalescent, and small translucent-to-white particles may appear. Discard the prepared solution if particulate matter, other than translucent-to-white particles, is observed.
Storage: If the prepared solution is not used immediately, store the diluted narsoplimab solution at room temperature [18 to 25 degrees C (64 to 77 degrees F)] for up to 4 additional hours. Discard unused narsoplimab solution if not used within 4 hours from the time of dilution to the end of the infusion.
Intravenous Infusion Bag (for individuals weighing 10 kg or more)
Withdraw the required volume of narsoplimab solution from the vial using a polypropylene syringe and dilute in a polyvinyl chloride (PVC) IV infusion bag of 5% Dextrose Injection to yield a final concentration of 0.8 to 8 mg/mL with a total volume not to exceed 50 mL.
Gently invert the infusion bag 10 times to mix the diluted solution. Do not shake.
 
Syringe (for individuals weighing less than 10 kg)
Withdraw the required volume of narsoplimab solution from the vial using a polypropylene syringe and dilute in 5% Dextrose Injection to yield a final concentration of 0.8 mg/mL with a total volume not to exceed 50 mL.
Gently invert the syringe 10 times to mix the diluted solution. Do not shake.
Remove air bubbles from the syringe prior to administration.
 
Intravenous Infusion
Administer IV over 30 minutes by gravity infusion or infusion pump for solution prepared in an IV infusion bag or by syringe pump for solution prepared in a syringe through a polyvinyl chloride (PVC) or PVC-lined infusion line with a 0.2 micron polyethersulfone (PES) in-line filter and a polyurethane catheter.
Flush the IV line at the end of the infusion with a sufficient volume of 5% Dextrose Injection to clear the line of narsoplimab infusion solution.
Do not co-administer other medications through the same IV line.
Missed Dose: If a dose is missed, administer the dose as soon as possible, then resume dosing at the regular scheduled time.
