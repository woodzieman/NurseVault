Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  General Information
  Splenic enlargement and subsequent potential for splenic rupture may occur as a result of plerixafor treatment. In animal studies, plerixafor doses approximately 4-fold higher than the recommended human dose were administered daily for up to 2—4 weeks. Higher absolute and relative spleen weights associated with extramedullary hematopoiesis were observed. The effect of plerixafor on spleen size has not been effectively evaluated in clinical studies conducted in humans. Patients receiving plerixafor in combination with filgrastim should have their splenic integrity evaluated in the event of left upper abdominal pain and/or scapular or shoulder pain.
 
Monitor white blood cell counts during therapy with plerixafor. Exercise clinical judgment when administering plerixafor to patients with a peripheral blood neutrophil count > 50,000/mm3.
 
Plerixafor should not be used for hematopoietic stem cell mobilization and harvest in patients with leukemia. During hematopoietic stem cell mobilization, plerixafor may cause mobilization of leukemic cells and subsequent contamination of the apheresis product.
 
Plerixafor is excreted mainly through the kidney. Pharmacokinetic studies indicate an increase in plerixafor exposure in patients with moderate and severe renal impairment compared to patients with CrCl >= 50 ml/min. Patients with moderate or severe renal impairment require dose reduction of plerixafor (see Dosage). Coadministration of plerixafor with drugs that reduce renal function or compete for active tubular secretion, may increase serum concentrations of plerixafor or the coadministered drug. The effects of coadministration of plerixafor with nephrotoxic drugs, drugs that are renally eliminated, or drugs that are known to affect renal function have not been evaluated.
 
Use with caution in patients with thrombocytopenia. Thrombocytopenia has been observed in patients receiving plerixafor. Platelet counts should be monitored in all patients receiving plerixafor who then undergo apheresis.
 
The safety and effectiveness of plerixafor have not been established in children.
 
Plerixafor is classified as FDA pregnancy category D. Plerixafor may cause fetal harm when administered to a pregnant women. There are no adequate and well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Plerixafor was teratogenic in animals with the majority of embryo-fetal toxicities occurring at doses 10 times the recommended human dose. Women of child-bearing age should be advised to avoid becoming pregnant while they are receiving plerixafor. If this drug is used during pregnancy, or if the patient becomes pregnant while receiving plerixafor, the patient should be informed of the potential hazard to the fetus.
 
According to the manufacturer, plerixafor or breast-feeding should be discontinued because of the potential for serious adverse reactions in nursing infants from plerixafor. Plerixafor excretion into human milk is unknown. Consider the benefits of breast-feeding, the risk of infant drug exposure, and the risk of an untreated or inadequately treated condition. If a breast-feeding infant experiences an adverse effect related to a maternally ingested drug, healthcare providers are encouraged to report the adverse effect to the FDA.

Description
  Text
  Plerixafor is a hematopoietic stem cell mobilizer indicated for use in combination with filgrastim to mobilize hematopoietic stem cells to the peripheral blood for collection and subsequent autologous transplantation in patients with non-Hodgkin lymphoma or multiple myeloma. Serious hypersensitivity reactions such as anaphylactic shock have occurred with plerixafor use. Plerixafor was approved by the FDA in 2008.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Plerixafor is a hematopoietic stem cell mobilizer. It is an inhibitor of the CXCR4 chemokine receptor and blocks binding of its cognate ligand, stromal cell-derived factor-1 alpha (SDF-1 alpha). SDF-1 alpha and CXCR4 are recognized to play a role in the trafficking and homing of human hematopoietic stem cells to the marrow compartment. Once in the marrow, stem cell CXCR4 can act to help anchor these cells to the marrow matrix, either directly via SDF-1 alpha or through the induction of other adhesion molecules. Treatment with plerixafor results in leukocytosis and elevations in circulating hematopoietic progenitor cells.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Subcutaneous Route
    Description
    Plerixafor exhibits linear kinetics between the 0.04 mg/kg to 0.24 mg/kg dose. The Tmax occurs about 30 to 60 minutes after a subcutaneous dose. In a population pharmacokinetic analysis (n = 61), the mean AUC(0-10 hours) increased by 1.43-fold following fixed-dose plerixafor (20 mg) compared with weight-based plerixafor (0.24 mg/kg) in patients with non-Hodgkin lymphoma who weighed 70 kg or less.
  Special Populations
    Name
    Renal Impairment
    Description
    Following a single subcutaneous dose of plerixafor 0.24 mg/kg, the clearance was reduced and the exposure was higher in subjects with renal impairment compared with patients who had normal renal function. In a pharmacokinetic study, the mean plerixafor AUC(0-24 hours) values were increased by 7%, 32%, and 39% in subjects with mild (CrCl, 51 to 80 mL/min), moderate (CrCl, 31 to 50 mL/min), and severe (CrCl, less than 31 mL/min) renal impairment, respectively. Renal impairment did not affect the Cmax. Additionally, increased plerixafor exposure was observed in patients with moderate or severe renal impairment compared with patients who had a CrCl greater than 50 mL/min in a population pharmacokinetic analysis.

  Text
  Plerixafor is administered subcutaneously. It is up to 58% bound to plasma proteins, has an apparent volume of distribution of 0.3 L/kg, and is predominantly confined to the extravascular space. Plerixafor is not metabolized using human liver microsomes or human primary hepatocytes. The major route of plerixafor elimination is in the urine. In healthy subjects, approximately 70% of the parent drug was excreted in the urine within 24 hours of a single dose of plerixafor 0.24 mg/kg. The terminal half-life of plerixafor was 3 to 5 hours in healthy subjects and patients.
Pharmacodynamics: In healthy subjects who received single agent plerixafor, peak mobilization of CD34 cells was observed between 6 and 9 hours after administration. When plerixafor was administered in combination with filgrastim, a sustained elevation in the peripheral blood CD34 count was observed from 4 to 18 hours after plerixafor administration with a peak CD34 count between 10 and 14 hours.
 
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes and drug transporters: none
