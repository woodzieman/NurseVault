Drug Class: Tirbanibulin is a topical microtubule inhibitor indicated for the treatment of actinic keratosis of the face or scalp.

Routes: topical; Common adult doses: 100 cm2 or 3.5 mg (1 unit-dose packet) topically per application once daily.

Common Side Effects (excluding routine GI upset): edema, erythema, pruritus, skin erosion, skin irritation, skin ulcer

In an Overdose: No overdose/antidote information is listed in this file.

Key Nursing Considerations: Contraindications/precautions: This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components. Avoid use of tirbanibulin until skin is healed from any previous drug, procedure, or surgical treatment.

    Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    There are no available data on the use of tirbanibulin during human pregnancy to inform a drug-associated risk of major birth defects, miscarriages, or adverse outcomes. Oral administration of tirbanibulin to pregnant rats during organogenesis at exposures at least 19-times the exposure associated with the maximum recommended human dose (MRHD) resulted in an increased incidence of fetal deaths and external, visceral, and skeletal malformations. In pregnant rabbits, oral doses administered during organogenesis that were 41-times the exposure associated with the MRHD resulted in reduced mean fetal weight and size. Systemic exposure to tirbanibulin following topical administration is low; hence, fetal exposure is expected to be low.

Adverse Reactions
  Headline
  Adverse Reactions
  Adverse Reactions
    Headline
    edema, erythema, pruritus, skin erosion, skin irritation, skin ulcer
    General Information
    Treatment with tirbanibulin has been associated with the development of local dermatologic reactions, including severe reactions. These reactions are more likely to occur when an occlusive dressing is used to cover the treated area. Safety data from 2 clinical trials were pooled and stratified based on severity of mild (slight, barely perceptible), moderate (distinct presence), and severe (marked, intense). Specific local dermatologic reactions and their reported severity of mild, moderate, or severe included erythema (22%, 63%, 6%, respectively), skin scaling or flaking (26%, 47%, 9%), skin crusting (30%, 14%, 2%), edema or swelling (29%, 9%, less than 1%), vesiculation or pustulation (7%, less than 1%, less than 1%), and skin ulcer or skin erosion (9%, 3%, 0%). Other dermatologic adverse reactions reported by recipients of tirbanibulin included application site pruritus (9%) and pain or skin irritation, including tenderness, stinging and burning sensation, (10%).

Description
  Text
  Tirbanibulin is a topical microtubule inhibitor indicated for the treatment of actinic keratosis of the face or scalp. The drug is available in unit-dose packets; only 1 packet is to be used per application. To avoid accidental transfer of the drug to the eyes or mouth, instruct patients to wash their hands after each treatment and to avoid applying the drug near the mouth or to the periocular area. Tirbanibulin may cause local skin reactions. These reactions are more likely to occur if an occlusive dressing is used to cover the treated area.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Tirbanibulin is a topical microtubule inhibitor approved to treat actinic keratosis of the face or scalp. According to the FDA-approved product labeling, the mechanism of action of tirbanibulin for actinic keratosis is unknown; however, studies indicate the drug has a dual mechanism of action consisting of Src protein tyrosine kinase (PTK) inhibition and tubulin polymerization inhibition. Increased Src activity has been observed in hyperproliferative skin diseases, such as actinic keratosis.

Pharmacokinetics
  Route Specific Pharmacokinetics
    Name
    Topical Route
    Description
    Following topical application of a mean daily dose of 348 mg (range: 224 to 435 mg) of tirbanibulin to a 100 cm2 contiguous area of the face or balding scalp for 5 consecutive days. On treatment day 5, the mean maximum plasma concentration (Cmax) was 1.32 +/- 0.74 ng/mL for patients who received treatment on the face and 0.71 +/- 0.31 ng/mL for patients who received treatment on the balding scalp. The mean systemic drug exposure (AUC) at treatment day 5 was 19.6 +/- 8.1 hour x ng/mL and 11.7 +/- 4.4 hour x ng/mL for patients who received the face and scalp treatment, respectively. The time to reach Cmax was approximately 6 hours.
  Text
  Tirbanibulin is administered topically. Once in systemic circulation, tirbanibulin is 88% bound to plasma proteins; protein binding is independent of concentrations in the range of 0.01 to 10 microgram/mL. In vitro studies suggest tirbanibulin is mainly metabolized by CYP3A4, and to a lesser extent, CYP2C8. The three predominant metabolites, KX2-5036, KX2-5163 and KX2-5180, are pharmacologically inactive. Drug excretion has not been determined.
 
Affected cytochrome P450 isoenzymes and drug transporters: CYP1A2, CYP2B6, CYP2C8, CYP2C9, CYP2C19, CYP2D6, CYP3A4, Multidrug and toxin extrusion (MATE1 and MATE2-K), Organic anion transporting polypeptide (OATP1B1 and OATP1B3), Organic cation transporter (OCT1 and OCT2)
In vitro studies show that at an IC50 value of more than 17 microM, tirbanibulin and the KX2-5036 metabolite directly inhibit CYP1A2, CYP2B6, CYP2C8, CYP2C9, CYP2C19, CYP2D6, and CYP3A4. Additionally, at an IC50 value greater than 1 microM, tirbanibulin and the metabolites KX2-5036 and KX2-5180 inhibited MDR1, BCRP, MRP2, BSEP, MATE1, MATE2-K, OAT1, OTA3, OATP1B1, OATP1B3, OCT1, and or OCT2. These findings suggest that tirbanibulin, at the recommended dosing, has no clinically meaningful effect on the pharmacokinetics of drugs metabolized by these enzymes or mediated by these drug transporters.
