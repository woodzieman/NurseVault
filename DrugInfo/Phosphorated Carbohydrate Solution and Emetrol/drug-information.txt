Drug Class: Phosphorated carbohydrate solution is an over-the-counter oral solution containing a mixture of dextrose (glucose), levulose (fructose) and phosphoric acid.

Routes: oral; Common adult doses: 5 doses (150 ml) in 1 hour.

In an Overdose: No overdose/antidote information is listed in this file.

Key Nursing Considerations: Contraindications/precautions: Phosphorated carbohydrate solution should not be used in patients with a known hypersensitivity or intolerance to fructose, dextrose or phosphoric acid. Patients with hereditary fructose intolerance should avoid use of phosphorated carbohydrate solution due to the significant amounts of fructose.

Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  General Information
  Phosphorated carbohydrate solution should not be used in patients with a known hypersensitivity or intolerance to fructose, dextrose or phosphoric acid. Patients with hereditary fructose intolerance should avoid use of phosphorated carbohydrate solution due to the significant amounts of fructose.
 
Caution should be taken if symptoms of appendicitis or inflammatory bowel disease are present such as abdominal pain, anorexia and diarrhea. Consult a health care provider prior to use of phosphorated carbohydrate solution if these symptoms are present.
 
Patients with diabetes mellitus should avoid or limit the use of phosphorated carbohydrate solution due to the significant amounts of dextrose (glucose) and fructose.
 
Phosphorated carbohydrate solution should be used with caution in the elderly due to risk of fluid and electrolyte loss due to vomiting.
 
Phosphorated carbohydrate solution should be used with caution in children due to risk of fluid and electrolyte loss due to vomiting. Phosphorated carbohydrate solution should not be used without a prescription in infants or if the child is under 2 years of age. Consult a health care provider prior to use in these age groups.
 
Phosphorated carbohydrate solution is classified as FDA pregnancy category C, as no adequate and well controlled studies have been conducted in pregnant women. While historically recommended by practitioners for treating mild morning sickness , the use of phosphorated carbohydrate solution is not mentioned as a treatment strategy in current ACOG guidelines.
 
Use caution in administering phosphorated carbohydrate solution to the breast-feeding mother. While there have been no documented problems in breast-feeding mothers, no formal data is available.

    General Information
    No adequate and well controlled studies have been conducted with phosphorated carbohydrate solution during pregnancy. While historically recommended by practitioners for treating mild morning sickness due to pregnancy, the use of phosphorated carbohydrate solution is not mentioned as a management strategy in current ACOG guidelines.   Dietary modifications (smaller meals, bland non-spicy foods, avoid offensive odors) and other treatment strategies appear effective for mild nausea and vomiting and are recommended first-line instead of the use of non-prescription treatment. When a pregnant person experiences weight loss or dehydration in the first trimester of pregnancy due to nausea and vomiting, then specific pharmacologic treatment and/or nutritional support may be indicated.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Use of phosphorated carbohydrate solution is compatible with breast-feeding. There have been no documented problems in the breastfed infant from maternal use of phosphorated carbohydrate solution during lactation. The main ingredients in the solution, dextrose and fructose, are not expected to be a safety concern. There are no formal data for phosphoric acid, but this ingredient is found in many processed food products and drinks (e.g., carbonated beverages such as colas) that are often consumed daily.

Description
  Text
  Phosphorated carbohydrate solution is an over-the-counter oral solution containing a mixture of dextrose (glucose), levulose (fructose) and phosphoric acid. Phosphoric acid is added to adjust the pH of the commercial product to between 1.5 to 1.6. Phosphorated carbohydrate solution is used for the relief of stomach upset associated with intestinal flu, stomach flu, or food or drink indiscretions. Phosphorated carbohydrate solution has been used in an attempt to alleviate nausea ('morning sickness') associated with pregnancy. However, while historically recommended by practitioners for this purpose , the use of phosphorated carbohydrate solution is not mentioned as a treatment strategy in current guidelines for treating nausea/vomiting of pregnancy. Phosphorated carbohydrates is a FDA Category III non-prescription (OTC) ingredient; there is insufficient evidence to establish or refute effectiveness as an antiemetic.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Mechanism of Action: Phosphorated carbohydrate solution is a hyperosmolar carbohydrate solution with phosphoric acid that relieves nausea through a direct, local action. Theoretically, the simple sugar carbohydrates inhibit gastric emptying and reduce gastric tone.
