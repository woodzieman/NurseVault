          Dosage Description
          6 mg/m2 IV over 10 minutes at a rate of 3 mL/minute. Initiate light delivery (689 nm wavelength laser light) 15 minutes after the start of the infusion (i.e., 5 minutes after the end of the infusion). The photoactivation of verteporfin is controlled by the total light dose delivered. In the treatment of choroidal neovascularization, the recommended light dose of 50 J/cm2 of neovascular lesion is administered at an intensity of 600 mW/cm2. The non-thermal laser is administered over 83 seconds. Re-evaluate the patient after 3 months, and if choroidal neovascular leakage is detected, therapy should be repeated. The safety and efficacy of verteporfin therapy beyond 2 years have not been established. The average number of yearly treatments during clinical trials were: 3.5 in the first year after diagnosis, 2.4 in the second, 1.3 in the third, 0.4 in the fourth, and 0.1 in the fifth year for age-related macular degeneration; 3.5 in the first year after diagnosis, 1.8 in the second, 0.4 in the third, 0.2 in the fourth, and 0.1 in the fifth for pathologic myopia; 2.9 in the first year after diagnosis, 1.2 in the second, 0.2 in the third, and 0.1 in the fourth for presumed ocular histoplasmosis.

          In patients who present the first time with eligible lesions in both eyes without prior verteporfin therapy, it is prudent to only treat the 1 eye with the most aggressive lesion first (e.g., 6 mg/m2 IV with light delivery beginning 15 minutes after the start of the infusion). Contraindications And Precautions
  Headline
  Contraindications/Warnings/Precautions
  General Information
  Verteporfin is contraindicated in patients with porphyria or hypersensitivity to any of the components of the product including egg hypersensitivity or porphyrin hypersensitivity.Patients should avoid exposure of the skin and eyes to direct sunlight (UV) exposure or bright indoor light (examination lamps, including dental lamps, operating room lamps, sky lights, unshaded light bulbs at close proximity, etc) for 5 days following verteporfin therapy. Patients with conditions associated with skin photosensitivity disorder may be at increased of photosensitivity reactions. During this period, patients should avoid exposure of unprotected skin, eyes, or other body organs to direct sunlight or bright indoor light. If patients must go outdoors in daylight during the first 5 days after treatment, they should protect all parts of their skin and their eyes by wearing protective clothing and dark sunglasses. Ultraviolet (UV) sunscreens do not provide effective protection against photosensitivity reactions because photoactivation of the residual drug in the skin can be caused by visible light. Patients should not stay in the dark and should be encouraged to expose their skin to ambient indoor light, as this will help inactivate the drug in the skin through a process called photobleaching. If emergency surgery is necessary within 48 hours after verteporfin infusion, as much of the internal tissue as possible should be protected from intense light. Dental work done under high power lighting should be avoided for 5 days after verteporfin administration.Avoid extravasation of verteporfin injection. In the event of extravasation during verteporfin infusion, the infusion should be stopped immediately and cold compresses applied. The extravasation area must be thoroughly protected from direct light until the swelling and discoloration have faded in order to prevent the occurrence of a local burn, which could be severe. To avoid extravasation, verteporfin should be administered through a free-flowing IV line and avoid administration through the small veins in the back of the hand. Due to the possible fragility of vein walls of some older patients, it is strongly recommended that the largest arm vein possible, preferable the antecubital vein, be used for intravenous administration.During preparation and administration, health care professionals should avoid skin and ocular exposure to verteporfin due to the potential for photosensitivity reactions upon exposure to light. The use of rubber gloves and eye protection is recommended to prevent accidental exposure of health care professionals. Contaminated materials should be disposed of in a polyethylene bag in a manner consistent with local regulations. Verteporfin is neither a primary ocular or dermal irritant. However, because of its photosensitizing ability, verteporfin might be an eye or skin irritant in the presence of bright light. Any overexposed person must be protected from bright light.Patients who experience a severe decrease in visual acuity within one week of therapy should not be retreated until their vision returns to pretreatment levels. The treating physician should carefully consider the potential benefits and risks of subsequent verteporfin treatment in these patients.Use of incompatible lasers that do not provide the required characteristics of light for the photoactivation of verteporfin could result in incomplete treatment due to partial photoactivation of verteporfin, overtreatment due to overactivation of verteporfin, or damage to surrounding normal tissue.There is no experience of verteporfin therapy in patients with moderate to severe hepatic disease or impairment. Therefore, the use of verteporfin in these patients is not recommended.Use of concurrent radiation therapy may enhance the uptake of verteporfin by vascular endothelium.Although no increase in adverse effects was noted in elderly patients, a reduced treatment effect is seen with increasing age. Patients >= 75 years and patients with dark irides, occult lesions, or with < 50% classic choroidal neovasularization were less likely to benefit from verteporfin therapy during clinical trials.Verteporfin is classified as FDA pregnancy category C. There are no adequate and well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Verteporfin should be used during pregnancy only if the potential benefit justifies the risk to the fetus.Verteporfin is excreted in human milk. The concentration of verteporfin in the breast-milk of one woman was 66% of the corresponding plasma concentration following a 6 mg/m2 infusion. After 12 hours, the drug was undetectable in the milk; however, the metabolite was measurable for up to at least 48 hours. It is unknown what effect this exposure would have on a breast-feeding infant. Because of the potential risk of toxicity to the nursing infant, a decision should be made whether to postpone treatment or withhold breastfeeding. If treatment can not be postponed, witholding breast-feeding for 48 hours following treatment may lessen the exposure and reduce the risk of toxicity in the infant.The safety and effectiveness of verteporfin in children have not been established.

    Hypersensitivity
    General Information
    This medication is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to it or any of its components. Cases of anaphylactic reactions have been reported with treatment; thus, medical supervision is recommended during the infusion. If an anaphylactic reaction or other serious allergic reaction occurs, discontinue the infusion immediately and initiate appropriate therapy.

    General Information
    Patients should avoid exposure of the skin and eyes to direct sunlight exposure or bright indoor light (e.g. tanning salons, bright halogen lighting and high power lighting used in surgical operating rooms or dental offices), and light-emitting medical devices such as pulse oximeters for 5 days following verteporfin therapy. If an emergency planned procedure (e.g. surgery, dental work) is necessary within 48 hours after verteporfin infusion, as much of the internal tissue as possible should be protected from intense light.     Avoid extravasation of verteporfin injection. In the event of extravasation during verteporfin infusion, the infusion should be stopped immediately and cold compresses applied.     Headline
    pregnancy
    General Information
    There are no adequate and well-controlled studies regarding the use of verteporfin during human pregnancy to inform a drug-associated risk. In animal studies, an increased incidence of anophthalmia, microphthalmia, and wavy ribs were observed in the offspring of rats administered an intravenous dose approximately 40-fold the human exposure at the recommended clinical dose during organogenesis. No adverse fetal effects were observed in rats or rabbits at exposures 6-to 20-fold the human exposure at the recommended clinical dose. Verteporfin should be used during pregnancy only if the potential benefit justifies the risk to the fetus.
    Headline
    breast-feeding
    General Information
    Avoid the use of verteporfin during breast-feeding. Verteporfin and its metabolite are excreted in human milk. The concentration of verteporfin found in human milk was 66% of the corresponding plasma concentration after administration of a 6 mg/m2 intravenous infusion. Within 24 hours, human milk concentrations declined below the limit of quantification (2 nanograms/mL); however, the metabolite was measurable for up to at least 48 hours.  Because of the potential risk of toxicity to the breastfed child, a decision should be made whether to discontinue breast-feeding or postpone treatment, taking into account the patient's clinical need for treatment.

Description
  Text
  Verteporfin is a porphyrin derivative present in a 1:1 ratio of regioisomers. As a photosensitizing agent, verterporfin is used as part of photodynamic therapy (PDT). Photodynamic therapy is a two-stage process requiring administration of the drug photosensitizer followed by an activating light source. With verteporfin, a nonthermal red light (689 nm wavelength laser light) is utilized. Verteporfin PDT is used as a selective treatment for choroidal neovasularization (CNV) associated with macular degeneration. The verteporfin procedure helps prevent progression of the disease and preserve a patient's vision. In comparison to conventional laser photocoagulation therapy, verteporfin PDT offers the advantage of selective tissue injury due to localization of verteporfin in the CNV complex and decreased retinal injury due to the use of a non-thermal light source. A report of two randomized controlled trials of verteporfin PDT in patients with CNV in age-related macular degeneration showed that 61% of eyes assigned to verteporfin compared with 46% of eyes assigned to placebo maintained baseline visual acuity. In subgroup analysis, verteporfin efficacy was demonstrated when the area of CNV occupied 50% or more of the entire lesion, especially when there was no occult CNV. A reduced treatment effect is seen with increasing age. Patients 75 years or older and patients with dark irides, occult lesions, or with less than 50% classic choroidal neovasularization were less likely to benefit from verteporfin therapy during clinical trials. Verteporfin is being studied for the treatment of CNV due to other causes including angioid streaks or idiopathic causes. Verteporfin is also under investigation for the treatment of skin cancer, psoriasis, and rheumatoid arthritis.The FDA approved verteporfin in 2000.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Mechanism of Action: Verteporfin photodynamic therapy (PDT) causes local damage to the neovascular endothelium and produces vessel occlusion. Verteporfin is transported thru the blood stream by low-density lipoproteins (LDL). The verteporfin-LDL complex is taken up by endothelial cells of the abnormal choroidal blood vessels, which exhibit high levels of LDL-receptors. Verteporfin is then activated by a red laser light (689 nm wavelength); verteporfin has no effect on tissues until it is activated by light. Following light activation of verteporfin, the excited verteporfin molecule initiates the generation of toxic oxygen radicals and chain reactions that damage endothelium. Spin electrion transfer from verteporfin to oxygen generates the oxygen free radicals. Subsequent radical reactions can form superoxide and hydroxyl radicals. These free radicals cause cytotoxic effects by damaging plasma and intracellular membranes. Damage to the treated cells is noted within hours of PDT treatment. A necrotic reaction and associated inflammatory responses evolve over several days. Cessation of normal cellular movement and formation of multiple membrane blebs characterize cell damage. Blebs are balloon-like protrusions from the cell membrane and indicate severe membrane damage. After bleb formation, the cell no longer divides and dies. Mitochondrial damage after PDT includes inhibition of oxidative phosphorylation and electron transport enzymes, and reduction in cellular adenosine triphosphate (ATP) levels. Damaged neovascular endothelium releases procoagulant and vasoactive factors, such as platelet activating factor and thromboxane A2, that lead to thrombosis and vascular occlusion, resulting in platelet aggregation, clot formation, and vasoconstriction. Ultimately, verteporfin PDT leads to the destruction of new blood vessels in the area of choroidal neovascularization.

Pharmacokinetics
  Special Populations
    Name
    Hepatic Impairment
    Description
    In a study of patients with mild hepatic dysfunction, the AUC and Cmax of verteporfin were not significantly different from the control group; however, the half-life was increased by about 20%.
  Text
  Verteporfin is administered intravenously. Verteporfin has an elimination half-life of 5—6 hours. Verteporfin is only metabolized to a small extent by liver and plasma esterases. Liver oxidative enzyme systems, including the cytochrome P-450 system do not appear to play a role in the metabolism of verteporfin. Verteporfin is eliminated in the feces.

    Injectable Administration
    Description
    Photodynamic therapy (PDT) with verteporfin should be administered under the supervision of a trained medical provider.
Administration of PDT with verteporfin is a 2-step process, first is intravenous administration of verteporfin and second is photoactivation by laser light 15 minutes after the start of the 10-minute verteporfin infusion.
Consult manufacturer information for details relating to lesion size determination and spot size determination. Light dose, light intensity, ophthalmic lens magnification factor, and zoom lens setting are important parameters for the appropriate delivery of light to the predetermined treatment spot. Follow the laser systems manual for procedure set up and operation.
Visually inspect parenteral products for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration whenever solution and container permit.
Avoid contact with the eyes and skin during the preparation and administration process. Due to the potential of inducing photosensitivity reactions, any exposed person must be protected from bright light.
The use of an opaque covering of the syringe and tubing during administration may decrease the photoactivation of verteporfin.

Dosage Adjustment Guidelines
  Hepatic Impairment
  Verteporfin is not recommended for use in patients with moderate to severe hepatic impairment.

  No dosage adjustment is recommended.
