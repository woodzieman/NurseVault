    Headline
    acute bronchospasm, asthma, wheezing
    General Information
    Methacholine is contraindicated in adults or pediatric populations with a baseline FEV1 less than 60% predicted and adults with FEV1 less than 1.5 L. Obtain baseline spirometry before starting a methacholine challenge test. Severe acute bronchospasm can result from methacholine administration, including at the lowest dose. Because of the potential for severe bronchospasm, methacholine is not recommended in individuals with clinically apparent asthma or wheezing. Only consider methacholine use in individuals on chronic asthma medication if the accuracy of the asthma diagnosis is in doubt. In these individuals, only administer methacholine if spirometry is normal after withdrawal of asthma medication under supervision. Ensure emergency equipment and medication are readily available to treat acute respiratory distress. If the baseline spirometry is not performed or measured accurately, the initial FEV1 may be underestimated. In this situation, decreases in FEV1 may not be detected after escalating methacholine doses, which may result in administration of unnecessary higher doses and an increase in the risk for excessive bronchoconstriction.

Description
  Text
  Methacholine is an orally inhaled cholinergic agonist indicated for the diagnosis of bronchial airway hyperreactivity (methacholine challenge test) in patients 5 years of age and older who do not have clinically apparent asthma. Severe acute bronchospasm can result from methacholine administration, including at the lowest dose. Methacholine use requires an experienced clinician and a specialized care setting. For safety and accuracy, methacholine use is limited to a pulmonary function laboratory or clinic, by adequately trained personnel and only under the responsibility of a healthcare professional trained in and thoroughly familiar with the aspects of the technique of the methacholine challenge test and the management of respiratory distress. The methacholine challenge test may occasionally be false-positive after influenza or other upper respiratory infection; after vaccination; in very young or geriatric patients; in patients with chronic lung disease (CLD), such as cystic fibrosis, sarcoidosis, tuberculosis, or chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD); in patients with allergic rhinitis with or without asthma symptoms; in tobacco smoking patients; or in patients after exposure to air pollutants.

Mechanism Of Action
  Text
  Methacholine is a cholinergic agonist which stimulates the muscarinic receptors of bronchial smooth muscles to induce bronchoconstriction. After oral inhalation, patients with asthma are more sensitive to methacholine-induced bronchoconstriction than are healthy subjects; this difference in response is the pharmacologic basis for a methacholine challenge test. Methacholine is a structural analog of acetylcholine. The addition of a methyl group at the beta-carbon atom of choline produces a longer duration of action and increased selectivity of action compared to acetylcholine. Unlike acetylcholine, methacholine is hydrolyzed only by acetylcholinesterase and at a much slower rate. Also, methacholine binds directly to muscarinic receptors of smooth muscles, glands, and the heart and possess weak activity on nicotinic receptors of skeletal muscles.

Administration
Since the temperature of the solution affects nebulizer output, remove any refrigerated diluted solutions from the refrigerator and allow to equilibrate to room temperature (approximately 30 minutes) before use.
Measure a post-diluent forced expiratory volume in 1 second (FEV1) after nebulization of the diluent or base solution at the start of the methacholine challenge test and before administering methacholine.
If the FEV1 measurement is not of acceptable quality, repeat the post-diluent procedure.
Do not proceed with methacholine administration if the post-diluent FEV1 decreases by 20% or more from the baseline FEV1 and administer an inhaled beta-agonist.
The methacholine challenge test is considered positive if there is a reduction in FEV1 of 20% or more from the post-diluent FEV1. Calculate and record the FEV1 reduction value before starting the test.
Administer methacholine by oral inhalation, with or without food, using either the 1-minute tidal breathing dosing method or the 5-breath dosimeter dosing method with the dose doubling or quadrupling protocols.
Empty the nebulizer between each dose; discard any unused solution from the nebulizer after each administration.
Stop dosing if the FEV1 decreases by 20% or more from the post-diluent FEV1 or the highest methacholine concentration (16 mg/mL) has been administered, whichever comes first, or if severe bronchoconstriction occurs.
After the methacholine challenge test, administer an inhaled beta-agonist to expedite the return of the FEV1 to baseline and to relieve any discomfort the individual may experience.
Wait 10 minutes and measure FEV1 and vital capacity.
Monitor the individual until the FEV1 has returned to within 90% of baseline.
Most individuals revert to normal pulmonary function within 10 to 20 minutes after administration of an inhaled beta-agonist.
Wash and clean reusable nebulizers thoroughly according to manufacturer's recommendations. Discard disposable nebulizers appropriately.
1-Minute Tidal Breathing Dosing Method
Post-Diluent FEV1 Determination
Using a 3 mL syringe and needle, draw up 2 to 3 mL of sterile 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection, or use the supplied base solution for the ready-to-use kit.
Instill the diluent or base solution into the nebulizer using a sterile bacterial-retentive filter (0.22 millimicrons).
Place the face mask loosely over the nose and mouth, or place the mouthpiece in the mouth and apply a nose clip.
Instruct the individual to hold the nebulizer to avoid warming the solution. Keep the nebulizer upright and vertical.
Set the pressure regulator to 50 psi and the flow controller to 4.5 L/minute. Start the stopwatch immediately.
Instruct the individual to relax and breathe the aerosol quietly (tidal breathing) for 1 minute of inhalation time.
After exactly 1 minute of tidal breathing, turn off the nebulizer and flow meter, remove the face mask (or mouthpiece), and discard any remaining solution.
Perform spirometry and measure FEV1 at 30 and 90 seconds after the end of 1-minute tidal breathing.
Methacholine Challenge
Using a 3 mL syringe and needle, draw up at least 2 mL of the recommended methacholine concentration according to the selected doubling or quadrupling dosing protocol.
Use the quadrupling dosing protocol with the methacholine ready-to-use kit.
Instill the methacholine solution into the nebulizer using a sterile bacterial-retentive filter (0.22 millimicrons).
Repeat the administration procedure described above (i.e., placement of the face mask or mouthpiece, nebulizer settings, 1-minute tidal breathing, nebulizer shutdown, and spirometry at 30 and 90 seconds after inhalation) for each methacholine dose.
5-Breath Dosimeter Dosing Method
Post-Diluent FEV1 Determination
Using a 3 mL syringe and needle, draw up 2 to 3 mL of sterile 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection, or use the supplied base solution for the ready-to-use kit.
Instill the diluent or base solution into the nebulizer using a sterile bacterial-retentive filter (0.22 millimicrons).
Place the mouthpiece in the mouth and apply a nose clip.
Instruct the individual to hold the nebulizer upright.
At the end of exhalation during tidal breathing (functional residual capacity), instruct the individual to slowly and deeply inhale through the mouthpiece for approximately 5 seconds and to hold the breath at total lung capacity for another 5 seconds. Trigger the dosimeter soon after oral inhalation begins.
Repeat until a total of 5 inhalations have been completed within 2 minutes.
Perform spirometry and measure FEV1 at 30 and 90 seconds after the fifth inhalation.
Methacholine Challenge
Using a 3 mL syringe and needle, draw up 2 to 3 mL of the recommended methacholine concentration according to the selected doubling or quadrupling dosing protocol.
Use the quadrupling dosing protocol with the methacholine ready-to-use kit.
Instill the methacholine solution into the nebulizer using a sterile bacterial-retentive filter (0.22 millimicrons).
Repeat the administration procedure described above (i.e., mouthpiece with nose clip, 5 dosimeter-triggered inhalations from functional residual capacity to total lung capacity, approximately 5-second inhalation followed by a 5-second breath hold, completion of 5 inhalations within 2 minutes, and spirometry at 30 and 90 seconds after the fifth inhalation) for each methacholine dose.
    Name
    Inhalation Administration
    Description
    For safety and accuracy, limit methacholine use to a pulmonary function laboratory or clinic, by adequately trained personnel and only under the responsibility of a health care provider trained in and thoroughly familiar with the aspects of the technique of the methacholine challenge test and the management of respiratory distress.
Methacholine may cause bronchoconstriction in the health care providers administering methacholine. To avoid accidental exposure, precautionary steps are necessary for health care providers and any other personnel involved in methacholine administration.
Do not handle methacholine powder if have hay fever or asthma.
Do not inhale the methacholine powder.
Apply a low resistance filter to expiratory ports of the dosing apparatus, as necessary, to prevent methacholine release in the room air.
Ensure emergency equipment and medication are immediately available to treat acute respiratory distress. If severe bronchoconstriction occurs, reverse immediately by administration of a rapid-acting inhaled beta-agonist.
